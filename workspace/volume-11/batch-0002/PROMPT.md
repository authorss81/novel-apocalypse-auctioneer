## 5. The relays and the method that keeps them alive

Five of the six protected relays go on once a chapter without being noticed: `the record about the not asking says not asked`; `read the number back to himself in a low voice`; `got it up about nine inches`; `by ten there were about nineteen people`; `the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given`. `was not asked about the eleven miles` goes on fourteen of fifteen. `at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones` is a floor and not a target and may be silent on days when a different paragraph happens to that man.

**There is an eighth string and it is measured, not promised, and you should know it exists before you read a chapter.** The second-to-last paragraph of all fifteen chapters of Batch 0001 is byte-identical for its first **258 characters, 52 words**, from `Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five` to `and nobody there said one word to her, and `, and only the clause after that span changes. **It is not a protected relay, it was never declared by the outline, and no one broke it by keeping it. It is the longest identical span in the last block and it is the reason the frame measures 137.7 shared twelve-word runs a chapter rather than lower.** You may keep it and you may not keep all fifteen of the 52 words. **The default for this block is to break the identical span deliberately: keep the two buckets and the woman of fifty-eight, keep her reading the three lines on her way past, and let the rest of that paragraph differ from chapter to chapter.**

**The duplication method, and this is the one thing worth carrying out of the last block.** Batch 0001's first sweep found nine duplicated sentences in ninety-one places, all in the daily frame, and the paragraph-size sweep found none of them. Every one was repaired by **appending a chapter-specific clause to the end of the sentence** — a fact about that morning — with no scene, figure, time, name or beat moved. **A frame sentence repeated on fifteen mornings is not a defect to be deleted and is not a figure to be raised. It is a sentence that needs a different tail.** Run both sweeps before you finish: the sentence-size one is the one that finds things.

**Both of them are blind below twelve words, and this block had an eleven-word run that both of them passed. If you sweep at one size you have decided what size to look at.** Measured over Batch 0001's fifteen files the figures are **2,066 shared twelve-word runs** and **2,165 at eleven words**, not the 1,857 and the 1,964 that the record printed, so use these.

**And the two sweeps were blind to something much longer than an eleven-word run, which is the lesson worth taking out of this.** A paragraph that varies in its *last* clause is a paragraph that varies, to both of those sweeps, and Batch 0001's closing paragraph did exactly that across fifteen chapters with **258 identical characters and 52 identical words in front of the varying clause. The three sweeps the block ran returned 0, 0 and a list of runs, and none of them told the writer that the block's closing page was the same page fifteen times.** So: **sweep the closing paragraph on its own, at the size of the whole paragraph, and not at twelve words.** That is a fifth check and it is the one this batch had to build.
