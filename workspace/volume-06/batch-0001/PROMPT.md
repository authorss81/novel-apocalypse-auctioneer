## The four rules this phase inherits

**These come out of the review of the Volume 05 close and they are the reason that review happened. They are rules, not history, and this block is judged on them.**

1. **A CHARACTER DESCRIPTOR IS NOT A FIGURE TO BE CARRIED FORWARD OR AVERAGED. Closed canon is the record and a later volume's own majority is not.** The close read fourteen passages of Volume 05 against three for a character's age, took the majority, and changed fifteen chapters of closed canon the wrong way round; and it read a four-chapter minority against the rest for another character's age, fixed that, and never noticed that the same descriptor had made a canon woman a man in thirty passages. **When a descriptor disagrees with an earlier volume, go and read the earlier volume's prose. 2. **A REPAIR CLAIMED AND NOT MADE IS WORSE THAN NO CLAIM, because a reader checks the chapter and not the record.** The close claimed a misspelt word in two files and there was no misspelling; it claimed two state-file corrections and made neither; and it printed a table column headed *What the ten files give* that was the block record's own list added up. **Every figure under a heading that claims to be measured is measured before it is printed, and every repair you claim in a record is applied or withdrawn in the same pass.**
3. **A NARROW SCAN CANNOT VERIFY A WIDE REPAIR.** The scan that was meant to prove a repair had returned to zero was the pattern `forty-eight who keeps a tally`, and the one line it missed was `the man of about forty-eight said that it was not`, two paragraphs under the line it had just changed, in the same scene. **Scan for the thing and not for the thing with its trade attached, and run the scan on the prose as it stands after your last edit and not on the prose as it stood when you started.**

## The craft brief, and the rules that hold in the prose

**1. Hold the scenes, and report the frames.** The measurements for Volume 05 are in `state/volume-05-roll-summary.md` section 10 and the method is printed in full in `state/volume-05-batch-0005-summary.md` section 7, **so a reader can rebuild the table; that matters, because the first draft of that table was not reproducible from its own method and sixteen of its twenty-three rows moved when it was rebuilt.** **Do not raise a frame to a target and do not lower one to a target; the number is a disclosure, and a disclosure that reports only the half that agrees is not a disclosure.** The frame that rose in Volume 05's last block and the frame that fell — the word *lock*, at zero, in the volume's own block about nine locks — are both disclosed in the block record and neither is to be smoothed.

**2. Do not let a footer be the last place a person appears, and five separate things in a block must cost somebody their body.** Volume 05's last block did it with a lane with a wire on the left, a shoulder in the cold with a drop on two sides, a barn with one window and a lantern on a shelf that was not lit, a stranger at a gate four hundred yards away, and a man who said no before he was asked. **The cost is in scenes and not in a footer, and it must be paid by a person the block is about.**

**3. No person is named in narration.** Everyone is referred to by description, exactly as Chapters 201 to 250 do. **No place is named on any document and none is named in narration.** The ninth place is what the documents say. The country above the water is not to be given any name — Volume 05 named it once, in a registrar's quoted speech at Chapter 223, and the close repaired it to *above the water*, and **that repair is in the prose and in the two live state files and a Volume 06 writer may not put the name back.** Nine structures in the water, four generations of people who keep them and have never been on a page, and caves nobody has emptied. **The Lock Council is an antagonist of method and not a person and it has acquired no face in a hundred and fifty chapters and must not acquire one here.**

**4. The counting motif is load-bearing and every figure in it must reproduce. Count the printed sentence; do not estimate it. Words separated by spaces; a hyphenated numeral is one word. A claim is one number word attached to one printed sentence. An all-caps restatement at the foot of a chapter that repeats a count must repeat the body's figure, and a quotation of an earlier chapter's figure must repeat that chapter's figure.** Volume 05 carries 242 class-one claims and every one reproduces, **and the way that is true is that every one was set by measuring the printed sentence and writing the claim afterwards, and that the first drafts of them were wrong and were corrected by recounting and not by shortening the prose.** Two classes of claim: a number word attached by a claim phrase — *counted it and got*, *was counted and got*, *it came to*, *came to* — to a printed sentence in a quoted paragraph, resolved forward; and a number word in the form *in N words*, also resolved forward, **and if there is no printed paragraph under it, the figure is not a figure and the span must be printed.** A detector that sweeps only the first class cannot see the second, and a detector that is not calibrated on a known block cannot count anything: **run it on Chapters 231 to 240 first and report the class counts and the mismatch count separately.**

**5. The counts that did not move must not move without a reason on the page.** Things this district does not have **5**, the fifth being a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and it must still not be paid or priced; instruments built and not named **6**, and no seventh was proposed in the last block; documents nobody owns **3**; protected things **5** and no sixth; conditions with no end on it **4**; refusals to join two things **13** and the registrar's own count **12**; refusals to read **9** with the departure unspent; refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given **8**; boards **5** with lines full **6** and the seventh ruled and empty; columns of not-askings **4**; a rate turning a year into coppers **none, and there never has been one**. **Anything a chapter adds to a list must be added on the page, in a scene, with a person in it.**

**6. Binding cannot create consent. A certificate is not consent. A person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way and the record says not asked. An address is not a bearer. A name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on. A year is not an asset. A toll is a thing a named person pays under a term, and a party is not a person. A job is not a figure. A mark may not carry a job. A page that names a house is a true page and is not a finding about a person. A resemblance noticed a second time is not a finding and a third time is a habit.**

**7. Feet, inches, yards, miles, quarters, pounds, ounces, coppers, pints, degrees and the twelve-hour clock only. No metric, no twenty-four-hour clock, no weekday on any date, no colon-times.** A character may speak a weekday while a clerk of nineteen years enters that no weekday is attached to any date in this district; **Chapters 203 to 226 used the pattern in nine chapters and Chapters 227 to 250 in none, and if you count, count the chapters.**

**8. One System panel at most in a chapter and at most two in the block, and one may be disagreed with out loud. A panel may not create consent and may not make a person a bearer.** The reliable test in this register: **a document is introduced as a thing somebody reads out and is read back afterwards because it is the rule of the counter; a panel arrives unasked and is then argued with or not argued with.**

**9. At most about thirty-five bold clauses a chapter; Volume 05's last block ran 7 to 16. The count is a disclosure and not a target, and no clause may be added to any chapter to raise it.**

**10. A count has to be walked across the chapters and backwards out of the block as well as forwards through it.** A count checked only inside the block cannot see a count that arrived already full.
