## Batch goal

Three things, in this order and not the other way round.

**First, pay for the last batch in public.** Selik's closure has to be argued in the yard and not only in a chamber, and the argument has to be about a **permanent Warden route** and about who pays for an inspection whose end nobody can see. The ward market has money and no trust; the copper merchants and the chandler have a queue of eleven claims; Oveta Kraus's dissent is on the record and is the seed of the counter-argument; and Delia Fenn's year-eight permit — a roll on the near abutment is the reason her brother's body was found — is the seed of the other one. **Selik must be right about the instrument and wrong about one thing, and the thing he is wrong about must be a thing Adrian can name.**

**Second, make the counterfeit a solved-by-service problem and a live political one at the same time.** The comparison table has to work for a week and then fail in a way that teaches something: a claim that is entirely honest, a carrier who cannot read, a household that cannot come to a table, and a case in which **disclosure and remedy have to travel to the person rather than the person travelling to the table.** Hask Vell's third broker, and the region beyond the tram line, and the fact that a folded page is a solved problem while a fast page is not. **Halloway must be better again, and must say so, and must not be humiliated.**

**Third, write the terms.** Chapters 34–40: the people the Hearth Line touches are convened — the clinic, the shelter, Ward Nine families, the signal crew, and the Unlisted witnesses who are willing — and they write terms instead of bargaining for an exception. The **first multi-bearer public auction fails**, and it fails because a **transferable right was made to look neutral before the public knew who could refuse**, and because the remedy cannot identify who will absorb a false-destination loss once the use is split. It is *not* another concealed return toll. Adrian's instinct to impose a clean schedule makes it worse, in public, and he is the one who says so. Mara forces a rewrite in which the release condition and the appeal path are visible. And Adrian posts a real, inspectable asset as distinct security.

## Two beats that must survive

**1. The multi-bearer failure is a governance failure, not a concealment.** The split of the route across several bearers is technically coherent and produces a worse outcome because the standing belongs to the wrong person and the remedy has no addressee. The cost must be real and specific: somebody with standing is asked to carry a term that was written for another person, and a group's count is wrong in a way that is discovered by a person who counted on a ramp, not by anybody with a book. **The failure must be visible in a yard before it is visible in an audit.** Adrian must say, out loud and first, that the schedule was his, and that he imposed it.

**2. The Binder transition is a posting, not a gift.** Adrian posts a real asset, distinct from any toll, distinct from Batch 0003's payment, and enters it in the public book in words other people can check. It must cost him something that is still his after he posts it. **He is Stage 1 and cannot post security** — the transition is a *changed decision inside a rule he does not yet have power over*, and the prose must make that visible rather than a triumph. If the batch makes him a Binder by having the Tally accept something, delete it and write the scene again.

## Chapter cards

### Chapter 0031 — What the Inspection Costs

**POV:** Ivo Stenn.

**Location/time:** the ward market's counting room, the terraces, and Lot 17's yard, in the days after the ninth.

**Immediate goal:** Find out whether the ward market can fund the public inspection the closure order requires, and what happens to eleven trades and a ward on the far bank if it cannot.

**Resistance:** The inspection is paid for by the ward market and nobody can see the end of it, and Stenn is the man who has to ask a room of traders to fund a thing with no date. Selik's own words are that the inspection must cost money the district feels every month. **Oveta Kraus's dissent is on the record and is the strongest argument on Stenn's side.** Adrian has nothing to offer in exchange because his road may not exist, and the market knows it. Stenn is not a villain and is not a coward: he is a clerk who has spent three weeks saying no to people and would like, once, to be able to say yes to something.

**Major turn:** Stenn discovers that the inspection and the counterfeit are the same argument. **A man who cannot examine a claim cannot fund a public process either** — the district's traders, eleven of them, are being sold certainty they cannot check by three men on a printing plate, and a market that pays for an inspection nobody can read is not a market, it is a tribute. The ward market's money is better spent on a **public reader** than on a bridge, and that is a genuine and defensible institutional conclusion.

**Emotional consequence:** Stenn, who has wanted a published three-day service since Chapter 16 and been refused twice, discovers that the thing he actually wants is not a road. It is a room where anybody can find out what a piece of paper says. He does not become a convert to Adrian; he becomes a customer of the process, and it is not the same thing and the prose should not pretend it is.

**Ending type:** discovery. A trader puts a claim on Stenn's table, and Stenn cannot tell whether the ninth line is fair, and the room discovers that **the ward market's own clerk is one of the people the counterfeit is succeeding on.**

### Chapter 0032 — A Table Is Not a Church

**POV:** Tove Alard.

**Location/time:** the tram arch, the arcades, the terraces, and a kitchen under the tram line, over a week.

**Immediate goal:** Get the comparison table to reach a person who cannot come to it, before somebody who cannot read signs a nine-line claim that is entirely honest.

**Resistance:** The table works on paper and fails on people. It needs a reader, and a reader is a person with an evening. The new counterfeit has no fold, so the third page and the fourth page are both out of date. Hask Vell's tea table on the upper terraces is where the honest-looking claims are now sold, and it is a place a carter goes on purpose. **A claim that is entirely legal, entirely legible, and still takes seven days of somebody's labour out of a household that cannot spare it** is the hardest case the service has, and the service has no answer to it yet.

**Major turn:** The service discovers that **disclosure and remedy have to travel to the person.** A courier network — Lina's, unlisted, protected, and not to be enrolled — carries a reader and a lamp to a household at the cost of a night's walking, and the batch must be honest that this is a *worse* system than a table and is the only one that reaches some people at all. Tove is sixteen and is not a courier and does not get to go; she watches it happen and resents it and learns something.

**Emotional consequence:** Tove learns that a printed page is a thing made by people and is made of people, and that the moment she can set type she has also become somebody who decides what a claim says, which is a power she did not ask for and cannot refuse. **Orren has to be the one who teaches her this, and he has to admit he already knew it.**

**Ending type:** political pressure. The service reaches a household it could not have reached, and the household's answer is not gratitude — it is a complaint about the ward market's own inspection levy, which has just been collected for the third time and has produced nothing anybody can see.

### Chapter 0033 — One Office

**POV:** Selik Marne.

**Location/time:** the span, the near abutment, the ward market, and a public yard, over days.

**Immediate goal:** Win the argument for a permanent Warden route in a room that can decide it, before the inspection's first published day makes the question academic.

**Resistance:** **Selik is right about the instrument and wrong about one thing, and the thing he is wrong about is this: a permanent office is a permanent exception, and a man who has spent nine years refusing exceptions is proposing the largest one in the district.** He cannot see it from inside, because from inside an office is a service and not a claim. Mara Quill can, and she says so in a yard rather than a chamber, and Adrian can name it, and Selik's answer must be that a revocable office is not a thing anybody has ever built. **He must be given the credit for having written *one office is responsible* into an order at all, because that sentence is the volume's best institutional work and it is his.**

**Major turn:** The permanent-route proposal is **tabled and not adopted**, and it is tabled for a reason Adrian did not expect: **the ward market will not fund an inspection it cannot see the end of and will not accept an authority it cannot remove**, and the vote that stops Selik is Stenn's traders, not Mara's procedure. Selik loses the argument and keeps the closure, which is the correct outcome and is not what he wanted.

**Emotional consequence:** Selik is not humiliated and does not sulk. He says in public that the district has refused the safest answer available to it and that he will hold the flag anyway, and he asks for the review of his own order on a stated date, which is a thing nobody asked him for and which is the beginning of the argument that will carry Volume 02.

**Ending type:** decision. The district has a closure, an inspection it is funding, a counterfeit it cannot yet stop, and no road, and it has refused the one offer on the table. **The next question is what the affected people do with that.**

### Chapter 0034 — The People the Road Touches

**POV:** Mara Quill.

**Location/time:** Lot 17, the school gym, the cooperative, and the kitchen exchange, over days.

**Immediate goal:** Convene the people the Hearth Line actually touches — not the groups, the people, counted — and stop asking them to bargain for an exception.

**Resistance:** Fen Ambrose's Chapter 17 warning, restated and obeyed: do not write *the school shelter group* and mean everyone under a roof. Stenn's traders want a schedule. The clinic wants a date. The Unlisted witnesses want a category and no names. **Lina's protected channel is now a public service with her name on it, and the first thing the room tries to do is use it as a loophole, and Lina has to refuse in front of everyone, including Adrian.** Adrian is in the room and is increasingly the wrong person in it, and the chapter should be visibly about that.

**Emotional consequence:** Mara is the one in charge of a process for the first time in her life and she has to be given terms by people she has been auditing, and she discovers that the thing she has wanted for eight years — a settlement people consent to rather than a process people comply with — is mostly reading.

**Ending type:** discovery. One of the people in the room asks a question that no charter in the book has ever asked, and the question is about what happens to the *idea* of the road if the route never opens.

### Chapter 0035 — Splitting the Road

**POV:** Adrian Vale.

**Location/time:** the back office, the yard, and the Road House bench.

**Immediate goal:** Write terms that let more than one person hold the route, so that the Hearth Line stops depending on a single man with a bad shoulder and a memory he cannot explain.

**Resistance:** He is a Stage 1 Reader. **He cannot split a use among bearers.** He can draft what a split would look like and he cannot close it, and the honest version of this batch is that **he writes a document he is not allowed to close and has to find somebody who can.** The three-part reading gives him one object at a time, and the three candidate bearers each fail a different part of the standing test. Tarin will say no and say why, and Bryn must stay out of it.

**Major turn:** The split is drafted as **four named bearers with a rotating return crew**, and the standing sentence is the trouble: a return point is a named pair, and **a named pair with four bearers is a sentence about four people's feet, and the charter cannot be a sentence about all of their feet at once.** Adrian solves it in the wrong way, on purpose, in front of the room, and the prose must make the solving visible and wrong.

**Emotional consequence:** Adrian is a man whose best idea is a schedule, applying a schedule to a thing that is not a schedule, and everybody who loves him can see it except him. **He is not having a breakdown; he is having a competence, and it is the wrong competence, and this is the exact shape of his flaw and the first time it is aimed at the work rather than at a person.**

**Ending type:** decision. The schedule is entered, publicly, as a proposal, and four people are named in it, and one of them has not been asked.

### Chapter 0036 — The Four Who Have Not Been Asked

**POV:** Fen Ambrose.

**Location/time:** the school gym, the far bank receiving ramp, and the yard.

**Immediate goal:** Get four named bearers into or out of a charter without a steward's discretion becoming the manifest.

**Resistance:** Her own Chapter 17 clause, which is the thing that now bites. **A steward who can add a child out loud up to the opening of the case is a steward whose discretion is a manifest, and a four-bearer charter multiplies that problem by four.** The signal crew's tenth hand has a copied page and can check numbers and will check them. The clinic crew's responsible party is on this bank and has been responsible for boxes and not for people. And one of the four named bearers is a person nobody has asked, because they were named by a schedule.

**Major turn:** Fen forces the count into the open and discovers that **the schedule's four bearers and the charter's beneficiary groups do not add up to the same people**, and the difference is four households who were in a room and are not in a book. She does it with a tally sheet and a school roll and the plainest possible sentence in the batch, and she does it in front of Adrian.

**Emotional consequence:** Fen has spent the volume arguing that a count is a public thing and has never once had to use it against a man she likes. She is not wrong and she does not enjoy it, and she keeps the school roll anyway.

**Ending type:** discovery. The count is wrong, and the correction is a person nobody had counted because she chose not to be counted, and the record has to carry both the wrong count and the reason for it.

### Chapter 0037 — A Remedy for a Person Who Is Not There

**POV:** Mara Quill.

**Location/time:** Lot 17, the hearing, and the kitchen exchange.

**Immediate goal:** Build a remedy for a split use that works when the person who would absorb the loss is not one of the bearers and will never be in the book.

**Resistance:** Rell Bode's challenge, again, at a larger scale: **a remedy reached by naming the person is not a remedy, and a split use makes it worse, because the false-destination loss of a split route lands on whoever is nearest the fault boundary, and that is a fact about geometry and not about consent.** The no-name remedy is available and it fails loudly, and by Chapter 24 the yard knows what a loud failure costs. **Mara cannot fix this and must say so**, and the room must be asked to accept a remedy that will fail in public on a stated day, at a stated cost, in exchange for a road that might not exist.

**Major turn:** The room accepts a remedy that is **a hearing plus a pooled count plus a payment rule**, on the explicit terms that a failure of it is written down as a failure of the remedy and not as a fact about the people it failed. This is the no-name remedy made general, and it is the volume's actual constitution, and it is built by a registrar who has been arguing for it since Chapter 14 and who is now arguing for it as a cost rather than as a protection.

**Emotional consequence:** Mara offers the room a remedy she knows will fail and can price, which is the opposite of everything she has done, and she does it because the alternative is a remedy that fails quietly, which is Rell Bode's whole argument from the fourteenth and which is now, in Chapter 37, a thing that would kill a person.

**Ending type:** decision. The remedy is entered, and the board is asked whether it will record a closing on a remedy that is known in advance to be dischargeable in public and not otherwise. **The board must take a full day to answer, and its answer is the batch's last breath before the failure.**

### Chapter 0038 — The First Split Auction

**POV:** Adrian Vale.

**Location/time:** Lot 17, the yard, and the staging line, one day.

**Immediate goal:** Close a public, multi-bearer, one-activation charter in front of the whole city and let the market decide whether a public route can be more than one man.

**Resistance:** There is no token and no road to open, so this is not a crossing — it is a **closing of terms** that can be recorded without an activation, which is a thing the rules do not currently permit and which the batch must establish carefully: **a terms-closing can be recorded, witnessed, and made public, and it does not authorise a use.** That is the whole legal innovation of Chapters 34–40 and it must be built in public, by a board, over two chapters, not announced. Adrian is not a Binder and cannot close it. **The remedy is known to fail. The remedy is a hearing. The remedy will be discharged in public and recorded as not discharged.** The people in the room know all of that and are in the room anyway.

**Major turn:** The closing is recorded. Nobody opens anything. And the batch's first honest sentence about the future is spoken in a yard by a person nobody expected: **the closing is a promise about a token that may never open, and everybody in the room has just signed a piece of paper about a road that does not exist, and the paper is the only thing that is real.**

**Emotional consequence:** Adrian gets the thing he wanted at twenty-one — a document that makes a room orderly and safe and complete — and it is complete, and it is about nothing, and the yard knows it before he does.

**Ending type:** earned hook. A term in the recorded schedule cannot be performed because one of the four named bearers is on the far side of a river with no crossing, and nobody noticed, and the count was wrong by a household, and the closing is suspended the same evening by the board on the ground that it cannot be performed and was never checked.

### Chapter 0039 — Four Streets and a Wrong Count

**POV:** Adrian Vale.

**Location/time:** the marked spur, the river wall, the top of the service cut, and the yards, in bad weather.

**Immediate goal:** Find out who the fourth bearer is and why the count was wrong before somebody who is not in the book is asked to hold a route.

**Resistance:** The hardest physical chapter in the batch and there is no bus in it. **What happens is a foot.** A party goes out to walk a line to the top of the service cut because the schedule says the fourth bearer holds the line from there, and the ground under the ballast moves — not dramatically, a foot, the way a plank goes — and the party is on a rail and a lamp and a Warden's rule that nobody may go past the second rail. **Selik's fourth term from Chapter 21 is now the rule that saves somebody and the rule that stops somebody.** A person is hurt who is not in a charter, and the person is hurt because a schedule in a public book said where a man would be standing.

**Major turn:** The wrong bearer is found, and the reason is **not sabotage and not an error**: the schedule named a *role* where it needed a *person*, and the role-holder was a man who had been in the yard for twenty minutes and had said nothing, and nobody had asked him because the charter's whole argument was that the terms were complete. **Adrian's schedule is the cause and he says so first, in the yard, before anybody else.**

**Emotional consequence:** Adrian is not forgiven and is not condemned; the room simply uses him, correctly, as the man who is answerable for a document, which is the first time in seventeen years anybody has needed him for that instead of for a reading. **The cost is a real body and it is somebody who cannot be named in a public book, because they are protected, and the remedy for it is a hearing that will be recorded as not discharged.**

**Ending type:** discovery. The person who is hurt is the one nobody counted, and the count was wrong because a protected household was counted by its steward instead of by itself, and the chapter ends with a steward and a registrar and a cook looking at a strip that does not match.

### Chapter 0040 — The Book Opens Again

**POV:** Mara Quill.

**Location/time:** Lot 17, the yard, the hearing, and the kitchen exchange, over two days.

**Immediate goal:** Reopen the lot book in front of the people the wrong schedule touched, in public, and record the failure in the same habit she used in Chapter 12 and Chapter 24 — the false alarm and the evacuation, the cost and the absence of a cost, in one entry, so that nothing in this passes as bad luck.

**Resistance:** A public lot book that has to record its own failure is a book that can end the trust it is built on, and the traders who read the November entries have already gone to the terraces. **Rell Bode is entitled to the floor and is entitled to say that a book which records its failures loudly is a book that will be used against the people in it**, and she should say it.
