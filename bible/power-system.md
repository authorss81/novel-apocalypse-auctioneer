# Power System

## Core premise

The power is **conditional value**, not raw strength. A person, team, or settlement can use a dangerous object when its use, toll, standing, and term are stated clearly enough for the Tally to recognize the settlement. The Tally records and enforces consequences; it does not grant the use by itself.

Adrian's special ability is the ability to read the relationship between an object and the situation around it. Other people can learn parts of this work, and the story must show that learning. Adrian's advantage is speed, pattern recognition, and a willingness to expose inconvenient terms.

## The Tally

The Tally is a distributed accounting presence made from emergency records, witness marks, and the unfinished Common Measure. It appears in three ways:

- as a short inscription in a physical ledger when several people witness a settlement;
- as a brief internal notice for a person touching or naming an anchor lot;
- as a brief internal notice for a **reader** touching a **public record in which a use, a toll, or a crossing is written**, where the record is one the reader has handled and knows the history of;
- as a physical mark on a charter, object, or affected place after a closing.

It has no avatar, no inventory, and no reliable quest voice. It does not know the future. It can see declared uses, recorded tolls, observed outcomes, and the standing of a bearer. It can miss hidden motives, counterfeit histories, unrecorded people, and lies made with genuine ignorance.

### Tally messages

Messages are plain, short, and written in the language of use and consequence. A typical message might be:

```text
LOT: THE LAST FARE
USE: ONE ROUTE ACROSS ST. ORRA. TWELVE MINUTES.
TOLL: EACH TRAVELER LOSES THE REASON FOR THE JOURNEY.
TERMS ARE INCOMPLETE. NAME THE RETURN POINT.
```

**Reading a record is the same act with a wider door, and the wider door has a cost.** It requires that the reader has handled the record and knows its history; it returns a reading and it returns the echo; and it may not be used to reach anything the reader could not have reached by reading the page himself. It has been used once, in Chapter 30, to produce a three-line answer about nineteen people accounted for by hand, and the answer was a refusal: the Tally declined to certify a passage it had no charter for. **A later writer must not use it to read a document nobody has handled, to read a person, or to read ahead.** It is a way of consulting the record, not a way of enlarging the reading.

The Tally may warn, refuse, or display a consequence. It does not display experience points, numerical stats, or a quest log. It may be wrong when the record is wrong. Adrian and the people around him must investigate and act.

## Required parts of a consensual charter

A valid charter has seven parts. Missing parts create different failures rather than a generic rejection.

1. **Lot:** the object, its current condition, and the Fault it belongs to.
2. **Use:** the one bounded change being authorized.
3. **Bearer:** the person or group that operates the object.
4. **Beneficiaries:** the people who receive the change and the people exposed to its effects.
5. **Toll:** what is paid, by whom, and at what point.
6. **Term:** how long the use lasts and where it applies.
7. **Witnesses and remedy:** at least two independent people who freely understand and accept the recorded role, plus what happens on breach, expiry, or refusal. Their assent must be fresh: given after disclosure, not supplied by a Custodian or inherited key.

A charter can be amended only by a new closing. A change hidden in a side promise is not part of the charter unless a witness understands and accepts it.

A witness's function outlives the closing, because a remedy that rests on appeal has to be described afterward by the people who accepted it. A person who travels on the lot they witness therefore cannot be counted in the required pair: the toll is taken from the same memory the appeal asks about. The repair is not to pretend the toll does not apply, but to keep the pair on the side that keeps its memory and to name the people who will answer on the far bank in the record. Where a witness is also a beneficiary's responsible party, the charter must give that duty to a named substitute.

An **inactive custody charter** is a narrow exception to the ordinary use charter. It names an unactivated lot, a finder, a receiving bonded depot, a custody term, two witnesses, and a remedy for unclaimed delivery. It grants physical custody only. It does not authorize a use, create a bearer role, or substitute for a valid closing. Contact with the lot is not activation.

### Founder override and forced records

A founder-mark closure is not a valid consensual charter. It is a mechanically recognized **forced record** attached to one or more already active charters. The Tally enforces the recorded use, toll, and permanent term, but the record does not prove that the named bearers, beneficiaries, or witnesses consented. Every forced record carries a visible override mark and remains challengeable through a later public closing.

The override bypasses exactly two of the Common Measure's safeguards: **fresh independent voluntary witnesses** and **a term limit on the linked settlement**. The First House substitutes the Custodian for independent witnesses and writes a permanent term. It does not bypass disclosure of the lot, use, beneficiaries, toll, or standing; it cannot create consent, erase backwash, or enroll someone in a new use that was not already present in an active charter.

A forced record is an emergency claim, not an ownership interest. A later valid consensual closing may replace it for the lots it names if the closing identifies the override, states the remedy, and receives acceptance from at least two fresh independent witnesses. The Tally gives that replacement priority because the founder's bypass cannot create a superior right. The witnesses and term limit do not “defeat” the override by defeating its own safeguards; they make the consensual replacement valid under the ordinary closing rules.

## The three readings

### Use

The physical or social change the lot can produce. It is the easiest reading and the least useful by itself. A bridge that opens is not safe if the bridge's other end is under a Fault.

### Toll

The price paid to keep the use active. A toll must be attached to a bearer, a beneficiary, or a place. It may be:

- a physical cost such as exhaustion, burns, blood loss, or lost years of health;
- a time cost such as a route closing, a harvest delay, or lost work;
- a memory cost such as forgetting a face, destination, skill, or promise;
- a labor cost such as maintaining a channel, guard, or shelter;
- a relationship cost such as being recognized by a household or refusing a family obligation;
- a rule cost such as nearby doors locking at dusk or all spoken promises becoming binding.

The Tally can record the type and timing of a toll. It cannot determine whether the toll is fair.

A toll may be due at activation, renewal, or breach. A bearer may knowingly offer to transfer or consume a right or asset under their control as a one-time toll, provided the choice is explicit in the closing. The Tally never conscripts another person's labor, memory, identity, or property into that payment. If a closing draws on the same holdings for toll and security, it must name the exact asset consumed as the toll and a distinct asset posted as security. The toll is spent when due even if the charter succeeds; the separate security is forfeited only when the charter breaches.

**A toll charged to a place, and a person inside a vehicle is not charged at all.** The Harrow Tongue's toll is the origin, and the origin is a place. Three consequences are now canon and must not be smoothed over:

1. **The object does not hold places and will not name one.** A reading of a lot whose toll falls on a place may state *what kind of thing* is charged and may not state which one, and it may not be pushed into naming it. **A reader must not supply the place by inference**, because the choice of which place is charged is a decision about who suffers, not a fact about the object.
2. **A person carried inside a moving vehicle is not a bearer and is not charged.** The reading of the Tongue states the toll is the origin and not the person, so the passengers on a reversed train pay nothing, and the yard's finding is that **the passengers are free and the place is charged.** The eighth line at Lot 17 — *a toll is charged to a person and not to a thing* — is a rule about roads Adrian Vale built, and it is not a law, and Volume 02 says so in a yard.
3. **The readings ledger must record that a toll's destination was not named.** Four candidate readings of one word are entered and none is adopted, because the board cannot strike a line and a book with one answer in it would be a book that has guessed.

### Standing

The conditions that make a bearer able to carry out the use without becoming the default victim of a breach. Standing may be physical, professional, relational, or geographical.

Examples:

- The Last Fare needs a driver who knows the route before the route opens.
- The Salt Loom needs a worker who has tended the channel and accepts a year-long obligation.
- The Hearth Bell needs a shelter with a public entrance and a keeper willing to remove its record afterward.
- A door lot may recognize a keyholder, a resident, or a person carrying a particular debt.

Where the toll takes the memory of purpose, the driver cannot be trusted to retrieve a destination from memory at the far end of the Fault, because what survives the toll is the place and the skill while the reason is gone. A return point must therefore carry the load memory cannot: it is a **named pair** of a place, an approach, and the person able to hold them, and the charter must also state a **far-side condition** covering what the bearer is authorized to do when the far side is not the road he left on. A landmark alone is not standing, because a landmark can be moved by a person with a spanner, and a person who has forgotten why he is driving cannot notice.

Standing is not a character level. A person can have standing for one lot and none for another.

## What Adrian can read

At first Adrian can see:

- the most likely use;
- one immediate toll;
- whether the object can be safely operated by the current bearer;
- whether a proposed term is physically possible.

He cannot see:

- a hidden second toll if it is concealed by a counterfeit or incomplete record;
- whether a witness is sincere;
- the full social consequences of a fair bargain;
- an object he has not touched, heard about, or seen used;
- the future result of a new combination of charters.

The Tally can expose a contradiction between known terms, but it does not tell Adrian whom to trust. His ability can make him persuasive and dangerous: a clear reading can become a public promise that people cannot easily challenge.

## How growth works

Growth is earned through four kinds of practice:

1. **Inspection:** Adrian studies an object, its history, and the conditions of its Fault.
2. **Experimentation:** he tests a small term, records the result, and accepts the cost.
3. **Relationship work:** he learns which people can witness, operate, and challenge a settlement.
4. **Changed decisions:** he gives up a profitable or impressive option because the toll falls on the wrong people.

He grows faster when he lets Mara, Tarin, Orren, Lina, guild inspectors, and affected communities correct his reading. A stage is not awarded by a panel. It is a new responsibility that becomes possible after repeated evidence and a real loss.

## Power stages

### Stage 0 — Practical broker

Before the Long Fracture, and at the start of the story, Adrian is a competent ordinary auctioneer. He can inspect property, detect common repairs, read a crowd, and write a clear lot description. He has no supernatural authority and no safe way to activate a Fault object.

**Failure:** a technically impressive sale can still conceal a social cost, because ordinary auctions do not require the Tally to accept responsibility.

### Stage 1

Adrian can perform a full three-part reading on one object after contact or a reliable account. He can close a simple charter with one bearer, a short term, and a visible toll.

**How earned:** he survives the first Last Fare failure, keeps a promise to an affected person, and learns to state what he does not know.

**Cost:** every reading leaves a **toll echo**. Adrian's body pays a fraction of the object's immediate toll: exhaustion, dizziness, pain, temporary loss of a sense, or a memory blur. A false reading can make him the default bearer until the settlement is repaired. He cannot read continuously without rest.

**The echo is a fraction of that lot's toll and it is specific to it.** On the Last Fare, which takes the memory of why a person is travelling, the echo cost an hour of not being able to tell whether a thing was nearer or further than it had been. **On the Harrow Tongue, whose toll is a forgetting of origin, the echo cost him the ability to say which way the river went** — for about fifty minutes, on a bank, nine miles from the book, with a keeper standing in the room. He wrote it down himself and it is in the public lot book. **The echo of an origin toll is a loss of spatial sense, and a person who has it must not hold a pair, and cannot tell which way a river is going, and must be walked to a door by somebody who can.** Do not use the echo as a generic cost; use the cost the lot's own toll implies.

**Hard limit:** one lot at a time; contact or history required; no prediction; no control of another person. **And a refusal to read is a reading too, and it is entered.** Adrian read the Harrow Tongue on the eleventh of the current month, was entered on the twelfth, and was given a fresh limit of not reading again before the eighteenth. He came back on the eighteenth, the day the limit permitted a reading, and did not read, and the interval was **six days, not a season** — he said in a yard that six days is not long enough for him to have learned anything and is long enough for him to have stopped. The non-event was entered with **no cost and no time**, and the district's tenth hand started a separate page of his own for the numbers he cannot put anywhere, and the refusal is on it. **A batch may not treat a refusal to read as cowardice or as discipline. It is neither and it is entered as neither.**

**The one-lot limit and a split, stated exactly (Chapters 35–40).** Standing is per bearer. A three-part reading can return the standing of **one** of several bearers; the others are not a reading, they are a list. Asked about four bearers of one lot, the object answers that the use and the toll are stated and the standing is stated *for one of them*, and Adrian cannot fill the rest by trying harder. **He also cannot close the split, cannot bind it, cannot transfer it, cannot renew it, and cannot post security.** A document he drafts is a **proposal**; somebody with the power to close it has to read the clauses aloud in a room. And the **held asset** of Chapter 40 is not a promotion: it is a changed decision inside a rule he does not have power over, and the entry that records it says so in the same line.

### Stage 2 — Binder

Adrian can write a bounded charter, split a use among several bearers, and close a transfer without changing the underlying object. He can make an ordinary lot portable or renewable under a stated term.

**How earned:** he survives a failed public auction, works with Mara to rewrite its terms, and accepts liability for a piece of his own inventory as security.

**Cost:** he must post **security** before closing: a real object, recognized standing, skill, labor, route access, or named obligation. If the charter breaches, the security is lost and the Tally may place a visible claim mark on his record. He cannot use a promise he has not personally inspected as security.

**Hard limit:** binding cannot create consent. A person who materially refuses cannot be counted as a willing witness, even if a coercive charter or a founder-mark forced record is mechanically recognized. **A person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way; the record must say `not asked`, and a charter that counts an unasked person is invalid at the point of the count rather than at the point of the closing. A remembering passenger is a condition on the use and not a bearer** unless a later Stage 2 charter expressly assigns one, and a Stage 1 reader has no power to make that assignment.

### Stage 3 — Steward

Adrian can maintain a public lot, coordinate more than one bearer, renew a charter, and arrange a dispute hearing. He can make the consequences of an object visible to a whole settlement.

**How earned:** he runs a market through a full Fault cycle, loses a valuable lot, and prevents the loss from becoming a private monopoly.

**Cost:** while a public lot is active, Adrian becomes the temporary bearer of any unpaid aggregate toll. He cannot leave the relevant Fault without a relief steward, and every affected person can see his name and security in the lot book. He is no longer a neutral broker.

**Hard limit:** he cannot authorize a use for people who have not been informed. Public visibility can expose him to pressure and sabotage.

### Stage 4 — Underwriter

Adrian can price linked lots, pool tolls, and anticipate where backwash will travel. He can negotiate a trade route whose safety depends on several charters rather than one magical object.

**How earned:** he works with Tarin and the Salt Verge through a failed harvest, exposes Nera Voss's hidden labor security, and chooses a route that will lose his own commission.

**Cost:** underwriting makes his decisions consequential beyond the immediate lot. A mispriced route can return physical debt and social accusation to him, his crew, and the people who trusted the figures. He can no longer pretend that a private bargain is harmless.

**Hard limit:** he can see likely connections, not certainties. A falsified record can make a sound route look unsafe, and a dishonest community can make an unsafe route look sound.

### Stage 5 — Convener

Adrian can negotiate a covenant among several communities, split one lot's use among separate bearers, and place different portions of a toll under different stewards. He can make a regional public market function without one central owner.

**How earned:** he gives up a monopoly in the Alder Reach market, works with Lina's Unlisted representatives, and accepts a settlement whose terms he could not have written alone.

**Cost:** his name becomes a public index. He can no longer disappear inside a private sale, and every community may call on him to answer for a term. He must accept a decision made by a group that does not always agree with him.

**Hard limit:** a covenant remains vulnerable to a single community's withdrawal. Adrian cannot bind people who are not represented.

### Stage 6 — Commonskeeper

Adrian can help establish a durable open market: local public lot books, rotating stewards, shared appeals, limited terms, and a process for revoking dangerous uses. This is the final power state, not a permanent throne.

**How earned:** he learns to teach the reading, gives up his private claim, and accepts that the Tally will be maintained by many people and imperfect records.

**Cost:** the exceptional reading mark burns out. Adrian keeps his trained eye and negotiating skill, but he can no longer see the Tally's deep layers, open a private charter, or serve as the sole guarantor. The market can continue without him.

**Hard limit:** the Civic Tally is vulnerable to low participation, corruption, and local abuse. It is infrastructure, not a spell that solves politics.

## Personal overdraft

When Adrian sponsors or underwrites more active claims than his recognition can support, he enters **overdraft**. He also enters it if he makes his recognition the shared security for a set of emergency lots opened under a public mandate: a political title alone causes no overdraft, but the enforceable recognition-backed claims count even after he withdraws the mandate.

Overdraft is visible in ordinary life:

- his signature is rejected by ordinary municipal contracts;
- his possessions appear as unclaimed in Tally notices;
- witnesses hesitate to trust his terms because his name no longer appears as standing;
- a charter he has not secured may treat him as the default bearer.

Overdraft is not a random debuff. It is the natural limit of making one person's recognition carry too many public promises. It can be reduced by settling debts, withdrawing a lot, and accepting a community's terms, but it leaves a physical cost: sleep disruption, tremors, temporary loss of a sense, and a visible mark on his palm. Adrian cannot use it as a limitless resource or hide it permanently.

## Failure modes

### Overvaluation

Adrian names a high enough price or an optimistic toll path that people accept a dangerous use. The charter is valid and can still fail. The resulting backwash is worse because no one prepared for it.

### Undervaluation

He prices a lot too low, making a private owner seem harmless while hiding the labor or memory extracted from others. The market expands quickly, then collapses when the hidden bill arrives.

### Missing witnesses

He records only the operator and buyer. The Tally may accept the formal closing, but affected people can challenge the record. A later backwash carries a social accusation against the witnesses.

### Counterfeit terms

A forged addendum hides a second toll. The Tally cannot identify the forgery by itself. The failure can destroy trust in the whole lot book.

### Overload

Too many active charters use Adrian's recognition as security. He becomes the bearer of their combined tolls and can be targeted or incapacitated.

### Destruction without replacement

A community destroys an anchor and loses a critical rule. The Tally reports `BLIND CHANGE. NO BEARER.` It cannot tell the community what the missing service was for.

### Permanent claim

A faction uses emergency law to turn a temporary charter into an inheritable right. The charter can appear valid, but its toll grows until the place becomes unsafe. The Tally can show the growth; only people can decide whether to revoke it.

## How other people compete

- Guild inspectors use instruments and checklists to verify common lots.
- Wardens use public authority to limit who enters a Fault.
- Underwriters use contracts and pooled security to magnify access.
- Memory keepers preserve records the Tally omitted.
- Unlisted brokers counterfeit only the parts of a charter the Tally cannot see.
- The Lock Council uses old keys and founder law to make exceptions appear normal.

No rival has Adrian's exact reading ability, and no rival lacks a way to counter his work. The final conflict is about who gets to define and maintain the market, not whether one character has the largest personal power.

## What the power can never do

It cannot:

- resurrect the dead;
- make a person consent;
- predict every outcome;
- erase a toll after it is paid;
- make an unsafe use safe through a better name;
- guarantee that a public system is good;
- give Adrian the right to decide the final value of a human life.
