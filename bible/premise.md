# Premise

## Working identity

- **Title:** *The Apocalypse Auctioneer*
- **Genre:** system apocalypse / market progression / civic fantasy
- **Target length:** 840 chapters across 17 volumes
- **Lead:** Adrian Vale, male
- **Relationship policy:** one slow-burn relationship, Adrian and Mara Quill
- **Ending destination:** the dangerous-use market becomes transparent, revocable public infrastructure rather than a private throne

## One-sentence premise

After the Long Fracture turns disaster zones into places where one ordinary object can bend a local rule, a practical municipal auctioneer must auction the right to use those objects without letting profit, fear, or the Tally turn temporary power into permanent ownership.

## Central promise

Every major object is a complete problem with three parts:

1. **Use:** the bounded change the object can produce.
2. **Toll:** the physical, temporal, social, or rule-based cost of producing that change.
3. **Standing:** the people, places, and conditions under which the use can be carried safely.

Adrian can read those relationships faster and more clearly than other valuers, but he cannot know the future or decide what a life is worth. The drama is not whether he can acquire a magic item. It is whether he can make the terms of its use honest, temporary, and answerable to the people who bear the toll.

## Opening situation

Seventeen years after the Long Fracture, Alder Reach still depends on a patchwork of civic services, caravan routes, and unlicensed salvage. The Tally has begun appearing in the margins of municipal ledgers: it records a proposed use, names a toll, and asks for witnesses. Most people treat it as a broken piece of the old administrative system.

Adrian Vale runs a small surplus-and-salvage auction from a converted tram depot. He is good at finding the one useful flaw in a damaged object, keeping a crowd from panicking, and making a sale sound simple. When he tries to value a brass transit token recovered from the St. Orra Fault, the Tally opens a new charter in his mind and forces him to confront a fact that ordinary auctions usually hide: the useful part of an object cannot be separated from the people who will pay for it.

The token, the **Last Fare**, can reopen a route across the fault for twelve minutes. Every traveler gives up the memory of why they were going somewhere. Adrian can sell its use to save a medicine convoy, but a private buyer could keep the route closed, resell access, or use the toll to identify and pressure the Unlisted.

## Protagonist contract

### External want

At the beginning, Adrian wants to buy a stable charter for the **Hearth Line**, the only practical route between Alder Reach's isolated neighborhoods. He tells himself that a functioning route will keep his workshop, his neighbors, and his own name solvent. He also wants to prove that his old mentor, Iven Tallow, did not build a system that merely turns survivors into accountable numbers.

By the middle volumes, this want expands: Adrian wants to prevent the Tally's coming **Great Closing**, a forced conversion of temporary use rights into one permanent settlement. He begins by seeking a larger personal mandate. By the end, he wants a system in which no one—including himself—can privately hold the right to decide how a whole region survives.

### Internal need

Adrian must stop treating value as a way to avoid responsibility. He has to state uncertainty, accept that some things cannot be honestly priced, share the authority to close a charter, and let other people revise his decisions. His final growth is not becoming a better monopolist. It is becoming a trustworthy witness who no longer needs to be the only witness.

### Central flaw

Adrian compulsively turns uncertainty into a price. It soothes him because a named cost feels like control. The habit appears as overconfident valuations, concealed security, a tendency to bargain with people as if their objections were bidding terms, and the belief that if he can calculate a loss, he has made it bearable.

His deeper fear is that people will follow his terms and die anyway. The event he cannot stop trying to prevent is a public evacuation in which he set the conditions, watched the route fail, and reduced the dead to a final balance.

### Fear that changes the ending

His fear is not simply that the Tally will fail. It is that failure will make people accept the first strong person willing to promise safety. In the final volume, that temptation is real: only one guaranteed founder can prevent immediate catastrophe.

## Engine of the series

The series is built from regional Faults, each with a different useful object and a different impossible allocation of cost. A local sale can save one district and expose another. A perfectly valid contract can still be cruel, and a compassionate choice can still be physically unsafe. Adrian's job is to negotiate among people who want incompatible good outcomes, then live with the settlement.

The market is not an endless dungeon crawl. It is a growing social institution that creates professions, laws, scams, rituals, rivalries, and civil war. The central mystery advances through objects and records, but every answer changes what the market is for.

## Genre boundaries

- No skill trees, experience bars, inventory screens, or automatic combat.
- The Tally gives incomplete readings and consequences; it does not solve a case for Adrian.
- No object is a universal solution. A useful lot has boundaries, a toll, and a failure mode.
- No person can be sold. Labor, memory, land, and future obligations can be negotiated, so those boundaries must be defended socially and legally.
- Destruction is not automatically the safe answer. Removing an anchor can erase a needed route, water supply, shelter, or medical rule.
- Adrian's rare ability must become less central as other people learn to value, witness, and steward.

## Reader promise by scale

- **Chapter:** one object, one immediate social problem, one changed situation.
- **Mini-arc:** an auction or charter is tested, a cost becomes personal, and a local promise is paid or deliberately refused.
- **Volume:** a regional crisis is resolved through a public market decision, while the meaning of ownership and responsibility changes.
- **Series:** the Tally's origin is understood, the founder's monopoly is confronted, and temporary use rights become a durable civic commons.
