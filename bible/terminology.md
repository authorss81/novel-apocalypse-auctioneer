# Terminology

Use these terms consistently. The definitions are author-facing canon; the fiction may introduce local variants, but a variant must not change the rule it names.

## Core terms

### Tally

The distributed accounting presence created from emergency records, witness marks, and the unfinished Common Measure. It records valid uses, tolls, terms, and consequences. It is not an all-knowing deity, a game interface, or a guarantee that a settlement is moral.

**Use in dialogue:** “The Tally says the route can open.”
**Avoid:** “the computer,” “the database,” “the algorithm,” or treating it as a person who gives quests.

### Fracture

The Year 0 collapse of linked civic service contracts and emergency rules. It caused the Long Fracture and created the conditions for Faults.

### Fault

A bounded area where one local rule has separated from ordinary cause and effect. A Fault contains an anchor lot and has a boundary, use, toll, standing, and term.

### Anchor lot

The primary dangerous object at the center of a Fault. It may be mundane. Its power comes from the rule it can sustain, not from rarity or innate intelligence.

**Avoid:** “magic item” as a general explanation. Use the specific lot name and its actual limit.

### Lot

Any object or claim being read, offered, or settled. A lot is not automatically an anchor lot; a mundane object can be part of a public sale, while an anchor lot can be offered without being understood.

### Reading

The process of identifying a lot's use, toll, and standing. Adrian's rare ability is a fast, accurate reading, not perfect knowledge.

### Charter

A temporary, bounded right to activate a defined use of a lot. A charter is not ownership of the object, the rule, or another person.

**Required fields:** lot, use, bearer, beneficiaries, toll, term, witness, and remedy.

### Closing

The moment when the required witnesses accept a charter and the Tally records the terms. A closing can be disputed later if a material term was withheld, but the Tally does not automatically reverse a valid record.

### Witness

A person who understands the material terms and accepts the stated role. A witness is not a decorative signature. An affected person can refuse, and a coerced witness creates a practical vulnerability even if the record appears valid.

### Toll

The price paid to keep a use active. A toll may be physical, temporal, memory-based, labor-based, relational, or a change in a local rule.

### Standing

The condition that lets a bearer operate a lot safely. Standing can be a skill, a relationship, a place, a record, or a physical capability.

### Backwash

The spill of an unpaid or failed toll into nearby active lots. It may be physical, cognitive, social, or rule-based. Backwash cannot be erased once it has crossed a charter.

### Toll echo

The physical cost Adrian experiences when he reads a lot. It is a fraction of the immediate toll, not a generic mana cost.

### Security

A real object, skill, labor term, access right, or named obligation that Adrian posts to support a charter. Security is lost if the charter breaches. A promise alone is not automatically valid security.

### Overdraft

The state caused by sponsoring more active claims than Adrian's recognition can support. His name, signature, and standing become unreliable. It is resolved by settling or withdrawing claims, not by waiting.

### Unlisted

A person or community absent from the official Tally record by choice, error, punishment, or backwash. The Unlisted are not inherently illegal; the category describes a dangerous relationship with public infrastructure.

### Open lot

A public auction with disclosed tolls, more than one eligible bearer or steward, and a visible remedy process. It is not free from politics, but no single person owns its terms.

### Common Measure

The unfinished pre-Fracture effort to place the basin's essential emergency rights into one open, public settlement. It is the historical project at the center of the mystery.

### Founder's mark

The right of emergency override held by the surviving architect of the Common Measure. It can force one closure, but it cannot create valid consent and each use produces visible strain. Iven Tallow holds the mark at the start of the final conflict.

### First House

The administrative remnant of the unfinished Common Measure. Its archivists and emergency officials claim custody of the Tally's unresolved obligations. It is not a separate magical realm; it is a fortified civic institution built around the oldest records.

### Custodian

The person or office temporarily holding an obligation that the Tally cannot safely assign to a willing community. Iven Tallow is the Custodian during the final conflict. The title is an emergency responsibility, not a title of moral authority.

### Civic Tally

The post-ending public form of the Tally. It is a network of local lot books, rotating stewards, public tolls, appeals, and term limits. The Civic Tally has no permanent founder or private inheritable charter.

### Great Closing

The planned or forced conversion of many temporary charters into one permanent settlement. The term can be used by characters before its full meaning is known; in canon, it is the final political and physical threat.

### Blank lot

An unsettled place in the Tally where a new charter can be written. Iven gave Adrian a blank lot during his apprenticeship. It is a seat-like responsibility, not a magical empty inventory slot.

## Object names

- **The Last Fare:** brass transit token; opens a Fault route for twelve minutes and takes the memory of each traveler's purpose.
- **The Hearth Bell:** cracked bell; calls a district to shelter and records every person who hears it for one night.
- **The Salt Loom:** wooden shuttle; repairs irrigation and stores a season, taking a year of memory from its worker.
- **The Cinder Compass:** points toward the nearest person in immediate danger and compels its bearer to help.
- **The Quiet Shoe:** lets one traveler cross a guarded boundary but removes the traveler's recognition of home for a night.

## Language rules

- Use “lot” for the item and “charter” for the temporary right to use it.
- Use “price” for the negotiated consideration, “toll” for the cost of activation, and “value” for the larger relationship among use, toll, standing, and consent. Do not use them as interchangeable words.
- Use “Tally” as the proper name of the System presence.
- Use “Fault” for the zone and “backwash” for the consequence of a failed toll.
- Use “closing” for a completed settlement and “auction” for the social process leading to it. An auction can fail; a closing is the record that results from acceptance.
- Do not use game jargon such as NPC, player, skill tree, quest, stat, mana, cooldown, or respawn. If a survivor uses a game-like word, it should be character-specific and treated as a metaphor, not a world rule.
- Do not describe the Tally as good or evil without a character's specific reason. It follows recorded conditions and can enforce an immoral bargain.

## Tally display style

A display may use short lines and plain nouns:

```text
THE SALT LOOM
USE: REPAIR THE EAST CHANNEL.
TOLL: ONE YEAR OF MEMORY. ONE WORKER.
TERM: UNTIL THE CHANNEL HOLDS.
REFUSAL RECEIVED.
```

Displays should be rare and connected to a decision. A panel never solves the scene by itself. The most important information is often what the Tally cannot say.
