## 4. The lock, and the two errors, and the two men who were right

**A lock is a frame of cast iron about four feet high with a screw through the middle of it, a handle about a foot and a half long, a pin through the screw where the handle is pinned on, and a stone sill with one notch cut in it. It is bolted to two pieces of oak let into the bank. It weighs about a hundred and ninety pounds. It stands at the sluice end of the loams and it was carried out of its channel onto a barrow on the fourth of the fifth month and put back on the same afternoon.** The barrow is not an instrument this district built and not named and did not become a seventh.

**The reading of it is wrong twice, in front of witnesses, by the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, and he says so out loud both times in his own words, and a clerk entered each time that neither saying is a figure and neither is a column.**

1. **Chapter 232, in a yard, in front of about nineteen people, in ninety-six words.** He said that a lock holds a road shut and **what it holds with is the pin, and the pin is the piece that takes the load, and a lock is a safe thing exactly as long as a man is near it.** **He is wrong, and the man of fifty-six is right, in a hundred and twelve words, with his own hand on the frame next to the other man's hand: the pin takes nothing, he has been putting a stick through it to turn the screw with both hands in the water for nine years, about four thousand times, and if the pin took the load his hands would have gone under the water with the screw, and what holds the channel is the frame and the weight of the water sitting on the frame, and the screw is a lever and not a wall.** **The man the figure of twenty-one years is against then said the whole of what he was wrong about out loud, in a hundred and forty-seven words, and said that a pin is not a wall, a screw is not a wall, a frame is not a wall, and a lock does not need a man to hold it and never did.**
2. **Chapter 236, on a bank four miles out past the loams, in front of about nine people, in ninety-one words.** He said that the notch in the sill is the record, that it is the height the water is held to, and that a mark like that is a gauge and a gauge wants somebody to read it. **He is wrong, and the man of about forty-one who keeps a road house at the top of the road is right, in eighty words, with his thumb in the notch: that is where the board goes, there is one notch in that sill and there has been one since before his father walked the road, it is a cut for the edge of a plank, and the water is held above the plank about a foot deep and no more, and the board is not there because it is May.** **The man the figure of twenty-one years is against then said the whole of what he was wrong about out loud, in a hundred and sixteen words: I read a mark as a job, and a mark may not carry a job.**

**A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the rest of the first one out loud in ninety-seven words and it is the block's method stated: a man who says out loud in front of other people that the thing he knows best in the world is not what he said it was is a seventh instrument, and it was refused in about four seconds.** The clerk refused the seventh and the six did not move. **On the eighth the clerk entered that the seventh has not been proposed a second time, and that a rule refused once and never again after that is a rule that has been learned and not a rule that has been dropped.** In Chapter 240 the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said out loud that he was the one who put the seventh up and that a rule about being wrong is the same shape as the rule about being thanked that one of the nine wrote four hundred years ago, and the clerk entered that saying so is not withdrawing it and that the six are still six.

**A lock holds a road shut, and what holds a lock is the frame and the weight of the water, and nobody in this district has ever been to look at one of the nine, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that out loud in Chapter 238 in eighty-nine words and added that he is not going to use this yard's rule about people on a piece of iron because the rule is about people and iron is not people.** That sentence is the block's finding and it is the reason the volume's next block has a subject.
