## 3. The three lists of nine, which are the volume's engine and must reproduce exactly

**These are the figures a Batch 0003 writer must not get wrong. All three were read out in a yard and two of them were written on one sheet in two columns and read back.**

**A. The stranger's schedule, the Registry's nine-row sheet, entered in this district's book on the tenth of April and fixed by Chapter 209:**

> **THE FIRST OF THE FOURTH MONTH. THE SECOND. THE THIRD. THE FOURTH. THE SIXTH. THE EIGHTH. THE TENTH. THE ELEVENTH.**

**Its ninth row carries a word and not a day. The word is IT IS NOT A ROAD.** A column on the left with a number in each row, a column on the right headed OPEN, **and no column between them for a keeper at all.**

**B. The standing orders of the nine locks, a single printed sheet that came down on the dray on the fourteenth of April and was read out in a yard the same afternoon:**

> **THE NINE NIGHTS ARE THE FIRST OF THIS MONTH. THE SECOND. THE THIRD. THE FOURTH. THE SIXTH. THE EIGHTH. THE TENTH. THE ELEVENTH. AND THE TWENTIETH.**
>
> **THE COLUMN HEADED KEEPER CARRIES A HOUSE ON EVERY ONE OF THE NINE ROWS AND A PERSON ON NONE OF THEM.**

**A number in a column on the left and a night in a column on the right and a column headed KEEPER between them.** The first eight nights are the stranger's eight days in the same order. **The ninth is the twentieth of April and the stranger's ninth is a word, and that is the block's finding: two lists of nine that agree on eight are not one list, and the one they disagree about is the ninth, and the ninth is six days off at the end of Chapter 213 and has been and gone by the end of Chapter 220.**

**C. The road house's book, at a road house at the second place, written in more than one hand, read out on the thirteenth of April:**

> **THE FIRST OF THIS MONTH. THE SECOND. THE THIRD. THE FIFTH. THE SEVENTH. THE NINTH. THE TWELFTH. THE THIRTEENTH.**

**These are the nights the man who keeps the road has walked the road in this run. He refused the ninth in forty-two words and gave a reason, so it is not one of the refusals with no reason.**

**The two-column sheet read out in Chapter 214, which is the checkable arithmetic of the block and must reproduce exactly:**

> **IN BOTH. THE FIRST. THE SECOND. THE THIRD.**
>
> **IN THE ROAD HOUSE BOOK AND NOT ON THE PRINTED SHEET. THE FIFTH. THE SEVENTH. THE NINTH. THE TWELFTH. THE THIRTEENTH.**
>
> **ON THE PRINTED SHEET AND NOT IN THE ROAD HOUSE BOOK. THE FOURTH. THE SIXTH. THE EIGHTH. THE TENTH. THE ELEVENTH. THE TWENTIETH.**

**Three in both, five in the book and not on the sheet, six on the sheet and not in the book. Of the five, three are the fifth, the seventh and the ninth, which are the three days of eleven that a stranger's column headed OPEN leaves empty, and two are the twelfth and the thirteenth, which are after the last day any printed column in this district carries.**

## 8. Guardrails a Batch 0003 writer inherits

**Do not name a place on any document or in any narration.** The ninth place is what the documents say and the Registry's place is named on the lease and nowhere else. **Do not name the Lock Council as a place; it is a body a letter comes from.** **Do not find or explain the office. Do not resolve the romance, and the volume must not end on a settlement. Do not join the ninth place to the ninth holding, the fourth holding to the road, the dray to the nights, the tank to the ninth, the twenty-first of April to the lease, the Council's letter to the lease, or the two lists to each other as if they were about the same thing. **A mark may not carry a job. A yoke-load is a job and is not a figure. Binding cannot create consent and a certificate is not consent. A person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way and the record says not asked. An address is not a bearer. A name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on. A year is not an asset and there is no rate in this basin that turns one year into coppers. A toll is a thing a named person pays under a term. A document this district has and did not write is a document this district cannot produce, and that is not the same sentence as a document nobody owns.** Feet, inches, yards, miles, quarters, pounds, ounces, coppers, pints, degrees and the twelve-hour clock only. **No public market is built out of one reader.**

**The counting motif is load-bearing and every figure in it must reproduce. Count the printed sentence, do not estimate it. Words separated by spaces; a hyphenated numeral is one word. A claim is one number word attached to one printed sentence. An all-caps restatement at the foot of a chapter that repeats a count must repeat the body's figure, and a quotation of an earlier chapter's figure must repeat that chapter's figure. This block states forty-five word-count claims. Forty-four of them were written as estimates, forty-three of the forty-four were low and one was high, and all forty-four were corrected by recounting and not by shortening the prose — see the block record at section 9.1.**

**An interval is a figure and you must count it, not remember it. February in this year had twenty-eight days. Count from the named day and say which day you counted from. Four standing anchors for the next block: the second of January, which is a hundred and nine days to the twenty-first of April and a hundred and sixteen to the last day of the month; the fifteenth of the second month, which is sixty days to the sixteenth of April; the first of the second month, which is seventy-six days to the eighteenth; and the nineteenth of October, from which the twelfth of April is twenty-five weeks.**

**A stay is counted without the day of arrival, and where the first day is counted in, the prose says so by naming that day.** **A count has to be walked across the chapters and backwards out of the block as well as forwards through it.** **One System panel at most in a chapter and at most two in the block, and one may be disagreed with out loud in a yard.** This block carried **two panels, at Chapters 216 and 218, and six runs of `> **` in five chapters, of which four are printed documents read out by a person** — a road house's book in Chapter 212, the standing orders in Chapter 213, a clerk's two-column sheet in Chapter 214, and the council's letter in Chapter 215. **The tests for telling them apart: a panel is a rule and a printed document is a record. A run introduced as a sheet somebody reads out, and read back afterwards because it is the rule of the counter, is a document. A run that appears with nobody having asked for it, and that a person then argues with, is a panel.**
