## 4. What the block is about

**THE BLOCK IS ABOUT ONE NUMBER.** A sheet at a gate says there are four hundred and eleven people in the ground in the shadow of the Hall whose names have never been entered, and it says the number was arrived at by counting the people who answered a door. **On the last day of the block a second book in this district's own records office has two open pages in it with forty-one lines on them, counted with a man's own finger at four in the morning on the seventh by a man who was not asked and did not ask, and a man of about thirty-four says out loud in a yard that three hundred and seventy is the difference between a number made by knocking and a number made by walking, that a subtraction of a printed number and the open pages of one book is arithmetic and is not a finding, and that the thing the difference is made of is three hundred and seventy people who have not been asked anything by anybody, and that this district has no way to price that and never has had one.**

**THE SECOND THING THE BLOCK IS ABOUT IS THAT A DOOR IS NOT A REPRESENTATION.** Nine charters in nine rooms, a covenant with a hole in it, a book a stranger may walk up to, a clerk of nineteen years — **and every one of those instruments is a door, and the people in the ground in the shadow of the Hall have spent nine years making sure that no door in this basin has anything to do with them.** The man the figure of twenty-one years is against has been in nine doorways in nine days and says the sentence himself in a lane. **A man of fifty-six gives him the volume's sentence and it is said once and not acted on in this block: you have been standing next to the thing and calling it representing it, and standing next to a thing is not standing in its place.**

**THE THIRD THING THE BLOCK IS ABOUT IS A SECOND BOOK.** The registrar of this district's records office wrote a rule on the twenty-first of the fourth month that says a book is kept in one room by one person and that person is the only one who may look in it. That rule is the only reason a notice four hundred miles away could be written, and it is the only reason a covenant can be in a book a stranger may walk up to. **In Chapter 256 she opens a second book in that room, and the second book is the only instrument in this volume that protects anybody, and it is also a violation of the rule that made the first instrument safe, and she says so out loud in a room with a shut door in it and nobody in this district tells her to stop.** The block's finding is not that she was right or wrong. It is that **a person who keeps a book that protects people and a person who keeps a book that can be read by a stranger are the same trade, and the trade cannot be done twice in one room by one person without one of the two books changing what it is.**

## 8. The counting method, the interval rules, the panel test, the guardrails, and the standing craft brief

**THE COUNTING MOTIF IS LOAD-BEARING AND EVERY FIGURE IN IT MUST REPRODUCE. COUNT THE PRINTED SENTENCE. DO NOT ESTIMATE IT.** Words separated by spaces; a hyphenated numeral is one word; the trailing full stop is not counted. **A class-one claim is a number word attached by a claim phrase — *counted it and got, was counted and got, counted what he said and got, counted what she said and got, the count came to, it came to, came to* — to a printed sentence standing in a paragraph that begins with a quotation mark, and it is resolved forward to that paragraph.
