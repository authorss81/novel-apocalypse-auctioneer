## 4. What the block is about

**THE BLOCK IS ABOUT A FIGURE FOUND BY WALKING OUT FROM A BELL, AND ABOUT WHAT A DISTRICT DOES WITH TWO FIGURES OF THE SAME LENGTH.**

**THE FIRST HALF IS THE ARRIVAL.** A man of about sixty-four comes through a gate that takes two goes at about a quarter past ten on the twenty-first of the seventh month with nothing in his hands, gives no name, is asked for none, and says he heard a bell. **He does not say what kind of bell or how far off it was, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard asked him whether he had heard anything and that nobody was going to.** A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing declines to draw anything out of the two men who have come through that gate in a month, one with a case and one without. ## 7. The frames and the nouns this block is about, and what is NOT in it

**THE BELL IS THE SUBJECT AND THE INSTRUMENT IS A DISTANCE.** *a bell* and *the bell* are **nineteen** and **six** across the block on the repaired text, whole-word and case-insensitive, and stand highest in Chapter 304, which is the chapter the earliest day belongs to. **THE FIRST TEXT OF THIS SENTENCE SAID EIGHTEEN AND SIX; A MEASUREMENT AFTER THE REVIEW REPAIRS RETURNS NINETEEN AND SIX, THE MISSING ONE BEING THE *A BELL* IN CHAPTER 301'S OPENING LINE, AND THE FIGURE IN THE TABLE IS THE MEASURED ONE.** *a ditch* is **nine**, and they are in Chapters 304, 305 and 308 — two, five and two — **and the first text of this sentence said they were all in Chapters 304 and 305 and was wrong about Chapter 308, which has two of them.** *a road* is **thirty-one**. *a name* is thirteen and ten of them are in Chapter 309, which is where a name is on the page and is not said. *courier* is six, in Chapters 302 and 309. *four hundred and eleven* is twenty. *a keeper* and the bare word *keeper* are twenty-three and **every one of the twenty-three is a denial, a printed rule about a job, a column that is empty, or a noun somebody has been told they are not.
