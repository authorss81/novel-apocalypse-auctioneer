## The numbers, fixed

- **The train on the siding** arrived on the **eleventh of February**. It has stood **thirty-nine days on the 22nd of March, forty-six on the 29th, forty-seven on the 30th, fifty on the 2nd of April, and fifty-one when the market's timetable was set on the 3rd.** Recompute from the eleventh of last month; do not carry a figure forward.
- **The stalls.** The line book has **eleven entries, two of which are one stall entered twice, so ten distinct stalls on the line**, all of them with a signal in them. The floor in the hut at the ninth place is the eleventh. **The twelfth is about ninety yards of embankment between the section post and the ninth place.** The keeper's twelve and the tenth hand's eleven both stand and **neither is struck**, and the finding is that **the line book is a book of signals and a stall with no signal in it cannot be entered**, so the count has been wrong for eight years in one direction only. The **fourth-place stall: 344 yards on the 9th of March, 361 on the 18th, 361 again on the 22nd, 361 on the 29th going and coming.** The **hut floor: 11 inches out of level at the left-hand end of the bench**, with a crack in the lime putty about 4 feet long and a folded blanket under the low leg.
- **The line book** is indexed to **906** movements since the flood year, of which **141** name the ninth place as the origin held; the tenth hand has **211** of the 906. Sef Anyard's hut book has **61**, four of them in a second ink. **Nineteen** reversals since the water came, **eleven** of which left a stall. Sef has held the hut **eleven years** and the Tongue **five**. **Twenty-nine** people worked the line in the first year, not thirty-one.
- **The section post** between the eighth and the ninth place: **one mile and three quarters**, which is the line book's own figure for the ninth place. **It is a mark, and a mark may not carry a job.**
- **The drain at the sixth place**: **4 inches** on the ballast, out of a drain off the top of the bank, going since before the Long Fracture. Its length is not known and never will be, because measuring it is what the refusal cost.
- **The walk**: the first version said *on the ballast* and *nobody inside the four-foot on a curve*, and both were true at once, **because the fourth place is a curve, and the ballast between the rails is the four-foot.** The second version says six-foot, shoulder, or cess. **Four changeovers**, the worst being a step down of **two feet six inches** into about a foot of standing water at the eighth, with a **shoulder 4 feet wide and a drop of 9 feet** on the river side. **Three hours on an ordinary morning; four and a half on the twenty-ninth.** **The first version is not struck.**
- **The refusal at the sixth place: one hour and eleven minutes**, and the column it is entered in is **what the thing goes on to next**, which is Bett Ruhl's name for it. **The figures are the cost of the room and not of the thing.**
- **The money.** The rate is **fifty coppers a quarter over four thousand and one households = two hundred thousand and fifty coppers a quarter.** The rate sheet of the 28th: **the ninth place 200, the two blocks west of the depot 300, the whole of the ground nobody has claimed 500, entered and not collected.** The two blocks: **22 people, 11 rooms, 6 occupied.** The **meter book: 4 coppers a quarter, 36 readings, 36 coppers in nine years**, now entered as a column and not a meter, a meter that is nobody's, and thirty-six coppers owed to nobody. The **standing order of the ninth costed: 8 posts at 11 = 88 coppers a quarter**, against a take of 200,050. **Printed matter: 6 coppers a quarter**, three out of the boards and three out of the oil. **Printing a run of five hundred: 906 coppers** (4 stock, 2 setting, 900 quoted on the 14th), the gap of **894** carried on a trade twice by the chandlers, **unpaid and not as nil**. **The rate on the line is 14 coppers a quarter and nobody proposed it** — lamp, bushel of chalk, 14 pints of white paint, oil, and a sheet; and **a sheet of 500 is 444 coppers, which is 74 a quarter spread over a year, and the sheet is the part of the fourteen that does not fit in it.** The standing order was answered by the ward market **seven to three with one abstention**; the line rate was **seven to three with one abstention** and **the same trade abstained with the same reason, entered for the second time: we are not a trade for the people the road is for.** The payment on the unstated claim is unchanged at **1,190 / NO SHEET / 1,430** and **nobody has been paid anything** at the end of this batch, for the second time running, nineteen days after the first entry said so, in a different hand. **Halloway Reed: 206 places at 4 coppers in about 40 minutes = 824 coppers**, against a whole year's line budget of 56.
- **The lease is surrendered**, on the 30th, in about eleven minutes, on a sheet headed **surrender of a lease** and not assignment and not transfer, and **assigned to nobody**, and entered on the rate sheet as **held by nobody.** Tarin Kest's three figures, given unasked: **eleven vehicles a week stabled there, four spaces in the Alder Reach yards, seven a week with nowhere to stand, 410 coppers of standing salvage worth that in Alder Reach and nothing at the ninth place.**
- **Orren Pike is paid 4 coppers a quarter** for the line book index, and it is a standing item and not a service. **He is seventeen and is not Adrian Vale's apprentice.**
- **The reading ledger is closed at one, for the second time in the same month.** One reading: the eleventh, about an hour, from a sheet, compared by two people. **On the eighteenth he did not read, on a day the limit permitted it, and on the twenty-fifth he did not read, on a day the limit permitted that too. Both non-events are entered with no cost and no time. The interval between the eleventh and the eighteenth is six days. There is no interval between the two refusals, because a refusal is not a reading. His next permitted hour is not before the fifth of April.** A refusal to read is a reading too and is entered; it is neither cowardice nor discipline and no batch may write it as either.

## The key rotation, fixed and continuous

- Hester Vane from the 26th of February for **six days**. Wren Ostler from the 2nd, and again from the 12th, four days each.
- **THE FIRST LAPSE: the 7th to the 11th.** The board named nobody; the case stood shut in a locked office on a term that had run out; entered **as a lapse and not as a theft**.
- Mrs Halder from the **17th** for four days, expiring midnight on the **21st**, and will not open the case alone and cannot get a second person.
- **THE SECOND LAPSE: the 21st and the 22nd.** The board had still named nobody. **Two lapses in one month, four days each, eight days in which a case stood shut on a term that had run out.** The finding on the notice board: **a rotation is only real when it turns; a board that asks at the end of a term has not asked in time; a board that cannot ask at all and leaves it to a crowd is a board whose rotation is a weather.**
- **Oswin Tace**, forty-one, a lamp-man who has never counted anything but lamps and has asked twice that it be said, named on the 27th, held from the **28th for four days, expiring at midnight on the 1st of April**, renewal on the 30th. His condition: **if nobody comes he will write down that nobody came, with the date on it, and will not say the case was open or that anybody was careless.** His reason: **his mother was on that stair when a bridge went and about nine people stood on a ramp for a night because nobody came, and it is not a lamp.**
- **Adrian Vale** named on the 29th, held from the **30th for four days, expiring at midnight on the 3rd of April**, renewal on the 2nd, **on the one-term condition he published in advance.** He said yes before the asking was finished and was made to state the difference between this and his refusal of the 26th of February out loud. **On the 26th of February he had put 1,536 coppers of his own inventory into a steward's hands, and if he had taken the key that afternoon then he had taken it back, and there is no version of taking it back that is not that.**
- **A man of about fifty-six off the lower terrace**, thirty-one years keeping a chandler's second counter, took it on the **2nd of April**, asked in writing that he does not read figures and that a reader at a counter is the only person who may read anything to him, and asked for four days, and got all three.
- **A term of four days is the shortest this board has ever set and has now been set four times.** The board **still cannot ask a person whether they will do a job**, and the yard has been doing it for eleven days.

## The two instruments this batch built

1. **A LINE ON THE GROUND AT THE WEST END OF THE SIDING AT THE NINTH PLACE, sixty yards west of the column, on the shoulder, where the ground stops being good ground.** Two named holders: **the keeper of the hut, and the woman at the column, who is also the water.** One movement: the person who says the word, the word is the word, and everything on the line stops and waits. **A term: from the 29th of March to the 29th of April, renewable by nobody except by asking both of them again, in a room, with the asking and the hour and the answer entered, whatever the answer is.** Deliberately worse than a case: no case, no counter, no key, no holder that can be inspected without walking nine miles, and **the nine miles is the point.** **Its standing failure is entered: it cannot be seen from the end it is for, and the fixing of it is one bushel of white paint and about a hundred and forty yards of it.** It is **not the fifth thing** and the board says so in the published entry.
2. **A DRAFT THAT CANNOT BE CLOSED, published whole.** Four bearers at the fourth, sixth, eighth and ninth places; **three named bearers and a slot**; standing is per bearer; **the sixth place is a drain and the eighth place has nowhere to stand**; security none offered and none posted. **A Stage 1 reader may draft a split and may not close one, may not transfer a use, may not renew one, and may not post a security.** A transferable slot is not a pair. **A board cannot name a person and a board asked by a crowd is not a board.**
