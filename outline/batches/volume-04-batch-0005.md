> **This card was written after the block and not before it. It is the last Volume 04 card and it is short on purpose: a Volume 05 writer needs the position, the calendar, the numbers, the counts that did not move, the guardrails and the threads, and does not need a section-by-section account of a block that is over.** **The volume roll is `state/volume-04-roll-summary.md` and it is the first document a Volume 05 writer should read.** Everything here is taken from the files. **There is no `outline/volume-04.md` and none was created; the repository's pattern for a closed volume is a roll in `state/` and the Volume 03 precedent has no `outline/volume-03-roll.md` and no roll section inside `outline/volume-03.md`, so no new outline file shape was invented here.** The five divisions in `outline/series.md` and the closed canon of Volume 03 disagree in four places and all four were carried forward rather than repaired; they are named in the Plan deviations section of `state/volume-04-batch-0005-summary.md`.

## 1. The position the volume ends in

**Volume 04 is closed at Chapter 200, on the thirty-first of the third month of the eighteenth year after the Long Fracture, and the first of April is one day past its end. The month turned three times in the volume, on the first of January in Chapter 155, the first of February in Chapter 167 and the first of the third month in Chapter 187, and there is no fourth turn inside Volume 04.**

**Spent:** the escalation in Chapter 198, the climax in Chapter 199, the concrete resolution in Chapter 200, the power change in Chapter 199. **Not spent:** the midpoint reversal. **A house's claim on one place in a flood shelter at the sluice passed on the twenty-fourth of the third month, on a day named on a sheet the family was not given, and the sheet did not name a building and did not name a family.** A sluice four miles out past the loams puts nine hours of water down a channel on a schedule a route keeper gave as two days and no figure, and it empties one woman's round of about ninety houses and fills a tank within a hand's breadth of the lip for the first time since the first of the second month, with the crack still in it and still weeping and no fifth mend. **A man stands at the back of a public book against the tolls of that water, a toll being one yoke-load on a day a route keeper names; a house's eighth sheet says the tolls are fastened to a person at the back of that book and names the person whose hand is on the page; and the registrar of this district's records office says in fifty words in front of about nine people that he knew it when he asked her for the book and did not say it, and he says in twenty-six words that he knew it, did not say it, and is not going to tell her that makes it a different thing.** **The first public court for debts in this district sat on the thirty-first of March and its fifth line says the terms of a house thirty-four miles east are valid until the people inside them can be heard, and the people inside them have not been heard. ## 2. The calendar, derived and never carried

- **The train arrived on the eleventh of February OF THE SEVENTEENTH YEAR, which is the year before the eighteenth year the volume closes in, and the eleven of February is a date in the volume's own calendar and not a day of the eighteenth year.** This is the one day-count in the volume with a year in it and the year is stated nowhere in the prose, so a writer who derives from *the eleventh of February* alone gets it wrong by a year. **The figure is a continuous tally from that date and it crosses the year boundary: seventeen days remain in February after the eleventh, March to August come to 184, so 201 stands at the last day of August; September to December add 122, so 323 stands at the last day of December and 324 at the first of January; January, February and March add 90, so 413 stands at the last day of March and 414 at the first of April.** It was read out loud in Chapter 200, in two runs of words with the sums under the figures, by the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray, and **no day-count in Volume 04 is carried forward.** **The eleventh of February is day zero of the tally, so the first of January is 324 because the eleventh of February to the thirty-first of December is 323 days. Taken as a plain interval inside a single year instead of a tally that crosses the boundary, the eleventh of February to the thirty-first of March is forty-eight, and forty-eight is the figure this volume's own method forbids, because a figure that does not reproduce is not a figure.** Two checks hold the year in place: the train stood 400 days on the eighteenth of the third month in Chapter 194, 393 on the eleventh in Chapter 191 and 408 on the twenty-sixth in Chapter 198, and the board has carried the twenty-fourth of December for 97 days at the last day of the volume, which is 413 less 316.

## 5. Guardrails a Volume 05 writer inherits

**Do not name Sarrow Water on a document, and do not name any city on any document.** The ninth place is what the documents say. **Do not find or explain the office.** **Do not resolve:** the romance (it is fractured, and a fracture is not a resolution), Iven, the blank lot's function, the origin of the Common Measure, the three unexplained things of Volume 01, the name of the city on the agent's ledger, the name of the agent in any register in the west bank, the fourth line of the offer. **Do not join** anything on the long list in `outline/batches/volume-04-batch-0004.md` section 7 or the one in `state/volume-04-batch-0005-summary.md`; **the twenty joins Batch 0004 added and the four Batch 0005 refused on the page stand, and a resemblance noticed again is refused the same way.** **A mark may not carry a job; a line of stones is not a mark and a line of charcoal drawn on a stone is not a mark either.** **Binding cannot create consent; a certificate is not consent; a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way and the record says not asked.** **An address is not a bearer and a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on.** **A year is not an asset, is not a security, and there is no rate in this basin that turns one year into coppers.** **The echo of an origin toll is not a generic cost and the iron does not hold places.** **Feet, inches, yards, miles, quarters, pounds, ounces, coppers, pints, degrees and the twelve-hour clock only; no metric, no twenty-four-hour clock, no weekday.** **No public market is built out of one reader.** **Selik is a wary ally and not a friend and was in none of Volume 04's fifty chapters.**

**The counting motif is load-bearing and a figure in it must reproduce.** This volume closes on a man of about nineteen who counts a speaker's words and gets a number, and **every figure in that motif is checkable by a reader, so every one of them is right. Volume 04 states three hundred and five of them and every one reproduces.** A Volume 05 writer who uses it must count the printed sentence and not estimate it, and must count the same way: words are separated by spaces and a hyphenated numeral is one word. **The claim is stated in three forms and all three are in use — *counted it and got N* above the sentence, *in N words* in the line that introduces it, and *it is N words* where nobody counted twice — and an all-caps restatement at the foot of a section that repeats a count must repeat the body's figure and not a remembered one.** Four restatements in Chapters 192 to 197 were stale on the last pass and were caught only by reading them against the sentence they summarise; they are itemised in `state/volume-04-batch-0005-summary.md`. **Count claims and not pattern matches when auditing this: a claim is one number word attached to one printed sentence, and three different detectors were run over this block during the last pass and returned sixty-two, forty-four and sixty-six.** **A number that does not reproduce is not a figure, which is the sentence this whole volume is about, and a volume that states that cannot be wrong about it in its own text.**
