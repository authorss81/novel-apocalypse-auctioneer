## The numbers, fixed

- **The train on the siding arrived on the eleventh of February.** It has stood **fifty-five days on the seventh of April and eighty-one days on the third of May.** Recompute from the eleventh of last month; do not carry a figure forward.
- **The Harrow Tongue.** About the length of a forearm and **four pounds and one ounce.** Wedge end cut to a wedge and worn to a shine about two inches along the top; a hole at the other end with a loop of red signal wire through it, **cut loose in the fourth year and not used as a loop since**, and the bed of the hole bright where nine years of cast iron has been polished by one wire in one direction. **It was in the frame at the ninth place until the sixth of April and is on a bench at the fourth place from the sixth**, carried eleven yards by a boy of nineteen who thought it was a bar. **Sef Anyard last had it in her hand on the eighteenth of March, when she took it out because the wedge end was catching, said *that'll do till the end of the month*, and put it back; that is the entry and it is her own account of her own hand and not a fact about a hut, and it is the evening she is sorry about.** The ninth place is the only frame on the line with a bracket the iron fits, the bracket is empty, and the fourth place has no frame and never had one. The train cannot go east and cannot go west.
- **The stalls.** Unchanged from Batch 0002. **The index now has twelve and the line book has eleven**, and the difference is **ninety-four yards** of embankment between the section post and the ninth place with no signal in it, measured with a tape by a bridge-office signalman on his second morning because he was told to walk the section twice a day. **The keeper's figure is about ninety, the tenth hand's is about ninety, and the ninety-four is the fourth figure, and none of the three is struck.** The line book is a book of signals and a stall with no signal in it cannot be entered.
- **The walk.** Alder Reach to the fourth place and Alder Reach to the ninth place are **five and a quarter hours in ordinary weather** with four changeovers in each; there and back is **nine hours**. The four changeovers are unchanged. A party on the seventh of April left at half past four in the morning, arrived about ten to ten, and stayed two nights.
- **The boundary's word is nine words long** and reads **whoever holds the thing that day has to stop and wait.** It was said **five times in the ten days from the twenty-ninth of March to the eighth of April** and every time it stopped a man with a barrow. **A word written about a train is now a word about people, and a word does not need its authors to agree with it.** **It was said five more times in a hut on the third of May before it was said like a person.**
- **The boundary.** Sixty yards west of the water column, on the shoulder, invisible from the west end of the siding. Two named holders: **the keeper of the hut at the ninth place, and the woman at the column, who is also the water.** **Renewed on the twenty-ninth of April for one month, from the twenty-ninth of April to the twenty-ninth of May, by the tenth hand of the signal crew, in a room, with the asking, the hour, both answers and the reading-back entered, whatever the answer is.** The term is **not** renewed on the thirtieth and is **not** renewed by a board. The standing failure is entered and the fixing is **one bushel of white paint and about a hundred and forty yards of it, and the rate voted on the second of April bought fourteen pints, and fourteen pints is not a bushel.**
- **The hut at the ninth place.** One man, **Corvin Sallow, thirty-nine**, a signalman of the bridge administration, **from the twenty-fifth of April for one month, to the twenty-fifth of May, renewed by the office, nobody asked.** The bench, the lamp, the chalk and the oil are **eleven coppers a quarter out of the Harrow works rate. The ninth can in the fortnight went up on the first of May and **a trader has four weeks of oil and nobody has asked her about the fifth.**
- **The Harrow works rate: one hundred and eighty coppers a quarter, nine years, unchanged. Six thousand four hundred and eighty coppers, paid, not arrears, not deferred,** out of a water office's quarterly return **the ward market has read every quarter for nine years.** **The whole cost of the west end of that railway for nine years is twenty-one thousand two hundred and twenty-two coppers**, in two books in two rooms in two hands. **The water is not in that figure and no instrument in this district can put it there.**

## The four instruments this batch built or changed

1. **THE FIVE WAYS OF ASKING A PERSON WHETHER THEY WILL DO A JOB, and a sixth thing with no name, and the reading-back.** On a wall in the counting room of the ward market. What it is in sentences a person who has never done it can understand; how long with an hour on it; how you say no and that no reason is owed and that nobody will ask afterwards; who answers for you while you are saying it; where you stand when you answer. **The sixth thing is a person in the room who is not the one being asked and is not the one asking, whose job is to say what the question was. It is not a power, it is not on the board, it belongs to Nessa Pell, and it was done in this batch by Rutt twice, unasked.** The reading-back is not on the wall, because you cannot describe to a person in advance what it is to hear your own no come back at you in a clerk's voice, and it is done anyway. **The method is not Adrian's and not the tenth hand's and not the board's, and the finding is that the board cannot ask a person whether they will do a job and the yard can, and the difference between those two things is the whole of what has gone wrong in this district since the eighth of March, and the tenth hand has said the other half of it fourteen times and nobody has heard it.**
2. **THE FIFTEENTH PART OF THE RESULTS, published on the first of May, in two halves.** The west end of the line, held by two people who can only be asked, and a hut held by an office that renews itself and asks nobody, both for one month. It confers no use, authorises nobody, seats nothing, **and no member of the board has been to either end of anything it has published.** With it, **the first page of standing failures gathered together, dated and not struck: one, the line of chalk cannot be seen from the end it is for; two, the word is a word about people and not about a train; three, the line does not know it has stopped working, because a line is not a person and there is nobody standing at the end of it to find out; four, the hut is held by an office that renews itself and asks nobody.** Under them, in the registrar's hand: **a failure that is entered is not fixed, it is known.**
3. **NOT ANSWERED, a heading.** A boy of fifteen's four questions — **who tells us, by whom, for how long, is anybody obliged to answer** — entered in the public lot book on the twenty-eighth of April with his name on the asking and not on a household, and **nobody is obliged to answer, and that is an answer.** The first line ever entered under that heading is **a woman's answer to a question nobody asked her: what happens on the twenty-ninth of May.** **A question is now a thing a book can hold, and the fourth instrument the district does not have is a column that is a place, and a licensed valuer of twelve years has said so in a document and did not know he was doing it.**
4. **A CONDITION WITH NO END ON IT, entered as the fourth kind of thing this district has built.** Not a charter, not the fifth thing, not a term, not a price, not a signature. **It cannot be ended by anybody including the person who made it, except by her saying so. Four of them are now standing: three said by women and one by a boy of nineteen, and not one of the four is in an office.** The woman of the column has four of her own.

## Guardrails restated for the next batch

- **The object does not hold places, will not name one, and repeats itself when asked for more. No reading may name a place.** It has been read twice, it gave the same word both times, and **it moved nothing.**
- **A toll on a place takes memory of departure, and a fraction of it, and the fraction is smaller the second time.** Do not use the echo as a generic cost and do not use it twice in the same shape.
- **A refusal to read is a reading too and is entered, and it is neither cowardice nor discipline.** His next permitted hour is not before the eleventh of next month.
- **A toll lands on a place, and a destination can be a victim too. The admission is spent; do not re-stage it.** It is referred to once, in Chapter 76, and not re-argued.
- **The stalls are the basin's own documented Fracture symptom. The twelfth was found by walking and the reason the count is wrong is that the line book is a book of signals.** Do not let a count of stalls become a mystery again, and **do not let the missing ground signal at the Harrow end become a mystery either: it is a fact about instruments.**
- **A mark may not carry a job, and the boundary was built around that rule and not in defiance of it.** Do not retire the rule. **A line of stones is not a mark, because a mark is cut and that is laid** — the batch recorded that hole and did not extend the rule to cover it.
- **The two lines holding the office's name are not joined, and the name is not spelled out on a page in Batch 0004, and the office is not found in this volume.**
- **Iven remains presumed dead from Adrian's point of view. Do not deepen the father-shaped influence past what a man would find in his own handwriting.** The blank lot is not put on the crate and not read.
- **The romance does not resolve and is not spoken about.** Adrian and Mara pass a slate in a kitchen and neither of them says a word, and a reader at a counter has written down that she is not going to ask.
- **Selik is not on the walk and does not stop things, and the twenty-ninth of May's asking is not his to make.** The renewal must be asked by somebody who is not Adrian and entered by somebody who is not Adrian, and it was done that way once, on the twenty-ninth of April, and the second asking must be done that way again or the rotation is a weather.
- **The woman of forty-five is not a name in the book and must not become one.** A chapel sexton of about sixty-eight has been given a calendar in Chapter 74 so that no other woman of forty-five can be mistaken for her.
- **Halloway is not to be humiliated, is not to be beaten by a printing press, and is right about four things.**
- **The protected category is not a bucket and the count is closed at five. Nothing in Batch 0003 touched it, and the four conditions with no end on it are a different class of thing and must never be added to that tally.**
- **The board has five powers and cannot ask a person anything, and the yard can, and that difference is now printed on a wall.**
- Feet, inches, yards, miles and quarters, pounds and ounces, degrees, coppers, pints, and the twelve-hour clock. No metric and no twenty-four-hour clock. **And no weekday attached to any date.**
- **Emphasis is a document register and it cannot cross a blank line.** Bold is the words of a page in a public book, a wall, a printed sheet, a finding as it is entered, or a sentence somebody has gone out of their way to say. **Narration keeps a plain lead-in and bolds the finding inside it**, and a speech that runs over paragraphs opens and closes in every chunk. **Every paragraph must hold an even number of `**`.** Thirty-four paragraphs in this volume broke that rule and the Batch 0003 review repaired the thirty-three in this batch; the seven still open in Chapters 51 to 69 are listed in the batch prompt and are canon.
- **No character quotes a relative date.** Three in Chapter 71 are left unresolved on purpose — the sixth of the month before last, twice, and the twenty-second of the month before last — and nothing in Volume 02 is anchored to them.
