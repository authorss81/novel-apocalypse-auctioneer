## The numbers, fixed

- **The train on the siding arrived on the eleventh of February.** It has stood **eighty-two days on the fourth of May, a hundred and three on the twenty-fifth, a hundred and seven on the twenty-ninth, and a hundred and twelve on the third of June.** Recompute from the eleventh of February; do not carry a figure forward. - **The three toll boards.** Nine feet by three feet on two posts, tarred at the foot. A heading across the top in letters three inches high. A column **four feet wide**, ruled in pencil at its two ends and **empty between them**, with the reason printed inside it at about the height of a man's eye. Along the bottom, three lines: a date, a keeper's name, and **one line anybody may write a single sentence on, which nobody will be asked to explain.** **Each board carries eleven lines and four of the eleven are about a place nobody can find.** They went up on the sixth, eighth and ninth of May at the east end, the ninth place and the fourth place.
- **The keepers, and none of them is paid out of a rate.** The east end: **Wren Ostler**, the ward market's second clerk, who keeps it in the hours the counter is shut and whose disclaimer is that she is the second clerk of this market and not the market. The fourth place: **Jessa Olwin**, about thirty-eight, a paperer who buys old paper off four houses once a month and keeps the board for the week she is on that bank and no other, and who is not a signal hand and does not know what a pair is and wants that on the board.
- **The name at the top, in full and not contracted, and not found in a register:** **THE STANDING OFFICE OF CROSS-DISTRICT PASSAGE, ESTABLISHED, AND NOT AT THIS PLACE.** It is at the top because **a name at the top is a heading and a name in the middle is an amount, and this district cannot print an amount.** It is on three boards and on a printed timetable, five hundred copies, printed on purpose. **Four people in four rooms spent about two hours failing to find it: a registrar through the municipal roll of public offices, a bridge clerk through his own office's standing instructions, a licensed valuer through the guild's register, and Abel Troth, a sign-writer of sixty-one, through thirty-one years of lettering jobs in which he found the phrase twice in his own hand on two boards he made in the sixth year for the water office, and has no office, no customer and nobody who gave him the words.** Nobody has found the office in any register this basin can reach.

  1. **A SECURITY IS A THING SOMEBODY ELSE CAN GO AND LOOK AT.**

## The four instruments this batch built or changed

1. **A TOLL BOARD**, defined above, and **the finding that produced it: a sheet can be taken cold by anybody in this basin and about four hundred people took one cold, and a board cannot be taken cold, because a board is in a place, and a place is the only thing in this district that has ever been able to make anybody stand still.** Three boards, three keepers, none paid. **The batch's answer to the four-day problem is that the fourth day is the day the keeper starts.**
2. **THE FOUR-BEARER CHARTER, REWRITTEN BY OTHER PEOPLE AND STILL UNCLOSABLE.** Three bearers hold a week at the ninth place, the fourth place and the east end, with named substitutes entered in advance; the first week is the keeper's because she is the only one of the three already there. **The fourth bearer is a person who ends the use, is not a slot, and is to be named by the three holders, and Mr Vale has no part in naming them.** The sixth and eighth places of the March draft were struck by the room, not by him. **The origin is named as the ninth place as a decision and not a reading, with the woman's answer in the order she gave it: I do not like it, and I say yes.** Her name is not on it; the place is. **The use is not granted by anything in this batch and nothing was closed.**
3. **THE SECOND IMPRESSION OF THE BROKEN CIRCLE, IN TWO LINES THAT ARE NOT JOINED.** A water office notice dated the second of the sixth year about the metered column at mile one and three quarters, folded once behind two ledgers on a dry board in the hut at the ninth place, **with an impression of a broken circle above the fold line where a name would go, and there is no name on it.** Found on the fourteenth of May by a boy of seventeen who was there to copy the line book. 4. **THE STANDING ORDER ANSWERED AGAIN, IN A MARKET, WITH THE COUNT READ OUT, AND THE DISTRICT VOTING AGAINST THE OFFICE AND FOR THE TIMETABLE.** Seven to three in March with no count read. **Five to five in May with the count read out in full, the office losing on the market's rule by trade, and the finding that the district could not agree about an office and agreed exactly and inversely about a sheet, and the difference between the two questions is that one of them can be ended and the other one cannot.**
