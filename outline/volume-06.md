## Central pressure

**A number and a day, on a sheet that names no place and no person, nailed to a gate nobody owns.**

A population is living in the ground in the shadow of the Hall, on the far side of a fault line, in the country above the water. Their names have never been entered anywhere that anybody in this district has ever been in. A sheet says how many of them there are and it says the day they are to be moved, and **it says the number was arrived at by counting the people who answered a door.**

Volume 06 is the volume in which this district's entire apparatus — nine charters in nine rooms, a covenant with a hole in its third column, a book a stranger may walk up to, a clerk of nineteen years who is nobody's clerk — is pointed at a question it was never built for. **Volume 05's method answers a question about people who can be asked in a doorway. This one is about people who have arranged not to answer a door.** Every instrument the district has is a door, and the population in the shadow of the Hall has spent generations making sure there is no door.

**And the bell.** A cracked bell in a timber frame can call a district into shelter in about nine minutes. It will also record the name of everybody who hears it, for one night, and a record of names in a book four hundred miles away is the exact thing the people in the shadow of the Hall have been avoiding for nine years. **A bell is the only instrument in this basin that solves a person's problem by taking their name, and the only one that cannot be argued with out loud.** It has been rung twice in nine years. It will be rung once in this volume and the sounding is the volume's reversal.
