## 2. Central pressure

**A number, a day, and a body four hundred miles off that prints and does not arrive.**

A sheet is nailed to the gatepost of a yard a stranger can walk into, at the height of a man's shoulder, and it has been there since the first of the sixth month. It names one building and no place and no person. It says the people living in the ground in the shadow of that building, whose names have never been entered, are to be moved; that there are four hundred and eleven of them; that **that number was arrived at by counting the people who answered a door**; and that they are to be moved **on or before the first day of the eighth month**, and that the number is not to be entered again and is not to be asked for a second time.

**The number did not move on any of Volume 06's fifty days. It is still four hundred and eleven, and it is twelve days off at the moment Volume 06 stops and eleven days off at the moment this volume opens.**

**THE PRESSURE OF VOLUME 07 IS THE PRESSURE OF VOLUME 06 SEEN FROM THE OTHER SIDE, AND THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE TWO IS THE WHOLE OF THIS VOLUME.** Volume 06's antagonist is a method: **a record that makes a person accountable by a count, and the count is a count of the people who answered a door.** Volume 07's antagonist is the same method with the other half of the method in it: **a record of who was there.** The sheet at the gatepost is made by knocking. The bell four miles out past the loams is made by standing still. **A knock and a standing still reach the same ground, the two records of that ground do not have to agree, and a body four hundred miles off is holding one of them.**

## 17. The counting method, the interval rules, the panel test, and the standing craft brief

### 17.1 THE COUNTING MOTIF IS LOAD-BEARING AND EVERY FIGURE IN IT MUST REPRODUCE

**A claim is one number word attached by one of the seven canon phrases — *counted it and got, was counted and got, counted what he said and got, counted what she said and got, the count came to, it came to, came to* — to a printed sentence standing in a quoted paragraph, and it resolves forward to that paragraph. **COUNT THE PRINTED SENTENCE. NEVER ESTIMATE IT. SET EVERY FIGURE BY MEASURING THE PRINTED SENTENCE AND WRITING THE CLAIM AFTERWARDS, AND IF A SENTENCE MOVES AFTERWARDS, RE-MEASURE EVERY CLAIM THAT SITS ON OR NEAR IT RATHER THAN RE-AIMING IT.**
