## 1. The range, and the arithmetic that decided it

**Volume 23 is Chapters 1051 to 1100. Fifty chapters. Fifty mornings. Mornings 335 to 384. `1051 + 49 = 1100`. `c = 0` is Chapter 1050 and the morning number is `334 + c`.**

**Five blocks of ten. `5 x 10 = 50`.** Volume 22 was forty mornings in four blocks and **paid its one promise on its own last morning**, which is a shape worth keeping and a shape worth not repeating. This volume pays its promise at `c = 44`, Chapter 1094, and **runs six mornings after the payment**, and the last morning of the volume is not the morning anything is paid on. That is the whole craft argument for fifty over forty, and it is the only one.

**The morning number does not land on an exact hundred anywhere in this volume.** `334 + c` reaches four hundred at `c = 66`, which is sixteen mornings past the end of this one. ## 2. The central pressure, in one sentence, and the five reasons it cannot be moved

**The wheel.** A wheel has turned at one speed at the near end of that first table for thirty-one years, and in the whole of that run it has been stopped at the end of a day and once, for about as long as a sound takes to leave a yard and come back to it. It is the only thing in that yard that has been saying something out loud, every morning, for longer than anybody in it has been counting, and nobody has ever asked it anything.

A reader could name that without a figure: **the yard has a voice in it that nobody has ever stopped, and this volume makes somebody stop it.**

### 2.1 The five reasons it cannot be moved, each with the thing that would move it

1. **Nobody may ask him about it.** Not the clerk, not the boy, not the man of about thirty-two, not the woman of fifty-two, not anybody. The asking is not stopped and not withdrawn and has not gone quiet; it is simply not there, and the readout may not print that in any form, including as a count.
2. **The wheel is not a figure.** It is not on the page at the back of that lot book, not in the lot book, not on a wall, not down that lane, not on a board, and no instrument may be pointed at it.
3. **There is nothing to thank him for.** He has not done a thing anybody can put a line to. The measure has never taken a line for anything he did and takes none now.
4. **He has given no reason and is not going to be asked for one.** He gets as far as the front of one sentence every morning and stops, and he has never told anybody that he gets as far as the front of it, and this volume does not tell it for him.
5. **Thirty-one years is not a figure anybody in that yard holds,** and this volume may not turn it into one, may not count the mornings it has turned, may not count the times it has been stopped, and may not print any difference between any two of those.

### 2.2 The cheaper moves, and why each fails

Giving him a reason fails — he has not got one and inventing one is a new institution. Giving him an answerer fails — every person who could ask is forbidden. Having somebody else stop it fails — a second pair of hands on that frame is a new person and a new authority. Having the page at the back of that lot book take a line for it fails — that page has never taken a line for anything anybody did at that frame and does not start now. And having him say the sentence fails outright: see §7.3.
