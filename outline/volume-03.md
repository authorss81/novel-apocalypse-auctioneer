# Volume 03 Outline: The Hearth Commons

Chapter range: 101–150

**This file was written at the opening of the volume, from `outline/series.md` and `outline/ending.md`, and Volume 03 Batch 0001 was written against it. Where a chapter and this file disagree, the chapter is canon and this file is corrected.**

## Central pressure

The **Salt Verge** is thirty miles east, on the wrong side of a river that came up nine feet in the flood year and has not gone down. It grows salt. Salt is the one thing this basin cannot do without for eleven months of the year, and the district eats what it can reach, which since the flood is a small part of what it grew.

The Verge's looms can restore the pans. A loom is a wind frame, a channel, and a bed of brine, and it is an anchor lot in the ordinary sense: it sustains one abnormal local rule — **the loom draws the water it is given and holds the channel exactly, and the salt comes out of the water whole** — and somebody pays its toll. The toll is **a year of a worker's memory, taken at the loom and not asked for by anybody, and the worker is bound to the channel for the length of the year.** Not a memory of a thing that happened. **A year.** A loom-worker at the end of a season can tell you what the water was doing and cannot tell you what last year was.

**A place that has a railway and four toll boards and a charter that can be revoked in a sentence has not yet found a way to say what a toll lands on a place, and the first large thing anybody in this district will be asked to do with a dangerous object since the ninth of March is put to a person whose body makes it work.** The volume's engine is the engine the last volume built: the same method, applied to a subject on which the method is not neutral, because the instrument the district lacks is the one that would let a stranger read a cost off a piece of paper instead of off a person.

The volume's question, carried from Volume 02 and not answered in it: **what is a public office that a signal can remember and a basin cannot find, and what was it for.** The name is spelled out in full on three toll boards, on five hundred printed sheets, on a security schedule and in the water office's own book. It is in five registers this basin can reach and it is in none of them. Eleven trades could not state what it did. Four people in four rooms said four things, all four about carrying something somebody would not carry themselves, and they arrived separately and nobody has joined them, and the registrar has refused six times and has said out loud that nobody has ever asked her why.

**Volume 03's work on that question is to make it harder, and to be refused.** It is not answered by a reading, by a document that turns up, by an inference, or by a person who remembers.

## Volume promise

By the end of Volume 03:

- **The Salt Loom charter is a public instrument with rotating workers, a visible memory ledger, and a right to withdraw before renewal**, and it holds for a crop. The first public-use precedent survives with the crop.
- **Alder Reach and the Salt Verge sign a food agreement**, and Adrian's market is a regional institution with a charter and a clerk, not a private business with a reputation.
- **Adrian is a Steward, and the promotion is not a screen.** It comes from maintaining a lot that is bigger than one room through a season in which he is one of about nine people keeping it, and it costs him the thing he has spent a volume refusing to spend: **the right to be neutral.** His name becomes the address for the aggregate unpaid toll, and a family he has never met is the reason the ledger exists.
- **The instrument that says what a toll lands on a place still has no name and no owner.** It gets used in anger by a keeper who is not paid and by a man of sixty-one with a frame, and nobody can say what it is, and the volume ends on that.
- **Nera Voss's first agents arrive with a ledger**, and the ledger shows the same memory tolls in a city that is not on any map this basin has.

## Starting state

### Practical position

- **A charter is closed, dated, and ends on the twenty-ninth of July.** Three bearers hold a week in an order settled on the back of a mechanic's pencil; a fourth bearer whose only standing is the ending ends the use by saying a reason out loud. The term runs from the twenty-ninth of June to the twenty-ninth of July and **nobody may extend it and nobody has.**
- **A security is posted and it is made of time and paper:** one day a week at the ninth place from the first hour of the morning until the hour a train is due to move, written in chalk on the fourth toll board, and three bound copies of the line book at eleven coppers each, inspectable by any person at any time without asking anybody's permission. **It ends on the twenty-ninth of July.**
- **A boundary at the west end** is two named people, a stated term, a published entry, and a hole in the ground that is not a mark. The ground is held by nobody. **It is due to be asked a fourth time on the twenty-ninth of July, from a counting room and not from a bank.**
- **The month is July. Last month is June. The month before last is May.** The batch opens on or after the eighth of July, which is where Chapter 100 closed.
- **The train on the siding arrived on the eleventh of February** and had stood a hundred and forty-seven days on the eighth of July. Any figure that depends on a date is derived from the eleventh of February and never carried forward.
- **A toll on a place is a countable number** and the first one anybody in this basin can state is one departure in a hundred and forty-two, and it is stated because the place it landed on was asked first.
- **A hundred and eighty-three coppers went on nothing at the ninth place** in the second week of last month and nobody can say who is owed it, and **the answer is not to be struck.** About nine hundred households in this district are in no row; nineteen people at the ninth place are not in a household; thirty-one of them live on ground held by nobody.
- **The span at St. Orra is still shut** with its instrument certificate suspended by two people who are not Selik Marne, the middle plank of the old footbridge in the service cut, and seventeen people on the far bank with a stamped book and no way home.
- **The west end cannot report itself**: no ground signal, a plinth with the bolt-holes in it and a cap of lead over them. The standing instruction to walk the line is **discharged and not satisfied.**

### Starting relationships

- **Adrian and Mara Quill:** further apart in law than they have ever been and closer in practice than they have ever been, and neither has said a word. A page in the public lot book carries two hands for the first time in nine weeks and the registrar wrote the inspectability clause under his name. A slate has gone past in a kitchen three times and a reader at a counter has written down that six people have now seen that kitchen and that she is not going to ask.
- **Adrian and Tarin Kest:** a Binder's counterparty, not an apprentice. He gave up the lease, the salvage and the standing; he has seven vehicles a week and no yard; he said in a yard that a thing a man gives up and a thing a man is given are not the same size.
- **Adrian and Selik Marne:** a wary ally and not a friend. His office was declined five to five in a public figure, his own man is unpaid, he asks for that man to be paid in writing and is refused in about four minutes, and he says *that is not enough* on published days and does not stop. **He is not on the boundary's walk and is not put on it.**
- **Adrian and Elias Rooke:** the man who ends the use, sixty-one, who has never met Adrian and about whom Adrian said in a counting room that he would have said no if he had been asked.
- **Adrian and Orren Pike:** seventeen, paid four coppers a quarter for an index, not his apprentice, and the person who has asked the registrar to keep four things apart six times.
- **Adrian and Lina Vale:** partly unrecorded, not thanked and will not be, with a booked autumn read-back.

### Starting power level

**Adrian is a Binder, as of the twenty-ninth of June at about half past eleven at night, in a hut, with no panel.** He may write a bounded charter, split a use among bearers, close a transfer, make a lot portable or renewable under a stated term, and post a security. **He may not maintain a public lot, coordinate more than one bearer, arrange a dispute hearing, or make the consequences of an object visible to a whole settlement, and this district has no public lot, four bearers and no hearing.** He has **one departure left** and the next permitted hour is not before the fifteenth of July. **Five refusals to read are on the record and none of them is written as cowardice or as discipline.**

## Major locations

- **The Salt Verge**, thirty miles east, across a river that has not gone down: the pans, the loams, the wind frames, the channel, the workers' row.
- **The Hearth Line junction** at the ninth place: a platform thirty feet long, a lamp on a post cut out of a green pipe, a rail along the back edge, a toll board with no heading on it, a hole in the ground that is not a mark.
- **Mara's records office** and the ward market counting room, and the public lot book in nine copies on eleven boards in the yard of Lot 17, where a stranger can take it cold and the room lets him.
- **The river market**, on the tide, where a woman of forty-four keeps a Road House and lends her yard once a month and is not thanked and insists it be entered as not a security.

## Major factions and pressure groups

- **Farmer cooperatives on the Verge**, who want the looms and know what the looms cost and have never been asked which of those two they want.
- **The market's own Sheet** and the Wardens, who fund what is written and refuse what is not asked for.
- **Lot Guilds**, whose certificate has twenty-nine columns and not one of them is a place, and whose inspector cannot refuse.
- **Nera Voss's first agents**, who arrive late in the volume with a ledger and a courtesy.
- **Worker councils**, which do not exist at the start of the volume and are the volume's last new institution.

## Escalation sequence

### Chapters 101–110: The twenty-ninth of July

Four things come to the end of a term on one day: the charter's security, the three weeks' rotation, the boundary at the west end, and the man who comes twice a month. The sixth search for the office is not for the name and not for the function but for **the work** — for the traces such an office would have left in the books it kept — and it finds four gaps in four books in four rooms, and a shape is not an office. The permitted reading hour opens on the fifteenth and nobody asks him, and he is asked on the sixteenth, and he refuses, and the refusal is the sixth and it is entered, and a refusal is a reading. **A term ends the use without the man who built the right to stop it standing up, and the twenty-ninth has no climax in it and that is the finding.** Nothing is running in the first week of August and nobody dies and nobody knows whether that is luck or a system. On the fourth day a keeper starts writing in a column that has been empty since the twenty-second of last month and cannot say what the instrument is. A hundred and eighty-three coppers go into a market book under a heading with no name against it, and a sentence somebody said out loud in a room comes within one step of the office question and is not joined to it, and the column for things that are not findings gets one more line.

### Chapters 111–120: A man from the salt pans

The first consequence of nothing running is a district with a railway and no schedule, and a man from the Verge walks eleven miles to ask whether the water at the ninth place can be counted. The party goes east. The looms are described by the people who work them, in their own words, and not by a reading. **Adrian tries to buy a loom outright and the worker who bears the memory refuses, and the reason is a sentence about a price and a body.** The first instrument the district builds is a thing that says what a loom costs, and it costs the man who fills it in a year of his own memory of the trade, which is the finding and is not a solution. The guild inspector certifies a consent that four people signed and one person did not attend, and the certificate is valid, and the registrar cannot strike it, and the registrar's own instrument is the thing that fails.

### Chapters 121–130: A year, taken and not asked for

The first channel fails. A food convoy has to choose between a public loom and a private one, and the private one is cheaper and the public one is asked. The aggregate unpaid toll becomes visible for the first time as a number against Adrian's own name, **and the number is not his fault and is his responsibility, and the entry says both.** A worker council forms because a loom-worker wrote down four things that had happened to four people and would not sign them. Nera Voss's first agent is courteous and does not hide what she is offering, which is worse. The romance moves one sentence and does not resolve.

### Chapters 131–140: The ledger and the right to withdraw

The memory ledger is built: what a loom takes, from whom, in what year, in a column anybody can go and look at. **A right to withdraw before renewal is the hardest instrument in the volume, because a worker who withdraws on the last day of a season is a worker who has already paid.** The batch that builds it fails once in public. The first irrigation channel fails and is written down with the date, the way the stall was written down with the date. Adrian's public status becomes a financial liability and he is told so in his own trade's words.

### Chapters 141–150: The crop and the precedent

The crop survives. The Salt Loom charter closes with rotating workers, a visible ledger, and a withdrawal right, and the closing is done by people who are not Adrian and entered by people who are not Adrian. **Alder Reach and the Salt Verge sign a food agreement and the agreement is witnessed by two people who will be somewhere else when the reason is read out.** The instrument that says what a toll lands on a place gets used four times in anger in the last ten chapters and still has no name and no owner. **Nera Voss's ledger shows the same memory tolls in a city that is not on any map this basin has.**

## Midpoint reversal — Chapters 121–126

Adrian attempts to buy a loom outright — a single purchaser, a single price, a single security, his own signature at the bottom — **and the worker who bears the memory refuses, and is right, and the refusal is not about the price.** An item's price cannot separate the person whose body makes it work. Everything the district has built for nine weeks assumes a place can be charged and a person can be asked, and this object requires both at once and offers neither.

## Volume climax — Chapters 146–150

A **shared Salt Loom charter** closes with rotating workers, a visible memory ledger, and a right to withdraw before renewal. The crop survives and the first public-use precedent survives with it. The charter is closed by a worker council and entered by a clerk of the ward market, and Adrian's name is on it as a guarantor of nothing and as the man who is responsible for the aggregate unpaid toll. **There is no panel.** The closing sentence in the volume is the entry that says what a stage is not.

## Concrete resolution

Alder Reach and the Salt Verge sign a food agreement on seasonal terms. Adrian's market is a regional institution with a printed charter, a rotating set of stewards, a dispute process that a person can reach, and a toll board at every place a thing stops. **The fifth instrument — a way of saying that a toll lands on a place — still has no name and no owner and is used every week by four unpaid keepers and a man of sixty-one with a frame.** The station at the fourth place has a person in it for the first time in nine years and the person is not a keeper and does not want to be one.

## Relationship progression

- **Adrian and Mara Quill** move from two hands on one page to a conversation of eleven minutes about a form, which is not the conversation, and the volume does not say so. The romance remains unresolved at Chapter 150 and Volume 04 opens on it unresolved.
- **Adrian and Tarin Kest** disagree in public about how much danger a community should be allowed to keep, and Adrian is wrong in the disagreement and is not let off.
- **Adrian and Elias Rooke** become two people who have met once and have both said so out loud.
- **Adrian and Nera Voss's agent** never meet Adrian. She deals with the worker council and Adrian reads the result.
- **Orren Pike** becomes a registrar with a limited term and independent authority in Volume 06, and in this volume he is a boy who is right about what a thing is and wrong about what it means, and the registrar refuses the wrong half again.

## Power progression

**Steward begins in Chapter 121 and is visible by Chapter 141, and it is never a screen.** He maintains a lot that is bigger than one room through a season in which he is one of about nine people keeping it. **He inherits the aggregate unpaid toll:** the memory that the looms take and that nobody has paid is entered as one figure against his name, and he cannot make it a priority for anybody, and he can close a transfer but cannot close one that has no named holder. **He loses the right to be neutral**, because a neutral man cannot be the address of a figure that is not a priority for anybody. He does not become replaceable — **nobody in this district knows how to make a man replaceable, and that is entered as not known and not as a delay.**

## Mystery progression

- **The office is not answered.** Six searches, then a search for the work, then a refusal to join four gaps. **The name is in five places and in no register. The function cannot be stated by eleven trades. The work cannot be found in four books. Nobody is to answer it in a guess.**
- **The registrar's seven refusals** and the sentence she said out loud on the fifth of July — that she has given the same reason six times and nobody has ever asked her why — are approached once in this volume, by a person who asks her why she holds two lines apart, and her answer is about holding two lines apart and is entered in the column for things that are not findings and **is not an answer to the office.**
- **The blank lot, Iven, the origin of the Common Measure, the First House, the removed signature, and the three unexplained things of Volume 01 are untouched.**
- **The new mystery opened by the volume** is Nera Voss's ledger: the same memory tolls, in a city that is not on any map this basin has. It is not answered here.

## Continuity guardrails

- **The object does not hold places, will not name one, and repeats itself when asked for more. No reading may name a place, and the office's name must never be written as a place.** The ninth place is what the documents say. **Sarrow Water is not written on a document by anybody, and above all not by a reading.**
- **A toll on a place takes memory of departure and a fraction of it, and the fraction is smaller the second time. The echo of an origin toll is a loss of spatial sense: a man with it cannot tell which way a river is going, cannot hold a pair, and must be walked to a door by somebody who can.** It is not a generic cost.
- **Binding cannot create consent.** A person who materially refuses cannot be counted as a willing witness. A person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and the record must say *not asked*. A remembering passenger is a condition on the use and not a bearer.
- **A refusal to read is a reading and is entered, and it is neither cowardice nor discipline.** Six are on the record at the end of Batch 0001. He has one departure left.
- **A destination can be a victim too. The admission is spent and is not re-staged.**
- **The stalls are the basin's own documented Fracture symptom and are not a mystery.** The west end's inability to report itself is a fact about instruments; the water office's form list is a fact about a form list; the signal's memory pattern is a fact about sixty-one copies of a column kept in drawers. **None of the three is joined to any other.**
- **A mark may not carry a job, and the boundary was built around that rule.** A line of stones is not a mark, because a mark is cut and that is laid. The standing test case is not extended and there is nobody who is to lay a stone.
- **The two lines holding the office's name are not joined; the two lines holding the impressions are not joined; the four entries in the column of sixty-one and the four in the keeper's ORIGIN HELD column are not the same four, so there is a fifth somewhere; and the shape is not joined to the mark on the iron.** Seven refusals stand at the end of Batch 0001 and a registrar has asked to be asked.
- **The fifth instrument — a way of saying that a toll lands on a place — is still unnamed and unowned.** Four toll boards and five hundred printed sheets and nobody's name on it. **Five instruments this district DOES NOT HAVE; six it has built and not named — a rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security. The two lists are not the same list.**
- **The protected category is not a bucket and the count is closed at five.** A passage twice, a loss once, a failure of a remedy once, a refusal to be asked again once. The four conditions with no end on it are a different class of thing and are never entered in that tally.
- **The woman of forty-five is not a name in the book and is not asked again.** Not asked is in the count, and a count is a column and not a protection.
- **Selik is a wary ally and not a friend. His span is still shut. He is not on the boundary's walk and is not put on it.**
- **Halloway Reed is right about speed, about days, about the morning a road fails, and about a road owing a correction, and wrong about a place costing four coppers.** He is not humiliated and not beaten by a printing press.
- **No public market is built out of one reader.** The twenty-ninth of July is asked by somebody who is not Adrian and entered by somebody who is not Adrian.
- **Feet, inches, yards, miles and quarters, pounds and ounces, degrees, coppers, pints, and the twelve-hour clock. No metric and no twenty-four-hour clock. And no weekday attached to any date.**
- **Emphasis is a document register and it cannot cross a blank line.** A speech running over paragraphs opens and closes in every chunk and the closing quotation mark goes only on the last chunk. **Every paragraph holds an even number of the bold markers.** The count of odd paragraphs in the whole of Volume 02 is exactly seven and is not to grow in Volume 03.
- **No character quotes a relative date inside a document, and any day inside a blockquote takes its month.** Narration uses a relative month freely; the risk is that a relative month in narration quietly becomes a document.
- **Five near-identical figures live at the west end of this line and a chapter may not merge them:** the chandler **Pell Anselm**, about thirty-four, a counter of oil on Ash Row and a stall in front of it; the **trader of fifty-one** on the lower terrace, who supplies the Harrow engine's oil out of her own till; the **woman of the column**, about fifty, who is the water at the ninth place; **Sef Anyard**, fifty-one, who keeps the only pair; and the **woman of forty-five**, who is not a name in the book. A cordwainer of about forty-eight in a doorway at the west end of Alder Reach is a sixth and belongs to none of them. **Give them a trade, a doorway or a stall in the same paragraph and never let a pronoun carry the identification alone.**
