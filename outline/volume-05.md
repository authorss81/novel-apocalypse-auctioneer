## Central pressure

The **Crownless Uplands** hold the oldest surviving civic records in this basin, in caves cut into a shoulder of rock that predates the Long Fracture and that four generations of keepers have never let be emptied. They were kept because there was nothing else to keep them in. They have been read by perhaps forty people.

In the Uplands there are nine structures that hold water back. The people who keep them call them **locks**. A lock does not open a road. A lock holds a road shut, and the water it holds decides which roads are passable, and **when a lock stands open every road below it opens with it, all at once, whatever anybody meant.** Nine locks have begun standing open on the same night, one after another, in a run of eleven days, and nobody in the Uplands has opened them.

**A single public charter could keep the watershed safe.** There is a version of it that is one sheet, one holder, one term with no end on it, and one name on it, and it would work tomorrow. It is the version the Lock Council has been writing since before this volume opened, and the reason it is available is that a person has to be at the back of the book for a toll to be fastened to anybody, and this basin has exactly one such person and he is standing at the back of one.

**And whoever writes that charter controls the next season.** Not next year. **The season.** The water decides what is sown and what is not, and the Uplands feed four districts, and a person who holds the water for one season holds the sowing for the next.

Volume 05's engine is the engine the last four volumes built: **ask a person in the six things, and then write down what they said in their own words, and keep the count of what was not asked in a column and not in a bucket.** Volume 04 proved that this instrument is also a door — a book that works if a person comes to the yard is a book a stranger can walk up to and read. **Volume 05 is the volume in which somebody walks up to it.** The first party in eighteen months who is not a neighbour and not a creditor comes to Alder Reach and reads the page, and what they want is on it.

## Volume promise

By the end of Volume 05:

- **Nine local charters stand around one watershed, each with its own bearers, its own term and its own right to withdraw**, and the nine locks stay finite. Nobody owns the water. Not Adrian, not the Council, not Orin.
- **The Upland assemblies adopt the first regional public covenant in this basin**, argued in public, hand-copied, read out at counters, and entered in a book that a stranger may walk up to — and it is entered with the hole in it on its face.
- **Adrian is a Convener, and the promotion is not a screen.** It is earned by convening nine parties who each thought the water was theirs, and it costs him the last private thing he has: **his name becomes a public index.** A stranger can now ask for Adrian by name instead of asking for a page, and that is a smaller, worse door than the last one.
- **The single-guarantor safeguard is shown to have been designed to distribute power and not to crown anybody** — nine people put their names to a rule that says no one may hold this alone, and the rule was written to be broken by exactly the kind of man it was written to stop.
- **The romance moves and does not resolve.** It has to move: eleven months of professional cooperation with nothing said in it is not a relationship, it is a habit, and the volume has to be honest that a habit is a thing two people can be inside without having chosen each other. **It must not end on a settlement.**
- **The ninth design signature belongs to Iven Tallow**, and Iven is not in this volume.

## Major locations

- **The Crownless Uplands** — a shoulder of rock with nine locks on the water above it and four districts below it, and four generations of keepers who have never emptied the caves.
- **The nine locks** — not nine gates. **The people who keep them call them locks and the Registry calls them gates and the two words are not the same word.**
- **The old record caves** — the oldest surviving civic records in the basin, in a hand four hundred years old in one case and in a column with no heading over it in another.
- **A winter market that accepts no Tally currency** — a market two days north where settlement is in salt, in labour-days and in a coin struck in a place that is not named, and where a person can buy a thing without anybody writing a page about it.
- **Alder Reach**, and specifically the yard of Lot Seventeen, which is where every batch in this volume that stays in the district happens.

## Major factions and pressure groups

- **The Lock Council** — administrators of the Uplands, most of them second-generation, all of them with a list. They are arguing with Selik Marne, who is a wary ally and not a friend and is in the Uplands for his own reasons.
- **Upland assemblies** — village bodies that meet in barns, decide by show of hands, and have never had to decide anything with water in it.
- **The Guilds** — inspectors who certify things and who have a form with no column for whether a person was in the room, and who will certify a defect on the same form and did.
- **Orin's Registry** — a body that holds records of holdings, prints a house and does not print a person, and that is looking for this district because this district is not on its list.
- **Independent memory keepers** — people who hold other people's winters in their heads and are not institutions.

## Escalation sequence

### Chapters 201–210: The first of April to the eleventh of the fourth month

**The lease arrives.** A Registry lease clerk of about forty-four walks into Alder Reach with a portfolio and a renewal of a lease on a place that is not named on the copy he is carrying, and the leaseholder is the Registrar-General, Cael Orin. He came because this district is not on the Registry's list, and the only reason he has found it is a page in a book a stranger can walk up to. **The batch's finding: this district's best instrument is a door, and the first party in eighteen months who has needed it did not come to the yard.** The batch ends with a dray going up a road with two people in it and a yoke on the tailboard that nobody asked the owner about.

### Chapters 211–220: the twelfth to the twenty-first of the fourth month

**The nine dates.** A copy of the nine locks' standing orders comes down with the first dray and gives nine dates, and a route keeper gives eight of the same nine differently, and the ninth he will not give. **The finding: two lists of nine that agree on eight are not one list, and the one they disagree about is the one that matters.** The Lock Council writes to Alder Reach for the first time and asks, politely, in writing, whether this district will send a man to a council. Adrian reads it out in a yard. The woman of fifty-eight gets her round back for eleven houses and not ninety, and says so.

### Chapters 221–230: the twenty-second of the fourth month to the second of the fifth month

**The Council's chair, and the record caves.** The Council offers Adrian the founder's chair over a single permanent water charter and he refuses in a room and then again in a yard. **This is the batch that spends the midpoint reversal Volume 04 did not spend: one of his own posted securities has been assigning unpaid labour to the registrar's registry, and the disclosure is made by a person who was not going to make it and made it because the Registry was in the room.** The caves give up a page with **nine names on it and one rule**, and the rule says that no person shall hold this alone, and the nine are the designers. **The month turns on the second of the fifth month and the ninth name is not on the page.**

### Chapters 231–240: the third to the twelfth of the fifth month

**The gate tolls arrive in villages that did not sign.** A lock's toll, distributed by the Registry, lands in four places that never agreed to it, and Adrian's own reading of a lock disagrees with what the Uplands say it does — **twice, in front of witnesses, and he is wrong both times and says so.** Mara's registry is named in a Registry notice as a rival record, and she is asked in the six things and answers.

### Chapters 241–250: the thirteenth to the twenty-second of the fifth month

**Nine charters, one watershed, nine rights to withdraw.** He convenes nine separate local charters and gives each settlement a right to withdraw without giving back a season. The locks stay finite. **Nobody owns the water.** The Upland assemblies adopt the first regional public covenant. The Lock Council loses the immediate vote and calls Adrian a founder in a letter that goes out on nine walls. **The ninth design signature is Iven Tallow's, and he is not in the volume and does not appear and is presumed dead from Adrian's point of view throughout.**

## Midpoint reversal — Chapters 224–227

**The Council offers Adrian the founder's chair if he will accept a single permanent water charter.** He refuses. **And the Upland records show that nine people deliberately designed the single-guarantor safeguard** — not to crown a king, but to forbid one — and that the rule was written by somebody who expected a man exactly like Adrian to come along eventually and try.

**The Volume 04 midpoint reversal is spent here and nowhere else, and this is the decision the volume-close record asked for.** It was not spent in Volume 04 and no file said which chapters would spend it. It is a disclosure that one of his own "secure" lots has been assigning unpaid labour to the registrar's registry. **It is disclosed by the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray, who is the fifth of five posted securities, because the Registry put a name on a page and the name was his and he read it in a room with the registrar in it.** He loses nothing that was his and pays for saying it. **Transparency alone does not prevent abuse, and the instrument that fails is the disclosure, not the secrecy.**

## Volume climax — Chapters 244–249

He convenes nine separate local charters around the watershed and gives each settlement a right to withdraw. **The gates stay finite. No one owns the water.** The Covenant of the Nine Locks is a regional public instrument with rotating stewards, public tolls, a stated term and an appeal path into another region. **It is the first regional public covenant in this basin and it is not a constitution and does not pretend to be.**

## Concrete resolution

The Upland assemblies adopt it, in public, in barns, by show of hands, and the four districts below the Uplands sign separately because they are not the Uplands. **Adrian learns that the safeguard was designed to distribute power, not to crown a king** — and that the nine who wrote it did not expect to be thanked, and one of them wrote a rule about being thanked.

## Power progression

**Underwriter to Convener.** Underwriter pools linked tolls and prices a route's dependencies; its limit is probability, not certainty. **Convener negotiates among communities and splits authority; its limit is that his name becomes a public index and he has no private neutrality left.** The change is not a screen: it is granted by the Upland assemblies, in public, and the price is that a letter calling him a founder goes out on nine walls and cannot be answered by a man in a yard.

**He does not become able to create consent, to hold two locks at once, to make an assembly decide, or to give a person a term they did not agree to.** Withdrawal and disagreement remain possible, and the volume says so on the page in at least three chapters.
