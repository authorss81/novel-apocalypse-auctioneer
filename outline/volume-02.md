# Volume 02 Outline: The Borrowed Road

Chapter range: 51–100

## Central pressure

The **Needle Tramway** — the elevated rail out of Alder Reach toward the coast and the Crownless Uplands — has begun **moving Faults along the line**. Since the Long Fracture the basin's own incident record has listed rail signals accepting destinations that were never entered as the first symptom of the collapse, and for nine years the Needle's symptoms have been stalls: a length of line where the ground no longer agrees with itself and a train can stand in it and neither the driver nor the signaller can say why.

The thing that answers a stall is a **reversal**. The **Harrow Tongue** — a cast-iron interlocking tongue taken out of the flooded Harrow interchange in the fourth year and kept in a signal hut at **Sarrow Water** — can send one vehicle back along the section it came from, out of a place with no other way out. It costs the origin. **The train forgets where it started, and the origin settlement loses the memory of its own departures.** Every reversal leaves a stall where the train stood, so the Fault walks west along the line, and the line's own stock has been stabled on a siding at Sarrow Water for nine years because the Alder Reach yards are full of things nobody can move.

Sarrow Water is a siding, a water column, and thirty-one people. It has no platform, it is in no rate sheet, it is not a stop, and the district's most admired roadkeeper signed the lease. Adrian Vale's reading of the tongue is technically correct and names no place, because the object does not hold places. **The batch problem, the volume problem, and the batch's own discovery are the same sentence: a destination can be a victim too.** The toll's destination is a village the train does not stop at, and every instrument Alder Reach built in five weeks — the proposal schedule, the count rule, the no-name remedy — was built for people who were asked, and the village of Sarrow Water has never been asked anything by anybody.

The immediate question is operational: can a railway be run in public at all, and can the trains be got home? The deeper question is the one Volume 01 set up: the volume that has spent five weeks proving that a limit is real must now discover that **a transferable right is not a neutral right** — that a train which can be sent back is a train whose toll belongs to whoever is holding the far end, and that the only thing standing between a route and a queue with a timetable is a person who can be replaced.

## Volume promise

By the end of Volume 02:

- **The Needle Tramway is a coalition route** with public toll boards, local stewards at named places, a published timetable with days on it, and a west end that is closed by a dated entry and not by an opinion.
- **Sarrow Water is in the public book** as a place with a count, a rate sheet, a named steward, and a standing entry that says what has been taken from it and by whom.
- **Adrian is a Binder, and the promotion is not a panel.** It comes from closing a rotating multi-bearer charter with a posted security, and it costs him the thing he has spent five weeks refusing to spend: a real asset, a real term, and the standing of a person who can hold more than one place.
- **Selik Marne is a wary ally, not a defeated friend.** His permanent-office proposal is answered, not buried, and the room with the door on it is opened and its contents published and the district still votes against him.
- **The broken circle appears a second time**, on a document Adrian has never seen, in a hut nine miles west.
- **The signal's memory pattern gives a name that is not a place**, and the name is the name of a pre-Long-Fracture public office, and not a guild, and nobody in the basin can find that office in any register they can reach. **This is the volume's unanswered question and it is not answered here.**

## Starting state

### Practical position

- The **Hearth Line exists and is finite.** One crossing was performed, on the eighteenth of the month before this one, with one bearer, one activation and one term of twelve minutes: twenty-six offered, twenty-four travelling, two declines entered and neither struck, thirty-one tolls across seventeen distinct people, seventeen people left on the far bank, a token cracked through and parted at the rim, a shipment uncomputed and not nil, and a hearing called and not discharged.
- **The Last Fare is in a public case** in a locked office behind the sale floor, entered as **a lot with a condition** and not as evidence: the crack through the plate, the chip out of the reverse, two hundred and four grains against two hundred and eleven, five openings, and *it is not known whether it will open again*. The key rotates. **Hester Vane holds it from the twenty-sixth for six days, and the term expires at midnight on the third of the new month** (the twenty-sixth, twenty-seventh, twenty-eighth, first, second, third — **six days, and Chapter 51's margin note says six; the earlier "seven days" in this file was the error and is corrected), and the standing instruction is that the holder on the day of expiry is named in the book **before** the key is held, or it is not held.
- **The far bank has no crossing of any kind.** The official span is shut to all carriage with its instrument certificate suspended by two people who are not Selik Marne. The middle plank of the old footbridge is in the service cut. The river stairs are a walk and not a crossing: one person at a time, about three hours each way in daylight, with a clerk's lamp at the top who can see nine feet and cannot see the turn.
- **Lot 17's public lot book is open** in nine copies on eleven boards, and it is the first thing in Alder Reach a hostile stranger can take cold and use.

### Starting relationships

- **Adrian and Mara Quill:** further apart in law than they have ever been and closer in practice than they have ever been, and not one word said about it in public or otherwise. She holds a book he cannot post into. He asked a question of the brass she built the instrument for. They pass a slate in a kitchen at a quarter to seven in the morning.
- **Adrian and Tarin Kest:** operational partners with a real unresolved argument. Tarin keeps the terms, Adrian keeps the limit, and Selik Marne has said in public that a limit checked by one man is a man and not a limit, and Adrian agreed it was fair. **Tarin's Road House holds a nine-year lease on the Sarrow Water siding and metered water, and he is about to be the person who has to answer for it.**
- **Adrian and Selik Marne:** argued with, never defeated. His order of the ninth has been reviewed by the market that funds its inspection and found correct, and the district does not want it. His permanent-office proposal is tabled and alive. **The standing order with no expiry, in a room on the second floor of the water office with a door on it, has still not been read by anybody in Alder Reach.**
- **Adrian and Orren Pike:** Orren is seventeen, a copy clerk with a sheet of eleven lines and his mother's name on the sixth of it, and he has said that nobody has offered him a job. **The Needle line book is an index nobody has built and he will build it whether or not he is paid.**
- **Adrian and Lina Vale:** a negotiated public participation. She booked the autumn. She has not been thanked and will not be. The protected category has carried a passage twice, a loss once, a failure of a remedy once, and a refusal to be asked again once, and it must not become a bucket.
- **Halloway Reed:** technically attractive, morally dangerous, honest on a tram arch with the toll on page one, forty-one customers, and right about the standing objection: on the morning a public road fails a book does not open a door and the only purchasable thing is speed.

### Starting power level

- **Stage 1 Reader, and the volume does not promote him for its first forty chapters.** He can read one contacted lot at a time, cannot predict, cannot read a document nobody has handled, and cannot close a split, transfer a use, renew a charter, or post security. **A reading of the Needle's object will be technically correct and will not name the place the toll lands on, and the book must not let him off by having the object say the name.**

## Major locations

- **The Needle line, Alder Reach to Harrow:** twelve places in the near section, elevated on a brick and iron viaduct for the first four, then on embankment and then on the top of a bank above the river. Sarrow Water is the ninth place and has a siding, a water column, a hut, and no platform.
- **Signal huts:** one keeper, one lever frame, one ground signal, one lamp, one book kept by hand. **The Alder Reach signal cabin** at the east end holds the line book.
- **Tarin Kest's Needle Road House:** the workshops, the stabling yard, and the lease.
- **Harrow interchange:** flooded since the flood year, the end of the line, the place the Tongue was taken out of.
- **The service-cut yards:** four streets west of the depot, two blocks the ward market has never had a rate sheet for, the people nearest the fault boundary, and the foot of the river stairs.
- **Lot 17:** the public lot book, the four-seat board, the case, the yard, and the staging line.
- **The ward market counting room and counter:** where the rate is voted and where a reader reads out whatever anybody puts on the counter.
- **The second floor of the water office:** a room with a door on it and a standing order in it with no expiry.

## Major factions and pressure groups

- **Road Houses:** independent workshops, stabling, and repair crews. Tarin Kest's is the best of them and holds the Sarrow lease. They keep the basin supplied and they are perfectly capable of taking a thing from a village by accident and then being right that it was an accident.
- **Civic Wardens and Selik Marne's bridge administration:** legitimate safety authority with a shut span, a suspended certificate, a correct order nobody wants, and a draft permanent office nobody has read.
- **Signal keepers and the signal crew:** the tenth hand's people, holders of a page, workers who hold a pair by standing and are not in any of the district's books.
- **Guild inspectors:** licensed valuers with checklists who can be right about a lot and wrong about a village.
- **The ward market and its trades:** eleven trades and four thousand and one households, capable of saying yes to a room and not yet to a road.
- **The village of Sarrow Water:** thirty-one people, no platform, no rate sheet, and a lease signed in Alder Reach.

## Escalation sequence

### Chapters 51–58: The line and the tongue

- The custody key's first expiry is watched and the standing instruction is very nearly not kept. The book needs a name, and the person who should hold it has never chosen one, and the district has to think to offer her a name. The term is shortened to four days because a rule nobody has had to keep is not a rule.
- One river-stair day is walked, and the count of seventeen is right in number and wrong in identity, and the reason is that a place travelled and a person did not. The foot of the stair is in the service-cut yards, and the ward market has no rate sheet for that ground.
- The standing order with no expiry is read aloud in the room with the door on it. Four drafts over nine years, no version written by anyone who read the last, competent, correct in intent, and unanswerable because nobody has read it. It cites a pre-Long-Fracture public office nobody in the basin can find in a register. **Selik asks for it to be published.**
- The object is introduced by a signal keeper in her own words, out of a hut, in weather, and not by a reading. A reversal saves a train and leaves a stall. The line book has a column nobody has a use for.
- The line is walked end to end, and the capacity of the line is found to be a sequence and not a number: a reversal at one place empties a stall into the next. **There is a ninth place that is not a stop, has no platform, and is a place in the line book.** Nobody in Alder Reach knew the line had a siding at Sarrow Water.
- Adrian reads the tongue. The reading is correct. The object does not hold places, and so the reading cannot say where the toll lands, and the reading is entered with its cost.
- The scarcity word fails again in a new shape, and the failure is worse because the scarcer direction is a flooded interchange with nobody on it.
- A boy who refused in public twice walks nine miles to a village that is not a stop and finds that nobody there can account for a single departure.

### Chapters 59–70: A destination can be a victim

- Adrian admits it in public and cannot price it, and three instruments built in five weeks fail on the same person for three different and correct reasons: the proposal schedule is about a use that is not being proposed, the no-name remedy is for people who refused a name and Sarrow Water refused nothing, and the count rule makes a village into a household, which is precisely the thing the count rule was written to stop being true of people.
- A payment is offered on an unstated claim in three columns, and the middle column says that no rate sheet exists for the ground.
- The route-keeper remedy, which Volume 01 wrote and never attempted, is attempted for the first time. **It is discharged for the train and not discharged for the village, and both halves are entered.**
- Selik closes the line and says the safest available action is to close the west end, and closing the west end strands every train at Sarrow Water.
- The first attempt at a multi-bearer charter fails, because a Stage 1 reader may draft a split and may not close one, and because a transferrable slot is not a pair.
- The ward market learns to say yes to a road once the road has a date, and the nine coppers Stenn refused comes back to his own room on its own.

### Chapters 71–80: A train that carries the anchor

- The Tongue begins travelling between Faults, and a lot that is in a hut eleven miles away is now in a hut four, and a keeper cannot hold a pair she has to take to another village.
- Selik's bridge administration is forced to staff a hut at Sarrow Water, and does it, and the district learns that the safest and the most hated are the same office.
- The counterfeit gets a schedule faster than the ward market can print one, and Halloway Reed sells a *timetable* with the toll on page one and is right about everything.
- Tarin and Adrian's crew protect the engine at Harrow without knowing who owns the signal's toll, and the answer is in a lease in a book in a room in Alder Reach.

### Chapters 81–90: Three stations

- The rotating multi-bearer charter is built by other people — a signal keeper, a Road House, a Road House that disagrees, a ward market, a village — and Adrian's part in it is to be the man who cannot close it and says so in a room.
- Public toll boards go up at three places on the line with a keeper at each, and the first thing written on a toll board is a name nobody at Alder Reach can find in a register.
- The second impression of the broken circle is found on a hut board, and the two impressions are entered in two lines and not joined.
- The standing order is answered in public by the district, in a market, and the district votes against the office and for the timetable, and the vote is close and is entered with the count each trade carried.

### Chapters 91–100: The Borrowed Road

- The west end is walked to a boundary and marked by people who do not own it, and the rule that a mark may not carry a job blocks the only instrument the district has, and the answer is two named people, a stated term, and a published entry.
- A train reaches three stations instead of one, under a charter that rotates bearers by the week, with a toll board at each station and a remedy that can be reached by a person the record cannot point at.
- **Adrian posts a real security and closes the split**, which is the Stage 2 change, and it is a term of labour and stock and not his recognition, and the entry says in the same line that a transferable right is not a neutral right.
- Selik is a wary ally. His span is still shut. His office proposal is answered and dead and he says he will keep saying so on published days, and nobody believes he will stop and he does not stop.
- The line book is copied to Sarrow Water, the ward market, and the Road House, and the third copy goes into the public lot book on a board of eleven, and a stranger can take it cold.
- The volume closes on the signal's memory pattern, on an entry whose origin is not a place, and on the name of a pre-Long-Fracture public office that is not a guild and that nobody in the Morrow Basin can find.

## Midpoint reversal — Chapters 69–74

Adrian's reading of the Harrow Tongue is correct: one use, one toll, one standing, all stated. The charter written on it is valid. The train comes home.

**The reversal is real, the toll is real, and the toll lands on a place.** Sarrow Water has lost its memory of departures — **one hundred and forty-one** movements have named it as the origin held since the flood year according to the line's own book (the outline's earlier figure of one hundred and four is superseded; the number in the prose is 141 and is not to be changed back), and not one person among thirty-one can account for a single one. The village is not refusing anything. It is not Unlisted, not protected, not sealed, not counted as a household and not counted as people. **It is simply a place that a lease was signed about, and the district's own central institutional rule — a person is accounted for by a count and not by a household — turned a village into a household and made thirty-one people invisible in the exact week it was written.**

Adrian is not wrong and cannot be made right. He says the sentence in a yard: **a destination can be a victim too.** The cost is that the public book, which is the best instrument anyone in this region has, cannot be the thing that fixes it, and that the man who spent five weeks building it has to say so in front of the people who are owed.

## Volume climax — Chapters 96–100

A train stands at Sarrow Water and cannot be reversed out, because reversing it charges the village again. Selik's bridge administration will staff the hut and will not accept a rotation. The ward market will fund a lamp and a stick of chalk and will not fund a timetable. The guild inspector says the lot is clean and cannot be sold to a village.

**Adrian and Tarin close a rotating multi-bearer charter** in which the use is split among four bearers in four huts, each bearer is a person with a place and an approach and a stated week, the toll is named as the origin and the origin is named as a place that has been asked, the term ends on a date, and two fresh witnesses who do not travel accept it. **The train reaches three stations instead of one.** The Fault does not disappear; it stops walking, and where it stopped is written down with the date.

The physical danger is not a monster. It is a lever, a flood, a wet evening, a train of nine vehicles that cannot be split, and a village of thirty-one people watching a railway leave their siding and not being able to say afterwards why any of them remembers it. Adrian does not extend the term. He ends it at the date he wrote, and something does not go over, and the yard sees him lose it.

## Concrete resolution

- **The Needle Tramway is a coalition route** with public toll boards at three places, local stewards named at each, a published timetable with days and a date, and a west end closed by an entry.
- **Sarrow Water is in the public book** with a count of thirty-one, a rate sheet of its own, a named steward, and a standing entry recording what has been taken from it and what is owed and by whom.
- **The Harrow Tongue is entered as a lot with a condition** in a **remote custody**: a keeper named, a rotation with a gap in it, the condition published in full, and the object described to anybody who turns up by the keeper herself, by hand, in a hut, with no case at all.
- **The route-keeper remedy is in the book twice** — discharged for a train, not discharged for a village — and its known failure is written where the people it failed can read it.
- **The district has a vocabulary problem it has solved once.** There is now a word on a wall and a sentence under it, and the sentence is that the word is a clock and not a word, and the clock is written down.
- **Selik's permanent office is answered and lost** and the district knows exactly what it declined and why, in a figure, in public.
- **The counterfeit's argument is still the best one in the book** and the answer is now longer and slower and has a date on it.
- **The First Tally remains limited and political.** It records a public settlement on a railway and does not declare the railway fair.

## Relationship progression

- **Adrian and Mara:** from working in parallel with no word spoken to **one instrument built together in public and signed by both of them in different hands.** They still do not say it. The volume's private movement is that Mara is the only person who can say *not asked* about a village, and Adrian is the only person who can price a reversal, and neither of them can do the other's job, and they stop pretending that is a problem to be solved later.
- **Adrian and Tarin:** the argument of Volume 01 comes due in cash. **Tarin signed the lease.** He is the man who parked a railway on a village out of ordinary competence and good intentions, and the volume does not let him off, and he does not ask to be let off, and he gives up the lease and the stabling yard and the standing that came with it.
- **Adrian and Orren:** Orren indexes the line book, is paid for it out of a rate the ward market voted, is still not Adrian's apprentice, and is **the person who finds the second impression of the broken circle** and who is right about what it is and wrong about what it means.
- **Adrian and Selik:** from argued with to **cooperating in public and disagreeing in writing.** Selik staffs a hut at a place he considers unsafe and says so every published day. He is a wary ally at the end of the volume and not a friend.
- **Adrian and Lina:** unchanged on the surface and not unchanged underneath. She is the only person who could have found Sarrow Water's courier route first, and she says in public that a courier network cannot be enrolled and that the reason is worse than a delay, and she is right, and it is the third time.

## Power progression

- **Stage 1 for the first four batches.** He reads the tongue once, correctly, and the reading costs an hour and gives him nothing he can price. He drafts a split and cannot close it. He is asked twice in the volume to close something he cannot close and says no both times in a room.
- **Stage 1 to Stage 2 — Binder, earned at the climax.** He posts a real security that is labour, stock, and standing, closes a four-bearer rotating charter, and takes a visible claim on the line that he cannot remove. **The entry says in the same line that he has gained a responsibility and not an authority, that a transferable right is not a neutral right, and that the charter can be revoked by four people who do not like him.** He does not become replaceable yet. That is Volume 05.

## Mystery progression

- **The Harrow Tongue's mark** is the same broken circle, found on a hut board at Sarrow Water, and the two impressions are entered in two lines and are not joined.
- **The line book has a column with no use**, headed for the origin the signal held, and for a number of entries the entry in that column is not a place. It is the name of an office. **The office is CROSS-DISTRICT PASSAGE and it is not a guild.**
- The same name appears in a draft in the water office with a door on it, as the authority a permanent crossing office proposes to succeed. **Two documents, two rooms, one phrase, entered in two lines, not joined, and the registrar has been asked to keep them apart and has agreed.**
- **Iven remains presumed dead from Adrian's point of view.** The volume must not deepen the father-shaped influence beyond what a person would find in his own handwriting, and must not name the Common Measure's origin, the First House, or the removed signature.
- The three unexplained things of Volume 01 — the bus that came out of a yard by itself, the pilot line's deviation, and the foot of the service cut — are **not joined to the line's stalls and are not joined to each other.** The Faults on the Needle are a documented pre-Fracture symptom and are therefore not a fourth unexplained thing.

## Next-volume question

The signal's memory pattern contains the name of a pre-Long-Fracture public office, **not a guild**, and the district's most valuable object is a lever that hands out destinations it does not own. **What is a public office that a signal can remember and a basin cannot find, and what was it for?**

## Final chapter image

A train stands at a platform that did not exist a year ago, at a place that is a station now because a village of thirty-one was asked and said yes and the book says so, and the toll board at the platform carries three tolls and a name that is not a place, and Adrian stands on the ballast at the far end of it not holding anything, and a signal keeper he has never met is the person who will end the use, and the term ends on the date.

## Continuity guardrails

- Do not make the Harrow Tongue a route key, a permanent claim, or a substitute for consent. Its toll is charged to an origin and an origin can be a place, and the whole volume is about that fact.
- Do not let the stalls be a new unexplained mystery. They are the basin's own documented Fracture symptom and the district has a name for them.
- Do not let the reading predict the future, name Sarrow Water, or identify a lie. **The object does not hold places and the reading must say so.**
- Do not let Tarin be absolved by accident. He signed the lease. He is the one who has to answer, and he is not the villain.
- Do not let Selik be defeated, converted, or made wrong. He staffs a hut he thinks is unsafe and says so in public on every published day.
- Do not let Halloway be humiliated, and do not let him be right about everything for long. He is right about speed and about the morning a road fails, and he is wrong about one specific thing, and Volume 02 finds out which.
- Do not resolve the romance, Iven's identity, the blank lot's function, the origin of the Common Measure, the office in the column, or the two unexplained things.
- Do not let a public market be built out of one reader. **The volume's proof is that the timetable, the toll boards, the lease, the count at Sarrow Water, and the boundary at the west end are all held by people who are not Adrian.**
