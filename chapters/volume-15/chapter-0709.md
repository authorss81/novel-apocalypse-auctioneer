# Chapter 709: On The Twenty-Third Of The Eighth Month A Stranger Came Down That Lane With A Page In His Own Right Hand

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the twenty-third of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and the light came sideways across that yard. About four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and thirty-seventh morning after the count. The second table was at the foot of that bank face up with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it, and that lot book was lying open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand. About nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings, and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it, and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

At about a quarter to eight, at the near end of that first table, a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and forty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. She entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-three this morning and that it moved this morning from twenty-two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The four off that wall were said in the ordinary voice by the man of fifty-six at about a quarter to eight, and all four of them were got without his looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. About four people were standing close enough to that wall at about a quarter to eight to hear it and about nine were standing close enough at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-fourth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and sixty-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and seven, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and twenty-three days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and thirty-seven, and five hundred and ninety-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

A stranger came down that lane at about half past nine this morning with a page in his own right hand, looked at that first table, which has nothing on it, and went on down to the foot of that bank, and read the five lines about nine feet off the man they are about, and said nothing to him, and went back up that lane.

The boy of about nineteen was at the top of that bank at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 56 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man went down that lane at about half past nine this morning with a page in his own right hand and he came to the top of that bank and he looked at that first table and it has nothing on it and he went on past that yard, and about four of you have worked out that he went on down to the foot of that bank and stood at the near end of that second table, and about four of you have worked out that I watched him do it and that I do not know what is on that page, and about four of you have worked out that he did not ask anybody anything and that the man who digs loam was nine feet off him and did not say one word, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen, and entered that it moved this morning because a boy of about nineteen said out loud in that yard at about ten what he had seen in that lane, and that she entered it after he had said it and not before, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven, and that is his own spade against that leg and his own two hands on that rail, and he said one thing out loud, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That man came down that lane this morning and he read those five lines and I was about nine feet off him and I did not say one word to him, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of those five lines tells a person to go down a lane and ask me and that he went down that lane, and about four of you have worked out that he did not ask me and that I did not tell him anything, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard sent him and that I am not going to say he should not have come, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody asked that man one thing about what he is to be used for, and that nobody has asked him that since he said no, and entered no name of the stranger because the stranger did not read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twelve and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That number is fifteen and it was fourteen at about a quarter to eight this morning, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard sent him and that a stranger is not a resident of this district and that I entered no name, and about four of you have worked out that a page that sends people to a place is only as good as the place and that the place this morning was about nine feet off a man with his own spade against the leg of a table, and about four of you have worked out that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a stranger is not a resident of this district and that no person was added this morning, and entered no name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own two hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. He saw the man with the page go up that lane and did not say one word about him, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the stranger read those five lines standing up about nine feet off him and that neither of them said one word.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a page that sends people to a place is only as good as the place, that the place this morning was a table at the foot of a bank, and that nobody has ever entered what that table is for, and that about four of them have said the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and that it moved because a boy of about nineteen said out loud what he had seen and not before.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and read the four figures off that wall as he went by and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it, and that the lane a stranger came down this morning is the lane he comes up twice a day.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man was asked no thing about what he is to be used for, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and forty-seven nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and forty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and forty-seventh of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight.

Three hundred and fifty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and twelve days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and seventeen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eighty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and ninety days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and twenty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and fifty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and two hundred and sixty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man with a page in his own right hand is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a number she entered because a boy gave it to her is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a person who comes down a lane is a person and not a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and ninety-eight. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list, and it was not added to this morning. Three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank, a shop counter is a place and is not on the list of four, and the door of that shop is propped open and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

One of the four conditions with no end on it is the fifth of those five lines, and the count of them is four and did not move, and that fifth line is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a person came down that lane. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a man who digs loam is not in it and the stranger is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who reads a page in a lane and says nothing is not a payment and a clerk entering a number she took from a boy is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody went up that bank after the buckets had been carried down it. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past the length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the twenty-third evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-fourth morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-third of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-three, and that a person came down that lane with a page in his own right hand and that no person was added to this district and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and moved this morning because a boy of about nineteen said out loud what he had seen, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
