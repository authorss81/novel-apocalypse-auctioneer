# Chapter 749: The Thirty Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Came Down That Bank With Nothing In His Own Two Hands

The man who puts tables up came down that bank at about a quarter to nine on the thirty-third morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and stood at the near end of that second table with nothing at all in his own two hands and nothing to do to it, and the boards of that first table at the top of the bank in the yard of Lot Seventeen were dry and bare with nothing on them at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard, and that was the two hundred and seventy-seventh morning after the count.

That second table was face up at the foot of that bank with the stone lying on it and its hollow one inch deep in the middle of it and nothing at either end, and two pages along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open on it with five lines on it, the fifth of them a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth nine words in that same hand, and about four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

She entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and eighty-eight days as the age of that figure, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eighteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixty-three this morning and that it moved this morning from sixty-two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got all four of them both times, and a clerk entered them once. About four people heard him say those four at about a quarter to eight this morning and about nine of them heard him say them again at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason has ever been entered for either of them and no date has been entered beside either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventy-fourth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and forty-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and forty-seven, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and sixty-three days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and seventy-seven, and six hundred and thirty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about a quarter to nine and said one thing out loud, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I was not at the foot of that bank yesterday and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to say where I was, and about four of you have worked out that I came down this bank this morning with nothing at all in my own two hands and that there is nothing left on that table to do, and about four of you have worked out that I am going to stand here anyway, and about four of you have worked out that a stranger read that fifth line on this table about nine feet off the man it names and that man is still not paid, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man came down that bank this morning after a morning he did not and that she entered no reason for that, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 50 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixty-three and I entered that about two hundred feet above the table it is a count of, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty-four and that I entered it on the word of a man who keeps a tally, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that it is not paid and that the count of the five is still five, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of the five is still five and that nothing was paid this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off that near end at about ten and again at about half past eleven with nothing at all in his own hands and a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat, and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning, and about four people near that wall have said since that two men have been at that table every morning this week and that about four of them have said that on one morning this week one of them was not, and that about four of them have said that neither of them has been asked one thing and that neither of them has been thanked for anything, and that is the whole of what they have got.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man came down that bank at about a quarter to nine yesterday and did not, and that about four of them have said that a clerk entered that and entered no reason, and that about four of them have said that the near rail has been on that table for twelve mornings and that nobody in that yard has thanked the man who put it there, and that about four of them have said that the man the fifth line names has been at the foot of that bank at about eleven on every morning of this month and has not been offered one thing, and that about four of them have said that the fifth of those five lines is face up on that book and is working exactly as it was written.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what he thinks of a man standing at a table with nothing to do to it, and that he has never once said.

A cart came up that bank at about three with its near wheel dragging on the stones, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank without standing at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went on up that lane about half an hour after the cart and did not stay at the top of that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man came down that bank this morning with nothing in his own two hands and stood there, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eighty-seven nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eighty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and eighty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and ninety-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and fifty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and fifty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and about four of them have said that a man standing at a table with nothing to do to it is not a rule. Four hundred and twenty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and thirty days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and sixty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Three hundred and six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and ninety-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man standing at a table with nothing to do to it is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a man saying he is going to stand there anyway is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Neither the four documents this district does not own nor the four places in this district where those three lines can be read moved this morning, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and it was not added to it. Up that bank a shop stands open with its door propped and three pages on its counter, it has not shut on a morning of this year, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The count of conditions with no end on it is four, it did not move this morning, the fifth of those five lines is one of the four, and it stands on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written with no hand on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said a thing out loud about his own two hands. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and the man who puts tables up is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of about thirty-four is not in it and the man who digs loam is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man standing at a table is not a payment and a man saying that man is still not paid is not a payment, and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventy-fourth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the thirty-third morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixty-three, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty-four and that she entered it on the word of a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally and entered no reason, and that a man who puts tables up said out loud in that yard at about ten that he was going to stand at that table anyway, and that she entered what he said and entered no name and entered no reason, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eighteen and that it did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked the man at the foot of that bank one thing about his own two hands, and the fifth of those five lines is on that book working exactly as it was written, and the man it names is still not paid, and that table is not coming up that bank.
