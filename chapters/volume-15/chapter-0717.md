# Chapter 717: The First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month Nobody In That Yard Has A Date For

Nobody in that yard had a date for the morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month. At the foot of that bank the second table was face up, and on it the stone lay face up with the hollow in the middle of that stone one inch deep and nothing at either end of it, and along the end of that table two pages with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open on it with five lines on it, the fifth of those five a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of those five nine words in that same hand. The boards of that first table at the top of that bank had a skin of water standing on them from the night before and the water was gone off them by about nine, and there was nothing on them at all. About four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and forty-fifth morning after the count. About nine people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for, and about four of them have said that they have looked at it about four hundred mornings.

At about a quarter to eight, at the near end of that first table, a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and fifty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-one this morning and that it moved this morning from thirty, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that she entered no date beside it this morning, and that there has not been a date to enter beside it since the morning before last.

The man of fifty-six said those four off that wall in the ordinary voice with his eyes on that yard and not on the wall, got all four of them, and said them again at about four and got them again, and a clerk entered them once. About nine people were standing within hearing of that wall at about four and about four of them were close enough at about a quarter to eight, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-second of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and eighty-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and fifteen, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and thirty-one days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and forty-five, and six hundred and six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about ten with his own two hands on that near leg and a length of wood lying along the top of that table clear of that book, and he had not cut it and he had not said what it was for. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about ten that a man was at the foot of that bank and entered no reason for it and entered that nobody in that yard had asked him one thing.

The woman of about fifty-eight was at the foot of that bank at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 62 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I read these three lines from the top of this bank for two years and I read them this morning from the top of it and I could not find the first of the three until I had stood a while, and about four of you have worked out that the book is at the bottom of this bank and not at the top of it and that it went down there on the thirty-first of the seventh month, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to read them from the top of this bank again and that I am not going to say that the book is in the wrong place, and about four of you have worked out that I have read them at the foot of that bank every evening this month and that nobody has asked me one thing about it, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman reading three lines of a book is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and that nobody has appointed her to it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered a number of mornings this morning and I entered no date, and about four of you have worked out that the length of that month has not been counted by anybody in this district and that I am not going to count a month I am standing in, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-one and that I entered it before any man in this district had been down that bank, and about four of you have worked out that there is no column in this district for the mornings that are not named, and that I did not rule one this morning, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a column of not-askings is not a column of mornings and did not rule a fifth, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank about a quarter to ten and stood about nine feet off the near end of that second table until about eleven with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The far end of that second table at about eleven was where a man of about thirty-one who digs loam stood, with his own spade against the leg of that table and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no. About four people at that low wall have said since that a woman said one thing out loud this morning that she cannot read three lines from the top of that bank any more, and that a man who puts tables up was at that end of that table at about ten and did not look up at her, and that neither of them was asked one thing by the other.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that there is no date on any page in this district for this morning, and that about four of them have said that a man who keeps a tally was asked by a boy in that yard after eight whether anybody knew what this morning was and that nobody knew, and that about four of them have said that the count of counted months is six and that the seventh and the eighth were counted by nobody.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there a while. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was not in that yard at about three this morning and had gone on up that lane before the cart reached the top of that bank, and about four people at that wall have said since that he has been at the foot of that bank at about ten on some mornings and in that yard at about three on other afternoons, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him which of the two is his work.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a woman could not read three lines of a book from the top of that bank this morning and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and fifty-five nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fifty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and fifty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and sixty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and twenty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and twenty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and ninety-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and ninety-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and thirty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and seventy-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and sixty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a woman who cannot read a book from the top of a bank is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a clerk entering that she ruled no column is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a person standing at the top of a bank reading a book that is not there is a person and not a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and six. A page in a hand this district does not know is not a document this district owns, and the count of those is four and did not move; the count of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move; a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. That shop up that bank is open, its door propped, with three pages on its counter, and it has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

That fifth line is one of the four conditions with no end on it, the count of them is four and did not move, and it stands on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a woman read three lines standing up at the foot of that table. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a woman of about fifty-eight is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a woman reading three lines of a book is not a payment and a clerk entering that she ruled no column this morning is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six and went straight back down it and read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she said one thing out loud at about ten about not being able to read them from the top of that bank and that at about ten to six she read them again from the bottom of it without looking for them, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-second morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen, on the first morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-one, and that a woman of about fifty-eight said a thing out loud in that yard and that she entered that she said it and entered no name and entered no date, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
