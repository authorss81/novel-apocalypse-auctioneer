# Chapter 747: The Thirty First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Keeps A Tally Said There Is No Line For What A Man Is

That first table at the top of that bank stood in that yard on the thirty-first morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month with its boards bare and wet at the ends and nothing on them at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and seventy-fifth morning after the count.

The second table at the foot of that bank was face up with the stone lying on it and its hollow one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and two pages along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open on it with five lines on it, the fifth of them a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth nine words in that same hand. The near rail on that table has been on it for ten mornings and the far rail has been on it since that table came down that bank, and about four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

The clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and eighty-six days as the age of that figure, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eighteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixty-one this morning and that it moved this morning from sixty, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The four off that wall were said out loud twice that morning in the ordinary voice by the man of fifty-six and he got all four of them both times and a clerk entered them once. He read it to about four people at about a quarter to eight and to about nine of them at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and nobody has entered a reason for either of them on any morning of this year. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventy-second of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and forty-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and forty-five, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and sixty-one days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and seventy-five, and six hundred and thirty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the near end of that first table at about ten with his own page open and said one thing out loud, in about 41 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got five things written down on that page about one man and none of the five of them is a reason, and about four of you have worked out that a rail went on that table in about ten minutes with his own two hands and that nobody asked him to and that nobody thanked him, and about four of you have worked out that I keep a tally of mornings and not of men, and about four of you have worked out that there is no line on that page for what a man is, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that no column was begun on that page for what a man is, and entered no name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 43 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eleven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The count of things this district has made is thirteen and it did not move on any of these mornings, and about four of you have worked out that a rail fitted by a man is not a fourteenth thing this district has made, and about four of you have worked out that a remedy is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty-three and that I entered that and entered no reason, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of the thirteen did not move on any of these mornings, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine with nothing at all in his own hands, and his own right hand was open and about nine inches of the skin was gone at the base of the thumb, and he did not shut it. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood about nine feet off that near end from about ten until about half past eleven with a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat and nothing in his own hands, and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning, and about four people near that wall have said since that the man who fitted that rail has not shut that hand once since the morning he cut the wood, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has asked him to shut it and that nobody has asked him how it got that way, and that is the whole of what they have got.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man has been at the foot of that bank on thirty-three mornings since that table came down it, and that about four of them have said that a man keeps a tally of the mornings a man has stood at that bank on his own page and that nobody in that yard asked him to keep that tally, and that about four of them have said that there is no line on that page for what any of them is for, and that about four of them have said that the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is ruled and empty at about six and that the man who keeps that tally is not in it, and that about four of them have said that a stranger read five lines at that table four mornings ago and is not in it either.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what he thinks of a page with five lines on it, and that he has never once said.

A cart came up that bank at about three going on one wheel better than the other, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and carried on up that bank without stopping at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went on up that lane about half an hour after the cart and did not stop at the top of that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who keeps a tally said out loud in that yard that there is no line on his page for what a man is, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eighty-five nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eighty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and eighty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and ninety-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and fifty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and fifty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and a page a man keeps on his own is not a rule. Four hundred and twenty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and twenty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and sixty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Three hundred and four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and ninety marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and five things written down about one man on his own page are not a fourteenth thing this district has made and are not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

That this district does not own four documents and that there are four places in this district where those three lines can be read are both of them the same as they were at about a quarter to eight, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. That shop up that bank is open, its door propped, with three pages on its counter, and it has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

One of the four conditions with no end on it is the fifth of those five lines, and the count of them is four and did not move, and that fifth line is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man keeps a tally. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and the man who keeps that tally is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the boy is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it, and it is empty and nothing has gone into it on any morning of this month.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a page a man keeps on his own is not a payment and a rail fitted by a man is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were brought back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six and went back down it, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she read her three lines in that lot book standing up and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventy-second morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the thirty-first morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixty-one, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty-three and that she entered it on the word of a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally and entered no reason, and that no column was begun for what any man is, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eighteen and that it did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked the man at the foot of that bank one thing about a page another man keeps on his own, and the fifth of those five lines is on that book working exactly as it was written, and the man it names is still not paid, and that table is not coming up that bank.
