# Chapter 744: The Twenty Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man With Wire Of His Own In A Bag

That first table at the top of that bank had a wind coming across it on the twenty-eighth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards of it were dry and swept and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and seventy-second morning after the count.

The second table was face up at the foot of that bank with the two rails on it and the stone lying on it with the hollow one inch deep in the middle of it and nothing at either end, and two pages along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open on it with five lines on it, the fifth in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth nine words in that same hand. About four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

The clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and eighty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seventeen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-eight this morning and that it moved this morning from fifty-seven, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He said those four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and a clerk entered them once, and he got all four both times. He read it to about four people at about a quarter to eight and to about nine of them at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and nobody has entered a reason for either of them on any morning of this year. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-ninth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and thirty-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and forty-two, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and fifty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and seventy-two, and six hundred and thirty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 45 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and eighteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a length of fencing wire in a man's own pocket at the foot of this bank every morning of this month and I have not taken one piece of it and about four of you have worked out that I have got wire of my own in a bag in this yard, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to say anything about his, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard has asked me one thing about that table and that I am not going to stand at that table and wait to be asked, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody asked him one thing about that second table and entered no name in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 50 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A page in a hand this district does not know is not a document this district owns and the count of those is four and did not move, and about four of you have worked out that three pages lie on a counter up this bank and that a shop counter is a place and is not the fifth place, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty and that I entered that on the word of a man who keeps a tally, and about four of you have worked out that a man came down that bank this morning after a morning he did not, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own did not move and that the count of the four places did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine with his own right hand wrapped in his own handkerchief and his own left hand along the top of that near rail, and he did not cut anything and there was nothing on that table to cut. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off that near end at about ten with a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat and a bag of wire of his own not on him, and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning, and about four people near that wall have said since that two men in that yard had wire about them this morning and that about four of them have said that neither of them has been asked one thing about the other's, and that about four of them have said that neither of them is in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and that is the whole of what they have got.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man came down that bank this morning after a morning he did not, and that about four of them have said that he had nothing at all in his own two hands and that the near rail has been on that table for eight mornings, and that about four of them have said that a stranger read those five lines seven mornings ago about nine feet off the man that line names and said not one word, and that about four of them have said that nobody has asked that man what the rail is for and nobody has asked the man it names what he is to be used for.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what he thinks of a man standing at a table with nothing in his own hands, and that he has never once said.

A cart came up that bank at about three going on one wheel better than the other, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and carried on up that bank without stopping at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went on up that lane about half an hour after the cart and did not look back at that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man with a bag of wire of his own said a thing out loud in that yard at about ten, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eighty-two nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eighty-third night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and eighty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and ninety-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and forty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and fifty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and about four of them have said that a man carrying wire of his own in a bag is not a rule. Four hundred and twenty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and twenty-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and sixty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Three hundred and one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eighty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man carrying a bag of wire is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a man with a length of wire in his own pocket is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Four documents this district does not own and four places in this district where those three lines can be read, and neither of those two figures moved this morning, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it. Up that bank a shop stands open with its door propped and three pages on its counter, it has not shut on a morning of this year, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The count of conditions with no end on it is four, it did not move this morning, the fifth of those five lines is one of the four, and it stands on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written with no hand on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man with a bag of wire said a thing out loud. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who cuts reeds is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who puts tables up is not in it and the boy is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a bag of wire is not a payment and a length of wire in a pocket is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody went up after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came down that bank at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-ninth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-eighth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-eight, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty and that she entered it on the word of a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally, and that a man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said out loud in that yard at about ten that he had wire of his own in a bag in that yard and that she entered what he said and entered no reason and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seventeen and that it did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked either of the two men with wire about the wire the other one is carrying, and the fifth of those five lines is on that book working exactly as it was written, and the man it names is still not paid, and that table is not coming up that bank.
