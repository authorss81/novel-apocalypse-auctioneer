# Chapter 706: On The Twentieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Puts Tables Up Said Out Loud That He Is Not Going To Be Thanked

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the twentieth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and that was the two hundred and thirty-fourth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. The second table was at the foot of that bank face up with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it and the copy is at the near end of those two, and that lot book was lying open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and forty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty this morning and that it moved this morning from nineteen, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number of mornings and did not go down that bank to look at the table it is a count of, and that she has never gone down that bank to look at it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about four people were standing within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine people were standing within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-first of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and sixty-two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and four, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and twenty days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and thirty-four, and five hundred and ninety-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about nine this morning with his own two hands on the near leg of it and he said one thing out loud, in about 51 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The soft side of that ground is the side a man puts his spade in and that is the side I put the near leg of that table on, and about four of you have worked out that I have not asked that man one thing and that he has not asked me one thing, and about four of you have worked out that I have been at the foot of that bank four mornings this week and that I am not going to be thanked for it and that I am not going to be asked about it, and about four of you have worked out that a man is not thanked in this district for a thing he does to a table, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that nobody thanked him this morning and that she entered that he was not thanked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 61 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and it did not move this morning, and about four of you have worked out that a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and that three pages lie on one up that bank with a door propped open, and about four of you have worked out that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and that I entered that number at the foot of that bank and that I did not enter it in that yard, and about four of you have worked out that a man who is not thanked for a thing he does to a table has not made a fourteenth thing this district has made, and that the count of things this district has made is thirteen, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and that she entered it at the foot of that bank and did not enter it in that yard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven, his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man who puts tables up said out loud at the bottom of that bank this morning that he is not going to be thanked for it, and that nobody thanked him.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man said one thing out loud in that yard about the soft side of the ground and about not being thanked for a thing he does to a table, and that about four of them have said that the two ends of that bank have had a man at each of them every morning this month and that neither of them has ever been asked why he is there. About four of them have said that a shop up that bank has been open every morning of this year and that three pages lie square on its counter and that a shop counter is a place and is not on the list of four, and that the woman who keeps that scale has not been asked one thing by anybody in that yard this month and is not going to be.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and nobody asked him one thing.

The cart came up at about three, the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, and he did not stay at the top of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man at the bottom of that bank said out loud this morning that he is not going to be thanked and that nobody thanked him, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and forty-four nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and forty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and forty-fourth of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and fifty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and fourteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eighty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and eighty-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and twenty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and sixty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and forty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying a thing out loud at the foot of a bank about the soft side of the ground is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a shop counter is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a leg of a table standing on a piece of ground is a leg of a table and not a thing this district has done.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and ninety-five. There are four documents this district does not own and the figure did not move, and there are four places in this district where those three lines can be read and that figure did not move either, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and the fifth of those five lines is one of the four and is still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said out loud that he is not going to be thanked. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who has said out loud that he is not going to be thanked is not a payment and a clerk entering the number of two pages is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the twentieth evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-first morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and thirty-one mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twentieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty, and that a man who puts tables up said a thing out loud in that yard about the soft side of that ground and about not being thanked and that she entered that he was not thanked, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
