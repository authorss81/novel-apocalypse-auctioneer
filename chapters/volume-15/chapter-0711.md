# Chapter 711: On The Twenty-Fifth Of The Eighth Month A Woman Said Out Loud That She Is Not Going To Look At The Fourth Line This Evening Or Any Other

The boards of that first table had a skin of water on them again this morning on the twenty-fifth of the eighth month and the boards were hard under it and there was nothing on them at all, and the fourth of those five lines in that lot book at the foot of that bank is still nine words in a clerk's hand. About four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and thirty-ninth morning after the count. The second table was at the foot of that bank face up with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it and the copy is at the near end of those two, and that lot book was lying open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched. About nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and fifty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. She entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-five this morning and that it moved this morning from twenty-four, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that the length of wood was still lying along the top of that second table and that she entered it and entered no reason for it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. About four people were standing within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine people were standing within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-sixth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and seventy-two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and nine, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and twenty-five days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and thirty-nine, and six hundred days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten this morning for the second time this month and stood at the top of it and looked down at that second table, and she said one thing out loud, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have gone down that bank twenty-five evenings and read three lines off that book standing up and I have not looked at the fourth one once, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to look at the fourth line this evening or any other, and about four of you have worked out that a woman who reads three lines of a book every evening is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and about four of you have worked out that the stranger who read all five of them last week read the fourth one longest and that he read it once, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the woman is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that nobody has entered her in it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning and neither of them is going to be altered by me, and about four of you have worked out that a man read all four of them at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got all four of them both times, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for, and about four of you have worked out that a figure that has been read four hundred and thirty-six mornings and been asked nothing is a figure in a better condition than a figure somebody has asked about, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that no figure on that wall was altered this morning and that the man of fifty-six was not asked about any of the four, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a woman of about fifty-eight said one thing out loud in that yard this morning that she is not going to look at the fourth line this evening or any other, and that about four of them have said that a woman has read three lines of that book standing up for twenty-five evenings and that the fourth of those five lines is still nine words in a clerk's hand and that nobody has corrected it and nobody is going to. About four of them have said that the man who puts tables up was not at the foot of that bank this morning and that his piece of wood was.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and read the four figures off that wall as he went by and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, and he did not stay at the top of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a woman said out loud at about ten that she is not going to look at the fourth line this evening or any other, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and forty-nine nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fiftieth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and forty-ninth of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and fifty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and fourteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and nineteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eighty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and ninety-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and twenty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and fifty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and two hundred and sixty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a woman saying out loud that she is not going to look at a line is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about two figures that are out is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a line a person has not read is a line and not a thing this district has done.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred. There are four documents this district does not own and four places in this district where those three lines can be read, and neither figure moved this morning, and a shop counter is a place that is not on that list and it was not added to this morning. Three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and one of those four is the fifth of those five lines, still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a woman said out loud that she is not going to look at the fourth line. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a woman of about fifty-eight is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a woman not looking at a line is not a payment and a clerk entering that no figure on a wall was altered is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past the length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and did not put her head on one side to get at the fourth, and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the twenty-fifth evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-sixth morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and thirty-six mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-fifth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-five, and that a woman of about fifty-eight said a thing out loud in that yard about the fourth of those five lines and that she entered that she said it and entered no name in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
