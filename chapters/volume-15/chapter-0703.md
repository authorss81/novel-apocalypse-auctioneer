# Chapter 703: On The Seventeenth Of The Eighth Month The Man Who Digs Loam Said One Thing Out Loud About A Line That Names Him

The wind came off that water in the middle of the morning of the seventeenth of the eighth month and lay the reeds over on the far bank and then went away again, and the boards of that first table had gone from hard under the water of yesterday to dry on the wind, and there was nothing on them at all. About four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and thirty-first morning after the count. That second table stands at the foot of that bank with the stone face up on it, a hollow in the middle of that stone one inch deep and nothing at either end of it, two pages along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of them, and that lot book open on it with five lines, the fifth of those five a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of those five still nine words in that hand. About nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings, that nobody has ever put a thumb in it, and that nobody has ever said out loud what it is for.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and forty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. She entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is seventeen this morning and that it moved this morning from sixteen, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years wrote a number of mornings down at about a quarter to eight and that she wrote it before any man in that yard had been down that bank.

The man of fifty-six said those four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. There were about four people within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine of them within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-eighth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and fifty-six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and one, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and seventeen days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and thirty-one, and five hundred and ninety-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that one man was at that table and entered no reason for it and asked him no thing.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said one thing out loud, in about 53 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been at the foot of that bank at about eleven on every morning since that table came down it, and I have got my own spade with me because the ground is soft at that end of it, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard has asked me one thing about what I dig loam for, and that nobody has asked me one thing about what I am to be used for since I said no, and about four of you have worked out that those are two questions and that I have been asked one of them, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who digs loam is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that she did not ask him one thing and is not going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 68 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their own hand is fourteen and it did not move this morning, and about four of you have worked out that a page in a hand this district does not know is not a document this district owns and that the number of documents this district does not own is four, and about four of you have worked out that a clerk who enters a number a boy gives her is not a reason for a hand to come down that lane, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of those five lines has been on that book since the thirty-first of the seventh month and that it has worked every morning of it and that nobody in that yard has ever had to decide whether it works, and about four of you have worked out that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a page in a hand this district does not know is not a document this district owns and entered no name, and that the count of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own two hands, about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. He has a length of fencing wire in his own pocket and a gap in the fencing at his own yard about the length of his own two hands, and he carried both of them to the foot of that bank this morning and mended nothing.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that the man who digs loam said one thing out loud in that yard this morning about his own spade and about two questions, and that about four of them have said that the fifth of those five lines tells a person to go down a lane and ask him and that he has never been asked one thing by anybody in that yard. About four of them have said that the man who puts tables up has not been at the foot of that bank at all this month and that he has been in the yard at the top of it at about three every afternoon, and that nobody in that yard has asked him why that hour and not another.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up at about three with its near wheel still dragging over that bank, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop there. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had been in that yard at about three with nothing at all in his own two hands and had gone on up that lane after the cart, and that is the hour that yard has seen him at since the second of January.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that one man was at that table this morning at about eleven and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and forty-one nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and forty-second night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and forty-first of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and fifty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and eleven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eighty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and eighty-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and twenty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and sixty marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and forty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying a thing out loud about his own spade is not a fourteenth thing and two questions nobody has asked him are not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a question nobody has asked is not a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and ninety-two. There are four documents this district does not own and four places in this district where those three lines can be read, and neither figure moved this morning, and a shop counter is a place that is not on that list and it was not added to this morning. Three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank this morning and that is the whole of what is on that counter, and the door of that shop is propped open, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

There are four conditions with no end on it and the count did not move this morning, and one of those four is the fifth of those five lines, which is on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said a thing out loud about his own spade. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who digs loam is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who puts tables up is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man with his own spade against a leg is not a payment and a clerk entering that she entered no name is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

There was nobody on that bank after the buckets but the two of them carrying them. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the seventeenth evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-eighth morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and twenty-eight mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the seventeenth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is seventeen, and that one man was at that table this morning and that she entered no reason for him and asked him no thing, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
