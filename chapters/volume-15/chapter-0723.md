# Chapter 723: The Seventh Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Woman Read Three Lines Standing Up At The Foot Of That Table And Did Not Stop

A woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went straight back down it, and read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table with her own back to the bank behind her, and she said nothing to anybody and did not stop. There was no rime on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the seventh morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and fifty-first morning after the count. The second table was at the foot of that bank face up with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book was lying open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand. About nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings without once putting a thumb in it, and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years set her own page on the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight and entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and sixty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-seven this morning and that it moved this morning from thirty-six, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that nothing had come down that bank by the time she wrote it, and that a man was at the foot of it at about ten.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without his looking at that wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. Nobody has ever entered how many people were within hearing of that wall, and what is entered is about four at about a quarter to eight and about nine at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-eighth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and ninety-six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and twenty-one, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and thirty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and fifty-one, and six hundred and twelve days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The boy of about nineteen was at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 53 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted that yard at about ten and I counted it again at about eleven and I got about nineteen both times, and about four of you have worked out that about four of them came up that bank before eight and went on up that lane and did not stop in this yard, and about four of you have worked out that a man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and that neither time was he at the foot of that bank, and about four of you have worked out that I have entered none of the people who go on past this yard because I do not know what I would enter them as, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the boy entered nobody he cannot name and that she did not ask him what he would enter them as, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 55 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered the fifth line of that book as one condition with no end on it this morning and the count of conditions with no end on it is four, and about four of you have worked out that that line has been on that book at the foot of that bank since the thirty-first of the seventh month working exactly as it was written and that nobody in this district has ever had to decide anything about it, and about four of you have worked out that a woman read three lines of that book this evening at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody, and about four of you have worked out that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman reading three lines standing up is not a payment and did not pay the fifth of the five, and that the count of the five is still five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off the near end of that second table at the foot of that bank at about ten, his own hands empty, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. At the far end of that second table at about eleven a man of about thirty-one who digs loam had his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a woman has read three lines of that book at the foot of that table every evening of this month without once looking at the fourth line, and that about four of them have said that the first of those three lines is the one she leans for and that she has never said out loud in that yard what it says, and that about four of them have said that a book that has to be read at the bottom of a bank is a book that has been put at the bottom of a bank.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and that nobody has ever stopped him.
The cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and went on up that lane past that yard. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was in that yard at about three with nothing at all in his own two hands, about nine feet off the near end of that first table, and he went on up that lane after the cart had gone by, and nobody has asked him why that hour and not another.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a boy said one thing out loud this morning about a man of fifty-six who has not come down that bank once this month, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and sixty-one nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and sixty-second night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and sixty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and seventy-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and twenty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and thirty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and forty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and eighty marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and sixty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a boy counting a yard twice is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a clerk entering a condition with no end on it is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a person a boy cannot name is a person and not a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and twelve. The number of documents this district does not own stands at four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read stands at four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. Three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

There are four conditions with no end on it and the count did not move this morning, and the fifth of those five lines is one of those four, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a boy counted that yard twice. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a woman of about fifty-eight is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a boy counting about nineteen people twice is not a payment and a clerk entering a condition is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she came up that bank and went straight back down it without stopping at the top, and that she read her three lines the way she reads them and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-eighth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the seventh morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-seven, and that a boy of about nineteen said a thing out loud in that yard and that she entered that he said it and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
