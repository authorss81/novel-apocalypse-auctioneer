# Chapter 719: The Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Cuts Reeds Counted The Marks In Chalk With His Own Finger An Inch Above Them

A man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the far end of that second table at about ten with his own finger an inch above the marks in chalk along the edge of it, and he was not touching them, and he had a bundle of his own under his own other arm. There was no rime on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the third morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and forty-seventh morning after the count. The second table was at the foot of that bank with that lot book open on it and the stone lying face up beside the book, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages lay along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book was open on it with five lines on it, of which the fifth is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched and the fourth is still nine words in that same hand. About four people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings, that nobody has ever put a thumb in it, and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

The first thing a clerk of nineteen years entered at about a quarter to eight, at the near end of that first table, was that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and fifty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-three this morning and that it moved this morning from thirty-two, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number before any man had been down that bank, and that two men were at that table within an hour of each other and neither of them went up that bank to say so.

At about a quarter to eight, and again at about four, the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four both times without looking at the wall, and a clerk entered them once. There were about four people within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine of them at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-fourth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and eighty-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and seventeen, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and thirty-three days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and forty-seven, and six hundred and eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about half past nine with his own two hands on that near leg, and a length of wood was lying along the top of that table behind him, and he had not cut it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about ten that a man was at the foot of that bank and entered no reason for it.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been cutting reeds at that ditch at the end of that lane for eleven years and I have never put a mark in anything but a length of wood, and about four of you have worked out that I counted those marks in chalk along the edge of that table with my own finger an inch above them and did not touch one of them, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has ever asked me what those marks are for and that I have not asked either, and about four of you have worked out that there is a man at that near end of that table about nine feet off me with a piece of wood behind him that he has not cut, and that neither of us said one word to the other, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who cuts reeds is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and that nobody asked him one thing about those marks, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered two figures about marks this morning and neither of them moved, and about four of you have worked out that one of those figures is cut into a board at the top of that bank and that the other is in chalk along the edge of a table at the foot of it, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this district has ever said out loud which of the two is the older, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that a piece of wood lying along the top of that table is not a fourteenth, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a length of wood lying on a table is not a payment and did not pay the fifth of the five, and that the count of the five is still five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about ten there was a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing at the foot of that bank about nine feet off the near end of that second table with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade propped against that leg and his own two hands resting on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no. About four people at that low wall have said since that three men were at that table at about ten on the same morning and that two of them said a thing out loud in that yard afterwards and that the third one did not.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who cuts reeds put his own finger an inch above the marks in chalk along the edge of that table and did not touch them, and that about four of them have said that a length of wood has been lying along the top of that table for more than a week now and that it has not been cut on any morning.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up at his usual hour and that about four of them have said that he has never once been asked what he does at the top of that lane and that nobody in that yard has ever thought to wonder it out loud.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stay at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was not in that yard at about three this morning and had gone on up that lane about an hour before the cart came, and nobody in that yard asked him why that hour and not another.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who cuts reeds said one thing out loud this morning about marks in chalk and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and fifty-seven nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fifty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and fifty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and sixty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and twenty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and twenty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and ninety-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and thirty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and seventy-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and sixty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man counting marks with his own finger an inch above them is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a piece of wood is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man who will not touch a mark has not made a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and eight. There are four documents this district does not own and four places in this district where those three lines can be read, and both of those figures did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. There is a door propped open on that shop up that bank and three pages lie on that counter, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

One of the four conditions with no end on it is the fifth of those five lines, and the count of them is four and did not move, and that fifth line is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man counted marks in chalk with his own finger. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who cuts reeds is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man who puts tables up is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man counting marks an inch above them is not a payment and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a piece of wood is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past that length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man who cuts reeds went away at about eleven with his own bundle under his own arm and that the woman read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-fourth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the third morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-three, and that a man who cuts reeds said a thing out loud in that yard and that she entered that he said it and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
