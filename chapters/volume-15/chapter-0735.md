# Chapter 735: The Nineteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Cut A Length Of Wood At About Half Past Eleven And Said Nothing Out Loud

That first table stood at the top of that bank in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the nineteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month with its boards bare and dry and nothing on them at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard, and that was the two hundred and sixty-third morning after the count.

That lot book was open on that second table at the foot of that bank with five lines on it, the fifth of them a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of them nine words in that same hand, and the stone lay face up beside it with the hollow one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and two pages lay along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two. That length of wood was along the top of that table and had not been cut.

She entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and seventy-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is sixteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-nine this morning and that it moved this morning from forty-eight, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and again at about four, got all four of them both times, and a clerk entered them once, and about four people were within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine were within hearing of it at about four. Neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and nobody in that yard has ever entered a reason for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixtieth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and twenty times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and thirty-three, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and forty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and sixty-three, and six hundred and twenty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The woman of about fifty-eight was at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 43 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twelve and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I saw that man go up this bank at about a quarter to nine this morning with nothing at all in his own two hands and I have watched that table from the top of this bank and from the bottom of it for two years, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to say what is going to happen to it, and about four of you have worked out that a thing lying on a table a fortnight is a thing somebody is thinking about and that I have no business saying it, and that is the whole of what I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman of about fifty-eight is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 49 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered no date this month and I entered no reason this morning, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-nine and that I entered it about two hundred feet above the table it is a count of, and about four of you have worked out that a man went up this bank at about a quarter to nine with nothing in his own two hands and that I entered that and entered no hour, and about four of you have worked out that the count of counted months is six and that nobody counted that month and that is not a debt, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that no column was begun and no date was entered this month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen was at the foot of that bank at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a man at that table with a saw in his own hands this morning and I have not heard him use it yet, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to stand here until he does, and about four of you have worked out that about nine people have looked at that piece of wood every morning for a fortnight and that nobody has touched it but one man, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody in that yard said one word to a man with a saw in his own hands, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past eleven the sound of that saw came up the bank from the foot of it for the first time since that table came down that bank, and it went on for about four minutes, and about four people in the yard of Lot Seventeen turned round at it and about four more did not, and nobody in that yard said one word while it was going. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had his own right hand open that morning and about nine inches of the skin gone at the base of the thumb, and he held the piece with his own left hand and worked with his own right, and the cut came off in one length and both its ends were square, and he put it down on the ground at the foot of that table with his own two hands and stood looking at it and said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail on the far side, and the sawing went on about nine feet off him, and he did not move his own spade and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off the near end of that table and about four feet off the cut piece, and he looked at the ground the whole time it was being cut and he did not say one word in that yard on that morning.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a length of wood came off that table this morning with a saw and that nobody in that yard had asked for it, and that about four of them have said that it is lying on the ground at the foot of that table with both its ends square, and that about four of them have said that two men were at that table this morning at the same time, and that the fifth of those five lines was on that book the whole of it and that nobody turned round to look at it.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at about half past one and that the road keeper was not asked about the wood that was cut this morning.

The cart came up that bank at about three dragging its near wheel, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stay at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up swept the ground at the foot of that table with his own hand and his own hand was not working properly, and he went on up that lane at about a quarter to four.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a sound of about four minutes came up this bank at about half past eleven this morning and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and seventy-three nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and seventy-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and seventy-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and eighty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and thirty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and forty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and thirteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and sixteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and fifty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and ninety-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and seventy-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man cutting a length of wood with a saw is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a boy standing at the foot of that bank is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and twenty-four. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. Three pages lie on that counter up that bank with the door of that shop propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

That fifth line is one of the four conditions with no end on it, the count of them is four and did not move, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man cut a piece of wood. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a woman of about fifty-eight is not in it and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man with the saw is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man cutting a length of wood at the foot of that bank is not a payment and a clerk entering that she entered no reason is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a piece of wood with both its ends square was lying at the foot of that table when she came down that bank, and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixtieth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.
