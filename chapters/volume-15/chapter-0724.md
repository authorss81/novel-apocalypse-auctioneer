# Chapter 724: The Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Clerk Entered A Number Of Mornings And Entered No Reason For The Man At The Foot Of That Bank

The first thing a clerk of nineteen years entered at about a quarter to eight, at the near end of that first table, was that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. About four people at that low wall have said since that she entered that number before any man in this district had been down that bank, and that a man came down that bank at about half past nine with his own two hands on a leg of a table about two hours after she wrote it. There was no rime on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the eighth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and fifty-second morning after the count. The second table was at the foot of that bank with that lot book open on it and the stone lying face up beside the book, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages lay along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book was open on it with five lines on it, of which the fifth is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched and the fourth is still nine words in that same hand. About four people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings, that nobody has ever put a thumb in it, and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

She entered four hundred and sixty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-eight this morning and that it moved this morning from thirty-seven, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about a quarter to eight, and again at about four, the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four both times without looking at the wall, and a clerk entered them once. There were about four people within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine of them at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-ninth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and ninety-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and twenty-two, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and thirty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and fifty-two, and six hundred and thirteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about half past nine with his own two hands on that near leg, and a length of wood was lying along the top of that table behind him, and he did not cut it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about ten that a man was at the foot of that bank and entered no reason for it and entered that she did not go down to see what he was doing.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard at about ten with his flat book under his own left arm and he said one thing out loud, in about 53 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been keeping a tally of mornings for longer than that table has been at the foot of that bank, and about four of you have worked out that I have not opened that book of my own this morning, and about four of you have worked out that a clerk of nineteen years entered a number of mornings at the top of that bank at about a quarter to eight and that a man was at the foot of that bank two hours later, and about four of you have worked out that there is no column in this district for a man at the foot of a bank and that there are four columns of not-askings and that I did not ask her to rule a fifth, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not ask her to rule a fifth column, and that the count of columns of not-askings is four and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 55 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered that number at about a quarter to eight and I entered that a man was at the foot of that bank at about half past nine and I entered no reason for either, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-eight and that I entered it at the top of that bank about two hundred feet above the table it is a count of, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that it did not move, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid this morning, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man at the foot of that bank is not a payment and did not pay the fifth of the five, and that the count of the five is still five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about ten there was a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing at the foot of that bank about nine feet off the near end of that second table with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade propped against that leg and his own two hands resting on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no. About four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a clerk entered a number of mornings at the top of that bank at about a quarter to eight and that a man was at the foot of it an hour and a half later, and that neither of those two facts has anything to do with the other one and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud that they do.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it, and that he has never once been asked to stop at the top of that bank.

The cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up and set the shafts down at the top of that bank and stayed there a while. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went on up that lane about half an hour after the cart and stood in that yard until about four with nothing at all in his own two hands.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man was at the foot of that bank this morning for the fourth morning in five, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and sixty-two nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and sixty-third night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and sixty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and seventy-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and twenty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and thirty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and forty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and eighty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and sixty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man with a flat book under his own arm is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a clerk entering a number of mornings is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a count of mornings is a figure and a man is not.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and thirteen. There are four documents this district does not own and four places in this district where those three lines can be read, and both of those figures did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. There is a door propped open on that shop up that bank and three pages lie on that counter, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

One of the four conditions with no end on it is the fifth of those five lines, and the count of them is four and did not move, and that fifth line is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said a thing out loud about a book he did not open. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man with a flat book under his own arm is not a payment and a clerk entering that a man was at the foot of that bank is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past that length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man was at the near end of that table until the light had gone off it and that the woman came down that bank after he had gone, and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-ninth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.
