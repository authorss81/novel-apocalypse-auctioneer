# Chapter 714: On The Twenty-Eighth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Puts Tables Up Said Out Loud That The Leg Is Not Square Yet

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the twenty-eighth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and that near leg of that second table stood in the soft ground the way it has stood since the day that table went down that bank. About four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and forty-second morning after the count. The stone lay face up on that second table at the foot of that bank, the hollow in the middle of it one inch deep and nothing at either end of it, two pages lay along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book lay open on it with five lines on it, the fifth of those five in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and not touched, and the fourth of those five still nine words in that same hand. They have said at that low wall, about nine of them, that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

The first thing a clerk of nineteen years entered at about a quarter to eight, at the near end of that first table, was that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and fifty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. She entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-eight this morning and that it moved this morning from twenty-seven, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that a man was at the near end of that second table at about nine before she had finished that entry.

The four off that wall were said in the ordinary voice by the man of fifty-six at about a quarter to eight, and all four of them were got without his looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. About four people were standing close enough to that wall at about a quarter to eight to hear what he said and about nine were standing close enough at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-ninth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and seventy-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and twelve, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and twenty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and forty-two, and six hundred and three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about nine this morning with his own two hands on the near leg of it, and he said one thing out loud, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have had my own two hands on that near leg on the three mornings of this week when I have been at that end of that table and I have not cut that piece of wood on any of them, and about four of you have worked out that I am going to cut it when I have got that leg square and that the leg is not square, and about four of you have worked out that a man who puts tables up does not say a thing out loud in that yard until he has done it, and about four of you have worked out that a man is not thanked in this district for a thing he does to a table and that I said that out loud eight mornings ago and I am not going to say it again, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that nobody thanked him this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 51 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The bid has been open three hundred and sixty-two days and it was not run this morning and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page, and about four of you have worked out that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and that it did not move, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that it did not move, and about four of you have worked out that a man with his own two hands on a leg is not a fourteenth thing and that a length of wood lying on a table is not a fifteenth, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the bid was not run this morning and that nothing was proposed about closing it, and that the count of things this district has made is thirteen, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

There was a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing at the foot of that bank at about ten, about nine feet off the near end of that second table and with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam stood at the far end of that second table at about eleven, his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who puts tables up said one thing out loud in that yard this morning about a leg that is not square yet, and that about four of them have said that a man who mends fencing has been about nine feet off the near end of that table every morning this month with a length of fencing wire in his own pocket and a gap in the fencing at his own yard about the length of his own two hands. About four of them have said that the two of them have been at the same end of that table at the same hour once this month and that neither of them has said one word to the other one on that morning, and that a man who mends fencing is not a man who mends tables and that a man who puts tables up is not a man who has been thanked.

About four people at that wall have said since that the road keeper came up that lane at his usual hour at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles.

At about three there was a cart at the foot of that bank and the near wheel of it was still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said out loud this morning that a leg is not square and that he is not going to cut anything until it is, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and fifty-two nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fifty-third night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and fifty-second of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and sixty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and seventeen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and twenty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and ninety-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and ninety-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and thirty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and fifty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and two hundred and seventy-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man with his own two hands on a leg is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a bid that was not run is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a hand on a leg is a hand and not a thing this district has done.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and three. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and so is the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. Three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank, a shop counter is a place and is not on the list of four, and the door of that shop is propped open and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and one of those four is the fifth of those five lines, still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said out loud that a leg is not square. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man with his own two hands on a leg is not a payment and a clerk entering that the bid was not run is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

No one in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past the length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the twenty-eighth evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-ninth morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and thirty-nine mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-eighth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-eight, and that a man who puts tables up said a thing out loud in that yard about a leg that is not square and that she entered no reason for that, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
