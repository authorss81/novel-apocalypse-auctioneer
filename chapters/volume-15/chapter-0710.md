# Chapter 710: On The Twenty-Fourth Of The Eighth Month Two Men Stood At One End Of That Table About Nine Feet Apart And Said Nothing

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the twenty-fourth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and that was the two hundred and thirty-eighth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. The second table was at the foot of that bank face up with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it and the copy is at the near end of those two, and that lot book was lying open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and forty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-four this morning and that it moved this morning from twenty-three, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered the number of people down that lane as fifteen at about a quarter to eight and that it is the same fifteen she entered yesterday afternoon, and that no man has come down that lane in the night.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about four people were standing within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine people were standing within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-fifth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and seventy times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and eight, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and twenty-four days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and thirty-eight, and five hundred and ninety-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about nine this morning with his own two hands on the near leg of it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about nine the same morning with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that table, and the two of them were at the same end of the same table at the same hour for the first time since that table went down that bank, and neither of them said one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two men were at the foot of that bank at about nine and entered no reason for either of them.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said one thing out loud, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have not cut that piece of wood and I have not laid it against that leg and I am not going to say out loud in that yard what it is for, and about four of you have worked out that I come down this bank on the mornings when I have got something to do at that end of it and that nobody has asked me which mornings those are, and about four of you have worked out that a man who puts tables up is not going to say a thing out loud twice about one piece of wood, and about four of you have worked out that a man who mends fencing was about nine feet off me this morning and neither of us said one word, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that nobody asked him one thing about that piece of wood, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 60 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-four and that is a count of mornings and it is not a count of men, and about four of you have worked out that two men were at that table this morning and that I entered two men and not a figure, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that it is not paid this morning, and about four of you have worked out that two men at one end of a table about nine feet apart is not a payment and did not pay it, and about four of you have worked out that the count of the five is still five, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and is not paid, and that two men at a table is not a payment and did not pay it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven, and that is his own spade against that leg and his own two hands on that rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that two men were at the same end of that table at about nine feet apart at about nine this morning and neither of them said one word, and that about four of them have said that a man who mends fencing has a length of fencing wire in his own pocket and a gap in the fencing at his own yard about the length of his own two hands and that he has been carrying that wire since the day before that table went down that bank. About four of them have said that a man who mends fencing was asked by nobody in that yard to look at that table and that he looked at it and said nothing and that a man who puts tables up was asked by nobody what the wood was for and did not say. About four of them have said that about two hundred feet of bank is between the two of them and that neither of them has crossed it this month and that a clerk entered two men and not a figure.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and read the four figures off that wall as he went by and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and went on up that bank and did not stay at the top of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that two men stood about nine feet apart at the bottom of that bank this morning and neither of them was given one word, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and forty-eight nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and forty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and forty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and fifty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and thirteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and eighteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eighty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and ninety-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and twenty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and sixty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and fifty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and two men at one end of a table is not a fourteenth thing and a man saying a thing out loud about a piece of wood he has not cut is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that two men standing near each other is two men and not a thing this district has made.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and ninety-nine. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list, and nobody in that yard added it this morning, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

That fifth line is one condition with no end on it, the count of those is four, neither moved this morning, the line is on that book at the foot of that bank, and no hand went on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because two men stood at the same end of a table. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who mends fencing is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying a thing out loud about a piece of wood he has not cut is not a payment and a clerk entering that a count of mornings is not a count of men is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the twenty-fourth evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-fifth morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and thirty-five mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fourth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-four, and that two men were at that table at about nine feet apart at about nine this morning and that she entered two men and entered no figure and entered no reason and asked neither of them one thing, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
