# Chapter 722: The Sixth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Bystander Named The Reason A Man Who Mends Fencing Said Nothing

The ground at the foot of that bank had gone hard again in the two days since the rain and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the near end of that second table from about ten until about eleven with a length of fencing wire in his own pocket and nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say one word out loud in that yard this morning. There was no rime on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the sixth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and fiftieth morning after the count. At the foot of that bank the second table was face up, and on it the stone lay face up with the hollow in the middle of that stone one inch deep and nothing at either end of it, and along the end of that table two pages with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open on it with five lines on it, the fifth of those five a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of those five nine words in that same hand. About nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it.

At about a quarter to eight, at the near end of that first table, a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and sixty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-six this morning and that it moved this morning from thirty-five, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that she entered that number before any man had been down that bank and that a man was at the foot of that bank at about ten and that she entered that she did not ask him one thing.

The man of fifty-six said those four off that wall in the ordinary voice with his eyes on that yard and not on the wall, got all four of them, and said them again at about four and got them again, and a clerk entered them once. About nine people were standing within hearing of that wall at about four and about four of them were close enough at about a quarter to eight, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-seventh of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and ninety-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and twenty, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and thirty-six days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and fifty, and six hundred and eleven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard at about ten with a flat book under his own left arm and he said one thing out loud, in about 55 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That man has a length of wire in his own pocket and a gap in the fencing at his own yard about the length of his own two hands, and about four of you have worked out that he has carried that wire since the morning before that table went down this bank, and about four of you have worked out that he has not taken it out of his own pocket on any morning this month, and about four of you have worked out that he has not said one word in that yard on any morning of that block and that he is not a man who mends tables, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard has ever asked him the difference between the two, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the man it is about said nothing out loud this morning and that she did not ask him one thing about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 55 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man said nothing out loud in that yard this morning and I entered that he said nothing and I entered no reason, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-six and that I entered it at about a quarter to eight at the top of that bank, and about four of you have worked out that the number of columns of not-askings is four and that I entered nothing in any of them this morning, and about four of you have worked out that a man with a length of wire in his own pocket is not a payment and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of columns of not-askings is four and that she ruled no fifth, and that the count of the five things this district does not have is still five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank about a quarter to ten and stood about nine feet off the near end of that second table until about eleven with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The far end of that second table at about eleven was where a man of about thirty-one who digs loam stood, with his own spade against the leg of that table and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man has been standing at the foot of that bank for about an hour with a length of wire in his own pocket and nothing in his own hands, and that about four of them have said that a man who mends fencing is not a man who mends tables and that a district did not ask him to be either, and that about four of them have said that the man of fifty-six was at the top of that bank at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and that the two of them have not been in the same place at the same hour once this month.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles. He has been coming up that lane twice a day for as long as anybody in that yard has been counting.
The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it, and stayed there until about four without turning round. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was not in that yard at about three this afternoon and had gone on up that lane before the cart reached the top of that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who mends fencing stood about nine feet off that table for an hour this morning and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and sixty nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and sixty-first night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and sixty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and seventy days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and twenty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and thirty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and thirty-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and seventy-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and sixty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man with a length of wire in his own pocket is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a man naming the reason another man said nothing is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man who has said nothing has not made a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and eleven. A page in a hand this district does not know is not a document this district owns, and the count of those is four and did not move; the count of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move; a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. That shop up that bank is open, its door propped, with three pages on its counter, and it has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

That fifth line is one of the four conditions with no end on it, the count of them is four and did not move, and it stands on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said a thing out loud about another man's pocket. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a man who mends fencing is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man with a length of wire in his own pocket is not a payment and a clerk entering that she ruled no column is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man who mends fencing went up that bank at about half past four and that he has a gap in his own fence the length of his own two hands and that he has had it since before that table came down, and that the woman read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-seventh morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the sixth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-six, and that a man who keeps a tally said a thing out loud in that yard about a length of wire in another man's own pocket and that she entered that he said it and entered no name in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
