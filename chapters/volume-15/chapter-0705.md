# Chapter 705: On The Nineteenth Of The Eighth Month A Woman Came Up That Bank And Said One Thing Out Loud About The Place A Page Is Read From

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the nineteenth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and the sky over the top of that bank had been the colour of a wet slate since before the light came and nothing came out of it, and that was the two hundred and thirty-third morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. That second table stands at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it and a hollow one inch deep in the middle of it and nothing at either end of it, and two pages are along the end of that table and the copy is the near one of those two, and that lot book is open on it with five lines, and the fifth of those five is a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that hand, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and forty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is nineteen this morning and that it moved this morning from eighteen, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that the man of fifty-six was reading the four off that wall at the same hour.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about four people were standing within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine people were standing within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirtieth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and sixty times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and three, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and nineteen days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and thirty-three, and five hundred and ninety-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the foot of that bank at about half past eight this morning and was gone from it by about nine, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was at the foot of that bank at about half past eight and entered no reason for it.

The woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten this morning and stood at the top of it and looked down at that second table for about the length of a page and then went back down it, and she said one thing out loud, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have gone down that bank eighteen evenings to read three lines off that book standing up and I have not looked at the fourth one, and this morning I came up that bank and stood at the top of it and I could not see the first line at all, and about four of you have worked out that I read that book from the top of that bank for two years and that I can no longer read it from there, and about four of you have worked out that it is the same five lines and that the lines have not moved, and about four of you have worked out that is not a failing and that the place a page is read from is part of the page, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the woman is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that nobody has appointed her to it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A page that sends people to a place is only as good as the place, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of those five lines has been on that book at the foot of that bank since the thirty-first of the seventh month and that it has worked every morning since and that nobody in that yard has ever had to decide whether it works, and about four of you have worked out that a woman who can no longer read a book from the top of that bank has not become a document this district does not own, and that the number of documents this district does not own is four, and about four of you have worked out that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a person is not a document and entered the count of documents this district does not own, and that the fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and has not been touched, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own two hands, about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a woman came up that bank at about ten and looked down at that table and went back down it, and that the man who digs loam watched her go down and did not say one word.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a woman of about fifty-eight said one thing out loud in that yard this morning about not being able to read that book from the top of that bank, and that about four of them have said that the place a page is read from is part of the page and that about four of them have said that a stranger who comes down that lane reads that book standing up at the bottom of that bank and can see all five of the lines, and that it is only the people who read it every evening who have lost the top of it. About four of them have said that a man who puts tables up was at the foot of that bank at about half past eight and was gone by about nine and that nobody asked him what for.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and nobody asked him one thing.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, and he did not stay at the top of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a woman came up that bank at about ten this morning and could not read a book she has read for two years, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and forty-three nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and forty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and forty-third of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and fifty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and thirteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eighty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and eighty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and twenty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and sixty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and forty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a woman standing at the top of a bank looking down at a table is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about the place a page is read from is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a person standing at a bank is a person and not a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and ninety-four. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a woman could not read that book from the top of that bank. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a woman of about fifty-eight is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a woman who can no longer read a book from where she used to read it is not a payment and a clerk entering that a person is not a document is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six and went back down it without stopping, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a woman who came up that bank at about ten this morning and could not read that book went down that bank for the nineteenth evening and read three lines of it standing up, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirtieth morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and thirty mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the nineteenth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is nineteen, and that a woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten and said a thing out loud in that yard and that she entered that she said it and entered no name in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
