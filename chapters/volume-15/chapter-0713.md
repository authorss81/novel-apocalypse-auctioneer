# Chapter 713: On The Twenty-Seventh Of The Eighth Month A Boy Of About Nineteen Said Out Loud That He Enters None Of The People Who Go On Past That Yard

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the twenty-seventh of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people went up that bank before eight and went on to the top of that lane without stopping, as they had gone on every morning of that month. About four other people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and forty-first morning after the count. The second table was at the foot of that bank face up with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it and the copy is at the near end of those two, and that lot book was lying open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand. About nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings, that nobody has ever put a thumb in it, and that nobody has ever said out loud what it is for.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and fifty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. She entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-seven this morning and that it moved this morning from twenty-six, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that a man has been at the foot of that bank on three of the last four mornings without once being asked what he is doing there.

The man of fifty-six said those four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. There were about four people within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine of them within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-eighth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and seventy-six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and eleven, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and twenty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and forty-one, and six hundred and two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about ten this morning with the length of wood still lying along the top of it and his own two hands on the near leg, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was at the foot of that bank at about ten and entered no reason for it.

The boy of about nineteen stood at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About four people come up that bank before eight every morning and go on to the top of that lane and do not stop in that yard at all, and about four of you have worked out that a man who puts tables up has come down that bank more mornings this month than he has gone up it, and that twice he had a length of wood in his own two hands, and about four of you have worked out that I have entered none of the people who go on past this yard because I do not know what I would enter them as, and about four of you have worked out that a boy who counts about nineteen people twice is not a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the boy entered none of the people who go on past that yard and that she did not ask him what he would enter them as, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 55 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have entered about nineteen people in that yard at about ten this morning and I have entered that figure on my own page and not on that wall, and about four of you have worked out that about four of those nineteen read those three lines at the foot of that bank and the rest do not, and about four of you have worked out that the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and that a shop counter is a place and is not on that list, and about four of you have worked out that the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is ruled and empty at about six and that I entered nobody in it this morning, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a count of people in a yard is a figure on her own page and is not a figure on that wall, and that the number of places where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the foot of that bank at about ten, about nine feet off the near end of that second table, with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. At the far end of that second table at about eleven there was a man of about thirty-one who digs loam with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a boy said one thing out loud in that yard this morning about the people who come up that bank before eight and go on past, and that about four of them have said that nobody has ever entered any of them and that a boy does not know what he would enter them as. About four of them have said that a clerk entered about nineteen people on her own page and not on that wall, and that a figure on a wall is read out loud twice a morning by one man and a figure on a page is read by whoever holds the page.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up at about three with its near wheel still dragging over that bank, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop there.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a boy said out loud this morning that he enters none of the people who go on past that yard, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and fifty-one nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fifty-second night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and fifty-first of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and sixty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and sixteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and twenty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and ninety-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and ninety-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and thirty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and fifty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and two hundred and seventy marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a boy saying out loud that he enters nobody he cannot name is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk entering a count of people on her own page and not on a wall is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a person a boy cannot name is a person and not a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and two. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list, and it was not added to this morning. Three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank this morning and that is the whole of what is on that counter, and the door of that shop is propped open, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

One of the four conditions with no end on it is the fifth of those five lines, and the count of them is four and did not move, and that fifth line is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a boy said out loud that he enters nobody. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who puts tables up is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a boy who counts about nineteen people twice is not a payment and a clerk entering a figure on her own page is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

There was nobody on that bank after the buckets but the two of them carrying them. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past the length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the twenty-seventh evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-eighth morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and thirty-eight mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-seventh of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-seven, and that a boy of about nineteen said a thing out loud in that yard about the people who go on past that yard and that she entered that he said it and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
