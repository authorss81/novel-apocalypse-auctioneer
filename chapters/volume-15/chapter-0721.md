# Chapter 721: The Fifth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Came Down That Bank With A Length Of Wood He Had Not Cut

A man came down that bank at about half past nine with a length of wood in his own two hands and put it down along the top of that second table clear of that lot book and clear of the stone, and he stood at the near end of that table and looked at the near leg of it and did not cut anything. There was no rime on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the fifth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and forty-ninth morning after the count. That lot book was lying open on that second table at the foot of that bank with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and the stone was lying face up on the same table with the hollow in the middle of it one inch deep and nothing at either end of it, and two pages were along the end of it with the copy at the near end of those two. About nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and sixty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-five this morning and that it moved this morning from thirty-four, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number about two hours before a man came down that bank with something in his own two hands, and that she was at the top of that bank when he did it and did not go down to see what it was.

The four off that wall were said in the ordinary voice by the man of fifty-six and all four of them were got without his looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. About four people were standing close enough to that wall at about a quarter to eight to hear it and about nine were standing close enough at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-sixth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and ninety-two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and nineteen, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and thirty-five days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and forty-nine, and six hundred and ten days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about half past nine with a length of wood in his own two hands, and he put it down and looked at the near leg of that table and did not cut it and did not say what it was for. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about ten that a man was at the foot of that bank and entered no reason for it and entered that nobody had asked him one thing about that piece of wood.

The woman of about fifty-eight said one thing out loud at about ten, in about 53 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That piece of wood has been lying along the top of that table for more than a week and it has not been cut on any morning since it came down that bank, and about four of you have worked out that a man carried it down in his own two hands and that nobody in this yard has asked him what it is for, and about four of you have worked out that I read these three lines at the foot of that table every evening and that the wood is laid at the far end of it from where I stand, and about four of you have worked out that I have not moved the book a foot in either direction and that nobody has asked me to, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the book has not been moved and that nobody asked her to move it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 55 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered that a man was at the foot of that bank at about half past nine and I entered no reason and no name, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that a piece of wood lying on a table is not a fourteenth thing this district has made, and about four of you have worked out that a remedy is not a fifteenth because a remedy is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that it is not paid, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a length of wood in a man's own two hands is not a payment and did not pay the fifth of the five, and that the count of the five is still five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten, about nine feet off the near end of that second table, with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. At about eleven the far end of that second table had a man of about thirty-one who digs loam at it, his own spade against the leg and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no. About four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man carried a length of wood down that bank this morning and that a woman said one thing out loud about it at about ten and that neither of them was at that other end of that table when the other one was there.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and he was not asked about the eleven miles, and nobody in that yard stopped him.
The cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up and did not stay at the top of that bank. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went on up that lane about half an hour behind the cart and stood about nine feet off the near end of that first table until about four.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man came down that bank this morning with something in his own two hands and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and fifty-nine nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and sixtieth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and fifty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and sixty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and twenty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and twenty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and ninety-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and thirty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and seventy-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and sixty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man carrying a length of wood down a bank is not a fourteenth thing and a woman saying a thing out loud about that wood is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a piece of wood a man has carried somewhere is a piece of wood and not a thing this district has done.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and ten. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. Three pages lie square on a shop counter up that bank and the door of that shop is propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and one of those four is the fifth of those five lines, and that line is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man carried a length of wood down that bank. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a woman of about fifty-eight is not in it and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a length of wood in a man's own two hands is not a payment and a clerk entering that she entered no reason is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past that length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man had been at the near end of that table all morning and that the woman came down that bank at about ten to six after he had gone, and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-sixth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the fifth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-five, and that a man was at the foot of that bank with a length of wood in his own two hands at about half past nine and that she entered that he was there and entered no reason and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
