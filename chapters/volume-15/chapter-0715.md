# Chapter 715: On The Twenty-Ninth Of The Eighth Month A Clerk Entered That A Man Has Been At The Foot Of That Bank With A Length Of Wood

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the twenty-ninth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and that was the two hundred and forty-third morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. That second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it and the hollow in that stone one inch deep with nothing at either end of it, and two pages were along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of them, and that lot book was open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that hand, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and fifty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-nine this morning and that it moved this morning from twenty-eight, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that a man came down that bank about half an hour later with something in his own two hands, and that the clerk was at the top of that bank when he did it and did not go down to see what it was.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about four people were standing within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine people were standing within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fortieth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and eighty times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and thirteen, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and twenty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and forty-three, and six hundred and four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about half past nine this morning with a length of wood in his own two hands, and he said one thing out loud, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have come down this bank with a length of wood in my own two hands on two mornings this month and nobody in this yard has asked me what for on either of the two of them, and about four of you have worked out that I have had my own two hands on that near leg for nine mornings and that I have not cut that wood on any of them, and about four of you have worked out that there is a man at the top of that bank who reads four figures out loud twice a morning and that he has not come down this bank once in the whole of this month, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to say what that wood is for until I have cut it, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that nobody asked him one thing about that piece of wood, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 59 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That man has been at the foot of that bank with a length of wood in his own two hands on two mornings this month and I entered that he was there and I entered no reason and I am not going to, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that it is not paid and that the count of the five is still five, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and it did not move this morning, and about four of you have worked out that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a length of wood in a man's own hands is not a payment and did not pay the fifth of the five, and that the count of the five is still five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten, about nine feet off the near end of that second table, with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. At the far end of that second table at about eleven a man of about thirty-one who digs loam had his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at the top of that bank at about a quarter to eight this morning while a man with a length of wood in his own two hands was about two hundred feet below him and that neither of them came the other way.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud in that yard this morning that a man has been at the foot of that bank with a length of wood in his own hands and that she entered no reason for it, and that about four of them have said that a piece of wood is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and that a remedy is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move this morning.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it, and that he came up that lane a second time in the afternoon and read them again as he went by, and that it was not asked about it.

The cart came up that lane at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, and he did not stay at the top of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and fifty-three nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fifty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and fifty-third of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and sixty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and eighteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and twenty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and ninety-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and ninety-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and thirty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and seventy-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and fifty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man with a length of wood in his own two hands is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk entering that she entered no reason is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a length of wood is a length of wood and not a thing this district has done, and that a remedy is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and four. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that line has not been touched this morning and has not been touched at all, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man carried a length of wood down that bank. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it and the man who mends fencing is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man with a length of wood in his own two hands is not a payment and a clerk entering that she entered no reason for it is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth. The fifth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say is a remedy and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard. Those are two different fifths and they are on this page one above the other and nobody in that yard has ever put them in the same clause out loud.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the twenty-ninth evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fortieth morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and forty mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-ninth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-nine, and that a man has been at the foot of that bank with a length of wood in his own hands on two mornings this month and that she entered that he was there and entered no reason for either of them and entered no name, and that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move this morning, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
