# Chapter 746: The Thirtieth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man With A Spade Said He Was Not Going To Let Go Of That Rail

There was no cloud on that bank on the thirtieth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen stood at the top of it with its boards hard and bare and nothing on them at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard, and that was the two hundred and seventy-fourth morning after the count.

That second table was face up at the foot of that bank in the flat light with both its rails on it and the stone lying on it with its hollow one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and two pages along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open on it with five lines on it, the fifth in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth nine words in that same hand. Nothing has been cut on that table since the length of wood came off it on the nineteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, and about four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and eighty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eighteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixty this morning and that it moved this morning from fifty-nine, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got all four of them both times, and a clerk entered them once. There were about four people within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine of them within hearing at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and a clerk has entered no reason for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventy-first of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and forty-two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and forty-four, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and sixty days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and seventy-four, and six hundred and thirty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the near rail, and he said one thing out loud, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man put a rail on the near side of that table on the twenty-first morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and about four of you have worked out that I have not asked him one thing about it since and that I am not going to, and about four of you have worked out that a man stood at that table yesterday and read that fifth line about nine feet off me and went back up that lane, and about four of you have worked out that I have my own two hands on that rail this morning and that I am not going to let go of it, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody asked him one thing about the rail or about what he is to be used for, and entered no name in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That fifth line is face up on that book this morning and it is one of the four conditions with no end on it and the count of the four did not move, and about four of you have worked out that it is working exactly as it was written and that no hand went on it, and about four of you have worked out that the count of the five things this district does not have is still five and that the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and about four of you have worked out that it is not paid and that a man with his own two hands on a rail is not a payment, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of the four did not move and that the count of the five did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine and had nothing at all in his own hands, and he had been standing at that near leg for an hour and a half with nothing in it. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off that near end at about ten and again at about a quarter to eleven with a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat and nothing else, and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning, and about four people near that wall have said since that two men were at that table at about eleven with nothing at all in their own hands, and that about four of them have said that neither of them has been asked one thing and that neither of them has offered one thing, and that is the whole of what they have got.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a rail went on to the near side of that table ten mornings ago in about ten minutes, and that about four of them have said that the man who put it there has not been thanked on any morning since and that about four of them have said that he has said out loud that he is not going to wait to be thanked, and that about four of them have said that a man has been at the foot of that bank on thirty-two mornings since that table came down it, and that about four of them have said that a man who digs loam has his own two hands on that rail on every morning of this month that he has been at that table and has not been asked one thing.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what he thinks of two men standing at a table doing nothing, and that he has never once said.

There was a cart at the top of that bank at about three with its near wheel dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that lane and did not stay at the top of that bank. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was still at that near leg when the cart came and went on up that lane about half an hour after it, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about the cart.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who digs loam said a thing out loud at that table about his own two hands, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eighty-four nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eighty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and eighty-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and ninety-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and forty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and fifty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and about four of them have said that a man saying he is not going to let go of a rail is not a rule and that nobody entered it as such. Four hundred and twenty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and twenty-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and sixty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Three hundred and three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eighty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man with his own two hands on a rail is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a man standing with nothing in his own hands is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Neither the four documents this district does not own nor the four places in this district where those three lines can be read moved this morning, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and it was not added to it. Up that bank a shop stands open with its door propped and three pages on its counter, it has not shut on a morning of this year, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

There are four conditions with no end on it and the count did not move this morning, and the fifth of those five lines is one of them and it is on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning and none has for as long as it has been on that book. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said a thing out loud about his own two hands. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who digs loam is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man who puts tables up is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man putting his own two hands on a rail is not a payment and a clerk naming it is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth, and a way to pay a person who is not in a household is named on every one of these mornings and is paid on none of them.

There was nobody at the top of that bank after the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five, and they were carried back up it full past that second table, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up it at about ten to six and went back down it and read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventy-first morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the thirtieth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixty, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty-two and that she entered it on the word of a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam said out loud in that yard at about eleven that he was not going to let go of that rail and that she entered what he said and entered no reason and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eighteen and that it did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked the man at the foot of that bank one thing about his own two hands, and the fifth of those five lines is on that book working exactly as it was written, and the man it names is still not paid, and that table is not coming up that bank.
