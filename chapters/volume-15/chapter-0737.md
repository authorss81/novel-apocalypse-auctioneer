# Chapter 737: The Twenty-First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Put A Rail On The Near Side Of That Table And Nobody Thanked Him

The boards of that first table at the top of that bank had nothing on them at all on the twenty-first morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and they were dry and hard, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and sixty-fifth morning after the count.

At the foot of that bank the second table was face up, and on it the stone lay face up with the hollow in the middle of that stone one inch deep and nothing at either end of it, and along the end of that table two pages with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open on it with five lines on it, of which the fifth is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched and the fourth is still nine words in that same hand. There was a rail along the far side of that table where it had been since the morning that table came down this bank, and there was a rail along the near side of it as well, put there at about half past ten that morning with a man's own two hands, and nobody had done anything to it since.

The first thing she entered at about a quarter to eight, standing at the near end of that first table with her own page on the boards, was that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and seventy-six days as the age of that figure, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is sixteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-one this morning and that it moved this morning from fifty, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard has ever heard the man of fifty-six stop in the middle of it: he said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice, got all four of them without looking at the wall, and said them again at about four and got them again, and a clerk entered them once. That wall was read out loud to about four people at about a quarter to eight and to about nine at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason has ever been entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-second of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and twenty-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and thirty-five, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and fifty-one days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and sixty-five, and six hundred and twenty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail that had gone on to the near side of that table at about half past ten, and he said one thing out loud, in about 51 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man put a rail on the near side of that table this morning about half an hour before I got here and he did it with his own two hands and nobody in that yard asked him to, and about four of you have worked out that I have put my own two hands on it and that nobody has asked me one thing about it, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of those five lines was on that book the whole of the time he was doing it and that nobody turned round, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has asked me what that rail is for and that nobody is going to, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for and that nobody has asked him that since he said no, and entered no name of that man in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 40 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The count of things this district has made is thirteen and it did not move this morning when that rail went on that table, and about four of you have worked out that a rail fitted by a man is not a fourteenth thing this district has made, and about four of you have worked out that a remedy is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and about four of you have worked out that nobody thanked him this morning and that I entered that he was not thanked, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of the thirteen did not move, and that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine and had his own right hand along that rail by about half past ten, and he did it himself, in about ten minutes, without asking anybody for anything, and after that he went back to the near leg with his own left hand on it while his own right hand stayed flat along the top of that rail. He said nothing out loud in that yard about it on that morning. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off that near end at about ten and again at about half past ten, and he watched the whole of it, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for and he did not say one word in that yard on that morning.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a rail went on to the near side of that table at about half past ten this morning with a man's own two hands in about ten minutes, and that about four of them have said that nobody asked him to and that nobody thanked him and that about four of them have said that a rail fitted by a man is a rail and not a thing this district has made, and that about four of them have said that the fifth of those five lines did not change in the ten minutes it took and that a man reading it at about eleven did not know that anything had happened at the other end of that table, and that about four of them have said that the count of the thirteen did not move and that it does not move when a rail goes on or when a cart comes up that bank or when a man comes down that lane with a page.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at about half past one and that about four of them have said that a rail went on to that table three hours before and that the road keeper was not asked about it and that he did not stop, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him about a rail or about a table or about a man at the bottom of a bank.

A cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stay at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was not in that yard at about three and had gone on up that lane about half an hour before the cart reached the top of that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a rail went on to a table at the foot of that bank this morning and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and seventy-five nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and seventy-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and seventy-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and eighty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and forty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and forty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and about four of them have said that it is the one thing anybody in that yard ever agreed on and that nobody in that yard can remember who put it into words. Four hundred and fifteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and eighteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and fifty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and ninety-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eighty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and the rail that went on that table at about half past ten this morning is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a man with his own two hands on it at about eleven is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and twenty-six. A page in a hand this district does not know is not a document this district owns, and the count of those is four and did not move; the count of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move; a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. That shop up that bank is open, its door propped, with three pages on its counter, and it has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

That fifth line is one of the four conditions with no end on it, the count of them is four and did not move, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning, and the count of the four did not move on the morning a rail went on to the table that book is lying open on. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man put a rail on a table. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who digs loam is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who fitted that rail is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a rail going on to a table is not a payment and a clerk saying that out loud is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she came down that bank at about ten to six and that a man was at the near leg of that table and that neither of them said one word, and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-second morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-first morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-one, and that a man put a rail on the near side of that table at about half past ten this morning in his own time and that nobody in that yard asked him to and nobody thanked him and that she entered that and entered no reason and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is sixteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
