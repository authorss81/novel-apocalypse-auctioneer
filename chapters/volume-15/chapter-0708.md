# Chapter 708: On The Twenty-Second Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Cuts Reed Came Down That Bank And Read The Marks In Chalk Along The Edge Of That Table

The mist came off that water in the first hour of the twenty-second of the eighth month and did not lift until the light had got up behind that bank, and there was no rime on the boards of that first table and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all. About four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and thirty-sixth morning after the count. The length of wood was lying along the top of that second table at the foot of that bank where a man had put it down yesterday morning, and the stone lay face up on it with the hollow in the middle one inch deep and nothing at either end of it, and two pages lay along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book lay open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand. They have said at that low wall, about nine of them, that about four hundred mornings they have looked at that hollow and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and forty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. She entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-two this morning and that it moved this morning from twenty-one, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that the length of wood was still on that table when she wrote it down, and that she entered the length of wood and entered no reason for it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and got all four of them without looking at that wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. About four people were standing within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine people were standing within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-third of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and sixty-six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and six, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and twenty-two days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and thirty-six, and five hundred and ninety-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came down that bank at about ten this morning and stood at the near end of that second table and read the marks in chalk along the edge of it, one after the other, with his own finger about an inch above them and not touching them, and he said one thing out loud, in about 49 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have cut reeds on that water since I was a boy and nobody in this yard has asked me one thing about it this month, and about four of you have worked out that I have stood at that table this morning and read those marks in chalk along the edge of it and that I have not put a mark in myself, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has asked me what those marks are for and that nobody is going to, and about four of you have worked out that the man who puts tables up is not at the foot of that bank this morning and that his wood is, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who cuts reeds is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that nobody asked him one thing about the marks and that she is not going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 61 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The figure on the second line of that book is three hundred and eleven days out of date and nobody has written a correct figure beside it, and about four of you have worked out that the third line is a date and that the date is a figure of a man, and about four of you have worked out that the fourth line says that he is not named here and that he is not named here this morning either, and about four of you have worked out that a figure in a book a stranger may walk up to and read is a figure of a person and that this district has spent five volumes saying so, and about four of you have worked out that I entered no hand on that book today and that the count of conditions with no end on it is four, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the figure on the second line of that lot book has not been altered and that nothing correct has been written beside it, and that the third line is a date and no hand went on that book this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the foot of that bank at about ten, about nine feet off the near end of that second table, with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. At the far end of that second table at about eleven there was a man of about thirty-one who digs loam with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who cuts reeds stood at that table this morning and read the marks in chalk along the edge of it and did not put a mark in himself, and that about four of them have said that a piece of wood has been lying on that table since yesterday morning and that the man who brought it was not there this morning, and that about four of them have said that a man who cuts reeds is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that a man who puts tables up is not in it either and that the column is ruled and empty.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up at about three, its near wheel still dragging over that bank, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop at the top of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man read the marks in chalk along the edge of that table with his own finger above them and did not touch them, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and forty-six nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and forty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and forty-sixth of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and fifty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and eleven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and sixteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eighty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and eighty-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and twenty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and fifty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and two hundred and sixty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man reading marks in chalk with his own finger an inch above them is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about the second line of a book is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man reading a mark is a man reading a mark and not a thing this district has done.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and ninety-seven. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list, and it was not added to this morning. Three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank this morning and that is the whole of what is on that counter, and the door of that shop is propped open, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and one of those four is the fifth of those five lines, still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man read a row of marks. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who cuts reeds is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man reading marks in chalk is not a payment and a clerk entering that she entered no hand on a book is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody was on that bank after the buckets but the two of them who carry them. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past the length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the twenty-second evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-third morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and thirty-three mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-second of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-two, and that a man who cuts reeds read the marks in chalk along the edge of that table and put no mark in himself and that she entered no reason for that, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
