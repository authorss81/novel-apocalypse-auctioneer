# Chapter 712: On The Twenty-Sixth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Keeps A Tally Said Out Loud That He Is Not Going To Enter It A Third Time

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the twenty-sixth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and that was the two hundred and fortieth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. A second table is at the foot of that bank and the stone on it is face up, and the hollow in that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and there are two pages along the end of that table and the copy is at the near end of those two, and that lot book is open on it with five lines, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is nine words in the same hand, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and fifty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-six this morning and that it moved this morning from twenty-five, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that a man who puts tables up came down that bank at about nine with something in his own two hands, and that the clerk was at the top of that bank when he did it and did not go down.

The four on that wall were said out loud in the ordinary voice by a man of fifty-six and he got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about four people were standing within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine people were standing within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-seventh of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and seventy-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and ten, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and twenty-six days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and forty, and six hundred and one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came down that bank at about nine this morning with a length of wood in his own two hands for the second time this month and stood at the near end of that second table with it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered at about ten that a man was at the foot of that bank at about nine and entered no reason for it.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with a flat book under his own left arm and said one thing out loud, in about 56 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have put a number of mornings in a book of my own twice this month and I am not going to put it in a third time, and about four of you have worked out that the number is twenty-six this morning and that a clerk entered it at about a quarter to eight at the top of that bank, and about four of you have worked out that my book is not a document this district owns and that I do not want it to be one, and about four of you have worked out that a man keeping a count in his own pocket and a clerk keeping a count on her own page are two numbers of the same thing and that there is no column for either of them, and that is the whole of what I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a book under a man's own arm is not a document this district owns and entered the count of documents this district does not own beside it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The count of counted months is six and it did not move this morning, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has counted the eighth month and that I am not going to and that I am not going to this month or any other, and about four of you have worked out that a month a person is in cannot be counted off a board, and about four of you have worked out that I have entered a number of mornings every morning this month and that the number is a count and not a date, and about four of you have worked out that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of counted months is six and that no month has been counted this month and that no date in this district has been altered, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten, about nine feet off the near end of that second table and with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who keeps a tally said one thing out loud in that yard this morning about a number in a book of his own, and that about four of them have said that a man has come down that bank with a length of wood in his own two hands twice this month and that neither time was he asked what for. About four of them have said that a clerk said out loud this morning that she is not going to count the month she is standing in, and that nobody in that yard has asked her to and that nobody in that yard is going to.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and went on up that bank and did not stay at the top of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man came down that bank this morning with a length of wood in his own two hands and was not given one word about it, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and fifty nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fifty-first night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and fifty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and sixty days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and fifteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and twenty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and ninety days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and ninety-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and twenty-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and sixty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and fifty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying a thing out loud about a number in a book of his own is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a month she is not going to count is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a number in a man's own pocket is a number in a man's own pocket and not a thing this district has done.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and one. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a book under a man's own arm is not a document this district owns, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

That fifth line is one condition with no end on it, the count of those is four, neither moved this morning, the line is on that book at the foot of that bank, and no hand went on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man put a number in a book of his own. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a number in a man's own pocket is not a payment and a clerk saying out loud that she is not going to count this month is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the twenty-sixth evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-seventh morning, and that a clerk said out loud in that yard this morning that she is not going to count the month she is standing in, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and thirty-seven mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is, and that a man at the bottom of that bank has not been asked one thing this month either.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-sixth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-six, and that no month has been counted this month and that no date in this district has been altered and that the count of counted months is six, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
