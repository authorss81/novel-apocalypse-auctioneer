# Chapter 730: The Fourteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Stranger Stood About Nine Feet Off That Man And Read The Fifth Line And Said Nothing

A stranger came down that lane at about half past nine on the fourteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month with a page in his own right hand, went on past that yard, and went on down to the foot of that bank, and stood about nine feet off a man of about thirty-one who digs loam and read the five lines on that book and said nothing to him and went back up that lane. The rail is not on that table yet, and the length of wood that is to go on the near side of it has not been cut. There was no rime on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank that morning and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and fifty-eighth morning after the count. That second table stood at the foot of that bank and on it were the stone, face up, with its hollow one inch deep in the middle of it and nothing at either end of it, and two pages along the end with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book lying open with five lines on it, the fifth of those five in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of those five still nine words in that same hand. About four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

She entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and sixty-nine days as the age of that figure. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-four this morning and that it moved this morning from forty-three, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard has ever heard the man of fifty-six stop in the middle of it: he said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice, got all four of them without looking at the wall, and said them again at about four and got them again, and a clerk entered them once. That wall was read out loud to about four people at about a quarter to eight and to about nine at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-fifth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and ten times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and twenty-eight, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and forty-four days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and fifty-eight, and six hundred and nineteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The boy of about nineteen was at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 63 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and sixty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man went down that lane at about half past nine this morning with a page in his own right hand and he came to the top of that bank and he looked at that first table and it has nothing on it and he went on down to the foot of that bank, and about four of you have worked out that he stood about nine feet off a man who digs loam and read those five lines and did not ask him one thing, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard sent him and that I watched him do it and that I do not know what is on that page, and about four of you have worked out that the man who puts tables up was at that near end of that table with his own two hands on it about nine feet off the other one, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is sixteen, and entered that it moved this morning because a boy of about nineteen said out loud in that yard at about ten what he had seen in that lane, and that she entered it after he had said it and not before, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and he said one thing out loud, in about 59 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That man came down that lane this morning and read those five lines and I was about nine feet off him and I did not say one word to him, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of those five lines tells a person to go down a lane and ask me and that he went down that lane and did not ask me, and about four of you have worked out that the place he came to is the bottom of a bank and that a table is at the bottom of that bank and that the table is the only thing in this district that fifth line sends anybody to, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard sent him and that I am not going to say he should not have come, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody asked that man one thing about what he is to be used for, and that nobody has asked him that since he said no, and entered no name of the stranger because the stranger did not read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 51 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That number is sixteen and it was fifteen at about a quarter to eight this morning, and about four of you have worked out that I entered it after a boy said it out loud and not before, and about four of you have worked out that a stranger is not a resident of this district and that I entered no name, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that it did not move this morning, and about four of you have worked out that a man is not thanked in this district for a thing he does to a table and that he was not thanked this morning, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of the thirteen did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine with his own two hands on that near leg, and he did not look up at the man with the page and he did not say one word to him. There was a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing at that table at about ten, nine feet off its near end, with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that the lane a stranger came down this morning is the lane he comes up twice a day, and that about four of them have said that he came up that lane afterwards and that nobody in that yard told him anything about the man who had just gone back down it.

The cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank without stopping at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went on up that lane about half an hour after the cart had gone by, and nobody in that yard thanked him and nobody has thanked him on any morning of this block.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man stood about nine feet off him this morning reading a page and that neither of them said one word, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and sixty-eight nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and sixty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and sixty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and seventy-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and thirty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and thirty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and eleven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and forty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and eighty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and seventy-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man coming down a lane with a page in his own hand is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a man fitting a rail to a table is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and nineteen. Four documents this district does not own, four places in this district where those three lines can be read, and neither figure moved, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. Three pages are lying on that shop counter with the door of that shop propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The count of conditions with no end on it is four, it did not move this morning, the fifth of those five lines is one of the four, and it stands on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written with no hand on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man came down a lane. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a man who digs loam is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the stranger is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a stranger reading five lines about nine feet off a man is not a payment and a clerk entering a number she took from a boy is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past that length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man stood about nine feet off that table at about eleven this morning reading five lines and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-fifth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the fourteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-four, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is sixteen and that it moved this morning because a boy of about nineteen said out loud what he had seen in that lane, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
