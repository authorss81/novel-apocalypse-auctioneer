# Chapter 725: Ninth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Asked Out Loud In That Yard Whether That Table At The Foot Of That Bank Is Coming Back Up

There was no rime on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the ninth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and fifty-third morning after the count. That second table stood at the foot of that bank and on it were the stone, face up, with its hollow one inch deep in the middle of it and nothing at either end of it, and two pages along the end with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book lying open with five lines on it, the fifth of those five in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of those five still nine words in that same hand. About four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

She entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and sixty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-nine this morning and that it moved this morning from thirty-eight, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that she did not say one word out loud in that yard at any hour of that day.

Nobody in that yard has ever heard the man of fifty-six stop in the middle of it: he said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice, got all four of them without looking at the wall, and said them again at about four and got them again, and a clerk entered them once. That wall was read out loud to about four people at about a quarter to eight and to about nine at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fiftieth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and twenty-three, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and thirty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and fifty-three, and six hundred and fourteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about ten with his own two hands on that near leg and a length of wood lying along the top of that table behind him, and he had not cut it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about ten that a man was at the foot of that bank and entered no reason for it.

There was a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing at that table at about ten, nine feet off its near end, with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. There was a man of about thirty-one who digs loam at the far end of that table at about eleven, his own two hands on the rail and his own spade against the leg of it, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no. Neither of those two men was in that yard at about eleven, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that two men were at that table at about eleven on the same morning and that not one of them was two hundred feet above it.

At about eleven that yard held about nineteen people in the open in daylight. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of it with his flat book under his own left arm, and he asked out loud, in about 5 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got thirteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Is that table at the foot of that bank coming back up it?**"

Nobody in that yard said a word. The wind came off that water flat and lifted the corner of that book on the table at the foot of that bank and let it go again, and the man of fifty-six went on reading the four off that wall at the top of that bank without looking up, and the quiet went by with the sound of about nineteen people not saying anything in it. The boy of about nineteen counted that quiet on his own two fingers, going past ten and starting again on the other hand, and got twenty-one, and said one thing out loud, in about 11 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got twenty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Twenty-one. That is the whole of what I got off that quiet, and I counted it on my own two fingers and I got it the same twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked a thing out loud in that yard at about eleven and entered the thing, and entered that no person in that yard said a word back to him, and entered no number, and entered that the boy said a number out loud in that yard and that she entered that figure nowhere and entered it nowhere on her own page either, and entered that a man who keeps a tally is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that the boy is not in it either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard at about eleven this morning and asked out loud whether that table at the foot of that bank is coming back up it, and that nobody in that yard said a word, and that a boy of about nineteen counted the quiet on his own two fingers and said the number out loud in the ordinary voice, and that the number is not on that wall and is not on any page in this district and was entered nowhere this morning. About four of them have said that a man of fifty-six was reading his four off that wall at the top of that bank through the whole of it and did not look up once.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man asked a thing out loud in that yard at about eleven and that the road keeper came up that lane afterwards and was not asked about it and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him about a question either.

The cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up and did not stay at the top of that bank. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was in that yard at about three with nothing at all in his own two hands and went on up that lane after the cart had gone by, and that is the hour that yard has seen him at since the second of January.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man asked a thing out loud in that yard at about eleven and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and sixty-three nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and sixty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and sixty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and seventy-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and twenty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and thirty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and forty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and eighty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and sixty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man asking a question out loud in a yard is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a boy counting a quiet on his own two fingers is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and the reason was entered out loud with that count on an earlier morning of this month, which is that a question is a person and not a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and fourteen. Four documents this district does not own, four places in this district where those three lines can be read, and neither figure moved, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. Three pages are lying on that shop counter with the door of that shop propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The count of conditions with no end on it is four, it did not move this morning, the fifth of those five lines is one of the four, and it stands on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written with no hand on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man asked a question in that yard and nobody answered it. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a boy who counted a quiet is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a question asked out loud in a yard is not a payment and a boy counting the quiet after it is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past that length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she came down that bank at about ten to six and that she was not in that yard at about eleven and that nobody told her about a question and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fiftieth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the ninth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-nine, and that a man asked a thing out loud in that yard at about eleven and that no person in that yard said a word back to him and that she entered the thing he asked and entered no answer and entered no number, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
