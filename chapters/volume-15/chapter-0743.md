# Chapter 743: The Twenty Seventh Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Woman Did Not Read The Fourth Of Those Five Lines

The light came over that bank early and hard on the twenty-seventh morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards of that first table at the top of it were pale and cold and had nothing on them at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and seventy-first morning after the count.

That second table stood at the foot of that bank with both its rails dry and the stone lying face up on it with its hollow one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and two pages along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open on it with five lines on it, the fifth of them in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of them nine words in that same hand. There was no man at that table at about half past nine and there was no man at it at about ten either, and about four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

She entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and eighty-two days as the age of that figure, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seventeen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-seven this morning and that it moved this morning from fifty-six, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got all four both times, and a clerk entered them once. That wall was read out loud to about four people at about a quarter to eight and to about nine at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason has ever been entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-eighth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and thirty-six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and forty-one, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and fifty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and seventy-one, and six hundred and thirty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The woman of about fifty-eight was at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 42 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read three lines of that book standing up for two years and this morning my own hand was on the fourth of them and I did not read it, and about four of you have worked out that those nine words have not changed since that table came down this bank and that I am not going to say what they are, and about four of you have worked out that there was no man at that table this morning when I looked, and that I am not going to say where that man was, and that is the whole of what I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman of about fifty-eight did not read the fourth of those five lines and that nobody asked her to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-nine and it did not move this morning, and about four of you have worked out that a man who puts tables up was not at that table this morning at all and that I entered that and entered no hour and entered no reason, and about four of you have worked out that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and that the fifth of those five lines is one of the four and that the count did not move, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard proposed a fifth this morning, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of conditions with no end on it did not move and that no fifth was proposed, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about ten and again at about half past eleven with nothing at all in his own hands and a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat, and he did not take it out and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came down that bank at about eleven with his own spade and stood at the far end of that table with his own two hands on the near rail, and nobody in that yard asked him one thing about the fact that there was nobody else at that table.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that the man who puts tables up was not at the foot of that bank for the first morning in a fortnight, and that about four of them have said that a clerk entered that he was not there and entered no hour and entered no reason, and that about four of them have said that the near rail has been on that table for seven mornings and that a man came into that yard about three yesterday with nothing in his own two hands, and that about four of them have said that a man who mends fencing was at that table with a length of wire in his own pocket and that he is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and never has been.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane and that about four of them have said that he did not stop at that table on his way, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him why not.

There was a cart at the top of that bank at about three with its near wheel dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that lane and did not stay at the top of that bank. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was in that yard at about a quarter to three with nothing at all in his own two hands and nobody in that yard said one word to him about the morning he had not come down that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a woman did not read the fourth of those five lines this morning and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eighty-one nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eighty-second night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and eighty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and ninety-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and forty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and fifty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and it did not change on the morning a man did not come down that bank. Four hundred and twenty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and twenty-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and sixty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Three hundred marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eighty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man not coming down a bank is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a woman not reading the fourth of five lines is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

That this district does not own four documents and that there are four places in this district where those three lines can be read are both of them the same as they were at about a quarter to eight, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. That shop up that bank is open, its door propped, with three pages on its counter, and it has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

One of the four conditions with no end on it is the fifth of those five lines, and the count of them is four and did not move, and that fifth line is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a woman did not read the fourth of those five lines. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a woman of about fifty-eight is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who digs loam is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man not coming down a bank is not a payment and a woman not reading a line is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were brought back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six and went back down it, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she read her three lines in that lot book standing up and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-eighth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-seventh morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-seven, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-nine and that it did not move this morning because the man who puts tables up was not at that table at all, and that she entered that and entered no hour and entered no reason, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seventeen and that it did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked the man who puts tables up one thing about the morning he was not there, and nobody asked the woman of about fifty-eight one thing about the fourth of those five lines, and the fifth of those five lines is on that book working exactly as it was written, and the man it names is still not paid, and that table is not coming up that bank.
