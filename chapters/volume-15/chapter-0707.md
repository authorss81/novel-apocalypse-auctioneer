# Chapter 707: On The Twenty-First Of The Eighth Month A Man Came Down That Bank With A Length Of Wood In His Own Two Hands

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the twenty-first of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and that was the two hundred and thirty-fifth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. The second table was at the foot of that bank face up with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it and the copy is at the near end of those two, and that lot book was lying open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and forty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-one this morning and that it moved this morning from twenty, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that a man came down that bank with something in his own two hands two hours after she had entered it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about four people were standing within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine people were standing within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-second of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and sixty-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and five, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and twenty-one days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and thirty-five, and five hundred and ninety-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came down that bank at about half past nine this morning with a length of wood in his own two hands and put it down along the top of that second table at the foot of the bank, clear of that lot book and clear of the stone, and he stood at the near end of that table and looked at the near leg of it and did not cut anything and did not take anything away, and a clerk of nineteen years entered at about ten that a man was at the foot of that bank at about half past nine and entered no reason for it and entered that she did not ask him one thing.

The boy of about nineteen stood at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted that yard at about ten and I counted it again at about eleven and I got about nineteen both times, and about four of you have worked out that a man has been at the foot of that bank six mornings this week and that he has been at the top of that bank for six months before that, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has asked him what for on any of the six mornings, and about four of you have worked out that I counted a yard twice and I have entered neither count anywhere, and about four of you have worked out that a man who comes down a bank and a man who goes up one are the same man and that I have not worked out what to do with that, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the boy entered neither of his counts anywhere and that she did not ask him for them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 63 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered the number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank at about a quarter to eight and it is twenty-one, and about four of you have worked out that a number of mornings is not a thing anybody can put a hand on and that I have not entered what the man who put tables up did at the foot of that bank this morning because I did not see it, and about four of you have worked out that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and that the fifth of those five lines is one of the four and that it did not move, and about four of you have worked out that a length of wood on a table is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and that the count of things this district has made is thirteen, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she entered no reason for a man at the foot of that bank and entered that a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At the foot of that bank at about ten there was a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing with nothing at all in his own hands, about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that there is a length of wood lying along the top of that second table at the foot of that bank this morning and that a man carried it down there in his own two hands and put it down, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has asked him what it is for and that a clerk entered that a man was there and entered no reason. About four of them have said that there is one table in that yard at the top of that bank and there is nothing on it, and that a length of wood is at the bottom of that bank and not in the yard, and that a length of wood is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and that a remedy is not a fifteenth because a remedy is a thing a person does.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, and he did not stay at the top of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man came down that bank this morning with something in his own two hands and was not given one word about it, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and forty-five nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and forty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and forty-fifth of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and fifty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and ten days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and fifteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eighty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and eighty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and twenty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and sixty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and fifty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man carrying a length of wood down a bank is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a number of mornings is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a piece of wood a man has brought somewhere is a piece of wood and not a thing this district has done.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and ninety-six. There are four documents this district does not own and the figure did not move, and there are four places in this district where those three lines can be read and that figure did not move either, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man put a piece of wood on a table. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who puts tables up is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a piece of wood on a table is not a payment and a clerk entering that she did not see what a man did is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past the length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the twenty-first evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirty-second morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and thirty-two mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-first of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is twenty-one, and that a man was at the foot of that bank at about half past nine and that she entered no reason and entered that nobody asked him one thing, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
