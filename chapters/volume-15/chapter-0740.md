# Chapter 740: The Twenty-Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man With A Cut On His Own Right Hand Was At That Table

That first table stood at the top of that bank in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fourth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month with its boards bare and dry and nothing on them at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard, and that was the two hundred and sixty-eighth morning after the count.

That lot book was open on that second table at the foot of that bank with five lines on it, the fifth of them a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of them nine words in that same hand, and the stone lay face up beside it with the hollow one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and two pages lay along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two. A rail was along the far side of that table and a rail was along the near side of it, and the near one had been on there for four mornings, and nobody had cut it again and nobody had put a mark on it and nobody had said one word about it in that yard.

She entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and seventy-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seventeen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-four this morning and that it moved this morning from fifty-three, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and again at about four, got all four of them both times, and a clerk entered them once, and about four people were within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine were within hearing of it at about four. Neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and nobody in that yard has ever entered a reason for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-fifth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and thirty times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and thirty-eight, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and fifty-four days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and sixty-eight, and six hundred and twenty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine with his own right hand wrapped in his own handkerchief and his own left hand flat on that near leg, and he said one thing out loud, in about 49 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got a rail on the near side of that table and nobody has thanked me for it and I am not going to wait to be thanked for it, and about four of you have worked out that I have a cut on my own right hand that I cannot shut my own hand on, and about four of you have worked out that I am not able to carry that table up that bank today and that I am not going to say when I will be, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of those five lines is on that book this morning and that I have not looked at it, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody asked him one thing about when that table will go up that bank and that he did not say, and entered that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 50 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The count of things this district has made is thirteen and it did not move on any of these mornings including the morning that rail went on that table, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-seven and that I entered that on the word of a boy of about nineteen and that I entered no reason, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that it is not paid and that the count of the five is still five, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of the thirteen did not move, and that the count of the five is still five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the near rail, and the man who puts tables up was about nine feet off him at that other end of that table, and neither of them said one word to the other, and nobody has asked the man who digs loam one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off that near end at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat, and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man has stood at that table every morning of this month with a length of wire in his own pocket and that about four of them have said that he has not been asked for one thing and has not offered one thing, and that is the whole of what they have got.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a rail went on to the near side of that table on the twenty-first morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month at about half past ten, and that about four of them have said that in the ten mornings of this block a man has been at the foot of that bank on eight of them and that the number of mornings he has stood there is twenty-seven and that he is not thanked and that he is not paid, and that about four of them have said that a stranger came down that lane two mornings ago with a page in his own right hand and read five lines and that he is not a fourteenth thing either.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what he thinks of a rail on a table at the bottom of a bank.

The cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank without stopping at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at that near leg when the cart came and went on up that lane about half an hour after it, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about any of that.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud at the foot of that bank this morning about his own right hand, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and seventy-eight nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and seventy-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and seventy-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and eighty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and forty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and forty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and eighteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and twenty-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and fifty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and ninety-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eighty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a rail on the near side of that table is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a man saying out loud that he is not going to wait to be thanked is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and twenty-nine. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. Three pages lie on that counter up that bank with the door of that shop propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

That fifth line is one of the four conditions with no end on it, the count of them is four and did not move, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning, and the count of the four did not move on any of these ten mornings. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said a thing out loud about his own right hand. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man who digs loam is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a rail on a table is not a payment and a man saying he is not going to wait to be thanked is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth. Those are two different fifths, and one of them is a thing a document has to say and the other is a thing a person is owed, and neither of them has been paid on any morning of this block.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that two men were at that table when she came down that bank and that neither of them said one word to her, and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-fifth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-fourth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-four, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-seven, and that a rail went on the near side of that table at about half past ten on the twenty-first morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month in his own time and that nobody in that yard asked him to and nobody thanked him and that she entered no reason and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seventeen and that it moved on the twenty-second morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month because a boy said it out loud in that yard before she entered it, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked the man at the foot of that bank one thing about the rail on the morning it went on, and nobody has asked him one thing about it since, and the fifth of those five lines is on that book working exactly as it was written, and the man it names is still not paid, and a man with a page in his own hand came down that lane two mornings ago and read it and went back up.
