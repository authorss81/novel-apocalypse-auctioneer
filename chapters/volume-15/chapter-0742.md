# Chapter 742: The Twenty Sixth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Keeps A Tally Said He Had Entered Four Things About One Man

Rain had come down that bank in the night and the boards of that first table at the top of it were dark at about a quarter to eight on the twenty-sixth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and seventieth morning after the count.

At the foot of that bank the second table was face up with the wet standing along the far rail and drying along the near one, and the stone lay on it with its hollow one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and two pages lay along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two. That lot book was open on it with five lines on it, the fifth in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth nine words in that same hand. Nothing has been cut on that table since the nineteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, and about four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and eighty-one days as the age of that figure, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seventeen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. The count of counted months is six and did not move, and the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-six this morning and it moved this morning from fifty-five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He read those four figures off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got all four of them both times, and a clerk entered them once. There were about four people within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine of them within hearing at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and a clerk has entered no reason for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-seventh of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and thirty-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and forty, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and fifty-six days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and seventy, and six hundred and thirty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the near end of that first table at about ten with his own page open on the boards and said one thing out loud, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got four things written down on that page about one man and none of the four of them is a reason, and about four of you have worked out that I keep a tally of mornings and not of men, and about four of you have worked out that the piece of wood he cut on the nineteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month is the rail that is on the near side of that table this morning and that I can tell by the cut at the end of it, and about four of you have worked out that I am not entering that as a thing this district has made, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a length of wood cut on the nineteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month is the rail on the near side of that second table, and entered no reason and entered no name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 46 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and it is not paid and the count of the five is still five, and about four of you have worked out that a man cutting a piece of wood at the foot of this bank is not a payment and a man writing four things about him on a page is not a payment, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-nine and that I entered that and entered no reason, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of the five did not move and that nothing was paid this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine with his own right hand wrapped in his own handkerchief and nothing at all in his own left hand, and he put that left hand flat on that near leg and left it there. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off that near end at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat, and he did not take it out and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man stood at that table yesterday with nothing in his own two hands and that about four of them have said that he stood at it again this morning, and that about four of them have said that he has not been asked for one thing and has not offered one thing, and that is the whole of what they have got.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a rail went on to the near side of that table six mornings ago and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard asked the man who fitted it to and that nobody thanked him, and that about four of them have said that a man has been at the foot of that bank on twenty-nine mornings since that table came down it, and that about four of them have said that the wet has not put a man off coming down that bank this morning, and that is not a thing anybody entered.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane through the wet and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him whether that lane is worse in the rain, and that he has never once said.

The cart came up that bank at about three, its near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and it did not stand at the top of that bank for a minute and it did not go back down it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went on up that lane about half an hour after that and did not stop at the top of that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who keeps a tally said a thing out loud in that yard at about ten about four things he has written down, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eighty nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eighty-first night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and eighty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and ninety days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and forty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and fifty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and about four of them have said that a man keeping a tally of mornings is not a rule and that nobody asked him to keep it. Four hundred and twenty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and twenty-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and fifty-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and ninety-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eighty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man writing four things about another man on his own page is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a rail going on to a table is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Neither the four documents this district does not own nor the four places in this district where those three lines can be read moved this morning, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and it was not added to it. The door of that shop up that bank is propped open and three pages are on its counter, and it has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

That fifth line is one of the four conditions with no end on it, the count of them is four and did not move, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning, and the count of the four did not move on the morning a man said out loud that he had written four things down about one man. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man keeps a tally. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who fitted that rail is not in it and the man who digs loam is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a tally a man keeps on his own page is not a payment and a man standing at a table doing nothing to it is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

There was nobody at the top of that bank after the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five, and they were carried back up it full past that second table, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up it at about ten to six and went back down it and read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-seventh morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-sixth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-six, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-nine, and that a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said out loud in that yard at about ten that he had four things written down on his own page about one man and that she entered what he said and entered no reason and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seventeen and that it did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked the man at the foot of that bank one thing about a tally a different man keeps on his own page, and the fifth of those five lines is on that book working exactly as it was written, and the man it names is still not paid, and that table is not coming up that bank.
