# Chapter 734: The Eighteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Boy Saw That A Man Had His Own Right Hand Closed Most Of The Morning

The light lay along the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the eighteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and there was nothing on those boards at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and sixty-second morning after the count.

Face up at the foot of that bank was that second table, and the stone lay on it with its hollow one inch deep in the middle of it and nothing at either end, and two pages lay along the end of it with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book lay open on it with five lines on it, the fifth in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth nine words in the same hand. That length of wood was along the top of that table and had not been cut, and it had not been moved since about half past nine the day before.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years stood at the near end of that first table and entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and seventy-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is sixteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-eight this morning and that it moved this morning from forty-seven, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The four off that wall were said out loud twice that morning in the ordinary voice by the man of fifty-six, and he got all four of them both times, and a clerk entered them once, and about four people were close enough to hear him at about a quarter to eight and about nine of them were within hearing at about four. Neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and there is no reason on any page in this district for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-ninth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and eighteen times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and thirty-two, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and forty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and sixty-two, and six hundred and twenty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The boy of about nineteen was at the foot of that bank at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 50 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That man has got his own right hand closed most of the time this morning and he has had it closed every time I have looked since about ten, and about four of you have worked out that the skin is gone in the middle of it and that it is not bleeding and that he is not carrying it and that he is working that leg with the other one, and about four of you have worked out that I did not ask him about it and that I am not going to, and about four of you have worked out that he did not come up that lane yesterday with anything in his own hands that I could see, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man was at the foot of that bank at about ten with his own right hand closed and that nobody asked him about it and that she entered no question and entered no reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered that a man was at the foot of that bank at about ten this morning and that I entered no hour and entered no name, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-two this morning and that a man who keeps a tally said that number out loud in that yard and that I entered it after him, and about four of you have worked out that nobody thanked him this morning and that nobody thanked him yesterday, and about four of you have worked out that I entered that he was not thanked, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that a boy is not in it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine with his own left hand flat on the top of that near leg and his own right hand closed most of the time, and he had not cut that length of wood and had not said one word about it. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off that near end at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and his own back to the boards of that first table at the top of that bank, and he watched a man work for about two hours and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man has been at the foot of that bank on twenty-two mornings since the morning that table came down this bank, and that about four of them have said that he has cut nothing on any of the twenty-two, and that about four of them have said that a table with one rail along the far side of it and an uncut length of wood lying along the top of it is a table that is being worked on, and that about four of them have said that a man in that yard at about nine in the morning on more mornings this month than he has been in that yard at about three is a thing about a man and not a thing this district has made.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at about half past one and went on up it and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to look at anything on his way.

At about three the cart came up that bank with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was still at that near leg when the cart came and kept his own left hand on the wood after it had gone.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a boy said a thing out loud at the foot of that bank this morning about a man's own right hand, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and seventy-two nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and seventy-third night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and seventy-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and eighty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and thirty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and forty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and it has not been written down anywhere but in that clerk's own page. Four hundred and twelve days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and fifteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and fifty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and ninety-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and seventy-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man with his own right hand closed at a table is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a boy saying so out loud is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and twenty-three. Nobody in that yard counted a fifth document or a fifth place this morning, and the four documents this district does not own and the four places in this district where those three lines can be read are both where they were at about a quarter to eight, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list. That shop up that bank stands open with three pages on its counter and its door propped, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one of four conditions with no end on it, the count of them is four and did not move this morning, and that line stands on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a boy said a thing out loud about a hand. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man with his own right hand closed is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man working a table with one hand is not a payment and a clerk entering that he was not thanked is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past that length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she came down that bank at about ten to six and that a man with his own right hand closed was still at that table and that neither of them said one word, and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-ninth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the eighteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-eight, and that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard at about ten and that she entered what he said and entered no question, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-two, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
