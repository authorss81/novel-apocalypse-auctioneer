# Chapter 731: The Fifteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Clerk Had An Empty Column And Nobody Proposed She Start Again

There was no rime on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the fifteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and fifty-ninth morning after the count.

That second table stood at the foot of that bank, and on it the stone lay face up with the hollow in the middle of that stone one inch deep and nothing at either end of it, and along the end of that table two pages with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book lying open with five lines on it, the fifth of those five in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of those five still nine words in that same hand. A length of wood lay along the top of that table and had not been cut, and about four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

At about a quarter to eight, at the near end of that first table, a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and seventy days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is sixteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-five this morning and that it moved this morning from forty-four, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said those four off that wall in the ordinary voice with his own eyes on that yard and not on the wall, got all four of them, and said them again at about four and got them again, and a clerk entered them once. About nine people were standing within hearing of that wall at about four and about four of them were close enough at about a quarter to eight, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-sixth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and twelve times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and twenty-nine, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and forty-five days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and fifty-nine, and six hundred and twenty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The woman of about fifty-eight was at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 61 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read three lines of that book standing up from the top of this bank for two years and this morning I came up it and stopped about halfway up and could not find the first of them, and about four of you have worked out that the book is at the bottom of this bank and not at the top of it, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard has ever told me to read it from the bottom, and about four of you have worked out that I have been saying the days of that month out loud to myself for two years and that I have stopped, and that I do not know what day this is, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to start again on a different number this morning, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a column that has carried a date on every morning of her working life was left empty this morning, and that she entered nothing in it and entered no reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered that number of mornings at about a quarter to eight at the top of this bank and I entered no date beside it, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this district has counted the length of that month, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to count a month I am standing in, and about four of you have worked out that the count of counted months is six and did not move this morning, and about four of you have worked out that a man was at the foot of this bank with his own two hands on that near leg at about half past nine and that I entered that and entered no reason and entered no hour, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she entered no date this month and did not begin another column, and that the count of the six counted months did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine with his own two hands on that near leg, and the length of wood was lying along the top of that table behind him, and he had not cut it and had not said what it was for. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at that table at about ten, nine feet off its near end, with nothing at all in his own hands, and a length of fencing wire was in the pocket of his coat and he did not take it out, and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered a number of mornings this morning at the top of that bank and that she entered no date beside it, and that about four of them have said that a man was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine and that nobody in that yard has ever said when that length of wood will be cut, and that about four of them have said that the rail on the far side of that table has been along it since the morning that table came down this bank, and that about four of them have said that the length of wood lying along the top of it came down that bank in a man's own two hands and has not been cut on any morning since, and that those are two different pieces of wood and that nobody in that yard has ever said so.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and that about four of them have said that nobody in this yard has ever asked him what he thinks of a man working at the bottom of a bank, and that he has never once said.

The cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was not in that yard at about three that afternoon and had gone on up that lane about half an hour before the cart reached the top of that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a woman said a thing out loud at the top of that bank this morning about a day she could not find, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and sixty-nine nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and seventieth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and sixty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and seventy-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and thirty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and thirty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and twelve days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and forty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and eighty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and seventy-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man with his own two hands on a leg is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a clerk leaving a column empty is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and twenty. The count of documents this district does not own is four and the count of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four, and neither figure moved this morning, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it. A door of that shop up that bank is propped open and three pages lie on that counter, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The count of conditions with no end on it is four, it did not move this morning, the fifth of those five lines is one of the four, and it stands on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written with no hand on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a woman said a thing out loud about a day. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a woman of about fifty-eight is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man who puts tables up is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk with an empty column is not a payment and a woman who cannot find the first of three lines is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past that length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she said a thing out loud in that yard at about ten and read her three lines at about ten to six and did not say one word the second time, and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-sixth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the fifteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-five, and that she entered no date this month and entered nothing in a column that has carried a date on every morning of her working life and that nobody proposed she start again, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is sixteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
