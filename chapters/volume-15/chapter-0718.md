# Chapter 718: The Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Road Keeper Came Up That Lane A Second Time And Nobody Stopped Him

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about three this afternoon, about nine feet off the near end of that first table, with nothing at all in his own two hands, and that is the hour that yard has seen him at since the second of January. There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the second morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and forty-sixth morning after the count. The second table was at the foot of that bank face up with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book was lying open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand. About nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings without once putting a thumb in it, and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years set her own page on the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight and entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and fifty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-two this morning and that it moved this morning from thirty-one, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that she wrote that number down before any man had been down that bank and that there was nothing in that yard at about nine to say she should not have.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without his looking at that wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. Nobody has ever entered how many people were within hearing of that wall, and what is entered is about four at about a quarter to eight and about nine at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-third of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and eighty-six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and sixteen, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and thirty-two days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and forty-six, and six hundred and seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with a flat book under his own left arm at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 53 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got a book under my own arm and I have counted that yard twice this morning and got about nineteen both times, and about four of you have worked out that I have counted that yard every morning of this month and that I have not entered a single one of those counts in that book, and about four of you have worked out that a road keeper went up that lane at his usual hour and came back up it again this afternoon and that nobody in that yard stopped him either time, and about four of you have worked out that a tally of a yard is not a tally of men and that I have not worked out what it is a tally of, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and that she did not ask him what his book is a tally of, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 59 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered that number of mornings at about a quarter to eight and I entered that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that it did not move, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that it is not paid and that the count of the five is still five, and about four of you have worked out that a man who puts tables up will be in that yard at about three this afternoon with nothing in his own hands and that I entered that he was not at the foot of that bank this morning, and about four of you have worked out that entering a man is not a payment and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that entering a man at the foot of that bank is not a payment and did not pay the fifth of the five, and that the count of the five is still five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

There was nothing at the foot of that bank this morning except a table with a stone on it and a book open on it and two pages at the end of it. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off the near end of that second table at the foot of that bank at about ten, his own hands empty, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. At the far end of that second table at about eleven a man of about thirty-one who digs loam had his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who keeps a tally has a book under his own arm in that yard and that a clerk of nineteen years has a book of her own, and that about four of them have said that neither of those two books is the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that the column is ruled and empty at about six on every morning of this month. About four of them have said that the length of that month is not known to anybody and that a man who keeps a tally has not entered it either.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles. He came up it again at about half past three and read the four figures off that wall as he went by a second time, and he was not asked about the eleven miles then either, and about four people at that wall have said since that he has gone up that lane and come back up it twice a day for as long as anybody in that yard has been keeping figures, and that nobody has ever asked him what he does at the other end of it.

The cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up, and the man who puts tables up stood about nine feet off the near end of that first table until the cart had gone by the top of that bank and then went on up that lane himself, and about four people at that low wall have said since that neither of those two men stopped at the top of that bank and that the cart-man stayed a while and the other man did not.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a road keeper came up that lane at about half past three while the man on those stones was there and that neither of the two of them said one word, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and fifty-six nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fifty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and fifty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and sixty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and twenty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and twenty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and ninety-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and ninety-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and thirty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and seventy-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and sixty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man with a flat book under his own arm is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a clerk entering a count of things this district has made is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a book a man carries under his own arm is a book and not a thing anybody in this district has been given.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and seven. The number of documents this district does not own stands at four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read stands at four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. Three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

There are four conditions with no end on it and the count did not move this morning, and the fifth of those five lines is one of those four, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man kept a tally out loud. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a tally of about nineteen people is not a payment and a clerk entering that a man was not at the foot of that bank this morning is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody went up that bank after the buckets had been carried down it. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that there was nothing at that table this morning except a book and a stone and two pages, and that the woman went down that bank again this evening and did not look for the first line, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-third morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen, on the second morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-two, and that a man who keeps a tally said a thing out loud in that yard and that she entered that he said it and entered no name and entered no column, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
