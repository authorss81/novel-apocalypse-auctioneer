# Chapter 733: The Seventeenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Cuts Reeds Would Not Say Which Way That Wood Wants To Lie

Nothing had been put on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the seventeenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, and they were dry and hard, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and sixty-first morning after the count.

The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, the hollow one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and two pages along the end with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open with five lines on it, the fifth of them in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of them still nine words in that hand. That length of wood was along the top of that table and had not been cut, and the rail on the far side of that table was where it had been since the morning that table came down this bank.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight, and the first line on her own page was that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and seventy-two days as the age of that figure. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is sixteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-seven this morning and that it moved this morning from forty-six, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He read those four figures off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight with about four people standing close enough to hear and again at about four with about nine of them within hearing of it, and he got all four both times and did not look at that wall once while he was doing it, and a clerk entered them once. Neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and a clerk entered no reason for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-eighth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and sixteen times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and thirty-one, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and forty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and sixty-one, and six hundred and twenty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the foot of that bank at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I cut reeds and I cut wire in the spring and I have cut a great deal of wood in my life and I can tell you from the cut at the end of a piece which way it wants to lie, and about four of you have worked out that I have not said which way that piece wants to lie and that I looked at it yesterday and I looked at it again this morning and that I am not going to say, and about four of you have worked out that a man has had his own two hands on that near leg for a fortnight and has not cut it, and that he was in that yard at about three yesterday and the morning before, and that I have not counted this afternoon because it is not three yet, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody asked him which way that piece of wood wants to lie and that he did not say, and entered that a man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That fifth line has been on that book working exactly as it was written since the morning that table came down this bank, and about four of you have worked out that it is one of four conditions with no end on it and that the count of them is four and did not move this morning, and about four of you have worked out that no hand went on that book today and that nobody in this yard has ever had to decide anything about that line, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-seven and that I entered it at the top of this bank, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of the four conditions with no end on it did not move, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at that table about nine feet off its near end from about ten until about half past eleven on that morning, and there was nobody within nine feet of him at that table at all, and a length of fencing wire was in the pocket of his coat and he did not take it out, and he looked at the near end of that table and then at his own boots and then at the ground between them, and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came up that lane at about half past two with nothing at all in his own two hands and stood in that yard at about three while the cart went by, and about four people at that low wall have said since that a yard which has seen that man at about three every afternoon since the second of January saw him at about three this afternoon and that nobody in it said one word to him about where he had been this morning, and that he went on up that lane about half an hour after the cart and did not stop at the top of that bank.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man came up that lane at about half past two with empty hands about an hour after him, and that about four of them have said that the two of them passed each other at the top of that lane and that neither of them said one word, and that the man who keeps a tally was not asked that either.

A cart came up that bank at about three, its near wheel dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank past that first table.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud at the foot of that bank this morning about a cut at the end of a piece of wood, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and seventy-one nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and seventy-second night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and seventy-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and eighty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and thirty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and forty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and a clerk entered it and entered no name against it. Four hundred and eleven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and fourteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and fifty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and ninety marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and seventy-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying out loud which way a piece of wood wants to lie is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and saying nothing is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and twenty-two. Four documents this district does not own and four places in this district where those three lines can be read, and neither of those two figures moved this morning, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it. That shop is open up that bank with its door propped and three pages on its counter, and it has not shut on a morning of this year, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

One of the four conditions with no end on it is the fifth of those five lines, and the count of them is four and did not move, and that fifth line is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said a thing out loud about wood. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who cuts reeds is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man of about thirty-four is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who will not say which way a piece of wood wants to lie is not a payment and a clerk entering the count of four this morning is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past that length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that there was one man at that table this morning and that she came down that bank at about ten to six and that he was not there, and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-eighth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the seventeenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-seven, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-one and did not move this morning, and that he was in that yard at about three with nothing in his own two hands and that she entered that and entered no reason, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is sixteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
