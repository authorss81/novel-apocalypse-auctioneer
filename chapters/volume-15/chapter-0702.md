# Chapter 702: On The Sixteenth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Keeps A Tally Said Out Loud That He Counts A Number Of Mornings And Not A Number Of Men

The sky over the top of that bank was the colour of an old plate on the sixteenth of the eighth month and stayed that colour all morning, and there was a skin of water standing on the boards of that first table from the night and the boards were hard under it and there was nothing on them at all. About four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and thirtieth morning after the count. The stone on that second table lay face up at the foot of that bank with the hollow in the middle of it one inch deep and nothing at either end of it, and two pages lay along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book lay open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand. Nine people at that low wall have said since that about four hundred mornings they have looked at that hollow, and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it, and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

She entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and forty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. She entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixteen this morning and that it moved this morning from fifteen, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years was at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight and that she wrote that number down before any man in that yard had been down that bank.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at that wall, and a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. About four people were within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine were within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-seventh of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and fifty-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and sixteen days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and thirty, and five hundred and ninety-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with a flat book under his own left arm and he said one thing out loud, in about 60 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I keep a tally of mornings and not of men, and about four of you have worked out that a clerk of nineteen years entered the number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank at about a quarter to eight this morning at the near end of a table in that yard, and about four of you have worked out that she wrote it down before any man had been down that bank that day, and about four of you have worked out that I have got a book under my own arm and she has got a book of her own and neither of them is the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and about four of you have worked out that a tally of mornings does not have anybody in it and that is the whole of what I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that no person has been appointed to it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 63 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That number is sixteen and it was fifteen at about a quarter to eight yesterday, and about four of you have worked out that I have entered it every morning since the table went down that bank and that the morning it went down I entered nothing, and about four of you have worked out that there is no column in this district for the mornings a thing has stood somewhere, and that there are four columns of not-askings and that I did not rule a fifth this morning, and about four of you have worked out that the number of documents this district does not own is four and it did not move, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and a man with a book under his own arm does not make a fourteenth, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixteen, and entered that a column of not-askings is not a column of mornings and did not rule one, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about ten the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank about nine feet off the near end of that second table with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the man who puts tables up has not been at the foot of that bank this month at all, and that the man who digs loam was at the far end of it at about eleven yesterday and again this morning, and that neither of the two of them was asked one thing about either morning.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and read the four figures off that wall as he went by and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up, set the shafts down at the top of it, and went back down that bank at about a quarter to four with his own hands back on the shafts. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had been in that yard at about three with nothing at all in his own two hands and had gone on up that lane before the cart reached the top of the bank, and that is the hour that yard has seen him at since the second of January, and nobody in that yard has asked him what for.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who keeps a tally said a thing out loud in that yard this morning about a tally of mornings and not of men, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and forty nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and forty-first night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and forty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and fifty days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and ten days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eighty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and eighty-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and nineteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying a thing out loud about a tally of mornings is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a column she did not rule is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a book under a man's own arm is not a thing anybody else in this district has been given. Two hundred and fifty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and forty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and ninety-one. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, a shop counter is a place and is not on that list, and it was not added to this morning. Three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank and the door of that shop is propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one of the four conditions with no end on it, and the count of them is four and did not move, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man kept a tally out loud. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and the man who puts tables up is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a tally of mornings is not a payment and a clerk entering that she did not rule a fifth column is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Not one person in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the sixteenth evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-seventh morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and twenty-seven mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the sixteenth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixteen, and that a man who keeps a tally said a thing out loud in that yard and that she entered that he said it and entered no name and entered no column, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
