# Chapter 736: The Twentieth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Held A Cut Piece Of Wood Against The Near Side Of That Table And It Was Long

There was no rime on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the twentieth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and there was nothing on them at all and they were dry and hard, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and sixty-fourth morning after the count.

That second table stood at the foot of that bank, and on it the stone lay face up with the hollow in the middle of that stone one inch deep and nothing at either end of it, and along the end of that table two pages with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book lying open with five lines on it, the fifth of those five in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of those five still nine words in that same hand. A rail was along the far side of that table where it had been since the morning that table came down this bank, and a piece of wood with both its ends square was lying on the ground at the foot of that table and had been lying there since yesterday afternoon.

At about a quarter to eight, at the near end of that first table, a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and seventy-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is sixteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty this morning and that it moved this morning from forty-nine, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said those four off that wall in the ordinary voice with his own eyes on that yard and not on the wall, got all four of them, and said them again at about four and got them again, and a clerk entered them once. About nine people were standing within hearing of that wall at about four and about four of them were close enough at about a quarter to eight, and Neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-first of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and twenty-two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and thirty-four, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and fifty days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and sixty-four, and six hundred and twenty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard at about ten with his flat book under his own left arm, and he said one thing out loud, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got twenty-four mornings written down on that page on which a man has been at the foot of that bank and I have entered every one of them, and about four of you have worked out that I count mornings and not men and that I have now entered three things about one man in a fortnight, and about four of you have worked out that none of the three of them is a reason and that I am not going to write one, and about four of you have worked out that this morning he has got a piece of wood with both its ends square and has not put it on that table, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the number of twenty-four, and entered that she entered it after he had said it out loud and not before and that she entered no reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered that number of mornings at the top of this bank and I entered no date beside it, and about four of you have worked out that the count of the five things this district does not have is still five and that the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and about four of you have worked out that a man at the bottom of that bank this morning is not a payment and that a man who keeps a tally writing a number down is not a payment either, and about four of you have worked out that nobody asked either of them, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have was not paid this morning, and that the count of the five is still five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came down that bank at about half past nine, picked that piece of wood up off the ground with his own two hands, and held it against the near side of that table for about half an hour, and it hung over the near end by about the width of his own hand, and he took it away again and put it down on the ground and did not cut it again and did not say what he was going to do about that overhang. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off that near end from about ten until about half past eleven, and he watched a man hold a piece of wood against a table for half an hour and put it down again, and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a piece of wood with both its ends square has lain on the ground at the foot of that table since yesterday afternoon, and that about four of them have said that a rail is a length of wood on the side of a table and that this one is not on the side of a table yet, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has asked that man where he is going to put it, and that about four of them have said that a clerk entered a number this morning and entered no hour and entered no name, and that about four of them have said that the boy read every one of those numbers back to himself and wrote none of them down.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane twice a day and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him about the eleven miles and that he has never once said, and that about four of them have said that a man was holding a piece of wood against a table at the bottom of that bank all morning and that the road keeper was not asked about it.

That cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging on the stones, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the foot of that bank with his own right hand wrapped in his own handkerchief at about a quarter to four and went on up that lane about half an hour after the cart had gone.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man held a piece of wood against a table for about half an hour this morning and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and seventy-four nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and seventy-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and seventy-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and eighty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and thirty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and forty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and it is not on that wall and is not on any page in that yard. Four hundred and fourteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and seventeen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and fifty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and ninety-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and seventy-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a length of wood lying on the ground at the foot of a table is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a man holding it against the side of that table is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and twenty-five. The count of documents this district does not own is four and the count of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four, and neither figure moved this morning, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it. A door of that shop up that bank is propped open and three pages lie on that counter, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

One of the four conditions with no end on it is the fifth of those five lines, and the count of them is four and did not move, and that fifth line is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man held a piece of wood against a table. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man with the piece of wood is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a piece of wood on the ground is not a payment and a piece of wood on a table is not a payment, and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she came down that bank at about ten to six and that a piece of wood with both its ends square was on the ground beside that table and that she did not look at it, and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-first morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twentieth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty, and that a man held a piece of wood against the near side of that table for about half an hour this morning and did not put it on and said nothing out loud in that yard, and that she entered that and entered no reason and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is sixteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
