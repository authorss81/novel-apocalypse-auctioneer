# Chapter 748: The Thirty-Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Was Not At The Foot Of That Bank And A Clerk Entered No Reason

The man who puts tables up was not at the foot of that bank on the thirty-second morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month. He had not come down it at about half past nine, and he had not come down it at about ten, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not there and entered no hour and entered no reason, and the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank did not move. That first table at the top of that bank stood in the yard of Lot Seventeen with its boards swept and nothing on them at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard, and that was the two hundred and seventy-sixth morning after the count.

The second table stood face up at the foot of that bank with both its rails on it and the stone lying on it with its hollow one inch deep in the middle of it and nothing at either end, and two pages along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open on it with five lines on it, the fifth of them in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth nine words in that same hand, and about four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

She entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and eighty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eighteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixty-two this morning and that it moved this morning from sixty-one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got all four both times, and a clerk entered them once. That wall was read out loud to about four people at about a quarter to eight and to about nine at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason has ever been entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventy-third of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and forty-six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and forty-six, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and sixty-two days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and seventy-six, and six hundred and thirty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The woman of about fifty-eight was at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 41 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There was no man at that table this morning when I looked at it from the top of this bank, and about four of you have worked out that he was at it yesterday with nothing at all in his own two hands, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to say that a man who works at a table has to be at that table every morning of a month, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to say where he was, and that is the whole of what I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman of about fifty-eight is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty-three and it did not move this morning, and about four of you have worked out that I entered that he was not at that table at all and that I entered no hour and entered no reason, and about four of you have worked out that the count of the five things this district does not have is still five and that the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and about four of you have worked out that a man not coming down a bank is not a payment of it, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank did not move this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table from about ten until about half past eleven with nothing at all in his own hands and a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat, and there was no man at the other end of that table and he did not say one word in that yard on that morning, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man stood at that table from about ten until about half past eleven yesterday and from about ten until about half past eleven this morning and that about four of them have said that on the morning before this one there was nobody at the other end of it, and that about four of them have said that he did not leave, and that that is the whole of what they have got.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man was not at the foot of that bank this morning, and that about four of them have said that a clerk entered that and entered no reason, and that about four of them have said that the near rail has been on that table for twelve mornings and that about four of them have said that the fifth of those five lines was face up on that book the whole of the morning and that about four of them have said that nobody turned round to look at it, and that about four of them have said that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that it did not move on the morning a man did not come.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him where a man goes when he does not come down a bank, and that he has never once said.

The cart came up that bank at about three, its near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and it did not stand at the top of that bank for a minute and it did not go back down it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was not in that yard at about three and had gone on up that lane about half an hour before the cart reached the top of that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that there was one man at that table this morning where there have been two on eight of the ten mornings of this month, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eighty-six nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eighty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and eighty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.
