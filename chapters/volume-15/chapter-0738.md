# Chapter 738: The Twenty-Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Stranger Came Down That Lane And Read Five Lines And Did Not Look At Either Rail

There were two rails on that second table at the foot of that bank on the twenty-second morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, one along the far side where it had always been and one along the near side where a man had put it at about half past ten two mornings before with his own two hands, and a man came down that lane at about half past nine with a page in his own right hand, went on down to the foot of that bank, stood at the near end of that table, read the five lines on that book, did not look at either rail and said not one word to anybody there, and went back up that lane about half an hour later. That first table at the top of that bank had nothing on it, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and sixty-sixth morning after the count.

The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, the hollow one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and two pages along the end with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open with five lines on it, the fifth of them in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of them still nine words in that hand. About four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight, and the first line on her own page was that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and seventy-seven days as the age of that figure. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is sixteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-two this morning and that it moved this morning from fifty-one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He read those four figures off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight with about four people standing close enough to hear and again at about four with about nine of them within hearing of it, and he got all four both times without looking at that wall once, and a clerk entered them once. Neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and a clerk entered no reason for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-third of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and twenty-six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.
