# Chapter 720: The Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Road Keeper Went Up That Lane Twice And A Cart Came Up At About Three

The road keeper went up that lane at about half past one and read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and he was not asked about the eleven miles. He went up it a second time at about half past three while a cart was coming up that same lane with its near wheel dragging, and he was not asked about the eleven miles then either, and neither of those two men stopped at the top of that bank. There was no rime on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the fourth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and forty-eighth morning after the count. That second table stood at the foot of that bank and on it were the stone, face up, with its hollow one inch deep in the middle of it and nothing at either end of it, and two pages along the end with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book lying open with five lines on it, the fifth of those five in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of those five still nine words in that same hand. About four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

She entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and fifty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-four this morning and that it moved this morning from thirty-three, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that she wrote that number down at about a quarter to eight and that nothing had come up that lane before she wrote it, and that a man came up it twice afterwards and that she entered neither of those passes anywhere.

Nobody in that yard has ever heard the man of fifty-six stop in the middle of it: he said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice, got all four of them without looking at the wall, and said them again at about four and got them again, and a clerk entered them once. That wall was read out loud to about four people at about a quarter to eight and to about nine at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-fifth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and ninety times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and eighteen, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and thirty-four days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and forty-eight, and six hundred and nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard at about ten with a flat book under his own left arm and he said one thing out loud, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That lane goes up past that yard and on to the top of that bank and a man has gone up it twice today and come back up it twice, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in that yard asked him one thing on either of those passes and that he read four figures off that wall as he went by and did not stop, and about four of you have worked out that the whole of that lane belongs to him and that this yard is not on it, and about four of you have worked out that I have counted that yard twice and got about nineteen and that I entered neither count in that book of my own, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a lane is not a document this district owns and that nobody in that yard asked the man who goes up it one thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel of it dragging and the man driving it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up past that yard, and about four of you have worked out that he did not stop at the top of that bank and that nobody in this yard called him back, and about four of you have worked out that a road keeper was going up that lane at the same time and that neither of those two men spoke to the other, and about four of you have worked out that a man with a length of wood in his own two hands was at the foot of that bank this morning and that none of the three of them came the other way, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a cart is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and that the count of things this district has made is thirteen, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 57 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man went up that lane twice this morning and nobody in this yard asked him one thing on either pass, and about four of you have worked out that I entered the number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank at about a quarter to eight and that it is thirty-four, and about four of you have worked out that a cart came up that bank at about three and did not stop at the top of it and that a cart coming up a bank is not a thing anybody in this district has to enter, and about four of you have worked out that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and that it did not move this morning, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and that it did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

There was a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing at that table at about ten, nine feet off its near end, with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. There was a man of about thirty-one who digs loam at the far end of that table at about eleven, his own two hands on the rail and his own spade against the leg of it, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a cart came up that same lane at about three and did not stop at the top of that bank.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles. He came up it again at about half past three and was not asked about the eleven miles then either.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank without stopping at the top of it, and set the shafts down there about ten minutes later and went back down that bank at about a quarter to four with his own hands back on the shafts. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was not in that yard at about three this afternoon and had gone on up that lane before the cart reached the top of that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and fifty-eight nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fifty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and fifty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and sixty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and twenty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and twenty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and ninety-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and thirty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and seventy-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and sixty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man going up a lane twice is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a cart coming up a bank is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a lane belongs to the man who goes up it and that a yard is not on it.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and nine. Four documents this district does not own, four places in this district where those three lines can be read, and neither figure moved, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. Three pages are lying on that shop counter with the door of that shop propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The count of conditions with no end on it is four, it did not move this morning, the fifth of those five lines is one of the four, and it stands on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written with no hand on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man went up a lane and came back up it. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a boy is not in it and a road keeper is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man going up a lane twice is not a payment and a cart coming up a bank is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past that length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a cart came up that bank at about three and went on up past that yard and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and forty-fifth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the fourth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirty-four, and that a man went up that lane twice and that a cart came up that bank at about three and did not stop at the top of it, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
