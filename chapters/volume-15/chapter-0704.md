# Chapter 704: On The Eighteenth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Puts Tables Up Was In That Yard At About Nine And Nobody Asked Him Why

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the eighteenth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and that was the two hundred and thirty-second morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. There is a second table at the foot of that bank with the stone on it face up, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep with nothing at either end of it, and along the end of that table are two pages with the copy at the near end of them, and that lot book is open on it with five lines, and the fifth of those five is a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is nine words in that same hand, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and forty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is eighteen this morning and that it moved this morning from seventeen, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that the man she entered it for had not come up that bank yet.

A man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about four people were standing within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine people were standing within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-ninth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and fifty-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and two, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and eighteen days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and thirty-two, and five hundred and ninety-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was in that yard at about nine this morning with nothing at all in his own hands, about nine feet off the near end of that first table, and he stood there until about ten and went back down that bank, and he had been in that yard at about three every afternoon for six months, and nobody asked him to be there any earlier and nobody has asked him why he is.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said one thing out loud, in about 51 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in this yard at about three every afternoon since the second of January and this morning I was in it at about nine with nothing at all in my own two hands, and about four of you have worked out that nobody asked me to come at that hour and that nobody has asked me what for, and about four of you have worked out that the boards of that table are dry and hard and there is nothing on them and I have looked at them, and about four of you have worked out that the ground at the bottom of that bank is soft on one side of it and that is the side I put a leg on, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that nobody in that yard asked him one thing about the hour he was there at, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man who puts tables up was in this yard at about nine this morning with nothing at all in his own two hands and he stood about nine feet off the near end of that table and he went back down that bank at about ten, and about four of you have worked out that I have counted that yard every morning this month and that there is one table in it and nothing on that table, and about four of you have worked out that a man standing in a yard at about nine is not a fourteenth thing this district has made, and about four of you have worked out that I do not know what I would count about a man standing somewhere, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who counts a yard and names nobody is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 72 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered that a man was in that yard at about nine and I entered no reason and I entered no hour and I entered no name, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is eighteen and that I entered it at about a quarter to eight, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and it did not move, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that it is not paid and that the count of the five is still five, and about four of you have worked out that the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is ruled and it is empty and that I entered a name in it this morning of nobody, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is ruled and empty and that no person was appointed to it and no stranger was entered in it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven, his own spade against the leg of it, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who puts tables up was in that yard at about nine with nothing in his own hands and that he had been in that yard at about three every afternoon for six months, and that nobody in that yard asked him to come earlier and nobody has asked him why he came, and that both of those are the same silence.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

At about three the cart came up that bank with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up, and he did not stay at the top of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man was in that yard at about nine this morning and was not given one word about it, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and forty-two nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and forty-third night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and forty-second of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and fifty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and twelve days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eighty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and eighty-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and twenty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and sixty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and forty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man standing in a yard at about nine is not a fourteenth thing and a boy saying a thing out loud about a man standing somewhere is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man in a yard is not a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and ninety-three. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man was in that yard at about nine. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man standing in a yard at about nine is not a payment and a clerk entering that she entered no reason is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the eighteenth evening and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-ninth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the eighteenth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is eighteen, and that a man who puts tables up was in that yard at about nine with nothing at all in his own hands and that she entered that he was there and entered no reason and entered no hour and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
