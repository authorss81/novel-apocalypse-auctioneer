# Chapter 750: The Thirty Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Stood At The Foot Of That Bank With Nothing In His Own Hands

The first table stood at the near end of the yard of Lot Seventeen at the top of that bank on the thirty-fourth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month with its boards bare and dry, nothing on them at all and no stone in it, and that stone lying face up at the foot of that bank on the other table, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard, and that was the two hundred and seventy-eighth morning after the count.

Face up at the bottom of that bank was that second table with a rail along the far side of it and a rail along the near side of it that a man fitted to it in about ten minutes with his own two hands on the twenty-first morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, and the stone lying on it with the hollow one inch deep in the middle of it and nothing at either end, and two pages along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two. That lot book was open on it with five lines on it, the fifth of them in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of them nine words in that same hand, and about four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years was at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight with her own page flat on those boards and her own pen on it, in the act of entering the count of that board, and she had not looked up. She entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and eighty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eighteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixty-four this morning and that it moved this morning from sixty-three, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got all four of them both times, and a clerk entered them once, and about four people were within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine were within hearing of it at about four. Neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and nobody in that yard has entered a reason for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventy-fifth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and fifty times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and forty-eight, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and sixty-four days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and seventy-eight, and six hundred and thirty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the foot of that bank with nothing at all in his own hands, and he said one thing out loud, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put that rail on the near side of that table in my own time in about ten minutes and nobody in that yard asked me to and nobody in that yard has thanked me, and about four of you have worked out that that is a thing a person did and not a thing this district has made, and about four of you have worked out that there is a rail on the near side of that table and one on the far side of it and that this table is not coming up that bank, and about four of you have worked out that I have nothing at all in my own hands this morning and that I am not waiting to be thanked for it, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a rail fitted by a man is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and that nobody thanked him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the near end of that first table at about ten with his own page open and said one thing out loud, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That second table has stood at the foot of this bank for sixty-four mornings and I entered that number this morning about two hundred feet above the table it is a count of, and about four of you have worked out that it is a count of mornings and not a figure behind which anything was bought or owed, and about four of you have worked out that no person has been added to this district this morning, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the tenure is a count of mornings and not a figure behind which anything was bought or owed, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The count of things this district has made is thirteen and it did not move on any of the fifty mornings of this volume, and about four of you have worked out that a rail fitted by a man is not a fourteenth thing and a remedy is not a fifteenth, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that it is not paid and that the count of the five is still five, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty-five and that I entered that and entered no reason, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of the thirteen did not move and that the count of the five is still five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the bottom of that bank at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of that second table and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing and nobody has offered him one thing. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the bottom of that bank from about ten until about half past eleven with nothing at all in his own hands and a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat, and he did not mend that table, nobody asked him to mend it, the sentence about what his yard is for was not said a second time, and he said nothing out loud in that yard.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a rail went on to the near side of that table thirteen mornings ago in about ten minutes with a man's own two hands, and that nobody asked him to and that nobody has thanked him since, and that the man the fifth line names is still not paid on any of them, and that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and that nobody has ever asked what that sheet is.

The road keeper came up that lane twice that day, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and nobody in that yard has ever asked him what he thinks of a table that is not coming back up a bank.

The cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stay at the top of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eighty-eight nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eighty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and eighty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and ninety-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and fifty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and fifty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and a rail fitted by a man is not a rule. Four hundred and twenty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and thirty-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and sixty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Three hundred and seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and ninety-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a rail on the near side of that table is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a remedy is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and thirty-nine. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. The shop up that bank is open with its door propped open and three pages lie on that counter, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and the tenure of that table in that yard is not a figure on this page at all, because that table is not in that yard, and a new person added to this district this morning is none.

There are four conditions with no end on it and the count did not move this morning, and the fifth of those five lines is one of them and it is on that book working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The count of them is four and not five. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and the stranger is not in it and the man who said no is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man of about thirty-four is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count of the five is still five.

The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, and it is not the sixth, and it was not paid again this morning.

Those are two different fifths, one of them a thing this district does not have and the other of them a thing a document that sets a lot out has to say, and on this page one of them stands directly above the other, and neither has been paid on any morning of this volume.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up about four feet off the fourth one and did not stop, and did not look at it, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirty-fourth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, the fiftieth morning of the nineteenth year after the Long Fracture, that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is sixty-four and that it moved this morning from sixty-three, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty-five, entered on the word of a man who keeps a tally with no reason entered, and that a rail went on to the near side of that table at about half past ten on the twenty-first morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month in his own time that nobody asked him to and nobody has thanked him, and that she entered no reason and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eighteen and that it moved on the twenty-ninth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month because a boy of about nineteen said it out loud in that yard at about ten before she entered it and that it did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked the man at the foot of that bank one thing about the rail, and the fifth of those five lines is on that book working exactly as it was written, and the man it names is still not paid, and that table is not coming up that bank.
