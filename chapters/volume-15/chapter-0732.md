# Chapter 732: The Sixteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Keeps A Tally Entered Twenty-One Mornings Of One Man And Entered No Reason

The boards of that first table at the top of that bank had nothing on them at all on the sixteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and they were dry and hard, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and sixtieth morning after the count.

At the foot of that bank the second table was face up, and on it the stone lay face up with the hollow in the middle of that stone one inch deep and nothing at either end of it, and along the end of that table two pages with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book open on it with five lines on it, of which the fifth is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched and the fourth is still nine words in that same hand. That length of wood was along the top of that table where it had lain since yesterday morning, and it had not been cut on that morning either.

The first thing she entered at about a quarter to eight, standing at the near end of that first table with her own page on the boards, was that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and seventy-one days as the age of that figure, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is sixteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-six this morning and that it moved this morning from forty-five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard has ever heard the man of fifty-six stop in the middle of it: he said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice, got all four of them without looking at the wall, and said them again at about four and got them again, and a clerk entered them once. That wall was read out loud to about four people at about a quarter to eight and to about nine at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason has ever been entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-seventh of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and fourteen times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and thirty, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and forty-six days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and sixty, and six hundred and twenty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard at about ten with his flat book under his own left arm, and he said one thing out loud, in about 57 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got twenty-one mornings written down on that page on which a man has been at the foot of that bank, and about four of you have worked out that I count mornings and not men, and that this is the first line I have ever entered in that book that is about one man, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard asked me to enter it and that I entered it, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has ever said to me when that length of wood will be cut and that I am not going to ask him, and about four of you have worked out that he has been at that near leg on eleven mornings and that I have entered every one of those, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the number of twenty-one and that she entered it after he had said it out loud and not before, and entered that she entered no reason for it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 57 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I entered that number of mornings at the top of this bank and I entered no date beside it, and about four of you have worked out that a man who keeps a tally said it out loud in that yard and that I entered it after him, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that a man being at the foot of that bank twenty-one mornings is not a fourteenth thing this district has made, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that it is not paid and that the count of the five is still five, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that the clerk is not in it either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about twenty to ten with his own two hands on that near leg and his own shoulder against the far rail, and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning and he cut nothing. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood about nine feet off the near end of that table from about ten until about half past eleven with nothing at all in his own hands, and he did not put a hand on that table on that morning and nobody in that yard said one word to him about it.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail that was already along that side, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a table was being worked on about nine feet off him on this morning and that he did not move his own spade and that nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man who keeps a tally said out loud this morning that he has got twenty-one mornings written down about one man, and that about four of them have said that the road keeper came up that lane afterwards and was not asked about the tally and was not asked about the man, and that he has never once been asked about either.

That cart came up that bank at about three with its near wheel still dragging, and the man who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and carried on up and did not stay at the top of that bank. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about a quarter to four with his own two hands still on that near leg when the cart went up, and he went on up that lane about half an hour after it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud at the top of that bank this morning about twenty-one mornings, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and seventy nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and seventy-first night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and seventy of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and eighty days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and thirty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and forty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and nobody in that yard has said it out loud twice. Four hundred and ten days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and thirteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and forty-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and eighty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and seventy-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a tally of twenty-one mornings about one man is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a piece of wood lying along the top of a table is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and twenty-one. A page in a hand this district does not know is not a document this district owns, and the count of those is four and did not move; the count of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move; a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. That shop up that bank is open, its door propped, with three pages on its counter, and it has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one of the four conditions with no end on it, the count of them is four and did not move this morning, and that line is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said a number out loud in that yard. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man of about thirty-four is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a tally of mornings is not a payment and a clerk entering a number a man gave her is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it and past that length of wood lying along the top of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man was at that near leg at about ten and another man at that far leg at about eleven and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the sixteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-six, and that a man who keeps a tally said a thing out loud in that yard at about ten and that she entered what he said and entered no reason, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is sixteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
