# Chapter 745: The Twenty-Ninth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Stranger Came Down That Lane Again And A Clerk Entered Eighteen

A man came down that lane at about half past ten on the twenty-ninth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month with a page in his own right hand, went on down to the foot of that bank, stood about nine feet off the far end of that second table, read the five lines on that lot book, did not look at either rail and said not one word to anybody there, and went back up that lane about half an hour later. That first table at the top of that bank had nothing on it, and about four people had come up that bank before eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and seventy-third morning after the count.

That second table was at the foot of that bank with a rail along the far side of it and a rail along the near side of it that has been there nine mornings, the stone lying face up on it with the hollow one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and two pages along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two. That lot book lay open on it with five lines on it, the fifth of them in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth still nine words in that same hand, and about four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody has ever said out loud what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and eighty-four days as the age of that figure, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seventeen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-nine this morning and that it moved this morning from fifty-eight, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and again at about four, got all four of them both times, and a clerk entered them once. That wall was read out loud to about four people at about a quarter to eight and to about nine at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason has ever been entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventieth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and forty times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and forty-three, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and fifty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and seventy-three, and six hundred and thirty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The boy of about nineteen was at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man came down that lane at about half past ten this morning with a page in his own right hand and he went on down to the foot of this bank and stood about nine feet off that far end and read those five lines, and about four of you have worked out that he did not look at either rail and that he said not one word to anybody standing there, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard sent him, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered no name in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud because that stranger did not read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine with his own right hand wrapped in his own handkerchief, and he said one thing out loud, in about 46 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and nineteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a rail on the near side of that table and there has been one on the far side of it since the morning that table came down this bank, and about four of you have worked out that a man stood at that table this morning and read that fifth line and did not ask me one thing about that rail, and about four of you have worked out that that table is not coming up this bank this year, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to say when it will and I am not going to say whether it will, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a table at the foot of that bank is not coming up that bank this year, and entered no date beside it and entered no name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 63 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eighteen and it was seventeen at about a quarter to eight this morning, and about four of you have worked out that I entered that after a boy said it out loud in this yard and not before, and about four of you have worked out that a stranger is not a resident of this district and that I entered no name, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-nine and that I entered that and entered no reason, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty-one and that the man who keeps a tally was not in that yard this morning, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that no person was added to this district this morning, and entered that the count of things this district has made did not move this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off that near end from about ten until about half past eleven with nothing at all in his own hands and a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat, and he said nothing out loud in that yard. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the near rail, and nobody in that yard asked that man one thing about what he is to be used for.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a stranger stood at that table this morning and read five lines about nine feet off the man that line names, and that about four of them have said that he did not look at either rail and that he said not one word, and that about four of them have said that a clerk entered that count of seventeen into eighteen only after a boy said it out loud, and that the fifth of those five lines is face up on that book and working exactly as it was written and that the man it names is still not paid.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that nobody told the road keeper one thing about that stranger.

At about three a cart came up that bank with its near wheel dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that lane without stopping at the top of that bank. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went on up that lane about half an hour after the cart, and his own right hand was open that morning and about nine inches of the skin was gone at the base of the thumb.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a stranger read five lines at that table at about half past ten and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eighty-three nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eighty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and eighty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and ninety-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and forty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and fifty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and about four of them have said that a man saying a table is not coming up a bank is not a rule and that nobody entered it as such. Four hundred and twenty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and twenty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and sixty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Three hundred and two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eighty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a stranger with a page in his own right hand is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a man saying out loud that a table is not coming up a bank is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

The number of documents this district does not own is four and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four, and neither figure moved this morning, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. That shop up that bank has its door propped and three pages on its counter and has been open on every morning of this year, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is a condition with no end on it, one of the four of them, the count of them is four and did not move, and that line is face up on that book working exactly as it was written and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a stranger came down that lane. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and the stranger is not in it and the man who puts tables up is not in it and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud that a table is not coming up a bank is not a payment and a clerk entering eighteen is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventieth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-ninth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-nine and that it moved this morning from fifty-eight, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is thirty-one and that she entered it and that the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was not in that yard that morning and she entered no reason, and that a stranger came down that lane this morning with a page in his own right hand and read those five lines and that she entered no name because a stranger is not a resident, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eighteen and that it moved this morning because a boy of about nineteen said it out loud in that yard at about ten before she entered it, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked the man at the foot of that bank one thing about a man who read that book about nine feet off him, and the fifth of those five lines is on that book working exactly as it was written, and the man it names is still not paid, and a stranger came down that lane this morning and read it and went back up.
