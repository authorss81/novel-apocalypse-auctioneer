# Chapter 739: The Twenty-Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Boy Said Out Loud About A Man With Wire In His Own Pocket

The light lay along the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the twenty-third morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and there was nothing on those boards at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and sixty-seventh morning after the count.

Face up at the foot of that bank was that second table with a rail along the far side of it and a rail along the near side of it, and the stone lay on it with its hollow one inch deep in the middle of it and nothing at either end, and two pages lay along the end of it with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book lay open on it with five lines on it, the fifth in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth nine words in the same hand. Nobody had put a mark on either rail and nobody had cut either one again, and the near rail had been on that table for three mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years stood at the near end of that first table and entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and seventy-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seventeen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-three this morning and that it moved this morning from fifty-two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The four off that wall were said out loud twice that morning in the ordinary voice by the man of fifty-six, and he got all four of them both times, and a clerk entered them once, and about four people were close enough to hear him at about a quarter to eight and about nine of them were within hearing at about four. Neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and there is no reason on any page in this district for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-fourth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and twenty-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and thirty-seven, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and fifty-three days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and sixty-seven, and six hundred and twenty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The boy of about nineteen was at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 56 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a man at the foot of that bank about nine feet off that near end every morning of this month and he has not done one thing to that table, and about four of you have worked out that he has a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat and that he has not taken it out on any morning of this month, and about four of you have worked out that he has a gap in the fencing at his own yard about the length of his own two hands and that he has not been at it, and about four of you have worked out that he has not said one word in this yard on any of these mornings and that I am not going to say why, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man of about thirty-four is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that nobody was appointed to it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-six and it did not move this morning, and about four of you have worked out that he was in that yard at about three yesterday with nothing in his own two hands and that he was not at the foot of that bank this morning at all, and about four of you have worked out that I entered that and entered no reason, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that it is not paid and that the count of the five is still five, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank did not move this morning and that she entered no reason for it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about ten and went down it and stood about nine feet off the near end of that second table until about half past eleven with nothing at all in his own hands, and there was a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat and he did not take it out and he did not say one word in that yard on that morning. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was not at the foot of that bank on that morning at all, and he came up that lane at about half past two and stood in that yard at about three with nothing at all in his own two hands and his own right hand wrapped in his own handkerchief, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about either of those two things.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man has been at the foot of that bank on twenty-six mornings since the morning that table came down this bank, and that about four of them have said that he was not there this morning and that this is the only morning of this fortnight on which that table has stood at the foot of that bank with nobody working at it, and that about four of them have said that the two rails on that table have been on it for three mornings and that a man in that yard has not looked at them this morning, and that about four of them have said that about four of the nineteen people in that yard have come down that bank for something and that one of them has been at the near end of that table for two hours with a length of wire in his own pocket.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at about half past one and that a man came up it at about half past two and that neither of them said one word to anybody at the top of that bank.

That cart came up at about three with its near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stay at the top of it. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went on up that lane about half an hour after the cart and did not stop at the top of that bank.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a boy said a thing out loud this morning about a length of wire in a man's own pocket, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and seventy-seven nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and seventy-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and seventy-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and eighty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and forty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and forty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and about four of them have said that a man fitting a rail to a table is not a rule and that it did not become one this morning. Four hundred and seventeen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and twenty days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and fifty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and ninety-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eighty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man standing at a table doing nothing to it is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a boy saying so out loud is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and twenty-eight. Nobody in that yard counted a fifth document or a fifth place this morning, and the four documents this district does not own and the four places in this district where those three lines can be read are both where they were at about a quarter to eight, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list. That shop up that bank stands open with three pages on its counter and its door propped, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

One of the four conditions with no end on it is the fifth of those five lines, and the count of them is four and did not move, and that fifth line is on that book at the foot of that bank still working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning, and the count of the four did not move on the morning a man was not at the foot of that bank. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a boy said a thing out loud about a man with a length of wire in his own pocket. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man of about thirty-four is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man standing at a table doing nothing is not a payment and a boy saying a thing out loud about him is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she came down that bank at about ten to six and that there was no man at that table this morning except a man who does not touch tables, and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-fourth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-third morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-three, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-six and did not move this morning, and that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard at about ten about a man with a length of fencing wire in his own pocket and that she entered what he said and entered no reason and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seventeen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
