# Chapter 728: The Twelfth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Read The Marks In Chalk Along The Edge Of That Table And Did Not Put One In

There was no rime on the boards of that first table at the top of that bank on the twelfth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and fifty-sixth morning after the count. The second table was at the foot of that bank face up with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it with the copy at the near end of those two, and that lot book was lying open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand. About nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings without once putting a thumb in it, and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years set her own page on the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight and entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and sixty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fifteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-two this morning and that it moved this morning from forty-one, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that she entered that number at about a quarter to eight and that the man who cuts reeds was at the foot of that bank two hours after she wrote it and that she was not there to see it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without his looking at that wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. Nobody has ever entered how many people were within hearing of that wall, and what is entered is about four at about a quarter to eight and about nine at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-third of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and twenty-six, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and forty-two days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and fifty-six, and six hundred and seventeen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

A man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds stood at the far end of that second table at about ten with his own finger an inch above the marks in chalk along the edge of it and did not put a mark in himself, and he counted them and said one thing out loud, in about 46 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted those marks in chalk with my own finger an inch above them and I did not put one in and I am not going to, and about four of you have worked out that the figures in chalk have been going along that edge of that table every morning since it came down this bank, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has ever asked me to add one and that nobody has ever asked me what they are for, and about four of you have worked out that a mark in chalk is a thing a person does and not a thing this district has, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not put a mark in and that nobody asked him to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 56 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are marks in chalk along the edge of that second table and there are marks cut into that board at the top of that bank and both of those figures moved one this morning and neither of them is a thing this district has made, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that a mark in chalk is not a fourteenth, and about four of you have worked out that the count of documents this district does not own is four and that chalk is not a document, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid and that the count of the five is still five, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a mark in chalk is not a payment and did not pay the fifth of the five, and that the count of the five is still five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off the near end of that second table at the foot of that bank at about ten, his own hands empty, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. At the far end of that second table at about eleven a man of about thirty-one who digs loam had his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no. The man who puts tables up was not at the foot of that bank this morning and was in that yard at about three with nothing at all in his own two hands.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who cuts reeds has stood at that table twice this block and counted those marks both times, and that about four of them have said that he has not put a mark in on either morning and that nobody has ever asked him to, and that about four of them have said that a man has been in that yard at about three every afternoon for as long as anybody in that yard has been counting, and that this morning he was not at the foot of that bank at all.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he has never once been asked to read those four figures aloud to anybody in that yard.
The cart came up that bank at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up, set the shafts down at the top of that bank, and went back down it at about a quarter to four with his own hands back on the shafts. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went on up that lane after the cart had gone by and that is the hour that yard has seen him at since the second of January.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who cuts reeds said one thing out loud at the foot of that bank this morning about marks in chalk and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and sixty-six nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and sixty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and sixty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and seventy-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and thirty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and thirty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and forty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and eighty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and seventy-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man counting marks in chalk with his own finger is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a clerk saying a thing out loud about chalk is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man who will not put a mark in has not made a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and seventeen. The number of documents this district does not own stands at four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read stands at four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. Three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

There are four conditions with no end on it and the count did not move this morning, and the fifth of those five lines is one of those four, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man counted marks and did not put one in. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who cuts reeds is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who puts tables up is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man counting marks in chalk is not a payment and a clerk saying a thing out loud about chalk is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the marks in chalk were along the edge of that table when she came down it at about ten to six and that she did not look at them and that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifty-third morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twelfth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is forty-two, and that a man who cuts reeds said a thing out loud in that yard and that she entered that he said it and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fifteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
