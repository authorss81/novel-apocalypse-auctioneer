# Chapter 741: The Twenty Fifth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Took A Length Of Wire Out Of His Own Pocket And Put It Back In

The boards of that first table at the top of that bank were bare and dry and hard on the twenty-fifth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and there was nothing on them at all, and about four people had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that was the two hundred and sixty-ninth morning after the count.

Face up at the foot of that bank was that second table, with a rail along the far side of it where it has stood since the morning that table came down that bank and a rail along the near side of it that has stood on it five mornings, and the stone lying on it with its hollow one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end of it, and two pages along the end of that table with the copy at the near end of those two. That lot book lay open on it with five lines on it, the fifth of them in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and untouched, and the fourth of them still nine words in that same hand. Nothing has been cut on that table since the length of wood came off it on the nineteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, and about four people at that low wall have said since that nobody has ever put a thumb in that hollow and that nobody in that yard has ever said out loud what it is for.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years stood at the near end of that first table with her own page flat on those bare boards and entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered four hundred and eighty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seventeen at about a quarter to eight and did not move. She entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-five this morning and that it moved this morning from fifty-four, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice at about a quarter to eight and again at about four, got all four of them both times, and a clerk entered them once. About four people were close enough to hear him at about a quarter to eight and about nine of them were within hearing at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and there is no reason on any page in this district for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-sixth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud nine hundred and thirty-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries six hundred and thirty-nine, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and fifty-five days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and sixty-nine, and six hundred and thirty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The boy of about nineteen was at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a man at the foot of that bank who had a length of fencing wire out of his own pocket for about two minutes this morning and has put it back in and has not gone near that table, and about four of you have worked out that he has a gap in the fencing at his own yard about the length of his own two hands and that he has not been at it on any morning of this month, and about four of you have worked out that he has not said one word in this yard on any of these mornings and that I am not going to say why, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a length of wire was taken out of a man's own pocket and put back in and was not used, and entered no name in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 43 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eleven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-five and it moved this morning from fifty-four, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-eight and that I entered that on the word of a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally and that I entered no reason and entered no hour, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that it did not move this morning, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine with nothing at all in his own two hands, and that the count of the thirteen did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table from about half past nine with his own right hand wrapped in his own handkerchief and his own left hand flat on that near leg, and there was nothing on that table for him to do and he has not cut anything on it since the nineteenth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about nine feet off that near end at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat, and he took that wire out at about ten and looked at it and put it back in and did not go near that table, and he said nothing out loud in that yard on that morning, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man has stood at that table every morning of this month with a length of wire in his own pocket and that about four of them have said that he has not been asked for one thing and has not offered one thing, and that is the whole of what they have got.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that the near rail on that table went on five mornings ago in about ten minutes with a man's own two hands, and that about four of them have said that a man has been at the foot of that bank on twenty-eight mornings since that table came down it, and that he is not thanked and that he is not paid, and that about four of them have said that a man stood about nine feet off that near end this morning with a length of wire in his own pocket, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what a length of wire in a pocket is for.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what he thinks of two rails on that table.

At about three a cart came up that bank with its near wheel dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that lane without stopping at the top of that bank. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at that near leg when the cart came and went on up that lane about half an hour after it, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about the cart or about the man who drove it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man took a length of wire out of his own pocket at the foot of that bank this morning and put it back in and did not use it, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eighty nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eightieth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and seventy-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and eighty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and forty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and forty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood, and about four of them have said that a rail fitted by a man is not a rule and that it did not become one this morning. Four hundred and nineteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that figure counts the mornings that have gone by and not the mornings a month has in it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and twenty-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and fifty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and ninety-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eighty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man with a length of wire in his own pocket standing nine feet off a table is not a fourteenth thing this district has made and a boy saying so out loud is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at six hundred and thirty. The number of documents this district does not own is four and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four, and neither figure moved this morning, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning. Three pages lie on the counter of that shop up that bank and its door is propped open and it has not shut on a morning of this year, and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

There are four conditions with no end on it and the fifth of those five lines is one of them, and that count did not move this morning, and that line is face up on that book at the foot of that bank working exactly as it was written, and no hand went on it this morning, and nobody in that yard proposed a fifth. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a boy said a thing out loud about a length of wire. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a man of about thirty-four is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man with a length of wire in his own pocket is not a payment and a boy naming him out loud is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she read her three lines and did not look at the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixty-sixth morning, and that he read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in that yard on the twenty-fifth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fifty-five, and that the number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is twenty-eight and that she entered it on the word of a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally and entered no reason and entered no hour, and that a man took a length of fencing wire out of his own pocket at the foot of that bank at about ten and put it back in and used none of it, and that she entered that and entered no reason and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seventeen and that it did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked the man at the foot of that bank one thing about the rail, or the morning it went on, or what a length of wire in a pocket is for, and the fifth of those five lines is on that book working exactly as it was written, and the man it names is still not paid, and that table is not coming up that bank.
