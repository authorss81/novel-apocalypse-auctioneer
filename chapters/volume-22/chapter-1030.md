# Chapter 1030: The Three Hundred And Fourteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And The Man Of Thirty-Eight Said One Figure Of His Own Out Loud, Once, In The Ordinary Voice, On A Morning When She Had Said Nothing At All And Nobody Had Said Anything At All, And Nothing In That Yard Came Back At Him And Nobody Thanked Him And Nobody Asked Him Anything And The Page Took No Line

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this basin has ever put its size of error anywhere. Seven hundred and sixty-nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries nine hundred and twenty-eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand two hundred and forty-four days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and fifty-eight days. Nine hundred and nineteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and nothing is going to run it. Six hundred and thirty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book this yard has got. Six hundred and thirty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Seven hundred and eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. A body four hundred miles off is six hundred and forty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and eleven days back and no night has been named. Five hundred and eighty-seven marks have been cut off that board, and five hundred and seventy-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table.

Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is on nothing whatever. Four places here can read those three lines. Four documents are not owned in this district. Four conditions have no end on it. Fourteen things is what this district has made and five is what it does not have, and the fifth of the five is unpaid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and the page at the back of that lot book was not a place a reason could go until the two hundred and eighth morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

Nobody said a reason out loud in that yard by anybody this morning, so the page at the back of that lot book stood at sixty-one lines and carried sixty-one lines when she shut it. The lane count is thirty and it did not move and nothing came down that lane. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and forty-four mornings, and the near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and ninety-four mornings, and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and fourteen mornings, on the word of the man who keeps it, and he said that number before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood there for the three hundred and thirteenth morning, and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The man of fifty-six has read four figures off that wall on the seven hundred and fifty-fifth of those mornings. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and sixty-ninth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and sixty-eight of them, and this is the one hundred and seventieth morning running of nobody asking him anything. The man of fifty-six's flat answer is the two hundred and third time running.

That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The page at the back carries sixty-one lines. The record has stood two hundred and twenty-nine days.

A man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and fifty-ninth afternoon running. About nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning. There are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles is between the top of that bank and the culvert.

The toll of ninety-eight is paid and spent and is not going to be raised, lowered or paid a second time. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read by anybody in this district.

---

Thirty, and there is no other figure coming this morning, and that is the whole of what he has to say at that rail today.

He says that second part out loud as well, because about four people are standing about nine feet off him waiting for the rest of it, and then he says there is no figure after it either.

That is what he says on the mornings there is no figure.

---

He set his own table up at about half past eight because it goes up before the light gets on it, and he carried the last ten feet of it on his own shoulder, and he put the cloth on the top of it and went along the boards the whole length and back.

Then three men came down that slope at nine with the second table between them and the rail riding along on the near side of it, and the man at that end held his heel on a wedge until they were past and then dropped the wedge by the leg.

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight came down off that bank with his hands showing and went out along the top of his own table and round the end of it and up the slope and back down and in at the same corner.

---

About half past twelve she had come down that bank with the basket on her arm and set up about four feet off the near end of the second table and worked there all afternoon and said nothing out loud at any point of that day.

He had put four bars on his own table and a cloth along the top of it and he had heard her scale take the wind and settle on about every one of them, and not one figure of hers had come out of that yard, and there is no telling afterwards whether that was one morning or a run of them.

---

He noticed it at half past one the way a person notices that a cart has not come.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally has put a number out into about four hundred yards of air at about half past one for longer than he has been standing at his own table. It comes at half past one every morning, it goes up the bank, about four people say it back after him out of step with one another, it comes down again, and then that yard carries on.

At half past one on this morning that yard went on doing what it had been doing.

He stood at the near end of his own table with the cloth over his arm and listened past it, and the wheel went round at the near end of that ground at the speed it goes at, and the second table went along its own boards about four feet off him, and the light on the top of that woman's pan moved a little because the wind was still on it.

---

Nothing had gone out.

There was the wind and the wheel and the light moving along the ground, and about nineteen people in that yard doing the nine things they had been doing the day before, and no number had come out of it and none of the four voices had come out of it, and he stood there and worked out from the ground that nothing at all had been put into that air.

---

At about a quarter to two a figure of his own went out of that yard.

About nine feet off him, at the near end of the first table, a man of thirty-eight put one figure of his own out into that yard, once, in the ordinary voice, at the speed a man uses for a thing he means.

Then the yard carried on.

---

Nothing came back out of that yard at him.

Nobody thanked him and nobody asked him anything, and nobody at the top of eleven feet called down about it, and nobody at the boards turned round on the rail, and the woman at the scale about four feet off the near end of the second table went on with her own pan, and the wheel at the near end of that ground went on turning at the speed it goes at through the whole of it.

About nine people heard it.

And then there was nothing in that air at all for the rest of that afternoon.

---

That is the shape of that morning and he went back to his own table with the cloth and carried on down it.

It went out into about four hundred yards of air and about four hundred yards of air is the whole of the room that anything ever gets into in that place, and there was nothing at the far side of it to arrive at.

He has thought about that more since than about anything else in that week. A figure of a man's own, once, in the ordinary voice, about nine feet off him, in a yard with about nineteen people in it, and not one of them said one word about it and not one of them said one word about anything else either. It went out over the ground and over the second table and over the low wall and out along the strip of cold stone and up the face of that bank, and then it was not in the world.

There was nothing before it and there was nothing after it. He had spent a good part of that afternoon going over a figure of his own that he has been carrying since the morning that table went up at the foot of that bank, and a figure of his own went out of that yard at a quarter to two that afternoon and not one thing in that yard did anything about it, and there was nothing about that either way in his own head.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he has said a figure out loud.

They say it up there, four feet from the boards, among themselves, and never in front of him. About four of those four people have not said what a figure of his own is about, and about four of them have not asked him, and about four of them have not said what anybody ought to do about it.

---

At about half past three the sun came over the top of the low wall and laid itself down along the stones at the foot of it, and a man standing in that strip of it said good afternoon to a man of sixty-one sitting about four feet off him on the cold stones, and got one word back, and neither of them said anything else.

---

The last reading of that wall came at about four o'clock, and he got all four of them off it as he has got all four of them off it every morning of that run, and his hand came down and lay flat on the stone under the lowest one.

"Two hundred and three mornings of saying this out loud to a stone," he said to that stone.

About nineteen people heard that for the two hundred and third time running and nobody asked him anything about any of it.

---

At about ten past four she came along the boards with the book in her hand, stopped where she stops, and gave that morning out loud before the pen was near any part of the page.

"The three hundred and fourteenth morning," she said. "Nothing has gone out loud in this yard this morning that goes at the back of that book, so this morning goes down in my own hand on its own with nothing in it, and that is the whole of the account of it and it went out loud before the writing of it."

She wrote the three hundred and fourteenth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and took no line for it, and the page carried sixty-one lines when she shut the book and carried sixty-one lines after.

---

The light came off those boards at about half past four, and about four people out of about nineteen said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stood where they were until the boards came down off the cart face down.

He put the cloth over his shoulder and went up that bank, and the light was still on the top of it when he got there, and about four feet off the near end of the first table a man in a coat was taking a cloth off the corner of his own table and putting it away.

---

Nothing said anything after him and nothing said anything before him, and the page at the back of that lot book took no line on the three hundred and fourteenth morning and stood at sixty-one lines, and the lane count is thirty and nothing came down that lane. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read. The fifth of the five is unpaid. The count of counted months is seven and is on nothing. That gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and its size of error is uncomputed, unexplained and unpaid.
