# Chapter 1019: The Three Hundred And Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And The Man Of About Thirty-Four Said One Reason Out Loud Once At About Ten To Twelve About Four Feet Off The Near End Of The Second Table And One Line Went On The Page At The Back Of That Lot Book Carrying The Morning And Not The Reason And Nobody Answered Him And Nobody Thanked Him And The Man Of Thirty-Eight Was At The Near End Of That First Table All Day And Said Nothing Out Loud At All

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and fifty-eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries nine hundred and seventeen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand two hundred and thirty-three days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and forty-seven days. Nine hundred and eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and is not open. Six hundred and twenty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book this district is. Six hundred and twenty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and ninety-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. A body four hundred miles off is six hundred and thirty-six days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred days back and no night has been named. Five hundred and seventy-six marks have been cut off that board, and five hundred and sixty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table.

Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is on nothing. Four documents this district does not own. Four places in this district can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it. Fourteen is what this district has made and five is what it does not have, and the fifth of the five is unpaid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

One reason was said out loud by the man of about thirty-four at about ten to twelve this morning, once, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and the page at the back of that lot book took one line for it and the line carries the morning and does not carry the reason. Nobody answered him and nobody thanked him. The lane count is thirty and it did not move and nothing came down that lane.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and thirty-three mornings and the near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and eighty-three mornings, and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and three mornings, on the word of the man who keeps it, and he said that number before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the three hundred and second morning. The man of fifty-six has read four figures off that wall on the seven hundred and forty-fourth of those mornings. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and fifty-eighth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and fifty-seven of them, and this is the one hundred and fifty-ninth morning running of nobody asking him anything. The man of fifty-six's flat answer is the hundred and ninety-second time running.

That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The page at the back carried fifty-nine lines and carried sixty lines after the line that went on it this afternoon. The record has stood two hundred and eighteen days.

A man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and forty-eighth afternoon running. About nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning. There are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles is between the top of that bank and the culvert.

The toll of ninety-eight stands paid and spent and will not be raised and will not be paid a second time. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read in this district.

---

At about half past nine he came down off that bank with his hands showing, which is what they do on a slope, and he went out along the top of his own table and round the end of it and up the bank and back down and in at the same corner.

His coat is over the back of the stool at the near end of that table. It has been over the back of that stool for as long as the stool has been at that end of that table.

The stool is there because there was a job at that end of that table once that needed sitting down for, and the job is not there any more, and the stool has stayed.

---

He has nine years of that round and he has never once done it in a hurry and he has never once done it waiting for anything. Out along the top and round the end of it and up the bank and back down and in at the same corner.

It is about the length of that yard and it takes him about as long as it takes the light to move off the boards and on to them again, and he has never measured it and has never once thought of measuring it.

---

The wind came over the top of that bank about eleven and went along the top of his boards and lifted the loose edge of the cloth on his own table and dropped it again, and he went over it with the flat of his hand and carried on.

Nine years of that round. Out along the top and round the end and up the bank and back down and in at the same corner, and the corner is where you come in and it is not a different corner at the end of the day than it was at the start of it.

---

At about ten to twelve the man of about thirty-four said something out loud.

It came out of him about four feet off the near end of the second table, in the ordinary voice, at the speed a man uses for a thing he means, and it was one reason and it did not take long to say.

He heard all of it. He was at the near end of his own table at the time with the cloth in his hand and he was not doing anything else, and about four feet of that yard was all that was between the two of them.

---

Nobody answered him.

Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him to say it again and nobody asked him anything else, and the wheel went on turning at the near end of that ground through the whole of it, and the woman at the scale about four feet off the near end of the second table went on with her own pan.

Then the man of about thirty-four went on standing where he was, and he did not say anything else that day, and nobody asked him about the thing he had said.

---

He went on along the top of his own table and round the end of it and up the bank and back down and in at the same corner.

There is a thing that happens in that yard when a person says a reason out loud, and it takes about half an hour to happen, and it happens at the boards.

---

The rest of that afternoon he went along the top of his own table four more times and the wind came round off the top of that bank and took the loose edge of that cloth twice and he went over it with the flat of his hand both times.

At about half past three the sun came over the low wall and laid itself down along the stones, and a man standing in that strip of it had been standing in it since about nine, and a man of sixty-one was sitting about four feet off him on the cold stones with his coat folded beside him.

---

At about ten past four the clerk came along the boards with the book in her hand and stopped where she stops and gave the account of that morning out loud before she had the pen near any part of it.

"The three hundred and third morning," she said. "One reason was said out loud in this yard this morning, at about ten to twelve, at this end of this ground. One line goes on the back of that book for it, and the line carries this morning and does not carry what was said in it. That is the account of it and it went out loud before the writing of it."

He was at the near end of the first table and he heard the whole of that and he did not move while she was saying it.

Then she wrote the three hundred and third morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and took a line for it.

---

He stood at the near end of his own table and watched her hand go along the page for about as long as it takes to write one line, and then he watched her shut the book and put the pen back into the holder.

There was a worn place in that paper about four inches in from the foot of the page and her line went over the top of it and did not sit level with the lines above it. She left it where it was.

---

He put the cloth down on the corner of his own table and took his coat off the back of the stool and put it on.

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that not one of the four has said what a figure of his own is about.

That is the nearest that yard has ever come to it and it has been said about four feet from the boards and not once in front of him.

---

There was a morning not long back when he put a figure of his own into that air himself, once, in the ordinary voice, and about four hundred yards of air took it and gave nothing back, and the wheel went on turning through the whole of that as well.

He has not been to the boards about it since. He has not gone up that hill with anything in his hand and he has not carried anything down it either. There is nothing in that yard that is his to carry anywhere, and he has not asked anybody for one, and nobody in that yard has offered him one.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six was at his own wall with his hand up on the stone, and got all four of them off it, and laid his hand flat on the stone under the lowest one and left it lying there.

"Whatever these four are for, it is not a thing this yard has been told," he said out loud to that stone.

About nineteen people heard that for the hundred and ninety-second time running and nobody asked him anything about it.

---

The light came off those boards at about half past four and about four people out of about nineteen of them said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not join in and stood where they were until the boards came down off the cart face down.

He said it. He has said that goodnight every morning of that run for nine years and nobody has ever said one word back to him for it, and tonight he said it standing at the near end of his own table with his coat on and then he went up that bank.

He went up it behind a man with a barrow and in front of a woman with a basket, and the light was still on the top of it when he got there, and he did not stop at the top.

At the top of that bank there is a place where you can stand and look back down at the whole of that yard, and he has stood at it on a good number of evenings, and what is to be seen from up there is the ground and the two tables and the low wall and the strip of stone at the foot of it and about nine people who have not gone home yet.

---

The clerk shut the book at about ten past four and the page at the back of that lot book carried fifty-nine lines before she wrote that afternoon and carried sixty lines after, and not one of the sixty is in that lot book. The man of about thirty-four said one reason out loud at about ten to twelve and was answered by nobody and was thanked by nobody. The man of thirty-eight was at the near end of the first table from about half past nine until the light went off that bank and said nothing out loud at all. The lane count is thirty and nothing came down that lane. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read. The fifth of the five is unpaid. The count of counted months is seven and is on nothing.
