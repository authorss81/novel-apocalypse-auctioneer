# Chapter 1027: The Three Hundred And Eleventh Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And She Said One Figure Of Her Own Out Loud At About Half Past One At Her Own Scale And Inside A Minute After It The Man Of Thirty-Eight Said One Figure Of His Own Out Loud, Once, In The Ordinary Voice, And The Page At The Back Of That Lot Book Took No Line For Either

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nobody has ever computed how far off it is. Seven hundred and sixty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries nine hundred and twenty-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand two hundred and forty-one days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and fifty-five days. Nine hundred and sixteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and will not be one. Six hundred and thirty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book the writing in this district has got. Six hundred and thirty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Seven hundred and five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. A body four hundred miles off is six hundred and forty-four days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and eight days back and no night has been named. Five hundred and eighty-four marks have been cut off that board, and five hundred and seventy marks in chalk along the edge of that second table.

Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is on nothing. Four documents are not owned in this district. Four places in this district can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it. Fourteen is what this district has made and five is what it does not have, and the fifth of the five is unpaid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and it was before the page at the back of that lot book could take one, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

Nobody said a reason out loud in that yard this morning, and so the page at the back of that lot book stood at sixty-one lines and carried sixty-one lines when she shut it. The lane count is thirty and it did not move and nothing came down that lane. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and forty-one mornings, and the near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and ninety-one mornings, and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and eleven mornings, on the word of the man who keeps it, and he said that number before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood there for the three hundred and tenth morning, and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The man of fifty-six has read four figures off that wall on the seven hundred and fifty-second of those mornings. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and sixty-sixth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and sixty-five of them, and this is the one hundred and sixty-seventh morning running of nobody asking him anything. The man of fifty-six's flat answer is the two hundredth time running.

That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The page at the back carries sixty-one lines. The record has stood two hundred and twenty-six days.

A man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and fifty-sixth afternoon running. About nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning. There are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles is between the top of that bank and the culvert.

The toll of ninety-eight is paid and spent and will not be raised and will not be paid twice. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read by anybody in this district.

---

The wind came over the top of that bank about eleven and stayed there all afternoon.

She came down that bank at about half past twelve, set up about four feet off the near end of the second table with the basket on her arm and the cloth off her own scale, and put a bar on her own pan.

The pan wanted to follow it. She let it want to go until it came still, and then she read the figure and wrote it on her own account and put the bar back in the basket and took the next one out.

---

Two more bars before the light came round onto the ground.

That is all the difference there is, and she has nine years of it and no way of telling in advance which mornings it will happen on. There is no signal in her body that anybody could read from the outside. She has looked.

The third bar went on and the fourth went on and the wind took the first of every one of those pans and she let it have it.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number into about four hundred yards of air, in the voice he keeps for it, and it went up that bank.

It went up the bank and four voices took it up out of step with one another and it came back down. She had the bar on the pan and she waited for it, because there is a place in the middle of that afternoon where a number comes out of that yard every morning and she has got a habit that sits either side of it.

Then she said a figure of her own out loud.

---

She said it once, in the ordinary voice, at her own scale, in about nineteen people's hearing, and it went out over that yard and off the face of that bank.

She did not shout it. That is not a thing she has ever done and there was no need for it. She said it the way a person says a thing they mean and are not asking anybody anything about, and it was out of that yard inside a second.

---

Inside a minute a second figure went out of that yard, out of the ground about nine feet from where she stood, and it was one figure of a man's own, said once, in the ordinary voice, at the speed a man uses for a thing he means.

The man of thirty-eight was at the corner he comes in at with the cloth in his hand.

She did not move. Her hand was on the rim of her own scale and the bar was on the pan and the wind was on both of them, and she heard the whole of it go out into that air and there was not one thing after it.

---

Nobody said a word.

Nobody at the foot of that bank joined one thing to the other one. Nobody at the boards turned round on the rail. Nobody at the top of eleven feet called down. The wheel went on turning at the near end of that ground at the speed it goes at, the man at the far end of the second table kept his head over his bench, and the man of about thirty-two went along the top of his own table and did not break stride.

One figure went out of that yard and a second figure went out of it after that, and the yard carried on with the middle of its afternoon exactly as it had been doing.

---

She lifted the bar off her own pan, put it back in the basket and had the next one out of it in one movement.

That is all she did about the pair of them. She did not go over to where he was. She had nine years of coming down that slope and not one morning of them had a thing in it that she did not already have a way of doing, and there was nothing in her about that one.

---

What she went back over afterwards was the wind.

The wind had been on that pan and on that cloth and along the top of that bank for two hours before half past one, and it had not dropped and it had not risen, and there was nothing in the air that had changed between the moment her own figure went out and the moment the second one did.

She has thought about that for as long as she weighed four more things, which was the rest of that afternoon.

---

Nothing on that page took a line for either of them.

She knew that by about four, the way everybody in that yard knows it, which is to say she heard the clerk read the morning out loud at the boards and the reading had nothing in it about either figure, and the pen went along the page once for the morning and the page was the same when the covers of that book went over it.

---

The man of thirty-eight was still at the near end of the first table with the cloth in his hand when the light came off those boards.

Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him anything. He put the cloth down on the corner of his own table, the way he puts it down at the end of every day of that run, and he went up that bank behind a man with a barrow.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that not one of them has said what a figure of his own is about, and that they have said it among themselves and never in front of her and never in front of him.

About four of those four people did not know what a figure of her own is about either, and there is not one word in this yard that connects the two of those sentences.

---

A little after half past three the sun came over the top of that low wall and put a strip of light down along the cold stones, and the man who had been standing in the shade at the foot of it said good afternoon to the man of sixty-one about four feet off him, and one word came back.

---

At about four o'clock he was still at that wall with his hand up on the stone, and he got all four of them off it, and then he put his hand flat on the stone under the lowest one and left it lying there covering about half of it.

"I have said this two hundred mornings and it is the two hundredth this morning," he said out loud to that stone.

About nineteen people heard that for the two hundredth time running and nobody asked him anything about any of it.

---

She came along the boards at about ten past four with that book open against the rail and gave the account of that morning out loud, and the pen was in the holder while she gave it.

"The three hundred and eleventh morning," she said. "Nothing has gone out loud in this yard this morning that goes at the back of that book, so the morning goes down in my own hand with nothing in it, and that is the account of it and it went out loud before the writing of it."

She wrote the three hundred and eleventh morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and took no line for it, and the page carried sixty-one lines when she shut the book and carried sixty-one lines after.

---

At about half past four the light went off those boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard went along the bank said by about four people out of about nineteen of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not join in.

She tied the cloth over her own scale and knotted it, put the basket back on her arm, and went up that bank in front of about nineteen people.

---

At about half past one on the three hundred and eleventh morning one figure of her own went out of that yard, and inside a minute a second figure of his own went out after it, and the page at the back of that lot book took no line for either. The lane count is thirty and nothing came down that lane. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read. The fifth of the five is unpaid. The count of counted months is seven and is on nothing. That gatepost carries four hundred and eleven.
