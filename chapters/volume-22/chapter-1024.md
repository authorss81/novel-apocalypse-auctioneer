# Chapter 1024: The Three Hundred And Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And One Reason Went Out Loud From The Top Of That Bank Without Its Owner Coming Down It, And One Line Went On The Page At The Back Of That Lot Book And She Was Not Thanked For It And Nobody Answered Her

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this district has ever set it against anything. Seven hundred and sixty-three days is the age of that figure.

The board carries nine hundred and twenty-two. The train on that siding has stood one thousand two hundred and thirty-eight days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and fifty-two days. Nine hundred and thirteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not open and no second opening of it has been made. Six hundred and twenty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book the writing in this yard has got. Six hundred and thirty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Seven hundred and two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. A body four hundred miles off is six hundred and forty-one days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and five days back and no night has been named. Five hundred and eighty-one marks have been cut off that board, and five hundred and sixty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table.

Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is on nothing. Four documents are not owned in this district. Four places here can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it. Fourteen things is what this district has made and five is what it does not have, and the fifth of the five is unpaid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before the page at the back of that lot book was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

One reason was said out loud from the top of that bank this morning at about half past eleven, once, without its owner coming down that slope, and one line went on the page at the back of that lot book for it, and that line carries this morning and does not carry what was said in it. Nobody answered her and nobody thanked her. The lane count is thirty and it did not move and nothing came down that lane.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and thirty-eight mornings, and the near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and eighty-eight mornings, and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and eight mornings, on the word of the man who keeps it, and he said that number before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood there for the three hundred and seventh morning. The man of fifty-six has read four figures off that wall on the seven hundred and forty-ninth of those mornings. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and sixty-third night of that run, having slept on six hundred and sixty-two of them, and this is the one hundred and sixty-fourth morning running of nobody asking him anything. The man of fifty-six's flat answer is the hundred and ninety-seventh time running.

That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The page at the back stood at sixty lines before that line went on it and carries sixty-one lines after. The record has stood two hundred and twenty-three days.

A man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and fifty-third afternoon running. About nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning. There are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles is between the top of that bank and the culvert.

The toll of ninety-eight is paid and spent and will not be raised and will not be paid a second time. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read by anybody in this district.

---

She did not come down that bank this morning and that is the first thing about it.

She came up it at the end of the day before with a frame under her arm and a bag in her hand, because she had gone down that slope twice to find out what this yard does with a person, and both times what it did was what it does with a post at that end of the ground.

So this morning she stayed at the top of it and she took the frame home and left the bag where it was.

---

There are about nine people at the top of eleven feet and a reason is a thing you can say to nine people or to none of them.

That is the whole of what that bank adds up to. Nobody at the foot of it is close enough for a person to say something to a person, and everybody at the top of it is far enough off to be a place where a thing can be said and not land on anybody.

She had the reason in her since before the light came over the top of that bank. She had it on the way up that hill in the dark and she had it standing at the top of it, and she had not opened her mouth.

---

She said it at about half past eleven.

It was one reason and it did not take long to say, and it came out of her up there in the ordinary voice, at the speed a person uses for a thing she means, and it went out over about four hundred yards of air and down the face of that bank.

It did not come down the bank.

---

That is the difference between saying a thing from the top of eleven feet and saying it at the bottom of them, and she had known it was a difference before she said it and she got it right.

Nobody at the foot of that bank took it up. Nobody called it back up the slope. The wheel went on turning at the near end of that ground and the second table went along its own boards about four feet along and a woman at a scale about four feet off the near end of the second table worked at her own pan without looking up, and the yard did what the yard does.

---

She stood at the top of that bank for the rest of that morning.

About half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale came down the slope and set up and went on with her work. At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number into about four hundred yards of air and about four people said it back after him out of step with one another, and it came off the top of that bank and past her.

She listened to it go past. She did not move her feet out of that strip of shade.

The rest of that day at the top of that bank is a long thing and it is made of nothing.

She had brought a coat and she had not put it on, because the sun comes over the top of that bank at about half past two and stays on the upper part of it for the rest of the day, and about nine people stand along that top in it. Nobody says very much up there. There is a woman who goes up that hill every day and stops halfway to put her hair up before she gets to the top. There is a man who leans on that gate and looks off along the valley behind this yard and has not said to anybody what he is looking at.

At about two she stood a few paces along and looked down at that yard from a different place, and from there the wheel, the two tables, the low wall and the strip of cold stone at the foot of it all sit in one line, and about nineteen people are going about nine things inside that line, and none of the nine things is in a hurry.

---

The thing she had not been able to get away from, in all the days she had carried that reason up that hill, was the not knowing.

From the top of that bank you can see the boards and you cannot see the page. A woman standing at that rail with a book open against it is nine feet from the people she is talking to and about four hundred yards from the woman who said the thing, and there is no telling from up there which of those two distances decides anything.

So she stood up there with about nine other people and watched the afternoon go, and nothing about that afternoon had anything to do with her.

---

At about half past three the sun came over the top of the low wall and laid itself down along the stones at the foot of it, and a man standing in that strip of it said good afternoon to a man of sixty-one sitting about four feet off him, and got one word back.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six was at his own wall with his hand up on the stone and got all four of the figures off it and laid his hand flat on the stone under the lowest one and left it lying there.

"Since before the middle of the winter came on my hand has gone to that stone twice every morning and a page at the bottom of that hill takes what I say and not what I am for," he said out loud to that stone.

About nineteen people heard that for the hundred and ninety-seventh time running and nobody asked him anything about any of it.

---

At about ten past four the clerk came along the boards to the near end of the second table and gave the account of that morning out loud before the pen was near any part of it.

"The three hundred and eighth morning," she said. "One reason was said out loud in this yard this morning, from the top of that bank, at about half past eleven, and one line goes on the back of that book for it. The line carries this morning and it does not carry the reason. That is the account of it and it went out loud before the writing of it."

Up on the bank there were about nine people who heard the whole of that and not one of them said anything about it.

She wrote the three hundred and eighth morning under the last line in her own small hand and took a line for it.

---

The line went over the worn place in that paper about four inches in from the foot of the page and it did not sit level with the lines above it, and she left it where it was.

Nobody thanked her. Nobody at the foot of that bank looked up at the top of it, and nobody at the top of that bank came over to her, and about half an hour after that pen had gone along that page nobody said one word to her about any part of it.

She had come up that hill on the strength of one thing going right, and one thing going right is not the same as being noticed, and she stood on that bank long enough for both of those to be a morning.

What the page did with it was put it in a book. She could not see that book from where she was standing, and she did not ask anybody what was in it, and by that evening there was one line more at the back of it than there had been at ten to eight, and she did not know that either.

---

At about half past four the light went off those boards, and about four people out of about nineteen said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard, and about nine people at the top of that bank stood where they were until the boards came down off the cart face down.

She said the goodnight with the rest of them and nobody said it back to her.

---

One reason went out loud from the top of that bank on the three hundred and eighth morning without its owner coming down it, and one line went on the page at the back of that lot book carrying the morning and not the reason, and she was not thanked for it and nobody answered her. That page stands at sixty-one lines. The lane count is thirty and nothing came down that lane. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read. The fifth of the five is unpaid. The count of counted months is seven and is on nothing. That gatepost carries four hundred and eleven.
