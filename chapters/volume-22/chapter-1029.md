# Chapter 1029: The Three Hundred And Thirteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And About Four People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Have Said Since That He Has Said A Figure Out Loud, And That About Four Of Them Have Not Said What A Figure Of His Own Is About, And That About Four Of Them Have Not Asked Him

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and how much of it is wrong is not a thing anybody has ever worked at. Seven hundred and sixty-eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries nine hundred and twenty-seven. The train on that siding has stood one thousand two hundred and forty-three days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and fifty-seven days. Nine hundred and eighteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not open and no second opening of it has ever been made. Six hundred and thirty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book the writing in this district has got. Six hundred and thirty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Seven hundred and seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. A body four hundred miles off is six hundred and forty-six days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and ten days back and no night has been named. Five hundred and eighty-six marks have been cut off that board, and five hundred and seventy-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table.

Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is on nothing at all. Four documents are not owned here. Four places in this district can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it. Fourteen is what this district has made and five is what it does not have, and the fifth of the five is unpaid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

Nobody said a reason out loud in that yard this morning, so the page at the back of that lot book stood at sixty-one lines and carried sixty-one lines when she shut it. The lane count is thirty and it did not move and nothing came down that lane. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and forty-three mornings, and the near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and ninety-three mornings, and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and thirteen mornings, on the word of the man who keeps it, and he said that number before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood there for the three hundred and twelfth morning, and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The man of fifty-six has read four figures off that wall on the seven hundred and fifty-fourth of those mornings. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and sixty-eighth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and sixty-seven of them, and this is the one hundred and sixty-ninth morning running of nobody asking him anything. The man of fifty-six's flat answer is the two hundred and second time running.

That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The page at the back carries sixty-one lines. The record has stood two hundred and twenty-eight days.

A man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and fifty-eighth afternoon running. About nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning. There are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles is between the top of that bank and the culvert.

The toll of ninety-eight is paid and spent and is not going to be paid twice. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read by anybody in this district.

---

He has said no more out loud since the morning he said that thing about his own corner.

That was ten mornings ago and it is the only thing anybody in this district has ever heard him say out loud, and it went up that page and nobody answered him and nobody thanked him, and he has had ten mornings since then to find out what a person is supposed to do with having said a thing and getting nothing, and he has not found out anything yet.

---

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight came down off that bank with his hands showing, and went out along the top of his own table and round the end of it and up the slope and back down, and in at the same corner.

About nine feet off him and about four feet off the near end of the second table there is a corner that is not square and a man with a cloth over his hand who looks at it every morning and does not touch it. That man has watched that round go every morning of that run and has never once heard one bit of it.

---

At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale came down that bank and set up about four feet off the near end of the second table and worked there all afternoon and said nothing out loud at any point of that day.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number out into about four hundred yards of air, and four people said it back after him out of step with one another, and it came off that bank and went on up the hill.

---

At about four o'clock his hand went up that wall and came down with all four of them on it, and he put the same hand flat under the lowest one and left it there, the way he leaves it, and the afternoon went on round him.

"I have said this out loud every morning of that run and every one of them is a sentence nobody has come back on," he said to that stone.

About nineteen people heard it, that was the two hundred and second time, and not one of them has asked him anything about any of it.

---

At about ten past four the clerk came along the boards with that book in her hand and gave the account of that morning out loud before any mark was made on it.

"The three hundred and thirteenth morning," she said. "Nothing has gone out loud in this yard this morning that goes at the back of that book, so this morning goes into my own hand with no line in it, and that is the whole of the account of it and it went out loud before any of it was written."

She wrote the three hundred and thirteenth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and took no line for it, and she shut the book on sixty-one lines and the pen went back in the holder.

---

The light came off those boards at about half past four and about four people out of about nineteen said the goodnight, and about nine people at the top of that bank did not join in and stood where they were until the boards came down off the cart face down.

He was one of the nine and he stayed with them.

---

There is a place at the top of eleven feet where about nine people stand at the end of a day, and it is the top of that bank by the end of the boards, and it is where the sentence gets said.

It has been said there more than once now, and it is said by about four of them, and it is said about four feet from the boards and never in front of him.

He has not said it himself. He is one of the four who have said it, and about four of those four have not said what a figure of his own is about, and about four of them have not asked him.

---

That is the arrangement at the top of that bank and nobody made it and nobody will ever hand it over.

A man goes round a table at about four feet off the near end of it all day and says nothing, and then on some mornings he says one figure out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, and about nine people hear it, and then the yard carries on with the middle of its afternoon.

Four people say so to each other up there. They say it as a fact, the way they would say that the light had gone off the boards a given number of times without anybody counting it, and it is the only time in that yard that anybody has ever made a sentence out of one thing another person did.

---

What they have not done is any one of the other three things.

They have not said what a figure of his own is about. That is not a question anybody up there has put to anybody else and it is not a gap they have noticed, and if you had asked any of those four people what a figure of his own was about they would have had to stand on that bank for a while before they found a sentence, and they would not have found one.

They have not asked him. Not one word has gone down that slope to the near end of that first table since he began working at it, from anybody on that bank or anybody at that rail or anybody at the foot of that wall.

And they have not said what anybody ought to do about it, which is the third thing that is not said up there, and he has never once heard anybody on that bank say what ought to happen.

---

He had a no in his mouth six mornings ago and he did not use it, and standing on that bank with about nine other people waiting for the light to go he found that the not-using of it had not gone anywhere either. It was still in there, and there was still no hour in that yard for it, and nothing at the top of eleven feet had changed that.

He put his hands in his pockets and looked down at about an acre of hard ground with two tables on it and a rail along the near side of one of them and a wheel at the far end of it standing still now.

---

Nobody was still at that table.

That is what he noticed, and it is a small thing, and it was the last thing about that day he noticed. The man of thirty-eight had gone up that bank already, and had gone on up it the way he went on up it at the end of every day, and there was a cloth on the corner of that table and nobody at the near end of it.

The light went off the top of that bank and about nine people at the top of it went down the far side, and the yard was a dark shape with two tables in it.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he has said a figure out loud, and about four of them have not said what a figure of his own is about, and about four of them have not asked him. Nobody said anything to him on the three hundred and thirteenth morning and nobody has said anything to him on any morning of that run. The page at the back of that lot book took no line and stood at sixty-one lines. The lane count is thirty and nothing came down that lane. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read. The fifth of the five is unpaid. The count of counted months is seven and is on nothing. That gatepost carries four hundred and eleven.
