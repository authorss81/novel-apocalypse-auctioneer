# Chapter 1046: The Three Hundred And Thirtieth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And Four Who Have Not Said

No hand in this valley has ever been put on that sheet and no hand ever will be, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and eighty-five days is the age of that figure.

Nine hundred and forty-four is carried by that board, which belongs to nobody here. The train on that siding has stood one thousand two hundred and sixty days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and seventy-four days at that rail. Nine hundred and thirty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long that bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open now and is not a lot and no second opening of it has been made in this district. Six hundred and forty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Six hundred and fifty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Seven hundred and twenty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. Six hundred and sixty-three days is how far past a printing it did not make that body four hundred miles off has got.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and twenty-seven days back. Six hundred and three marks have been cut off that board. Five hundred and eighty-nine marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months a clerk has counted and there is no list for them. Four documents are not owned in this district. Four places here can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it. What this district has made is fourteen things and what it does not have is five, and the fifth of those five has not been paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, before the page at the back of that lot book was anywhere a reason could go, and no line was written on that page for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because before that morning it was no such place, and that reason is counted nowhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

Nothing has gone out loud in that yard this morning that goes at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that lot book stood at sixty-two lines from the first board going up to the last one coming off. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and sixty mornings. The near rail has stood on the near side of it three hundred and ten mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has been at the foot of that bank three hundred and thirty mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and twenty-ninth morning and it is neither the second table nor the near rail. A man of fifty-six has read four figures off that wall on the seven hundred and seventy-first of those mornings. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and eighty-fifth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and eighty-four of them. A man of sixty-one sat on those stones for the two hundred and seventy-fifth afternoon running and one word is the whole of that afternoon. And that is the one hundred and eighty-sixth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. That flat answer goes to that stone twice a morning and this morning it was the two hundred and nineteenth time running.

That lot book is open along those boards and nothing has been entered in it. Five lines are ruled across the first of its pages, and down the side of the second of them is a column with one name in it under a heading which still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty and nobody has ever been asked to fill it, including by naming it. Nothing has come down that lane in this run of mornings and the count of what has come down it is thirty. That record has stood two hundred and forty-five days and nobody has asked what it is for. About nine people are on that ground, which has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

Nothing in this district has ever been put on either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

The toll of ninety-eight is paid and spent. Three lines out of the first thirty-three on that page are still unread in this district and are not going to be read this morning either.
---

There are about nine people at the top of that bank on any morning and about four of them have been to the top of eleven feet together for a run of mornings, and all four of them have the same thing in them and not one of them has ever said what it is.

She is one of the four.

She has got as far as the front of that sentence three mornings running now and she stops at the front of it every time, and the reason she stops is not that the sentence is too long. It is that both of the ways it can finish are questions.

---

A man of about twenty-nine brought a cart through the gap at about eleven and stopped it at the top of that bank, and about four of them standing there looked at him and about four of them looked at the yard below, and none of them said anything.

"You have got that cart up here every morning this week," she said.

"It is quicker up here than round."

"It is not quicker up here. You come up the bank for nothing and you come back down for nothing."

"You would not know," said the man of about twenty-nine. "You have been on the top of this bank three mornings and you would not know."

He brought the cart about ten feet along and put the bar over the wheel and sat down on the edge of it with his hands between his knees, which is what he does every morning of that run and has done for about as long as she has been coming up here.

---

The man of about thirty-four came up that bank about a quarter to twelve with his cloth over his arm and stopped about four feet off her and looked out over the yard instead of at her.

She had the sentence ready. She had it ready because she had been getting it ready since about ten and she had got it as far as the front of it and she had not said it.

"A figure has gone out into that yard more mornings than a line has gone on that page," said the man of about thirty-four.

She waited.

He waited.

"That is the first of it," said the woman of about fifty-two. "That is as far as you got as well."

"I got four words further than I got on the two mornings before."

"Which four."

He did not say them. He stood there with the cloth in his hands for about as long as it takes the light to move a foot down the boards, and then he said:

"That is not the same four as yours and it is not a different four either and I cannot put a frame round the pair of them."

That is all he said. He did not ask her anything. She did not ask him anything. The bag was in her hand and the form was folded in four in the bag and the second space on it was blank and it went up that bank blank in the middle of that afternoon the way it goes up every morning.

He said his four words and went back down his own slope to his own boards, and about four feet along from the second table there was a foot and a bit of pallet lying on top of that table's boards where he had put it, and it was still there, and he had not laid it anywhere.
---

The boy came up that bank about one with his hands in his pockets and stopped about four feet off the four of them and did not say anything for about a minute, which is about as long as he ever goes.

"You have all four of you up here saying nothing," he said.

"Two of us are up here saying nothing at the same time," said the woman of about fifty-two, "and the other two are up here saying something about something else and not this, and there is a difference, and you have not got to that yet."

"What is the difference."

"The difference is that one of them has a reason and one of them has not," she said, "and I will not tell you which is which, and I have said more in that sentence than I have said up this bank in a run of mornings, so you may put your pencil away."

The boy put his pencil away. He had not taken it out.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the figure that had gone out of that yard on the morning before. About four of them have not said what it was. About four of them have not said what anybody ought to do about it.

There is no arrangement in that yard for either of those two things, and there is not one for a person who has noticed that a man four feet along from her has got the front of the same sentence in him that she has, and there is not one for a person who would rather he had not.

---

The turner came through the gap about twenty past one and stopped about four feet off her where she was standing at the top of eleven feet, which he does perhaps twice a week and never on a morning when she has not come down first.

"You have not been down since you got here."

"I have been down twice and up twice and I have said nothing to anybody either time."

"That is not the same as not having come down," said the turner. "Going down is a thing you can account for. Going up again after you have gone down is a thing that wants an account and there is not one in this district that will take it."

"You have got about four feet of bank there and it is the only part of it in the sun."

"It is," said the turner, "and I have thought about that before and I have not got any further with it than you have."

He went back to his frame and left it there.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six got all four off that wall with his hand up before the light came over the top of it and said his four words to the stone, and the four of them at the top of that bank heard the shape of that going out across the yard and not one word of what it was.

---

At about half past one the number the man of about forty-eight keeps went up out of that yard and about four voices took it back after him out of step with one another, and the four of them at the top of that bank heard the whole of that the way they hear everything in that yard, which is to say that they heard a shape arrive and did not need it.

The man of about twenty-nine said good afternoon to nobody and went down the slope with the cart and came back up it, which is four trips a day and has been for as long as anybody up there can remember.

---

At about half past two she went down that bank for the first time in three mornings and stood at the bottom of it with the bag in her hand, and the yard did not stop for her going down it.

The turner was at the frame with his hand off it and did not stop either.

"You have come down."

"I have come down for the light. It comes over that wall in about ten minutes and there is four feet of it down there and no four feet of it up here."

"You have got a form in that bag."

"You have got a frame under your arm and that is the whole of what is in either of ours," said the woman of about fifty-two. "The form is folded in four and there is a space on it that has been blank every morning for longer than I care to put a figure on, and nobody has ever come up this bank and said a word to me about it, and I have stopped waiting for one, which is the only thing I have done about it."

She stood in the four feet of sun for about as long as the light took to move a foot along the stones, and then she went back up the bank with the frame knocking against her leg, and she did not fill it in on the way.

A little after three the sun came over the top of that low wall and laid a strip of it along the stones at the foot of it, and a man standing in that strip said good afternoon to a man of sixty-one sitting about four feet off him on those stones, and got one word back, and neither of them said anything else about it.

At about four the man of fifty-six had his hand up on that wall and got all four off it and laid his hand flat on the stone under the lowest one and left it lying there.

"Two hundred and nineteen mornings," he said out loud to that stone, "and I have never once been able to hand one of the four to anybody who wanted it."

About nineteen people heard that, and it was the two hundred and nineteenth time running, and nobody has ever asked him anything about it.
---

At about ten past four the clerk was at those boards with that book open against the rail and gave the account of that morning out loud with the pen in the holder.

"The three hundred and thirtieth morning," she said. "Nothing has gone out loud in this yard this morning that goes at the back of that book, so this morning goes down in my own hand with nothing in it, and that is the whole of the account of it, and it went out loud before the pen was near the page."

She wrote the three hundred and thirtieth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and took no line for it.
---

At about half past four the light came off those boards and about four people out of about nineteen said the goodnight, and the four of them at the top of eleven feet did not say it.

About nine people at the top of that bank heard the figure that went out of that yard on the morning before, and about four of them have not said what it was, and about four of them have not said what anybody ought to do about it. The page at the back of that lot book stood at sixty-two lines and did not move. The lane count was thirty and nothing came down that lane. Three lines of the first thirty-three on that back page have gone through this district without anybody hearing them read. The fifth of the five is five and unpaid. Seven months is counted by a clerk and it is on nothing. Four hundred and eleven is on the sheet at that gatepost.
