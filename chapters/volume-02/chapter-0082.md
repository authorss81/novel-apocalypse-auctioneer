# Chapter 82: The Name at the Top

The first thing anybody wrote on a toll board in this district was a name, and it was written on the sixth of this month by a man of sixty-one who charges two coppers a board and who began at the top left because that is where he has begun ten thousand times.

He had a pot of white paint and a stencil and a length of twine and a boy of about nine holding the other end, and the boy was not asked to hold it and was not told to let go of it, and the minute enters him as present and not asked.

The board stands against the wall of the signal cabin at the east end of the line. It is the east end, and it is eleven minutes' walk from the ward market counter, and anybody in this city can get to it without a bank and without a changeover.

The name went on first and it went on in letters three inches high and it took about nine minutes, and it is the whole name and not a contraction, because the man who wrote it was given the source and told to copy it exactly, and he copied it exactly.

> **THE STANDING OFFICE OF CROSS-DISTRICT PASSAGE, ESTABLISHED, AND NOT AT THIS PLACE.**

The stencils came off at about twenty past ten and the boy was sent home with a shilling for a job that took him two hours, and about forty people were standing in the lane by the cabin, and none of them had asked to be there.

---

**And then about eleven of them stood in front of a nine-foot board in Alder Reach and asked whether the name should be lower down, and the answer took four minutes, and the answer is the shape of the whole instrument and it is one sentence long.**

A name at the top is a heading. A name in the middle is an amount. This district cannot print an amount.

"So it goes at the top," said the tenth hand.

"It goes at the top because a heading can be a thing nobody can find, and a figure in the middle of a board is a promise, and nobody in this district is in a position to make one," said Wren Ostler, who had come out of the counter in her own time and not on the market's, and the entry says so.

"And why is the column four feet wide."

**"Because a column printed narrow looks like a column that has been kept quiet,"** said the man of sixty-one, who writes signs. "And that is not my sentence, it is the market's, it is printed on a sheet that came off a press in this city, and I have set type for two men in Alder Reach and one of them paid me and one of them did not, and both of them are right about the type, and I have thought about that for about a fortnight and I have not worked out what to do with it."

---

The rest of the board was written over the rest of that afternoon, and the middle of it is empty, and the emptiness is printed and not left, and there are eleven lines on that board and four of the eleven are about a place nobody can find.

Under the name, in letters two inches high:

> **THE TOLL ON THIS LINE IS THE ORIGIN. THE ORIGIN IS A PLACE AND NOT A PERSON. THE LINE'S OWN BOOK HAS NINE HUNDRED AND SIX MOVEMENTS IN IT SINCE THE FLOOD YEAR. A HUNDRED AND FORTY-ONE OF THEM NAME THE NINTH PLACE AS THE ORIGIN HELD.**
>
> **NOBODY IN THIS BASIN CAN FIND THAT OFFICE IN ANY REGISTER THIS BASIN CAN REACH. THE NAME IS SPELLED OUT IN FOUR ENTRIES IN A COLUMN OF SIXTY-ONE THAT THE LINE BOOK HAS NOT GOT, AND IN FOUR ENTRIES IN A KEEPER'S COLUMN HEADED ORIGIN HELD, AND THE FOUR IN ONE BOOK ARE NOT THE FOUR IN THE OTHER, SO THERE IS A FIFTH SOMEWHERE.**

And then the column, ruled at its two ends in pencil, four feet wide and empty from one end to the other, and inside the column, at about the height of a man's eye and not filling a fifth of the width:

> **THE AMOUNT OF THE ORIGIN.**
>
> **NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT CAN STATE IT. THIS IS NOT A SECRET. IT IS A GAP. THE COLUMN IS PRINTED AT FULL WIDTH SO THAT A STRANGER CAN SEE IT IS EMPTY ON PURPOSE.**

And along the bottom, three lines in pencil: a date, a keeper's name, and one line for a sentence that anybody who stands in front of the board may add and that nobody will be asked to explain.

---

**And the room tried three times in twenty minutes to put the ninth place in the middle of that board, and refused three times, and the reason is the oldest thing anybody in this district has written down and it is on a wall in Alder Reach and it has not got a date on it because it has not changed.**

The question came from a man who sells nothing and has a use, and it was a fair question and it was the right question and the room turned it down three times.

"The origin held a hundred and forty-one times is the ninth place. So write the ninth place in the column. That is not a reading. That is a number off a book, and a book is a book."

"No," said the tenth hand, and then he gave the reason, and the reason is four words and it has been on a wall since the seventh of March.

**"A reading may state what kind of thing is charged and may not state which one."**

"That is a reader. I am talking about a board."

"A board is worse than a reader, and I will tell you why and then I will not say it again. A reading is done by one man in a hut, and about an hour is taken, and two other people compare the hand afterwards against his own. A board stands in a street for eleven years and gets read by people who will never meet the man who wrote it. **A reader is the only instrument in this district that takes the whole of a thing away from him before he writes it down. A board keeps it.**"

---

The afternoon of the sixth was spent on the order the lines went on, and the order is on the board and anybody can see it, and it is the only part of this district's public furniture that has ever been designed by a room arguing about the order of its own sentences.

The name goes at the top, because it is a heading. Then the two lines about the origin, because a person who has read the heading will not know what it is a heading about. Then the column head, and then the column, and then the reason inside the column, and not the reason under the column.

"The reason goes inside," said Wren Ostler. "If the reason goes underneath then the column looks like a column that has been left empty and the reason is somewhere else, and a person standing in front of this is going to read the column first and the reason second if it is anywhere at all. **It goes inside the column, at the height of a man's eye, and it is printed, and it is not carved and it is not chalked.**"

"It will fade," said the man of sixty-one.

"It will fade," she agreed, "and in nine years somebody will have to do it again, and that is the first thing anybody in this district has designed that somebody will have to come back and do, and I have written that on the bottom line of the minute with a date on it and not a name on it, because I do not know whose job it is yet and it is going to be somebody's."

And the keeper's line went last, and the argument about the keeper's line took about nine minutes, and what came out of it is that the keeper's line carries a name and a week and nothing else, and that the bottom line carries a sentence anybody may add, and that the two are different and a keeper is not a person who answers questions.

**"A keeper is not somebody you go to,**" said the woman of thirty-eight with the hook, who was still standing there and had still not been asked to do anything. "**A keeper is somebody who is there. If you want a question answered you want a person who is answerable, and there is one of those at the ninth place and he is thirty-nine and he will tell you he is not anybody.**"

"Write that on the board," said the tenth hand, "and write her name against it, because in about a fortnight a stranger is going to stand in front of a board in a lane and ask who is in charge of it, and the right answer is nobody, and the second right answer is a woman who comes up a bank once a month for her own reasons and is not on any roll in this city."

---

By about four o'clock on the sixth of this month a man of about forty-six who was on his way home from the lower terrace stopped in the lane by the cabin and read the whole of the board, and it took him about three minutes, and he read the middle of it twice.

He is a plasterer. He is in nine weeks of refusals in a book in Alder Reach, in his own words, because he was on a roof at the far end of the terrace and would not get down twice in a week for anybody's drawer, and he came down that afternoon for no reason anybody could find out afterwards except that he said he had been thinking about the column since the twenty-second of March.

"That's my name," he said, when he got to the bottom line, in the tone of a man who has found his own handwriting in a strange place. "That's not my handwriting."

"It is not," said the tenth hand. "It is a man who has been dead nine years and it is a notice about a drain."

"**Well, he is wrong then,**" said the plasterer, "**because there is nothing on that line.**"

And he wrote one sentence on the bottom line in chalk, and it is the first of the seventeen, and it is eleven words long, and the finding entered on the eighth of this month is that **the first thing a stranger wrote on a toll board in this district was a correction of a dead man by a man who had refused this district three times, and neither of them knew the other one existed.**

---

**And then four people in four rooms tried to find that office in a register on the seventh of this month, and it took about two hours in all, and the result is in a public book and the result is that nobody can.**

A registrar of thirty-four went through the municipal roll of public offices for the years before the Long Fracture, which is the only complete run in this basin, and she came back with the finding that the run is a list of things that collected money, and that about a third of the entries have no money against them at all because they were never funded, and that she has found nine such entries in nine years and cannot rule out ninety.

A bridge clerk of thirty-four went through the standing instructions of his own office, which are four drafts of a document with a door on it, and which cite the phrase four times in nineteen pages, and he did not tell the room that until the eighth, and when he did tell it he said the reason first: **because a man who has found a thing in his own office's papers and has not said so for a day is a man who has had a day to decide what it looks like, and I am not going to be the second man in this district to do that.**

A licensed valuer of forty-one, who certified a lot as clean in April and wrote on the back of his own certificate that his form has twenty-nine columns and not one of them is a place, was asked whether the Valuers' Guild had a register of public offices, and said that it did, and that the guild's register is a list of offices that were still collecting fees in the fourth year, and that an office not collecting fees in the fourth year is not on it, and that he had looked, and that he had hoped somebody would ask him.

And a man of about sixty-one who writes signs said that he had a book of every lettering job he has done since the sixth year, with the customers' names on the spine, and that he went through it on the seventh of this month, and that he found the phrase, twice, in his own hand, on two boards he made in the sixth year for the water office.

"So you have it," said somebody.

"I have my own hands on it. **I do not have the office, and I do not have anybody who gave me the words, and I was given a board to paint and not a question, and if I had asked a question in the sixth year somebody would have told me, and nobody has told me in nine years, and I am sixty-one and I have been carrying two boards' worth of it in a book on a shelf.**"

---

And the fourth question nobody asked on the sixth of this month was the one that was asked on the seventh, at the counter, by a stranger, and it is the reason a nail is going into a board.

"**If I want to complain about that, who do I complain to?**"

It is in the counter's book of read-alouds and the reader is the one who wrote it down and the finding under it is nine words long.

**He can complain to nobody. That is the toll.**

---

**And the third keeper of the three boards was found on the ninth of this month at the fourth place, and she was found because she is the only person in Alder Reach who goes up that bank on a day of her own choosing, and she is a paperer, and her whole trade is other people's documents.**

She is called Jessa Olwin and she is about thirty-eight and she buys old paper off four houses once a month, and she has been doing it for nine years, and she goes up in a barrow with a hook and a sacking sheet and she pays in coppers and she weighs what she takes. She was the woman at the back of the counting room on the fourth of this month who said the question out loud before anybody answered it, which is the sixth thing, and she was not asked to do it and has not been thanked for it and says she does not want to be.

The board at the fourth place is nailed to the outside of a hut that has no frame in it and has never had one, and in which a boy of nineteen is asleep on a bench with four pounds of cast iron beside him.

She said yes in about four seconds and then asked for two things, and both of them are on the board.

"One. The barrow. I go up on a day I choose, because I go to four houses and I know which day each of them has paper on. I am not going up there for a railway and I am not going to be at a board at a time I chose for a house. **So the week it says on the board is the week I am on that bank and not any other.**"

"Two. I am not a signal hand and I never will be and I do not know what a pair is. If somebody comes and asks me why a line has an end on it, I am going to say what the board says, and if they want more than the board says then they want a signal keeper, and there is one at the ninth place, and he is thirty-nine and he is in an office and he will tell you he is not anybody. That is on the board too. I want it on the board."

---

The board at the fourth place went up at about eleven in the morning on the ninth of this month and the boy of nineteen was awake, which was unusual, and he came out of the hut and looked at it for about four minutes without saying anything and then went back in and pulled the door to behind him about a foot.

"He looked at it the way you look at a thing that is going to be there after you have gone," said the woman with the hook, "and I have been in four houses on this bank for nine years and I can tell you that is not a man looking at a sign. That is a man looking at a fence post. **He wanted to know whether it was going to be somebody's job.**"

"Is it," said the tenth hand.

"It is going to be mine, one day a month, and I have told him that in a sentence, and he said a thing I am not going to repeat because he said it on his own bench and not to me. He is nineteen and he is not on a roll in this city and he is not anybody's keeper and he has said so in about four rooms. Whatever is on that board is going to outlive him being nineteen and he knows it, and it is not the board that frightens him. It is the week underneath it."

---

**And two hundred coppers came in on the seventh of this month, and it is the first money the ninth place has ever paid this market, and nobody there was asked whether they agreed to pay it, and both halves of that are in the minute.**

Four households at fifty coppers, collected in coppers out of a pail of change by a man of about forty-six who does not read figures and who is the market's own collector and who was given the figure and not the reason. It is entered as received on the seventh of this month. It is the first movement in that column since the twenty-eighth of last month, and six of the two hundred has gone on a sign and eleven of it on a bound copy of a line book, and a hundred and eighty-three of it has not gone on anything.

"The sheet that carried the rate says on its face that a rate sheet is a document that says a place is a place, and it does," said Ivo Stenn, "and I have been sitting on it for four weeks with two hundred coppers in a column and no way to spend them, and on the fourth of this month somebody spent six of them on a sign, and that is the first time a rate has been spent on the thing it was collected for, and I want that entered. And I want the other half entered too, which is that nobody at that place was asked whether they agreed to the rate, and the sheet went up on the twenty-eighth of March and was read by us."

Nobody argued with him about it, and nobody in that room could say what should have happened instead, and the entry ends with the registrar's sentence, which is the fourth time she has written it and the second time it has been about a document: **a rate that is not asked for is a rate that has not been agreed to, and this market has now collected two hundred coppers from four households on a sheet it made for a place it had never met.**

---

The second board went up on the eighth of this month at the ninth place, on the shoulder, about eleven feet above a drop of nine feet, where a person can stand in front of it without going over the edge, and a man of thirty-nine held it while two others hammered and would not let go of it while they did.

There is no carriageway at that end of the line. There is a siding, a water column, a hut, a chapel with a bell that is not rung, and four houses, and about nine hundred feet of embankment with a nine-foot drop along one side of it. **Every person who reads that board has to stop on a six-foot shoulder on bad ground, in whatever is coming down, at the edge of something, and the finding entered on the ninth is that the most-read board in this district is on the most dangerous piece of ground anybody in this basin has put a sheet of paper on, and that the two facts have nothing to do with each other and were both true on the same day.**

And the finding of all three, entered on the ninth at about six in the evening at a counter in Alder Reach in the ordinary voice and not by a clerk, is that **three boards standing a day apart from each other on the same line are the same board, and the reason is that all three of them say the same eleven lines and the middle of all three of them is empty, and nobody in this district has ever put three copies of one sentence in three places before.**

And the fourth thing that is on the boards, under the same heading as the name, and not in the middle, because it is not a toll and it is not an amount and it is a fact about a building.

> **THE HUT AT THIS PLACE IS HELD BY AN OFFICE UNTIL A DATE. THE DATE IS WRITTEN HERE. THE OFFICE HAS NOT ASKED. THE OFFICE CONSIDERS THIS PLACE UNSAFE AND HAS SAID SO IN A PUBLIC YARD ELEVEN TIMES IN EIGHT YEARS.**

The fifth thing is still unnamed. The column is still empty. And the name at the top of three boards in three places, nine miles apart, is a name that no person in this basin can enter into a register, and the finding under all three boards is that **a toll nobody can name is still a toll, and a board that says so in a street is worth more than a book that says it in a room, and the only thing this district has that is a place is a board, and there are now three of them, and not one of them is going to say what the amount is.**
