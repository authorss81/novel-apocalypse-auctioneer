# Chapter 63: The Answer, and the Figure

The man from the hauliers was standing in the doorway of the counting room at a quarter past nine in the morning on the twenty-fifth of this month with his hat in his hand and he had been there for about eleven minutes before anybody spoke to him, and the reason he had been standing in the doorway is that the ward market's rule is that a trade which has been asked a question has four days to answer it, and the market had put this question on the twenty-first of this month, and the twenty-fifth was the fourth day, and the man in the doorway knew exactly what day it was because he had counted.

"Are you the answer?" said Nessa Pell, from the table, without looking up.

"**I am one third of the answer,**" said the man. "**And I have been in this doorway since a quarter past nine because you put a question to a market on the twenty-first and I am a trade and I have four days, and today is the fourth day, and the whole of Alder Reach has been standing in this building for two days asking me whether I have come to say no.**"

"And what have you come to say?"

"**I have come to say that the hauliers do not want an office they cannot end,**" said the man, "**and that I do not think any trade in this room does, and that the reason is not money, and I want the reason written down as the reason and not as an excuse, because in four years somebody is going to come in here with a document that says the ward market refused safety, and the ward market's own page is where the reason has to be.**"

---

**And the reason was written down, and the figure was written down under it, and the figure is the reason the market is going to be misunderstood for the rest of this volume's business.**

The standing order of the ninth had been read aloud in a room with a door on it on the fifth of this month, in four versions, none signed, three rooms, four authors in seven years, and not one of them had known the others existed. It had been published in four versions with the name of the administrator who let it sit in a room against the front of it, and a paragraph on the front page that was written on a staircase landing in about fifty seconds by a fitter holding a toolbox, and nobody in Alder Reach ever learned who wrote it. **A closure without a ground is a safety measure, and a safety measure is not subject to appeal.** And the last page, which says that the order is not subject to revocation by any body existing at the date of it, and that the body which may revoke it is created by it, and that it consists of the administrator and two persons named by him who hold no office, and that **it may be dissolved only by the body which may revoke it.**

The ward market was asked to answer it. **The market was three days late, and the lateness is entered, and the reason for the lateness is the market's own rule about four days, and the entry says that a rule which gives a market four days to answer a question about the safety of a crossing has been used for the first time on a question about the safety of a crossing, and that the clerk noted it.** Nobody found that funny. The clerk found it extremely funny and did not say so.

**The vote was by trades, seven against, three for, one abstaining**, and the count each trade carries is not read out, because the market has never in eleven years read it out on a question that was not a rate, and **that omission is entered, because the district's answer to this document is four batches away from now and the count each trade carries will be read then.**

And the figure is this, and it is the figure the market will be quoted for, and it is correct.

> **THE EIGHTH PAGE OF THE STANDING ORDER OF THE NINTH, WRITTEN IN THE FIFTH YEAR, IS A SCHEDULE OF EIGHT PLACES WITH A DUTY AGAINST EACH. IT IS THE ONLY PAGE IN NINETEEN THAT IS ABOUT A RIVER. IT IS THE ONLY DOCUMENT IN NINE YEARS THAT ANYBODY HAS PUT IN FRONT OF THIS MARKET THAT WAS ABOUT A RIVER.**
>
> **COSTED AT THIS MARKET'S OWN RATE FOR A STANDING POST — ELEVEN COPPERS A QUARTER, WHICH IS THE FIGURE THIS MARKET PAYS ITS OWN PUBLIC READER AND WHICH IS A MATTER OF PUBLIC RECORD — EIGHT POSTS ARE EIGHTY-EIGHT COPPERS A QUARTER.**
>
> **THE WHOLE OF THIS MARKET'S QUARTERLY TAKE AT FIFTY COPPERS OVER FOUR THOUSAND AND ONE HOUSEHOLDS IS TWO HUNDRED THOUSAND AND FIFTY COPPERS. EIGHTY-EIGHT IS NONE OF IT. THIS MARKET COULD PAY THAT OFFICE ELEVEN HUNDRED TIMES OVER WITHOUT ALTERING THE PRICE OF A SINGLE THING IT SELLS, AND IT IS GOING TO VOTE AGAINST IT, AND THE FIGURE IS ENTERED BECAUSE A FIGURE THAT GOES AGAINST A DECISION IS THE ONLY THING THAT KEEPS A DECISION FROM BECOMING A FEELING.**

Selik Marne was at the back on his feet, in his hat, and he had not asked for anything, and he said one sentence when the figure was read out and it was the only thing he said for about an hour.

"**Then the market is refusing it because of what it is and not because of what it costs, and that is the correct reason, and I have been trying to get this district to refuse me for money for nine years because I did not know there was another reason available and I have been the weaker one for a week.**"

---

**And the market's reason is four sentences long and it is in the minute in the secretary's own hand, and the secretary is Ivo Stenn, and he is six years a clerk and two a secretary and he has said no to about four hundred things and he has never once said no to a document.**

"A rate buys a service and does not buy a seat," he said. "**I wrote that on a wall on the thirteenth of this month about a lamp and a bushel of chalk and I did not know I was writing a constitution.**

"An office is a seat with a duty against it. The eight places on the eighth page are eight seats. **And a seat that may be filled and cannot be emptied is not a service, it is a condition, and this market collects fifty coppers a quarter off four thousand and one households for a promise of a sheet of paper it has not printed since the flood year, and the reason the sheet is not printed is that this market has spent eleven years paying for a condition and calling it a service, and I am not doing it twice on purpose with a river.**"

Nobody argued with him for about nine seconds, and then the chandler did, and she was right.

"**Then say what you will fund.**"

"**Six coppers a quarter for printed matter,**" said Stenn. "**Out of the boards column and out of the oil column, three and three, and the reason is entered in both, and the six is for a sheet and nothing else and a sheet is the only thing this market has ever been able to buy.**"

"And when is it printed?"

"**When there is a timetable to print,**" said Stenn, "**and there is not one, and there has not been one since the flood year, and the man on the tram arch has had one printed for about a fortnight and I have read it and it is honest and it is the best printing in this district and it is a claim on a closing that does not exist.**"

---

**And then the third man of the three said the thing that the market voted on, and it was not the constitution, and it is in the minute, and it is the only technical objection anybody in Alder Reach has made to that document in nine years.**

It came from a cooper off the lower terrace who has sat on that market's rate committee for six years and who is not a Warden and is not a signal hand and has never in his life held a pair.

"**Page eleven. The eight places.** I have the fair copy. **The eighth of the eight is Harrow.**"

He put the sheet on the table and put his finger on the eighth line and read the place out and it is in the book as he read it, and the room did not make a sound.

"**Harrow has been under nine feet of water since the flood year. It has been under water since the year the low end went under, which was the fourth year, and the fair copy is the tenth, and the schedule was written in the fifth, and the schedule has been carried forward in three later drafts and it has never been struck out, because nobody has ever read page eleven of it out loud in a room where somebody who works on a railway was sitting.**

"So one of the eight posts in the only river document this district has ever been handed is a post at a place there is no longer any ground at. **And that is not a small objection. That is the document proving on its own face that it was not maintained, and the district has quoted it as though it were maintained because nobody read to the eighth line.**"

"**Then amend it,**" said Selik Marne, from the back.

"**No,**" said the cooper. "**You cannot amend a document that says it may be dissolved only by itself. That is the whole difficulty and you are the man who published it. If we amend page eleven we are the body that revokes it, and the only body that may revoke it is the one it creates, and so the moment this room writes a word on it the order is void and every duty on the other seven lines goes with it, and there is no crossing, and the span stays shut, and the plank stays in the service cut.**"

And the room sat with that for about a minute and a half, and the finding went in the minute in the registrar's hand, and the finding is the best thing the morning produced and nobody wanted it.

> **THE WARD MARKET'S ANSWER IS ENTERED IN TWO HALVES AND BOTH HALVES ARE CORRECT AND THEY CANNOT BOTH BE ACTED ON, AND THAT IS WHY THE ANSWER IS A LOSS AND NOT A REFUSAL: THE MARKET IS RIGHT THAT THE EIGHTH PAGE CARRIES A PLACE THAT IS UNDER NINE FEET OF WATER, AND THE MARKET IS RIGHT THAT AN OFFICE WHICH MAY BE DISSOLVED ONLY BY ITSELF IS A CONDITION AND NOT A SERVICE, AND THE MARKET CANNOT ACT ON EITHER WITHOUT VOIDING THE DOCUMENT AND LOSING THE SEVEN OTHER PAGES WITH IT, AND IT HAS VOTED AGAINST THE DOCUMENT AND HAS ASKED FOR SOMETHING ELSE, AND THE SOMETHING ELSE IS SIX COPPERS A QUARTER FOR A SHEET OF PAPER.**
>
> **AND THE EIGHTH OF THE EIGHT IS HARROW. IT IS ENTERED HERE AND IT IS NOT JOINED TO ANYTHING ELSE IN THIS BOOK, AND IT IS A PLACE AND NOT AN OFFICE, AND THE OFFICE IS A SEPARATE MATTER IN TWO LINES.**

---

**And Mara Quill wrote all of it down herself, in her own hand, in about two hours and ten minutes, and then had to do the part that was hers and nobody else's, and the part that was hers took about nine minutes and it is the second part of her entry and it is the first time in the history of the public lot book that a thing has been entered that Lot 17 did not witness and did not hold and could not have refused.**

Mara Quill is the registrar of the public lot book and she is not a vote and has never been a vote, and the book records things that happen at the rail of a yard and things that happen at a counter, and on the twenty-fifth of this month she entered a decision of the ward market into the public lot book for the first time in nineteen years of being a registrar, and she had to write two reasons and not one.

"**The market's reason, and the market has given it and it is good,**" she said, at about half past twelve, to about forty people in a counting room with the shutters open. "**And it is not the reason.**

"**The reason is that the market has been asked a question it cannot answer, about a document it is not the authority over, in a room where the person who is the authority over it is standing at the back in his hat saying nothing.**

"And so I have to enter the second reason, and the second reason is mine, and I am a registrar and I do not have opinions and I have got one, and here it is. **A market cannot be the body that answers a standing order, because a standing order is a claim that somebody in particular is responsible, and a market is a claim that everybody is responsible, and this district has spent five weeks learning that the difference between those two is the difference between a lot and a queue.**"

Nessa Pell said, "**That is not a reason anybody can vote on.**"

"**No, it is not,**" said Mara. "**It is the reason the answer is going to be misunderstood in four years and I am putting it in the book so that the misunderstanding has something to be wrong about. The market's reason is in the minute and it is good and the market may have it. This one is mine and it is worse and it is about the shape of the room and not about the money and I have printed it in a public book for that reason.**"

---

**And at about ten past two in the afternoon the twenty-fifth came, and the reader was permitted to read, and he did not read, and there is no reading in the ledger for it, and the month's ledger closes at one for the second time in the same month.**

Nessa Pell wrote the entry and it is the shortest she has written in nineteen years and it took her about eleven seconds.

> **THE TWENTY-FIFTH. THE READER WAS PERMITTED FROM THE TWENTY-FIFTH AND DID NOT READ. NO COST. NO TIME. THE ENTRY IS OF A NON-EVENT AND IS ENTERED AS ONE, AND IT IS THE SECOND SUCH ENTRY IN THIS MONTH, AND THE MONTH'S LEDGER CLOSES AT ONE.**

Ivo Stenn was still in the building and he asked him about it in the doorway at about twenty past two, and Adrian Vale told him, and told him the whole thing, and Stenn listened and then said the thing that is in the file because Stenn writes down the things he hears.

"**You have now refused twice and both times it is in a book, and I want to say to you as a man who has spent six years saying no that a no is a thing that gets more expensive every time anybody sees it, and there is a day when four people have watched you say no twice in one month and the fourth one stops hearing the reason and starts hearing the habit.**

"**And if you keep refusing, somebody is going to call it a practice. And you will not be able to tell them it is not one, because you will not know either, and neither will I, and neither will anybody in this city, and that is the honest position and I would rather be told it by a freight clerk than find it out in a year.**"

"I do not know what it is," said Adrian Vale. "**I have got a date on a wall with four names on it and a boundary to walk in four days and a sheet that says the toll is the origin, and if I put my hand on that iron today and get five better lines than I had on the eleventh, then on the twenty-ninth I am a man with better five lines standing in a village, and there is a woman of sixty-one on that bank who found out what was wrong with my best work in a rainstorm, and I would rather be the man I am with the five lines I have got.**"

"That is a good reason."

"**It is a reason for Friday,**" said Adrian, "**and I have said the other one and it was better, and both of them are in a file now, and Noticed, please: I have never once said the same reason twice, and that I do not think that is a discipline. I think it is a man looking for a door.**"

"**Then stop looking for it in me,**" said Stenn, "**because I have got nothing for you, and I have got six coppers a quarter and a sheet of paper, and on Friday a man with a printing press is going to sell four hundred places on a walk that four men are going to do on foot in rain, and if you want to know whether that is a scandal or a service then the answer is that neither word is in my vocabulary and both of them are in his.**"

"That is the second time this month you have told me what was coming and been right."

"**It is the fourth,**" said Stenn. "**And I wrote the first one down on the eighth of this month in my own book, and I have started a page for that, because a man who is right four times in a month and is not thanked for it has found out what he is for, and I have not the least idea what that is, and it is the first time in six years that has happened to me.**"

And at about half past three in the afternoon a woman of sixty-one came down the bank at the ninth place with a boy of about nine and asked the keeper whether the sheet had gone up, and the keeper said that it had, and the woman said one thing about it and the keeper wrote it down, and it is in the rate sheet's file because Stenn asked for it, and **it is the only line in that file that is not about money.**

"**You have put a number on us.**"

"Yes," said Sef Anyard. "Two hundred coppers a quarter. Thirty-one people. Four houses. And a drain at the sixth place."

"**You have put a number on us and I have been waiting nine years for somebody to put a number on us, and I am going to tell you what that feels like, and then I am going back up the bank because it is four o'clock and the light goes off that bank at half past four in this month.**

"**It feels like being counted. That is all of it. Nobody has ever counted us. We have been a thing that people have had opinions about for nine years, and a thing is not a thing that gets counted.**"

And she went back up the bank, and the boy of nine went with her, and the keeper stood in the doorway of a hut with a pencil in her hand and a sixty-one-year-old woman's sentence in her own book, and she has never had a page in anything, and she has had a book of her own for eleven years, and the difference between the two had never occurred to her before that afternoon and it occurred to her in about four seconds.

And then the sixth coppers went into the rate sheet's second column, split three and three out of the boards and the oil, and the finding underneath both of them is the same sentence in both columns, and the sentence is Bett Ruhl's, and Bett Ruhl cannot read, and she was at the counter at four o'clock and Rutt read it out and Bett Ruhl made her read it twice, and it is the sentence she had chalked on her own wall in the second week of last month and has never been thanked for.

> **WHAT A SHEET COSTS TO PRINT IN THIS DISTRICT, FROM A RATE SHEET MADE BY A FREIGHT CLERK IN THREE EVENINGS: FOUR COPPERS FOR THE STOCK, WHICH IS THE WARD MARKET'S OWN BLANK CLAIM STOCK AND ALREADY EXISTS AND IS PAID FOR. TWO COPPERS FOR THE SETTING. NINE HUNDRED COPPERS, AT THE FIGURE THE LAST INDEPENDENT PRINTER IN THIS CITY QUOTED, WHICH WAS IN THE FOURTEENTH, FOR A RUN OF FIVE HUNDRED. AND THE SUM OF THAT COLUMN IS NINE HUNDRED AND SIX COPPERS, AND WHAT THE COLUMN BUYS IS TWELVE, AND THE GAP IS THE REST, AND THE FINDING IS THAT A RATE OF SIX COPPERS A QUARTER BUYS A SHEET ONCE AND A RATE BUYS A SERVICE, AND A SHEET ONCE IS NOT A SERVICE, AND THE SIX COPPERS IS WRONG, AND THE MARKET KNOWS IT IS WRONG, AND THE MARKET PUT IT IN ANYWAY BECAUSE IT IS THE ONLY THING IT CAN AFFORD AND BECAUSE A FIGURE IN A COLUMN IS A PROMISE THAT SOMEBODY WILL HAVE TO ANSWER FOR IT.**

---

**And Selik Marne said from the rail at about four in the afternoon, on a published day, to about ninety people, that that was not enough, and entered that he did not accept the answer, and that he would say so in a public yard on every published day for as long as he held the post, and nobody in that counting room believed he would stop, and he did not stop, and he has not.**

He also said the other thing, at about ten past four, to nobody in particular, and about eleven people heard it and one of them wrote it down.

"**The market has offered six coppers a quarter for a sheet of paper in place of an office for eight places, and I have been a Warden for nine years and I want to say that I do not think that is foolish, and I want to say that I think it is a great deal more foolish than it sounds, and here is why.**

"**Six coppers buys a sheet that tells you what a train is going to do. Eighty-eight coppers buys a man at Harrow, which is under water, and a man at four other places that may or may not still be the places they were in the fifth year. And the reason the market is right and I am not able to say it out loud in that room is that the market is right about a document and I am right about a river, and those are two different claims, and only one of them can be entered on a rate sheet, and it is not mine.**"
