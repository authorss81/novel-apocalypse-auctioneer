# Chapter 68: Held by Nobody

The counts came east on the back of a sheet of paper about two inches by three, and it went into a sleeve in the yard of Lot 17 on the morning of the thirtieth of this month at about ten past nine, and it was the first time in the history of that book that a walk's counts had been taken at all, and the finding is on it and it took a clerk of nineteen years and about four minutes to see it.

There were two counts. **The sheet said the counts were to be taken going and coming, and it did not say what was to be counted, and nobody asked, and the two of them are different numbers and neither is struck.**

The tenth hand counted stalls. He counted ten going and ten coming, and he had marked ten, and he had been for three weeks certain that the line had ten in the book and that the woman who lived in a hut nine miles on had said twelve because she counted the floor she stood on.

**And he counted eleven coming.**

"There is one between the eighth and the ninth," he said. "**It is not the one in the floor. It is a length of about ninety yards on the embankment where the cess has gone soft and the six-foot has dropped and the ballast has come up through it in three places, and it is between the section post and the ninth, and it is not in the line book.**

"And I did not see it going, at ten to eleven in the morning, from the six-foot, with the light on the river side. **And I saw it coming at ten past four in the afternoon, off the shoulder, with the light behind me and the water gone out of the hollow.** I have been past that ground eleven times since the nineteenth of last month and I have never seen it and I have never seen the sign of it, and it has been there the whole of eight years."

"Why is it not in the book?"

"**Because the line book is a book of signals,**" said the tenth hand. "**A place post, a signal post, a section post. A stall with a signal in it gets entered, because the thing that happened at a signal is written down. A stall with no signal in it does not get entered, because there is nobody standing at it whose business it is, and there has not been anybody standing at it since the fourth year.**

"So the count of stalls on this line has been wrong for eight years and it has been wrong in one direction only, and the direction is **down**, and every stall that is short of a signal is a stall this district has never had in a book, and I do not know how many of them there are, and I am not going to guess, and the answer is that somebody walks every yard of it. Not the fourth place to the ninth. **Every yard.**"

---

**And the second count was Ivo Stenn's, and it is six marks going and seven marks coming, and it is the first count anybody in this district has taken of places a rate could be written on, and he did it because he had promised a rate sheet and had run out of lines and had started counting things that were not houses.**

"Going, I had six," he said. "**The fourth place, which is a lamp and a nameplate and no keeper. The fifth, which is a culvert. The seventh, which is a length of green pipe with one end in the ballast. The sixth, which is a drain. The eighth, which is a shoulder with a drop off it. And the fourth's boundary post, which is a post.**

"Coming, I had seven. **I added the place I did not have going, and I did not have it going because I was counting things a rate could be written against and I did not know it was one.**

"And it is a section boundary stone, and it is a mile and three quarters from the east end of the section, and it says so in a face cut by somebody about forty-odd years ago, and **it is the only fixed point on this line that anybody has ever looked at, and it has a number in it, and the number is the same number the line book has for the ninth place, and nobody has ever compared a post with a book.**"

---

**And Rutt read all of it out at the ward market counter on the afternoon of the thirtieth, and about two hundred and sixty people heard it over two days, and the standing practice held, which is: if anybody in this city has told you a different number, bring it and I will read it out again in the words you did not bring.**

What broke at the counter on the afternoon of the thirtieth was not a number. It was a woman of about sixty with a stick, who came at about four o'clock and had been in the place two days before, and who asked Rutt, in the words she brought, what was going to happen at the west end.

Rutt read her the twelfth part. She read all of it, both times, in the voice she uses for tolls, and she read the two things it is not and she read the line about the fifth thing.

"You have told me who holds it and for how long," said the woman with the stick, "**and I can read that. I have wanted to know since the twenty-first and I have got it.**

"**And you have not told me what is not going to happen, and there is a line on that sheet that says so, and I have been in this city nine days trying to get somebody to read me an absence and you have just read me one saying there isn't one.**"

"Yes," said Rutt.

"**Then the fifth thing is the one that matters and it is the one that nobody has a name for, and you should notice that a woman who cannot read a sentence has just located the hole in your district's constitution more accurately than anybody in a yard has in six weeks.**"

And Rutt wrote it down on her working page, which is not a public record, and gave it to Nessa Pell the same afternoon, and it went into the public lot book on the thirtieth in the registrar's hand, and it is the last line of the thirtieth.

---

**And the key turned on the twenty-ninth, at about ten to seven in the evening, eleven minutes after the twelfth part was published, in the same yard, with the same four seats named aloud, and the board asked nobody and the yard asked.**

Oswin Tace held it from the twenty-eighth for four days and it runs to midnight on the first of the next month, and the renewal was set for the thirtieth, and the board cannot ask a person whether they will do a job, and so on the evening before the renewal the yard did it, and about nine people said no in about ten minutes for reasons that went in, and the finding is the finding of the month and it is at the top of the notice board under two lapses.

> **A BOARD THAT ASKS AT THE END OF A TERM HAS NOT ASKED IN TIME. A BOARD THAT CANNOT ASK AT ALL AND LEAVES IT TO A CROWD IS A BOARD WHOSE ROTATION IS A WEATHER. TWO LAPSES IN ONE MONTH, FOUR DAYS AND TWO DAYS, SIX DAYS IN WHICH A CASE STOOD SHUT IN A LOCKED OFFICE ON A TERM THAT HAD RUN OUT. AND THE OBJECT INSIDE IT IS A PIECE OF BRASS THAT HAS A CRACK THROUGH IT AND WEIGHS TWO HUNDRED AND FOUR GRAINS AGAINST TWO HUNDRED AND ELEVEN, AND NOBODY HAS TAKEN ANYTHING.**

---

**And about nine people said no in about ten minutes, and the reasons are in the book, and three of them are the reasons the twenty-ninth needed, and the tenth person who said yes said it in eleven seconds and the yard went quiet for about four.**

**And the term the board set, all four seats named aloud, at about ten to seven on the twenty-ninth, eleven minutes after the twelfth part was published in the same yard, was four days, from the thirtieth of this month, expiring at midnight on the third of the next month, and the renewal is on the second, and the second is inside the week in which the ward market votes a rate and prints a timetable, and the board entered that it had not noticed that when it set the term, and entered it a second time about nine minutes later when the yard told it.**

The reasons the nine gave were ordinary and are printed at length because ordinary reasons are the ones a stranger will not believe: a man who has a shift; a woman who has a child and cannot be in a yard at seven; a cooper who said *I counted your lamps and I never counted your book and I would rather keep it that way*; a fitter who said *the last time I held a key I found a thing I wanted*; a signal hand who said *I am one of nine and if I am in a book by name in Alder Reach then the west end is a place a man is sent to and I go where it is sent*.

**And the tenth was Adrian Vale, and he said it before anybody had finished asking, and there is no sentence in this book about him on that evening that is longer than this one.**

"Yes."

**"You refused it on the twenty-sixth of last month in about eleven seconds,"** said Nessa Pell, "**and the refusal is in this book and it is one of the best things anybody has ever said in this yard, and I want you to tell me the difference out loud before you have it, because if you take it without saying the difference then you have taken it back.**"

"I have not taken it back," said Adrian Vale. "**On the twenty-sixth of February I had put one thousand five hundred and thirty-six coppers of my own inventory into a steward's hands, and the key was the same key, and if I had taken it that afternoon then I had taken it back, and there is no version of taking it back that is not that.**

"And there is no charter and no payment and no charter and no road. **The case is a case. There is a cracked piece of brass in it that weighs two hundred and four grains against two hundred and eleven and it has a crack through the plate and it may never open again and nobody has been paid anything, and the only thing standing between it and a stranger is a key and a book and a woman at a counter four days a week.**"

"And will you hold it past one term?"

"**I will hold it one term and then the yard will ask somebody else in public, and I am asking for that to be written on the sheet with my name on it, because the standing objection to everything I have done in five weeks is one man's limit checked by one man, and it is a fair objection, and the only answer anybody has ever found to it is a notice nailed up in eleven places.**" He turned round to the yard. "**And I am not a Binder and I cannot close anything and I cannot post a security, and a key is not a charter and I am not being made one, and if anybody in this yard has come here this evening expecting a road to open then they have come to the wrong yard and I would rather they had come to the right one and been told this in these words.**"

"Nobody thanked him," said Orren Pike, at the rail, in his own voice, to about ten feet of nobody. "**And nobody is going to, and he would not want it to, and I want it entered that I said so and that it is the fourth time this month that a person in this district has done something and not been thanked, and there is no column for that either.**"

---

**And Fen Ambrose was in that yard on the thirtieth, and she had not been asked to come and came anyway, and she is the person who wrote the form that counted a village as a household and who wrote the fifth rule of the index, and she has been four hundred yards away from all of it for ten days and has read every sheet on the wall.**

"I wrote the fifth rule," she said, to about nine people, at about five o'clock. "**In a yard of about six hundred people, in the rain, in the second week of last month, and I argued for it, and I have never once regretted it.

"And this morning it stopped a thing. **It stopped the district putting a job on a person who would not be named, and it was written for that, and it did that, and print this: on the twenty-ninth of this month a rule of ours cost us a convenience and worked. And print it next to the finding that a rule which has only ever stopped a person from doing something has not yet been tested against the district, and has now been, and cost us a boundary for about a day and a half.**"

"And the first version of the walk sheet is not struck," said the tenth hand.

"**No,**" said Fen Ambrose. "**And the fourth person's clause is not struck, and the mark is not struck, and the rule is not struck, and the first version of the sheet is not struck, and there are five things in this district's public record that are all wrong and none of them may be taken down, and I wrote two of the five.**"

---

**And the lease was surrendered at about half past two in the afternoon on the thirtieth of this month, in the yard of Lot 17, in about eleven minutes, and it is the shortest document Tarin Kest has ever signed and it is the only one he has ever signed on a public table with his own name at the top of it, and he read it out first.**

It is one sheet. It is headed *surrender of a lease* and not *assignment* and not *transfer*, and Stenn picked the word, and Tarin agreed to it, and the reason is in the minute: **you cannot assign a lease to a village, because a village is not a person and cannot hold paper, and if you assign it to the ward market then the ward market is the holder of a siding at a place it does not serve, and the market would be a road house with a different name.**

So it is not assigned to anybody.

> **THE LEASE OF THE SIDING AND THE METERED COLUMN AT THE NINTH PLACE, SIGNED IN THE NINTH YEAR BY T. KEST, EXPIRING IN THE FOURTH MONTH OF NEXT YEAR, IS SURRENDERED IN THE FOURTH MONTH BEFORE IT RUNS OUT, AND IS ENTERED IN THE WARD MARKET'S RATE SHEET OF THE TWENTY-EIGHTH AS HELD BY NOBODY. AND THE COLUMN IS ENTERED AS A COLUMN AND NOT AS A METER, AND THE METER IS ENTERED AS A METER THAT RAN FOR NINE YEARS AND THAT IS NOBODY'S, AND THE THIRTY-SIX COPPERS ARE ENTERED AS OWED TO NOBODY.**
>
> **AND HELD BY NOBODY IS A LINE THAT THIS MARKET HAS NEVER PRINTED, AND IT IS THE ONLY LINE ON THE SHEET THAT IS NOT ABOUT MONEY, AND IT IS THE SAME LINE AS THE FIRST ONE AND THE SHEET HAS TWO NOW.**

Tarin Kest read it out, and then he said the numbers, and he said them without being asked, and about two hundred and forty people were in that yard and it is in the book that nobody asked him for a figure and he gave three.

"**Eleven vehicles is a week's stabling on that siding in a normal month and I have run nine years on it. The Alder Reach yards take four. So there are seven vehicles a week in this district with nowhere to stand and I am the man who signs for the work and I have been signing for it on somebody else's ground since I was thirty-four.**

"**Four hundred and ten coppers. That is what the standing salvage in the Road House yard is worth in Alder Reach on a day when there is work. It is worth nothing at the ninth place, because there is no work at the ninth place and there is no street and there is no wagon road, and the difference between those two figures is the Road House and I have been living in the difference for nineteen years.**

"And the third figure is the one to be entered, and it is not a figure at all, and put it in the middle of the sheet where nobody skips it. **I have not staffed a span, I have not put a man on a line, and I have not asked anybody for anything, and I have done this before a wall went and not after, and not after a train was late, and I have said the harder half of it out loud on my own ballast on the ninth of this month in front of three people and it is in a book.**"

**And he was not let off, and he did not ask to be let off, and the woman who did it was the woman of fifty from the ninth place, and she had walked east on the thirtieth with the counts, and she came about ten feet forward in a yard of about two hundred and forty people and asked him a question that took about forty seconds and that has been the cost of this transaction ever since.**

"**Mr. Kest. I have got a question and I have had it since the ninth of this month and you gave me an answer on that day that was not the answer to it.**

"You told me you signed a piece of paper in about eleven minutes. So: **what did the water cost.**"

"Not thirty-six coppers—"

"**Not the money. I have seen the meter book, a clerk brought it to me on the thirtieth and told me the number himself, because he said a person who has been at a place ought to know what a place earns.**" She was not shouting and she had not come further than ten feet. "**What did the water cost me.**"

And Tarin Kest, who is forty-three and who has answered about a lease and a stabling yard and a repair crew and his own standing in about eleven minutes without stopping, stood in a yard in Alder Reach and could not answer that, and it took him about twenty seconds to fail to answer it and about ten seconds to say what his failure was.

"**Nobody cost it,**" he said. "**I drew that water off twice a week for nine years through men I paid, and four of them a week, and none of them ever asked you a question about anything except the water, and I have known that for nine years and I did not think of it as a cost, and it is not in any book in this city, and I have been the person who owned the meter and not the person who asked what the meter was for.**"

"**Then pay for it,**" said the woman of fifty. "**Not the water. I know what water costs. I have had it for eleven years for nothing and I have never once minded, and I have not said so to anybody, and I am saying it now so that there is one thing about this morning that is not a mess.**

"**What I have got is that I have had to fill two pails a day for eleven years off a thing a man in Alder Reach decided I should have, and I have never been asked whether I wanted it, and I would have said yes, and you have never once been in a room with me, and on the twenty-second of this month you were on my hard ground with three other people and you told me about a lease and I asked you whether the train was going to be moved in the night and you said you did not know.**

"So that is the cost. **Nine years and a man four times a week who has never once asked me a question about anything, and I have kept it in my head since the flood year and I have not got a column for it and nobody in this district has ever asked me for it.**"

And Nessa Pell, who is a clerk of nineteen years and who has been one of the only four keys in that lot for five weeks and who has never in her life put a sentence into a book that she did not have from somebody else, got up off a seat at the side of the yard and did the only thing she was able to do with it, which was to ask her one question, in the plainest form she had.

"**Will you say it to me now, in those words, and shall I write it down?**

"**And I want to say before you answer that I have been a clerk for nineteen years and I have never once put a mark on a page, and I have started one this month because a boy of fifteen handed me a sheet with a question on it and I refused to put it in the book, and it has been sitting on my desk for a fortnight, and it is the only piece of paper on my desk that anybody in this city is waiting for.**"

And the woman of fifty said it again, in the same words, and Nessa Pell wrote it down, and it is the last entry of the thirtieth of this month, and it is the first entry in that book that is a cost somebody has agreed to be paid, and **the name of the payer is not in it, and it is not going to be, and the clerk who wrote it entered that as her own decision and not as a rule.**

And the train was on the bank for the forty-seventh day, and the line was on the ground sixty yards past the column, and nobody had been paid anything, and the rate sheet had two lines on it that were not about money, and the boundary was a term and a month, and the yard emptied at about five, and the wall had six sheets on it and a hole where a seventh would go.
