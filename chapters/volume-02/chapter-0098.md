# Chapter 98: What the Iron Said

At about half past ten at night on the twenty-ninth of this month a man wrote nine words on a sheet of paper on a platform thirty feet long in the rain, and the nine words are the reason the reading was for, and he read them out afterwards in the ordinary voice to about forty people, which is the fourth time anybody has read him out to a room.

A woman of about sixty with a stick had asked for them on the twenty-eighth, at a counter, in the ordinary voice.

"I want the why on a page and not only the when."

"I can do that."

"**Then write it, because in about four years somebody is going to find that page and read the when, and if there is no why on it they will decide that you did it on purpose, and you did not, and they will be half right.**"

The nine words are these. **A bracket is the only part of this that is locked, and nobody knows what the iron says until a person reads it, and the reading may be wrong, and nine vehicles will already be on the loop.**

---

The reading was done at eleven o'clock at night on the boards of the platform with about forty people on the shoulder and one lamp at the west end and one at the east end, and it took about an hour, and nobody stood on the ballast, and the frame at the east end of that loop was locked, and the key was in the keeping of the only pair on that line.

> **A READING MAY BE SPENT ON ONE LOT YOU HAVE TOUCHED. IT COSTS ABOUT AN HOUR AND A COST, AND THE COST IS A PART OF THAT LOT'S TOLL.**
>
> **THE TOLL ON THIS IRON IS THE ORIGIN. AN ORIGIN TAKES MEMORY OF DEPARTURE. YOU HAVE PAID IT TWICE AND YOU HAVE TWO LEFT, AND TWO IS A NUMBER AND NOT A PRINCIPLE.**
>
> **THE IRON DOES NOT HOLD PLACES AND WILL NOT NAME ONE. IT WILL NOT TELL YOU WHETHER THE BRACKET TAKES IT.**
>
> **A READING MOVES NOTHING ON THAT LINE. A READING ALSO DOES NOT MAKE A MAN ABLE TO DO A THING HE COULD NOT DO AN HOUR AGO. IT IS NOT A REWARD AND IT IS NOT A STAGE.**

It came back in one line, in the form of March and the form of April, word for word the form both of them took, and it is in the book in his own hand and it is nine words long. **THE BRACKET IS CUT FOR IT AND THE BRACKET WILL TAKE IT.**

Nobody in that room had been told what that would sound like and about fourteen people said afterwards that they had expected something longer.

The cost was specific to that lot's toll and it was not the cost of the twenty-seventh of April repeated. **An origin takes memory of departure.** In March it took a direction. In April it took the memory of which gate he went out of his own city by, on the seventeenth of February at six in the morning, and it had not come back. This time it took the twenty-second of last month at about six in the morning, when he left Alder Reach to walk to the ninth place for the first time in nine weeks, got as far as the second changeover, and turned back and came down the bank again.

He wrote it down himself before he could stand up. The woman who keeps the only pair on that line read it back, because that is her job on a term of thirty days and not as a courtesy, and the entry is in her hand and not in his, and the finding of the thirtieth is that **the first time in nine weeks that a cost has been entered in somebody else's hand, the somebody was the keeper of the pair, and a keeper of a pair is not a person anybody can be sent to, and the two halves of that are now on the same line.**

He has one left, and the figure is in the book as a figure and not as a principle.

The echo was the echo of an origin toll, which is a loss of spatial sense. A man with it cannot tell which way a river is going and cannot hold a pair. **Corvin Sallow took him by the arm at about ten past midnight and walked him thirty feet to the door of the hut and would not let go of him until he was on the step, and the entry says in both their hands that the man who was walked said the word *which* four times on the way and could not finish any of the sentences.**

---

**And then the security was posted, and the split was closed, and neither of those two things has ever happened in this district, and the entry that records them is four paragraphs long and it is in the public lot book and it is on the fourth toll board at the platform in the same words.**

A security is a thing somebody else can go and look at. That is the definition this district worked out in May, it is nine words long, and it is the only definition anybody here has ever used.

His is a term of labour and a term of stock, and not one thing of either that anybody could call a recognition. The labour is one day a week at this place from the first hour of the morning until the hour a train is due to move, from the twenty-ninth of this month to the twenty-ninth of next month, and **the day is written on the fourth toll board in chalk, and anybody may go and stand on that platform on that day and see whether he is on it, and nobody is paid for it and he is not paid for it.**

The stock is three bound copies of the line book at eleven coppers each, thirty-three coppers, out of his own hand. One is on a shelf in the hut at this place, one is in the ward market counting room, and one is in the Needle Road House, and anybody may go and look at all three of them, and any of the four people who can revoke this charter may send any of them anywhere.

> **THE SECURITY POSTED ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JUNE BY MR VALE IS A TERM OF LABOUR AND A TERM OF STOCK AND IT IS NOT HIS RECOGNITION AND IT IS NOT HIS MARK AND IT IS NOT ANYTHING HE HAS ACCUMULATED IN NINE WEEKS. IT IS ONE DAY A WEEK AND THREE BOUND COPIES OF A LINE BOOK. IT ENDS ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JULY.**
>
> **A TRANSFERABLE RIGHT IS NOT A NEUTRAL RIGHT. THE WEEK MOVES. WHOEVER HOLDS IT DID NOT ASK FOR IT AND THE PEOPLE IT LANDS ON WERE NOT ASKED. HE HAS GAINED A RESPONSIBILITY AND NOT AN AUTHORITY. HE IS NOT ONE OF THE FOUR BEARERS AND HE IS NOT A KEEPER OF A BOARD, AND THE LINE ON PAGE ONE SAYING HE IS NOT ONE OF THE FOUR BEARERS IS THE FIRST LINE IN THIS DISTRICT'S HISTORY THAT IS STILL TRUE OF HIM FOUR MONTHS AFTER IT WAS WRITTEN.**
>
> **THIS CHARTER CAN BE REVOKED BY FOUR PEOPLE WHO DO NOT LIKE HIM, AND NONE OF THEM NEEDS HIS PERMISSION, AND THE FOUR ARE THE THREE WHO HOLD THE WEEKS AND THE MAN WHO ENDS THE USE.**
>
> **A PERSON WHO CAN BE REPLACED HAS NOT BEEN WORKED OUT AND NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT KNOWS HOW TO DO IT, AND THAT IS ENTERED AS NOT KNOWN AND NOT AS A DELAY.**

Two fresh witnesses who do not travel accepted it. One is a boy of seventeen who keeps an index, is paid four coppers a quarter, and is not Adrian Vale's apprentice. The other is a woman of about sixty with a stick who is on no roll and is not in a row and has been in four rooms in nine weeks.

The two who were on the bank were not asked to witness and the two who were not on the bank were, and the finding of the thirtieth is that **a witness is a person who will be somewhere else when the reason is read out, and this district has been choosing its witnesses out of the people who walk for a living for nine weeks and has never once asked whether they would be there.**

He could do it. Nobody made a ceremony and there was no panel.

And there is a thing about the twenty minutes between the closing and the throw that four people wrote down and none of them put in a book, and it is that he sat down on the boards of his own platform for about two minutes and did not say anything. He has been asked about it twice since and has given the same answer both times, and the answer is nine words long and it is not in the book.

"I had been told no four times and I did it once, **and I wanted two minutes of not being asked anything, and I have not had that since the eighth of March, and there is a man on that bank who has had it every day of his life for nineteen years and I understand now what it is for.**"

The minute says in his own words that he noticed the moment and could not say what he had noticed, and the clerk wrote *the stage is not a moment* in the margin and underlined it twice, and he asked for both underlines to stay.

---

The iron went into the bracket at about twenty minutes to midnight, and the only pair on that line put it in and nobody else was allowed to touch it, and the throw of the points was at about a quarter to twelve.

Nine vehicles standing for a hundred and thirty-eight days came out of a siding facing west and went out backwards and round that loop and came out onto the bank facing east, and it took forty minutes, and nobody in this district had ever watched it happen.

The engine had not been steamed since the flood year and a man of sixty-eight had it ready at about nine o'clock, and the first thing anybody heard was not the engine. It was eleven men on a shoulder shouting one word at each other over the sound of rain on a platform, and then it was the engine.

The ninth vehicle cleared the points at about twenty minutes to twelve, and **the boy of seventeen wrote the time down, and it is the only time in nine hundred and six movements in that line book that the ninth vehicle is the ninth vehicle, and the finding of the thirtieth is that a line book has had a column for the number of a vehicle since the flood year and nobody had ever had a reason to write in it.**

The train went east and stopped at the fourth place at about ten past midnight, where there is a toll board and no platform and no station, and four people got off it onto a shoulder in the dark, and three of them had asked to be put down there and one of them had not been asked and is in the count as not asked.

One of the three was a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and he had said in four rooms in nine weeks that he could not get a person off a place in the dark, and he got off one, and a boy of nineteen who is nobody's keeper walked him two hundred yards to a gate with a lamp he did not have lit and did not light, and the entry says the boy did it because the gate is the fourth of four houses a paperer buys paper off and the boy knows it.

It stopped at the east end at about half past midnight, eleven minutes' walk from the ward market counter, and a clerk of that market who is not the market stood in the rain on a platform she had not asked for and watched nine vehicles go past her and out into the yards where they had been standing for nine years.

And then it came back, and it came back to this place. **The run was two movements and the second one was east, and the toll was charged once, and the origin was the ninth place, and the ninth place had been asked, and not one of the hundred and forty-one had ever been asked before that.**

---

**And the thing this district has wanted for nine weeks did not happen, and the thing that did happen was written down with a date, and the date is the twenty-ninth of June, and it is on the board at the platform in chalk and on the boy of seventeen's index and in the public lot book in Alder Reach and in the water office's own book, and the four copies are not joined.**

A reversal leaves a stall where the train stood. Every one of the hundred and forty-one left one. The line's own book has nine hundred and six movements in it, the index at Alder Reach has twelve stalls in it and the line book has eleven, and the difference is a stall with no signal in it, ninety-four yards long, measured on the second morning of May by a signalman who had a tape because that is what a signalman has.

The boy of seventeen had the tape on the night of the twenty-ninth and walked the shoulder twice before and twice after, and the finding is in his hand and it is the finding he is proudest of and he has said so out loud exactly once.

> **THE DIFFERENCE IS NO LONGER A LENGTH. THE DIFFERENCE IS NOTHING. THE INDEX HAS THIRTEEN STALLS IN IT AND THE LINE BOOK HAS THIRTEEN, AND THE ONE THAT WAS MISSING IS THE ONE AT THIS PLACE, AND IT IS THIRTY-ONE FEET LONG, AND THE PLATFORM IS THIRTY FEET LONG, AND NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAD EVER PUT THOSE TWO LENGTHS ON THE SAME LINE.**

And then the part that is not the finding, which he wrote underneath it, and the registrar has asked that it not be joined to the part above it.

> **AND THE STALL DID NOT WALK WEST. IT IS WHERE THE TRAIN STOOD AND IT IS STILL THERE, AND NOBODY WENT AND LOOKED FOR IT AFTER THE FOURTH DAY, AND A MAN WITH A TAPE WALKED THE NINETY-FOUR YARDS AND THE TWO HUNDRED YARDS AND THE NINE HUNDRED FEET OF EMBANKMENT BEYOND THE DRAINAGE CUT AND FOUND NOTHING THAT MOVED IN FOUR HOURS.**

"Nobody knows why," said the tenth hand, in a yard in Alder Reach at about thirteen in the morning on the thirtieth, to about sixty people, and it went in the minute in those words, and then he said the second half of it, and the second half is the reason the finding is a finding.

"**A district that has measured a thing and does not know why is in a different position from a district that has not measured it, and the difference is about four hours of walking and a tape, and it is not about the ground.**"

---

And at about twenty past one in the morning the water came up over the far leg of that loop, and the fourth bearer ended the use.

Nobody on that shoulder knew what they had been standing in the middle of, and the entry for the twenty-ninth says so in a line that the clerk of nineteen years wrote and did not soften, and the line is that about forty people watched nine vehicles come out of a siding and go round a loop in the dark in the rain because a man they had all said no to had said he would read an iron and then had not said no.

**Nineteen of the forty were people who are not in a household and are not in no row and are not counted anywhere including on the sheet that collects the four thousand and one, and they were on that shoulder because somebody held a door, and the finding is that the nineteen were the only people there who had not been asked anything about it and were the only ones who did not appear to want to have been.**

One of them is a man of about forty-one who has been at this place three years and is not on any roll, and he was asked on the twentieth of this month whether he wanted a platform and said yes in about two seconds, and at about ten minutes to midnight on the twenty-ninth he said the only sentence anybody said that night that was not about a railway.

"I do not know what that was," he said. "**I have been standing on a bank in the rain since about eight o'clock and a thing went round and I could not tell you what it was and I am not going to pretend to anybody in the morning that I could.**"

The tenth hand wrote that down in his own hand and it went in the book in his own hand, and the finding of the thirtieth of this month is that **this is the first sentence anybody in this district has said about a railway that was not a finding, and it was said by a person who is not in a household, and it went into the public lot book before anything else that night did.**



He is Elias Rooke, sixty-one, who has held a pair on this line for thirty-four years and has kept the frame at the twelfth place since the fourth year, and he had been in the hut at this place with two other people in it since about ten o'clock, and he did not know until the moment who the two other people were going to be.

He put his hand on the lever and reversed what was running and said the reason out loud, and the reason went into a public book, and the reason is nine words and it is not about the water.

> A THING THAT HAS STOOD FOR A HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHT DAYS IS NOT ENDED BY A FLOOD.

"That is a good reason," said the man of sixty-eight, from the ballast, at about twenty-five past one in the morning, with the water coming over a rail two hundred yards away.

"It is the only reason I have got," said the fourth bearer. "**I have been on my own in a hut for nineteen years and I have never had a reason in writing before tonight, and I have written nine words and I would like somebody to tell me if nine is too many.**"

Nobody told him. The minute enters that nobody told him, and that the reason went on the fourth toll board at the platform at about half past two in the morning in chalk, in a hand that is not anybody else's in this district, and that it is the twenty-first sentence on that board and the first one anybody has ever written on a board at twenty-five minutes past one in the morning and had it read out by somebody else in front of forty people in daylight.

---

**And the toll landed on the ninth place at about a quarter to two in the morning and nobody there felt it, and the finding of the thirtieth is that the cost of a thing is always paid by somebody who is not the person who caused it and is always the last thing anybody looks for.**

The keeper of the book of departures is fifty-one and has been eleven feet above that flood line for a year, and she and the woman of the column went through the book together from about half past two until about four in the morning, with a lamp, in the rain, page by page.

One hundred and forty-one out of one hundred and forty-one was what this place could produce at eight o'clock the evening before. One hundred and forty out of one hundred and forty-two is what it can produce now. **The one it cannot produce is the one they made, and neither of the two women knows that anything has happened, and the difference between those two facts is the whole of what a toll on a place is.**

Forty people were on that platform or in that room or on that shoulder, and not one of them can say afterwards what the nine vehicles looked like going round, and two of them were on the ballast at the far end of the platform with their faces turned and were looking straight at it.

And there is one more thing about the toll and it is in the book in two hands and neither hand is his.

The woman of the column wrote under the keeper's figures, in her own hand, that she had stood on that bank for eleven years and had been the water for eleven years and had never once been asked what the water cost, and that on the twenty-ninth of this month she was asked, and that the answer she gave was a figure and not a feeling. **She would like it entered that she would not have been able to say that figure on the twenty-eighth, and that the difference is a night with forty people on it.**

**And the entry for the thirtieth is that this district spent nine weeks trying to stop a train costing a village something, and that on the twenty-ninth of June a train cost that village exactly one departure out of a hundred and forty-two, which is about a half of one in a hundred, and that a half of one in a hundred is the first toll anybody in this basin has ever been able to state, and that it is stated because the place it landed on was asked first, and was not asked first by accident.**

In the hours the counter was shut on the thirtieth of this month, a clerk of the ward market who is not the market wrote the night into the line book in about twenty minutes, and the finding is that it took her twenty minutes to write six lines and that four of the six lines are the same four words this line book has had since the flood year.

> **THE NINTH PLACE. THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JUNE. A PLACE A THING STOPS AT. NOT A SIGNAL. ONE MOVEMENT. THE ORIGIN HELD. THE NINTH PLACE.**
>
> **AND UNDER IT, IN THE COLUMN FOR THE NUMBER OF A VEHICLE, WHICH NOBODY HAD EVER WRITTEN IN, ONE WORD. NINE.**

And a registrar entered the delay for the whole of the twenty-ninth, which is the seventh delay this district has published, and the reason is the reason she gives every time, and the reason on this one is that three books in three rooms had to have a night in them and the night happened on a platform nine miles away.

And on the morning of the thirtieth, in a yard in Alder Reach, a mechanic of forty-three read the whole of it out of a book to about thirty people of his own, and said one thing at the end of it, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**I signed the paper that made this a place a man could put a post in, and I gave the ground up, and I posted the salvage that is now a platform, and there are nine vehicles standing on a place that did not exist in March, and I have a yard on the tide once a month for a rehearsal and no yard for a cart. I am not asking for any of it back. I am asking that the entry say that a thing a man gives up and a thing a man is given are not the same size and that this district has only ever found out which was which after the giving.**"
