# Chapter 93: Twice Is Not a Rotation

On the eleventh of this month the reader at the ward market counter read out a date, and then read out that there was nothing under it, and about thirty people stood at that counter, and she is paid eleven coppers a quarter for four days a week and the term has no clause for a shoulder.

The date is the eleventh of this month. The lot offered an hour on the eleventh of this month, and the eleventh of this month was the first day the limit permitted one since the twenty-seventh of April, and he was in the yard of Lot 17 at about ten in the morning and he was asked by a man who does not sell anything, and he said no in one word, and the word was short, and everybody in that yard had heard him say a longer version of it four times in nine weeks.

"**Entered,**" said Nessa Pell, who is a clerk of nineteen years and the sitting steward of the public lot and who has, without being asked, been the person in the room who is neither the one asking nor the one being asked for nineteen years. "**The question is entered. The hour is entered. The non-event is entered with no cost and no time. And nobody in this book is going to write it as cowardice and nobody is going to write it as discipline.**"

"And if either word is written against it," she said, "I will take it out, and I have never taken a line out of anything in nineteen years and I am not starting with this one."

---

**And the finding of the eleventh of this month is that the reason he gave is a number, and that the number is the only part of it that is new, and it is a number he arrived at on the twentieth of last month and has not used until now.**

The cost of a reading is not a figure and is not a duration. It is a countable number of the specific thing it takes, and the specific thing this iron takes is memory of departure, and he has paid it twice.

In March it took his sense of which way a river went, for about fifty minutes, on a bank nine miles from the book.

In April it took the memory of which gate he went out of his own city by, and there is no west gate, and the departure it took is dated on the seventeenth of February of this year at six in the morning to look at a pumping engine, and twenty-three days after the reading it had not come back, and nobody in this district can say whether it will.

"**Two left,**" he said, in the yard, to about sixty people, on the eleventh of this month, and it is in the minute in those words, "**and I am not going to spend one this morning on a thing I do not yet know whether I am allowed to do, and that is the whole of it, and I would like it entered that I have said that sentence four times and it has got shorter every time and that today it was a number instead of a sentence and that is new and I have noticed it.**"

The intervals have gone up on use: seven days, then eleven, then fourteen. Ivo Stenn entered in April that this is not a rule getting stricter, and that a rule that gets stricter gets stricter for a reason and says so, and that he would not defend it.

"**A number is not a plan,**" said Stenn, at the counter, afterwards, to a man who had asked him about it. "He has turned a principle into a figure and that is progress, and a figure is not a plan, because a plan says what the hour is for and he has not got one, and I have been waiting nine weeks for a man with a reading to tell this district what an hour is for, and he has not done it, and I do not think he is going to."

"The next permitted hour is not before the twenty-fifth of this month."

"Then we have a fortnight," said somebody.

"**We have a fortnight in which somebody in this district has to do something that is not reading.**"

---

The entry of the eleventh of this month is the shortest search this district has ever run and the longest fortnight anybody in it has spent not knowing something, and it is in the public lot book in a clerk's hand with the no in it in his own words and the count in front of it.

> **THE ELEVENTH OF JUNE. THE LIMIT PERMITTED ONE HOUR AND IT WAS OFFERED AND IT WAS NOT TAKEN. THE QUESTION WAS ASKED BY A MAN WHO IS NOT AN OFFICER OF ANYTHING AND CANNOT BE PAID. THE ANSWER WAS ONE WORD. THERE IS NO COST IN THIS ENTRY AND THERE IS NO TIME IN THIS ENTRY. THE NUMBER OF DEPARTURES HE HAS LEFT IS TWO AND HE SAID THAT NUMBER OUT LOUD IN A YARD OF ABOUT SIXTY PEOPLE, WHICH IS THE FIRST TIME HE HAS GIVEN A FIGURE INSTEAD OF A PRINCIPLE, AND HE ASKED FOR IT TO BE ENTERED THAT HE HAD NOTICED.**
>
> **A REFUSAL TO READ IS A READING AND IT IS ENTERED. IT IS NOT COWARDICE AND IT IS NOT DISCIPLINE. IF EITHER WORD IS EVER WRITTEN AGAINST THIS ENTRY THE CLERK WHO HAS KEPT THE BOOK FOR NINETEEN YEARS WILL TAKE IT OUT, AND SHE HAS NEVER TAKEN A LINE OUT OF ANYTHING, AND SHE IS NOT STARTING WITH THIS ONE.**

And the next permitted hour is not before the twenty-fifth of this month, and nobody wrote a reason for that on the page, and Stenn wrote one on a separate sheet and read it out at the counter the same afternoon, and the separate sheet says in eleven words: **the intervals have gone up on use and a rule that goes up on use is a man buying days, and I will not defend it and I am not going to pretend it is anything else.**

---

And the other thing that happened on the eleventh of this month is the reason there is going to be a third asking on the twenty-ninth, and it is that the woman at the column asked a market for something in May and the market has entered that it will be done and has not decided when, **and it turns out that the thing it was waiting for was a walk.**

Her request is in the public lot book in her own words, entered on the first of June out of a room nine miles away, and read out twice by a paperer who had never read anything out loud in her life.

The next time this market sends anybody up this bank, it sends them up with the sheet. Not with a bag. With the sheet, and somebody who can read it out loud in this room, and the reading-back, and the reason, which is that a stranger is owed the reading.

The market has entered that it will be. The market has not decided when. And for eight weeks the reason it had not decided was a fact about money, **and on the eleventh of this month the reason it had not decided turned out to be that nobody had ever put the two halves of the sentence in the same order.**

The first half is the sheet. The ward market's rate sheet for a place with four households on it, and a figure on it, and a column of expenses in four lines.

The second half is that reading a sheet aloud in a room nine miles and five and a quarter hours away takes a whole day, and the reader at the ward market counter is a woman of about fifty with a shoulder and is paid eleven coppers a quarter for four days a week, and cannot do it, and nobody had asked her.

---

The room found the person who could do it in about eleven minutes on the twelfth of this month, and the finding is that the person was a woman of about thirty-eight with a hook and a sacking sheet who buys old paper off four houses once a month, and that nobody in that room had thought of her until somebody said her trade out loud.

"**Whose trade is it to read a sheet to a room,**" said the clerk of the ward market, who had said in a room in May that it would not be her again and had said it in nine words in front of about thirty people. "**Not mine. Mine is figures. Not hers, and hers is figures in other people's writing, which is not the same thing, and there is exactly one person in this district who gets paid for reading other people's documents and it is not a market and it is not a guild and it is not in the count of trades in our room.**"

She is Jessa Olwin. She is about thirty-eight. She goes up the bank on a day she chooses, because she goes to four houses and she knows which day each of them has paper on, and she has been doing it for nine years.

She said yes before she was asked, which is the sixth time in nine weeks that somebody in Alder Reach has answered a question about themselves before it was put, and she gave two things with it and both of them are in the minute.

"One. **I go up on the day I go up, and the day I go up in this month is the twenty-ninth, and that is not a coincidence and it is not a favour, because the four houses and I have been doing this on the twenty-ninth since the flood year and it is the day the second house clears its back room.**

"Two. You are not buying a day. I am going up there on the twenty-ninth whatever this is, and I am going to be four houses deep, and the fifth thing about where you stand is not going to happen at a house, and it is going to happen at about eleven o'clock at night at the end of the second house, and if the two of you are standing there then it happens, and if you are not then you have come four hours for nothing and I am not sorry for you."

The market paid her eleven coppers, and the entry is in the minute in the secretary's hand, and it says in full **that the eleven coppers are for reading a sheet aloud and for the reading-back, and are not for the day, and that the day was hers.**

They came out of the unassigned thirty. The rate is fifty coppers a quarter; fourteen is the line, six is printed matter, and **thirty of the fifty has never been assigned to anything by anybody and has now paid a woman eleven coppers to stand in a room and say what a sheet says, and the entry notes that this is the first thing in eleven weeks that the unassigned thirty has been asked to do.**

---

And on the twelfth of this month the salvage went up the bank, and it went up in eleven loads on a cart that a firm that gave up a lease in March no longer owns. **The finding attached to it is that four hundred and ten coppers' worth of old sleepers has been sitting in a yard in Alder Reach for nine years in the rain because nobody could work out what a security was for.**

There are about a hundred and forty sleepers and about four hundred and ten coppers' worth of boards, and the sleepers are creosoted and the boards are not, and a mechanic of forty-three who founded the Needle Road House and who signed a nine-year lease on this ground in the ninth year, and who surrendered that lease on the thirtieth of March on a sheet headed surrender assigned to nobody, loaded every one of them himself.

Two men went with the cart. The third man of the party was a boy of nineteen who asked to come and was not asked and has not been asked anything since, and who said on the bank, in front of all three of them, that he was nobody's keeper, and that he had come because there is a piece of cast iron on a bench four hours further on and he wanted to see the hut from the outside again.

The clerk wrote all of that down and read it back to him on the shoulder, which is her job and not a courtesy, and **the entry on the twelfth records that he was on the bank for no reason that can be entered, and that a person present for no reason is present, and that this district has entered that sentence four times in eleven weeks and has never once been able to say what it is for.**

"Where are we taking them," said the boy, at the fourth place.

"To a place that has never had one," said the mechanic.

---

**And the sheet for the twenty-ninth went into the public lot book on the thirteenth of this month at nine in the morning, and it is the first sheet this district has published that begins with a person who is not an officer, a clerk, or a reader, and it is four lines long and the fourth line is the one that matters.**

> **THE ASKING WILL BE DONE BY A PAPERER OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WHO BUYS OLD PAPER OFF FOUR HOUSES ONCE A MONTH AND WHO IS PAID ELEVEN COPPERS OUT OF THIRTY THAT NOBODY HAS EVER ASSIGNED TO ANYTHING, AND THE ELEVEN COPPERS ARE FOR READING A SHEET ALOUD AND FOR A READING-BACK AND ARE NOT FOR THE DAY, AND THE DAY WAS HERS ALREADY.**
>
> **THE HOUR IS A DAY AND A NIGHT. FIVE AND A QUARTER HOURS GOING AND FIVE AND A HALF COMING AND ELEVEN AND THREE-QUARTERS IN THE DAY, WHICH IS TEN AND THREE-QUARTERS OF WALKING AND AN HOUR OF STANDING STILL, AND THAT FIGURE IS THE PERSON'S AND NOT THE TENTH HAND'S AND NEITHER OF THEM IS GOING TO ARGUE ABOUT IT IN FRONT OF TWO PEOPLE.**
>
> **THE RATE SHEET GOES UP FIRST AND IS READ ALOUD BEFORE ANY QUESTION IS ASKED, BECAUSE A STRANGER IS OWED THE READING, AND THE READING-BACK IS DONE BY THE TENTH HAND, WHO IS NOBODY'S EMPLOYEE AND CANNOT BE PAID AND HAS SAID HE WILL DO IT.**
>
> **MR VALE IS NINE MILES EAST AND IS NOT ASKED AND HAS SAID SO IN ADVANCE. THE WOMAN OF FORTY-FIVE IS NOT ASKED AND IS IN THE COUNT AS NOT ASKED AND IS NOT NAMED. NOBODY FROM A RIVER ADMINISTRATION IS IN THAT ROOM.**

The registrar entered the delay for it herself, which is the sixth time, and the reason she gives is the reason she gives every time, and the reason on this one is a new clause and it is that the sheet was decided in a counting room in Alder Reach and a sheet about a place nine miles away is a sheet about a place a stranger cannot see, **and that is the second time this district has published a delay and the first time it has published a delay for a document that was not late.**

---

**And the finding of the thirteenth of this month is the one the whole of this batch turns on, and it is four sentences long, and it was said by a man who is not an officer of anything and cannot be paid, and nobody argued with it.**

A rotation turned in April on a key and turned in May on a boundary. Both of them cost somebody a day.

The first asking was done by a signalman of the tenth hand, who walked five and a quarter hours up a bank and asked two women a question in a room and read the whole of it back, and who is not paid and cannot be.

The second was done by a clerk of the ward market, who said in a room in May that she would do it once and that a clerk is a person and a person can be replaced, and she has said since that it will not be her.

"A rotation that has turned twice has not been shown that it can turn," the tenth hand said. "It has been shown that this district will pay for the walk twice. The first one cost a man a day he was not paid for. The second one cost a woman a day and a night and eleven and three-quarters hours, and she told this room the figure herself, and it is her own figure and it is not mine, and neither of us is going to argue about it in front of two people.

"That is a subsidy and not a rotation. **A subsidy is a thing you stop. A rotation is a thing you do not have to pay for, and the difference between those two things is the whole of what a boundary is and the whole of what an office is**, and the difference between them was written on a board at the east end in May by a signalman who is not a bearer, and I would like somebody in this room to read that board out loud some morning.

"The third one is the first one nobody has had to be given a day for. And I want that written down, **and I want it written down by a person who is not me, because I have been the man in the room for nine weeks and I have noticed that the things I say get entered and the things other people say get read out, and both of those are wrong and only one of them is my fault.**"

And the clerk of the ward market read it out at the counter at about eleven o'clock that morning, in the ordinary voice, and about forty people stood there.

---

And the question of whether anybody was asked was asked in the same room by the tenth hand, and the answer was that four people are not on the ninth place's rate sheet and thirty-one people are on the bank. **A rotation which turns on a walk is a rotation which depends on somebody with a spare day, and a spare day is a wage, and nobody in this district pays a wage for going up a bank.**

The finding entered is that **the district asked forty-one people a question about the walk in nine weeks in May and got two yeses, and one of those two yeses was a person who was already going, and that the reason the second one was cheap is the only reason the third one is free, and that this is the first time in nine weeks that something this district needed turned out to be cheaper than the thing it was replacing.**

And a boy of seventeen who keeps an index and is not Adrian Vale's apprentice said one thing in the room, and the clerk wrote it down and read it back, and the clerk is the woman of about thirty-eight with the hook and reading somebody else's sentence aloud is her trade.

"Twice is not a rotation," he said. "**Twice is a market deciding what a day costs. I have been paid four coppers a quarter for an index since the fourteenth of last month and I have never once been asked whether I wanted the index, and nobody in this district asks anybody anything before they pay them, and that is not a small thing and it is the same shape as the walk and everybody here can see the shape.**"

Nobody answered him and the minute enters that nobody answered him and that he was not thanked and that he had said the same class of thing four times in eleven weeks and been wrong about the meaning three of them.

And what she said about the four houses, in the room, afterwards, unasked, was that she is not going to say what the four houses gave her and never has, and that a person who buys other people's paper for a living is the only person in this district who is handed a document without being asked for it, and that **this is why she can say what a question was better than anybody who has been trained to say it, and it is not a skill and it cannot be put on a wall, and the wall has five things on it and none of them is the sixth one.**

The sixth thing is a person in the room who is not the one asking and is not the one being asked, whose job is to say what the question was. It is not a power, it is not on the board, and it belongs to a clerk of nineteen years.

On the twenty-ninth of this month it is going to be done in a front room nine miles from Alder Reach by a paperer of about thirty-eight who came into the counting room in May, said the question out loud before anybody answered it, gave her name when she was asked for it, and has not been thanked once in three months.

---

The third asking is on the twenty-ninth of this month. It is entered on a sheet with the hour on it, in the clerk's own hand, and the sheet says five and a quarter hours going and five and a half coming and eleven and three-quarters in the day, which is ten and three-quarters of walking and an hour of standing still, and the sheet says that figure is hers and not anybody else's.

**And under the heading on a wall in Alder Reach that says NOT ANSWERED, the third line anybody has entered in nineteen years went in on the thirteenth of this month, and it is eleven words long, and it names a person and does not explain her, and it is the answer to a question she asked herself in May.**

**THE PERSON IS A PAPERER AND SHE IS NOT ASKED YET.**

And the last thing on the sheet of the twenty-ninth is a line that the market's secretary wrote and could not make fit in either column, and it is this: **the sheet goes up with the sheet. The reading happens before the question, because a stranger is owed the reading. And the fifth thing about where you stand has not been moved to suit anybody, and the room is a front room with a board over one pane of the window, and if the two of you cannot stand in it then this is not the asking.**
