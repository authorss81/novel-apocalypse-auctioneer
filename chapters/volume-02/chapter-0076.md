# Chapter 76: The Name on the Lease

At about ten in the morning on the twentieth of this month a woman of about sixty-three turned a book round on a table so that the spine faced the man standing in front of it, and then she took her hand off it, and the gesture was so complete and so unhurried that nobody in the room ever afterwards described it as anything except a woman putting a book where a man could reach it.

The room is on the ground floor of a building behind the River and Carriage offices in the ward market district. There is a printing press in the front half and a fire in a grate in the back half and a window with a board nailed over the broken pane. There is one table, four chairs, and a book about two inches thick with eleven leases in it, and the book has a brass hook at the spine and the hook is what the room is for.

She is called Junia Crale. She has been in that room twenty-two years and she has read out, by her own count, four thousand and something leases to people who did not want to hear them.

---

**And what came out of it in a morning is three things, and only one of them is an answer, and the two that are not answers are the reason this room is going to be quoted in this district for longer than anything on the eleven boards.**

The three came out in this order.

First, the hook. There are eleven leases on it and she has never once been asked to say what the commonest thing about them was, and it is in the book in her own words because Orren Pike asked her, in a room with a fire in it, at about eleven o'clock, in the plainest voice he had.

"**Nine of the eleven name a firm,**" she said. "**The Knowles and Fenn Dairy. The River and Carriage Company. The Two SistersLaundry, which was one firm and is two now and I have not had a paper about it since the fourteenth year. The Needle Road House.**"

"And the other two," said Orren Pike, who is seventeen and has been paid four coppers a quarter to know where every object on this line is.

"**Name people. And both of them have been renewed.**"

---

**And the second thing is the one the morning was for, and it took about eleven minutes to say and about an hour to believe, and Adrian Vale wrote none of it down because he had nothing to write and said so.**

The lease is dated the ninth year. It is one page and it is the plainest document any of them had ever read, and Crale read it out in the ordinary way at about a quarter to eleven, twice, because the first time Orren Pike did not follow the second line and the second time he did.

> **THE SIDING AT THE FIGURE ONE MILE AND THREE QUARTERS ON THE NEEDLE LINE, THE WATER COLUMN UPON IT, A SHED OF SIXTEEN FEET BY THIRTY FEET ON THE NORTH SIDE OF THE SIDING, AND THE GROUND BETWEEN THEM. HELD FOR NINE YEARS FROM THE DATE ABOVE. METERED WATER AT FOUR COPPERS A QUARTER.**
>
> **THE LESSEE IS THE NEEDLE ROAD HOUSE, A FIRM, AND ITS PLACE OF BUSINESS IS ENTERED IN THE WARD MARKET'S OWN BOOK OF TRADES, AND THE SIGNATORY IS TARIN KEST, MECHANIC, WHO SIGNED IN THE ROOM OPPOSITE IN ABOUT ELEVEN MINUTES.**

Nobody in the room said anything for a while.

Then Tarin Kest said, "**That is me.**"

"**That is you,**" said Junia Crale. "**And the paper does not name you, and I want to be exact, because this is the part everybody gets wrong when they hear about it afterwards. The paper does not say a man. The paper says a firm, and the firm is entered in another book, and the other book has got an address on it and a man behind the address and about nineteen other men, and the paper has got a hook number on it in my hand, and the hook number is a room.**"

"**So the hook is the holder,**" said Adrian.

"**The hook is where I keep it,**" said Crale. "**And that is the whole of my job and I have been doing it for twenty-two years and I have never had it explained to me and I never asked, and you may note that in your book, because I have noticed this morning that it is the first time in twenty-two years that four people have stood in this room and none of them wanted a lease.**"

---

And then the question this district has been arguing about for five weeks was asked in that room, and it took about four minutes, and it was asked by the lease clerk and not by anybody who came up the bank, and neither of them had thought of it.

"**Ask me who holds it,**" she said.

Nobody asked her.

"**That is the answer,**" said Junia Crale. "**Twenty-two years, four thousand leases, and the answer to the question is that nobody has ever stood in this room and asked me who holds the paper, and it is not because you did not think of it. It is because you have all been going round the outside of this building for seven weeks asking rooms whether they can be asked, and this is a room, and I have been in it the whole time, and I am not on a board and I am not a fifth power of anything.**"

And then she said the sentence that is in the book in her own words and that the registrar asked to have it indexed, and Orren Pike wrote it on the back of his own hand in pencil and then, after a moment, crossed it out and wrote it on the sheet, because a thing on the back of a hand is a thing a man can lose.

> **THE PERSON WHO HELD THE PAPER IS NOT THE PERSON THE PAPER NAMES. THE PAPER NAMES A ROAD HOUSE. THE ROAD HOUSE IS NOT THERE ANY MORE. I HAVE HAD THAT LEASE ON MY HOOK SINCE THE NINTH YEAR AND I HAVE HOOKED IT EVERY DAY AND I NEVER LOOKED AT THE BOTTOM LINE, BECAUSE THE BOTTOM LINE HAS NEVER MOVED AND A THING THAT DOES NOT MOVE IS A THING YOU STOP LOOKING AT, AND THAT IS NOT A THING ANYBODY HAS EVER WRITTEN DOWN ANYWHERE.**

Adrian Vale stood at the end of that table for a while and then said the thing that made the morning into what it was, and he said it to the table and not to a yard, and the registrar was not there and it is in the book because Junia Crale wrote it down herself and signed it and dated it and sent it up.

"**Then the answer to who pays for the engine is nobody,**" he said, "**and the answer to who holds the toll is nobody, and both of those are correct, and this district has spent five weeks trying to invent an instrument for a thing it cannot name, and it turns out that the instrument exists, and it is a book at the end of a table in a room with a board over the window, and it has been answering for twenty-two years in a woman's hand, and the reason nobody has ever asked it anything is that it does not hold a use. It holds a shed.**"

---

**And the shed is not there, and that is the sentence that ends the morning, and Tarin Kest gave it to himself in about forty seconds, and nobody had to ask him.**

The lease says sixteen feet by thirty feet on the north side of the siding. There is a survey of the ninth place in the ward market's own files from the ninth year, because a lease cannot be drawn without a survey, and Orren Pike had it sent for and had it in his hand inside an hour.

The shed is on the survey. It is drawn in ink. The north wall of it is eleven feet from the ballast and the door is in the south end and the roof is a pitch and there is a water butt drawn against the east side in a different ink, which is a tradesman's addition, and Crale said that a different ink on a survey is a man who came back with a bit of chalk.

And the survey has a second mark on it, four feet off the shed's east wall, and that is a line of stones.

"**Who put that line of stones there,**" said Orren Pike.

And Tarin Kest said, "**My father.**"

And then nobody said anything for about a minute, and Crale filled it in her own way, which was to say that she had read the survey out loud twice in the ninth year to two men and a boy and that nobody had asked her about a line of stones because a lease is about premises and a line of stones is not a premise.

"**It is not a lease,**" she said. "**It is not a rate sheet, it is not a boundary, it is not a mark, because a mark is cut and that is laid. It is about four feet of stones laid in a line by a man who then wrote a lease for the ground on one side of it and not the other, and there is no document on this table or any other table in this district that says what it was for, and I have been twenty feet from it since the ninth year.**"

"So the ground of that place is in two pieces," said Adrian, "**and the piece the village is on has never been on a sheet that says it is a place.**"

"**It has been on a sheet since the twenty-eighth of last month,**" said Crale, "**and it is on yours, and it says two hundred coppers a quarter, and it says held by nobody, and I have read it, because I read everything that comes through this room, and I would like to say one thing about that line and then I will stop.**"

"Go on."

"**Held by nobody is the most accurate four words I have read out in twenty-two years,**" said Junia Crale, "**and I would like whoever wrote it to know that it is accurate and that I have no idea what to do about it, and I am sixty-three and I have four years and I would like to spend two of them learning.**"

---

Then the man who signed the paper in the ninth year was asked about the line of stones, and he answered it in about forty seconds, and nobody had to ask him twice, and the answer is the only thing said in that room that nobody in this district could have written down on their own.

"My father laid them," he said. "**On the day. I signed at about two in the afternoon in the room opposite and he laid them at about six in the morning because he was up at first light, and he did it in about two hours, and he used about nine stones to a foot and he laid them across the overflow.**"

"Across the overflow," said Adrian.

"**The column overflows. It has overflowed since before the water came and it comes out on the north side onto ground that was ours under that lease, and it runs along the line of the stones and off the end of them, and the ground on the other side is the village's, and the water is where the water is because of where the stones are.**" He put his thumb on the survey. "**And that is all he ever said about it. He did not say it was fair and he did not say it was not fair. He laid a line of stones across a thing that was running, and the water found the end of the line, and that is the whole of the work.**"

"**And the woman at the column has drawn two pails off that overflow every day for eleven years,**" said Adrian, "**and the water arrives where the stones send it, and she has never been told that, and she is not going to be told that by anybody in this room.**"

"**No,**" said Tarin Kest. "**She is not. And I will tell her, and I have been telling myself I would do it on the way up, and I am not going to, and I would like somebody in this room to tell me why I am not going to, because I have not got a reason and I have got a feeling, and a feeling is not a reason and I have been one of those men for nine years.**"

Orren Pike answered him, and he is seventeen, and the book says he was not asked and answered anyway, which is the finding of the whole afternoon and the fourth time it has happened in seven weeks.

"**Because you laid them,**" the boy said. "**Your father laid them, on your day, under a paper you signed, and if you go up that bank and ask her about the water then whatever she says is a thing she has said to the man the stones are against, and she will say the true thing, because she says true things, and the true thing will be the wrong thing to hear from him, and in about four years you will have told this story in a Road House yard and the story will be about you finding out. It will not be about her.**"

"**Then what do I do with it.**"

"**Nothing, on your own, ever,**" said Orren Pike. "**Put it on a sheet. Ask it in a room, with somebody in the room whose job is to say what the question was, and ask her twice, once without the stones in the question and once with them in it, and enter both answers, and let the room have the shape of the question and not just the answer.**"

And Tarin Kest looked at a boy of seventeen for long enough that the room got uncomfortable, and then said, "**On the twenty-ninth.**", and Adrian Vale said, "**Not by you.**", and Tarin Kest said, "**I know. That is why I said the date.**"

"Go on."

"**Held by nobody is the most accurate four words I have read out in twenty-two years,**" said Junia Crale, "**and I would like whoever wrote it to know that it is accurate and that I have no idea what to do about it, and I am sixty-three and I have four years and I would like to spend two of them learning.**"

---

And then a book came out of the water office in a satchel on the afternoon of the twenty-second and it did the thing nobody expected, and the thing it did was to prove that the engine had a payer after all, and to make the district's refusal of a rate on a line look the way it actually looks.**

Petrus Nye had sent it up himself and he came up the bank with it in a satchel against his chest, in the rain, on the second day, and he had walked five and a quarter hours to hand a book to three people in a kitchen and then walked five and a quarter hours back and was in the counting office in Alder Reach at about ten at night.

The water office keeps a quarterly return on the Harrow works. It has kept one since the flood year. It is a printed sheet with a column for each thing the office maintains, and the Harrow works is nine columns and one hundred and eighty coppers a quarter, and the sheet has gone to the ward market every quarter for nine years and the ward market has read it every quarter for nine years.

> **HARROW WORKS, MAINTENANCE, ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY COPPERS A QUARTER, NINE YEARS, UNCHANGED. OF WHICH THE PUMPING ENGINE AT THE VIADUCT END IS THE LARGER PART AND THE OUTFALL AND THE BRICK HOUSE ARE THE REST. PAID. NOT ARREARS. NOT DEFERRED.**

Nye put it on the table and said the four sentences the board asked him for on the eleventh and then went back down the bank.

"**One. The office has paid for that engine out of a rate on its own works for nine years and has never published it and I have read it in a return and never once looked up the column.**

"**Two. The oil has never been in that column, because the office does not buy oil, because the office did not know it was running, because the office wrote the works sheet in the flood year off a list of what was standing, and the engine was standing, and the man who was running it belonged to a firm.**"

"**Three. The eighth of the eight places on page eleven of the standing order of the ninth is HARROW, and this market could not amend that document without voiding it, and that finding is on a wall in your counting room, and it is the one place on the sheet that somebody has actually been paying for since the flood year, and the paying for it was done out of a river office and not out of a market, and neither of them told the other.**"

"**Four. I am a bridge clerk and I have been six years careful about one river and I have now been up a bank three times in a fortnight, and I would like it entered that all three times I was asked to look at something and twice I was asked not to stay, and the one time I was asked to stay I was asked by a lamp-man and not by an office, and I do not know what to do with that and I have not had time.**"

---

**And Ivo Stenn did the arithmetic in the counting room on the twenty-second with a sheet of his own, in about eleven minutes, in front of nobody, and then entered it in the minute, and the minute says that it was done in front of nobody because he wanted it to be a sheet and not a speech, and the finding is that this is the first time in six years he has produced a figure nobody asked for.**

The Harrow works rate is a hundred and eighty coppers a quarter. Four quarters is seven hundred and twenty a year. Nine years is **six thousand four hundred and eighty coppers, paid, not arrears, not deferred, out of a river office, for a brick house and a pump and an outfall on a railway with no passenger stop in nine years.**

The oil is fourteen thousand seven hundred and forty-two coppers out of a Road House float, over the same nine years, on a sheet in a tradesman's hand.

> **THE WHOLE COST OF THE HARROW END OF THE NEEDLE LINE FOR NINE YEARS IS TWENTY-ONE THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-TWO COPPERS. IT IS IN TWO BOOKS, IN TWO ROOMS, IN TWO HANDS, AND IT IS NOT ONE FIGURE IN ANY DOCUMENT, AND THE FIGURE I HAVE JUST WROTE DOWN IS THE FIRST TIME THE TWO HAVE BEEN IN THE SAME PLACE, AND IT IS BECAUSE A MAN OF SIXTY-EIGHT CAME UP OUT OF A BUCKET AND A BOY OF SEVENTEEN ASKED A QUESTION IN THE WRONG ORDER.**

And then he wrote the sentence underneath it that has since been read out at three counters, and it is nine words long and it is his and he was not proud of it and he said so.

> **THE MARKET REFUSED EIGHTY-EIGHT COPPERS A QUARTER FOR EIGHT POSTS. A RIVER OFFICE PAID SIX THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND EIGHTY. NEITHER ASKED THE OTHER IF THE THING EXISTED.**

---

**And the last thing on the twenty-second of this month is a printed notice, and it came up the bank on the afternoon of the twenty-second in the same satchel with the return, and it is dated the twentieth, and it is the first document this district has had from the bridge administration that anybody could read without sitting down.**

It is half a sheet. It has a heading in the office's own type and no seal and no signature, and it says four things in order, and the fourth is the reason the yard was in a state for two days.

> **THE BRIDGE ADMINISTRATION WILL STAFF THE SIGNAL HUT AT THE FIGURE ONE MILE AND THREE QUARTERS ON THE NEEDLE LINE FROM THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF THIS MONTH.**
>
> **THE POST IS ONE MAN, A SIGNALMAN OF THIS OFFICE, WHO WILL HOLD A PAIR AND KEEP A GROUND BOOK AND WALK THE SECTION TWICE A DAY. THE POST IS ONE MONTH. IT IS RENEWED BY THIS OFFICE AND NOBODY IS ASKED.**
>
> **THE POST IS PAID OUT OF THE HARROW WORKS RATE AT ELEVEN COPPERS A QUARTER FOR THE BENCH, THE LAMP, THE CHALK AND THE OIL. THE MAN IS NOT PAID OUT OF IT AND AN ADMINISTRATION DOES NOT PUT A MAN'S WAGE IN A RATE.**
>
> **THE ADMINISTRATION CONSIDERS THAT PLACE UNSAFE. IT HAS SAID SO IN A YARD ELEVEN TIMES IN EIGHT YEARS AND IT IS GOING TO PUT A MAN IN IT.**

The finding of the twenty-second is that the yard read those four lines in about four minutes and came out with three positions and no fourth, and the three are in the book in the words of the people who held them, and the registrar entered that all three are correct and that the document is the safest thing anybody in this district has published in a month, and that the district's objection to it is not to the safety.**

"It is the third paragraph," said Ivo Stenn. "**One month, renewed by this office, and nobody is asked. We have spent a month writing down that a rotation is only real when it turns, and a term that renews itself is not a rotation, it is a bar with a man behind it, and the man is the best signalman in this city and the bar is the office that shut a bridge and got seven to three.**"

"And it is a man in a hut in the rain at a place with no platform," said Tarin Kest, "**and there is nobody in that hut now, and there has been one woman in it for eleven years, and this is the first time anybody in this district has sent anybody to that hut for any reason at all, and it went up the bank on a satchel with a book of engine receipts in it.**"

"And it is on a printed sheet with a date on it," said Adrian Vale, "**which is the third time in three weeks that a document with a date on it has been the thing that worked, and the first two were the ward market's timetable and a counterfeiter's Monday, and I would like the record to show that the thing that has made this district say yes three times in a month is not virtue, it is a date.**"

"**It is the twenty-ninth,**" said Stenn.

Nobody had said it and everybody had been thinking it, and Ivo Stenn said it out loud in a kitchen at about four in the afternoon on the twenty-second of April, and the finding under it is that **the two terms in this district that have ends on them are now the twenty-ninth of this month and the twenty-fifth of this month, and one of them is held by two people who can only be asked and one of them is held by an office that does not have to be, and the two are the west end of the same railway, and nobody has connected them because connecting them would be deciding and not recording.**

And the registrar, who was not in that kitchen and heard about it on the twenty-third, entered one line on the twenty-third and the line is her own and it is the reason she gives every time and she has given it four times this month.

> **TWO THINGS THAT ARRIVED SEPARATELY STAY IN TWO LINES UNTIL SOMEBODY PUTS A THING ON THE PAPER THAT JOINS THEM, AND NOBODY HAS, AND I AM NOT GOING TO BE THE ONE WHO SAYS THEY BELONG TOGETHER.**

And the finding of the twenty-third is that the yard did not think of the twenty-ninth once between the sixth and the twenty-second, and that both women had been asked about a lot and neither had been asked about the month, and that **a term which is renewed by asking somebody is not a term that runs out quietly. It is a term that has to be noticed.**
