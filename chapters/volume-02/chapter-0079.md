# Chapter 79: The Two Who Hold It

At about half past ten in the morning on the twenty-ninth of April four people and a reader sat down at a table in the front room of a house at the ninth place, and the fifth person had been standing in the doorway since before any of them arrived, and nobody asked him to come in and nobody told him to go away, and the minute enters him as present and not asked.

The four are the tenth hand of the signal crew, a reader who is a signal clerk of about fifty and reads at a ward market counter four days a week at eleven coppers a quarter and whose term has no clause for a shoulder, and two people who have not spoken yet. The fifth is a signalman of the bridge administration of about thirty-nine who went up the bank on the twenty-fourth and is going to sleep in the hut, and who is not on the roll of this house and is not on the roll of that one either, and who is entered on the sheet this morning as a man who was in a room.

**And the asking took fifty-one minutes and eleven of those minutes were the fifth thing on a slate, and the two people who were asked both said yes, and the room in which they said it had a table in it and a pail by the door and a window with a board over one pane, and about twenty-two people from four houses came in and stood at the back and were not asked to leave and were not asked to speak.**

---

The first thing is what it is, and the tenth hand had to say it without using the word the board would use, and he had about nine attempts in him and used four.

"**What I am going to say is what the job is and not what the instrument is, and I have been up a bank four times in three weeks to learn the difference, and I am going to get it wrong, and if I get it wrong you are to stop me and nobody is to be polite about it.**

"**So: the job is this. There is a line of chalk on the shoulder sixty yards west of the water column. It is about five feet across the six-foot and it goes up the bank about eleven feet. It is in the book. You can go and look at it and it is not much to look at, and that is the second half of what the job is.**

"**The job is: you hold the west end of this railway for a month. There is one thing about that which people get wrong, so I am going to say it slowly. There is no train. There has not been a train at this end of this railway since the eleventh of February and there is going to be no train here tomorrow, and the thing that could turn one is in a hut four hours up the bank on a bench and it is not coming back in a hurry. So you are not holding anything back. You are standing in a place and saying a nine-word sentence, and if anybody comes up that bank while you are saying it, he stops and waits, and then he goes on.**

"**That is the whole job. It is not a fence. It does not stop a thing. It stops people, about nine times a year, and a haulier with a barrow stood on this bank for a minute and a half in the second week of last month because of it and he was not angry, and I want that in the book in that order, and I want it said that the sentence is nine words long and it is in the book in her words and I am not going to say the words because they are hers and not mine.**"

"And what do you say when you say it," said Sef Anyard.

"**Whoever holds the thing that day has to stop and wait.**"

"That is the whole of it," she said, "**and it took you four attempts and about a hundred and forty words to say nine, and I am not being unkind. I have had that sentence for a month and I have watched it turn into a different sentence in a book, and it is going to keep turning into a different sentence, and that is why I said yes when I said it and I am going to say yes again and here is what I have to attach to it.**"

---

The second thing is how long, with an hour on it, and it is the fifth thing on the slate and it is the one that went wrong four times before it went right, and the entry has all four attempts in it because Nessa Pell entered them on the first of next month and did not strike any of them.

"**I cannot give you a term,**" said the tenth hand. "**I want that said first, in front of these people, because everybody in Alder Reach thinks the term is mine and it is not, and the term is a month, and it was a month the last time, and I am asking for a term and I am not offering one, and if you say a day I will write a day and if you say a year I will write a year and if you say until somebody comes I will write that and it will be the worst term anybody in this district has ever printed.**"

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"**A month,**" said the woman of the column, who is the water, straight away, and then said the rest of it. "**A month from today. And the reason it is a month and not a day is that a day is a thing you can hold on to when somebody is cross with you, and a month is a thing you cannot, and the reason it is not a year is that I am fifty and I have not promised anybody a year since I was thirty-four.**"

"And I have one condition and it is not a condition, and if anybody calls it a condition they can take it out of the book," said Sef Anyard. "**The man in the hut has to be able to say the word. He has been in my hut since the twenty-fifth of last month and he can hold a pair and he has not got the word and I am going to teach him, and he is going to say it in front of me once before he is allowed to say it in front of anybody else, and if he cannot say it like a person then I will not hold this a second month by myself.**"

"That is a condition," said somebody at the back.

"**That is a job,**" said the tenth hand. "**A condition has an end on it and a job does not, and she has not got a price and she is not going to get one, and if the four of us start writing those down as terms we will be doing to her what was done to a village in March.**"

---

The third thing is how you say no, and it took about a minute, and both of them answered it before it was finished, and the entry says that and says that the reader at the counter read it back and that it was in the book on the first of next month in the same words twice.

"**You say no by not saying yes,**" said the tenth hand. "**No form. No reason owed. Nobody asks you for one afterwards, and if somebody does, it goes in the book against them.**"

"**Then say that again in nine words,**" said the woman of the column, "**because that is the sentence I have to be able to hand to a man in a good coat who is the first man in nine years to ask me a question, and I am not going to hand him a paragraph.**"

"You say no by not saying yes," said the tenth hand. "**No reason owed. Nobody asks afterwards.**"

"Nobody asks afterwards," said Sef Anyard. "**And you read it back to me before anybody leaves this room, and if you read it back wrong I will know, and I have been in this hut for eleven years and I know what a wrong sentence sounds like.**"

---

The fourth thing is who answers for you while you are saying it, and it is the one this district has never had to answer, and the finding of the morning is that two people answered it in about a second each and neither of them had to think about it, and the registrar entered that the district has spent seven weeks building instruments and the fourth of the five things took two people four seconds and cost nothing.

"**Who answers for you while you are saying it,**" said the tenth hand.

"The boy," said the woman of the column, at once. "**He is nine and he is in the room and he is the only person on this bank who will be here in ten years and he does not have to be asked anything and he is not on a rate sheet and he is not going to be.**"

"**Corvin Sallow,**" said Sef Anyard, "**who is in that doorway, and who has not been asked to come in, and who will be the person who is here at six in the morning and at six at night for as long as an office keeps him, and I am not going to pretend that is a person I chose. I chose nobody. He came. And if the office stops sending him I am still here, and that is the difference between him and me and it is the reason he is the fourth thing and I am the second.**"

And from the doorway, in the ordinary voice, a man of thirty-nine said the thing that the room wrote down, and it is in the book in his words, and the finding under it is that it is the fifth time anybody in this district has answered a question about themselves before they were asked it, and that four of the five were women, and that the room noticed the fifth one happening and did not stop it, and that stopping it would have been the only thing this district knows how to do with a person who answers before they are asked.

"**I will answer for her about a pair and I will not answer for her about the word,**" said Corvin Sallow. "**The pair is mine and I know it and I am responsible for it and if I am wrong about it somebody will find out. The word is hers and if I hold it I will be a man holding another person's sentence and there is no arrangement on this earth for that.**"

---

The fifth thing is where you stand, and the woman of the column answered it before the tenth hand got to it, and the answer took eleven words.

"**Where you stand when you answer. I stand where I am standing. I have stood in this doorway for eleven years and I am not going to stand anywhere else to say a yes to a man from Alder Reach.**"

"Then it is in this room," said the tenth hand, "**in daylight, with four houses behind us, and about twenty-two people at the back who were not asked to come in and are not going to be asked to leave.**"

And the sixth thing, which has no name, and which has been done by two people in three weeks and neither of them was asked to do it either, and which is a reader's job and not a power: Rutt turned round on her chair and said what the question had been, in her own words, so that the sheet would carry the question and not somebody's memory of one.

"**The question is this and it is one question and I am going to say it flat so that nobody has to guess at it later. Two women are being asked whether they will hold the west end of a railway for a month. The term is a month. The word is nine words long and it is theirs and neither of them is being asked to say it. The line of chalk is sixty yards west of this column and it cannot be seen from the west end of the siding and nobody is going to fix that in a month. And the person asking is a signalman of the tenth hand and he is not an officer of anything and he cannot be paid and he has not been paid since the eleventh of March.**"

"**Read that back on the first,**" said Sef Anyard, "**at that counter, out loud, in the ordinary voice, with both my answers under it, and I want the words *the line cannot be seen from the west end* in the reading-back, because that is the sentence that is going to be true on the day somebody needs it.**"

---

**And then the thing the morning was actually for, and it was not on the slate, and it was asked by a woman of fifty-one in a room with a pail by the door, and it is the reason this entry is the largest in the history of that book.**

"**Before I say yes I am going to ask for two things and I want the room to hear them in this order.**

"**One. The word is a word about people. It has been said five times since the twenty-ninth of last month and every single time it stopped a man with a barrow, and I liked it, and I did not tell anybody I liked it, and it is in a book in Alder Reach as a limit on a train, and it is not a limit on a train, there has not been a train here since the eleventh of last month, and I will not have a book that says my sentence is about a train. Write down what it is about. That is all. Write down that it is about whoever is on the line.**

"**Two. There are stones.**

"There is a line of stones about four feet long in a field I have never been into, on the other side of the overflow, and they are nine to a foot and they were laid on one day in the ninth year, and I have known about them for eleven years, and I have never seen a man from that side of them and I have never told anybody from this side of them, and I have been waiting eleven years for somebody to ask me a question about water that was not about how much of it there is.

"**And I know what I am doing. A boy of seventeen in Alder Reach has told you how and I can hear that he has, because he said it out loud in this room like a man reading a charge sheet, and I want it entered that I know he told you and I am not angry. He is right. So: you ask me the first time now, without the stones in it. And then you ask me the second time with them in it. And both go in the book and neither one is struck.**"

They asked her the first time and it took about four minutes and her answer was eleven words long.

"**Somebody should have told us.**"

And the tenth hand read it back to her in the same words, and then asked the second question, and the room went completely quiet, and the reader at the counter wrote both down on one sheet in one hand and did not stop between them.

"**Somebody laid them and somebody signed the paper the same afternoon and neither of them came up here and told us, and I am not going to say take them up, and I am not going to say they were right, and I would like it in the book in my own words and not in yours, and the words are: nobody is to lay a stone.**"

"**Then the two answers are not the same,**" said the tenth hand, "**and I want it entered that they are not the same and that they were four minutes apart and that the second one is the first one with a name in it, and that is the first time this district has costed a question, and it cost two questions and about four minutes and nobody has to pay anything.**"

---

---

And then, before anything else was asked, the woman of the column asked a question that is not one of the five things and that nobody in that room had thought of, and it took about forty seconds, and the answer to it is in the book in the tenth hand's words, and it is the answer to the fourth question a boy of fifteen put on a wall two days before.

"**What happens on the twenty-ninth of May.**"

"**Somebody has to come up this bank and ask you,**" said the tenth hand. "**In a room, with the asking and the hour and the answer entered, whatever the answer is. That is the whole of the term and it is the only clause in it. If nobody comes, it ends.**

"**And if nobody comes?**"

"**Then it ends,**" he said, "**and the word stops, and the line has no west end, and nothing is held. And I am not going to tell you that is a scandal, because it is not a scandal. It is what a thing is when the people who gave it to you are not there any more, and it is not a failure of the word and it is not a failure of you. It is a term. Terms end.**"

"**Write that down,**" said the woman of the column, "**under the heading they put on a wall in Alder Reach two days ago. Under NOT ANSWERED. Write it as the answer to a question nobody asked me, and put the date on it, and put my name on it as the person it was asked to, because I have been in this doorway for eleven years and there is not one sentence about a person in any of the books in this city, and there is a heading on a wall with my question under it that I did not write, and I would like one line in one of those books with something about a person in it, and it can be this.**"

And the reader at the counter wrote it down in her own hand at the bottom of the same sheet, and it is in the book on the first of next month under the heading with the boy's four questions on it, and the finding printed under both entries is that **the first line anybody has entered under that heading in nineteen years was not an answer to a question anybody had asked, and it was put there by a woman who was not owed anything and was owed a great deal, and neither of those facts is in the line, and the registrar says that is correct and is the best possible condition for a book.**

**And the last thing asked in that room was asked by Sef Anyard, and the answer to it is refused, and the refusal is the finding of the twenty-ninth of April, and the reason for it is the third fault of the bridge administration, and the administration was not in the room and does not know.**

"**Then hold it at the fourth place as well,**" she said. "**Because the west end is not one place any more. The thing that turns a train is in a hut at the fourth place and the bracket in my hut is empty and there is no frame at the fourth place at all, and a word that only works here is half a limit, and I am not going to hold half of a thing for a month and be asked why I am not holding the other half.**"

"**I can put a signal up there,**" said Corvin Sallow from the doorway, in the ordinary voice. "**There is a plinth on the embankment thirty yards east of the engine house with the bolt-holes in it and a cap of lead over them, and I could have a ground signal in it in a morning, and it would work, and it is the only instrument on this line that has ever been taken out and put back.**"

"**No,**" said the tenth hand, and it was the fastest no in the morning, and he gave the reason at once and in full and it is in the book.

"**Because a signal at the fourth place would report to a hut nine miles east that has got one woman in it and a frame with an empty bracket and no iron in it, and there is nothing anybody can do with that report except be frightened, and this line has eleven stalls in its book and nine of them were reported once and then reported every morning, and a place that reports every morning is a place nobody goes to.**"

"That is a reason," said Sef Anyard.

"**It is a reason and it is a railway reason and it is the only kind I have got, and I would like the room to notice that the woman who asked me for it asked me for it knowing the answer, and that she asked because a limit that is half a limit is worse than no limit, and she is right, and the answer is still no, and the answer is going to be no in about a fortnight as well.**"

"**Then write down that I asked for it,**" said Sef Anyard. "**Write down that it was refused, and write down that I am holding a half a thing for a month, and write down that a line does not know it has stopped working, because a line is not a person and there is nobody standing at the end of it to find out, and write down that I am the nearest thing to a person at this end of it and I did not know until the tenth of this month, and that is the whole of what I have.**"

---

**And the woman of forty-five was not asked, and she was not there, and the entry says so in four words and not one more, and nobody in that room said anything about it except the tenth hand, who said it once, out loud, at the rail in Alder Reach two days later, in front of about twenty people, and it is in the book.**

"**There is a woman on that bank who said no to this in March and her reason is in a book in this city and it is the best sentence anybody has said about anything in seven weeks, and she has not been asked about a month, and she was not asked about this, and not asked is in the count, and the count is the only reason any of the rest of it is honest.**"

And the sheet went down the bank at about one o'clock in the afternoon on the twenty-ninth of April in a satchel against a boy's chest, and it is five and a quarter hours with four changeovers in it, and the boy got it to the yard at about seven in the evening, and it went into the book on the first of next month at nine in the morning, and it was read out at that counter at ten the same morning, and the delay is entered, and the registrar entered it herself, and the line she entered is the fourth time she has written it and the third time it has been about a document.

> **THE SHEET WAS ASKED IN A ROOM AT THE FIGURE ONE MILE AND THREE QUARTERS ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF LAST MONTH AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING AND IT WAS IN THIS BUILDING AT ABOUT SEVEN IN THE EVENING THE SAME DAY AND IT WAS ENTERED HERE ON THE FIRST OF THIS MONTH AT NINE IN THE MORNING. THE DELAY IS NINE MILES, A BANK, FOUR CHANGEOVERS, A NIGHT AND A DAY. THE DELAY IS MINE AND NOT THE SHEET'S AND NOT THE WOMEN'S, AND THE WOMEN DID NOT KNOW THERE WAS A SHEET.**

And the entry is in two halves on one page, because a line of chalk and a hut are the same west end, and the finding under both halves is the finding of the twenty-ninth of April and it is the shortest thing the registrar has ever written and she wrote it late at night on the first of next month at a table in a room with a door on it, and did not show it to anybody until the second.

> **ONE HALF OF THIS IS HELD BY TWO PEOPLE WHO CAN ONLY BE ASKED AGAIN, IN A ROOM, WITH THE ASKING AND THE HOUR AND THE ANSWER ENTERED, AND IT IS ASKED. THE OTHER HALF IS HELD BY AN OFFICE THAT RENEWS ITSELF AND ASKS NOBODY, AND IT WAS NOT ASKED, AND THE OFFICE IS THE SAFEST OFFICE IN THIS BASIN AND IT LOST A VOTE BY SEVEN TO THREE IN MARCH.**

---

**And the last thing that happened on the twenty-ninth of April happened at about five o'clock in the afternoon, on the shoulder, going down, and it took four seconds and it was said by a woman of about fifty-three who had come up the bank that morning to carry a pail back and had not been asked anything and had stayed for an hour, and it is the standing failure of the instrument, and it was found by accident by four people who had been at the far end of it that day.**

"**You cannot see it from down there,**" she said.

Nobody argued. That is the whole finding, and it is in the book with a date on it, and the reason it took nine minutes to establish is that nobody in Alder Reach had been at the fourth place and at the ninth place on the same day since the twenty-ninth of last month, and everybody in that party had, because the book had put them there in the wrong order.

The fixing of it is in the book as it has been since the twenty-ninth of last month: one bushel of white paint, and about a hundred and forty yards of it. And the rate on the line, voted on the second of this month by seven to three with one abstention, contains fourteen pints of white paint. **Fourteen pints is not a bushel. And nobody in Alder Reach has a bushel of white paint in it this month, and the district has spent a fortnight arguing about a column printed at full width with nothing in it, and the column it needed was a bushel.**
