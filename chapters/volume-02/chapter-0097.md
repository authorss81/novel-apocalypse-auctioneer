# Chapter 97: The Twenty-Ninth of June

Eleven coppers were counted onto a table in a counting room in Alder Reach at about eight in the morning on the twenty-ninth of this month, and the woman who was going to be paid them watched the counting and then put one of them back and had it counted again, and the secretary entered that the second count was right and that the woman had said why without being asked.

"Eleven is for reading a sheet out loud and for a reading-back," she said. "**Eleven is not for a day, and I am going up that bank anyway on the twenty-ninth because I have been going up it on the twenty-ninth since the flood year, and if the eleven is for the day then I have been paid for a day a hundred times and nobody has ever told me.**"

Her name is Jessa Olwin and she is about thirty-eight and she buys old paper off four houses once a month and she has been going up that bank for nine years and she keeps the toll board at the fourth place on the week she is on that bank for her own trade. She is not a signal hand, she does not know what a pair is, and she has wanted both of those on that board since the ninth of last month and they are on it.

The eleven came out of the unassigned thirty. The rate is fifty coppers a quarter, fourteen is the line, six is printed matter, and **thirty of the fifty has never been assigned to anything by anybody and has now paid a woman eleven coppers to stand in a room and say what a sheet says, and the entry notes that this is the first thing in eleven weeks the unassigned thirty has been asked to do.**

---

The party left at half past four in the morning. It was raining and it had been raining since the fourth, and the river had been coming up on the ninth place's column for a week, and the woman at the column said on the fourteenth of this month that the column's mark had been under for six days and that she was still the water and was not going to be anything else about it.

The paperer's own figure went into the book before anybody left, in her own hand, and she read it out to the room.

"**Six hours and a quarter going and five and a half coming is eleven and three-quarters in the day, which is ten and three-quarters of walking and an hour of standing still, and the six and a quarter is not a guess. It is what a loaded cart did on the twenty-second of this month and I wrote it down at the time.**"

It is five and a quarter hours from Alder Reach to this place in ordinary weather with four changeovers. The district carried nine hours for the day in a sentence that the rehearsal of the twenty-first of last month found wrong. The figure on the sheet is Jessa Olwin's and belongs to Jessa Olwin and nobody in Alder Reach is going to correct it.

Four of the eleven and three-quarters hours is somebody else's and one hour of it is standing still in a front room. **The entry for the twenty-ninth is that this is the first time anybody walked that bank for a document and not for a line, and the first time the document was a rate sheet with a figure on it, and the first time the people being asked knew what the line cost before they were asked.**

---

And at a rail in Alder Reach on the morning of the twenty-eighth, on a published day, a bridge administrator of nine years said two things about a rate sheet being read out to a village, and the first was that it is a good idea and the second was the sentence he says on published days.

He is Selik Marne. He is not on the walk and has not been up that bank in nine years and the room in Alder Reach knows both of those facts and has entered both of them, and a clerk asked him on the twenty-eighth whether he wanted the sheet sent to him and he said no in four seconds and gave a reason that went in the minute.

"I am the office that has been paying a hundred and eighty coppers a quarter on the Harrow works for nine years," he said, "**and if a sheet is read out to thirty-one people because somebody finally asked them, then the office that never asked them is not a place a sheet goes to. Put me last. Put me last on every sheet for four years and see whether anybody notices.**"

And then the second thing, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people at a rail, at about ten in the morning.

"That is not enough. **And I am going to keep saying it on published days for as long as I hold this post, and nobody in this district believes I will stop and I am not going to stop, and I have said that for nine weeks and it is still true, and the only thing that has changed since April is that the sentence has more facts in it and the facts do not make it shorter.**"

Nobody argued with him and the minute enters that nobody argued with him, and that the man who is not on the walk said on the twenty-eighth of this month that he would rather be last on a sheet than first in a room, and that nobody in the room believed him and that the tenth hand wrote it down as a thing that had been said rather than a thing that was true.

---

**And the finding of the twenty-ninth that is not about the two women is that a timetable with a train on it cannot be printed until somebody settles what the fourth place is, and that four people tried to settle it at half past six in the morning and did not.**

The first timetable this district printed runs from the ninth of this month to the twenty-third, and every day on it is a day on which nothing was going to happen. It carries the ninth place and the east end. It does not carry the fourth place, and the reason on the sheet is not a decision. The reason is that a station needs somebody at it, and there is nobody at the fourth place, and a keeper of a board is not a person anybody can be sent to.

A board went up there on the ninth of last month. It has a keeper on it, and a woman who goes once a month for her own trade, and a boy of nineteen who has said four times in three rooms that he is nobody's keeper and who had a piece of cast iron four feet from the end of a bench on the day the board went up, **and who has not had one there since the twenty-second of this month, when it went down that bank in a rope to the hut at the ninth place and he held one end of the rope and did not go in.**

So the fourth place has a toll board and no station, and a train that reaches three stations will put people down on a shoulder two miles west of a platform with nobody standing on it. **The finding of the thirtieth is that a station with nobody at it is the fourth thing this district does not have, and that it has now got a name for the other three.**

The three instruments this district does not have are all in books. A way of saying that a toll lands on a place, which is printed as an empty column on three boards and five hundred sheets and has nobody's name on it. A column that is a place, which a licensed valuer's certificate does not have in twenty-nine columns. And an office, which is spelled out on all of the same things and cannot be found in any register this basin can reach.

Nobody put the fourth on the list. The registrar entered the finding, and entered that the list has three and the district has four, and that the fourth has no number and no date and a person attached to it. **A person attached to it is a boy of nineteen who will be there and who has not been asked and who says he is nobody's keeper.**

And the same hour produced the other thing nobody had asked about, which is that the oil for that lamp at the ninth place comes off a card with a man's name on it since the third of this month, and that a trader of fifty-one on the lower terrace has been paying out of her own till for the Harrow works since the first of last month and has asked twice in two rooms that it be said that nobody asked her either. **Two cards in this district name a person instead of a firm, and both of them were found by accidents, and neither of them was found by anybody going looking for a card.**

---

**And then the rate sheet was read out in the front room of the second house before any question was asked of anybody, and it took nine minutes, and nobody in that room had ever heard a rate sheet read out in the room it was made for.**

It is the ward market's rate sheet for a place with four households on it. Fifty coppers a quarter over four thousand and one households. Fourteen of the fifty is the line. Six is printed matter. Thirty of the fifty has never been assigned to anything. Two hundred coppers a quarter was collected at this place on the seventh of last month out of a pail of change by a man who does not read figures and who was given the figure and not the reason. **Six of it went on lettering, eleven on a bound copy of the line book, and a hundred and eighty-three on nothing.**

**The hundred and eighty-three on nothing was the sentence that stopped the room, and it was not the figure and it was not the two hundred, and the finding of the twenty-ninth is that a district will sit in a front room at eleven at night and be made silent by an amount of money that went nowhere.**

The woman at the column asked for the reading-back, and Jessa Olwin gave it, and then asked one question.

"Who is owed the hundred and eighty-three."

Nobody in the room could answer it and nobody in the room had ever asked. **A market has entered that it will do a reading when it sends anybody up that bank, and a yes without a date is a yes that can be asked again, and the twenty-ninth of this month is the date, and it went in the book in a clerk's hand.**

Getting thirty-one people into one front room took about half an hour and there is an entry about it because there is an entry about everything that happened on the twenty-ninth, and the entry is that four households came as four households and nineteen came as nineteen and the nineteen were counted at the door by the tenth hand with a pencil on the back of a sheet, and that nobody in that room knew that was a count until he said it out loud.

"**You are all being entered as thirty-one," he said, "and there are four of you who are households and there are nineteen of you who are not, and I am entering those as two things and not one, because if I enter them as one then a stranger can take this entry cold and read one number, and the one number is a household, and a household is the thing this whole bank was built not to be.**"

A woman of about fifty-one who is the trader on the lower terrace and who has been paying for nine feet of water out of her own till said the only thing she said that night, and she said it to him and not to the room.

"Then the two hundred coppers a quarter is one number and there are four households on it, and I am one of the four and I did not know that until the sixth of this month when a man read it out of a pail of change, and if the sheet had been read out in this room in May then a person would have known in May."

Nobody knew in May. **The sheet was read out in this room on the twenty-ninth of this month at about four in the afternoon, and the woman who asked for it said in May that she had been waiting eleven years for somebody to ask her a question about water that was not about how much of it there is, and what she got instead was a figure and a column and a bushel of white paint.**

The room was a front room with a board over one pane of the window, which is what the sheet of the thirteenth of this month said it would be. Thirty-one people got into it over about half an hour and twenty of them were standing in the passage, and both of the women it was for could stand in it, which was the condition, and the condition was the only thing anybody in Alder Reach was certain of when the party left at half past four in the morning.

---

The asking took forty-four minutes and nineteen of them were the sixth question, and the finding of the twenty-ninth is that the method did not get cheaper the third time anybody used it. It got dearer.

The first asking was fifty-one minutes with eleven of them the fifth thing, done by a signalman of the tenth hand who cannot be paid and who walked five and a quarter hours up a bank to do it.

The second was thirty-nine minutes with nine of them, done by a clerk of the ward market who said in a room in May that it would not be her again. It has not been her. She has said since that a clerk is a person and a person can be replaced and that is the point of her.

The third was forty-four minutes with nineteen of them the sixth question, done by a paperer of about thirty-eight whose whole trade is being handed other people's documents without being asked for them.

> **WHAT A PERSON OUGHT TO KNOW. THE SIXTH QUESTION HAS NO NAME AND IS NOT ON THE WALL AND BELONGS TO A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS.**
>
> **ONE. THAT THE TOLL ON THIS LINE IS THE ORIGIN AND THE ORIGIN IS A PLACE AND NOT A PERSON. TWO. THAT ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-ONE MOVEMENTS IN NINE HUNDRED AND SIX HAVE NAMED THIS PLACE AS THE ORIGIN HELD. THREE. THAT A TOLL ON A PLACE TAKES MEMORY OF DEPARTURE AND NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAS EVER PAID IT IN COPPER. FOUR. THAT NOBODY IN THIS ROOM CAN STATE WHAT ONE OF THOSE HUNDRED AND FORTY-ONE IS, INCLUDING THE WOMAN WHO KEEPS THE BOOK, BECAUSE SHE WAS ELEVEN FEET ABOVE THE WATER AT THE TIME. FIVE. THAT THE WORD BEING ASKED ABOUT ENDS ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JULY AND NOT ON A FEELING. SIX. THAT A PERSON MAY ANSWER NO TWICE AND BE ASKED TWICE, AND THAT A NO IS A COLUMN AND NOT A DOOR.**

The five things on the wall are how a person is asked whether they will do a job. The sixth is what a person ought to know before they say yes, and nobody has ever given it a name. Jessa Olwin read the six questions off the back of a sheet in her own trade and read them slowly, and the room said that was the first time anybody had read them aloud. **The reason the sixth question took nineteen minutes is that this is the first of the three askings in which the two people being asked knew what the line costs.**

The woman at the column said one sentence in the middle of it, and it is the reason she is the water and has been for eleven years.

"**I have always known what the toll is and I have never once known what the amount is, and there are four people standing in this room who know the amount and none of them can state the toll, and the two halves of that sentence have been in this district in two books since the eighth of last month and nobody has joined them.**"

The registrar was not in the room. A clerk of the ward market, who was not there either, wrote it down afterwards from Jessa Olwin's own hand and did not join the two halves, and the reason she gives is the reason she gives every time.

---

And both of them said yes, for a fourth month, and the order of the two sentences has not changed since the twenty-ninth of March, and the minute enters that it has not changed.

"I do not like it, and I say yes."

A yes is not a hold and a hold is not a term, and a term is not a promise, and those are four different things. **This district has spent nine weeks learning them one at a time and has not yet learned to write all four on one line.**

And then the keeper of the hut did the thing she attached in April, and nobody in that room had asked her to, and about thirty people watched it.

Corvin Sallow is thirty-nine and is a signalman of a bridge administration and he has been in that hut on a term of thirty days since the twenty-fifth of April, which the office renewed without asking. He is not paid. He keeps a toll board in the hours nobody is on a bank. He is not a bearer and has said in three rooms that a keeper of a board is not a person anybody can be sent to.

He said the nine-word word out loud, in the front room, in front of about thirty people, at about ten minutes to twelve. He got four of the nine words right and the woman of the column said the fifth one for him and then stopped, the same as on the sixth of this month, and he said the rest of it himself.

The condition she attached in April was that the man in the hut has to be able to say the word and she will teach him and he says it in front of her once before anybody else. **If he cannot say it like a person then she will not hold it a second month by itself, and he said it, and the minute records that it took him about a second and a half, and that she then said yes to the fourth month, and that the order of those two things was the word first and the yes second, and that the order was hers and not the room's.**

Nobody wrote anything under the heading that says NOT ANSWERED on that wall except the two lines that have been there since the thirteenth of this month, and one of them had stopped being true, and the registrar entered the change in the margin with a date and no comment.

**THE PERSON IS A PAPERER AND SHE IS NOT ASKED YET.** She was asked on the twenty-ninth and she was asked first and she is a paperer, and the line is struck and the strike has a date on it, and the woman of forty-five is not asked and is in the count as not asked and is not named in that room and was not in the room.

---

And then the tenth hand read the whole of the twenty-ninth out loud in that front room, in the ordinary voice, standing up, in about thirteen minutes. **It is the fourth time anybody has read an entry out on the day it was made, and the first time it has been read out to the people it was made about.**

Reading a stranger's sentence back is the paperer's trade and the reading-back in the method is the tenth hand's, and the two of them did it in that order. Jessa Olwin read the sheet and the tenth hand read the answer, and the answer was in his own hand and not in hers.

The minute says that the man who wrote the answer and the woman who asked the question were in the same room and that neither of them wrote the other's words down. **This is the first time in nine weeks that two people have kept two halves of one entry in two hands in one room, and neither of them has said a word about it, and the reader at the ward market counter wrote down on the thirtieth of this month that six people have now seen that room and that she is still not going to ask.**

The woman at the column asked for one more thing before anybody moved, and it is in the book in her own words, and it is the first request she has made in eleven years that anybody in that district could have refused.

"**Write the day it ends on the board on the platform. I do not want it in a book nine hours away.**" She put her hand flat on the boards. "**I want a person standing on thirty feet of board at eleven at night to be able to put a hand on a board and find out that this word of mine runs out on a day.**"

A man of sixty-one painted a word at the height of a hand on the twenty-sixth of this month and a man of about fifty keeps that board and wipes the bottom line when it is wet, and somebody chalked the date on the kerb rail at about half past ten that night. **The finding is that a date is the only line anybody has ever put on that board that nobody is allowed to argue with, and it took a woman who has been the water here for eleven years to find that out about a piece of chalk.**

---

**And the whole of what the twenty-ninth added is four sentences long, and the tenth hand said them in the room, and then asked for them to be entered by somebody who was not him, and a woman of about thirty-eight read them back.**

> A rotation that has turned once has been shown that it can turn.
>
> **A rotation that has turned twice has been shown it can turn twice, and the two turnings cost a man a day he was not paid for and a woman a day and a night. A day and a night is a wage, and a wage is a thing you stop.**
>
> **THE THIRD TURNING COST A PAPERER OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT NOTHING AT ALL, BECAUSE SHE WAS GOING UP THAT BANK ON THE TWENTY-NINTH FOR FOUR HOUSES AND HAD BEEN GOING UP IT ON THE TWENTY-NINTH FOR NINE YEARS. A THING WHICH TURNS AT NO COST IS NOT A ROTATION, AND THIS DISTRICT NOW HAS THREE SAMPLES OF A ROTATION AND NO ROTATION AND ONE SUBSCRIPTION, AND THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THOSE TWO WORDS IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT A BOUNDARY IS.**

And she proposed the fix, in the room, in one sentence, and the room entered it without voting on it because there was nothing on it to vote about.

"**Ask me next month from the counting room and I will come down, and ask the keeper from the yard on the tide, and neither of them has to walk.**" She was quiet for about four seconds. "**Then write down that a boundary held by two people who are each asked from a different room is a boundary either room can end, because I would rather be ended by a room than by a flood.**"

---

And the party walked to the west end of the platform at about ten o'clock at night with eleven lamps and about forty people. The train was where it has been for a hundred and thirty-eight days, and a man of sixty-eight got into the cab of the first vehicle and came out again in about two minutes and said one word to the foreman of twenty-two years.

The word was *ready*. Nobody entered the word and everybody heard it.

The iron was on the bench under the window in a bag and it was brought out into the wet and put on the boards of the platform and left there, because the frame is at the east end of that loop and the bracket is in the frame, and the frame has a door and the door has a lock, and the key is in the keeping of the only pair on that line.

**And the reading of the twenty-ninth is the first entry in this district's history that was read out three times on three days by three people who are not the reader, and the finding of the thirtieth is that a thing read out three times stops being a record and becomes a habit, and this district does not have a word for the good kind of habit and is not going to get one out of this.**
