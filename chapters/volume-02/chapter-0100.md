# Chapter 100: The Board of Eleven

Sixty-one pieces of paper were laid out on the floor of a counting room in Alder Reach on the fifth of this month at about nine in the morning, and a clerk of the ward market who keeps the line book in the hours the counter is shut was on her knees on the boards with a pair of scissors, and a clerk of nineteen years was standing over her, and about forty people were in the room and none of them knew what the sixty-one pieces of paper were.

They are the whole of a column. **It is not in the line book, it has never been in the line book, and it is the only record this railway has of what its signals were held for.** There are sixty-one lines in it, and every signalman on this line has copied it out by hand nine times over nine years and put the copy in a drawer because the line book has no column for it.

**THE FINDING OF THE FIFTH OF THIS MONTH IS THAT A SIGNAL REMEMBERS A SHAPE, AND THAT THE SHAPE IS A BROKEN CIRCLE, AND THAT SIXTY-ONE PIECES OF PAPER CUT OUT OF NINE DRAWERS IN NINE HOUSES MAKE IT, AND THAT A SIGNALMAN WHO COPIED A COLUMN OUT NINE TIMES OVER NINE YEARS HAS NEVER ONCE DRAWN IT.**

"I did not draw it because I did not know it was a shape," said the man of about thirty-one, who is a signalman of the district's own crew and holds the second of the three weeks, and who had brought the fourth copy in a tin, **and who is the only person in that room who has been on this line for nine years.** "**I copied sixty-one lines because there were sixty-one lines. That is all a signalman is. If you had asked me in the fourth year I would have told you it was nothing, and I would have been wrong, and I would have been wrong in the way that costs a place its memory.**"

He laid them out in order and the order is the order they were copied in, and the order is in the drawer. **The finding of the fifth is that the order is the shape and not the shape being the order,** and that the two of those are the same sentence and that it took sixty-one pieces of paper on a floor and a pair of scissors and about four minutes.

---

And then the registrar was asked to join two things, and she refused, and it is the sixth time, and the two things were on the floor of the room in about nine hundred and six movements' worth of paper and one impression of a mark, and the reason she gives is the reason she gives every time, and this time she noticed out loud that she was giving the same reason for the sixth time and that she had never once been asked for a different one.

"The two lines are the office's name and the shape," she said, **and she was standing on the edge of the paper and not on it, and the minute says so, and a man who was there says he noticed that and has not been able to say why he noticed it.** "**I have held them apart five times and I will hold them apart a sixth, and the reason is the reason, and I would like it entered that I have said the same sentence six times and that nobody in this district has ever asked me why, including a man who has stood in that room four times about this page.**"

He asked for it entered and it was entered, and the entry says in his own hand, on the margin, that he noticed, and that a man who has asked the same question four times and has never once asked why is a man who did not want the answer, and that the two halves of that sentence are about this page and not about anybody in it.

And the finding of the fifth of this month is that a signal remembers a shape and a lever carries a mark, and that those are two objects doing two different jobs. **This district put them side by side on a floor because side by side is how a person sees a thing, and the broken circle on a water office notice dated the second of the sixth year and the broken circle made of sixty-one entries in nine drawers are not joined, and the page says they are not joined, and it is the fourth page in this district that says so in those words.**

---

> **THE NAME IS IN FOUR OF THE SIXTY-ONE AND IT WAS FOUND IN MAY BY A BOY OF SEVENTEEN IN A COLUMN THE LINE BOOK HAS NOT GOT, AND IT IS NOW SPELLED OUT IN FULL ON THREE TOLL BOARDS AND FIVE HUNDRED PRINTED SHEETS AND ON A SECURITY SCHEDULE AND IN THE WATER OFFICE'S OWN BOOK. IT IS IN FIVE REGISTERS THIS BASIN CAN REACH AND IT IS NOT IN ANY OF THEM.**
>
> **THE FOUR IN ONE BOOK ARE NOT THE FOUR IN THE OTHER, SO THERE IS A FIFTH SOMEWHERE, AND THE FIFTH HAS NOT BEEN LOOKED FOR, AND THE REGISTRAR HAS BEEN ASKED TO KEEP THAT IN A SEPARATE LINE FROM THE OTHER TWO AND HAS AGREED.**
>
> **IT IS A PUBLIC OFFICE AND IT IS NOT A GUILD. NOBODY IN THIS BASIN CAN FIND IT. DO NOT WRITE A PLACE.**

And on the evening of the fifth, in a counting room in Alder Reach, a registrar entered a question in the public lot book as a question, with a date on it, and **it is the first question this district has ever entered as an open question, and the finding of the fifth is that a question in a public book is the only kind of sentence in it that nobody can answer wrong.**

> **WHAT IS A PUBLIC OFFICE THAT A SIGNAL CAN REMEMBERS AND A BASIN CANNOT FIND, AND WHAT WAS IT FOR. ENTERED ON THE FIFTH OF JULY. FOUR PEOPLE HAVE LOOKED FOR IT IN A REGISTER IN TWO HOURS AND ELEVEN TRADES HAVE FAILED TO STATE WHAT IT DID. THE NAME IS ON THREE BOARDS AND FIVE HUNDRED SHEETS AND IN FIVE REGISTERS AND IN NONE OF THEM. THE NAME IS NOT JOINED TO THE SHAPE ON THE FLOOR OF THIS ROOM AND THE TWO LINES ARE HELD APART AND THE REASON IS ON A SEPARATE PAGE.**
>
> **NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT IS TO ANSWER THIS IN A GUESS. A GUESS ENTERED IN A PUBLIC BOOK IS A THING A STRANGER CAN TAKE COLD IN FOUR YEARS, AND THIS DISTRICT HAS HAD ENOUGH OF THOSE.**

Nobody argued about it and nobody voted on it and the market did not fund it, **and the entry says that the market was not asked,** and that a market not asked about a question is a market that has not declined it, and that those are different and that the difference is one of the four things this district learned this year and is going to have to learn four more times before somebody can say it out loud in a room.

And then four people in four rooms tried to say what the office was for, on the sixth, in about an hour and a half between them, and the finding of the sixth is that four people who have never met gave four different answers and that all four of them were about carrying something somebody would not carry themselves.

**A registrar said it was a place a person could refuse**, which is a thing a bridge administration has been for nine years without the word. **A bridge clerk said it was a place a name could be charged to**, which is a thing a line book has been for nine years without the word. **A licensed valuer said it was a place a cost could be put in front of before it was paid**, which is a thing a rate sheet has been for nine years without the word. A sign-writer of sixty-one said he did not know, and that he had written the phrase twice in his own hand and had a customer for neither.

**Four answers, four rooms, four months, and the entry of the sixth says that the four of them may be four halves of one thing and that nobody is to join them, and that the reason is the reason, and that the reason given on this one is that they arrived separately and a person who joins four things that arrived separately is a person who has found four things.**

The water office notice is on the second floor of a room with a door on it, in a drawer that is not locked, and it is dated the second of the sixth year and it is about the metered column at mile one and three quarters, and it has an impression of a broken circle above the fold line where a name would go, and there is no name on it. It was found on the fourteenth of last month on a board in a hut at the ninth place, behind a book, by a boy of seventeen who was there to copy a line book.

Six impressions now, in two lines. Five on five documents that have nothing to do with each other, and one on a notice its own office has no copy of, and the water office's own numbered form list is complete to the sixth year and does not contain that column's number.

**A second impression is not a second answer, and what the sixth one changed is one thing, and the one thing is that this district now knows where the first one is not, and a page in the public lot book says that in those words with a date on it, and the page is the fourth page this district has written for the purpose of refusing to know something and it is the one page in nine weeks that nobody has argued about.**

And on the seventh of this month the woman of the column said the thing she said in March and again in the middle of last month, and it is four words long, and it went in the book in her own hand.

"Nobody lays a stone."

And the clerk of the ward market entered that a boundary which is two named people and a stated term and a published entry and a hole in the ground that is not a mark is now due to be asked again on the twenty-ninth of this month, from a counting room, by somebody who is not the man who went up the bank twice, and that the asking will be done in a room and not on a bank, and that the woman who proposed it said she would rather be ended by a room than by a flood, and that the room has not yet agreed to end anything.

And on the sixth of this month a stranger took a copy of it cold off a board of eleven in the yard of Lot 17, in about two minutes, and did not ask anybody anything, and walked off with four hundred and eleven pages under his arm. **The room let him and the minute says the room let him. And the entry of the sixth says that this is the fourth time a stranger has taken that book cold since the first of last month and that nobody has ever stopped one of them.** The reason nobody has ever stopped one of them is that a book on a board is the only thing this district has ever built that a hostile stranger can use without permission.

The line book went to three places in the first four days of this month and all three of them are nine hours apart or more, **and the third copy is the one on the board of eleven, in the yard of Lot 17, in the open, on eleven boards.** **The finding of the sixth is that a line book a stranger can read is worth more than a line book four keepers agree to keep, and that this district has spent nine weeks finding that out backwards.**

One copy is on a shelf in the hut at the ninth place, beside a stove, eleven coppers. **One is in the counting room of the ward market.** One is on a board of eleven in the yard of Lot 17, and a stranger can take it cold, and a fourth person can take it and photograph it and send it to a person in a room this district has never had a name for, and the entry of the sixth says all of that and adds that the district has decided to publish rather than to prevent, and that this is the first decision in nine weeks that has cost nothing and that nobody argued about.

---

And the span is still shut. The instrument certificate of the crossing at St. Orra is suspended by two people who are not Selik Marne and the middle plank of the old footbridge is in the service cut, and seventeen people are on the far bank, added to on the fifth of last month to eighteen, with a stamped book and no way home.

Nobody opened that on the seventh of this month and **the entry says nobody opened it,** and the finding is that a man at a rail has spent nine weeks being the only person in this district who is right about a shut span, and that a district which has a charter and a platform and three toll boards has not once had to open the span, and that nobody knows whether that is luck or a system, and that in about four years somebody will be able to tell.

And on the seventh of this month a reading of the ledger was read out at the counter and it is one for April, none for May, and one for the twenty-ninth of last month, and the number of departures left is one.

**The entry of the seventh says what the twenty-ninth of last month made him able to do and what it did not, and it is four lines long, and the four lines are the reason this volume ends where it ends.**

A binder may write a bounded charter, split a use among several bearers, and close a transfer without changing the object underneath it. He may make an ordinary lot portable, or renewable, under a stated term. He may post a security, and he did, and it is a day a week and three bound copies of a book.

He may not maintain a public lot, coordinate more than one bearer, arrange a dispute hearing, or make the consequences of an object visible to a whole settlement. **And this district has no public lot, four bearers and no hearing, and the four things a man cannot do are the four things this railway has spent nine weeks not having, and the entry says that neither of those two facts is a coincidence and that the district is not going to guess which of them caused the other.**

And on the seventh of this month, in about seven minutes, a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was thanked in a room by about twenty people and asked a question about the fourth place, and the two halves of that are in the minute separately because they are two different facts.

The thanking was for the twenty-seventh of last month, when he was the fourth time in nine weeks that somebody had been laughed at for a correct thing. **The room gave him the thanking because a man who has been laughed at once for a correct thing is not a man who can be hurried,** and that sentence went in the book, and he said it was the first time anybody had said that about laughing.

The question was about the fourth place. There is a board there and a keeper on it and a boy of nineteen and no platform and no station, and the printed correction sheet says NOBODY IS THERE, and a train stops there once a month in the dark, and he said that a sheet a stranger can take cold is the only instrument in this district that a person can decline to be on, and that he does not know whether that is the best thing about it or the worst, and he said it twice, once to the room and once to the man who sells nothing and has a use, and got the same answer both times, which was that he did not know either.

---

And on the seventh of this month the woman of forty-five was not asked for a fifth month, and the entry says she was not asked and that not asked is in the count, and that the count now stands at four not-askings and one standing refusal, and that the registrar entered that the count is a column and not a protection and that she is the registrar and not a vote and that a column is the only thing she has ever been able to offer anybody.

She was asked twice, in March and in the middle of last month, in a room, in front of about twenty-two people, and said no twice, and she is not in the book and nobody has named her, and her second answer was four sentences long and none of them was the nine-word word, and she asked for the column to be on a wall and not in a book about a railway. **It is on a wall.**

And on the seventh of this month, at about a quarter to seven in the morning, a slate went past in a kitchen under the Ward Nine tram line, and neither of them said a word, and a reader at a counter wrote down that six people have now seen that kitchen and that she is still not going to ask, **because the answer would be in a room and not in a book, and not asked is in the count.**

And on the seventh of this month, at about half past four in the afternoon, a train stood at a platform that did not exist in March, at a place that is a station now because a village of thirty-one was asked on the nineteenth of last month and twenty-five of them said yes and one said yes with a condition and the book says so in six separate figures, and the train had been standing there for eight days, and the line book has nine hundred and six movements in it and one more, which is the twenty-ninth of last month, and nobody has struck the eight days.

**The toll board at the platform carries three tolls and a name that is not a place.** The name is at the top of three boards and five hundred printed sheets and is in five registers and is not in any of them. A man of sixty-one painted four words on that board at the height of a hand, and the man of about fifty who keeps it has said since the second of this month that he does not know what the argument for the column is and that there are now three things in it instead of none.

The rain had stopped about two days before and had not come back. **There were about nineteen people on that platform and a boy of seventeen with a slate and a man of about fifty with a cloth,** because the bottom line of a board is wiped when it is wet and it had not been wet since the fifth, and a man of sixty-one was in Alder Reach and not there, and a trader of fifty-one on the lower terrace had sent nine gallons of lamp oil up the bank on a card with a person's name on it and had asked that it be said that nobody asked her either.

The lamp was lit by whoever was standing on the platform, and the day was the day, and the day is written in chalk, and a chalk line is not a mark.

**Six things this district has built and cannot name, and the sixth of them went into a public book on the fifth of this month, and not one of the six has been given a name.** A rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security, and the finding is that a name is the last thing an instrument gets and not the first, and that all six of them were made out of people and dates and books. **The wall in a counting room still has five questions on it under a heading that says NOT ANSWERED, and a sixth that has no name and is not on the wall, and nobody knows what any of them is owed.**

Adrian stood on the ballast at the far end of it, at about twenty to five in the afternoon, **not holding anything, and the only thing he had posted was a day a week and three bound copies of a book,** and the day was written in chalk on the board, and the twenty-ninth of this month was written in chalk on the kerb rail, and both of those were things a stranger could read in the dark by putting a hand out.

**He was not the fourth bearer and he was not a keeper of a board and he was not one of the three who held the weeks.** The man who will end the use is sixty-one years old and keeps a frame at the twelfth place two miles west and has held a pair on this line for thirty-four years, and had been in a hut on the night of the twenty-ninth of last month with two other people in it whose names he was not told in advance, and who had put his hand on a lever and said nine words out loud and had them read out to forty people in daylight.

Adrian had never met him and had said in a counting room in Alder Reach on the eighteenth of last month that he would have said no if he had been asked, and that saying no is not in anybody's hands any more.

**The term ends on the twenty-ninth of this month. Nobody can extend it, nobody extended it, and four people who do not like him can revoke the whole of it in a sentence, and the man who ends the use has not been asked whether he will still be there on that date, and the entry of the seventh says he will be, because he comes twice a month and the twenty-ninth is one of the two, and it says that a man who comes twice a month for nineteen years is not a substitute and is not a keeper and is the only person in this district that the railway cannot be sent to anybody else instead of.**
