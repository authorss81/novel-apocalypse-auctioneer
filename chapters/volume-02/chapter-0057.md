# Chapter 57: The Word for a Train

Ivo Stenn had been the ward market's freight clerk for six years and its cooperative secretary for two, and in that time he had said no to about four hundred things, and on the thirteenth of this month he was going to say yes to nine coppers, and he had known for a week that he was going to, and he had spent that week doing the arithmetic that a man does when he has already decided and is looking for a figure to put in front of a room.

The nine coppers came back to his own room on the eighth. He had written them in himself, in his own hand, in the fourth column, with the reason, because that is how the levy has been kept since the flood year: **nine coppers a quarter, standing crew at St. Orra, refused, and the reason is that the thing has no day on it.** He had said it out loud in a counting room in front of eleven trades in the third week of last month and the sentence he used was *a rate paid for a thing with no day is this market's own paper levy with a different word on it*, and it had been received as a piece of principle, and he had gone home and written it in the fourth column and had not looked at the fourth column since.

He looked at it on the eighth because a reader had come and told him that a lot of cast iron in a hut nine miles west had been read, and that the reading said the toll was the origin, and that the origin was not a place the reading would name.

"A date," said Ivo Stenn, to nobody, in his office, with the fourth column open. "**A date. That is all that has been wrong with this entry for a month and I did not have a date to fix it with, and now I have four hundred and eleven movements with times in them, which is a date with a train in it.**"

---

**The counting room on the thirteenth of this month had about thirty people in it and eleven trades and a chair for each trade and a clerk, and the paper on the wall was the rate as voted on the thirteenth of the month before last, in columns, with three unassigned coppers and a reason, and it is the first rate sheet in this district anybody can read.**

> **FORTY-ONE COPPERS A QUARTER, FROM EVERY HOUSEHOLD OF TWO ADULTS AND ABOVE. NINETEEN TO THE WARDEN'S PUBLISHED INSPECTION. ELEVEN TO THE PUBLIC READER AT THE COUNTER. SIX TO THE BOARDS AND THE APPOINTED CLERK'S BOARD. TWO TO OIL. THREE UNASSIGNED, WITH THE REASON ENTERED, WHICH IS THAT A RATE WHICH PAYS FOR A THING NOBODY CAN NAME IS THE RATE THIS MARKET HAD BEEN CHARGING FOR ELEVEN YEARS.**

"I have put one line on the paper," said Stenn, "and I have not put it in the fourth column, and I have not called it an increase, and I want the room to understand both of those before I say the number.

"**Nine coppers. A bushel of chalk and a lamp at one signal hut on the Needle line, for one quarter, on the ninth place.**"

He had not got to the vote, because a chandler of about fifty called Hessa Krell had got to it first, and she had done it in one sentence, and it is in the minute because Stenn asked for it to be.

"**You are putting it at the ninth place.**"

"I am putting it at the ninth place."

"That is the place nine miles out where the line stops."

"**That is the place nine miles out where the line stops, Mrs. Krell, and it is the only place on that line where a person is on a shift, and if I put a bushel of chalk anywhere else then the ninth place does not get it.**"

"And I am to pay nine coppers a quarter for a lamp in a hut nine miles out," said Mrs. Krell, "and I have got four hundred and eleven households behind me and not one of them walks nine miles to look at a signal, and Tell me what the people at the counter are going to say to me."

"They are going to say nothing at all," said Stenn, "**because you and I both know that the answer to your question is that this is not a service for your street, and it is the first entry in this market's book that is not a service for anybody's street, and I have put it to you anyway, and Enter the reason before anybody votes.**"

"Enter it," said Marda Osk, who had come and had not expected to be there.

"**Enter it as this: the ninth place is a place where a person is on a shift, and the things this market funds are the things a person on a shift needs in order to be found, and a person on a shift who cannot be found is a person who is doing a job and is not in the book.** And add the other half. Add that a rate buys a service and does not buy a seat, and that nine coppers buys a lamp and a stick of chalk and **nothing else, and does not buy a place on a train, and the reason that is on the paper is that this market has been collecting a rate for eleven years out of people who could not get to a counter, and I am not doing that again at fifty coppers.**"

---

**The trade that said yes was the hauliers', and it took them about four minutes, and the reason they said yes is the only reason in this chapter that anybody will remember.**

"**You are asking us to pay nine coppers for the lamp at the place where we park our stock.**"

A man of about sixty at the third chair, in a coat with a chalk line on the cuff, whose name is entered as the hauliers' seat and not as a person, because that is what the seat is.

"I am asking everybody to pay nine coppers for the lamp at the ninth place."

"You are. And we are the only trade in this room who has been to that place, and we go twice a week, and we have a barrow-load of water off that column every week, and there are nine vehicles of ours sitting on that siding since the eleventh of last month because your yards are full, and **the man that leased it signed it, and he is not in this room, and if anybody in this district is going to pay for a lamp at that place out of a rate then the trade that caused the problem ought to be the first to put its hand up and not the last.**"

The hand went up about four minutes after the objection, and it went up by the man in the coat, and nobody in that counting room had asked it to and Ivo Stenn entered the fact that it was not asked for.

The vote was by trades, each carrying its own count read out, as it was on the rate day. Eleven carried. **One abstained**, and the abstention is entered, and the abstaining trade is a trade that has no vehicles and no stock and no use for a lamp and did not argue and simply sat there, and **Stenn entered that there is still no trade in this market for the people the reader is for**, and the entry is two years old and he has now written it for the third time.

**The rate is fifty coppers a quarter and the columns are five and the fifth is the line.**

> **NINETEEN TO THE WARDEN'S PUBLISHED INSPECTION. ELEVEN TO THE PUBLIC READER AT THE COUNTER. SIX TO THE BOARDS AND THE APPOINTED CLERK'S BOARD. TWO TO OIL. NINE TO THE LINE: ONE SIGNAL HUT, THE NINTH PLACE, ONE LAMP AND ONE BUSHEL OF CHALK, ONE QUARTER. THREE UNASSIGNED, WITH THE REASON ENTERED, WHICH IS: THE COST OF A SHEET NOBODY HAS BOUGHT.**

---

**And the word, and it failed again, and it failed in a worse shape than the last time, and about forty people were in the room for it.**

The room had been failing at a word since the twenty-fourth of the month before last. On a medicine case in a yard it had produced *urgent*, eleven, *scheduled*, seven, *priority*, four, all entered, none adopted, and the line had gone in the order the responsible party put it in with a reason and a clock on a lid in chalk. **A railway is not that.** A railway is not a queue, and Ivo Stenn said so four times in about ten minutes and was right four times.

"The line does not have a scarce thing in it," he said. "**It has a scarce direction.** Trains come east. That is the way everything moves. The west end has been under water since the flood and there is nothing at the other side of it to go and nothing at this side to fetch. So there is no contest about who goes first, because they all go the same way, and the question is not order, it is **how many of them can be out at once, and when the answer is none, what is in the yard instead.**"

Somebody said the word *priority* and Ivo Stenn said, "No, and the reason no is the reason we wrote on the wall, and the reason is that priority means somebody decided, and the somebody has to be somebody."

"Then *traffic*."

"No, because traffic is a count and we have a count and the count is not the difficulty."

"*Next out.*"

"No, because a train is not a person at a door. **A train is placed, not arrived. It is not next out, it is next set, and the setting is done by a signal, and the signal does not know it is in a queue.**"

"*By standing.*"

And that got a laugh, and Stenn allowed it, and then said the thing that is the finding of the thirteenth:

"**Five words proposed. None adopted. And I am going to say why, and then I am going to stop, and I am not going to propose a sixth, and if somebody else proposes a sixth I will move that there be no more.**

"**It is not that we have not got a word. It is that we have got one word and there are two different things being scarce, and they are shaped nothing like each other, and the shape of the word is the problem.**

"On a medicine run, the scarce thing is a place on a vehicle and it arrives at a door, and a man with a clock can order it. On a railway, the scarce thing is a **direction**, and a direction is not a thing anybody can queue for, and the scarcer direction on this line is the one going **west**, and there is nobody standing at the west end of it, because the west end is under nine feet of water.

"So the hardest scarcity in this district is a direction nobody wants, and **we have spent a month failing to name a queue for a hole.**"

---

**And then the thing that worked, and it worked in about ninety seconds and it was not a word, and the man who did it was not in the room and had said in front of about three hundred people on a ballast six days ago that he would like it noted that he was not frightened.**

Osgood Trill's page had come to the market, because the line book is public and a copy clerk had indexed it and the ward market's counter will read anything anybody puts on it. It is his working page for the east end, and it has no heading and no preamble, and it is written in the third person about himself, and the first line of it is this:

> **A TRAIN THAT CANNOT BE MOVED IS NOT WAITING. A TRAIN THAT CANNOT BE MOVED IS THE END OF THE LINE. WHEN A MAN STANDS IN A YARD AND SAYS HE IS WAITING FOR A TRAIN, ASK HIM WHICH WAY THE TRAIN IS GOING, AND IF THE TRAIN IS NOT GOING ANYWHERE THEN HE IS NOT WAITING, HE IS OCCUPYING, AND OCCUPYING COSTS SOMEBODY SOMETHING, AND THE SOMEBODY IS NOT THE MAN WITH THE TRAIN.**

"It is not a word," said Stenn. "**And it is the first thing anybody has written down about this that is a sentence with a thing in it on both sides, and it is the reason I have got a number to put in front of this room and it is not a word at all.**

"**Nine vehicles. Twenty days. That is what is in the yard, and that is not four hundred and eleven households' money, that is one siding and one village and a bank, and the number that matters today is twenty, and the number that mattered on the sixth was thirty-four yards.**"

---

**And at about half past two in the afternoon, in about ninety seconds, Halloway Reed's man put a timetable on the counter and asked for it to be read out, and it was read out, and it was better than anything in that room, and it was better than the four sheets of paper on the wall.**

It is a single sheet. It has four columns: a time, a place, a claim, and a toll. The times are plausible. The places are the twelve on the near section and Harrow, which is under water, and the claims are for a place on a train to Alder Reach at a stated time, and the toll is in its own column, **which is a new thing and it is the cleverest piece of paper anybody has put in Alder Reach this year, because a toll in a column is a toll that can be compared and a toll in a sentence is a toll you have to trust.**

It is printed on the same blank claim stock the comparison table uses. It is honest on its face. It says at the bottom, in the same size as everything else, that no such timetable exists and that the claim is a claim on the **next** closing, and there is no next closing and everybody in that room knows there is no next closing, and the sheet says so itself.

"**He is better than he was in the twelfth of last month, and I want that read out twice,**" said Ivo Stenn, "and I want the fourth column noticed. He has moved the toll into a column so that a woman who cannot read a sentence can compare two of them, and he has done it to sell something that does not exist, and **it is the best printing in this district and it is the second-best argument anybody has made this month.**"

"What is the first?" said Hessa Krell.

"The first is the sentence on the bottom of that sheet," said Stenn, "and it is that he prints the falsehood on the front in the same type as the truth, and he would not do that if he thought anybody was frightened, and **he is not frightened and he is not cruel, and there is a woman in this district who was sold a copy of that on a tram arch in a yard of nine hundred people, and the toll was on page one, and she has never once said she was tricked.**"

"So what do we do?"

"We print ours," said Stenn, "and there is not one to print, **and that is the correct state of a market and I want the minute to say I said so, because in six weeks somebody is going to put that man's sheet on this wall and call it the ward market's timetable, and the difference between them is that ours would have a train behind it and his has a printing press, and the press is nine coppers and the train is a line.**"

And the rate is fifty coppers, and the fifth column is the line, and the sheet on the wall has five columns and no words in it, and **the timetable is not on the wall, and the wall says why.**

---

**And at four o'clock in the afternoon Rutt read it out at the counter, because that is the standing practice, and nine people brought claims, and the practice broke, and the way it broke is the most useful thing anybody did on the thirteenth of this month.**

The rule of the counter is one sentence and it has been in the book since the twenty-sixth of the month before last and it is the reason the counter exists: **if anybody in this city has told you a different number about any of it, bring it and I will read it out again, in the words you did not bring.**

She could do it all day. She read the four columns out four times and she read the line at the bottom that says no such timetable exists, and she read it in the voice she uses for tolls, and there were about twenty people in that part of the market by half past four and nine of them brought something.

Five of the nine were honest. That is the figure and it is the same figure as the last time and nobody in that room treated it as a coincidence, because it is not a coincidence and the whole point of a comparison table is that the proportion of honest paper does not improve because the paper gets prettier.

What broke the practice was the ninth person, and she was a woman of about sixty with a stick, and she had not brought a claim. She had come to ask whether the timetable was true.

"**It is not true and it says so on the front**," said Rutt, "**and I can read you every word of it, and that is the whole of what this counter does, and there is no claim on it, because there is nothing to claim against.**"

"Then I would like to know what is true."

And Rutt, who is about fifty and reads four days a week at eleven coppers a quarter, put the sheet down and said the thing that is in the book on the thirteenth in her own words and which is the finding of the day:

"**I cannot read you an absence.** That is what I have found out this afternoon and I have been at this counter for two years and I did not know it until you asked me.

"I can read you a claim, because a claim has words on it and I read the words. **I cannot read you the train that is not going to come, because there is nothing printed about it, and my job is words, and I have spent five weeks in this district being the person who says things out loud so that nobody can be tricked by a person who does not.**

"So I am going to tell you the only true thing I can, and it is short, and it is not what you came for, and you are not obliged to like it. **There is no timetable. There are nine vehicles standing at a place nine miles west, and they are not coming, and I cannot tell you when, and there is a man with a book who comes to this counter on Fridays and if a thing is going to happen he writes it in his book and I read it out. And on the thirteenth of this month his book says nothing about a train to Alder Reach, and that is not the same as a train not coming, and I have never in my life been able to turn one into the other, and I do not think anybody in this district can.**"

The woman stood at the counter for a while. Then she said, "That is the first true thing anybody has said to me about that sheet and I have had it nine days."

"It is on page one. On the bottom. In the same type."

"It is in the same type," she agreed, "**and a man who has to print a lie in the same type as the truth is a man who does not think he is telling it, and I have been trying to work out what he is, and I could not.**"

And Rutt entered on the counter's own sheet — she keeps one, it is not a public record, it is her working page — the line: **ONE ASKED FOR AN ABSENCE. I CANNOT READ ONE. THE NEXT THING THIS COUNTER NEEDS IS A WAY OF SAYING WHAT IS NOT GOING TO HAPPEN, AND THERE IS NO COLUMN FOR IT, AND THAT IS NOT MY FAILURE, IT IS THE FAILURE OF EVERY INSTRUMENT IN THIS DISTRICT, WHICH WERE ALL BUILT BY PEOPLE WHO WERE RECORDING SOMETHING.** And at the bottom of it, in a different ink, she wrote: **and one of us has to build it, and it is not going to be the counter, because the counter opens at six.**

---

**And the last thing that happened in that counting room on the thirteenth of this month was not a vote and it is in the minute, and Stenn wrote it himself, and it is the fourth unassigned copper's reason finally being finished.**

"Two things before we go.

"**One. The two blocks west of the depot.** A woman who lives at the top of a stair that is the only way out of her own two blocks is not on a rate sheet because the two blocks are in no ward, and a market cannot levy a household that is not in a ward, and I have known that for six years and never had to write it. **So the market is going to make a rate sheet for ground that nobody has claimed, and I am going to do it out of the fourth column, and it will take me a fortnight, and I am aware that I am the freight clerk of a market and not a man who walks about asking people where they live, and I am going to have to do that part, and I am not happy about it.**

"**Two. Rutt's eleven coppers.** Four days a week at the counter, and I have had that rate challenged twice this year by men who thought a day of reading was worth more than a quarter of it, and I could not answer them, and the answer is that the money is not there. **It is not there because this market is a market of small things. I have spent six years finding out what a market can refuse and I have discovered this week that what a market of small things can do is fund a lamp, a bushel of chalk, a reader, a clerk's board, and a rate sheet for two blocks of ground that nobody has claimed.**

"And I want that written down in my own hand, because in a year somebody is going to come in here with a man who prints timetables and say that the ward market is the obstacle. **And we are not the obstacle. We are the only thing in this district that has ever paid for anything, and what we have paid for is a room, a light, and a sheet of paper, and a road is not a room, a light, and a sheet of paper, and I have been saying so since I was thirty and I have just been given the figures for it.**"
