# Chapter 78: The Same Word

At about eleven in the morning on the twenty-seventh of this month a woman of about fifty read a page of the public lot book out loud at the rail in the yard of Lot 17, in the ordinary voice, to about thirty people, and the page had eleven lines on it written by the man whose hour it was, and nine of them were about the fact that he had not used it, and the tenth said that the reason was not a rule and not a limit and not a discipline, and the eleventh said that he was going to use it and that it was going to cost.

The page is in the book in full and it is the first entry in the history of that book that is a page about a man deciding to do a thing, and the registrar wrote under it, in her own hand, that the ledger for the new month opens with a blank and two non-events carried forward from the month before, and that **a blank is not a refusal and is not a yes, and this district has three words for a person who is not there and it has only just worked out that it needs a fourth for a person who is standing right there.**

---

**And the asking was done in a yard in nine minutes on the twenty-sixth of April, and it was the first time in seven weeks that the method was used on the man who invented most of it, and the tenth hand did it in the ordinary way and did not enjoy it and said so afterward.**

The five things are on a wall in the counting room of the ward market. They are what a job is, in sentences somebody who has never done it can understand; how long, with an hour on it; how you say no, and that no reason is owed and that nobody will ask for one afterwards; who answers for you while you are saying it; and where you stand when you answer. They were worked out on the sixth of this month in a yard in about nine minutes by a chandler of about fifty-six off the lower terrace who wanted to know how to ask somebody whether they would do a job, and there is a sixth thing that has no name and belongs to a clerk with a pen.

"So the thing is an hour," said the tenth hand, "and it is on a lever of cast iron and it is lying on a bench in a hut four hours up a bank because a boy of nineteen carried it eleven yards in the rain, and the man who has it has a certificate about it with the word CLEAN on it and a standing line he could not fill in."

"And nobody has read it since the eleventh of last month," said somebody at the back.

"**Nobody has read it since the eleventh of March,**" said the tenth hand. "**And I am going to say the rest of it and then I am going to stop, because it is not mine to ask and it is being asked. Mr Vale: it is an hour. You will read it on a bench. The boy is in the room and he is nineteen and he is not on a roll and nobody is going to ask him anything. How you say no is by not going, and no reason is owed, and I am not going to argue with you if you say no and neither is anybody else on this wall.**"

And then the sixth thing, which has no name, and it was done by the reader at the counter, who is on her fourth day of four and who came up on the tram line at a quarter to six as she does every day she is on it, and **about nine people did not know she was in the yard.**

"The question that has been asked," said Rutt, "is this, in my words, and I am not the one asking it and I am not the one being asked, and my job is to say what it is so that the entry in about a fortnight is the question and not somebody's memory of one. **Is the reader willing to read a lot he has read once, on a bench, in a hut, for an hour, and to pay whatever it costs, and to be held to the toll of it in a public book, on a day anybody can name?** That is the question. Does anybody want it said differently, now, while it can be."

And Adrian Vale said the fourth thing before anybody had got to the fourth thing, and he said it in about four seconds, and it is in the book, and it is the reason the reading happened at all.

"**Bett Ruhl,**" he said.

Somebody laughed. He did not.

"**Who answers for you while you are saying it. Bett Ruhl. She is at the place with the tea and she cannot read and she has read a release condition out in a yard of about nine hundred people twice, and she does not know what is in this and will not be told, and she is not here and I have not asked her, and I am going to write her name down and I am not going to go and ask her, because if I ask her then it is a job and a job can be refused and a reader is not a job.**"

And the finding under that is in Nessa Pell's hand and it is that **a reader who names the person who answers for him has done the one thing this district has not done for seven weeks, which is to say out loud in advance who is standing behind the thing being done, and the person he named cannot read, and the district has spent nine weeks treating literacy as a condition of being asked, and the reader has just appointed somebody who is not eligible.**

---

And before any of them went up the bank he asked the keeper, in person, on the shoulder at the fourth place, on the morning of the twenty-seventh, whether she wished to speak before he started and not after, because that is what she asked for on the eleventh of last month and it is in the book with an hour on it, and he asked it at about nine o'clock and she said yes, and she had one thing ready, and **she said it in about eleven seconds and it is the reason for everything that happened in that hut.**

"**The iron is not in my hut,**" said Sef Anyard. "**It has not been in my hut since the sixth of this month and I have known that since the sixth, and there is nothing in the room you are going to sit in except a frame with a hole in it and a bench and a stove. So you are going to sit in a hut four hours from the place the lot is in and read it, and that is a thing this district has never done, and I would like it entered that I said so and was not argued with.**

"**And the second thing. You asked me at ten to five on the eleventh of last month, two hours after you sat at my table, whether I wished to speak. Do you know why that was the sentence I noticed?**"

"No."

"**Because it was the first one in eleven years that was about me,**" she said. "**And now there is a man asleep on that bench and he is nineteen and he is not on a roll and he carried a thing eleven yards in the rain and slept beside it and nobody told him anything, and you are going to read that thing on his bench, and I want his name in your book before the iron's, because he is a person and the iron is a lever, and I am not sorry for the order and I am not going to apologise for it.**"

---

---

And then they went up the bank, all four of them, at about half past four in the morning, and it is five and a quarter hours with four changeovers in it and they did all four, and they were in that hut by about ten.

And then the thing said one sentence at about ten past one in the afternoon, before the hour started, in the same words it said the other time, and it is the third thing anybody in this district has ever read off a lot in a room with a person in it, and it is the first time the thing has said anything before the hour instead of after it, and Rutt wrote it down as it came and Nessa Pell compared the two pages on the twenty-eighth and **the two are the same in every word, and that is entered as a finding and not as a comfort.**

> **A LOT MAY BE READ TWICE. A READING IS AN HOUR AND A COST, AND THE COST IS A PART OF THE TOLL THE LOT CHARGES, AND IT IS THE SAME TOLL EVERY TIME.**
>
> **A CERTIFICATE THAT SAYS A LOT IS CLEAN IS NOT A READING AND CANNOT BE WRONG ABOUT THE TOLL, BECAUSE IT HAS NO COLUMN FOR THE TOLL.**
>
> **A HOLDER WHO IS NOT AT THE CUSTODY IS A HOLDER WHO IS NOT THERE. A CUSTODY MAY BE ENDED BY THE PERSON WHO HOLDS IT. IT MAY NOT BE ENDED BY A PERSON WHO WAS SENT TO FIND OUT WHETHER IT IS ENDED.**
>
> **YOU MAY NOT READ A DOCUMENT NOBODY HAS HANDLED. YOU MAY NOT READ A PERSON. YOU MAY NOT ASK A LOT WHERE THE TOLL LANDS, AND IF YOU ASK IT FOR MORE YOU WILL BE GIVEN WHAT YOU HAVE BEEN GIVEN ALREADY.**
>
> **NONE OF THIS IS AN ANSWER. YOU MAY STILL GO UP THE BANK AND READ IT, AND YOU WILL PAY, AND WHAT YOU WILL GET IS WHAT YOU GOT THE LAST TIME.**

He read it out loud to the three people in the room before he did anything else, in his own words, and the words were the words, and one of the three — the tenth hand, who has argued exactly one rule in his life — asked him the only question anybody asked in that hut all afternoon, and it took about four seconds.

"**You have read this before. Is that what it is going to do.**"

"**It is what it did last time,**" said Adrian Vale. "**I asked it for more in March in a room in Alder Reach with about four hundred people in it and it gave me the same four lines, and the second time it did not even stop. I am going to get the same thing. I want that written down before the hour and not after, because after the hour I will not be able to say it as clearly.**"

And the tenth hand wrote it down, and the entry says that the sheet that went into the sleeve on the twenty-eighth has a sentence at the top of it in the reader's own hand that is not a finding and is not a toll and is the first time this district has entered a prediction as a prediction, and the entry under that says the finding is not that he was right and it is not that he was wrong, it is that **a man who knows what a thing is going to say still has to go and hear it say it, and there is no instrument in this basin that lets anybody stand in a room instead of a bench, and that this is not a failure of instruments, it is what an hour is.**

The bench was about four feet off the floor and the boy had been on the floor because he said the bench was his and nobody argued. The rain had come through the two places it comes through and the light in that hut in the afternoon comes off the west window and lies along the floor and not on the bench, so the reading was done in a grey room with a lamp on a ledge.

Before he started, Adrian Vale looked at the two ends of it for about four minutes, because a licensed valuer from the ward market district had looked at them nine days before and written about them in a certificate that said CLEAN, and a man who has been handed somebody else's clean is entitled to his own four minutes.

The wedge end is cut to a wedge and worn to a shine about two inches along the top, and it is the shine of a thing that has been pushed a great many times by somebody who knew what it was for. The other end has a hole in it and a loop of red signal wire through the hole, cut loose, and the ends of the wire are black with nine years of being cut and left. He put his thumb in the loop and turned it, and it moved, and the bed of the hole under the wire is bright where nine years of cast iron has been polished by one red wire in one direction.

"**Nine years,**" he said. "**One hole, one wire, and a man pushing it the same way every time, and the hole has been polished by it. That is not in the reading and it is not in the certificate and it is the only thing about this object I have ever been able to see with my own eyes, and I have been putting numbers on things for seventeen years and a polished hole is the only number I have got.**"

Nevin Roke said, from the floor, without moving, "**That is the end that is cold,**" and nobody asked him how he knew, and he did not say.

**And the reading was done on the bench in the hut at the fourth place on the afternoon of the twenty-seventh, from about two o'clock, for about an hour, and here it is in the form it was given, and it is the form it was given in on the eleventh of March, word for word, and that is the whole of what happened.**

> **LOT: THE HARROW TONGUE. CAST IRON. TAKEN OUT OF THE FLOODED HARROW INTERCHANGE IN THE FOURTH YEAR. USED THIRTEEN YEARS. IN A FRAME AT THE NINTH PLACE FIVE, AND ON A BENCH AT THE FOURTH PLACE SINCE THE SIXTH OF THIS MONTH.**
>
> **USE: SEND ONE VEHICLE BACK ALONG THE SECTION IT CAME FROM. ONE MOVEMENT. ONE WAY. THE USE IS NOT GIVEN BACK.**
>
> **TOLL: THE ORIGIN.**
>
> **STANDING: A PERSON WHO CAN HOLD A SIGNAL.**
>
> **AND THIS IS THE FINDING AND IT IS ENTERED AS A FINDING AND NOT AS A CONCLUSION: THE OBJECT DOES NOT HOLD PLACES. THE TOLL IS NOT STATED IN A FORM THAT CAN BE READ AT A PERSON, OR A HOUSEHOLD, OR A TOWN, OR A WATER, AND THE OBJECT DOES NOT SAY WHAT THE ORIGIN IS, AND ASKED FOR MORE IT REPEATS ITSELF.**
>
> **AND: ELEVEN USES ARE ON THIS LINE AND THE OBJECT DOES NOT COUNT THEM AND DOES NOT KNOW THEM AND HAS NEVER ONCE BEEN ASKED, AND IT HAS SAID ELEVEN IN MARCH AND IT HAS SAID ELEVEN IN APRIL.**

Nobody in that hut heard anything except what is written down, because there is no such thing as hearing a thing like this, and the entry says that the room was silent for about the hour and that the boy of nineteen sat on the floor with his back against the stove and did not move, and that at about three o'clock he got up and went out and stood in the rain and came back in, and that neither of them said anything about that either, and that the tenth hand entered it because a thing somebody does while a man is working is a cost and the cost belongs in the book.

And the sheet went into a sleeve and it went into the book on the twenty-eighth at about ten past eleven in the morning, read by Nessa Pell and Marda Osk together against his own hand on two pages, and the practice is the practice, and a sheet that goes into a book loose is a sheet that comes out loose.

---

**And between the first hour and the second hour the same thing happened that happened on the eighteenth of last month in a room over a sale floor, and the district found out in about four minutes on the twenty-eighth that the word *repeat* had been in the book since the twelfth of March as a piece of Latin and had turned into English overnight.**

He could not remember which gate he had gone out of.

That is the whole of it and it took four minutes to find out and it took the rest of the morning to be entered, and the entry is in his own hand at the top of the page and the registrar did not touch it.

"**I went out of this city on the seventeenth of February at six in the morning to look at a pumping engine and I have got the entry in my own book in front of me and it says the west gate, and there is no west gate. I know there are two ways out of Alder Reach and I have walked both of them hundreds of times, and one of them is not there, and I did not lose a direction this time like I did in March. I lost the way I went out of the door of my own city.**

"**I have been in this district for seven weeks, and I have not been home, and there is nothing at the other end of it except my own front step, and I would like it entered that the loss is exact and that it is about a departure and that a departure is what this thing charges, and that in March it took a direction off me and today it took a way out, and both of those are the same sentence and the sentence is the toll.**"

And the finding printed under it, in the tenth hand's hand, and it is the finding of the whole of the twenty-eighth of April, and it is eleven words long.

> **A TOLL ON A PLACE TAKES MEMORY OF DEPARTURE. HE HAS PAID IT TWICE AND GOT A PIECE OF IT EACH TIME AND THE SECOND PIECE IS SMALLER.**

---

And the last thing about the reading is not about him, and **it is the part the district cannot use, and it is on the same page in a clerk's hand.**

The interval has gone up three times. After the reading of the eleventh of March the limit entered was not before the eighteenth. After the twenty-fifth of March, on a day the limit permitted, the next permitted hour was not before the fifth of this month. **After the twenty-seventh of this month the limit entered is not before the eleventh of next month. Seven days, then eleven, then fourteen.**

"**That is not a rule getting stricter,**" said Ivo Stenn. "**A rule that gets stricter gets stricter for a reason and says so. That is an interval that has gone up every time a man has used one, and I have been a clerk for six years and I know what an interval that goes up on use is. It is not a limit. It is a man buying days, and the days are getting more expensive and the price is not printed anywhere, and I am not putting it in a column, and I want it in the book as a thing I have noticed and will not defend.**"

And Adrian Vale said the thing that is in the book under his name and that nobody argued with, and it is the fourth time in seven weeks he has said a thing about himself in a room and it is the shortest.

"**I do not know what it is and I am not going to stand here and help anybody to name it. It is not cowardice, because a coward would not have spent the hour. It is not discipline, because a discipline has an end in it and mine has not. I have been asked three times what this is and I have now said the same true sentence three times and the sentence has got shorter, and a sentence that gets shorter when you say it a fourth time is a sentence you are learning, and I would like somebody to notice that and to write down that I noticed.**"

---

**And on the afternoon of the twenty-eighth, at about four o'clock, a sheet that had been on a desk since the middle of March went on a wall in Alder Reach with a heading nobody in this district had ever printed, and the heading is two words long and it is the finding of the second half of the day and it is not a debt and it is not a rate and it is not a question.**

The heading is **NOT ANSWERED.**

Beneath it are four questions, in a school hand, and they are Teo's, and he wrote them in the middle of March and gave them to Nessa Pell and she did not put them in the book for a month because she has never put a question in that book in nineteen years. She put them in on the twenty-eighth. Not as a question. **As four lines under a heading that says what is true about them.**

> **ONE. WHO TELLS US.**
>
> **TWO. BY WHOM.**
>
> **THREE. FOR HOW LONG.**
>
> **FOUR. IS ANYBODY OBLIGED TO ANSWER.**

And under the four, in Nessa Pell's hand, and she wrote it and then read it out in a yard and then asked whether she was allowed to keep it on the wall, and the answer was that the wall does not require permission and never has, and that this is the fifth time she has been told that and the fifth time she has asked.

> **ENTERED THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF THIS MONTH BY NESSA PELL, CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS, WHO REFUSED TO ENTER IT FOR A MONTH. THE BOY IS TEO, FIFTEEN, AT THE FIGURE ONE MILE AND THREE QUARTERS, AND HE ASKED ON HIS OWN BEHALF AND ON BEHALF OF THIRTY-ONE PEOPLE, AND THE FIRST THREE ARE ONE QUESTION IN FOUR PARTS AND THE FOURTH IS A DIFFERENT QUESTION AND IS ALSO HIS.**
>
> **I HAVE NEVER PUT A QUESTION IN THIS BOOK BECAUSE A QUESTION IS A THING SOMEBODY IS OWED, AND IF I PUT ONE IN THEN I AM THE CLERK WHO PUT IT IN, AND A CLERK CANNOT BE OWED ANYTHING, AND I WOULD RATHER IT WAS ON A WALL WHERE IT IS NOBODY'S.**

Rutt read all of it out at the rail at about six o'clock on the twenty-eighth, in the ordinary voice, including her own reason and including the fact that it is hers, to about fifty people, and nobody answered any of the four, and the finding under it is entered in her hand and it is the reading-back and it is the reason the reading-back exists.

> **A REFUSAL IS A THING SOMEBODY DID AND AN ANSWER IS A THING SOMEBODY GAVE, AND A THING THAT WAS DONE CAN BE READ BACK IN THE ORDINARY VOICE, AND A THING THAT WAS GIVEN HAS TO BE READ BACK IN THE SAME VOICE AND IT COMES OUT DIFFERENT, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS THAT NINE PEOPLE IN THAT YARD LOOKED AT THE SHEET WHILE SHE READ IT AND ABOUT NINE DID NOT LOOK AT IT AT ALL, AND ONE OF THE NINE WAS A BOY OF FIFTEEN WHO READ IT FIRST.**

And the boy was in the yard and he said one thing, and Nessa Pell entered it in full, and the entry says he asked whether it was going in and he was told it was, and that he then asked for the fourth question to be printed at the size of the other three and not smaller, and that it was, and that it is on the wall that way.

"**I have been waiting a month,**" he said. "**And it is in a book with my name on the asking, and not my house and not my mother's kitchen and not a household, and I wrote my own name on it and nobody made me.**

"**And I asked whether anybody has to answer, and the answer is no, and the no is on the wall, and that is an answer. I have had a no before off a woman in a yard in front of six hundred people and it cost her something and it cost me something and neither of us was wrong. This no cost nobody anything and it is the first one I have had that was put in a book.**

"**So: no. Nobody has to answer. And it is on a wall in Alder Reach, and it is on eleven sheets, and a man on a tram arch has a sheet with a day on it, and the ward market has a sheet with a day and an hour on it under a column with nothing in it, and this district has spent seven weeks finding out how to ask somebody a question, and the method is on the same wall, five things and no name on the sixth.**

"**And I am fifteen and I am going home, and I would like it entered that I said the method was on the wall, and that is the fourth thing I have said today that somebody did not ask me for.**"

---

**And the last thing in that yard on the twenty-eighth of April is about a boy of nineteen who is not on a roll, and it is the closing of the only thread anybody in Alder Reach opened on the seventh of this month, and it was closed in about four minutes, at a rail, in the ordinary voice, at the request of the person it was about.**

He had come in off the bank at about ten in the morning because there were people in his hut, and he had not asked to be in a yard in Alder Reach in his life and did not know that he was allowed to stand at the rail.

"**I am not anybody's keeper,**" said Nevin Roke, nineteen. "**I have not said that to anybody before today and I am saying it because there is a book and I have seen what is in it about me, and the word in it is *nobody*, and I would like it to stay *nobody* because nobody is better than keeper.**

"**And I do not know what it is. It is on my bench because the roof goes and it was cold. I carried it eleven yards off the ballast by the tap in the rain and I thought it was a bar and it was not a bar, and I have not slept properly since the sixth and I am not going to carry it anywhere again, and if anybody in this city wants it they are to come up that bank and get it themselves, and there are four changeovers and I am telling them so in advance.**

"**And I would like the man who reads it to have my name on the sheet, before the iron's, and I am not asking for anything else and I am not asking to be asked anything.**"

And it is in the book in his words, and the finding under it is entered in the same hand as the other two and it is the last line of the twenty-eighth of April.

> **A CUSTODY WITH NO CASE, NO COUNTER AND NO KEY IS NOT WORSE THAN A CASE. A CUSTODY WHOSE KEEPER IS ENTERED AS NOBODY IS NOT A CUSTODY. IT IS A PLACE WHERE A THING IS, AND THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THOSE TWO SENTENCES IS THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN A HELD THING AND A LOST ONE, AND THE LOT IS ON A BENCH IN A HUT AT THE FOURTH PLACE AND THE WEST END OF THIS RAILWAY IS A MILE AND THREE QUARTERS EAST OF IT, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED EITHER WOMAN ON THE BANK WHAT THAT MEANS.**
