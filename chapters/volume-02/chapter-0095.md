# Chapter 95: Thirty Feet of Platform

The first thing that went onto the ninth place's platform on the nineteenth of this month was not a sleeper. It was a chalk line, and it was drawn by a woman of about thirty-eight with a hook and a sacking sheet who had come up the bank on the fifteenth for her own trade and had been asked to stay an extra two days and had said yes, and had asked for the extra two days to be entered as hers and not as anybody's.

She drew a line thirty feet long and nine feet back from the ballast, and she drew it twice, and the first one was wrong, and the entry says so in the keeper's hand because the keeper wrote it down.

"I have been buying paper off four houses for nine years," she said, "and I have never once been the person who says where a thing goes, and a line is not a paper and I do not know why I am doing this, and I have been asked and I said yes, and I would like the yes and the not-knowing in the same line in whatever book this goes in."

---

And the thirty-one people on that bank were asked about a platform on the nineteenth of this month, and it took two days instead of an afternoon, **and the reason is the count, and the count is the reason the whole of this volume exists.**

There are four households at that place. There are thirty-one people. Nineteen of them are not in a household, and they are not in no row, and they are not in the about nine hundred who are in no row in this district and not counted anywhere including on the sheet that collects the four thousand and one.

The count rule says a person is accounted for by a count and not by a household. **So the nineteen are accounted for by the number thirty-one and by nothing else, and there is no list of them and no sheet and no name, and a person who cannot be named cannot be asked, and asking somebody whether they will have a platform is a thing that has to be done to a person.**

Four households were asked on the nineteenth in about nine minutes. The nineteen took until the evening of the twentieth.

The last of them was a man of about sixty-one who was asked in a doorway at about ten at night, and he said yes in about two seconds and then asked a question that four people carried back nine miles on foot, and it is in the public lot book in his own words because a clerk read it out at a counter on the twenty-first and it is the first line anybody in this district has entered about a platform that is not about a railway.

"**If I say yes to a platform, is it still a place I can stand in,**" he said.

Nobody in the doorway had an answer. **Nobody in that doorway had an answer because nobody in Alder Reach can say what a platform does to the place it is built in, and the four people who walked back nine miles had four different guesses and one of them was a man of sixty-eight who said it would be a good thing and did not know why.**

And the answer was given on the twenty-first by a woman of about fifty who is the water at that place and has stood in her own doorway for eleven years, and it is eleven words long and it is in the book because it was said in a room with a reader in it.

"**It is a place where a thing stops. That is all a platform is. Come and stand on it any day.**"

---

**And the entry of the twentieth of this month is the first time in nine years that anybody has asked a village of thirty-one what they wanted done to the ground they live on, and the count is nineteen yeses, one yes, and one no, and the no is not against the platform.**

The no is a woman of about fifty-one who is the keeper of the only pair on that line, and she gave a reason in about eleven words, and the reason is in the book in her own words and it is not a refusal of the platform.

"I will hold a word and I will not hold a thing you can stand on," she said. "**A platform is a thing a stranger stands on and a stranger is not a person I can ask. So yes, and do not write my name on the platform, and put the name of the place on it, which is what the woman at the column said in May and was right about.**"

**The count is nineteen yeses from the nineteen who are not in a household. Four yeses from four households. One yes with a condition. One no with a reason that is about the standing of a name and not about thirty feet of board. And the registrar entered all six figures separately, because the count rule says a person is accounted for by a count and a destination and a remedy, and the six figures are six counts and one destination and one remedy, and entering them as one number would have been a household.**

> **THE ASKING WAS DONE ON THE NINTEENTH AND THE TWENTIETH OF JUNE BY SIX PEOPLE WHO WERE NOT ASKED WHETHER THEY WOULD GO, AND THE SIX WERE A KEEPER, A WATER, A PAPERER, A SIGNALMAN OF THE TENTH HAND, A CLERK OF THE WARD MARKET, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING AND CAME ALONG. NOBODY FROM A RIVER ADMINISTRATION WAS ASKED. NOBODY FROM A FIRM WAS ASKED.**
>
> **NINETEEN PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT IN A HOUSEHOLD WERE ASKED SEPARATELY AND NINETEEN SAID YES. FOUR HOUSEHOLDS WERE ASKED AND FOUR SAID YES. ONE KEEPER SAID YES WITH A CONDITION AND THE CONDITION IS THAT HER NAME IS NOT ON THE PLATFORM. ONE KEEPER SAID YES AND ASKED FOR HER NAME NOT TO BE ON IT. THE TOTAL IS TWENTY-FIVE YESES, WHICH IS NOT THE NUMBER OF PEOPLE, AND THE ENTRY SAYS SO, BECAUSE FOUR HOUSEHOLDS IS A COUNT AND A PERSON IS A COUNT AND THE TWO COUNTS ARE NOT ADDED ANYWHERE IN THIS BOOK.**
>
> **AND THE FINDING IS THAT THE ONLY QUESTION THAT HAD TO BE ASKED TWICE WAS ASKED ONCE, AND THAT THE ONLY PERSON WHO ASKED FOR SOMETHING TO BE LEFT OFF WAS ASKED FOR IT BY HIMSELF, AND THAT NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAS EVER BEFORE ASKED A VILLAGE WHAT IT WANTED DONE TO THE GROUND IT STANDS ON, INCLUDING THE MAN WHO LEASED IT FOR NINE YEARS.**

---

The platform was built on the twentieth and the twenty-first of this month by nine men and one boy of nineteen and a woman of about thirty-eight with a hook, and it took about two days, and it cost nothing, **and the finding is that it is the most expensive thing anybody in this district has done in eleven weeks and it cost nothing.**

The eleven coppers the market pays a quarter for the bench, the lamp, the chalk and the oil is the whole of the money. Nine men lost two days of work, a boy of nineteen lost a day, and a paperer lost a day of a trade she goes up that bank to do, and **none of that is in a rate sheet, because a rate sheet cannot hold a day.**

It is thirty feet long and nine feet of face. It is about a hundred and forty sleepers laid on the ballast and levelled, and boards on top, and a kerb of old rail on the front edge, and it is two feet above the shoulder so that a person can get off it and get back on the path, and at the west end of it there is a lamp on a post that was cut out of a green pipe lying across a cess at the third changeover.

**Nobody laid a stone. Nobody put a mark in the ground. **The platform has four feet of face and thirty feet of length and no mark on it of any kind, and the finding is that a platform is not a mark and a mark may not carry a job, and a platform is not asked to do any job at all except stop being the place where a thing stops, and that is the whole of what the rule was for.****

The man who posted it said one thing about it on the evening of the twenty-first, in a yard, to about thirty people, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"I posted four hundred and ten coppers of standing salvage on the seventeenth of May so that a man could walk up that bank and look at it. **There is a platform on it now. In August a man will walk up that bank, see a platform, and write down that there was a security here, and he will be right, and he will be wrong, and both of those are going to be in the same book and I am not going to be able to explain the difference to him.**

"So I want the entry to say it was spent and not lost, and I want it to say it was spent on the thing it was posted for, and I do not want it back, and I would like somebody to write down that I said I do not want it back before anybody offered."

The registrar entered it, and the entry on the twenty-second of this month is one line long, and it is in her hand.

> **THE FOURTH SECURITY WAS SPENT ON THE TWENTIETH OF JUNE AND NOT LOST. IT WAS THE GROUND AT THE NINTH PLACE, HELD BY NOBODY, POSTED ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF MAY BY THE NEEDLE ROAD HOUSE IN THE NAME OF TARIN KEST, AND IT IS NOW A PLATFORM, AND A PLATFORM IS THE EASIEST THING IN THIS DISTRICT FOR A STRANGER TO GO AND LOOK AT.**

---

And on the twenty-first of this month a woman of forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide lent her yard for the third time, and the third rehearsal in this district's history was walked in it, **and it is the first rehearsal anybody has held that was not on the ground the thing was for.**

A rehearsal is a walk done before the day it is for, on the same ground, by some or all of the people who will do it, for the purpose of finding out whether the walk can be done. The first was on the twenty-second of March and it was in the rain. The second was on the twenty-first of last month and it was up a bank. The third was in a yard on the tide, on cobbles, with a barrow, a coil of rope and two men shouting, and it took about an hour and a half.

She is not paid and is not thanked and insisted, as she did in May, that it be entered as not a security, and it is, and the reason she gives is that in about four years somebody will write that the Tidefall Road House stood behind a public charter, and it lent a yard.

**And the finding of the twenty-first is that a yard cannot rehearse a loop, and that this district has now held three rehearsals and has discovered the limit of the instrument for the first time, and the limit is that a rehearsal can find out whether a walk can be done and cannot find out whether a ground will do what a person says it will do.**

"A line is not a yard," said a foreman of twenty-two years whose name is on the four hundred and ten coppers, because he entered it at his own request in April. "**In a yard the ground is flat and it is dry and there is no flood and no cut and no shoulder and no nine-foot drop and no eleven feet of bank. In a yard I can tell you four men can walk a rope out of a barrow in about a minute. In a yard I cannot tell you anything at all about what nine vehicles do on a loop.**"

"So rehearse the walking."

"**I will rehearse the walking and I have rehearsed the walking and the walking is not the thing that has not been done.**"

The hour and a half was spent on a cart, a rope and a barrow, and it found out two things, and the two things are in the minute, and the first is that the four changeovers on that bank are the same in every direction and the second is that nobody rehearsed the turning, because there is nothing to turn in a yard.

---

And on the afternoon of the twenty-second of this month, at about four o'clock, four pounds of cast iron went up a bank for the second time in this year, and the second time it went in a rope with a lever handle through the loop at the wedged end, and the boy of nineteen held one end of the rope and did not go in the hut.

He said beforehand that he would hold it and would not be the one who went in, and he said it in a yard in Alder Reach on the nineteenth and it is in the minute, and **he held it for about four hours and forty minutes and did not go in, and the keeper of that hut, who is fifty-one and has held the only pair on that line for eleven years, carried it in herself with two men on a board.**

The line is five and a quarter hours with four changeovers. **It took six hours and a quarter in the rain, and the figure is in the boy's index on the twenty-second of this month, and beside it, on the same line, are the eleven hours the party came back in May, and the five and a quarter the tenth hand wrote down in March, and the four and three-quarters that four people believed for a fortnight, and none of the five has been struck, and the entry says that a district which has been wrong five times about a walk and right once is not a district that should be asked for a correction.**

The train on the siding had stood a hundred and thirty-one days on the twenty-second of this month. It is nine vehicles and it came in on its own wheels with a man in the cab who said it was stabled, and it has not moved, and on the twenty-second of this month there was a piece of cast iron in a bag on a bench in a hut at the fourth place, four hours up a bank, and the bracket at the ninth place was still empty.

---

And on the evening of the twenty-first, at a rail in Alder Reach, on a published day, a bridge administrator of nine years said three things about thirty feet of platform. **The first was that it is not a station, the second was right, and the third is the sentence he says on published days and has said for nine weeks.**

He is Selik Marne. He is argued with and never defeated and never converted and not wrong. He is not on any walk and has not been up that bank in nine years, and the room in Alder Reach knows both of those facts and has entered both of them, and the woman at the column has said in a room that she does not want to be asked about a doorway and has not been asked about one.

"That is not a station," he said. "It is thirty feet of board and a lamp cut out of a green pipe, and there is no office, no keeper's wage, no relief, no record of who is on it, and if a train stands there in the dark at four in the morning then a person gets off it onto a shoulder with a nine-foot drop and a path four hundred yards long."

"That is all true," said the tenth hand, from about nine feet away.

"**And it is not the finding, and I have had it for a fortnight, and I have been waiting for somebody to build something so that I could say it about a real thing instead of a plan.** The finding is that a platform is a place a thing stops and a person is not a thing, and this district has spent nine weeks being careful about that difference and has in two days built thirty feet of board that a person has to get off. The reason nobody stopped it is that the woman of the column said come and stand on it any day, and she was asked whether she wanted thirty feet of board and she was not asked whether a man of sixty-one could find it in the dark."

Nobody argued with him and the minute enters that nobody argued with him and that the reason was that he had been right about a drop in the dark in April and had been laughed at in May, and the entry says that a man who has been laughed at once for a correct thing is not a man who can be hurried.

"The third thing," he said, "is that is not enough, and I am going to keep saying that on published days for as long as I hold this post, and nobody in this district believes I will stop and I am not going to stop, **and I would like it entered that the man who posted the security on that platform has lost something that cannot be got back and that the man at the rail has not lost anything at all, and that the difference between those two facts is the whole of what I have been trying to say to this district for nine years.**"

The finding under the third thing is that he did not say whose security, and that he had been told on the nineteenth, and that the woman of the column has said in public that a river office cannot be asked and has never once said that it is not listening.

---

And on the twenty-second of this month, in the hours the counter was shut, a clerk of the ward market who is not the market wrote a place into the line book. **It is the first place this line has ever had in that column that was not a signal, and the entry is four lines long.**

The line book is a book of signals. A place, a time, a direction, and a lever. It is kept by hand at the east end and it has nine hundred and six movements in it since the flood year, and a hundred and forty-one of those movements name the ninth place as the origin held, and it counts movements and not passengers and it has no column for a person.

She wrote it in the four minutes the counting room was shut and she said out loud, twice, in the empty room, what the six things on a wall say a person should say before they do a job, because the sixth thing has no name and belongs to a clerk of nineteen years and she was not going to do it on a page where nobody could hear her.

> **WRITE THE PLACE. WRITE THE DATE. WRITE THAT IT IS NOT A SIGNAL AND THAT A SIGNAL IS WHAT A PLACE IS WHEN SOMEBODY STOPS A THING.**
>
> **THE NINTH PLACE. THE TWENTY-SECOND OF JUNE. A PLACE A THING STOPS AT. NOT A SIGNAL. THE COLUMN FOR IT IS THE SAME COLUMN AND THE HAND IS THE SAME HAND AND THE ENTRY IS A LINE LONG AND IT IS THE FIRST LINE IN NINE HUNDRED AND SIX THAT A PLACE HAS BEEN WRITTEN IN IT ON ITS OWN.**

And the finding on the twenty-third of this month is that a line book kept by hand for nine years has no column for a station and has had a column for a signal for nine hundred and six movements, and that a station is a place that somebody stops a thing at, **and that the whole difference between a railway and a line is four letters in one column and nobody in this district had ever needed to write them until a woman of thirty-one sat down in a shut counting room on the twenty-second of June and could not find anywhere else to put it.**

---

**And on the twenty-second of this month a fourth toll board went up at the ninth place, and it went up on the platform and not on the shoulder, and the reason it went on the platform is that the first three went where a person could stand and this one goes where a person gets off a thing.**

It is nine feet by three feet on two posts, tarred at the foot. The two posts are the two ends of the platform and they are not posts, they are the ends of the platform's own kerb rail, cut and bolted, and the man of sixty-one who wrote signs for two coppers a board was not paid for this one because nobody had lettering money and the market had not voted any.

So the fourth board is the only one of the four in this district with no heading on it, and it carries the same four-foot column ruled in pencil at its two ends and empty between them, and the reason printed inside the column at the height of a man's eye, and the same three lines along the bottom, and one of the bottom lines is a sentence anybody may write on it.

The finding is in the minute on the twenty-third of this month, in the clerk's hand, and it is that **this district has four toll boards and three of them have a name at the top in letters three inches high and the fourth one has nothing at the top, because there was no money for letters, and that the first board in this district with an empty place where a heading goes is the one a person stands in front of after a thing has stopped.**

"You have printed the gap by accident," said the man of about thirty who sells nothing and has a use, when he was told.

"**We have printed the gap by accident and the accident is on the board and that is where the accidents in this district go, and I would like the minute to say that the day comes when somebody cannot read the fourth board is the day the four of them stop being the same board, and that the day may be four months off or it may be four years.**"

And a keeper was asked on the twenty-second, in five things, in a yard, in about four minutes, and the keeper is a man of about fifty who has kept a board at the ninth place since the eighth of last month, who is a signalman of a river administration, who is not a bearer, and who said yes, and gave one condition and it is on the board.

"I will wipe the bottom line when it is wet and I will keep the date and I will not decide anything. **And I am not the keeper of that platform. I have said since the eighth of last month that keeping a board is not holding a pair and I am not going to take a different sentence now that there is something to stand on.**"
