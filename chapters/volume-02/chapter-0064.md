# Chapter 64: The Fare Is Complete

At ten past six in the morning on the twenty-sixth of this month the ward market counter had been open for ten minutes and had nobody in it, and that is normal, and the reason it is normal is that the counter opens at six and the first person to arrive has been the same man for two years and he arrives at about ten past six, and on the twenty-sixth of this month the first person to arrive was a printer.

Rutt was four days a week at eleven coppers a quarter and her shoulder had been bad since the flood year and the term has no clause for a shoulder, and she had walked nine miles on it on the fifteenth of this month and told nobody, and she was standing behind a counter on the morning of the twenty-sixth with her reading sheet in front of her and a man of about forty putting a sheet of paper on it.

"I have been selling on that arch since the flood year," said Halloway Reed. "**And I have never once come to a counter, and I have come to this one, and enter that I came to it, because in about four years there is going to be a book somewhere that says a great many things about me and one of them ought to be that I walked into a ward market when I did not have to.**"

Rutt looked at the sheet.

Then she read it out.

She reads everything that is put on the counter. That is the practice and it was learned in the last month before last and it is the reason the counter exists, and it is one sentence long and it is in the book: **if anybody in this city has told you a different number about any of it, bring it and I will read it out again, in the words you did not bring.** She read his four columns out twice and then she read the bottom of it, which is where he puts the thing that makes it honest, and she read that twice as well, and it took her about four minutes, and about nine people came and stood about four feet back and listened to all of it, which is what happens at that counter.

> **A TIMETABLE OF THE NEAR SECTION OF THE NEEDLE LINE, TWELVE PLACES AND HARROW. FOUR COLUMNS. A TIME, A PLACE, A CLAIM, A TOLL. THE TOLL IS IN ITS OWN COLUMN SO THAT A PERSON WHO CANNOT READ A SENTENCE MAY COMPARE TWO OF THEM.**
>
> **AND AT THE BOTTOM, IN THE SAME TYPE AS EVERYTHING ELSE: THERE IS NO SUCH TIMETABLE. THIS IS A CLAIM UPON A CLOSING THAT HAS NOT BEEN CALLED. YOU PAY FOUR COPPERS AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT YOU PAY.**

"The word is **toll**," said Rutt, "**and the column says four coppers, and the bottom says that is the whole of what you pay, and the bottom is printed in the same type as the price, and I am obliged to read it out twice, and I have read it out twice.**"

"That is the whole of it."

"You are going to ask me what the toll is and I am going to tell you what I know, which is that the word toll is a word this district has been using for five weeks for two different things, and I can read you a claim and I cannot read you an absence, and that is a thing I have said out loud at this counter and it is in a public book and it is my own fault."

"I am not asking you what the toll is," said Halloway Reed. "**I am asking you to read it again, because I printed it and I have never heard anybody read it in the voice it is supposed to be read in.**"

So she read it again, and about eleven people heard it, and then he asked her the thing he came for, and it took him about ninety seconds and she answered it in about nine words and it is the finding of the morning and it is in the ward market's file because a freight clerk was in the queue and heard it.

"You are the reader at the counter," he said. "**Four days a week. Eleven coppers a quarter. And on the thirteenth of this month a woman of about sixty with a stick asked you whether my sheet was true, and you told her it was not true and that it said so on the front, and then she said that a man who has to print a lie in the same type as the truth is a man who does not think he is telling it, and that she had been trying to work out what he was for and could not.**

"**I have thought about that every day for thirteen days.** And I have printed since, and I have printed a fare, and I have printed the bottom of it, and I want to tell you the trade I am in and I want to tell you the only part of it that matters."

---

**And he said it standing at a ward market counter at about twenty to seven in the morning on the twenty-sixth of this month to about eleven people, and Rutt wrote all of it down on her own working page and gave it to Nessa Pell the same morning, and it is in the ward market's file, and it is the best argument anybody has made against the public book in six weeks.**

"I am not in the business of fooling people," he said. "**There is no business in that. I have tried it and I will tell you the two things I know about it, and one of them is that it does not work on anybody who has been sold something before, and the other one is that the second time you are sold something you check it, and the third time you do not bother, and by the fourth time the man selling is part of the street.**

"That is the whole of it. There is nothing clever about a lie. **A lie only works while it is untidy, and the moment somebody writes your lie down properly it stops working on anybody who can read, and starts working on everybody else, which is worse, and which is why the tidiest sheet in this district is the most dangerous thing in it.**

"So I print it. Four coppers, a time, a place, a claim, and the truth at the bottom. **And the reason I print the truth at the bottom is that the person who buys it can then do the one thing that has never once failed anybody on this line, which is check.**"

"And the fare is four coppers and that is the whole of it," said Rutt.

"**Yes.**"

"**And it is the whole of it,**" said Rutt, "**and I want to say why it is the whole of it, and I am a signal clerk and not a reader and I was a clerk at the signal cabin for eleven years before this shoulder, and there is a man in Alder Reach who read a piece of cast iron on the eleventh of this month for an hour and got five lines out of it, and one of the five lines is that the passengers pay nothing.**

"**Nothing. He has got that in a public book with a sleeve number on it and two people compared the hand. And the standing line says the only carriage is a person carried as a person. And I have read both of them at this counter twice in the last fortnight, and you have read both of them as well, because you are a better printer than anybody in this city and you read the same paper everybody else reads.**

"**So your fare is right. That is the difficulty and I am going to say it slowly.**"

---

**And Ivo Stenn was fourth in the queue and he had been listening for about six minutes, and he came forward, and he did not say anything for about eleven seconds, and then he said the thing that is the whole of the twenty-sixth of this month and that nobody in Alder Reach has been able to put down since.**

"**Two hundred and six places at four coppers on the twenty-first, in about forty minutes, on a tram arch, in front of a yard. That is eight hundred and twenty-four coppers.**"

"That is right."

"**The whole of what this district will spend on the Needle line for a year is sixty coppers.**" He had the rate sheet out of his coat and he did not read it and he did not need to. "Nine of them a quarter for a lamp and a bushel of chalk. Six of them a quarter for printed matter that has not been printed. The rest is not on the line."

"Then I have made more in forty minutes than the ward market has spent on that railway since the flood year."

"**You have, and I have written it in my own book and I want the number of it in a column of its own, and the column is going to be headed *what the counterfeit got there first with*, and I have wanted that column since the eighth of this month, and you have just put the figure in it, and I am not going to thank you for it because I have not got a way of doing that which is not a lie.**"

"**Then do not thank me,**" said Halloway Reed. "**I have never wanted to be thanked. I want to be accurate, and being accurate is not the same as being right, and I want those two words separated in whatever book you keep, because a man who is accurate and wrong is a much more useful thing to a city than a man who is vague and right, and make sure the record has got that.**"

And Stenn said, "**Say the rest of it.**"

"**The rest of it is the fare.**"

---

**And he said the rest of it, and it took about four minutes, and Rutt read it out afterwards to anybody who wanted it and about forty people wanted it over the following two days, and it is in the ward market's file, and Ivo Stenn has entered in his own hand that it is the fourth time in this month that a man has been right in front of him and that he has had nothing to say back.**

"**I did not print four coppers because I thought four coppers was the price. I printed four coppers because a fare that is complete is worth more to the person paying it than a fare that is a question.**

"Here is the whole trade and it is three sentences. A man who buys a question knows he is buying a question, and he keeps it, and he asks about it in a week. A man who buys a complete fare takes it home and puts it on a table and tells his household that the thing is settled, **and a household that has been told a thing is settled stops asking, and a household that stops asking is the only reason anybody ever gets anything done anywhere in this basin.**

"So a complete fare is a service and a question is a nuisance, and I sell the service because the service is what people are actually buying, and I do it in the same type as the truth because the second time somebody checks me is the day I lose, and I would rather lose on the second time than be right on the first."

Somebody at the back said, "**And the second part.**"

"**The second part is this.**" He put two fingers on the bottom of the sheet. "**I have printed that there is no such timetable, in the same type, twice, in front of a yard of four hundred people, and it has cost me about two hundred places that I could have sold by printing the words nobody wanted. I have given that up. And I did it because on the twelfth of this month a reader read my sheet out in a yard and said that a sheet is honest on its face if it says on its face that it is not true, and I have been arguing with that for thirteen days and I have lost.

"**Your book is going to find out that the other half of that four coppers exists. It is not going to be this month and it is not going to be by anybody arguing with me, because I have printed the toll in its own column and the column says the price is the whole of the price, and I printed that out of the public book, from page eleven, in the correct type, correctly, and honestly, and I was right.**

"And the day you find the other half, the sheet I have printed this month is the only document in this city that said the price was four coppers, **and that is a better thing to be than a man who printed a question.**"

---

**And Rutt said the thing that is the finding of the twenty-sixth and it is in the ward market's file, and she said it to him and not to the room, and she said it with her shoulder against the counter because that is how she stands.**

"**Mr. Reed. I have a thing in a public book in this city with my name against it, and the thing is that I can read a claim and I cannot read an absence, and it is the third of five instruments this district does not have, and it is in a debt list, and I am on it because somebody asked me on the twenty-first whether I would take it on and I said yes and nobody told me I was being asked.**

"**And I am going to tell you the half of it that is mine, and it is the half that is going to lose you, and you should have it from me and not from a book.**

"**The thing I cannot do is say what is not going to happen. And a fare that is complete is a sentence about what is not going to happen. You have printed about four hundred of them. And I cannot read you one of them out loud, and I have been asked about it, and I have had a woman of sixty with a stick ask me to, and I have not been able to.**

"**So I do not know whether you are a man who is right about fares and wrong about this line, or a man who has found a hole in a public book and printed over it. And I do not know which, and it is going to matter, and I am telling you that I do not know.**"

"That," said Halloway Reed, "**is the only thing anybody has said to me in six weeks that I am going to remember, and print it somewhere, and print it without my name on it, because a man who has a sentence like that about him wants it kept.**

"**And Mrs Rutt, the woman with the stick bought one on the twenty-first and came back this morning and stood where I am standing. She is outside. She has been outside since a quarter past six.**"

Rutt looked at the door.

"Then read her sheet out again," she said, "**in the words she did not bring, and ask her what she came for, and write down what she says, because that is the only thing this counter has ever been any use for and I have been doing it in the wrong order.**"

And Rutt read it out again, and asked the woman what she came for, and the woman said she had come to ask whether the four coppers was the whole of it, and Rutt said she could not read her an absence, and the woman said she had been told that on the thirteenth and had walked nine miles since to find somebody who could say it, and Rutt said no she could not, and the woman said then she would wait, and **and Rutt wrote that down in the counter's own working page, which is not a public record, and it is the only line on that page that is not about a person.**

> *One asked for the whole of the price. I cannot read an absence. She is not angry. She has walked nine miles to be told no by somebody who is paid eleven coppers a quarter to say yes, and she said she would wait, and I have entered that she said it, and the entry is on a page that is not a public record because I am not allowed to make one of this, and that is the fourth time in a month that the shape of the room has been the problem and not the words.*

---

**And on the twenty-seventh of this month the ward market spent its six coppers of printed matter before the light went, and found out what a sheet costs, and the finding is on the printer's own invoice and is unpaid and not as nil.**

There is one independent printer left in Alder Reach and he is about seventy and he has set type by hand since before the water came, and he quoted nine hundred coppers for a run of five hundred on the fourteenth of this month, and nine hundred coppers is seventy-five times the whole of what the market has voted for printed matter in two quarters, and **Ivo Stenn went to him on the morning of the twenty-seventh with six coppers in his hand and came back at about half past four in the afternoon with five hundred sheets and a figure on a piece of paper.**

> **NINE HUNDRED AND SIX COPPERS, WHICH IS FOUR FOR THE STOCK AND TWO FOR THE SETTING AND NINE HUNDRED QUOTED ON THE FOURTEENTH, PAYABLE BEFORE THE RUN. SIX COPPERS A QUARTER, VOTED THE TWENTY-FIFTH, EQUALS TWELVE COPPERS FOR TWO QUARTERS. THE GAP IS EIGHT HUNDRED AND NINETY-FOUR COPPERS AND IT IS ENTERED AS UNPAID AND NOT AS NIL, BECAUSE A NIL WOULD BE A NUMBER THE PRINTER CHOSE, AND HE DID NOT CHOOSE IT, HE QUOTED IT.**

He took it on a trade. That is a thing printers do and it is not charity; a man who prints on a trade puts the trade's name on the sheet and the trade has told everybody it is a sponsor, and the printer is owed and the debt is somebody else's for about a year. **The trade that took it was the chandlers, and Hessa Krell put her stall's name at the bottom of it in type the size of the date, and she told the printer and Stenn and about nine other people in the same breath that she was doing it because this market has printed nothing since the flood year and the first thing it printed should not have somebody else's stall on the bottom of it, and nobody believed her, and the entry says the reason was entered by the person it belongs to.**

And this is the sheet.

> **THE WEST END WILL BE WALKED ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF THIS MONTH. FROM THE FOURTH PLACE TO THE NINTH, ON THE SIX-FOOT, ON THE SHOULDER, AND ON THE CESS WHERE THE SIX-FOOT IS GONE. FOUR PEOPLE GO. THE WALK IS FREE. NOBODY IS ASKED FOR ANY MONEY AND NOBODY WILL BE ASKED FOR ANY MONEY AND IF ANYBODY IS ASKED FOR ANY MONEY THEY ARE NOT THIS MARKET.**
>
> **AND AT THE BOTTOM, IN THE SAME TYPE, THE SENTENCE THAT WAS ON THE FIRST SHEET AND IS THE ONLY REASON ANY OF IT IS HAPPENING: THIS IS A DATE A PERSON COULD BE WRONG ABOUT.**

It went up on eleven boards and into the file and it was read out at the ward market counter at six the next morning by a woman who reads four days a week at eleven coppers a quarter, and about two hundred and eighty people heard it in two days, and **it is the second thing in this district that a stranger can take cold and use. The first one was a public lot book. The second one is a single sheet of paper with a chandler's stall on the bottom of it and eight hundred and ninety-four coppers of unpaid printing on the invoice behind it.**

---

**And Halloway Reed printed a second one on the same morning and put it up at four coppers, and it is the same walk and the same four names and the same free, and the difference between the two sheets is that his has a place on it to stand.**

He put it up himself at about eleven, four hours before the market's run came off the press, and he was still on the arch when Stenn came past at about noon, and for about four hours the only two sheets about that walk in this city were one printed by a man selling something and one printed by nobody at all, and the district's was not on the wall yet and was not on the arch and was in a printer's shop being set by hand, and the two sheets were forty feet apart in the same street, and Stenn stood in the middle of them for about four minutes and then said the thing that is the second entry in a column he has started keeping and has told exactly one other person about, and that person is a cook who cannot read.

"**He has beaten this market by a day and a half for six weeks and this morning he beat it by four hours, and neither of those two facts has anything to do with the public interest.**"

"Are you going to say it is a scandal?"

"**I am going to say that the column is going to have two entries in it by the end of this week and that both of them are the same entry, and that a man who is a day and a half ahead for six weeks and four hours ahead on the day is telling us something about what a day and a half is worth. The answer is that a day and a half was worth two hundred places at four coppers and a day is worth a chandler's name on the bottom of a sheet, and I do not know what to do with that, and I have been a freight clerk for six years and I have always known what to do with a number.**"

"And the four coppers?"

"**The four coppers is a place to stand in a yard,**" said Stenn, "**and he told that to about two hundred people on the twenty-first in the only words available to him, which were *standing room only, in weather*, in the same type as the price. He has been printing the truth on the front since the twelfth and he has also been printing the price of a thing he is not selling, and both of those are honest, and a man can be honest in six directions at once and it is not a contradiction. It is a profession.**"

Then on the twenty-eighth, at about one, when the market's five hundred sheets were a day old and on eleven boards, the printer's boy came past with a bundle of them under his arm, and stopped at the arch, and looked at the two of them for a moment, and Halloway Reed gave him one, and the boy gave it back, and he said the only sentence anybody said that week about either sheet.

"**I have been asked for four hundred of his and none of yours, and I have carried both of them up and down this street all week, and I would like it entered that both of those facts are about me and neither of them is about the walk.**"
