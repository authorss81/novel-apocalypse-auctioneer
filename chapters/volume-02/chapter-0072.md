# Chapter 72: A Hut Four

At about seven in the morning on the seventh of this month a boy of nineteen was woken up on a bench in a signal hut at the fourth place by a man holding a lamp, and there was a cast-iron interlocking tongue lying along the length of the bench beside his head, about the length of a forearm and four pounds of it, one end cut to a wedge worn to a shine and a red signal-wire loop through a hole at the other, and it had not been there when he went to sleep.

The rain had come through the roof of that hut in four places for as long as anybody could remember and it had come through two of them all night. There is a stove in the corner of it with a door that shuts and a chimney that draws, and there is a bench bolted to the floor along the back wall, and there is a lamp on a post outside the door with a nameplate under it that says **HARROW**, and the nameplate is the thing and not the place, and there has been no keeper in that hut since the flood year because there is nothing coming from that end.

The boy on the bench is called Nevin Roke and he is nineteen and he is not on a roll in any book in Alder Reach and has not slept anywhere else since the flood year, and there is nobody in this district who knew that until the eighth of this month.

He was not a signal hand. He has never held a pair. He did not know what the thing on the bench was and he still does not.

---

The three people who came up that morning left Alder Reach at about half past four and got to the fourth place at about ten past seven, and it is four hours on foot with four changeovers in it and they did all four, and it took them five and a quarter because they were carrying a lamp and Rutt could not manage the step down of two feet six inches at the eighth with a lamp in her hand.

The man from the water office was Petrus Nye, thirty-four, a bridge clerk of six years who has never been west of the second place in his life and who had been at the fourth place once, on a bicycle, in the flood year, to look at a nameplate.

He was sent by Selik Marne and the sending is in the water office's own book in two lines: **TO THE FOURTH PLACE ON THE NEEDLE TO LOOK AT THE SIGNAL HUT AND REPORT. NOT TO STAY. NOT TO OPEN ANYTHING.**

Rutt came because a reader reads things and because the finding about a slot in the unclosable draft was hers and because she is on four days and the fourth of them was that day. Orren Pike came because he is paid four coppers a quarter to know where every object on this line is, and if a thing he has indexed four hundred and eleven movements of is not where he has it written down, then the index is wrong, and he would rather be the one who found that out.

The tenth hand came because the fourth place is on a curve and the ballast on the inside of the curve was up to his chest when he put a tape in it on the ninth of last month.

**They found the boy and the iron and about four other things in about eleven minutes, and the order they found them in is the order they are in.**

Nye put the lamp on the bench and the boy woke up and said something that nobody has repeated, and then the boy said the next thing, which was that the iron had been on the bench when he came in off the bank on the night before last and he had put it inside his coat because the roof was going and it was cold and it had been in the hut when he got back from the water office at about nine.

"And where did you get it," said the tenth hand.

"**Off the six-foot at the ninth place, about eleven yards, by the tap, on the ballast, in the rain, at about half past eight, and it was lying on the ballast in the water and I thought it was a bar and I picked it up and it wasn't a bar.**"

"And you brought it up the bank."

"**I brought it up the bank because I'm here and there's nobody here, and I have been here since the water came and nobody has ever come up this bank except the haulier and a man with a meter and you four, and I did not think anybody was going to come up this bank ever again, so I thought I'd bring it up here where it was dry.**"

Rutt asked the question that got the whole chapter, and she asked it in the ordinary voice she uses at a counter, and it took her about four seconds.

"**Did you tell anybody?**"

"No."

"**Why not.**"

Nevin Roke looked at the floor of the hut for a while.

"**Because there's nobody to tell.**" He said it without any weight on it at all, which is what made it go round the room. "**I've been in this hut four years and nobody has ever asked me one thing. I don't know the water office's name and I don't know the Road House's name and I don't know if there's a book anywhere with my name in it and there isn't, because I checked, because I went down to the yard once in the flood year with a list of people in the shelter and nobody could tell me where the shelter was and I never went back.**"

"And you carried a lot eleven yards," said the tenth hand, "**and slept beside it, and it rained on you all night, and nobody in this district has any instrument for any of that, and you are not in a book and you could not be made into a bearer if we wanted one because a bearer is a person who can hold a signal and you have never held anything in your life except that.**"

"**I can hold that,**" said the boy.

"**I know you can,**" said the tenth hand. "**That is precisely the problem and it is not yours.**"

---

**And they did the thing that this district does, which is to write down what they can stand behind, and about four minutes of that is the whole of the fourteenth part of the results and the rest of it is what the book could not say.**

Orren Pike got the first entry into the copy at about twenty past seven and then stopped with the pen above the page, and the reason he stopped is the most useful thing anybody in Alder Reach did all morning.

"**I can't put the date on it,**" he said. "**I want to write the sixth of April because that's when it turned up on the bench. But she says it was on the ballast at half past eight on the sixth, and if I put the sixth then it looks like it was there on the sixth and it was, and if I put the seventh then it looks like nobody knows when it came and nobody does.**"

"**Then put both,**" said Rutt. "**Put the sixth and put the seventh and put what is between them, which is a night, and put *found on the seventh, taken on the sixth by a person who is not on a roll, and the person is not struck and is not asked to be a bearer and is not a witness and is not entered as anything.***"

Nobody argued with that, and it went in as Rutt said it, and the finding underneath is the district's, and it is not a mystery and the minute says so in the same line: **this is not a thing that walked. This is a thing a person carried eleven yards in the rain because he was cold and there was nowhere else to take it. The district has eleven instruments for a train being turned and it has never had one for a boy in a hut, and the reason it has never had one is that there has never been a boy in a hut that anybody knew about until the eighth of this month.**

And Petrus Nye wrote something into the water office's own book on the way back, in his own hand, and it is in the book because the water office's book is copied twice a quarter and the copies go to a market that reads them.

> **THE TONGUE WAS IN THE HUT AT THE FOURTH PLACE, ON A BENCH, AT SEVEN IN THE MORNING ON THE SEVENTH OF THIS MONTH, AND IT HAD BEEN TAKEN FROM THE BALLAST AT THE NINTH PLACE ON THE EVENING OF THE SIXTH BY A BOY OF NINETEEN WHO SLEEPS IN THAT HUT AND IS NOT ON A ROLL. THE NINTH PLACE IS FOUR HOURS ON FOOT WITH FOUR CHANGEOVERS IN IT AND THE OBJECT IS FORTY POUNDS HEAVIER THAN NOTHING.**
>
> **THE BRIDGE OFFICE HAS NO USE FOR THIS INFORMATION. I WAS SENT TO LOOK AT A HUT AND NOT TO STAY. I AM WRITING IT DOWN BECAUSE A BOY CARRIED SOMETHING HE DID NOT UNDERSTAND ELEVEN YARDS AND WAS ASLEEP BESIDE IT AND WAS NOT TOLD ANYTHING BY ANYBODY, AND BECAUSE THE BOOK I WRITE IN IS COPIED TWICE A QUARTER AND GOES WHERE HE IS NOT.**

---

**And then four people stood on the six-foot at the fourth place for about half an hour and worked out what the line had lost, and the answer took about four minutes to say and took the rest of the morning to be believed.**

The Harrow Tongue is the only thing on the near section that throws the points. It was taken out of the flooded Harrow interchange in the fourth year and it has been in use thirteen years, five of them in a frame at the ninth place and eight of them in eleven huts, three sheds and a box in a wagon. It is the only iron on this line that can turn a train.

"There is no frame at the fourth place," said the tenth hand. "**There is a lamp and a plate and a bench and a boy. There is no frame and there never was one, and the ninth place is the only frame on this line with a bracket in it tall enough for that iron, and I know that because I put the iron in it five times.**"

"So the only thing on this railway that can turn a train is in a hut that cannot turn a train," said Orren Pike.

"**Yes.**

"**And the train is on a siding a mile and three quarters from here with no iron in the frame, and it cannot go east, and it cannot go west, and it has stood fifty-five days.**"

"And the boundary," said Rutt.

They all stopped for that one, because the boundary is the only thing this district has built in six weeks and it is a line of chalk about five feet across the six-foot and eleven feet up the shoulder, sixty yards west of the column, and it is held by two named people, and the word is nine words long and it means that whoever holds the thing that day has to stop and wait.

"**The word is about a train,**" said the tenth hand. "**That is what the woman wrote when she wrote it and the book has it in her words: whoever holds the thing that day has to stop and wait. There is no thing. She held the thing. She does not hold it now. The word is still perfectly good and it is not attached to anything at all.**"

"So what does it do."

"**It is stopping people,**" said Rutt. "**And I want that written down as what it does and not as what it was for.**"

And Nevin Roke, from the doorway of the hut, in a voice nobody had been listening for until then: "**I've been up here four years and I have never had anything to stop.**"

---

**And the woman at the column, who is the water, said the thing that made the batch's problem, and she said it to four people in a doorway on the eighth of this month and it is in the book in her words, and it is the first thing she has said in nine days, and she had not been asked.**

"**You are all standing in my hut talking about a lever,**" she said. "**So I will tell you what your word has been doing since the Friday.

"**It has been said five times. It stopped the haulier twice, because he was on the line with a barrow and he put the barrow down on the ballast and stood on the bank for about a minute and a half and then went up, and he was not angry, and he has come up the bank since. It stopped a man with a barrow and a dog on the Saturday, and the dog did not understand it at all. And it stopped a boy with a handcart on the Sunday, and that one is a boy of about eleven and he cried, and his mother took him home.**"

"Then it is doing something," said the tenth hand.

"**It is doing the only thing it can do, which is stopping people, and it is not a railway instrument and it has not been a railway instrument since Friday morning, and none of the three of you noticed, because all three of you were in this room looking for a lever.**"

Nobody argued with her. **And the finding the district published about it two days later is in her words and it is not softened, and the part that matters is the part where she says that a word which was written about a train has quietly become a word about people, and that the two women who hold it have not been asked about that and are not going to be asked about that, because a word is a word and it does not need its authors to agree with it any more.**

> **THE WORD WAS WRITTEN ABOUT A TRAIN AND IT IS NOW A WORD ABOUT PEOPLE. IT IS STILL NINE WORDS AND IT STILL WORKS AND IT WAS WRITTEN BY A PERSON WHO HAS NOT BEEN ASKED ABOUT THIS AND WILL NOT BE. A WORD DOES NOT NEED ITS AUTHORS TO AGREE WITH IT. NOTHING ELSE IN THIS DISTRICT'S PUBLIC RECORD IS TRUE OF ANYTHING ELSE, AND THIS IS THE FIRST ENTRY THAT SAYS SO, AND I HAVE SAID IT AND I AM THE WATER AND NOT A CLERK.**

---

They went back up the bank on the tenth of this month, in the rain again, because a bracket with nothing in it is not a thing anybody can be shown in a yard, and there are about four hundred people in Alder Reach who have never seen the ninth place and would not have believed a word about it.

The frame is against the north wall of the hut with the bench and it is a cast frame about as tall as a man's chest with two levers in it and a quadrant at the top with notches cut in it, and the bracket is at shoulder height, and the bracket is empty, and there is a clean pale rectangle in the grease and the rust where a thing four inches wide stood against the frame for five years.

Sef Anyard stood in front of it for about a minute and a half without touching it and then she put two fingers on the pale rectangle and took them off again.

"**Five years,**" she said. "**Thirteen years in use and five of them in here and I put it in myself the first week I had this hut after the last man left, and I did it wrong the first time and had to lift it out and do it again because I had it the wrong way round for the wedge, and there is a mark on the frame where the corner of it caught on the casting, and it is about the size of a thumbnail, and it is still there and nobody has looked at it in five years.**"

"And now somebody has."

"**Now somebody has, and there is nothing in the bracket, and I would like it entered that the two of us stood in this room on the tenth of this month and looked at an empty bracket and neither of us said anything for about a minute and a half, and that the minute and a half is in the book, because I have been in this hut for eleven years and a thing has not been in that bracket since the Friday and I have not slept properly since the Friday, and the reason I have not slept is that I keep getting up and looking at it, and that is what a thing you are responsible for does to you, and I want it written down as a cost and not as a symptom.**"

That went in the book in her words, and it is the first entry anybody at Lot 17 has ever entered at the request of the person it is about, and Nessa Pell wrote a note under it saying that she had been asked whether she wished it entered, and that she had said yes, and that the asking had an hour on it.

And the fourth thing on that morning, which nobody had asked for and which Petrus Nye put in the water office's book without being asked and which is two lines long, is that the bridge office has an empty room over the counting office on the second floor, with a bed in it and a window that shuts and a stove that wants cleaning, and it has been empty for two years because the office has had nobody to put in it, and that he knows it is there because he has the keys, and that he wrote it down because a boy of nineteen slept on a bench in a hut four hours away with rain coming through two holes in a roof.

He did not say anything about it to anybody in this district and it was in the water office's book and the water office's book is copied twice a quarter and the copies go to a market that reads them, and the second copy of that book is made in about three weeks.

---

**And on the eleventh of this month the board published the fourteenth part of its results, all four seats named aloud, in daylight, and it is the shortest one since the twelfth and it is the first one in this district's history that says out loud that it cannot count a thing.**

> **14. THE HARROW TONGUE IS NOT AT THE NINTH PLACE. IT WAS AT THE FOURTH PLACE ON THE SEVENTH OF THIS MONTH AND IT WAS TAKEN FROM THE BALLAST AT THE NINTH PLACE ON THE SIXTH BY A PERSON OF NINETEEN WHO IS NOT ON A ROLL AND IS ENTERED AS NOBODY.**
>
> **THE TWO LEVERS AND THE FRAME ARE AT THE NINTH PLACE AND THE BRACKET IS EMPTY AND THE MARK ON THE FLOOR OF THE HUT IS NOT SCRAPED, BECAUSE A MARK IS NOT STRUCK.**
>
> **AND THE OBJECT IS THE ONLY IRON ON THIS LINE THAT CAN TURN A TRAIN, AND THERE IS NO FRAME AT THE FOURTH PLACE, AND THE NINTH PLACE IS THE ONLY FRAME ON THE LINE WITH A BRACKET IN IT THAT THE IRON FITS, AND THEREFORE THE ONLY PLACE ON THE LINE THAT CAN TURN A TRAIN IS A PLACE THAT DOES NOT HAVE THE THING IN IT.**
>
> **AND THE WEST END OF THIS LINE IS NOT A PLACE ANY MORE. IT WAS ONE PLACE WITH A FIGURE IN THE BOOK AGAINST IT AND IT IS NOW AN ORDER, AND THE ORDER AS FAR AS ANYBODY KNOWS IT IS THE NINTH PLACE AND THE FOURTH PLACE, AND THERE ARE TEN OTHER PLACES ON THIS LINE THAT NOBODY HAS STOOD IN, AND THREE OF THE TEN HAVE HUTS IN THEM THAT ARE IDENTICAL TO EACH OTHER, AND A LINE IS AN ORDER AND NOT A LENGTH, AND A DISTRICT THAT CAN ONLY COUNT THINGS IS GOING TO FIND OUT ABOUT THAT IN ABOUT A WEEK AND THE FINDING WILL NOT BE A FIGURE.**
>
> **IT CONFERS NO USE. IT AUTHORISES NOBODY TO MOVE ANYTHING. AND IT SEATS NOTHING. AND THE BOARD HAS NO POWER TO MOVE A LOT AND NO POWER TO FIND ONE AND THIS IS THE SAME GAP AS THE FIFTH THING AND IT IS DATED.**

And at about half past four that afternoon a man of thirty-four from the bridge office stood at the rail at the fourth place with a lamp and a book and read all of it out in the ordinary way to about nine people, and then he said the thing that is in his own book and that the board asked him for and that took about four minutes.

"**I have been in this hut since eight o'clock this morning and I have written down one thing and I have been asked for a second thing and I am going to say the second thing and then I am going to go back to a bridge.

"**There is no keeper in this hut and there has not been one since the flood year and the reason there has not been one is that there is nothing coming from this end, and that is written on the flood sheet and it is correct. And I have been in this hut for eight hours today and there is a boy asleep in it, and that is not on the flood sheet, and he is nineteen, and he was here before the water came, and the reason he is here is that the hut is dry and nobody has ever told him he is allowed to be.

"**So I have two things to say and they are both about a hut and neither of them is about a lever.

"**One: a hut with a door and a stove and a chimney that draws is a place a person can be, and this district has one of them that nobody has counted, and the man who found him is the man who is paid to look at nameplates.

"**Two: the reason this line has gone wrong in the last nine years is not a stall and it is not a Fault and it is not the ground. **It is that nobody was ever asked to be anywhere on it.** And I am a bridge clerk, and I have been paid to be a careful man about one river for six years, and the safest thing I have ever done in this job was write down the day the water came. And I am not going to stand in a hut and pretend that I am the answer to that, because I am not, and I am going back to the water office at seven o'clock and I would like the book to say that I asked to go back and was not refused.**"

Nessa Pell entered all of it, including that he asked, and the finding under it is that the first man this district has sent to look at a railway in nine years asked to go back within eight hours, and that this is not entered as a complaint and is entered as a fact about a man and not about a bridge office.
