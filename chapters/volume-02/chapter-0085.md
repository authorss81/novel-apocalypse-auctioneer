# Chapter 85: Thirty-Six Coppers

On the eighteenth of this month a bushel of white paint came up the bank on a handcart with two men pushing it, and it had been standing behind a cooper's shop in Alder Reach for eleven days with the lid on and the date of purchase written on the lid in a tradesman's hand, and the reason nobody had gone and looked for it is that the district spent a fortnight in April arguing about a column printed at full width with nothing in it.

It cost four coppers. The standing failure that the line of chalk cannot be seen from the west end of the siding, which is the end it is for, is entered as fixed on the eighteenth of this month, and the fixing is one bushel of white paint and about a hundred and forty yards of it, and the fourteen pints the rate bought in April are in the entry and are not struck.

Somebody asked a chandler. That is the whole of the finding and it is the fourth time this district has had it.

The two men who pushed the cart up were the lamp-man of forty-one and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a counter of oil, and they took five and a quarter hours with four changeovers in it, and the man of thirty-four came back down the same day because he had a stall and lost a day of it, and nobody paid either of them, and the entry says that the paint was bought out of the two hundred coppers the ninth place paid on the seventh, and that of the two hundred, four coppers have now been spent on a sign and eleven on a bound copy of a line book and four on a bushel of white paint.

---

**And a security is not a promise and it is not a price and it is not a held asset, and the definition that is now on the sheet in four hands is the only definition of a security anybody in this district has ever worked with, and it is nine words long.**

A security is a thing somebody else can go and look at.

The four-bearer draft in the public lot book has carried the words *security none offered and none posted* since the second of April, and the words have been true for seven weeks, and the reason they have been true is that the only man in this district who could have posted one is a reader, and a reader may not.

"I cannot post a security," said Adrian Vale, in about four seconds, on the evening of the seventeenth, in the counting room, in front of about thirty people, and he had said it in March and in April and the room did not stop him, and the minute entered it as the fourth time and entered the count.

"Then somebody else has to," said the mechanic. "And I would like to be that somebody and I am not going to pretend I have not thought about it for three days, and I have thought about it in a way I am not proud of, and I am going to describe the way so that the room can refuse it."

He said that the thing he had been thinking about was that a security on a railway is worth what a railway can be made to cost, and that the west end of this railway can be made to cost a great deal, and that a man with a yard and a stock and eleven vehicles a week and nowhere to put them has a figure in him that would frighten anybody, and that a figure like that in a public book is a weapon and not a security, and that he had worked that out on the third day and had not slept on the fourth.

"That is the correct answer," said the woman of forty-four, who keeps a Road House on the tide, "and I arrived at it in about four seconds and I have been insulting you for three days for taking three."

---

And four things were posted in about two hours on the evening of the seventeenth, and three of them are money and one of them is ground, and the finding is that this is the first week in nine years in which anything at the west end of that railway has been put up by a person who was put up by this district.

The Needle Road House posts the standing salvage: **four hundred and ten coppers worth it at Alder Reach and nothing at the ninth place.** It is his own figure, entered at his own request in April by a foreman of twenty-two years who found out about a lease in a room with a fire in it, and he posted it in about eleven words, and the words were *it is already in a book and I would like it to be in two*.

"You cannot inspect that," said somebody. "It is salvage on a bank four hours from here."

"Then a man comes up that bank and looks at it," he said, "and I will pay for the cart, and if it is not there I will put it back out of my own stock before he gets home, and that is what a thing somebody else can go and look at means. **The inspection is not the looking. The inspection is that a man can come.**"

The ward market posts **eleven coppers a quarter** — the bench, the lamp, the chalk and the oil at the ninth place. It is the figure a river office has been paying out of its Harrow works rate for one month, and the market can vote on it and the office cannot be asked, and the entry says the eleven coppers is a service and **a rate buys a service and does not buy a seat, and the man who sleeps on that bench is still not paid and that is still true on the day the market voted it.**

The ninth place posts the ground: the siding, the column, the hut and the ground between them, which a lease named a firm and which was surrendered on the thirtieth of March and has stood on the rate sheet since as **HELD BY NOBODY**, and is now posted by two named people and is valued by nobody at nothing.

And the fourth is thirty-six coppers, and it is the smallest and the strangest, and the room argued about it longest.

---

---

**And before any of the four were posted, the four people who were going to post them were asked in the method on the wall, in the room, in about twenty minutes, and the reason that is in the minute is that it is the first time in nine weeks the method has been used on people who are all in one room and all know each other's answers.**

It is what the job is, in sentences somebody who has never done it can understand. You put a thing in a public book that a stranger can walk to and look at, and if the thing is gone or worth nothing, the people who put it there are answerable to the people who can look. It is not a promise. It does not cost you anything today. It costs you the thing, on the day anybody finds out.

It is how long, with an hour on it. A month, and a month again after that, and no end of it yet, and the person it is said to has not been asked how long and is asked in the fourth thing.

It is how you say no. Two of the four said no inside eleven minutes and neither of them gave a reason, and the reasons are in the book anyway because the two of them gave them afterwards in the ordinary way in a room with thirty people in it, and the minute enters that the rule and the practice are not the same thing and that this district has not found out which one is the rule.

It is who answers for you while you are saying it, and the mechanic answered it for the man of the counter of oil before anybody asked, and the answer was the name of a woman with a hook who buys old paper once a month, and the room wrote it down and she was not in the room.

It is where you stand when you answer. In front of the table, in daylight, where thirty people can see you, and the woman of the column said the fifth thing before it was asked and said that she had been standing in a doorway for eleven years and she was not going to stand anywhere else to say a yes to a man from Alder Reach, and that is in the book in her words and it is the same answer she gave on the twenty-ninth of April.

---

And the fourth thing on that wall has now been answered correctly four times in nine weeks by four different people and in one of those four times by a person who is a Room House and owes nothing to anybody in the room, and the finding entered on the nineteenth of this month is that **the fourth question is the one this district has been getting free, and it is the only one that cannot be asked by a board, and it is now the second thing on the minute of everything that matters.**

---


**And the thirty-six coppers are water, and they have been owed to nobody for nine years, and they are now posted by the people they are owed to, to nobody, and the finding is that this is the only security in the district that cannot be taken away because there is nobody entitled to take it.**

Four coppers a quarter, thirty-six quarterly readings, thirty-six coppers in nine years, metered at the column to a firm that gave up a lease, handed over unasked by a mechanic with the sentence *it is on an invoice and I pay invoices*, and the column is now a column and not a meter and the meter is a meter that ran for nine years and is nobody's and the thirty-six coppers are owed to nobody.

Sef Anyard put the third one in the book and the woman of the column put the fourth one in, and neither of them would say the number out loud until the number was on the paper.

"Thirty-six coppers in nine years," said the woman of the column. "That is four a quarter. That is what a firm paid me for water out of my own column for nine years and then stopped on the thirtieth of March, and this market has charged the same four coppers for eleven years for a sheet it never printed, and I am not going to be the woman who says the two are the same, because they are not the same, and one of them is a rate and one of them is a bill.

"So the thirty-six goes in as what it is. It is money nobody owes anybody. And we are posting it against a railway, and the clerk can write in the minute that a sum of money owed to no person by no firm is not a security and cannot be one, and the reason we are posting it anyway is that a security is a thing somebody else can go and look at, and anybody can go and look at the meter book, and it has been on the counter in Alder Reach since the twenty-third of March and it is in this room in a bag."

And the minute says exactly that, and says the third thing, which the woman of the column said out loud before she would let it be written: **and if anybody in this district ever finds out who was paying for that water, I want it back off this sheet in a week, and I do not care whose name it is.**

"**Then it is not a security, it is a hostage,**" said the mechanic.

"Then call it a hostage and put it in," she said. "I have watched three men in this district put a figure in a book to frighten somebody and I have watched what it did to them and I would rather put a figure in that nobody can cash. **A hostage can be taken off you. Thirty-six coppers that nobody owns cannot be taken off anybody, and if that is not a security then this district has not got a word for what it is.**"

And it went in as a security with the word *hostage* written after it in brackets, and the bracket is in the book, and the finding is that **this district now has a security that cannot be taken and a book that has admitted in print that it does not know what to call it.**

---

And a woman of forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide refused to post anything, and gave a reason that has been entered, and then gave the room something else that is not a security and is entered as not one.

"No," said Ottoline Merrick. "And I am not going to give you a figure and I am not going to give you a thing, because the day a Road House posts a security on a line is the day every haulier who comes through my yard is told what that line costs, and he is told it by me, because I am the door, and I have spent nineteen years being a door that does not cost anything.

"So you can have my yard, once a month, for as long as this is in being. That is all. It is not money and it is not stock and it is not standing, and I am not offering it as a security, and I would like the minute to say that I offered it as not one, because in about four years somebody is going to write that the Tidefall Road House stood behind a public charter, and it did not, and it lent a yard."

"A yard is not a security," said the mechanic.

"A yard is not a security and cannot be inspected and cannot be taken and is not money," she said, "and the thing about it is that a rehearsal happens in a yard, and a rehearsal is a walk done before the day it is for, to find out whether the walk can be done, and there has been one rehearsal on this line in nine years and it was on the twenty-second of March and it took six hours each way and it was done by four men in the rain. So you may have my yard once a month to walk a thing in before you do it, and I will not be paid, and I will not be thanked, and if anybody asks me in four years I will say I lent a yard."

And it was accepted, and the finding is that **a thing this district could not pay for and could not inspect was accepted from the one person in the room who said out loud what it was, and the saying is the security.**

---

---

And then somebody asked the question that had been standing at the back of that room since the eighteenth of April, and it was asked by a mechanic and it was about two hours further west, and the answer went into the book in about nine words and it is the reason the fourth thing above is a security and not a formality.

"Somebody has to go and look at the fourth security," he said, "and the fourth security is a piece of ground at a place where the only frame on the line has a bracket in it that the iron fits, and the bracket is empty, and the iron is on a bench four hours up the bank in a hut with a boy in it. So a man who goes to look at the fourth security walks past the only place on that railway where anything can be turned and finds nothing in it, and he comes back and writes *I have seen it* in a book, and the book will be true.

"Now do that at the Harrow end. There is a pumping engine on a brick plinth in a brick house about eleven feet above the flood, and it has kept the interchange at nine feet for nine years, and without it the water would be at fourteen within a month. The man who keeps it is sixty-eight and is not paid and is given what is left in a tin. The oil has cost fourteen thousand seven hundred and forty-two coppers in nine years out of a Road House float that was surrendered in March. And the district has been debating the toll board for a fortnight.

"So I am posting the fourth security on the strength of a walk past an empty bracket, and I am telling the room that I cannot look at the fourth thing and I have not looked at the fifth, and the fifth is nine miles further on and it is a machine, and the man who keeps it was not asked what his job is until the eighteenth of April by a lamp-man who counts lamps and not signals."

Nobody in that room had anything to say about that, and the minute says so, and it says that the argument for posting the fourth security was made by the man who signed the lease that made the fourth place a place, and that he made it in order to be refused the right to inspect it.

---


**And on the margin of the same sheet, in a hand that is not the clerk's, a man of thirty-nine wrote one line about himself and then did not write anything else, and the line is in the public lot book in the fourth place where anybody can read it.**

> **AN OFFICE IS NOT A PERSON AND I AM NOT ONE OF THE FOUR AND NOBODY HAS ASKED ME TO BE.**

And beneath it, in the same hand, two more lines, added on the eighteenth of this month after somebody pointed out that a margin is not a public document:

> **AND I WOULD LIKE IT READ OUT AT A COUNTER SOMETIME AND NOT AT A RAIL.**

And the clerk who is going to go up the bank on the twenty-ninth read it out at the ward market counter at about ten o'clock on the nineteenth of this month in the ordinary voice, and about thirty people stood there, and the finding entered the same day is that **a man in an office asked to be read out and not spoken at, and that this is the first request of its kind anybody in this district has made, and that it was made by the man nobody wanted standing in that hut in the first place.**

---

**And the sheet went down the bank at about eleven o'clock that night and it went up the bank at about six the next evening and it went into the public lot book on the eighteenth at nine in the morning, and the thing that is in the book is not a sum, it is four things.**

It is four hundred and ten coppers, and eleven coppers a quarter, and a piece of ground held by nobody, and thirty-six coppers owed to nobody. And there is also a yard that is not a security and is entered as not one, and the finding is that **for the first time in nine years the west end of that railway has standing behind it that is not a rate, and every part of it can be walked to, and not one of the four can be turned into a sum without a rate meeting, and a rate meeting cannot amend a document it has already refused.**

And the fourth bearer is still not named, and the minute says the fourth bearer is not a slot and is not vacant, and that he will be named by three people who are not in this room, and that Mr Vale has no part in naming him.
