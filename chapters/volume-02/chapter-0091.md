# Chapter 91: The Hole

Nobody was allowed to fill in the hole, and the man standing next to it at about eleven in the morning on the fourth of this month had a shovel in his hands and had been told twice, and he put the shovel down in the ballast and said the thing that is in the minute.

"I dug it," said Tarin Kest. "I dug it at about half past seven this morning with a barrow I did not pay for, and I am not going to be asked about the barrow, and I dug it because a man cannot measure sixty yards off a water column in the wind with a string and a bucket, and I have measured things for thirty years and I will tell you exactly what the problem is."

The hole is at the ninth place, on the shoulder, about four feet back from the ballast and eleven feet above a drop of nine feet. It is a foot across and a foot and a half deep, and it has a string pegged across it with a knot in the middle, and the string runs sixty yards west along the shoulder to a second peg, and the second peg is the west end of the boundary as it has stood since the twenty-ninth of March.

It has been raining since about four. It is not cold. **The whole point of the morning is that it is not cold and is not bright, and is the kind of day on which a man who has never been on that bank would say the weather was fine.**

---

**And the finding of the fourth of this month is that a hole dug to put a post in is not a post, and a post is a mark, and a mark may not carry a job, and this district has one instrument for a boundary on the ground and it is the one instrument it is not allowed to use.**

The rule is not new and it has not been argued about in nine weeks and nobody in that room wanted to argue about it. **A mark is the standing record of a person's refusal of a name.** It may not be used as a bearer, a holder, a witness, a responsible party, or a person who is asked, and it may not carry a job, and the reason is that the same mark in two documents for two jobs is a claim that two people are one person, made by people who each thought they were helping.

The west end of this line is held by a woman of fifty-one and a woman of about fifty. Both of them are people, and both of them have said yes three times, and the word they say is nine words long, and it stops about nine people a year.

"I have been thinking about this since the tenth of May," said Sef Anyard, "**and I have got to the bottom of it, and the bottom of it is that a mark is a person-shaped hole in a piece of paper, and I have spent eleven years learning what a person-shaped hole costs.**"

She was in a doorway, not the hut, because the hut has a bench in it with a lamp book and a chalk box and a can of oil on the desk and no room for eight people, and the eight of them stood in the doorway and in the front room of the nearest of the four houses.

"Then say it," said the tenth hand, who had come up on the previous night with four others and had slept in the room over the counting office that has a bed in it and a window that shuts.

"I am not going to say it. **I am going to ask somebody else, and then I am going to agree with them.**" She turned round. "Ask her."

---

The woman at the column is about fifty and has been the water at that place for eleven years and has stood in her own doorway for eleven years, and has said no to a fourth place twice, and has said yes to a word three times, and the two facts are in the same book and neither has been struck.

She was at the door of the second house with a pail in her hand when the room came out, and she put the pail down, **which is the third time in nine weeks that anybody in this district has entered her putting a pail down as an act.**

"**A line of stones is not a mark,**" she said. "**And that is not a new rule and I am not asking you to make one. A mark is cut and that is laid, and there is no rule about stones, and I have known about a line of stones on this bank for eleven years, and nobody has laid one this year and nobody is going to lay one.**"

"Agreed," said the tenth hand. "It is not extended. It is the standing test case and it stays where it is."

"Then you have nothing," said the mechanic, "because a hole in the ground is not a cut, and a post in a hole is a cut, and if a post cannot stand here then what is standing here."

The answer came from the doorway of the second house, and it took about eleven seconds, **and it is the answer of the whole of this volume so far, and it was given by a person who does not read figures and has never been in a room in Alder Reach.**

"**Two people, a term, and an entry, and a hole. That is all of it. You have had two people since the twenty-ninth of March and a term since the twenty-ninth of April and an entry since the second of April, and every one of those three is in a book nine hours away, and the hole is the only one of the four that a person can stand next to. Leave the hole.**"

Nobody argued with her about it, and the minute says that nobody argued with her about it, and it says in a different hand, lower down, **that the reason nobody argued with her about it is that she had been in that doorway for eleven years and had watched four things get put on that bank and taken off it again, and that a fifth thing coming out of the ground and staying in it was the first one anybody had ever offered her.**

---

And the ground they are marking is held by nobody. **That is the only reason any of them can mark anything on it, and the entry says so in a line that is the whole reason this volume has a boundary in it.**

The siding, the column, the hut, and the ground between them were demised in the ninth year to a firm, by a man who signed it in about eleven minutes, and the shed of sixteen feet by thirty that the lease describes is not there, and the lease was surrendered on the thirtieth of March on a sheet headed surrender, assigned to nobody, and the rate sheet has carried the ground since the twenty-eighth of March as **HELD BY NOBODY**.

"That is the whole of it, and I want it in the book before anything else is," said Tarin Kest. "I signed the paper that made this a place a man could put a post in, and the paper is gone, and the only reason there is a hole in the ground at all is that a man in a counting room struck my firm's name out of a rate sheet. If he had not, none of the eight of you could stand here and put anything anywhere, and the boundary would be two women standing in two doorways shouting at each other across four yards."

"A mark on ground somebody owns is a claim," said the tenth hand.

"It is," said Kest, "**and I have been claiming this ground for nine years by paying for water out of a meter standing on it, and nobody asked me to and nobody stopped me, and that is what a claim is, and I am the only man on this bank who can tell you what one costs.**"

---

The walk went west at about half past twelve on the fourth, and it is five and a quarter hours to Alder Reach in ordinary weather, and the party did it in five and three-quarters because of the rain, and nobody has entered a corrected figure, on the grounds that the figure on the sheet is the clerk's and belongs to the clerk and a number a woman wrote for herself is not a number this district gets to correct.

Two of the eight had never been on that bank at all, and one of them was a woman of about thirty-eight with a hook and a sacking sheet, who comes up once a month for her own trade and buys old paper off four houses, and who has now been on it four times this year and has said each time that she is not here for the railway and each time come further than she had to.

The boundary runs sixty yards west of the column and then it stops, because sixty yards is where it was pegged on the twenty-ninth of March by a signalman who could not be paid, and nobody has walked the rest of it since. **They walked the rest of it.** It is a footpath on the shoulder for two hundred yards, then the shoulder ends at a drainage cut four feet deep that goes down to the flood line, and beyond the cut there is nine hundred feet of embankment with a nine-foot drop along the river side and nothing on it at all.

A woman of about fifty-one said that the two hundred yards is the first part and that it is ground a person can stand on, and that the drainage cut is the end of the boundary, and that she has known for eleven years that the drainage cut is the end of it because in the fourth year a man put a train over the cut and it went in. It is written in her own book, in her own hand, and she wrote it in her own book and not in anybody else's.

Nobody extended it past the cut. The minute enters that four people asked and one person answered, and that the answer is a sentence about a drainage cut and a flood, and that **it was given by a person who has been the keeper of the only pair on that line for eleven years and who has never once been asked to walk a boundary by anybody.**

The tenth hand asked her one question on the shoulder, at about half past two, which was how she knew the cut was the end of it. She said that she did not know, and that she had been about eleven feet above the water at the time, and **that a person remembers the shape of a hole and not the reason for it, and that the reason was in a book in Alder Reach and had been in a book in Alder Reach since the fourth year, and that nobody had ever come up this bank and asked her for it.**

---

**And on the fifth of this month the district tried four things in about two hours and could not use one of them, and the two hours are in the book because a failure that is entered is not fixed, it is known.**

The stone is a line of stones, and the standing finding is unchanged, and is not extended.

The post is a mark, and a mark may not carry a job, and a post in the ground with a number on it is a mark with a job, and the tenth hand said so in four seconds and somebody else said it after him about forty minutes later. **The finding is that this district has spent nine weeks building a public book and has discovered, at the end of it, that the one thing it cannot put on the ground is the one thing that makes a thing public, and that this is not a defect in the book, it is a fact about the ground.**

The gate is worse, and it went further, because somebody in the room pointed out that a gate is not a mark and is not a person and carries no name, and four people in that doorway went about eleven minutes on a gate before the woman of the column said one sentence and finished it.

"You are all trying to build a thing that is there," she said. "**Nobody is to lay a stone. Nobody is to put a post in. And a gate is a thing that shuts, and what shuts is people, and I am the water, and I have been shut for eleven years by a river, and I am telling you now that I know what it is to be shut, and it is not what any of you are planning.**"

The number is the fourth thing and it went furthest, because **a number is not a mark**, and the number was the distance from the column to the drainage cut, and the number is two hundred and sixty yards, and the minute carries it.

And then a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, who had come up on paid work and had not asked to, said the thing that ended it. He had said a version of it once in a market in May and he was not thanked for it then either.

"**A number is not a boundary, it is a measurement of one, and the difference is that a measurement can be taken by anybody at any time, and a boundary can only be held by somebody who is asked to hold it. You have got a hole, a number and a distance. You have not got anybody. Write the three down, and then ask the two of you whether you will do it again for a month, and if you will, then you have a boundary, and the hole is where it is measured from and not what it is.**"

---

**And the answer came on the sixth of this month in the second hour, and it is four lines long, and it is the first instrument in this district's history that consists of nothing whatever but people and a date, and the whole of it fits under the heading a mark may not carry a job.**

The boundary at the west end of this line is held by two named people, for a stated term, and is entered in a public book on a stated day, and **there is no mark on the ground, and there is a hole in the ground that is not a mark and is not the boundary and is where the sixty yards is measured from.**

The two people are the keeper of the hut and the woman at the column, and neither of them is paid, and neither of them owns the ground, and the ground is held by nobody, and the entry says all of that on the face of it.

The term is a month and it runs from the twenty-ninth of May to the twenty-ninth of June, and the twenty-ninth of June is the third asking, and it is four days off, and nobody has been given the job of going up that bank.

The entry is in the public lot book in Alder Reach, and it is in the water office's own book, which is copied twice a quarter and goes where a sheet does not go, and the two entries are not joined to each other, and the registrar was asked to keep them so and agreed and gave the reason she gives every time.

> **THE WEST END OF THIS LINE IS HELD BY TWO NAMED PEOPLE FOR A TERM AND IS NOT MARKED ON THE GROUND. THE GROUND IS HELD BY NOBODY. THE SIXTY YARDS IS MEASURED FROM A HOLE, AND THE HOLE IS NOT A MARK AND IS NOT THE BOUNDARY, AND IT IS A PLACE WHERE A PERSON STANDS. THE TOLL BOARD AT THIS PLACE SAYS ON ITS FACE THAT IT IS NOT A WAY OF SAYING THAT A TOLL LANDS ON A PLACE, AND IT IS NOT, AND NEITHER IS THIS ENTRY, AND NEITHER IS ANYTHING ELSE IN THIS DISTRICT.**
>
> **AND THE REASON IS THE REASON IT HAS BEEN SINCE THE SEVENTH OF MARCH, AND NOBODY IN THIS ROOM ENLARGED IT. A MARK MAY NOT CARRY A JOB. A LINE OF STONES IS NOT A MARK, BECAUSE A MARK IS CUT AND THAT IS LAID. THERE IS NO RULE ABOUT STONES AND THERE IS NOBODY WHO IS TO LAY ONE.**

---

And the man of thirty-nine was in the room, and had not been asked anything, and entered himself for the third time in two months as present and not asked. The entry about him on the sixth of this month is that he had been in that hut forty-two days on a term of thirty, and that the word under the date on his desk was still *held*, and that under it he had written again that he does not know whether it is the correct word.

"You have been here forty-two days," said the woman of the column, from the doorway, with a pail in her hand again.

"Forty-two," he said.

"And the sheet says thirty."

"And the sheet says thirty, and the office renews it, and the office has not asked, and I have not asked to be asked, and both of those are in your book from the twenty-fifth of last month."

"Then say the word," she said, "and say it to me and not to the book, because I am the only person in this room who has said it to a person this month."

He said it, and it took him about a second and a half, and he got four of the nine words right, and the woman of the column said the fifth one for him and then stopped, and the rest of it he said himself. The minute records that the fifth word was supplied by a person who is not in an office, and that she supplied one word and did not supply the other four, and **that the finding entered is that a man who has been somewhere forty-two days instead of four is a different man, and that the difference is in the fifth word and not in the other four.**

---

And on the way down the bank on the evening of the sixth, the party stopped for about nine minutes at the third changeover, where there is a length of green pipe lying across the cess with one end in the ballast and one end in the air, put there before the water came by somebody who was going to come back. **The clerk who was going to ask in May stopped there on purpose.**

She read her own sentence out loud twice, in the ordinary way, and the party stood still while she did it, and the tenth hand said afterwards that this was the second time he had watched her do it, and that she had got shorter both times, and that he did not know whether that was because the sentence was getting simpler or because she was getting better, and that he had not asked and was not going to.

**And the finding of the sixth, entered at nine in the morning on the seventh with the delay in the registrar's hand, is that the two people on that bank said yes to a fourth month on the sixth of this month without anybody using the method at all, in about four minutes, standing up, in a doorway, in the rain, and that the two of them were not asked anything by anybody who had come up a bank to ask them.**

"Write that down properly," said the tenth hand, when they were in Alder Reach. "Not that they said yes. Write *how*."

"They said yes in a doorway in the rain in four minutes to a question that was not asked," said the clerk, "and I have written it that way, and I have entered that this is the third yes and the first one that did not cost anybody a day, **and I do not yet know what the third one costs, and I am not going to guess at it in a book.**"

---

Nobody up that bank asked about the train. That is in the minute, and it was asked about afterwards in a counting room, and the answer was that eight people had gone up a bank for five and three-quarters hours to mark a boundary and had come back without once looking at nine vehicles standing on a siding, and the reason is that a boundary is a thing that can be asked about and a train is not.

This is the fourth time in nine weeks that a party has gone up that bank and not looked at the train, **and the finding is that a district will walk five and a quarter hours to look at a hole in the ground and will not turn its head at nine vehicles, and that this is not a fact about the train.**

The boy who keeps the index is seventeen, and is paid four coppers a quarter, and is not Adrian Vale's apprentice and has said so in a yard in front of more than one person. He wrote the figure on the sixth, in the evening of the same day, in a hand that is not anybody else's, and **the train on the siding had stood a hundred and fifteen days.** Beside it in the index there are now four dates on one line and the fifth of them is the twenty-ninth of this month, and nobody has put them in a column.

The minute says that not putting them in a column is deliberate, **and that a column is for things that go the same way, and these five do not.**

**And the entry of the sixth of this month closes with the sentence this district has been getting to since the second of April, and it is in the tenth hand's hand, and it is one line, and the line is this: a hole in the ground is the only thing this district has been able to put on a boundary in six weeks, and it is not a mark and it holds nothing and it is not going to hold anything, and a person can stand next to it, and in about four years there will be a stranger standing next to it who will not know what it is for, and that is the correct condition for it to be in.**
