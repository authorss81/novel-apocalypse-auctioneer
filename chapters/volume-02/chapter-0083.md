# Chapter 83: The Man Who Cannot Close It

At about half past eight in the morning on the tenth of this month a man of about thirty-four carried a table up four flights of stairs above a chandler's shop on the lower terrace, and a woman of about thirty-eight carried two chairs, and the room they put them in cost six coppers for the day, and the six coppers came out of the two hundred the ninth place paid on the seventh.

The room is eleven feet by thirteen and it has one window and a door that does not shut on a latch, and about four things in it: a table, two chairs, a bench along one wall, and a lamp on the bench that is not connected to anything. There were eleven other people in it by half past nine and nine of them came up a bank on the previous night and slept in a room over the counting office that has a bed in it and a window that shuts and has been empty for two years, and which a bridge clerk of thirty-four has the keys to and did not say anything about at the time.

**And the five people in that room who were not there to listen to Adrian Vale were a signal keeper of fifty-one, the woman of the column who is the water, a mechanic of forty-three, a woman of forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide, and a clerk of six years who is the ward market and is not a person and had said so out loud twice this month.**

Adrian Vale had the chair by the window. He was the man who wrote down what the other five decided, he had been told that in advance, and he had said yes to it in one word, and nobody in that room thanked anybody for anything at any point in the morning, which is in the minute as a fact about the room and not as a virtue.

---

The two of them came down on the ninth and it took the best part of a day, and neither of them was asked to and neither of them would have said no.

Five and a quarter hours with four changeovers: two hundred yards past the fourth place, a cess four feet deep with standing water in it; a brick culvert at the fifth with eleven inches of silt; a length of green pipe lying across the cess at the seventh, put there before the water came by somebody who was going to come back, with one end in the ballast and one end in the air; and the step down of two feet six inches into about a foot of standing water at the eighth, with a shoulder four feet wide and a drop of nine feet off it on the river side.

A cart took them the first four miles and cost eleven coppers each way, which came out of the two hundred, and the last mile and a quarter they walked.

"I have not been off that bank since the twenty-ninth of April," said the woman of the column, in a room in Alder Reach, at about nine in the evening on the ninth, "and I am going to be honest with a room about why, because I have had a month to think about it and I would rather be honest in here than be pleasant in there. It is not that I do not want to come. It is that every time somebody in this city comes up that bank they come with a document, and every document so far has been about the railway, and **the railway is four hundred yards from my door and I am not the railway.**

"So I said I would come, and I said I would come without a document, and I have come without one, and I would like that written down at the point where somebody asks me what I think of a place being named as the origin of a toll."

---

**And the first thing the room did was throw away two of the four places in the draft, and the draft is his, and it is dated the nineteenth of March, and it has been published in the public lot book in Alder Reach in full since the second of April because a document nobody can close should not be in a drawer.**

The sixth place is a drain and has been going onto the ballast since before the Long Fracture. The eighth place is a curve with a shoulder four feet wide and a drop of nine feet off it on the river side. Both reasons are in the book. Neither is struck. Neither was found by the man who drafted the split, and the tenth hand said so at about ten past nine and it went in the minute.

"I am not striking them," said the woman who came down the bank. "I am telling you the room has struck them, and that the room struck them at about ten past nine in the morning on the tenth of this month, and **that the reason is in your mouths and not in your book, and I would like the minute to say so.**"

"It will say so," said the clerk.

"And there are three places and not four," said Sef Anyard, "and I want to say why before anybody asks me, because it took me eleven years and one train to work it out. There are three places on this line where a person can stand and there is a roof over them. The fourth place has a boy asleep in it and a piece of iron on a bench and no frame and never had one, and that is a place a thing is in and not a place a person is at. The east end has a cabin and a line book and a woman has just said yes to keeping a board in it. This hut. And the Harrow end, which has a brick house in it and a man of sixty-eight who is not paid."

"Four bearers," said the mechanic, "or it is not a split and you have written a rota, and a rota is a worse word than what you had."

"Four bearers," said Sef Anyard, "and three of them hold a week, and the fourth one is not a fourth place."

---

The mechanic had said almost nothing in the first hour and everybody had let him, and the minute says that he was asked twice whether he wished to speak before the asking started, in the manner a reader is asked before a reading, and that he said no twice.

He has been on that railway his whole working life. He took a nine-year lease on a siding, a water column, a shed sixteen feet by thirty and the ground between them, and signed it in a room in about eleven minutes, in the ninth year, and a man in this city found out about the lease on the ninth of April, in a room with a fire in it, and the shed is not there and the firm on the paper is not there either, and he surrendered the lease on the thirtieth of March on a sheet headed surrender, assigned to nobody.

"Ask me what I think of a place being named as the origin of a toll," he said, "and I will tell you the thing I have been not saying since the eighteenth of March, which is that I have known since the eighteenth of March that the origin is that village, and I have not said it, and I did not say it in April and I did not say it in May, and every one of those three not-sayings cost somebody something, and the somebody was never me.

"So if you put my name next to a decision I did not make, then the decision is not mine, and I will not be the man who is leaned on for it, **and I would rather be leaned on for a number in a book.** I have four hundred and ten coppers of standing salvage that is worth something in Alder Reach and nothing out there, and it is in the book with my name on it because a foreman of twenty-two years entered it at his own request, and that I can be leaned on for. **I did not choose the village. I chose a shed and a column and I did not ask anybody what a column was.**"

---

**And that is where the room stopped for about eleven minutes, and the eleventh minute is the first time anybody in this district can remember that the thing this district has been failing to build was built in a room by people who are not him.**

Nobody spoke for about four minutes, and then the woman of the column, who had not spoken since about a quarter past nine, said the thing that turned the morning.

"Whose week is the first one."

That is the question the draft cannot answer and the ninth-year lease cannot answer and the four drafts in a room with a door on it cannot answer, and it took the room about seven minutes to find out that nobody in it had thought about it, and the finding is entered in the tenth hand's hand and it is that **a rotation is not a rotation until somebody has written down the order of the weeks, and eleven weeks of asking whether a thing could be held produced nobody who had ever written down which week came first.**

Three bearers hold a place, a week at a time, in turn, and each of the three has a named substitute for the weeks they are not there, and the substitute's name goes on the sheet in advance and is a person.

The fourth bearer has no place and no week and no pair.

"The fourth bearer is the person who ends it," said Sef Anyard.

The order of the weeks was then settled in about nine minutes, and it was settled by a mechanic with a pencil on the back of a trade's invoice because nothing else was in the room to write on, and the invoice went into the minute and the order went into the public lot book, and the first week on it belongs to the keeper of the hut because she is the only one of the three who is at that end of the line every day, and the finding is that **a rotation starts with the person who is already there and not with the person who is most senior.**

Nobody spoke for about four seconds.

"The charter has to end, and it cannot end on a date, because a date is a thing that arrives, and the thing that arrives is a train that cannot be turned because the iron is in a hut four hours up a bank. So somebody has to end it, in a room, out loud, in front of two other people, and it has to be somebody who is not travelling and is not in an office and is not the man who wrote it.

"**And it is not going to be a slot, and I am going to say the reason.** A slot is a thing you can fill in later. This is a person who has to be found before a thing starts, and if the three of us who hold the weeks have to name them, then the man who wrote the document does not get a part in it, and that is the whole of the point of having three."

---

And Adrian Vale, in the chair by the window, said the thing he had come to that room to say, and he said it in about four seconds, and it is in the minute in those words, and it is the whole of his part in that room.

"**I cannot close it. I may draft a split and I may not close one, and there is no version of me that closes this, and that has been in the book since the second of April and it is not going to stop being in the book because five people have been kind to me about it.**"

"We know," said Wren Ostler.

"Then I would like it in the minute that I said it before the room got to it, and not after, because a man who has been asked to stop saying it every week will eventually say it as a way of not being in the room, and I would rather be in the room."

"You said it four times in March and April," said the tenth hand, from the bench. "I have the count."

"Then put the count in the minute as well."

"It is in the minute. It is also on the notice board at Lot 17 in a chalk line that a man of about fifty-six wrote, and it is a rotation is only real when it turns, and that sentence is yours and you gave it away in a yard in nine minutes in April, and it is the reason this room exists. You do not have to be the instrument. You built the method that finds the instrument. **Those are two different jobs and one of them is yours and one of them is not.**"

---

The fourth place in the charter is the origin, and the room named the ninth place in about four minutes, and the naming was a decision and not a reading, and the minute says so in the room's own words, and the two women it was done to are both in the room and were both asked before it was decided.

The toll on the line is the origin. The object does not say which one. Four candidate readings were put into a book in the first week of March and none of them was adopted, and a yard of about five hundred people was told that choosing between them is a decision and not a reading.

"So it is a decision," said the woman of the column. "I have had a month to sit with that word. I did not like it in March and I do not like it in May and I am going to say yes anyway, and I want the two things in the minute in this order: I do not like it, and I say yes.

"And I want my name off it. Not yours. Mine. The place goes on it and not me, and I am the water, and a name in a book is a door and a door is for sending a man away, and I have said that in four rooms now and I am not going to say it a fifth."

The mechanic put both hands flat on the table at this point and said nothing for about a minute, and the minute records that, and it records that he said afterwards that he had been arguing for a different thing all morning and had been told by a woman of forty-four that he had the wrong reason and she had the right one, and that he was going to think about it.

"And I want one sentence in it in my own words," said Sef Anyard, "and the sentence is: the word is said by a person and not by a board, and if anybody ever puts that word on a board I will stop holding this and I will say so out loud on the day I stop. I have had that sentence since the twenty-ninth of April and I am saying it again because the ninth place has had a board up on it since the eighth of this month."

Both answers went in, in that order, in the words they were said, and the room decided the ninth place, and the entry under the decision is that **a decision and a reading are two different things and only one of them is on this sheet**, and the name of the place is on it, and the object has not been asked and did not agree.

---

**And the last hour of that morning went to a woman of forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide and who had said on the way up that she would not be here by eleven, and had been here from half past eight, and had said nothing at all until eleven.**

Her name is Ottoline Merrick and she is not against the charter and she is not for it and she is against one clause in it, and she read the clause out loud, and then sat down.

"The clause that says any one of the four bearers may call the use to an end at any time without a reason. I will not sign that and I will tell you what I will sign.

"I will sign a clause that says any one of the three who hold a week may call the use to an end at any time, and the fourth bearer may call it to an end at any time, and every one of those four has to say the reason out loud in a room with two other people in it, and the reason goes in a book, and the book is a public book.

"Because a man who can stop a railway by being tired of it is not a limit. A man who can stop a railway by saying out loud in front of two people why he is stopping it is the only kind of stop anybody in this district has ever had."

"Write hers," said the mechanic, "and write mine underneath, because I have been arguing for that clause since March and I have been arguing for it for the wrong reason, and she has just given me the right one in ninety words and I am not going to be the man who takes it off the table."

"It is the right one," he said. "I have been arguing that a reason is a courtesy to the man who is stopped. It is not. **A reason is for the people who are standing there when he stops it.**"

And at about twenty past twelve the room broke for food, and the six coppers came out of the same two hundred, and two of the six went on a lamp and three on bread and one went back into the tin, and the minute says that the arithmetic of the lunch is in the minute because the room was in a building bought out of a rate collected from four households nobody asked.

---

The whole of the morning was read back in the room at about twenty to one, before anybody stood up, by the tenth hand, who had been sitting on the bench for five hours saying almost nothing and who said in the minute that he had said almost nothing on purpose and that the room had not noticed and that this was the first time in his working life that not saying anything had been useful.

He read all of it, including the two answers in the order they were given and including the words *I do not like it, and I say yes*, and Sef Anyard stopped him at one point and had one word corrected, and the word was *board*, which she had said as *a thing in the street*, and **the two were not the same word, and the board was the thing and the street was where it was, and she did not want them run together.**

Then the clerk of the ward market said what the question had been, all of it, in about ninety words, in her own words, so that the sheet would carry the question and not somebody's memory of one, and the minute entered that this was the sixth time anybody in this district had done that, and **the first time it had been done by a person who was one of the five being asked.**

---

**And the sheet went down the bank at about one o'clock in the afternoon on the tenth of this month, in a satchel against a boy's chest, and it is five and a quarter hours with four changeovers in it, and the boy got it to the yard at about seven the same evening, and it went into the public lot book on the eleventh at nine in the morning, and the delay is entered, and the registrar entered it herself, and the line she entered is the fourth time she has written it and the fourth time it has been about a document.**

> **THE SHEET WAS DECIDED IN A ROOM ABOVE A CHANDLER'S SHOP ON THE TENTH OF MAY AT ABOUT HALF PAST EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND IT WAS IN THIS BUILDING AT ABOUT SEVEN IN THE EVENING THE SAME DAY AND IT WAS ENTERED HERE ON THE ELEVENTH OF MAY AT NINE IN THE MORNING. THE DELAY IS THE BOARD'S AND NOT THE WOMEN'S AND NOT THE ROOM'S. THE WOMEN DID NOT KNOW THERE WAS A SHEET AND THE ROOM WAS TOLD THERE WOULD BE ONE AND AGREED TO IT.**
>
> **AND THE PART OF THAT ENTRY THAT IS ABOUT THE MAN WHO WROTE IT DOWN IS ONE LINE AND IT IS THIS. HE DRAFTED IT AND HE COULD NOT CLOSE IT AND HE SAID SO AT ABOUT HALF PAST ELEVEN IN THE MORNING BEFORE THE ROOM GOT TO IT, AND HE WROTE DOWN WHAT THE ROOM DECIDED AND NOTHING ELSE, AND HE IS NOT ONE OF THE FOUR AND HE IS NOT A KEEPER OF A BOARD AND HE IS NOT ON THE COMMITTEE, AND THIS IS THE FIRST DOCUMENT IN THIS DISTRICT'S HISTORY WHERE THAT IS TRUE OF HIM ON THE FACE OF IT.**

And the two women went back up the bank on the eleventh, five and a quarter hours with four changeovers, in a cart the first four miles, and nobody paid them either time, and the entry says that and does not apologise in so many words.
