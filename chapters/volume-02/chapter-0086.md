# Chapter 86: Who Goes Up the Bank

At about a quarter to eight in the morning on the twentieth of this month Wren Ostler wrote a time on the top of a sheet of paper in the counting room of the ward market, and then put her hand flat over it, and then crossed it out, and the finding of the day is that she did that three times in about four minutes and the reason is the second thing on a wall eleven feet from where she was standing.

The job is a walk. It is five and a quarter hours from Alder Reach to the ninth place in ordinary weather with four changeovers in it, and the worst of the four is a step down of two feet six inches into about a foot of standing water, and the figure this district carries for the day is nine hours, and the finding of the twenty-second of this month is that nine hours is not the day. A night is needed on top of it, because the asking itself took fifty-one minutes in April in a room nine miles from where the walking starts.

The second thing on the wall is how long, with an hour on it. And the clerk of the ward market stood in her own counting room on the twentieth of this month and could not put an hour on the only job anybody in Alder Reach had ever given her, because nobody knows how long a question takes when the asking is five and a quarter hours away.

"I can tell you how long it takes a man to walk up that bank," she said later, "because I have watched men not agree about it for six weeks. I cannot tell you how long a question takes, and I have written two in my hand and cut both of them out, and a person asking a person to do a job has to know how long the job is or the person being asked is the one carrying the guessing."

---

**And fourteen people said no in nine days, and the reasons are in a book in their own words, and four of the fourteen gave the same reason and the reason is the walk, and the district's finding is that it has now asked forty-one people a question in nine weeks and has got two yeses and both of the yeses came out of a room and neither of them came off a board.**

A haulier who is on that bank twice a week and cannot be absent from it twice in a week, and who is the man the word stopped twice in ten days in March, and who stood on that shoulder for a minute and a half and was not angry, and who said: "I have been on that bank about four hundred times in nine years and I have never once been asked what for. If you ask me now I will tell you, and I will probably say no, and then you will have asked, and that is worth a fortnight of the walk on its own."

A plasterer, who is on a roof at the far end of the terrace, and who is the eleventh name on a sheet of nineteen refusals in a book in Alder Reach from the sixth of April, and who said: "I refused a drawer in April in front of about sixty people and a woman read it out at the counter the next morning with the word NO in front of it, and I have not stopped thinking about it, and I am not going to be a man who says yes to everything after that."

A woman who keeps a counter of fish, who did the same thing in April for a drawer and would not do it twice in a year, and whose reason is in the book and is one sentence: "I told you in April what it is like to hold a thing for four days and come back and find it was open the whole time and I am not doing it again for a river."

A man of about thirty-one with a hand that will not hold a pen in the cold, who said so plainly, in eleven words, and would not be asked about it twice.

A man who has to be at a burial on the twenty-ninth.

A woman of about thirty-five who said she would go if somebody else would go and be told what to ask, and was told that there is no script, because there is no script, and she withdrew the offer in writing the same afternoon, and the clerk entered the withdrawal and the reason and did not enter the word *understood*.

Nine more said they would go if the walk were shorter. None of them was argued with.

"They are not being difficult," said the tenth hand. "**They are being exact. Fourteen is not fourteen refusals. It is fourteen people telling you the true shape of the job, and the true shape of the job is that it is nine hours on feet and a night in a bed that is not yours and a question at the end of it that may be a yes or may be a no, and nobody in this district has ever put a time on that because nobody in this district has ever asked somebody to do it.**"

---

**And the lot offered him the hour on the morning of the twentieth, and he did not take it, and the refusal is entered with no cost and no time, and Stenn asked him in the counting room at about eleven o'clock whether it was cowardice or discipline and got nine words.**

> **A READING MAY BE SPENT ON ONE LOT YOU HAVE TOUCHED. IT COSTS ABOUT AN HOUR AND A COST, AND THE COST IS A PART OF THAT LOT'S TOLL.**
>
> **THE TOLL ON THIS IRON IS THE ORIGIN. AN ORIGIN TAKES MEMORY OF DEPARTURE. YOU HAVE PAID IT TWICE. THE SECOND TIME TOOK LESS THAN THE FIRST.**
>
> **THE IRON DOES NOT HOLD PLACES AND WILL NOT NAME ONE. ASKED IT FOR MORE, IT GIVES YOU WHAT YOU HAVE BEEN GIVEN.**
>
> **A READING OF A BOARD IS NOT A READING. A DOCUMENT NOBODY HAS HANDLED MAY NOT BE READ. A PERSON MAY NOT BE READ. YOU MAY NOT ASK A LOT WHERE THE TOLL LANDS.**
>
> **A READING MOVES NOTHING ON THAT LINE. YOU HAVE BEEN TOLD THIS AND YOU WROTE IT DOWN. YOU MAY TAKE THE HOUR OR NOT TAKE IT AND NEITHER IS A FAVOUR TO ANYBODY.**

He had read it twice, on the eleventh of March and on the twenty-seventh of April, from a sheet, compared by two people against his own hand, about an hour each. The first cost was his sense of which way a river went, for about fifty minutes, on a bank, nine miles from the book, with a keeper in the room. The second cost was the memory of which gate he went out of his own city by, and there is no west gate, and it had not come back in twenty-three days.

"Neither," he said.

"That is not an answer to either question."

"**It is the only answer I have got to either of them, and I have been asked four times now and I have said it four times and it has got shorter each time and I would like the clerk to enter that I noticed.**"

"Entered," said Nessa Pell, who is a clerk of nineteen years and the sitting steward of the public lot and who is also, and has been for nineteen years and without being asked, the person in the room who is neither the one asking nor the one being asked, and whose job is to say what the question was. "And the question you were asked is entered, and the hour is entered, and the non-event is entered with no cost and no time, and nobody in this book is going to write it as cowardice and nobody is going to write it as discipline.

"And if either of those words is ever written against this entry I will take it out, and I have never taken a line out of anything in nineteen years and I am not starting with this one."

"Then what will it say," said the man who sells nothing and has a use.

"**It will say that a man was asked on the twentieth of this month whether he would do a thing he is able to do, and that he said no, and that nobody has yet found out why, and that the reason he gave the only time he has given is that a fourth reading will give him a fourth piece of the thing that the iron takes, and the thing the iron takes is a departure, and he has got two left.**"

Nobody in the counting room had thought of that and the entry says so, and the finding of the twentieth of this month is that the cost of a reading is not a figure and is not a duration and is **a countable number of the specific thing it takes, and nobody in this district had been counting it, and a man who counts it can say no with a number in his mouth instead of a principle.**

---

And the man himself did not go up the bank on the twenty-ninth, and said so in advance, in a room, on the evening of the twentieth, in eleven words, and the words are in the book because he asked for them to be.

"I am not coming up that bank and I would like it in the book that I said so in advance."

He was asked for a reason, once, by a man who does not sell anything, and he gave one and it is the reason the whole of it turns on and it is not a good reason and everybody in that room knew it.

"**I have been the man on every page of this since the eighth of March. If I am the man who goes up there then the twenty-ninth of May is a thing I did, and it will be in a book as a thing I did, and in about four years there will be a woman in a room somewhere in this basin saying that nothing on that railway happens unless a man in a good coat is on the bank, and she will be right, and it will be my fault, and the way to stop that sentence being true is for me not to be on the bank.**"

The tenth hand put the other half of it in the same entry, because he was there and because the entry would have been wrong without it.

"**And the second reason is that he does not know how long the asking takes and he would be guessing in front of a person about the length of a room, and a man guessing about the length of a room in front of the two people who live in it is a man deciding something about them, and that is the thing this district has spent nine weeks learning not to do.**"

---

**And on the twentieth of this month at about ten in the morning, in the counting room, the clerk who was going to ask four people the question that decides whether she asks it at all said yes, and she said it before she was asked, and it is the fifth time anybody in this district has answered a question about themselves before it was put.**

"I will go," said Wren Ostler. "And I have not been asked, so I am going to be asked now, and I am going to answer it properly, and you are going to do all five of the things and I am going to watch you do them, because I have watched you do them twice and you are not good at the third one.

"And the reason is short and it is not modesty. I held that key twice in March, on the second and again on the twelfth, four days each time, and the first time it was a lapse and nobody came, and the second time a woman came and I had asked her and she said yes and she was right to, and it was the first turn anybody in this district could call a turn. **A rotation that turns once is a rotation that has been shown it can.** That is a sentence with a date on it and I have had it on a board since the first of this month and I have been waiting for something to do about it.

"So: four and three-quarter hours up there is nothing to me. I have sat behind the same counter as a man who could not read his own figures for four days in April and I did not find out until the second of May. If I go up there and I stand in that room and I ask those two women a question, then this district will have a rotation that has turned twice, and the second turn will be a clerk, and a clerk is a person, and a person can be replaced, and I would like the whole of that on the sheet in that order."

"Who answers for you while you are saying it," said the tenth hand.

"**A woman of thirty-eight with a hook is on that bank on the fifteenth of every month and she is not on any roll in this city and she knows what I am saying better than I do, because her trade is other people's documents, and I would like her to stand in the room and say what the question was before I ask it, and I would like it entered that I chose her and that she did not agree to be chosen and said yes afterwards.**"

---

And then three more people were asked, on the twenty-first and the twenty-second, and two of them said no and one said no in a different way, and the finding is that the fifteenth refusal on this subject was a man who said he would go if somebody went with him, and that this is the same shape as the question the room is trying to answer.

And on the twenty-second of this month a man of about thirty-four, who mends fencing and who has come up that bank on paid work four times in nine years, said yes, and then on the morning of the twenty-third withdrew, in writing, with the reason that his mother is not well and that he had not asked anybody's permission and did not need to.

The clerk entered both, and entered the withdrawal after the yes, and entered that **a yes is not a hold, and a hold is not a term, and the difference between those two things is the whole of the instrument this district has been trying to build since the second of April.**

---

And on the twenty-first of this month four people walked up that bank as a rehearsal, which is a walk done before the day it is for, on the same ground, by some or all of the people who will do it, for the purpose of finding out whether the walk can be done, and which is the first rehearsal in this district since the twenty-second of March and the fourth in nine years.

They left at half past four in the morning. They were the clerk, the tenth hand, Pell Anselm the chandler with the counter of oil on Ash Row, and a young man of nineteen who asked to come and was not asked and has not been asked anything since and said on the bank, in front of all three of them, that he was nobody's keeper and that he was not going to be anybody's anything, and that he had come because there was a boy his age asleep in a hut four hours further on and he wanted to see the hut from the outside, and the clerk wrote all of that down and read it back to him on the shoulder.

The rehearsal took five and a quarter hours going and five and a half coming. The going figure is the tenth hand's and has been since March. The coming figure is a mile longer than anything anybody had written down, because the third changeover is in mud for about a quarter of a mile after rain and the twenty-first was two days after rain.

It took eleven and three-quarters in all, which is ten and three-quarters of walking and an hour of standing still. Forty minutes of that hour was spent at the fourth changeover, where a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was on the shoulder because he had come up the bank on paid work four times in nine years, and he told them at length what he was going to be asked and what he was going to take back if he said yes, and he told it twice, and the tenth hand wrote none of it down. The other twenty minutes was the clerk at the third changeover saying her sentence out loud twice to be sure of it, and the finding entered is that the hour is the finding and not the walking.

The finding entered on the twenty-second is that **the walk is not four and three-quarter hours and never was, and the only number anybody in this district has for it is a number two men wrote down in March on a day when two of them were wrong, and the number on the sheet that the clerk is going to carry up on the twenty-ninth is five and a quarter going and five and a half coming and eleven and three-quarters in the day, and it is on the sheet in her own hand and not carried forward.**

At the third changeover the clerk stopped the party and made all three of them stand still while she said, out loud, in a spot where anybody coming round the cess could hear her, the sentence she was going to say in a room nine miles further on.

"I am a clerk of a market in Alder Reach. I have not been asked to hold anything. I have been asked to come up here and ask two women whether they will hold the west end of a railway for a month, and I do not know what they will say, and I have been told there is no correct answer. I have come to the end of my asking."

And the tenth hand said that that was the sixth thing and that she had done it four times in nine weeks and got better at it each time, and that the version on a bank is the version that worked, and that she would not need it in a room, and that the reason she would not need it is that **a person being asked something by somebody whose job is to ask things is the only situation in this district in which the sixth thing has ever been unnecessary, and that is worth finding out and not worth assuming.**

---

**And the entry of the twentieth of this month is the shortest search this district has ever run and the longest fortnight anybody in it has spent not knowing something, and it is in the public lot book in a clerk's hand with the noes in their own words and the counts in front of them.**

> **FOURTEEN SAID NO IN NINE DAYS AND THE REASONS ARE IN THE BOOK IN THEIR OWN WORDS AND FOUR OF THE FOURTEEN GAVE THE SAME REASON AND THE REASON IS THE WALK. ONE SAID YES ON THE TWENTIETH OF MAY BEFORE SHE WAS ASKED. ONE SAID YES ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF MAY AND WITHDREW IN WRITING ON THE TWENTY-THIRD OF MAY. THE FIFTH THING WAS USED FOUR TIMES. THE FOURTH ONE WAS USED AND WAS ANSWERED CORRECTLY BY A PERSON WHO WAS NOT IN THE ROOM ON TWO OF THE FOUR OCCASIONS. THE READER'S JOB WAS GIVEN TO A PERSON WHO IS NOT ON ANY ROLL. THE ENTRY WAS ALSO MADE BY A BRIDGE CLERK OF THIRTY-FOUR IN THE WATER OFFICE'S OWN BOOK AND NOT ON A SHEET, BECAUSE THE WATER OFFICE'S BOOK IS COPIED TWICE A QUARTER AND GOES WHERE A SHEET DOES NOT GO, AND BECAUSE A SHEET TOOK A NIGHT AND A DAY TO COME DOWN THAT BANK ON THE SECOND OF APRIL AND A BOOK THAT IS COPIED TWICE A QUARTER GOES TO FOUR ROOMS AND A SHED.**

And under the heading on a wall in Alder Reach that says NOT ANSWERED, under the first line anybody has ever entered under it, which was a woman of about fifty asking what happens on the twenty-ninth of May, the second line went in on the twentieth of this month, and it is nine words long, and it is the answer to her own question, and it names a person and does not explain her.

**THE PERSON IS A CLERK OF A MARKET AND SHE IS NOT ASKED YET.**
