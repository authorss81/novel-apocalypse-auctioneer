# Chapter 87: The Twenty-Fifth

At about six in the morning on the twenty-fourth of this month a man of thirty-nine in a signalman's coat picked up a folded sheet of paper from under the leg of a bench in a hut nine miles west, unfolded it, read it, folded it again and put it back where he had got it, and the sheet is his own month and it is stamped by his own office and it is dated the twenty-fifth of last month, and it is in the hut and it is not in a book.

The reason it is under the leg of the bench is that the desk in that hut is in use. The desk is in use for a lamp book, a chalk box, a can of oil, and a sheet of paper on which somebody has written a date nineteen times. The bench has been eleven inches out of level at the left-hand end since before the water came, with a folded blanket under the low leg, and a man who has been sleeping on it for twenty-nine days puts things under the low end so that they do not slide.

"It is my own term," he said, to nobody, on the twenty-fourth. "It runs out tomorrow. There is no clerk's letter about it because there is no need of one, because it renews itself, and I have known that since the twenty-fifth of last month and I have not once asked anybody whether that was a good arrangement, because an office does not ask an office whether it is a good arrangement."

He had asked to be sent. Nobody sent him. There is a piece of paper in a file in Alder Reach that says so, and Selik Marne asked for it to be read out at a rail in April because, in about four years, somebody is going to write that this district put a man in a hut, and the reason it is in the book with the man's name on it is that it is the only part of it that was anybody's choice.

---

**And what an office does when its own term expires is nothing, and the finding of the twenty-fifth of this month is the most useful sentence anybody has put in a book about a river office in nine years, and it is nine words long.**

An office that lets a term lapse has not ended anything. It has stopped saying when.

The hut was there on the twenty-fifth and the man was there on the twenty-fifth and the bench was there and the lamp was there and the oil went in at about six, and nobody came up the bank, and nobody was sent, and nobody was asked, and at about six in the evening he wrote the date on the sheet of paper on the desk for the nineteenth time, and the date is the twenty-fifth of this month, and under it he wrote one word, and the word is *held*, and the finding under the word is that he does not know whether it is the correct word and has entered that he does not know.

The oil went in at six and the man of thirty-nine did the whole of it himself, and the chamber was silted, because it silts in a fortnight, and he cleared it with a shovel that belongs to the office and that he is allowed to use, and the entry on the twenty-fifth is four lines long and none of them is about the water.

---

**And the same day, at about a mile and three quarters up the same bank, somebody painted a second line on a board that went up on the eighth of this month, and the second line is in the same size of type as the name at the top, and the name at the top is a name nobody in this basin can find in a register, and the second line is about a hut.**

> **THE HUT AT THIS PLACE IS HELD BY AN OFFICE UNTIL A DATE. THE DATE IS WRITTEN HERE. THE OFFICE HAS NOT ASKED. THE OFFICE CONSIDERS THIS PLACE UNSAFE AND HAS SAID SO IN A PUBLIC YARD ELEVEN TIMES IN EIGHT YEARS.**

The eleven lines under the name are not set out again here, because they are the same eleven lines and they are on a board four hours away, and what is set out is the one line that is not one of them. It is set out because a sentence painted on a board and then written out in a book has been read twice, and this district has spent nine weeks arguing about things that had been read once.

It is the first line on a public board anywhere in this district that has been written by somebody who is not the office it is about. It went into the public lot book in Alder Reach on the twenty-fifth at nine in the morning with the delay entered, and the date it carries is a date an office wrote for itself, and **the entry says that a thing nobody was asked to do has been entered by somebody who was not asked to enter it, and the reason anybody may read it is that it is nailed to a board in a street.**

The man who painted it is thirty-nine and he painted it in about nine minutes with a pot of white paint that came out of a bushel that cost four coppers, and he said afterwards that the sentence was true on the twenty-fifth of April as well and that he had not written it on the eighth because on the eighth there were four people standing behind him asking him about a barrow and a week.

---

**And the question the district put to itself on the twenty-fifth, in the counting room, with about thirty people in it, was whether this district can make an office ask, and the answer took eleven minutes and it is no, and nobody argued with it, and the finding under the no is that the district has spent nine weeks building a way to ask a person and has not built one, and a board cannot ask, and an office is not a person, and the two things on this line that cannot ask each other were standing four hours apart on the same morning and neither of them noticed the other.**

The way the no was arrived at is in the minute, and it is four questions and four answers, and they are short.

Can a rate stop an office sending a man. No. The rate buys the bench, the lamp, the chalk and the oil, and it has never bought the man, and the man is not on the rate.

Can a board require a date. No. A board has one line for a date and it is the keeper's date.

Can a public book refuse to enter a renewal. It can refuse to enter it as a public act, and that would leave the office with a hut and a bench and no record, and the man in the hut would be the only proof, and he is thirty-nine.

Can anybody in Alder Reach make a river office explain itself. **No. And a bridge administrator has said on published days for nine years that the reason is that an office that has to explain itself is an office that can be stopped, and this district has agreed with him eleven times and has never once given him a reason why it is wrong.**

What the district can do, it did, in about four minutes, and it is in the book.

**It can stop paying for the hut.** The market's rate of eleven coppers a quarter buys a bench, a lamp, a chalk and oil, and the market can vote on it and the office cannot be asked about it, and the finding is that the money and the man came apart in the ninth year and have been apart ever since, because an administration does not put a man's wage in a rate. **So there are two numbers in a public book about one man. Eleven coppers a quarter for the bench. Nothing for the man, and not a nil, because nobody in this district has ever costed a signalman and this district has costed a pump.**

The vote is on the twenty-seventh. The room was told the date on the twenty-fifth, and about nine people said the date was a thing to put in a column, and one woman of about fifty said it was a thing to be at, and the second one was the one that was right.

---

**And on the afternoon of the twenty-fifth of this month a bridge administrator said three things from a rail in Alder Reach in about four minutes, on a published day, and the third one is the sentence he is going to keep saying, and nobody believed he would stop and he did not stop.**

He is Selik Marne and he has been in the post nine years and he is argued with and never defeated and never converted and not wrong, and he is not on any walk, and he has not been up that bank in nine years, and the room in Alder Reach knows both of those facts and has entered both of them.

"My office staffs that hut because I sent him and I sent him because I have known for nine years that a place with nobody in it is a place that gets used by whoever comes up the bank, and I sent a man and he is not paid and I have never once pretended to anybody that he is."

"The first thing. I said last month and I will say it on every published day of this year and after: that is not enough. It is not enough because it is a hut, and a hut is not a line, and there is a train on that siding that has stood a hundred and three days and cannot go east and cannot go west."

"**The second thing, and this is the one that matters, and I am going to say it plainly because I have found out what happens to me when I do not. An office that has to ask is an office that can be refused. And there is a bridge in this city that has had a shut span and a suspended certificate and a public review of its own order since the eighth of March, and I have carried four boxes a day across it in the meantime because nobody could refuse me, and if I can be refused today then I can be refused in April, and the river does not care.**"

"Then say what you want," said the tenth hand, from about nine feet away, in the ordinary voice.

"**I want the river office to be able to staff a hut at a place it says is unsafe without putting the name of a man who can be asked into a book, and I want that in writing, and I want it for as long as I hold the post, and I know what I am asking for. I am asking for the one thing in this district that is cheaper for a man in an office than it is for a woman in a doorway, and I have thought about that for a week and I am still asking.**"

"**And I will tell you the reason I am asking, because you have never given one and I have noticed. You have given the river twice. The river is a real reason and it is a good one and it is not the reason. The reason is that on the ninth of April a boy of seventeen stopped a mechanic of forty-three from going up a bank to ask a woman a question, and he was right, and the mechanic accepted it, and named a date. And that boy is not going to stop me, because I am not a mechanic and I am not going up a bank. So the district's one working instrument against a thing like you is a boy who happens to be in the room.**"

---

And the tenth hand was asked, in front of about twenty people, whether that was fair, and he said that it was the fairest thing anybody had said about his own office in nine years and that he was going to write it down exactly, and he did, and the minute says that it was entered as a finding about the district and not as a finding about Selik Marne, and that Mr Marne read it and said that was correct.

---

And on the afternoon of the twenty-fourth of this month, before anything else on it happened, a man of about twenty-eight walked into the ground-floor office of a river administration in Alder Reach and asked a question at the counter, and the question was: when does the term at the ninth place end.

He is a copy clerk at the ward market and he went on his own time and nobody sent him and he had written the question down first, which is in the minute and which the clerk behind the counter noticed and mentioned.

The clerk behind the counter was about forty-five and has worked there eleven years and he did not say that the term was in the file. He went and got the file. The term is on the file. It is the twenty-fifth of this month, and the sheet is stamped, and the signature on it is a man's name that two people in the room said they recognised and could not place.

"Then can I ask who holds it," said the man.

"No."

"Then can I ask who signed the last one."

"No, but I can tell you that the last one was signed."

"Is the next one signed yet?"

**"There is no next one. That is what I am telling you. The sheet renews itself. I do not send it anywhere. It came back on the quarterly copy in April and I put it in the file and it is in the file, and if you want to know when it ends then it ends on the twenty-fifth, and if you want to know whether it is renewed then you are asking a person and there is not a person.**"

The man wrote all of that down in his own hand and took it away, and it went into the public lot book on the twenty-fifth at nine in the morning.

The finding entered under it is that **a public office told a stranger the date on the twenty-fourth of this month in about ninety seconds, and told him in the same ninety seconds that there was nobody to ask, and neither of those two facts is unusual and both of them are in the same minute, and the finding is that the difference between a public office and a public board is that a public office will tell you when and will not tell you who, and a public board will tell you who and cannot tell you when.**"

---

And on the twenty-fifth, at about four in the afternoon, the man of thirty-nine came down the shoulder to the ninth-place board to wipe the bottom line because it had been rained on, which is the job, and he read the seventeen sentences on it standing up, from the top, which he does about twice a week.

"You have written the sentence about your hut on the top of the board," said the woman of the column, who was on the bank with a pail and had not been asked anything and had stopped, which is now the third time in nine weeks that she has found a standing failure by carrying water.

"I did it on the twenty-fifth of this month and it took me nine minutes."

"You did it on the twenty-fifth and you are telling me on the twenty-fifth, and I want to know whether anybody else knew you were going to."

**"Nobody. I got the paint out of the same bushel they used on the line and I did it on my own and I would have told a committee if there had been one, and there is not one, and that is the first thing that has happened in this hut in nine years that nobody had a meeting about, and I have not decided whether that is good.**"

---

**And a woman of about fifty who is the water and has been in that doorway for eleven years was asked on the evening of the twenty-sixth of this month whether anybody in this district wants its own offices to be askable, and she said yes, and the reason she gave is about a bench and not about a man, and the entry is in her own words and is under a heading that says NOT ANSWERED.**

"Nobody is to lay a stone, and I said that on the twenty-ninth of April, and nobody has, and I want to say one more thing and then I am finished with this month.

"You are all talking about the man. Write down what I am talking about. The man in that hut is not the problem. If you take the man out tomorrow the bench is still there and the lamp is still there and the oil is still there and the word is still mine, and the woman at the ninth place is still the woman at the ninth place. I have been in this doorway eleven years and I have been the water for eleven years and in all that time the only thing anybody has ever given me that I could not hold with my hands is a piece of paper with a date on it. And a man in a hut is a piece of paper with a date on it, and it renews itself, and I did not ask for it and I do not want it, and I want the office to be askable because then the piece of paper has somebody's name on the other side of it and not a stamp."

"And can I say what I want as well," said the tenth hand.

"You may say what you want, and you may not say it in my doorway as though the doorway is a room, and you may not say the word *shop*. I have had *shop* four times in eleven years and I have not answered any of them."

"The word I was going to use was *water*. And I was going to say that the west end of this line is a stream and not a wall, and that you have been holding a wall for eleven years and calling it a stream, and that I would like to be the person who says that to you in a room on the twenty-ninth of this month with a clerk in it, and not at a rail in a street with twenty people in it."

---

And the clerk who was going up the bank read that whole of it back at a counter in Alder Reach at about ten o'clock on the twenty-sixth of this month, in the ordinary voice, with both of the answers under it and the word *shop* in it, because she was told to read it back exactly or not at all.

The ninth-place board's bottom line has seventeen sentences on it and one of them is a woman's, and it is the longest one, and it is the only one anybody has written with a full stop in the middle of it.
