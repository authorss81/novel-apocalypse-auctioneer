# Chapter 90: The First to the Third of Next Month

On the second of this month a trestle table went up in the yard of Lot 17 in Alder Reach with a bundle of five hundred sheets on it still tied, and the first thing anybody did was untie it and turn a sheet over and look at the back, and the back is where the count is.

It came off a press in Alder Reach on the first of this month, on the trade of a man who was paid and asked for it to be entered that he was paid. A run of five hundred is nine hundred and six coppers and the market's unassigned column has thirty a quarter, which is a hundred and twenty a year, and the difference between the two is eight hundred and ninety-four, and the difference is printed at the foot of the sheet in the same type as everything else, with a stall's name under it, because the woman who carries it was asked about it in a room in May and refused in those words and the market printed what she said.

**The month turned between yesterday and the day before, and the new month is June, and last month was May, and the sheet on the trestle table is the first document in this district's history that has been printed in one month and read in the next.**

There were about forty people in that yard by ten o'clock and by eleven there were about a hundred, and eleven sheets went off the table in an hour and went out of the gate with people who live four, nine and eleven miles away and who intend to put them on walls.

---

**And the sheet has a name across the top of it, in letters three inches high, and the name is the same name that is on three boards nine miles apart, and it is a name that no person in this basin can enter into a register, and the district has printed it five hundred times on purpose.**

Under the name, in smaller type, in the market's own words: **THE TOLL ON THIS LINE IS THE ORIGIN. THE ORIGIN IS A PLACE AND NOT A PERSON. NOBODY IN THIS BASIN CAN FIND THAT OFFICE IN ANY REGISTER THIS BASIN CAN REACH.**

Then the timetable: day of the month, hour, place, train, toll. The days run from the ninth of this month to the twenty-third and they are days of the month and not days of the week, and at the foot of that column, printed, is the reason.

> **THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE A CALENDAR. A DAY OF THE WEEK IS A THING NOBODY HERE CAN CHECK AGAINST ANYBODY ELSE. A SHEET WITH A WEEKDAY ON IT IS A SHEET THAT SAYS SOMETHING THAT CANNOT BE FOUND OUT, AND THIS MARKET HAS ALREADY PRINTED ONE OF THOSE BY ACCIDENT AND IT WAS ON A TRAM ARCH AND IT WAS WRONG.**

Then the toll column, four feet wide, ruled at its two ends, and empty from one end to the other, with the reason printed inside it at about the height of a man's eye, in four lines, and this is the same sentence that is on three boards and it is the fourth time this district has printed it.

> **PASSENGERS PAY NOTHING. THE CHARGE FALLS ON THE ORIGIN, WHICH IS A PLACE. NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT CAN STATE THE AMOUNT OF AN ORIGIN. THE COLUMN IS PRINTED AT FULL WIDTH BECAUSE A COLUMN THAT IS PRINTED NARROW LOOKS LIKE A COLUMN THAT HAS BEEN KEPT QUIET.**

And at the foot of the page, below a rule, in the same type as the toll column: **THE COUNT EACH TRADE CARRIED, READ OUT AT A COUNTER ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF LAST MONTH BECAUSE A STRANGER IS OWED THE COUNT.** Eleven rows and four thousand and one, and under it, **AND ABOUT NINE HUNDRED HOUSEHOLDS IN THIS DISTRICT ARE IN NO ROW AND ARE NOT COUNTED ANYWHERE INCLUDING ON THE SHEET THAT COLLECTS THE FOUR THOUSAND AND ONE.**

---

**And the fifth thing this district does not have was tested on a trestle table in a yard and it passed, and the finding is that a sheet with an empty column in the middle of it is worth more than a sheet with a price in it for about four days, and that this market has answered the four-day question with a person.**

The man who printed it had said, in April, in his own type, at the foot of his own sheet, that a column with nothing in it is worth more than a column with four coppers in it until about the fourth day, and that after the fourth day a column with four coppers in it is worth more. He was not wrong about that and this district cannot show he was wrong about that in a book, and the entry on the second of this month says so.

"A stranger takes that sheet cold on the ninth of this month and the empty column is the most honest thing on the page," said Ivo Stenn, "and he takes the same sheet cold on the thirteenth and the empty column is now a thing somebody could have written in and did not, and on the seventeenth it is a thing somebody is hiding, and he cannot tell which, and neither can we, and that is four days.

"And a price in that column is a lie, because we cannot state it, and a lie in a public book that has been believed for four days has been believed for four days. So the column stays empty.

"And the fourth day is the day the keeper starts. **That is the answer and it is nine words long and it is the first time in this district that a person has been paid to leave a space alone.**"

There are three such people and none of them is paid out of a rate for it. A clerk of the ward market at the east end. A woman of about thirty-eight with a hook, once a month, at the fourth place. A man of thirty-nine in a hut, at the ninth place, who is not paid by anything and says so every time he is asked.

---

And a woman with a stall on the lower terrace put a question on a slip of paper into a satchel on the first of this month, and the satchel went up the bank on a cart that belongs to a firm that gave up a lease in March, and the slip is the first time anybody in nine years has asked about the fifth can.

Nine cans in a fortnight at seven coppers is sixty-three coppers, and the last of that fortnight went up on the first of May, and the trader had written nine words against it, and they are in the book and they are not repeated here: *he has got four weeks of oil and nobody has asked him about the fifth.*

"Ask him," said the clerk who was going up the bank, and she wrote it on the slip, and the slip said: the oil comes on a standing arrangement and the arrangement names a firm and the firm gave up its lease on the thirtieth of March and there is a man in a tin at the other end of the pipe who is not paid, and I want to know what you have been doing since March and who has been paying you.

And the trader wrote back four lines, and the fourth one is the sentence that is going up the bank in a satchel and into a public book on the third of this month.

> **I HAVE BEEN DRAWING IT AGAINST A CARD THAT NAMES A ROAD HOUSE THAT IS NOT THERE ANY MORE. I DID NOT KNOW THAT FOR THREE WEEKS. I HAVE BEEN PAYING OUT OF MY OWN TILL SINCE THE FIRST OF MAY AND I AM NOT COMPLAINING AND I WOULD LIKE IT SAID THAT NOBODY ASKED ME EITHER. THE CARD WANTS A FIRM'S NAME AND I AM NOT A FIRM AND I AM FIFTY-ONE.**

And a rate is a thing that is on you, and a debt is a thing that is on somebody else.

The third of this month is the day a card at a chandler's on the lower terrace stopped naming a road house and started naming a person, and

The finding entered is that **Nine years of an engine's oil were bought against a name that stopped existing in March, and the woman who kept supplying it is the only person in this district who has been keeping nine feet of water where it would otherwise be fourteen out of her own pocket, and the man who puts his hand on the pipe at six every evening is sixty-eight and is given what is left in a tin, and the sheet on the trestle table has none of that on it and the toll board at the ninth place has it in a margin in a signalman's hand.**

The man himself was asked, on the second of this month, in a room in Alder Reach, and he said no for about nine minutes and then said yes, and the nine minutes are in the book, and the reason for the no is the best thing anybody said about that engine in nine years.

"You are asking me to put my name on a card at a chandler's," he said, "and I have been at that engine since the flood year and my name has never been on anything for it, and there is a reason for that which is that a man who is named can be sent for. In nine years nobody has ever sent for Gilman Trew. **You are asking me to become findable. Findable is what a person is when somebody wants something from him, and I have wanted nothing from anybody in nine years except oil.**"

"And you said yes," said the man who sells nothing and has a use.

"I said yes at about the second hour, on the grounds that I have four weeks of oil and a trader who has been paying out of her own till, and one of those two things has to stop and it is not going to be the oil."

---

And the key to the case of Lot 17 turned twice in the month before this one, and the notice board under the two lapses has four lines under it now, and the fourth was not written by anybody who can be found, and it is nine words long and it is on the wall in chalk and it will be there in a year.

A rotation is only real when it turns. A board that asks at the end of a term has not asked in time. A board that cannot ask at all and leaves it to a crowd is a board whose rotation is a weather. A yard asked and a yard got a yes, and a yard is not a board.

> **AND A CLERK WENT UP A BANK AND ASKED TWO WOMEN A QUESTION AND GOT TWO YESES AND CAME BACK.**

And the first page of standing failures now has three of the four on it and not the fourth, and the registrar's line under all four is the same nine words she wrote on the first of May: **a failure that is entered is not fixed, it is known.** And the difference is that one of the four has been given a date in public, on a board, in a street, by somebody who was not asked to enter it, and the other three have not, and the entry says that a standing failure with a date on it in a public place is not less of a failure and is a different kind of known.

---

**And a man and a woman stood at opposite ends of a trestle table on the second of this month and agreed on one thing in about eleven seconds and neither of them said a word about anything else, and it is in the book, because everything is in the book, and it is three lines long and it is in a registrar's hand and it is the only entry in the public lot book in nine weeks that is about the two of them.**

She read the sheet before it was allowed off the trestle table, because it makes a public claim, and she refused one sentence on it and let the other eleven hundred and forty through.

"You have written *the service is assured* in the foot of this," she said. "Take it out. **A timetable is a list of days on which a thing has not happened yet, and a sheet that says a service is assured is a sheet that has been wrong before it has been printed.**"

"You have not written that."

"You have written it in your own book on the nineteenth of March and you have carried the sentence into the foot of a printed sheet without looking at it, and I want it entered that you said you had not written it, and that you then read the line again for about four seconds and said nothing, and that I am the registrar and this is my instrument and I am striking it out of the printing and not out of your book."

"Then strike it out of the printing," he said.

And the reader at the counter wrote down, of her own accord, in the counter's own book of read-alouds, that a slate had gone past in a kitchen under the Ward Nine tram line on the morning of the second of May and again on the morning of the third of this month, and that five people had told her about the first one and one about the second, and that she is still not going to ask, and that not asked is in the count, and that the count is the only reason any of the rest of it is honest.

---

**And the last two things in this month's book are a number and a morning, and they are on the third of this month, and the number is a train and the morning is a man with a lamp in a hut.**

The train on the siding arrived on the eleventh of February and on the third of this month it had stood **a hundred and twelve days**, and the figure is in a boy's hand in an index, and beside it there are now four dates on one line, and nobody has put them in a column. The twenty-ninth of April, asked. The twenty-fifth of May, when a hut in an office's name came to the end of a month an office renewed by itself. The twenty-ninth of May, when a clerk went up a bank. **And the twenty-ninth of next month, and there is nobody in Alder Reach who has been given the job of going up that bank, and the clerk has said in a room that it will not be her, and a clerk can be replaced, and that is the point of her.**

And at about six in the morning on the third of this month, in a hut nine miles west, a man of thirty-nine who had been there thirty-nine days on a term that ended on the twenty-fifth of May said a nine-word sentence to himself in an empty hut, and got it right the first time, and wrote it on a sheet of paper on the desk in the same hand he writes the date in, and the date is the third of this month and the word is *held*, and under it he wrote, again, that he does not know whether it is the correct word.

**And the fourth bearer is still not named, and the iron is still on a bench four hours up the bank with a boy of nineteen beside it who is not anybody's keeper, and a sheet came off a press in Alder Reach in the first week of this month with a day on it and an empty column in the middle of it and a name at the top of it that nobody in the basin can find, and the day it names is a day on which nothing is going to happen yet, and the order of those things is the finding, and the finding is in that order and not in the other one.**
