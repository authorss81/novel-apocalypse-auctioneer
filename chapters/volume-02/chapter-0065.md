# Chapter 65: A Mark May Not Carry a Job

Orren Pike had the index of marks open on a kitchen table under the Ward Nine tram line at about six in the morning on the twenty-eight of this month, and it is not a large document. It is eleven leaves in a fair copy with a piece of tape down the middle of the spine, and it is the only piece of administration anybody in this district has ever asked for, and it was asked for by a clerk in the third week of the month before last because a book of marks was a book in which a person could be found twice, and a person found twice in this district is a person who has been made into an officer by accident.

**A mark is a person's refusal of a name. A mark is not a person. It may not be used as a bearer, a holder, a witness, a responsible party, or a person who is asked.** Nessa Pell reads the index aloud when anybody asks her to and the reason it exists is in the entry at the front of the first leaf, which is that a mark is a decision somebody made about themselves at some cost, and the only honest thing a book can do with a decision like that is to hold it still.

**And a mark may not carry a job.** That is the fifth rule and it is the one Fen Ambrose fought for in a yard of about six hundred people in the second week of last month, and it is the reason no protected person in this district has ever been made an officer by an accident, and the boy of seventeen who is paid nothing had found the fifth rule in about a minute and a half on the morning of the twenty-eighth because somebody had finally asked him a question about it.

"Nobody asked me," said Orren Pike. "**Nessa Pell did. Yesterday afternoon, at the counter, over the back of a queue, and she asked me whether a mark can hold a stopping order, and I said I did not know and would find out.**"

"And how long did it take you?"

"**A minute and a half, and about eleven minutes of standing in a kitchen reading my own handwriting,**" he said, "**and I have been paid nothing for nine weeks and I want it entered that the finding is dated by whoever asked the question and not by whoever found it.**"

---

**The question was this: the west end of this line is to be walked on the twenty-ninth, and the west end is a place where something has to stop, and a place where something stops needs somebody to stop it, and that is a job, and the district's own rule says a mark may not carry a job, and the boundary post between the eighth place and the ninth place is a mark.**

**And the fourth person on the walk is a mark, and the clause she dictated in a yard on the twenty-first is a job.**

That is the whole of the twenty-eighth of this month up to about eight in the morning, and it was found in a kitchen under a tram line by a copy clerk of seventeen who is not Adrian Vale's apprentice and is not going to be one, and Adrian Vale was not there, and Nessa Pell was not there, and neither of them had thought of it.

Orren Pike wrote it out on a sheet of his own and took it to Lot 17 at about half past eight and put it on the crate, and about sixty people were there because the walk sheet had been up two days.

> **THE FIFTH RULE OF THE INDEX: A MARK MAY NOT CARRY A JOB.**
>
> **AND THE TWO THINGS IT BLOCKS.**
>
> **ONE. THE BOUNDARY. THE WEST END OF THIS LINE IS A PLACE WHERE A THING STOPS AND SOMEBODY HAS TO STOP IT AND SOMEBODY HOLDING A STOP IS A JOB. THE POST BETWEEN THE EIGHTH PLACE AND THE NINTH PLACE IS A MARK. A MARK MAY NOT CARRY A JOB. THEREFORE THE POST CANNOT HOLD THE BOUNDARY, AND IT HAS BEEN HOLDING IT FOR FORTY ODD YEARS, AND NOBODY NOTICED, BECAUSE THE ONLY JOB IT WAS HOLDING WAS A NUMBER AND A NUMBER IS NOT A JOB.**
>
> **TWO. THE CLAUSE. THE FOURTH PERSON OF THE FOUR ON THE WALK HAS DICTATED A CLAUSE AND IT IS ON THE SECOND VERSION OF THE SHEET AND IT SAYS THAT THE PERSON WHO SAYS THE WORD AT THE NINTH PLACE IS A PERSON, AND THE WORD IS A WORD, AND WHOEVER HOLDS THE THING THAT DAY HAS TO STOP AND WAIT. THE FOURTH PERSON IS A MARK. HOLDING THE THING THAT DAY IS A JOB. A MARK MAY NOT CARRY A JOB. THEREFORE, ON THE FIFTH RULE, THE CLAUSE CANNOT BE PERFORMED BY THE ONLY PERSON WHO WROTE IT.**

There was a silence in that yard of about eleven seconds and then the woman who could read from the back said, "**Then it was a week of work.**"

"**It is not a week of work,**" said Orren Pike. "**It is a week of work by about four people, and the reason is that nobody has ever asked whether the district's own rules are any good, because the rules have only ever been used to stop people doing things and not once to stop the district doing something, and that is the first time in five weeks and it has been found by a person nobody pays.**

"**I am not complaining and I want it entered that I am not complaining, and I have a sheet of eleven lines in my coat and nobody has offered me a job, and this is the first week of my working life in which anybody has used one of my pages before I have got it into a book.**"

Nessa Pell read the two findings out twice, and the second time she read them in the voice she uses for tolls, which is slower, and about a hundred and forty people heard them on the twenty-eighth and about four hundred heard them on the twenty-ninth, and **the clause is on the sheet and the clause is not struck, and the fifth rule is on the wall and the fifth rule is not struck either, and what came out of it is on eleven boards in words that four people agreed on and one of the four is a woman who is not named.**

---

**And the resolution is the narrowest thing this district has built in five weeks, and it is narrow enough that the tenth hand of the signal crew said out loud that the narrowness was the difficulty.**

They sat on the crate at about nine in the morning, all four of them, in daylight, and the tenth hand did most of the talking because the tenth hand is the man who is allowed to argue a rule, having argued one once, in a yard, in the rain, and it held, and a rule is a rule because it was argued in public by somebody who is not the man who wrote it, and once it has been argued and held its author may not argue it again and must use it. **He may not argue Fen Ambrose's fifth rule, and he is not going to, and what he does instead is put the two halves of it next to each other and read them out and let the room find the gap, which took about nine minutes and which nobody could have done faster.**

> **A MARK IS A PERSON'S REFUSAL OF A NAME. A MARK MAY NOT CARRY A JOB.**
>
> **AND THE FINDING, WHICH IS FEN AMBROSE'S AND NOT MINE AND I AM ENTERING IT AS HERS BECAUSE SHE WROTE THE RULE AND I AM USING IT: A JOB IS SOMETHING SOMEBODY GIVES YOU. YOU CAN BE GIVEN A JOB AND YOU CAN REFUSE A JOB AND IF YOU REFUSE A JOB YOU DO NOT HAVE ONE AND NOBODY HAS GIVEN YOU ONE. THE FIFTH RULE STOPS A MARK FROM BEING MADE INTO AN OFFICER. IT DOES NOT STOP A PERSON FROM SAYING NO, AND SAYING NO IS NOT A JOB, AND A PERSON WHO SAYS NO TO A TRAIN HAS NOT BEEN GIVEN THE TRAIN.**
>
> **SO THE CLAUSE IS PERFORMABLE. THE CLAUSE HAS ALWAYS BEEN PERFORMABLE. IT WAS NEVER A JOB AND THE THING THAT MADE IT LOOK LIKE A JOB IS THAT IT SAYS WHOEVER HOLDS THE THING THAT DAY HAS TO STOP AND WAIT, AND THAT HALF IS NOT HERS. THAT HALF IS THE LEVER. AND THE LEVER IS AN OBJECT AND A MARK MAY NOT CARRY AN OBJECT EITHER, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED HER TO CARRY IT.**

"That is the whole of it," said the tenth hand, "**and it is about nine inches wide, and if I put my weight on it it breaks, and I am telling you that it is nine inches wide on the day I found it and not on the day somebody puts their weight on it.**

"**And the other half is not solved at all. The boundary is a job. There is no way round that and I have been walking the line for a week trying to find one.**"

---

**And they went up the bank at about half past ten in the morning on the twenty-eighth with two questions and no forms, and there were two people to ask, and both of them were asked in front of the others, and both of them were asked what the job was before they were asked whether they wanted it.**

The keeper said yes in about four seconds, without putting down the pencil.

"**I have held the only pair on this line for eleven years and nobody has ever asked me whether I would hold a second thing, and the answer is yes, and the answer has been yes the whole time and the reason nobody knew is that nobody ever asked.**"

"And what is the job?"

"**That is the question I would have liked somebody to ask eleven years ago,**" said Sef Anyard, "**and I am going to answer it properly and then I am going to tell you what it is not.**

"**The job is this. There is a line on the ground at the west end of that siding, and it is not a fence and it is not a wall and it does not stop anything. It says: this is as far as anybody sends anything on this line today. And the reason it has to be a line and not a word is that a word is in Alder Reach on a sheet in a yard and the train is nine miles west, and there is no wire between the two.**

"So the job is: if anybody at all asks anybody at all to move anything past that line, one of two people says the word, and everything on this line stops, and waits, until somebody comes and looks. **And I have been doing that for eleven years without a line and without being asked, and I did it about nine times a year and never wrote it down, and if you put a chalk line on the ground at the end of the siding then I will do it about nine times a year with something to point at.**"

"**And that is the whole of it and I want the term stated and I want it short.**"

"The term is the boundary's," said Ivo Stenn. "It is not the board's. The board cannot name you."

"**Then somebody has to ask me and write down what I said and the hour,**" said Sef Anyard, "**and I have noticed that is the third time in eleven years that anybody has said that sentence to me and the third time is the twenty-eighth of this month, and put it in the book that the three of those sentences are the reason I have stayed.**"

---

**And the second person was the woman at the column, and she was asked in front of the other three, and she said yes, and the yes cost her something she named before she said it, and the naming is the reason the two of them are the two of them and not the keeper and the woman of forty-five.**

She is about fifty. She has had the water off that column for eleven years and filled two pails a day off the overflow with a boy of about nine, and she has a boy of nine and a husband who is the fitter's father-in-law and a man of seventy who looks at the wall behind the train every evening and has seen a bank go before and it went in a night, and **she is the only person at the ninth place who is findable whether she is in a book or not, because four men a week from a road house in Alder Reach have stood on that hard ground for nine years and she has spoken to every one of them about the water.**

"He is putting a line on the ground," said the tenth hand, "**and the line says that today nothing goes past it, and two people have to be able to say the word, and the keeper has said yes, and I am asking you whether you would say yes.**"

"I have a question and it is not about the line."

"Ask it."

"**What happens to the water?**"

Nobody had thought about the water. It is a column with a tap and a lock and a brass plate on it, and the lock is a road house lock, and the meter is a road house meter, and the water has been drawn off twice a week by men the road house pays, and **if a line goes on the ground at the end of that siding, then a man who is not on the line cannot come up the bank and fill two pails, and the woman on that bank has thirty-one people on it and there is no other water within two miles.**

That is the finding of the twenty-eighth of this month and it is the first time in this volume that anybody has put a thing on the ground and discovered that it has a use which is not on the sheet, and it is in the book in Ivo Stenn's own hand with the finding underneath it.

> **A LINE ON THE GROUND AT THE WEST END OF A SIDING, SAYING THAT NOTHING GOES PAST IT TODAY, TAKES THE WATER OFF WITH IT, BECAUSE THE ONLY WAY UP THAT BANK IS THROUGH THE END OF THE SIDING AND THE PACE IS BEHIND THE PACE. FOUR MEN A WEEK FROM THE NEEDLE ROAD HOUSE HAVE FILLED AT THAT COLUMN FOR NINE YEARS AND THE METERS ARE IN A BOOK IN ALDER REACH AND THE TOTAL IS THIRTY-SIX COPPERS AND THE COLUMN IS THE ONLY WATER FOR TWO MILES.**
>
> **AND THEREFORE THE LINE CANNOT BE AT THE END OF THE SIDING. THE LINE IS PUT SIXTY YARDS FURTHER WEST, PAST THE COLUMN, WHERE THE GROUND STOPS BEING GOOD GROUND AND STARTS BEING THE EMBANKMENT SHOULDER WITH A DROP OF NINE FEET OFF IT ON THE RIVER SIDE. WHICH IS WHERE THE TRAIN CANNOT STAND AND WHERE A MAN CAN WALK AND WHERE THE ONLY THING THAT IS TAKEN AWAY BY PUTTING A LINE ON IT IS A PLACE WHERE A TRAIN COULD BE PUT IF SOMEBODY PUT ONE THERE.**

**And the woman at the column said yes after that, and she said it in about nine seconds, and then she said the thing that is in the book in her words because the standing practice is that the person is in the form.**

"**I will say the word. And I want it written down that I have said yes to a thing that will make me findable, and here is the whole of what findable means and I have thought about it since a quarter past ten this morning and it is this.**

"**That column is a road house column and it is metred to a road house and there is a number on the brass and a man comes up here twice a week for water. And not one of those men has ever asked me a question about anything except the water, in nine years, and I have noticed that and I have not minded it, and that is what I have.**

"**So if my name goes in a book in Alder Reach then in four years there will be a man up here who has read it. And he will be polite. And he will ask me how I am, and I will say thank you, and inside a year he will be asking about the column, and inside two he will be asking about the siding, and there is nothing in the world I can do about that except not be in a book. And I have decided to be in a book.**"

"Why?" said the tenth hand, who had not expected to be asked it.

"**Because I have been fifty years old for two months and I have never once in my life been findable on purpose,**" she said, "**and I find out that I would rather be findable and have it be done to me than not findable and have it be done to me anyway.**"

---

**And the fourth person was asked last, at about twenty past two in the afternoon on the twenty-eighth, at the top of the bank with the light going, and she said no, and her reason is the sentence this district is going to be reading for a year or more, and nobody argued with it and it is in the book in her words and it is not struck.**

"**I have said the word on this bank to four people and I have said it in your yard to four hundred and I have never given one of them my name, and you are not asking me for my name, and I want that said first, because I know what you are doing and I am not against it.**

"**You have four people on a walk and three of them are in books. Tarin Kest. Ivo Stenn. The tenth hand, who is a seat and not a person and who has told me that every morning for a week. And me. And you want a second pair of hands at a line on the ground and you have got a keeper and you have got a woman of fifty with a boy of nine, and either of those two would be a better answer than me, and you know that, because the woman of fifty came down here with me on the second and she is the only person on this bank I would call to my house.**

"**So say it and I will say the rest.**"

He said it.

"**No. And here is the reason and it took me from one o'clock to two.**

"**I am forty-five. I have got no children grown and none at all. I have a house with one room in it that somebody shares. And every name in this book is a thing that can be pointed at. Tarin Kest's name points at a yard with nineteen vehicles in it. His name points at a woman with a canvas bag who cannot say what was in it. The tenth hand's name points at a page with nine hundred and six movements on it and two hundred and eleven of them are his.**

"**And a name with nothing behind it is a name a man can use. It is the cheapest thing I own and I have been the cheapest thing in every room I have been in since I was nineteen, and if I put my name on a line on the ground then in four years somebody is going to come up this bank and use me, and there will be no house behind me to send them to.**

"**The woman of fifty has a boy of nine. You put her name down and if a man comes up here she can send him to the boy. That is what a name is. It is not a handle for you to find me with. It is a door for me to send him to.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**I said the word on the fifteenth and I said it in your yard and it is on a sheet and it is the only clause in that book that I wrote, and I will say it again on the ballast tomorrow and I will say it four times if it is needed. And I will not hold the line. Those are two different things and I have worked out which one I am doing and it has taken me three hours and a half.**"

---

**And the key of Lot 17 turned on the twenty-eighth at about four in the afternoon, for the second time in a month, and the board has still no power to ask anybody, and so the yard asked, and the yard has got good at asking in about nine days.**

Oswin Tace is forty-one and he holds lamps on the Ward Nine line and has never taken a count of people in his life, only of lamps, and he has asked twice that it be said there as well as here that he counts lamps, and it is said there and here, and it is in the book above his name.

"You have a case in that yard with a cracked plate in it and a key that has been standing open for two days, and I understand that nobody was named, and I understand that is because your board cannot ask a man whether he will do a job." He put the satchel down on the table. "**I have a qualification and it is not a good one and I am going to say it before anybody offers it to me.**

"**I count lamps. I have counted lamps since I was nineteen and I have never counted anything else and I am not going to start on a Saturday.**

"**And I do not know the hours you people keep, and if I open that case and there is nobody in the room then I am going to write down that nobody came, in a book, with the date on it, and I am not going to say the case was open or that anybody was careless. I am going to write that nobody came.**"

Nessa Pell said, "**That is the standing rule and it is written down, and you will be the fourth holder of that key in a month and the first one to have said it out loud before anybody asked.**"

"**Then I will hold it,**" said Oswin Tace. "**From the twenty-eighth, and I am a man and not a seat, and if you want to know why I am saying yes: my mother was on that stair when a bridge went in the flood year and about nine people stood on a ramp for a night because nobody came, and it is not a lamp, and I have thought about it for a fortnight, and I am not going to be the reason a book has nothing in it.**"

The board set the term at about half past four, all four seats named aloud, and the term was **four days, from the twenty-eighth, expiring at midnight on the first of the next month**, and the renewal is on the thirtieth, and **the thirtieth is the day after four people have walked five miles of railway and the day a mechanic gives up a lease, and the board entered that it had not noticed either of those when it set the term, and that this was the third thing in a month it had set without asking anybody whether it collided with anything.**
