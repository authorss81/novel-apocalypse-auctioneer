# Chapter 81: Three Boards

At about ten in the morning on the fourth of this month Wren Ostler put a piece of chalk against a board in the counting room of the ward market and drew a rectangle on it, and the first thing she said was a complaint about the width.

"**This is wrong,**" she said, "and I have drawn it twice and thrown it away twice and I am telling you what is wrong before I draw it a third time, because the third one is going to be a thing in this district and not a thing in a counting room."

She is the market's second clerk. She has been six years at a counter and in no book until somebody asked her, in the second week of last month, whether she had a name she was willing to have written in one, and she gave it with three conditions and a disclaimer, and it is in the book spelled with an e on the end. She has held the key to the public case at Lot 17 twice, for four days each time, and the first term was a lapse and the second one was not.

"The middle is too wide," she said.

"I know," said the tenth hand of the signal crew.

"**You have not looked at it. It is too wide, and I want it said that I said so first,** because in about three weeks there is going to be a man standing in front of one of these saying a column printed narrow looks like a column that has been kept quiet, and he will be right about the sheet he printed and wrong about the reason, and I would like the width on the record before either of those men turns up."

She is right about the width. The column is four feet wide and the board is nine feet long, and the other five feet are the heading at the top and the keeper's line and the date and one line for a sentence, and the proportions were argued about for about half an hour and came out where she put them.

---

**And what a toll board is, is a place to write down what a toll is, and nobody in this district can state the amount of one, and so the middle of the board is empty on purpose, and the emptiness is printed and not left, and the sentence printed underneath it is the most important thing on the thing.**

It is nine feet by three feet, on two posts, tarred at the foot so the wet does not get into the end grain. Across the top, in letters three inches high, a heading. Down the middle, a column four feet wide, ruled in pencil only at the two ends, with nothing between them. Along the bottom, a line for a keeper's name, a line for a date, and a line that anybody who stands in front of it may write one sentence on, and nobody will be asked to explain the sentence.

It is not the fifth thing. That is on the second board in the same size of type as the heading.

> **THIS IS NOT A WAY OF SAYING THAT A TOLL LANDS ON A PLACE. THIS DISTRICT HAS NO NAME FOR THAT AND NOBODY HAS ASKED IT TO BE NAMED. A BOARD IS A PLACE TO WRITE DOWN WHAT A TOLL IS. A PLACE TO WRITE DOWN WHAT A TOLL IS CANNOT STATE WHAT A TOLL IS. A BOARD CANNOT ASK THE OBJECT ANYTHING AND THE OBJECT CANNOT BE WRITTEN ON, AND THE DAY SOMEBODY PRINTS A NUMBER IN THAT COLUMN THE COLUMN IS A LIE AND THE DATE ON IT WILL BE THE DATE THE LIE WENT IN.**

It came from a reader, and the reader did not have the idea, and the finding of the fourth of this month in the counter's own book of read-alouds is that the instrument came out of the only job in this district which is to be handed a thing by a stranger and to say out loud what is written on it.

Rutt is about fifty. She was a signal clerk before her shoulder went and she has read at that counter four days a week at eleven coppers a quarter for about a year, and the term has no clause for a shoulder, and she has walked nine miles on it twice and told nobody about either time.

---

On the second of this month a fifth stranger came to that counter and asked one question, which was whether the sheet on the wall said everything about the railway.

"I said it says everything anybody in this district can state, and that the part it cannot state is printed on it in the middle at full width with the reason printed inside the column, which is the only sentence on that sheet I would stand behind in a market."

"And did he understand that," said somebody.

"He read the middle for about a minute and a half, which is longer than anybody has ever stood at that counter, and then he said **he had come four miles to be told that something was missing and that he had been told so in the correct type,** and he gave me a penny and I would not take it and he would not have it."

"Then it worked," said the tenth hand.

"**It worked on the day, and it will have stopped working by about the ninth, and that is the whole of what I know about it and I have known it since the second of March and nobody asked me.** **A sheet can be taken cold by anybody in this basin and about four hundred of them took one cold in the first week it was on that wall. A board cannot be taken cold, because a board is in a place, and a place is the only thing in this district that has ever been able to make anybody stand still.**"

She gave that account four times over two days to four different people without once being asked for it twice, and the minute says that she was asked once and answered twice, and that both of those facts were entered.

---

**And at about half past eleven the thirty of us who were in that room walked out of the counting room and up a street to a signal cabin, and the walk is eleven minutes and it is the shortest walk anybody in this district takes to reach anything that is on a railway.**

The cabin at the east end is a brick hut about eight feet by eleven, with a door that has been painted nine times and a window facing the line. Inside it there is a stool, a lamp with a three-day wick, a hook, a shelf with the line book on it, and about nine inches of standing room. The line book is a book of signals: a place, a time, a direction, and a lever. It is kept by hand and it has nine hundred and six movements in it since the flood year, and a hundred and forty-one of those movements name the ninth place as the origin held.

"This is the end of the line, and it is the only end of the line that anybody in this city can get to without a changeover," said the tenth hand. "**If a board is going up anywhere it goes up here first, and if it does not go up here then it is not a public thing, it is a thing two people told each other about.**"

Wren Ostler stood in the doorway of that cabin for about four minutes and did not go in past the stool, and then she said the thing that turned an argument into a decision.

"I have been behind that counter for six years, and in six years I have never once been inside this cabin, and I could tell you what is on every shelf in the counting room and I could not have told you there was a book in here until the second of last month. **I am not complaining about that.** I am saying that the east end of this railway is eleven minutes from my counter and four hours from the ninth place, and if the three places this district is going to write things on are the east end, the fourth and the ninth, then one of them is in walking distance of the only counter in this city where a stranger can be handed a sheet and a sentence and told the truth about both."

---

The argument about which three places went on for twenty-two minutes and produced three and not twelve, and the reason it is three and not twelve is the reason a mark may not carry a job, arrived at in about ninety seconds by a clerk who had not been in the room when that rule was made.

The fourth place. The ninth place. The east end.

**The sixth place is a drain and has been going onto the ballast since before the Long Fracture. The eighth place is a curve with a shoulder four feet wide and a drop of nine feet off it on the river side, and a man who has to hold something at the eighth place has to stand somewhere.** Neither is used. Both reasons are in the book and neither is struck.

"You have described three places and no people," said a man of about thirty at the back, who sells nothing and has a use.

"We have described three places and two people," said Wren Ostler, "and one of the two is a man in a hut nine miles west and the other is a clerk at a counter eleven minutes from the east end, and I have said yes to mine and I have not asked anybody else. **This room has no power to make a person stand at a board. Neither does any room in this city.** If you want four places on a board you will get four places on a board, and in about two years somebody will be asked to stand at the fourth one in a hurry because somebody in an office filled in a space on a form."

"Then who is the third keeper."

"**I do not know, and nobody in this room is going to find out today, and the day somebody does find out I want it written down that the finding was not done in a room like this one.**"

---

**And the wall was used at about one o'clock in the afternoon, and it took about nine minutes, and nobody re-derived it, and the finding is that a method that is written down on a wall is not a method, it is a rule, and the difference between those two things is that a rule is cheaper.**

The wall in the counting room carries the five things in letters a foot high. The sixth thing has no name on it and is not a power. The reading-back is not on the wall, because you cannot describe to a person in advance what it is to hear your own no come back at you in a clerk's voice.

The first thing is what the job is, in sentences somebody who has never done it can understand and not in the word anybody else would use. **You keep a board.** Nine feet by three, tarred at the foot. People come and read it and some of them write one sentence on the bottom line. You wipe the bottom line when it is wet and you keep the date. You do not decide anything. There is no number in the middle of it and there is not going to be one, and if a person asks you what the toll is, you say that nobody in this district can state it, and **the column is printed at full width so that they can see it is empty on purpose.**

The second thing is how long, with an hour on it. A month, in weeks, with the week named on the board.

The third thing is how you say no, and that no reason is owed and nobody asks afterwards. You say no by not saying yes.

The fourth thing is who answers for you while you are saying it, which two people answered in about a second each the last two times it was asked and which nobody in this district has ever had to think about.

The fifth thing is where you stand when you answer: **in front of it, in daylight, at the end somebody can walk to.**

---

The second keeper was asked properly on the afternoon of the fourth, and he was the first person this district has ever asked a question of in that method, and he was asked by a man who is not an officer of anything and cannot be paid, and it took about four minutes and eleven words to get the answer and nine words to get the reason.

His name is Corvin Sallow. He is thirty-nine and he is a signalman of the bridge administration and he went up the bank on the twenty-fifth of last month and is sleeping in a hut, and his term ends on the twenty-fifth of this month, and the office that sent him renews him or does not, and has not asked him, and he has not asked to be asked.

"Yes," he said. "And I want the reason on the board in my words, because I have been the reason two people gave a no this month and I am not going to be the reason a third person gives one.

"**Keeping a board is not holding a pair. Nothing is charged to the person who keeps it. Nobody can be sent to a board, and I cannot post a security on a board, and I cannot close anything that happens at a board, and if the only thing at the board is a piece of chalk then I am the keeper of a piece of chalk.**"

"Then you are a bearer," said somebody at the back.

"I am not anything. I have said that already in a different room in front of a different set of people and I am not going to say it twice in one month. **A bearer is a person and an office is not a person and a slot is not a person and a line of stones is not a person. This is none of those four things. This is a board.**"

---

The first keeper was asked before the second, on the morning of the fourth, and she said yes in about four seconds and then said nine words, and the nine words are the reason the third keeper was left until the ninth of this month.

"Yes. And I am not a signal hand and I do not know what a pair is, and I have a counter and there is a queue at it, and I will do it in the hours the counter is shut and not in the hours it is open, and the board will say which hours. I am a person in a building and I am not going to pretend to anybody at that cabin that I am a woman who keeps a line."

"That is three conditions."

"**It is three conditions and a disclaimer, and I have given three conditions and a disclaimer in every room I have been in since the seventh of last month,** and if anybody wants them on the board they go on the board, and **the disclaimer is that I am the second clerk of this market and not the market, and if the market is ever asked about a board it is to say that a clerk keeps it.**"

---

The room then spent about eleven minutes failing to find the third keeper, and the failure is in the minute at length because the tenth hand insisted, and it is the first failure in nine weeks that is entered as a failure rather than as a refusal.

Three names were put forward. A lamp-man of forty-one who counts lamps and not signals and who asked everybody in this district what their job was in April and is the only man anybody knows of who walks the length of that line every week without being sent. A haulier who goes up the bank twice a week and who is the man the word stopped twice in ten days in March and who was not angry about it. And a man of about fifty who keeps a counter of oil and has stood in a doorway at the west end of this district for about four years and has never once been asked what he is for.

All three were talked about for eleven minutes and none was asked, and the reason is in the minute in the tenth hand's words: **we have spent four minutes finding out that we do not know what the job is, and the only two people in this district who can tell us are a clerk at a counter and a man in a hut, and neither of them has ever asked anybody anything, and the man at the west end has never been asked in four years and I am not going to be the man who goes and asks him in a room with thirty people in it.**

"You could go and ask him on your own," said the man of about thirty who sells nothing.

"I could," said the tenth hand, "and there is a boy of seventeen in this city who worked out in four minutes in a hut in April that if you ask somebody a question in front of a crowd, the true thing comes back to the wrong man, and in four years the story is about the man who went and asked. So the answer is no, and the answer is no in three weeks as well, and what it is instead is a person who goes up that bank once a month for her own reasons and hears what is said in four houses while she is there."

And the woman with the hook, who had not been asked anything and who was still standing at the back, said, "Then I will be the one who hears it, and I will not be asked, and you will find out what she said when somebody asks her properly in a room." And the minute says that this was entered, and that she was not thanked, and that she said beforehand that she did not want to be.

---

**And the cost of three boards is six coppers of lettering, and it is the first money anybody has ever taken out of the two hundred coppers a quarter that the ninth place owes, and the column that money came out of has not moved since the twenty-eighth of March.**

The lettering is Abel Troth, who is sixty-one, who writes signs for a living, who is in no roll in Alder Reach, and who charged three coppers a board and then took two. **He said the second price was a rate and not a favour, and that he would not be thanked for it,** and he was not thanked, and he set out the letters of the first board's heading with a stencil and a length of twine in about fifty minutes.

Two hundred coppers a quarter, four households, entered on a rate sheet on the twenty-eighth of March and not collected, because a rate is collected in the quarter in which it is voted and a market cannot go back. The finding of the fourth of this month, in Stenn's hand, is that the ninth place's rate is **the first money this district has spent at a place it did not have a rate sheet for four weeks ago**, and that it is six coppers, and that the sheet it came off says on its face that a rate sheet is a document that says a place is a place.

And under that, in Hessa Krell's own column, which was opened on the second of last month at her own asking and has one line in it and eight hundred and ninety-four coppers of carried printing gap on it: **A RATE IS A THING THAT IS ON YOU. I HAVE SAID THAT. THIS IS A RATE.** She is not thanked and is not going to be.

---

Adrian Vale said four sentences in that room on the fourth of this month and the minute records all four, and he did not draft anything and nobody asked him to, and the reason is that the thing being built is a board and a board is the one instrument in this district he is not needed for.

"**I have had nothing to do with this and I want it in the minute that I had nothing to do with it.** I have spent nine weeks being the man who writes the thing down, and today a woman who has been at a counter for six years designed it in about four minutes, and I did not contribute a sentence and I was not asked for one.

"And I would like it entered as a standing instruction that **the next time something like this comes up, the first question asked is not what I think of it.** There is going to be a second one of these inside a month and there is going to be a man in a good coat with an answer before anybody has asked a question at all, and the reason he will be first is that he is always first, and **the only instrument this district has against being first is to have a question asked before he arrives.**"

And the sixth thing, which has no name and is not a power, was done at about two o'clock in the afternoon by a woman of about thirty-eight who was at the back and whom nobody had asked to do anything, and her job is not in this district.

"The question is this and I am going to say it flat so that nobody has to guess at it later," she said. "**Three people are going to be asked whether they will keep a board at three places on a railway, and the boards are nine feet by three and the middle of them is empty, and the third person has not been found, and nobody in this room has any power over the third person.**"

She gave her name into the minute when the clerk asked her for it, which is **the first time in nine weeks that a person standing at the back of a room in Alder Reach has given a name to be written down,** and the entry says that she gave it and not that she was asked to.

**The first board goes up on the sixth, at the east end, in the open, eleven minutes' walk from the ward market counter, and the thing the clerk wrote at the bottom of the minute in her own hand, and asked to have it entered in the order she wrote it, is that a board with nothing in the middle of it is the first public document in this district that is going to be read by somebody who does not live here.**
