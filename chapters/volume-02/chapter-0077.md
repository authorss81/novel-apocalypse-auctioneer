# Chapter 77: The Safest and the Most Hated

At about two in the afternoon on the twenty-fifth of this month a man of about thirty-nine got down on his knees on the floor of the signal hut at the ninth place and put a folding rule under the left-hand end of the bench, and then said, to a woman of fifty-one standing in the doorway, that the floor was eleven inches out of level and that he was going to sleep on it.

He had been in the hut about a quarter of an hour. He had a case with a change of clothes in it and a bag of books and a lamp he had not lit and a sheet of his own in his own hand with a date at the top of it, and he was going to sleep there for a month.

He is called Corvin Sallow and he is a signalman of the bridge administration and he has been thirty-nine years old for two years. He kept the Harrow flood sheet for six years. He is the only man in this basin who can hold a pair and answer to an office, and on the twenty-first of this month he was sent up a bank by a man who had never spoken to him, and he has not been able to make that sentence true yet, and the entry says so in his own words.

---

**And the reason he is the man in that hut is four links long, and every link is a thing somebody wrote down for a different reason, and the registrar put the four in a row on the twenty-sixth and the finding underneath them is that nobody in this district has ever put a chain like this in a book on purpose, and that the only reason it is a chain and not four coincidences is that somebody at the end of it was willing to be sent.**

The first link is on the seventh of this month, in a book in the water office, in the hand of a bridge clerk of thirty-four, and it says that there is a boy asleep in a hut at the fourth place and that the bridge office has no use for the information. The second link is that the water office's book is copied twice a quarter and the copies go to a market that reads them. The third link is the empty room over the counting office on the second floor, with a bed in it and a window that shuts and a stove that wants cleaning, which has had nobody in it for two years because the office has had nobody to put in it. The fourth link is a lamp-man's pocket book, written on a viaduct on the eighteenth, which is in a sleeve in Alder Reach with a number on it, and the office's second copy of its own book came in on the nineteenth.

"**I read the room's own book first,**" said Corvin Sallow. "**I have been in that room two years and I have read it four times because there was nothing else, and on the nineteenth there was a line in it about a boy and a roof, and I went and read the sheet in Alder Reach because the second copy names a sleeve, and the sleeve is in a yard, and I asked a woman at a counter whether a stranger could look at a sleeve and she said yes and wrote my name on a piece of paper, which is in the book.**"

"**You asked for it,**" said Nessa Pell.

"**I asked for it,**" he said. "**Nobody sent me. That is in the piece of paper too. I asked to be sent and I was sent in about a day and a half, and I have been trying since the nineteenth to work out whether that is a thing this district does or a thing I did, and the answer I have got is that it is a thing I did, and the district only held the door.**"

---

What the office actually gave him is in a printed notice with four lines in it, and the yard has it, and the four lines are the whole of the bargain, and **there is not one word in them that anybody in this district likes except the man they were written for.**

The post is one month, from the twenty-fifth of April. **The post is renewed by the office and nobody is asked.** The bench, the lamp, the chalk and the oil are eleven coppers a quarter out of the Harrow works rate. The man is not paid out of that rate, and the notice says in its own third line that an administration does not put a man's wage in a rate, and the finding printed under the notice is that the third line is the whole of the district's five-week argument in one sentence and it is true and it is why the district lost the vote by seven to three on the twenty-fifth of last month, and that the man who wrote it wrote it because he agrees with it.

And the fourth line is the reason there was a scene in a yard.

**The administration considers that place unsafe.**

The town that came up the bank on the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth to look at a man in a hut was not a town that came to look at a hut. It came because a printed half-sheet with a date on it had gone onto eleven boards in Alder Reach and the River and Carriage notice board and the tram arch, and about ninety people took the bank, which is four hours each way with four changeovers in it, and came, and the finding of the twenty-sixth is that **they came to be angry and stayed to look and went back still angry, and all three of those are in the book.**

The first of them up the bank was a woman of about forty-nine who keeps a counter of dry goods and who was at the counting room on the twenty-fifth of last month and voted for the office, and she said the whole of it at the doorway in about eleven seconds and Rutt wrote it down.

"**I voted for that office eight weeks ago and I would vote for it again tomorrow,**" she said. "**It is a better paper than anything else anybody has put in front of this market and it has been in a room with a door on it for nine years and you are all waiting for somebody to have an accident so you can be proved right about it.**"

"And now it is in a hut," said the tenth hand.

"**And now it is in a hut,**" she said, "**on a bank nine miles west, at a place with no platform, at the end of a railway that has not carried a passenger in nine years, and it is going to be there a month at a time for as long as anybody can stand it, and the reason it is there is that the office says the place is unsafe, and the office is right, and I would like it read into the book that the most hated office in this district has put a man in the one hut on this line where the ground stops being good ground, and that nobody in this district would have, and that the district spent a month refusing to pay for exactly him.**"

Nobody argued with her and the tenth hand entered it and the entry says that it is the third finding in eleven weeks to arrive in a doorway, and that the three have all been said by women who are not trades and are on no roll, and that the district has still not found a way of putting that in a column, and that the reason it has not is that a column is a thing you can vote in and a doorway is not.

---

**And what the man in the hut did with the anger is the finding of the twenty-sixth, and it took him about four minutes, and he had it ready before he was asked for it, and the tenth hand wrote it down and the registrar asked to have it indexed.**

"**I have been told what this district thinks of my office since I came up here on the twenty-fourth to look at the floor,**" said Corvin Sallow, from the bench, with the folding rule still in his hand. "**I have been told it by about forty people and none of them was rude, which is worse. So I am going to say the four things I can say, and then I am going to go and wash, because there is one stove between three of us.**

"**One. This hut has a frame in it and the bracket is empty and the iron is four hours away on a bench in another hut, and this office considers that place unsafe, and this office considers this place unsafe, and there is no reading of that sentence in which the office is wrong.**"

"**Two. I am not here because the office has decided that a village needs a keeper. The office has decided that a section of a railway with no keeper on it is a section of a railway that cannot be reported, and that is a fact about iron and not about people, and I would like it entered that I heard that sentence for the first time in a bed room in Alder Reach and not from a man in this district.**"

"**Three. I do not know what the boundary is.**"

"**You will find out,**" said the woman of the column, who is the water, from the doorway of her own house eleven feet away, and who had not said anything for about an hour.

"**I will find out,**" he said, "**when somebody asks me, and not before, and I am entered here as not asked, and I would like the not asked to be in the book, because a man who is sent to a place is not a man who is owed that place.**"

"**It is in the book,**" said Nessa Pell, who had walked up the bank on the twenty-fourth with a sheet of her own and had been in the hut since six in the morning.

"And the fourth thing," said Corvin Sallow. "**The office renews me. Nobody asks. I know what that is; I have read the minute of the twenty-fifth of last month, I was told to read it before I came, and the finding in it about a rotation that is a weather is correct, and it applies to me, and I am not going to stand in a doorway and pretend it does not.**"

---

**And then Orren Pike did in about four minutes what the tenth hand has said three times in seven weeks that he is the only instrument in this district for, which is to find the thing that is being asked for, and he asked for it out loud in a hut with the door open, and the man in the hut answered before anybody had finished the sentence.**

The published draft has four bearers at the fourth, the sixth, the eighth and the ninth. The fourth entry is a slot. The sixth place is a drain and the eighth place is a curve with a shoulder four feet wide and a nine-foot drop, and there is nowhere at the eighth place to stand.

A bearer is a person who can hold a signal and who has a place and an approach and a stated week.

"So you are three of the four things the eighth place needs," said Orren Pike. "**You can hold a pair. You have got a place, because you are standing in it. And you have got a stated week, because it is on a notice with a date on it that anybody can take off a wall. The only thing you have not got is somebody asking you, and nobody has got that, and the reason nobody has got that is that the board cannot name a person.**"

"**No,**" said Corvin Sallow.

The whole of the hut turned round.

"**No,**" he said, "**and I want it in the book that I said it before I was asked, and I want the reason in my words, and the reason is that I read your published draft on the twenty-fourth on the way up in a satchel because they make a bridge clerk read the day's papers, and I have read that page about nine times on the way up four changeovers, and page two of it is the standing line, and a standing line says a person who can hold a signal.**"

"**You can hold a signal.**"

"**I can hold a signal and I am an officer of the ninth ward of a river office,**" said Sallow, "**and if I stand at the eighth place with a pair in my hand then the standing of that lot is the standing of my office, and my office has a room over a counting office with a bed in it, and the bed has been empty for two years, and if the standing of a lot is the standing of a room with a bed in it then there is nobody standing there at all. There is an office standing there. And I have never in nineteen years known a public thing that survived an office finding out about it.**"

And then Orren Pike, who is seventeen, said the thing that made the finding official, and it is nine words long and it is the fourth shape on the tenth hand's page.

> **A BEARER IS A PERSON. AN OFFICE IS NOT A PERSON. A SLOT IS NOT A PERSON. A LINE OF STONES IS NOT A PERSON.**

And the sentence under it, in the tenth hand's hand, is that **the page has four shapes of the same hole on it now, and that he wrote the fifth one in a wet pocket book on a viaduct, and that he has stopped trying to name them, and that this is a change in him and is entered as a change and not as a finding.**

---

**And the twenty-sixth of April is the day the district found out that the safest and the most hated are the same office, and the finding is not a sentence anybody said, it is a count, and the count was taken by a reader at a counter who does not read figures and who was on her fourth day, and the finding is entered in her hand and is nine lines long.**

She read it out at the ward market counter at about six in the evening on the twenty-sixth to about sixty people and it took her about four minutes, and it is the first count in this district that is not about a rate, and the finding under it says so.

> **THE WARD MARKET REFUSED THE STANDING ORDER OF THE NINTH BY SEVEN TO THREE WITH ONE ABSTENTION, THE NINTH OF WHICH THE EIGHTH OF THE EIGHT PLACES ON PAGE ELEVEN IS HARROW.**
>
> **THE SAME OFFICE HAS PAID A HUNDRED AND EIGHTY COPPERS A QUARTER ON THE HARROW WORKS FOR NINE YEARS AND THE MARKET HAS READ THAT RETURN EVERY QUARTER FOR NINE YEARS.**
>
> **THE OFFICE HAS NOW PUT A MAN IN A HUT NINE MILES WEST, ON THE ONE SECTION OF THAT RAILWAY WITH NO KEEPER ON IT, AND CONSIDERS THE PLACE UNSAFE, AND WILL RENEW HIM WITHOUT ASKING.**
>
> **THE DISTRICT HAS SPENT A MONTH REFUSING TO PAY ELEVEN COPPERS A QUARTER FOR EIGHT POSTS ON THAT SAME SHEET, AND THE FIGURE WAS EIGHTY-EIGHT COPPERS A QUARTER AGAINST A TAKE OF TWO HUNDRED AND THOUSAND AND FIFTY, AND THE MARKET COULD PAY IT MORE THAN TWO THOUSAND TIMES OVER, AND IT VOTED NO.**
>
> **SO: THE SAFEST OFFICE IN THIS BASIN IS THE ONE NOBODY WOULD VOTE FOR, IT HAS BEEN PAYING FOR A PUMP ON A DEAD LINE SINCE THE FLOOD YEAR OUT OF A RIVER, IT HAS SENT A BETTER RAILWAYMAN THAN ANYBODY THE DISTRICT HAS TO A PLACE NOBODY WOULD NAME, AND THE ONLY REASON THE DISTRICT KNOWS ANY OF THIS IS A SEAT ON A SATCHEL IN ANOTHER OFFICE'S BOOK.**

And Selik Marne was at the rail at six o'clock on the twenty-fifth and at six o'clock on the twenty-sixth and said **that is not enough** on both, in his hat, to about a hundred and forty people, and the minute of the twenty-sixth records that the count of how many times he has said it was again not taken, and the reason is the tenth hand's, and the reason is the same one.

And then he said the rest of it, and the rest of it is in the book because the board asked him for it, and it took about four minutes, and he did not soften it.

"**I have put a man in a hut at a place I have called unsafe in a yard eleven times in eight years, and the district is astonished, and the district has been wrong about that for about a day and a half, and I would like the yard to understand that I am not a man who has changed his mind. I am a man who has been asked a question I could not answer with a sentence, and I have answered it with a man.**

"**And the man is not mine. He is a signalman and I did not choose him out of a room with a door on it. He asked to be sent. I have the paper and it is in the book and it has his name on it and not mine, and I would like that read out, because in about four years somebody is going to write that the ninth ward put a man in a hut, and the reason it is in the book with his name on it is that it is the only part of it that was anybody's choice.**"

And then he said the thing that is the finding of the whole of the twenty-sixth, and it is in his own words and the registrar asked for it in the same sentence she asked for the rest of it.

"**A limit checked by one man is a man and not a limit. I have been saying that at this rail for eight years and I have been right, and the same sentence with one word changed is the reason a man of mine cannot be one of four bearers at the eighth place, and I would like the board to enter that I did not notice the resemblance until the twenty-sixth of this month, at about half past six in the evening, on a step, on my way home.**"

---

**And the last two things on the twenty-sixth are small and both of them are the district's own, and neither of them was asked for.**

The first is that on the morning of the twenty-sixth Corvin Sallow walked the section, because he had been told to walk the section twice a day and he is a man who does what he is told, and he came back at about eight with a figure in a pocket book and asked the tenth hand what he should do with a place where the cess has gone soft.

He had measured it with a tape. It is **ninety-four yards**, and it is the twelfth, and it is the same place the keeper's twelve and the tenth hand's eleven have been arguing about since the twenty-ninth of last month, and nobody struck a number. **The keeper's figure is about ninety and the tenth hand's is about ninety and Sallow's is ninety-four, and the three are in the book side by side, and Orren Pike entered the fourth as a fourth and not as a correction, and the index now has twelve stalls in it and the line book has eleven, and the reason for the difference is that the line book is a book of signals.**

The standing instruction of the thirtieth of last month — that somebody walks every yard of this line and not the fourth place to the ninth — has been carried out for ninety-four yards by a bridge officer on his second morning, because he was told to walk the section twice a day, and nobody asked him to, and he is not going to be asked to, and **the finding is that a thing gets done in this district when it is somebody else's ordinary work, and that this is the second time in eleven weeks and both times it has been an accident.**

---

On the evening of the twenty-sixth, in the last hour of light, two women taught a man of thirty-nine the two things this district has that he did not have, and both of them made it a condition, and neither of the conditions was a condition of a term, and **the finding is that the district has now collected a shelfful of sentences that begin *and I would like it entered that*, and that nearly all of them are a refusal of a courtesy, and that one of them is an instruction.**

Sef Anyard did hers at the frame, with the empty bracket, with two fingers on the pale rectangle and off again, and it took about four minutes and she did not use a single word from an instrument.

"**There is one pair on this line,**" she said. "**It is in that box and it is a hundred years older than you are and it is not the office's and it is not the district's and it has never been anybody's except the man holding it. You will learn it in about a week because you are a signalman and signalmen are not slow. And I am going to ask you one thing about it and you will not know the answer for about a year.**

"**What is the answer.**"

"**Whether you would take it off me,**" she said. "**That is the answer and I have never had to say it out loud to anybody because there has never been a second person in this hut. I am not asking you to promise me anything. I am telling you that for eleven years this hut has had a thing in it that anybody could have taken, and the reason nobody took it is that nobody came. And now you have come and you are not going to take it, and in about a year you are going to be asked to leave, and on the day you are asked I am going to want to know the answer to the question I asked you tonight.**"

And the woman of the column did hers from her own doorway, with a pail in her hand, and hers was about nine words and it is the shortest instruction anybody has given anybody in this district in a month.

"**You will not say my word,**" she said.

"**I would not dream of—**"

"**You will not say it,**" she said, "**and I will tell you why, and it is because the word is mine and his, and it was said five times in ten days and every time it stopped a man with a barrow, and I liked it, and I did not tell them I liked it, and if an officer of an office says it then it is an order and a man with a barrow does not get to stand on a bank for a minute and a half and think.**"

"**Then I will not say it,**" said Corvin Sallow.

"**You will say it,**" she said, "**and not for a year, and not for the office, and here is how you say it and here is what it costs and both are mine to set. You will say it in front of me and I will decide whether you said it like a person or like an office, and if you say it like an office you do it again, and there is no third time, and I will not tell the tenth hand and I will not tell the reader and I will not write it down.**"

"**That is fair,**" said the tenth hand, from the bank.

"**It is not fair, it is mine,**" she said, "**and it is in your book or it is not, and you can put it in your book, and I would like it in your book, and I would like it in your book in the shape of a thing I have said and not in the shape of a thing I have permitted.**"

And the entry is in the book in those words, and the finding under it is that a condition which is not a term and not a price and not a signature and has no end on it is the fourth kind of thing this district has built, and that it is not a charter and it is not the fifth thing, and that **it cannot be ended by anybody, including the woman who made it, except by her saying so, and that the number of those now standing in this district is four, and three of them were said by women and one of them by a boy of nineteen, and not one of the four is in an office.**

And the second small thing is that at about four in the afternoon on the twenty-sixth, at the rail, a man of about forty-six from the counting room read out the twenty-ninth of this month to about twenty people and asked whether anybody in that yard knew what was going to happen to the west end of a railway on that day, and about eleven people said that the market had said it was the twenty-ninth and nothing else, and nobody knew that the market had not asked the two women who were holding the line, and that the two women had not been asked by anybody, and that the asking had to be done by somebody and it had not been done yet, and that it was **three days** away.
