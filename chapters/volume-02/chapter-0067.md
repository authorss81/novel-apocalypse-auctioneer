# Chapter 67: Two Names, and a Line on the Ground

The light goes off the embankment at half past four in this month, and at about ten minutes to four in the afternoon on the twenty-ninth there were four people standing on the good ground at the ninth place with a piece of chalk and a roll of tarred twine, and the train was behind them, and a man of seventy was sitting on the low wall behind the fourth house with his stick across his knees and his back to it.

The line was to go sixty yards west of the column. That had been worked out on the twenty-eighth by Ivo Stenn and a woman of fifty while they were standing at the tap, and the working out was a hundred yards for the water and sixty yards for the train, and the sixty was the number that mattered because a nine-vehicle siding is about a hundred and forty yards of track and the far end of it is about sixty yards from the column.

"So it is on the shoulder," said the tenth hand, "**and it is at the point where the cess opens out and the ballast comes up through it in three places, and it is forty feet from the drop.**"

"**And it is on the only hard ground a nine-vehicle train can stand on for two miles, and the man of seventy is sitting on the wall behind it.**"

"Yes."

**And Tarin Kest took the chalk out of the fourth person's hand and put it in his own, and then stood there with it, and about nine seconds went by, and he said the thing that is the whole of the twenty-ninth of this month and that the whole of the lease turns on, and it took him about forty seconds and it was not a confession.**

"I am not marking this," he said.

"You have it in your hand."

"**I have it in my hand because I have been carrying chalk in this coat for nineteen years, and I am not putting it on the ground, and I have thought about it for about a minute and a half and I have not been able to do it and I have had to say why out loud to three people instead of going and doing it.**

"**Because I signed the lease.** I signed a nine-year lease on this siding in the ninth year in about eleven minutes in a room in the ward market district when I was thirty-four and I had nineteen vehicles and nowhere to put them, and it expires in the fourth month of next year and I have not renewed it and I have not told the other side, and I have not told that woman by the column, and I have been carrying that since the ninth of this month and I said it on my own ballast before we were two miles out and nobody said anything for two miles.

"And if I put that line on this ground today then the ground has a leaseholder and a boundary and a man who marks it, and all three of those are the same man, and **the line says that nothing goes past it and the man who marks it is the man who signed the paper that put the thing past it there.** And you are all going to say that is fine because he has never done it. And I have never done it because I have not had the nerve. That is not a reason, and I am asking for it in the book that I said so."

"**Then who does it,**" said the tenth hand.

"**The two people who said yes yesterday, and neither of them owns an inch of it, and that is the only reason they are the right two and it is why I cannot be one of them.**"

---

**And that is the finding of the twenty-ninth, and it is the first instrument this district has built in six weeks that is worse than a case, and it took about four minutes to say and about six weeks to arrive at.**

The chalk did not go down for another forty minutes because the four of them tried every instrument the district owns and every one of them failed, in order, and the failures are in the book in the order they happened, and the first of them is the one that cost the district a fortnight in the middle of a month.

> **THE ATTEMPTS, IN ORDER, ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF THIS MONTH, AT ABOUT TEN PAST FOUR IN THE AFTERNOON, ON THE HARD GROUND AT THE NINTH PLACE, IN FRONT OF ABOUT TWENTY PEOPLE.**
>
> **ONE. A MARK. THE POST BETWEEN THE EIGHTH PLACE AND THE NINTH PLACE IS A MARK AND A MARK MAY NOT CARRY A JOB AND HOLDING A LINE IS A JOB. REFUSED BY THE FIFTH RULE, WHICH IS FEN AMBROSE'S, AND THE FIFTH RULE IS NOT STRUCK AND WAS NOT ARGUED WITH, BECAUSE THE PERSON WHO MAY ARGUE A RULE IS THE TENTH HAND OF THE SIGNAL CREW AND HE HAS ARGUED ONE RULE IN HIS LIFE AND IT WAS SELIK MARNE'S AND IT HELD.**
>
> **TWO. THE BOARD. THE BOARD OF THE PUBLIC LOT AT LOT 17 CAN SET A CUSTODY TERM AND PUBLISH A RESULT AND CALL A REVIEW AND UPHOLD A CHALLENGE IN PART AND REFUSE TO RECORD A CLOSING. IT CANNOT NAME A PERSON. IT CANNOT NAME A PERSON AND THE JOB IS TO BE NAMED TO SOMEBODY, SO THE BOARD CANNOT DO IT. AND THE BOARD CANNOT GET TO THIS GROUND IN ANY EVENT; IT IS NINE MILES AWAY AND EVERY ONE OF ITS FIVE POWERS IS ABOUT A THING IN ITS OWN BUILDING.**
>
> **THREE. THE LEASEHOLDER. REFUSED BY THE LEASEHOLDER, IN FRONT OF WITNESSES, AT TEN PAST FOUR, IN THE WORDS HE USED AND THEY ARE IN THE LEASEHOLDER'S OWN PAGE IN THE FILE — AND THE REASON HE GAVE IS IN HIS OWN HAND AND IT IS THAT A LINE ON THE GROUND IS THE FIRST THING HE HAS EVER PUT ON THAT VILLAGE'S GROUND AND HE HAS PUT A SIDING ON IT AND A METER ON IT AND NINE VEHICLES ON IT AND HE IS NOT PUTTING ANYTHING ELSE ON IT.**
>
> **FOUR. A ROAD HOUSE. REFUSED IN ADVANCE BY THE MAN WHO OWNS THE ROAD HOUSE, IN A YARD, IN THE WORDS THAT HE SAID NO IN ADVANCE AND WOULD LIKE IT IN THE BOOK THAT HE SAID IT BEFORE A TRAIN HAD BEEN LATE, AND THAT WAS THE NINTH OF THIS MONTH AND THE BOOK HAS IT.**
>
> **AND SO THE FOURTH ATTEMPT, WHICH WORKED, AND IT IS THE ONLY THING IN SIX WEEKS THAT HAS WORKED, AND IT IS SMALLER THAN ALL THE OTHERS.**

---

**And the fourth attempt is this, and it took about a minute and a half to say out loud, and the tenth hand wrote it on the page and then had to stop writing for a moment and went and stood at the cess and looked at the water for about a minute, and nobody followed him.**

**Two named people. A stated term. And a published entry.**

"**The job is two people,**" said the tenth hand, "**and it has to be two because one of them cannot be alone, and I want to give the reason for the two and not the reason for the one, because the reason for the one is that a keeper of a hut on a bank over a river cannot be the only person who can stop a train, and the reason for the two is that a person at a tap who says the word alone will be doing it alone by about the third time.**"

"And what is the term?"

"**From the twenty-ninth of this month to the twenty-ninth of next month. One calendar month. And it is renewable by nobody except by asking both of them again, in a room, with the asking and the hour and the answer entered, whatever the answer is.**" He wrote it. "**And the reason it is one month and not the four days this board has been setting on a key is that four days is a term for a drawer in a building that is about nine miles from here, and a month is the shortest term I can stand behind and still be at the north end of a viaduct about once.**

"And the published entry is not a courtesy. **It is the third of the three and it is the one that makes the other two real, and if it is not published then a line on the ground is a private arrangement between four people and a chalk mark, and a private arrangement is the thing this district spent five weeks learning about and it does not survive a stranger.**"

---

**And the woman at the column dictated her own half of it, and it took about nine minutes, and about twenty people of the ninth place stood about twelve feet off and heard every word of it, and the boy of nine heard all of it and has since been asked about it twice by people who were not us and has told them the same nine words both times.**

She is about fifty. She has a boy of nine. She has not been named in a public book in her life and she has been findable for nine years.

"**I have two things and the first one is the one I am going to say twice.**

"**Put my name down and the second thing I have not got.**

"No — I know what I have to say. Listen to it once. **A name in a book is a door, and a door is for sending a man away, and I want it in the book in that shape and not in any other shape, and if anybody in Alder Reach writes my name on a line at a place they have to write what I am, and I am the water.**"

"And what are you."

"**I am the water off that column, I have been the water off that column for eleven years, and every man who comes up that bank knows it, and a man who has read a book and comes up here to ask me about a boundary is going to find out about the water in about four minutes because there is nothing else here.**" She looked at the boy. "**That is the door and I am telling you what it is made of so that you cannot put anything else in it.**"

"**Second thing. Nobody stops coming up that bank.** I am not turning the water off and I am not locking anything and I am not going to be a woman at the end of a road with a stick. If a haulier comes up here on a Tuesday for two pails he comes up on the Tuesday, and if he is on the line when the word is said he waits on the bank until it is over, and that is all. **And I want it entered that a man waited on a bank once, and not that we kept him off the road.**"

Nobody wrote the second thing down for about twelve seconds and then the tenth hand wrote it down exactly.

---

**And a man of about thirty-one said one sentence at about half past four, standing on the hard ground with his hands in his pockets, and it is Bev Tollard's sentence and he did not want it in the book and then he did.**

"**My brother went from here on a train. Or he did not. I know it was us. I could not have told you it was us from here. Those are two different things and I have been the second one all week, and it has been eight years.**

"So I am going to say the thing that I think is coming and I am going to say it now while there are people here.

"**In a year there is going to be a book in that yard that says two people held a boundary at Sarrow Water. And a stranger is going to take that book cold and read it and think that this place is a station.** And it is not. It is a bank with a column on it and four houses and thirty-one people and a man of seventy who looks at a wall. **And I want it in the book that the two people are not a station and did not become one, and that a line on the ground is not a platform, and that nothing that happened today made this place a place a train is meant to stop at, and if anybody reads it in four years and thinks otherwise, that is on the sheet and not on us.**"

"**Enter that exactly,**" said the tenth hand, "**and put his name against it, because a man who is entered saying what a thing is not is a person with a standing and not a signature, and I am going to have to learn that difference in about a week, and somebody ought to have used it first.**"

---

**And at four o'clock the fourth person of the four said the word, once, standing about four feet off the line, in front of three men and about twenty people of the ninth place, and it took about nine seconds and it was not a sentence and it was not a form and it was not a signature, and it is in the book in her words and it is nine words long and it is the shortest entry in the history of the public lot book and it is not struck and it is not held by anybody.**

"**I said it. And it worked, and there is no train, and I want that written down. I have said the word three times in my life on that bank and this is the first time anything happened, and what happened was the other three of us stopped walking and stood still for one hour and eleven minutes.**"

And then Tarin Kest marked the line, at about ten past four, with the chalk he had been holding for forty minutes, and it took him about eleven minutes, and it is a line about five feet long across the six-foot and then up the cess and onto the shoulder for another eleven feet, and it is tar-white on grey ballast and it is not visible from the west end of the siding at all, and **the tenth hand entered that on the thirtieth, after walking it back, because a boundary nobody can see from the other end of the thing it is a boundary for is a boundary that only works for the people who made it, and that is the standing failure of this instrument and it is entered as one.**

---

**And the board published it at about half past six in the evening on the twenty-ninth of this month, all four seats named aloud, in the yard of Lot 17, to about two hundred and forty people in the last of the light, and it is the second of the five things the board can do, and it is the shortest of the eleven results it has published, and it is in the book in the registrar's own numbering and the numbering now runs to twelve.**

> **12. A LINE ON THE GROUND AT THE WEST END OF THE SIDING AT THE NINTH PLACE, SIXTY YARDS WEST OF THE COLUMN, ON THE SHOULDER, WHERE THE GROUND STOPS BEING GOOD GROUND. PUBLISHED BY THE BOARD OF THE PUBLIC LOT AT LOT 17, ALL FOUR SEATS NAMED ALOUD, ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF THIS MONTH. IT CONFERS NO USE, IT AUTHORIZES NOBODY TO MOVE ANYTHING, AND IT SEATS NOTHING.**
>
> **THE JOB: two named people, and only two, may say the word; the word is the word and not a form and not a signature; and when it is said, everything on this line stops and waits until somebody comes and looks.**
>
> **THE TWO ARE THE KEEPER OF THE HUT AT THE NINTH PLACE, WHO HAS HELD THE ONLY PAIR ON THIS LINE FOR ELEVEN YEARS AND WAS ASKED ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH AND SAID YES IN FOUR SECONDS, AND THE WOMAN AT THE COLUMN, WHO IS NAMED IN THIS ENTRY AS SHE ASKED TO BE NAMED AND WHO IS ALSO THE WATER.**
>
> **THE TERM: from the twenty-ninth of this month to the twenty-ninth of next month, and renewable by nobody except by asking both of them again, in a room, with the asking and the hour and the answer entered, whatever the answer is.**
>
> **AND THE THINGS THIS ENTRY IS NOT, AND THE BOARD OWNS ALL OF THEM AND NONE OF THEM IS A COMPLAINT:**
>
> **IT IS NOT A PLATFORM. IT IS NOT A STATION. IT IS NOT A CUSTODY. IT HAS NO CASE, NO COUNTER, NO KEY, AND NO HOLDER THAT CAN BE INSPECTED WITHOUT WALKING NINE MILES, AND THE NINE MILES IS THE POINT.**
>
> **AND IT IS NOT THE FIFTH THING. THE FIFTH THING IS A WAY OF SAYING THAT A TOLL LANDS ON A PLACE, AND NOBODY HAS A NAME FOR IT, AND IT IS NOT ON THIS ENTRY, AND THE BOARD HAS POWER TO PUBLISH A RESULT AND HAS NO POWER TO PUBLISH A CHARGE ON A PLACE, AND BOTH OF THOSE ARE ENTERED AS THE SAME GAP AND IT IS THE GAP THAT MATTERS.**

---

**And at about ten past five in the evening, on the ballast at the fourth place, with about sixty people standing in the failing light, Selik Marne said the standing sentence and nobody argued with him, and then a man he had argued with about nine times in five weeks asked him a question, and it is the first time anybody has asked Selik Marne for anything.**

"That is not enough," said Selik Marne, from the rail, in his hat, to about sixty people, and it is the eighth time he has said it on a published day in this month and the first time he has said it and then been asked something, and nobody in Alder Reach believed he would stop and he did not stop and he has not.

Then Tarin Kest, who is forty-three and who said *no* to staffing a reopened span in advance in a yard on the ninth of this month, walked about ten feet along the ballast and stopped and said:

"**Mr. Marne. You are the only man in this district who has been wrong about a road in nine years and I want to ask you for the thing nobody asked you for.**

"**That line is five feet across the six-foot and eleven feet up the shoulder and it is tar-white on grey ballast and it cannot be seen at all from the west end of the siding, which is the end the train comes from. So it works for the four of us and for the two women and for nobody else, and I have been standing next to it for an hour working out that I have built a wall that faces the wrong way.**

"**Is that the whole of what is wrong with it, and if it is not, tell me the rest, and I will put it in the book in your words and not in mine.**"

Selik Marne looked at the line for about eleven seconds.

"**It is not the whole of what is wrong with it, and I am going to say the other two, and I am not going to be thanked for them, and I want them entered as coming from me and not from the Warden office, because the Warden office is shut and I am a man standing on a ballast.**

"**One. It is a line, and a line is a thing you see, and it is not visible from the end it is for, and that is the first fault and it will be the fault that kills it, and it will not be killed by a train. It will be killed by a person who walks up that bank in the dark and does not know it is there, and there is no fence and there is no ditch and there is a drop of nine feet on the other side of it, and a fence on a shoulder above a river is a fence that somebody will be asked to build and somebody will be paid for and the payment will be the end of it.

"**Two, and this is the one that matters, and it is the reason I have been saying that is not enough since the eighth of this month: it is a boundary held by two people, and in about a month one of the two of you will be tired of it, and in about a year one of the two of you will be gone, and there is nobody after them, because the whole of the population of that place is thirty-one and about eleven of them are not asked anything and are not in any book and one of them is a woman who told you an hour ago that her name is a door she wants for sending men away.**

"So you have built a thing that two people hold on the strength of their own goodwill, and the term is a month, and the month is up on the twenty-ninth of next month, and on that morning somebody is going to have to go up that bank and ask two women to say yes again, and if one of them says no then the line stops working and the line does not know it has stopped working, because a line is not a person and there is nobody standing there to find out.

"**And I have been in a job for nine years where that is the whole of the risk and the whole of the job, and you are doing it on a bank with two women and a chalk, and I am telling you that the safest version of this is the one I published on the fifth and the district has just voted against it by seven to three, and the reason it lost was a post at a place called Harrow that is under nine feet of water.**

"So the choice in front of this district is a line two women hold, or an office eight men sit in that nobody can end, and I want it entered that I have said that those are the two and that I think the first one is better and that I still do not think it is enough, and I have said *that is not enough* on published days for eight years and I am not going to stop on the twenty-ninth of this month because a mechanic asked me nicely.**"

And Tarin Kest wrote it down in his own book in his own hand and gave it to the tenth hand to put in the public lot book, and it is the thirtieth of the twelve parts, and it is in the book as **a statement by the Warden administrator of St. Orra, made on the ballast at the fourth place on the twenty-ninth of this month, and he is not a member of the board and the board could not have asked him for it and did not**, and the tenth hand entered that the only reason he said it is that somebody asked him, and that in nine years of the district's public life nobody has ever asked him for anything at all.

**And he entered the second half of it as the reason a man of seventy is still sitting on a wall behind nine vehicles at nine miles west, and the second half is that the line cannot be seen from the end it is for, and the entry says what the tenth hand found at ten past four with his face about forty inches from the ballast, which is that the line is invisible from the west end of the siding, and the entry says that the fixing of it is one bushel of white paint and about a hundred and forty yards of it.**

"And one bushel of white paint," said Ivo Stenn, from about eight feet back, "**is in the ward market's rate for this line. It is the ninth coppers, a quarter, a lamp and a bushel of chalk, and the market has been arguing about that nine coppers for three weeks and it turns out that the ninth coppers was the right figure for the wrong thing, and there is going to have to be another vote, and I have got a column for that now too.**"
