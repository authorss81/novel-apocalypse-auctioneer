# Chapter 73: Clean

The man from the Valuers' Guild set his case on the crate in the yard of Lot 17 at about ten past two in the afternoon on the fourteenth of this month and opened it, and inside it there were nine sheets of a printed form with a column ruled down each of them, and he had already filled in eight, and every box on all eight had a tick in it, and he put the ninth sheet down on top of them, and the tick at the top of the ninth was the only one in his case that had been made that afternoon.

He is called Garnet Sill. He is forty-one, he has held a guild licence for twelve years, and he has certified about two hundred and six lots in that time and has found nine of them with anything wrong on them, and he can tell you what the eight of the nine were without looking them up.

**The thing about his visit, and the thing that is in the water office's book and the ward market's minute and the notice board of the River and Carriage, is that he came twelve miles to certify an iron that was not in the county, on his own fee, on two days' notice, off his own book, and nobody in Alder Reach asked him to.**

The fee schedule is a printed sheet and the part of it that matters is in the third column, and it says: **LOT IN A CASE OR A CAGE, AT THE OFFICE OR THE COUNTER: FOUR COPPERS. LOT IN A CUSTODY WITHOUT CASE, COUNTER OR KEY, EXAMINED IN THE PERSON OF THE KEEPER: ELEVEN COPPERS. LOT NOT PRODUCED, EXAMINED IN THE PERSON OF THE KEEPER WHO CANNOT PRODUCE IT: NOT CERTIFIABLE AND NOT CHARGEABLE.**

Garnet Sill read that last line out in the yard on the fourteenth of this month to about forty people, and then he said the thing that made the next three days, and it took him about nine seconds.

"**I am going to charge eleven coppers and not four, and I have come two days, and the guild pays me travel at a rate and I have claimed it, and I would like the market to understand that I am the most expensive thing in this yard this month and that I have earned it, and I would like it entered that I said so myself before anybody else did.**"

---

He went up the bank on the twelfth, twice on the thirteenth, and once on the fourteenth, and he walked the four changeovers in good boots and said out loud, on the shoulder at the eighth, the sentence that is in his own report: **"A man of fifty-four in good boots needs both hands and a woman of forty-five in walking shoes would not come this way in the rain at all, and the difference between those two facts is about nine inches of shoulder and it is the whole of what is wrong with this railway."**

At the ninth place he did not examine an object. He examined a custody, and he said so before he began, in the form's own words, because the form has a box for it and he filled the box in.

> **LOT: EXAMINED AS A CUSTODY. NO CASE, NO COUNTER, NO KEY. EXAMINED IN THE PERSON OF THE KEEPER.**
> **CONDITION OF THE CUSTODY: A CAST FRAME WITH TWO LEVERS AND A QUADRANT; A BRACKET AT SHOULDER HEIGHT, EMPTY; A CLEAN PALE RECTANGLE IN THE GREASE AND THE RUST WHERE A THING FOUR INCHES WIDE STOOD AGAINST THE FRAME FOR FIVE YEARS; A MARK ON THE CASTING ABOUT THE SIZE OF A THUMBNAIL WHERE A CORNER CAUGHT; THE FLOOR OF THE HUT ELEVEN INCHES OUT OF LEVEL AT THE LEFT-HAND END OF THE BENCH WITH A CRACK IN THE LIME PUTTY ABOUT FOUR FEET LONG, MEASURED BY OTHERS AND NOT BY ME.**
> **HOLDER: A PERSON WHO IS NOT AN OFFICER, A SEAT, OR A MEMBER OF ANY BODY, WHO ANSWERS TO NOBODY HERE, WHO HOLDS THE CUSTODY AND WHO MAY END IT WHENEVER SHE LIKES AND NOBODY IN THE COUNTY CAN STOP HER.**

And then he walked to the fourth place on the fourteenth and examined the iron on the bench, and the iron was there, and it had not moved, and he did what he has done two hundred and six times.

He put it in his hand. He weighed it against a spring balance he carries, four pounds and one ounce. He looked at the wedge end, which is worn to a shine, and at the red signal-wire loop through the hole at the other end, which was cut loose in the fourth year when the thing was taken out of the flooded interchange and has not been used as a loop since. He looked for a second piece of iron married to it, a second wire, an added tongue, a filed-off number, a maker's mark with a number beside it that ought not to be there. He ran a thumb along the casting where a thin plate can be let in and covered.

"There is no plate," he said. "**There has never been a plate on this, and if there had been one there would be four rivet holes and a seam, and I have my thumb on the seam and it is not there.**"

"How long does that take you," said Orren Pike.

"**Nine minutes for a big one and about four for something the size of your forearm.**" He wrote it down. "**And I want it understood that four minutes is not a formality. Nine-tenths of what I do is looking for a thing somebody has added, and I have found nine in twelve years and eight of the nine were added.**"

And he filled in the rest of it, standing on the six-foot of a viaduct with the wind off the river, in about eleven minutes, and then he read the top of the ninth sheet out loud in a hut at the fourth place to nine people, and the tick was at the top of the ninth and it was under a word with a line under it.

> **CLEAN.**

And then, in the hut at the ninth place, on the twelfth, at about eleven in the morning, in front of the keeper and four other people, Garnet Sill did the only thing in twelve years of examining other people's work that he has ever done in another office's premises, and it took about two minutes and it is the shortest interview anybody at Lot 17 has published.

He put the certificate face down on the bench and asked Sef Anyard a question that is not on the form.

"**If it were gone, would you know?**"

"**No,**" she said.

"**Would anybody?**"

"**No.**"

"**Then I want to write that on the back and I want you to tell me whether to write it. I am not asking to take anything off your custody and I am not asking you to give it up. I am asking whether the custody has a hole in it, because a hole is a thing a man can find, and there is no hole in a case and that is the whole of what a case is for.**

"**And you have no case. And a custody that is better than nothing is still not a case, and a custody with no hole in it is a custody with no way of knowing.**"

"**Write it,**" she said, "**and write that I have known that for eleven years and did not write it down anywhere, and that I did not write it down because there was not one person in nine miles who could have asked me about it and not made me into something, and that the reason there is not one person now is that this is the eleventh of April and not the first.**"

He wrote it, and it is on the back of the certificate under the finding about the standing line, and it is the only entry in a licensed document in this district that is about a person and was written at that person's request.

And then the woman at the column, who is the water, asked him the question that the whole batch turns on, and she asked it in front of about sixty people in the doorway of her own house, and it was the shortest question anybody has asked in this district in a month and it was not an argument.

"**Are you going to write down whether my sister is in there?**"

"No," said Garnet Sill.

"**Why not.**"

"**Because I do not know, and because a man from a guild who writes down what he does not know about a village is a man who has been asked to write down what he thinks about a village, and the day I do that I am not a valuer, I am a man with a shop on a tram arch, and there are about four of those in this city and one of them is very good at it.**

"**And I will tell you what I will write down, because I can stand behind it. I will write down that the lot is clean, that it cannot be sold to a village, that the standing line cannot be filled in, and that the custody has no way of knowing. That is a certificate. If I put your sister in it, it is a complaint, and you would be owed an answer to a complaint, and I could not give you one, and you would be worse off than if I had said nothing at all.**"

She looked at him for about four seconds and then said, "**That is the first time a man has told me no in nine years and given me the reason he could not, and it is the same reason, and it is the only reason anybody has ever given me for anything, and you may write my name against nothing.**"

---

**And the sentence he said in the yard of Lot 17 at about half past three in the afternoon on the fourteenth of this month, to about a hundred and forty people, is the sentence the district has been arguing with for the rest of the month, and it is the truest thing anybody has said about that iron in thirteen years, and nobody in Alder Reach was able to answer it and nobody tried.**

"**I have to tell you two things and they are not the same shape, and I have been trying all the way up the bank to find a way of making them the same shape and I cannot, and a man who cannot find a way of making two true things the same shape should not be allowed to be asked.**

"**First. The lot is clean.** Not cleanish. Clean. I mean that in the guild sense and the guild sense is exact: there is no concealed toll, no concealed term, no concealed party, no side promise, no forge, no second piece, no person entered as a person who is not a person, no term longer than the term, and no use that is not the use. I have been able to certify two hundred and six lots and I have found nine with something on them and this is not one of the nine. **Whatever is wrong with this iron, it is not that anybody has cheated anybody with it.**

"**Second. It cannot be sold to a village.**"

Somebody in the yard said *why not* and about forty people said it at once.

"**Because a sale is to a person.** That is not poetry, it is the beginning of my trade's oldest form, and a village is not a person, and I could not sell a kettle to a street and neither can I sell this to thirty-one people in four houses. **And there is a second reason and it is arithmetic and I would like the room to have it because it is the half of it that a guild can prove.**

"**Nineteen takings. One object. And in every one of the nineteen the charge fell on the origin, and the origin is a place. So the thing that has been bought, nineteen times, by nobody, at no price, is not a passage and not a fare. It is a village's memory of its own departures. And there is no instrument in this basin, in the guild or out of it, that can name that as the thing being sold, because the seller has to name it and the seller cannot say what it is, and a document that cannot say what it is cannot be given to anybody.**"

---

The finding underneath, entered by Nessa Pell and entered in the fifteenth part of the results, is that a licensed valuer of twelve years' standing stood in a public yard in this city and said the truest sentence anybody has said about that iron, and that the room did not argue with him, and that the reason the room did not argue with him is that there is nothing in the district's public record to argue with, and that a man from the Guild did in about ninety seconds what this district has failed to do in seven weeks, which is to describe the fifth instrument's shape exactly, and did not know he was doing it.

"**And I want to be fair to him,**" said the registrar, "**and the fairness is this. He is right about the object and he is wrong about the village, and both halves of that are correct, and the reason they are both correct is that his form has a column headed CONDITION and he wrote CLEAN in it, and a lot like this one has no condition, and the iron's condition is not the question and he is the only man in this basin who has said so out loud in a document.**

"**And his form has no column for where the toll lands. He did not leave it out. It is not in the instrument. A guild sheet in this basin has twenty-nine columns and not one of them is a place, and he has used them all and filled them in, and a man who fills in twenty-nine columns and leaves the one that matters absent is not a liar and is not a fool and is doing his work.**"

---

**And then a ward market secretary of six years beat a licensed valuer of twelve in about nine minutes, and did it with a figure, and nobody in the room had thought of it, and he had been waiting two months for somebody to ask him for a number.**

Ivo Stenn had the guild fee schedule in his own book and had had it in his own book since the second, when a clerk from the River and Carriage had come to the counting room to talk about the standing order of the ninth and had left it on the table, and Stenn had kept it for the reason he keeps everything, which is that a rate buys a service and a service has a price and this district had never once written a price on a Guild.

"**Read the third column again,**" he said. "**Not the third column of the second page. The third column.**"

Garnet Sill read it.

"**LOT IN A CUSTODY WITHOUT CASE, COUNTER OR KEY, EXAMINED IN THE PERSON OF THE KEEPER: ELEVEN COPPERS.**

"**And the ninth place has no case, no counter and no key, and the keeper is not an officer and answers to nobody here. So it is eleven, and it is the highest number on that sheet, and this market pays it.**

"**Now read the line under it.**

"LOT NOT PRODUCED, EXAMINED IN THE PERSON OF THE KEEPER WHO CANNOT PRODUCE IT: NOT CERTIFIABLE AND NOT CHARGEABLE."

"There is no such line under it," said Garnet Sill. "That is not on my sheet."

"**It is on the sheet the River and Carriage uses and you have got the wrong one, and I am sorry, and it is not your fault, and it is a nine-copper difference and the market has paid it out of a column and would like it back.**"

The finding is entered in the minute in the secretary's own hand and it is the first time in this district's history that anybody has entered a figure against a Guild, and the sentence under it is the one he has been wanting to say since the second of last month and has not been able to say to anybody:

> **A GUILD CERTIFICATE IS A LICENSED ACT AND THE LICENCE IS ABOUT THE ACT AND NOT ABOUT THE ARGUMENT. THIS MARKET HAS SPENT FIVE WEEKS ARGUING WITH A COLUMN AND IT HAS COST US A CERTIFICATE, AND THE MAN WHO ISSUED THE CERTIFICATE WAS CORRECT AND WE ARE REFUNDING HIM FOUR COPPERS ON THE ELEVEN AND HE HAS ASKED FOR THE NINE AND I AM NOT ENTITLED TO GIVE HIM THE NINE.**

Garnet Sill took the refund, and then did the thing that made the rest of the month possible, and he did it at his own cost and out of the hour and did not charge it and would not explain why until the seventeenth.

It was a second sheet, on the back of his own, and it was not a certificate and it had no guild mark on it and it began in his own hand:

> **THIS IS NOT A CERTIFICATE AND IT IS NOT A VALUATION. I AM A LICENSED VALUER AND I HAVE WRITTEN THIS AT MY OWN COST OUT OF AN HOUR I WAS NOT PAID FOR AND I AM NOT GOING TO SAY WHY UNTIL I HAVE SAID THE OTHER THINGS.**
>
> **THE LOT IS CLEAN. I HAVE EXAMINED IT TWICE, IN THE PERSON OF THE KEEPER, AND THE KEEPER IS A PERSON AND NOT AN OFFICER AND I WOULD LIKE THAT IN EVERY CERTIFICATE IN THIS BASIN.**
>
> **THE LOT CANNOT BE SOLD TO A VILLAGE, AND THE REASON IS NOT A BAR, IT IS ARITHMETIC: NINETEEN TAKINGS, ONE OBJECT, NO INSTRUMENT THAT NAMES THE PLACE BEING CHARGED. A DOCUMENT THAT CANNOT NAME WHAT IS BEING SOLD CANNOT BE GIVEN TO ANYBODY, AND THAT IS TRUE OF THE GUILD'S OWN FORM AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME IN TWELVE YEARS I HAVE WRITTEN IT DOWN.**
>
> **MY FORM HAS TWENTY-NINE COLUMNS AND NOT ONE OF THEM IS A PLACE. I FILLED ALL TWENTY-NINE IN. YOUR TIMETABLE HAS A COLUMN AT FULL WIDTH WITH NOTHING IN IT AND THE REASON FOR IT IS PRINTED INSIDE IT. MINE IS NARROWER THAN YOURS AND I DID NOT KNOW IT EXISTED, AND I HAVE BEEN TOLD THAT A COLUMN THAT IS PRINTED NARROW LOOKS LIKE A COLUMN THAT HAS BEEN KEPT QUIET, AND I HAVE HAD TO PUT MY HAND ON MY OWN HEAD.**
>
> **AND THE STANDING. THE FORM HAS A LINE FOR IT AND THE LINE SAYS A PERSON WHO CAN HOLD A SIGNAL. I AM A LICENSED VALUER AND I CANNOT FILL THAT LINE IN AND I HAVE WRITTEN NONE ON THE FACE OF THE CERTIFICATE, AND I HAVE ISSUED THE CERTIFICATE ANYWAY, AND ON THE BACK OF IT, IN MY OWN HAND, IS THE REASON: A CLEAN LOT AND AN UNFILLED STANDING LINE ARE BOTH TRUE, AND MY FORM HAS NO LINE FOR A CUSTODY, AND A FORM WITH NO LINE FOR A CUSTODY IS A FORM THAT CANNOT BE REFUSED, AND A CERTIFICATE THAT CANNOT BE REFUSED IS NOT A CERTIFICATE, IT IS A RECEIPT.**

---

**And Selik Marne said the standing sentence at the rail on the fourteenth, and then said the other thing, and the other thing is the only time in nine years anybody has heard him say a kind word about a piece of paper another office produced.**

"**That is not enough,**" he said first, from the rail, in his hat, to about a hundred and ten people, **and it is the first time he has said it in this month and he said it eleven times in the week of the fifth and he will say it on every published day of the ninth year of his post and nobody in this district believes he will stop and he does not stop.**

Then:

"**And I am going to say one other thing, and I am not going to stand here and tell you to put that certificate in the fire, and I would like the minute to say that I was asked what I thought of it and that I answered.**

"**A man has spent twelve years and two hundred and six lots learning how to look at an iron and this morning he found nothing, and what he found nothing at is the only good news anybody has had about this railway in four weeks. That is a true sentence and it is in his own hand and it is worth more than anything on the eleven boards.

"**And a certificate is not a safety instrument. Neither is a timetable. Neither is the standing order that this district voted against by seven to three on the twenty-fifth of last month, and I have said for nine years that the standing order is a better paper than either of them, and I am not going to vary that this morning. I have said *that is not enough* every published day for eight years and I am not going to stop on the fourteenth of this month because a Guild clerk in a good coat told a yard the truth.

"**And one more, and it is the half of it that matters: he is the first man in nine years to put a document on the wall of that yard that says where the toll lands, and he did it by accident, and he has told me he is not going to explain how he knew to do it until the seventeenth, and I have not asked him and I am not going to ask him.**"

And on the seventeenth of April, at the counter, in front of about thirty people, Garnet Sill did explain, and it took him about four minutes, and it is in the book because Rutt wrote it down and Nessa Pell compared the hand.

"**A man from the Guild goes into a village to certify an iron and he asks the woman holding it whether anybody has ever been sold anything here, and she says no, and he writes down no, and then he asks her who has, and she says you, on Tuesday, you were here Tuesday, and you asked me a question about water, and it is the first question anybody has asked me about water, and she says it as a complaint and he hears it as a complaint.

"**And that is it. That is the whole of my twelve years. You go into a place to look at a thing and the place asks you a question and the place is the only party in the room with nothing to sell.**

"**And I am going to say the unprofessional part, because I am told this yard has room for one. I have certified two hundred and six lots and in every single one of them the thing that was wrong was in a column, and not one column in this guild, or in this basin that I have seen, is a place.**"
