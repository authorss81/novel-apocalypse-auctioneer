# Chapter 89: Asked a Second Time

At about half past ten in the morning on the twenty-ninth of this month a woman of about thirty-one came into the front room of a house at the ninth place and moved a pail out of the way of the table before she put her coat down, and nobody mentioned that she had done it, and the reason nobody mentioned it is that in April the pail had been by the door and in the way of a chair for the whole of a morning, and the person who asked the question then had not moved it because it was not his.

The room has a table in it and a window with a board over one pane and a pail and about twenty-two people from four houses standing at the back who were not asked to come in and were not asked to leave.

**The asking was done by a clerk of the ward market who is six years at a counter, and she is the second person to ask these two women anything on that bank, and the first was a signalman of the tenth hand who cannot be paid, and the difference between the two is the whole of what anybody in this district learned on it.**

A woman of about fifty-one who has held the only pair on that line for eleven years. A woman of about fifty who is the water. A man of thirty-nine who is in the hut and is in the room and was not asked anything and entered the entry himself as **present and not asked**, for the second time in two months. A woman of about thirty-eight with a hook and a sacking sheet, who came up the bank for her own trade and is here on the twenty-ninth because she was asked to be, and who stood by the door and said the question out loud before anybody answered it, which is the sixth thing, and which she has now done three times and was thanked for none of them.

Nobody from a bridge administration is in that room and nobody from the ward market is in that room except the clerk, and the room in Alder Reach knows both of those facts and has entered both of them. Adrian Vale is nine miles east and entered himself, on the evening of the twentieth, as not coming, and the entry gives the reason in eleven words: **I AM NOT COMING UP THAT BANK AND I WOULD LIKE IT IN THE BOOK THAT I SAID SO IN ADVANCE.**

---

**And the asking took thirty-nine minutes and nine of them were the fifth thing, and the clerk got a word shorter at it than the signalman did in April, and the finding is that a method gets cheaper the second time anybody uses it and that this is the first thing in this district that has ever got better by being done twice.**

The first thing, what the job is, in sentences somebody who has never done it can understand and not in the word anybody else would use. You hold the west end of this railway for a month. There is no train at this end of this railway and there is not going to be one, and the thing that could turn one is in a hut four hours up the bank on a bench with a boy asleep beside it. You stand in a place and you say a nine-word sentence, and if anybody comes up that bank while you are saying it they stop and wait, and then they go on. That is all. It is not a fence and it does not stop a thing. It stops people, about nine times a year.

The keeper asked her one question about the first thing before she finished it, and it was the same kind of question she asked in April, and it took eleven seconds.

"You have said *railway* twice and you have said *bank* once. In April he said *this railway* eleven times and it is the same word. Is that a thing you did on purpose?"

"No," said the clerk. "**I did it because I read what he wrote down before I came up here, and because it is his bank and not mine, and because I have never walked it, and I walked it on the twenty-first as a rehearsal and it took me five and a quarter hours going and I am not going to be able to tell you what is at the fourth changeover except that a man of about thirty-four told me and he was standing in the water when he said it.**"

"That is the right answer," said the keeper, "and I would like it in the book, because in April the answer he gave was that he had been up a bank four times in three weeks to learn the difference between a word and a place, and yours is better."

The second thing, how long with an hour on it. This is the one that took nine minutes, and the clerk said before she asked it that she had crossed a time out three times on a sheet on the twentieth of this month because she did not know how long the job was.

"**A day and a night.** I leave here at half past four in the morning because it is five and a quarter hours with four changeovers in it and the worst of the four is a step down of two feet six inches into a foot of water, and I am going to be back in Alder Reach at about ten at night, and I am not doing that twice in a year for anybody. That is the hour. That is on the slate. If you want less than that I cannot promise it and if you want more than that I cannot give it."

"You did that on the twenty-first in front of three people," said the woman of the column.

"I did, and I wrote it down wrong, and I have said so in the book, and the number on the sheet I am carrying is eleven and three-quarters for the day and that is five and a quarter going and five and a half coming and it is my own figure and it is not the tenth hand's figure and neither of us is going to argue about it in front of you."

The third thing, how you say no, and that no reason is owed, and nobody asks afterwards. Both of them answered it before it was finished, in April and in May, in the same words, and the clerk said that out loud so that the room would know they were not new sentences.

"You say no by not saying yes," said the clerk. "No reason owed. Nobody asks afterwards."

"Nobody asks afterwards," said the keeper.

"And you read it back to me before anybody leaves this room, and if you read it back wrong I will know, and I have been in this hut for eleven years and I know what a wrong sentence sounds like."

"You have said that before," said the woman of the column. "**In April. Word for word. And I want it entered that you have said it twice and that the second time it was not a rehearsed thing, it was a habit, and that a habit is the only instrument I have ever trusted.**"

The fourth thing, who answers for you while you are saying it, was answered by the man of thirty-nine before the clerk got to it, from the same chair he had been in since half past nine, and nobody had asked him and the entry says so.

"**I will answer for her about a pair and I will not answer for her about the word, and I said that in April and nothing has changed except that I have been here thirty-four days instead of four, and a man who has been somewhere thirty-four days instead of four is a different man, and I would like the room to have both of those sentences in the book together.**"

The fifth thing, where you stand when you answer, was answered before it was asked, in eleven words, in the same eleven words as April, and about nine people at the back of that room did not look at the sheet at all.

"**Where I stand. I have stood in this doorway for eleven years and I am not going to stand anywhere else to say a yes to a man from Alder Reach.**"

---

**And the answer was yes from both of them, and the second asking was better than the first in three ways, and all three of them are in the book, and the third one is the only one anybody in this district would have thought to do.**

The first difference is the person asking. **A rotation is only real when it turns. It turned in March on a key, and it turned in April on a boundary, and on the twenty-ninth of this month it turned on a bank, and it was turned by a clerk, and a clerk is a person, and a person can be replaced, and the whole reason this district put a counter between itself and the public in the first place is that a counter can be replaced and a man cannot.**

And the clerk said her own reason out loud, in the room, and it is the reason she gave on the twentieth in a counting room, and the keeper wrote it down and read it back, and the entry has it in the room's words rather than the clerk's.

The second difference is that a woman on that bank who said no in March was asked again, in the same room, in front of the same twenty-two people, and she said no again, and she is not in the book and was not named in the room, and the reason is in the count, and not asked is in the count, and **a no that is asked twice is not a refusal that expires.**

"I was not asked about the second month in April," said the tenth hand's entry, which is in a different hand from a different room and is four sentences long. "And the reason nobody asks me is that I am the cheapest door in this district and I have told them so. A no that is asked once is a no that a person can come back from in a bad afternoon. A no that is asked twice is a standing answer, and a standing answer is not a refusal, it is a column, and I would like the column on the wall and not in a book about a railway."

The third difference is that this renewal carries a **renewed refusal**, and it is in the same entry, and it is not struck, and it is the refusal of the twenty-ninth of April given again in May with a reason and a date on it, and it was the first thing said in the room.

"**The word is not held at the fourth place,**" said Sef Anyard, before she said anything else, and nobody had asked her and she had been going to be asked. "I asked on the twenty-ninth of April and I was refused and the refusal was on a sheet, and I am asking again and I am going to be refused again, and I would like the refusal dated, because a refusal that has a date on it is a thing somebody can come back to when the ground at that end stops being ground.

"And I will hold it a third month. And the second month is the one I will not hold alone, and I have not held it alone, and there is a man in my hut who cannot say my sentence like a person yet and who gets it right about one time in five, and on the fifth of this month he said it the way I say it and I have not told anybody that either until now."

---

**And the woman of the column said yes and then asked a market for something nobody in this district has ever thought to ask a market for, and it is in the book under a heading that says NOT ANSWERED, and it is the third line anybody has entered under that heading in nineteen years.**

"Yes, and a month, and not a year, and I am fifty and I have not promised anybody a year since I was thirty-four, and that is the same reason I gave in March and I am not going to improve on it.

"And now I am going to ask for something, and I have been waiting eleven years to ask it, and I am aware that the place to ask it is a front room on the last day of my term, and I am asking it anyway.

"The rate came to this bank on the seventh of this month in a pail of change and nobody asked me whether I agreed to it. It is on a sheet that says a rate sheet is a document that says a place is a place, and it is a good sheet, and I am not asking anybody to take the money back, because two hundred coppers is six coppers of lettering and a bound copy of a book and I have had eleven years of not having anything, and I am not going to be the woman who says take the money back.

"**So: the next time this market sends anybody up this bank, it sends them up with the sheet. Not with a bag. With the sheet, and somebody who can read it out loud in this room, and the reading-back, and the reason, which is the same reason as the twenty-ninth of April, which is that a stranger is owed the reading.**"

And the clerk read that back too, and did not strike a word of it, and the minute says that a woman of about fifty asked a market for the method that a man of about fifty-six worked out in a yard in nine minutes on the sixth of last month, eleven weeks after she had watched two people do it in her own front room and said nothing.

---

**And the whole of the twenty-ninth of this month was read back in that room before anybody stood up, and the reader was the woman with the hook, and she had never read anything out loud in her life and read it in four minutes.**

She read the asking and the hour and both answers and the three differences and the renewed refusal and the request, in the order they happened, and she read the request last, and she read it twice, and nobody asked her to.

The finding is entered on the first of next month at nine in the morning, in the reading-out book at the ward market counter, and it is that **a paperer read a woman's request for a market to read its own sheet aloud back to a room, and the reason the finding is worth entering is that the fourth instrument this district does not have is a column that is a place, and this is the first time in nine weeks that a person who handles other people's documents for a living has read a public entry out loud, and the trade is not in the count of trades in that room.**

And the sheet was entered in the public lot book in Alder Reach on the first of next month at nine in the morning with the delay in the registrar's own hand, and it was entered a second time in the water office's own book on the second, out of a copy made in the room before anybody left, and the second entry is the reason the first one was not lost.

> **THE ASKING WAS DONE AT THE FIGURE ONE AND THREE QUARTERS ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF THIS MONTH AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN IN THE MORNING BY A CLERK OF THE WARD MARKET AND IT WAS ENTERED HERE ON THE FIRST OF NEXT MONTH AT NINE IN THE MORNING. THE DELAY IS NINE MILES, FOUR CHANGEOVERS, A NIGHT AND A DAY. THE DELAY IS THE BOARD'S.**
>
> **IT IS ALSO ENTERED IN THE WATER OFFICE'S OWN BOOK, WHICH IS COPIED TWICE A QUARTER AND GOES WHERE A SHEET DOES NOT GO, AND THE ENTRY IN THAT BOOK WAS MADE BY A CLERK OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO WAS NOT IN THE ROOM AND WHO SAYS SO. AND THE ENTRY IN THAT BOOK HAS A FIFTH LINE ON IT WHICH IS THE ONLY LINE IN IT THAT IS ABOUT A PERSON AND IT IS THIS: THE PERSON WHO WROTE IT DOWN IN THE ROOM WAS A CLERK AND SHE ASKED FOR A READER AND WAS GIVEN A PAPERER.**

And the woman of the column's request went up the bank to a counting room and then down a bank to a press, and the request is that a rate sheet is read out loud in the room it was made for, and the market has entered that it will be, and the market has not decided when, and **the market's answer, when it comes, will have a count in it, because the count is now on the table and a count that has been read out once is never off a table again.**

And a timetable has been voted for six to four, and it comes off a press in Alder Reach in a fortnight, and it will have days on it, and the days will be days of the month and not days of the week, because this district does not have a calendar and a day of the week is a thing nobody here can check against anybody else. And the ninth place holds a line for a month that somebody will have to come up this bank and ask about on the twenty-ninth of next month, and the clerk who has just done it said, in the room, at the end, in nine words, that she is not going to be the one who does it again, and a clerk can be replaced, and that is the point of her.
