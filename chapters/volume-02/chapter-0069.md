# Chapter 69: Three Reasons, and the Word Close

He had a sheet folded in four in his coat and he took it out at about ten in the morning on the thirtieth of this month and put it on the crate in the yard of Lot 17 and squared it with his thumb, and it is the first time in nineteen years that he has squared a piece of paper before putting it down in front of a room, and Orren Pike, who was at the rail, noticed the thumb and has said since that it is the only time he has ever seen the man do it.

**There is no charter, no closing, no activation and no reading in any of it, and the sheet is a draft, and a draft is a proposal, and this one cannot be closed by anybody who exists.**

The idea had come off the walk. It came off the sixth place and the eighth place and the sixty yards past the column, and it was the first idea anybody in Alder Reach had had in a month that was not a copy of something, and Tarin Kest had it in about nine words and then refused to have it, and what he said is the fourth reason and it is at the end of the morning.

**Four bearers. One lot. The use split among four places on the line, so that no single place holds the whole of it, and so that when the origin is taken, the origin is taken four ways and a village is not charged alone.**

That is the whole idea and it is a good one and it is the correct response to everything Adrian Vale admitted in a yard of about five hundred people on the eighteenth of this month. **A toll lands on an origin. An origin is a place. A place is not a person. So make the origin four places and nobody is alone with it.** It is in a book of his own, in his own hand, dated the nineteenth of this month, and nobody has argued with it and it is the best thing he has written since the four lines at the kitchen table.

He drafted it in about an hour and a half on the morning of the thirtieth and it is in the public lot book on the first of next month, in full, and the entry says that the draft is published and cannot be closed and that publication is the second of the five things a board can do and that the board published it because nobody could close it and a document nobody can close should not be a document in a drawer.

---

**And the room found out in about eleven minutes, and the eleven minutes were the longest of the month and nobody has ever written them down in a way that made them shorter.**

They were in the yard in daylight because the yard is where this district does things, and all four seats of the board were not there because the board had nothing to sit on, and the people who were there were Mara Quill as registrar and not a vote, Nessa Pell as sitting steward, the tenth hand of the signal crew as a seat and not a person, Tarin Kest who had surrendered a lease on the previous day and had come in at nine because he had nowhere to put seven vehicles a week, Ivo Stenn who had a rate sheet and a column, and Rutt, who reads four days a week and came on the fifth day of that week because of what was on the crate.

Adrian Vale put the sheet down and squared it again and read the whole of it out loud in about four minutes, and it is in the book, and here it is as far as the terms go.

> **LOT: THE HARROW TONGUE, HELD IN REMOTE CUSTODY AT THE NINTH PLACE, CONDITION PUBLISHED IN FULL, NO CASE, NO COUNTER, NO KEY, THE TERM BEING THE KEEPER'S.**
>
> **USE: SEND ONE VEHICLE BACK ALONG THE SECTION IT CAME FROM. ONE MOVEMENT. ONE WAY. THE USE IS NOT GIVEN BACK.**
>
> **TOLL: THE ORIGIN.**
>
> **BEARERS, FOUR: THE FOURTH PLACE, THE SIXTH PLACE, THE EIGHTH PLACE, THE NINTH PLACE. A BEARER IS A PERSON WHO CAN HOLD A SIGNAL AND WHO HAS A PLACE AND AN APPROACH AND A STATED WEEK.**
>
> **SECURITY: NONE OFFERED AND NONE POSTED, AND NOBODY IN THIS ROOM HAS THE POWER TO POST ONE.**

And then the sheet stopped, and what follows is what came off the object when it was asked, and Adrian Vale read it out too, and it is the only System panel in this volume's second ten chapters and it is in the book in his own hand because the book has no other place for it.

> **A USE MAY BE SPLIT AMONG BEARERS. A SPLIT IS DRAFTED BY A READER. A SPLIT IS CLOSED BY A BINDER.**
>
> **A STAGE 1 READER MAY DRAFT A SPLIT. A STAGE 1 READER MAY NOT CLOSE ONE, MAY NOT TRANSFER A USE, MAY NOT RENEW A SPLIT, AND MAY NOT POST A SECURITY.**
>
> **STANDING IS GIVEN TO A BEARER AND NOT TO A SLOT. A SLOT NOBODY STANDS IN IS NOT A BEARER. A PAIR IS A PLACE, AN APPROACH, AND THE PERSON ABLE TO HOLD THEM. A SLOT IS NOT A PAIR.**
>
> **A BOARD CANNOT NAME A PERSON. A BOARD THAT IS ASKED BY A CROWD IS NOT A BOARD AND IS NOT A HIRING OFFICE.**
>
> **AND NONE OF THIS IS AN ANSWER. IT IS THE SHAPE OF THE THING YOU ARE TRYING TO DO. YOU MAY STILL DRAFT IT AND YOU MAY STILL SHOW IT TO A ROOM. YOU MAY NOT CLOSE IT, AND NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT CAN CLOSE IT FOR YOU, AND THE ONLY PERSON WHO CAN CLOSE IT IS SOMEBODY WHO IS NOT YET A THING THAT EXISTS.**

---

**And then he said the three things himself, in his own words, in about four minutes, and he said them in the order they had hurt, and the yard has them in a fair hand because Rutt wrote them down and Nessa Pell compared the hand against his and the finding is in the book.**

"One. **I may draft a split and I may not close one.** I am a Stage 1 Reader. It is painted on a wall in this yard and it is in a sleeve in this building with a number on it, and I have said it in a yard about nine times in five weeks, and I said it at a rail on the twenty-first in front of four hundred people about being replaced by a room with four seats in it. **I have no power to close this. There is no version of me that closes this.**

"Two. **I cannot post a security, and a four-bearer split with no security is four people on a hook with nothing behind them.** On the nineteenth of the month before last I wrote an undertaking on a piece of paper and stamped it with my own stamp and put only one line on the public sheet saying a security was offered in the room and the entry in the public book was to follow the return term, and the board entered the press and the crew as *available, not posted*, and that entry has been in this book ever since and it is correct. **It is not a security. It is a real obligation that nobody else can inspect, and if one of those four bearers fails on a Tuesday, there is nothing in the world behind him but a man with a sheet of paper who is not a Binder.**

"And I have been here seven weeks telling this district that a thing you did not consent to is a breach, and it turns out that is a rule about roads I built and not a law, and this draft is a road I am building, and I am doing it again, and this time it is worse because this time there are four people in it.

"Three. **A transferable slot is not a pair.**"

That is the one that took the room.

"**The fourth entry in this draft is a slot.** I wrote it at about eleven o'clock this morning and I have been not looking at it for an hour, and it is a slot because there are four places and I could name three people and there are three signal hands in this district who are not the tenth hand and who have all been home since the eleventh and twelfth of last month and who I have not asked, because I have not asked, and the reason I have not asked is that I have had them in a room at some point in my life and I did not like it.

"So the draft has three bearers and a vacancy. And standing is per bearer. **So the standing of this split is the standing of three men, and the approach at the fourth place is covered by nobody, and the eighth place is covered by a slot that nobody stands in, and a slot nobody stands in is not a bearer at all. It is a name I have not got.**"

---

**And the fourth reason came off the floor from a mechanic of forty-three who had surrendered a lease the day before, and it took him about forty seconds, and he said that it was the reason he had refused to have the idea at all on the sixth place on the twenty-ninth of this month, standing in four inches of drain water.**

"**That is the best idea anybody has had in this district in a month, and it is wrong on the ground, and I am the man who said so on the sixth place and I was right, and the book is to have both.**

"**Your four places are the fourth, the sixth, the eighth and the ninth. I have walked all four in the last four days, because I was on the walk and then I went round it afterwards, and I have a thing to say about two of them.**

"**The sixth place is a drain.** There is a drain off the top of that bank at the sixth place and it goes onto the ballast and it has been going onto the ballast since before the water came, and on the twenty-ninth a woman stood in it for an hour and a quarter and said the word, and if I put a bearer on the sixth place then a man is holding a pair in four inches of running water on a curve, and you cannot be seen from the ninth place and you cannot be seen from the third.

"**And the eighth place is on a curve.** The second rail on a curve is a foot and a half and the ballast is up to your chest, and the shoulder there is four feet wide with a drop of nine feet off it on the river side, and I know this because I went round the changeover on it in walking shoes and came back with the shoe full. **A man who has to hold a pair at the eighth place has to stand somewhere, and there is nowhere at the eighth place to stand.**"

"So what does that make it?"

"**It makes it a paper with two holes in it and one vacancy, and it is the best paper in this district and it is still a paper.**" He put his hand flat on the crate. "And it is not a hole because you made it. **It is a hole because a railway is a drain, and a line is an order and not a length, and every one of the twelve places on this line was put where somebody wanted to put it and not where a man could stand.** And there is no instrument in this district that says where a man may stand, and the answer to that on the twenty-eighth of this month was a line of chalk on a shoulder that cannot be seen from the end it is for."

---

**And Rutt read the draft out in the yard at about twenty past eleven, because a reader reads things, and it is the fifth day of her four and she was there and nobody asked her to and she has been at this counter since a quarter to six every day she is on it for two years.**

She read the lot and the use and the toll and the four bearers, and then she got to the fourth and stopped for about four seconds.

"**The fourth is a slot. I am going to read a slot out loud and I want to say before I do that I have read about nine hundred claims in this city and I have never read a slot and I did not know until this morning that there was such a word in this trade.**"

"Read it," said the tenth hand.

She read it.

> **THE EIGHTH PLACE: A SLOT, TO BE FILLED BY A PERSON WHO CAN HOLD A SIGNAL.**

"**There,**" said Rutt. "**That is the fourth bearer, and I have just read it out, and it took about a second and a half, and it is the only line in a public document in this city that takes a second and a half to say, and it took the second and a half because there is no person in it.**

"And I want to say what a reader does with that, and I do not know how to say it, so I am going to say it the way I would say it at the counter. **If a woman brought me a claim with nothing in it, I would not tell her it was false. I would read her the lines that are in it and I would say, and here is the shape of the hole, and the hole is about the size of a name, and you have not put a name in it, and I cannot put one in for you, and a blank is not a refusal and it is not a yes.**"

"And it is the third thing," said Nessa Pell. "A draft is not a proposal and a draft is not a refusal."

"**It is a third thing and nobody has a name for it either, and I have started to think that is the shape of this whole month,**" said Rutt. "**A mark, a slot, and a holding entry. Three words for a person who is not there, and every one of them is doing the same job, and the job is so that a person does not have to be a person to be counted.**"

And Orren Pike said, at about eight feet, in a voice nobody expected, "**There are three signal hands in this district who are not the tenth hand and I can name all three and Nobody has ever asked me whether I want the job, and nobody has ever asked any of the three of them either, and I have been paid nothing for nine weeks for an index of a line book that has now found a hole in it, and both of those facts go next to each other or the book is not honest.**"

"And if we asked you?"

"**Then I would tell you that the eighth place has a curve in it and a shoulder with a drop of nine feet and nowhere to stand, and that I am seventeen and I have never held a pair, and I would want to be asked again in a year.**"

---

**And then Mara Quill asked him to close it, and she asked him properly, with the book open, and she knew the answer, and it is in the book that she asked anyway.**

"Adrian."

"**No,**" he said, and it was about a second and a half, and there is no speech in front of it and there is none after it, and it is the shortest refusal of anything in this volume.

"I want it on the record that I am asking in the ordinary way and that I have read the entry of the twelfth of this month and the entry of the eighteenth and I know what you can and cannot do and I have asked in the ordinary way because you have spent six weeks telling this district that a refusal nobody asks for is not a refusal."

"Then say it into the book, and say it long."

"**No, Mara. And I am not going to say it long to you, because you are the registrar and you have the book and you have said to me in a yard on the twenty-first that there is no one in this district who can do it and you were right and I have printed it on eleven boards. You have known since the twelfth. And I have written a sheet and squared it with my thumb and read it out in daylight, and neither of those two things was necessary, and I did them because somebody was going to be disappointed in about an hour and I have been disappointed by other people in this yard about nine times this month and I know exactly what it costs.**"

"Then give the three reasons again, in one sentence, for the page."

"**I cannot close it, I cannot secure it, and there is a hole in it with a slot in the hole.**"

"That is the whole of your case and it is correct," said Mara Quill, "**and the whole of my case is that a document nobody can close is the best thing in this district's public record in a month, and I have written it up, and it is going on the wall tomorrow, and read the wall sheet before you leave the yard, and not because it says anything about you.**"

---

**And on the first of the next month, at about eleven in the morning, in the yard of the Needle Road House, in front of about nine of his own men, Tarin Kest asked him again, and this time he gave a reason, and the reason was not the same as Mara Quill's, and Adrian Vale's second no was four times as long as his first.**

"I have surrendered a lease," said Tarin Kest. "**I have nineteen vehicles and four spaces in this city and seven a week with nowhere to stand, and a stabling yard that a good half my work was built on, and a standing on that line that I have had since I was thirty-four and that four other Road Houses have tried to buy off me twice. And I did all of that on Wednesday because a woman asked me a question about water and I could not answer it, and I would do it again on Friday, and I am not asking you for a favour. I am asking the man who wrote the proposal schedule to close the one document in this district he is legally permitted to write and not permitted to close, and get it done before my foreman finds out and comes and asks me why we are idle.**

"**Can you close it?**"

"**No.**

"**Say the reason.**"

"**The reason I am not going to say is that the three reasons I said on Wednesday are the reasons, and they have not changed, and if I say them a second time in a yard with nine of your men in it then the three reasons are a thing I say at Road Houses, and in about a year they will be a thing I say at a counter, and a reason that is in your ear and mine is not a reason. It is a thing somebody has learned.**

"And the other reason is the word. **Close.** I have been asked by the registrar and by a mechanic and in both cases somebody wanted the same word and it means two different things and one of the two things is me. I am a Stage 1 Reader. I have drafted a split. **A draft is a man holding a door open and asking a room to fill it. Closing it is putting my name on it, and my name on it is standing, and standing is what a man gives when he is replaceable, and I am not replaceable yet and there is a man at the rail in this district who has told me in a yard that I ought to be, and he is right, and he is right in about a year and not in a yard, and if I close this thing this month then the thing is closed by the one man in this district who is not replaceable, and it is the same sentence I have been saying for six weeks except that this time it is in a document and not in chalk.**"

"And your foreman."

"**Will find out on Friday, will come and ask me why we are idle, and I will tell him, and he will be angry, and he has a right to be, and I will not tell him the reason and he will know that is deliberate, and I will have to live in that.**"

**"That is the second time anybody has asked you and it is in the book, and the second no is four times the first, and the room recorded that the two reasons were not the same and did not enter an opinion on whether that was good."**

---

**And at about a quarter to seven in the morning on the second of the next month, before the table opened, in a kitchen under the Ward Nine tram line, Adrian Vale and Mara Quill passed a slate, and there is no account of what was on it in any file in this district, and neither of them said one word about anything that happened in the yard, and the exchange's bank is not in the book and has never been in the book.**

**And what is in the book about the second is the draft, and the wall sheet, and the fact that the board published a document that cannot be closed, and the reason it published it.**

> **PUBLISHED THE SECOND OF THIS MONTH, ALL FOUR SEATS NAMED ALOUD, BY THE BOARD, WHICH CANNOT GRANT A USE AND CANNOT CLOSE ANYTHING AND CAN PUBLISH A RESULT:**
>
> **A DRAFT SPLIT OF THE USE OF THE HARROW TONGUE AMONG FOUR BEARERS AT FOUR PLACES. IT IS PUBLISHED IN FULL. IT IS NOT A PROPOSAL IN THE MEANING OF THE SCHEDULE, BECAUSE THE SCHEDULE IS FOR A USE SOMEBODY IS PROPOSING AND THIS IS A USE THAT HAS BEEN PERFORMED NINETEEN TIMES. IT IS NOT A CHARTER, NOT A TERMS-CLOSING, NOT A PAYMENT, AND NOT A READING.**
>
> **AND IT CANNOT BE CLOSED. THE READER WHO DRAFTED IT IS A STAGE 1 READER AND MAY NOT CLOSE A SPLIT, MAY NOT TRANSFER A USE, MAY NOT RENEW ONE, AND MAY NOT POST A SECURITY, AND HE SAID ALL FOUR OF THOSE IN A ROOM IN HIS OWN WORDS AND WAS ASKED TWICE TO CLOSE IT AND SAID NO TWICE, AND THE SECOND NO WAS FOUR TIMES AS LONG AS THE FIRST.**
>
> **AND THE BOARD PUBLISHES IT BECAUSE A DOCUMENT NOBODY CAN CLOSE SHOULD NOT BE IN A DRAWER. AND THE BOARD HAS NO POWER TO MAKE IT CLOSABLE. AND THE GAP IS ENTERED AS THE SAME GAP AS THE FIFTH THING AND IT IS THE SAME GAP, AND IT IS DATED.**
