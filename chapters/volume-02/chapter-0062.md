# Chapter 62: The Meter Book

The book that turned out to matter was not a rate sheet and it was not in a public book, and it was the fourth book of nine that Ivo Stenn went and asked to see between the twenty-second and the twenty-fourth of this month, and the first two of them he did not get, and the reason he did not get them was not anybody's fault, which was unusual enough that he wrote it down.

The first was the ward roll of the two blocks west of the depot, and there is no ward roll of the two blocks west of the depot, because the two blocks are in no ward, and the reason the two blocks are in no ward is that the two blocks are the foot of two hundred and sixty-one steps of river stair and the stair has no vehicle road up it, and a man who surveyed this district for a roll in the flood year went as far as the bottom of the stair and could not turn round, and his sheet says in his own hand at the bottom of the page: *no cart road. did not ascend.*

The second was the household book of the ninth place, and there has never been one, and the reason there has never been one is that nobody with a household book has ever been to the ninth place. And the reason nobody with a household book has ever been to the ninth place is on a public sheet in eleven boards, in a woman's own words, said on a bank in the rain on the fifteenth of this month: we have never once been a household, we are a place, and you cannot lose what was never entered, and that is not a mercy, that is a hole with a shape of our own making. A household book is a thing you take to a household, and there has never been one at the ninth place, and there is not one now, and the absence is the sheet's fault and not the village's.

And a third book was asked for and refused by a person entitled to refuse it, and the refusal is in the file, and it is the only refusal in the file, and it came from the woman of about sixty-one at the fourth house on the afternoon of the twenty-third, and it is in the file in her words because the clerk wrote it down without changing one of them: you want a list of who lives where, and I can give you that, and I have given you that, and I have been giving it to you for nine years in the only shape that works, and if you take it away from me in a new shape then somebody is going to lose a door they know.

Which is true. Stenn entered it as a refusal with the reason attached and not as an obstruction, and then he did the thing he has done every time in two days, which is to write the reason under the refusal and ask whether the reason holds, and it did, and he said so out loud in a counting room with the shutters open, and nobody argued with him about it.

And the fourth one was in a drawer in a counting room in the ward market district, in a deal box with eleven others in it, and it was the meter book of the Needle Road House, and it was taken out and put on a table at about two in the afternoon on the twenty-third by a man of forty-three who had asked for it and had not been asked for it, and who said one sentence when he put it down.

"**It is thirty-six coppers for nine years,**" said Tarin Kest, "**and I have never known what it was worth and I have never asked, because it was on an invoice and I pay invoices.**"

---

**And then Stenn did the arithmetic that the whole of the next three days is built out of, and he did it in a counting room with the door shut and the shutters open, and he has entered in his own hand that he did it in private before he did it in public, and why.**

He had promised a rate sheet for ground nobody has claimed on the thirteenth of this month, in a counting room, in front of about thirty people, and he had given it a fortnight, and the fortnight runs out on the twenty-eighth. He had done it out of the fourth column of the paper levy, and the fourth column had nine coppers in it on the thirteenth and a bushel of chalk and a lamp at the ninth place attached to it since the eighth, and he had said in public that he was aware he was a freight clerk and not a man who walks about asking people where they lived.

He walked. On the twenty-third he went up the two blocks on foot and came back down and did it again in the evening, because he did not trust the first one.

> **THE TWO BLOCKS WEST OF THE DEPOT. TWENTY-TWO PEOPLE. ELEVEN ROOMS. SIX ROOMS WITH SOMEBODY IN THEM THAT ANYBODY COULD FIND SOMEBODY IN, AND FIVE ROOMS WITH NOBODY IN THEM THAT ANYBODY COULD FIND, AND THE FIVE ARE NOT EMPTY AND ARE NOT ABANDONED AND ARE NOT IN ANY STATE THAT CAN BE PRINTED ON A SHEET.**
>
> **ENTERED AS SIX HOUSEHOLDS, BECAUSE THAT IS WHAT THE FORM ASKS FOR. THE FORM IS NOT STRUCK. THE FORM IS WRONG ABOUT THIS PLACE AND IT IS NOT WRONG ABOUT ANY OTHER PLACE IN THIS CITY, AND THE ONLY REASON IT IS WRONG ABOUT THIS PLACE IS THAT THERE ARE NO STEPS INTO IT.**

Then he went out on the ballast on the twenty-third, five miles, with the tenth hand and a man who is paid nothing, because the boy had heard him say on the twenty-second that he was going to have to walk onto the ground and count and had put his hand up and been told to, and neither of them said a word about it in either direction.

**And at the ninth place there were four houses, occupied eleven, seven, nine and four, and a chapel with a bell that is not rung, and thirty-one people, and the count had been taken on the fifteenth by a reader and put in a public book, and it was the same count, and the first line of the rate sheet is the first line because of what happened in about nine minutes at the fourth house.**

The woman at the column had told him on the twenty-second, and she had told it to him as a fact and not as a favour: **the count of thirty-one is right and the count has been right since the fifteenth and it does not need doing again.** What it needed was a household, because a rate sheet is a list of households, and there is no household, and everybody at the ninth place fills in the form like a household because that is what the form is.

So Stenn stood at the fourth house door at about half past two in the afternoon on the twenty-third and asked the question he had come out with, and the woman who opened the door was about sixty-one, and she is the one who walked to Alder Reach on the footbridge in the flood year with a child and is the only yes in the count of departures, and she answered the freight clerk's question in four sentences and it took the whole of a rate sheet out of his hands.

"**You have come up that bank to find out how many people are in this house.**"

"Yes."

"**There are three in it and I have said so every time anybody has asked me in nine years, and there is a woman of sixty-one in it and I am the woman of sixty-one, and I have filled your form in twice and both times it wanted four houses and a chapel, because a form that wants a household is going to get a household out of a place every time you put it in front of one.**"

"Then tell me what to put in it."

"**Put the place.**" She did not raise her voice and she did not go inside. "**You want a sheet with a rate on it and a rate is on a thing that is the same shape as a thing with a rate on it in a street in this city, and this is not that, and you have walked five miles to find that out and it has taken you two days and I have had nine years.**"

And Ivo Stenn stood on a bank nine miles west of his own desk and understood that he was being told to write a rate sheet in a shape that no rate sheet in the ward market's history has ever been in, and that the woman telling him to do it was sixty-one years old and had never been asked anything by anybody, and that **a rate sheet is a document that says a place is a place, and there is no line on any sheet in this district for that, and a man cannot invent a line on a rate sheet and call it the same sheet.**

---

**So he went back and wrote it in three evenings, and the three evenings are the work, and the result went to the counting room on the twenty-eighth, and this is the sheet, and it is the first sheet this market has ever produced that does not begin with a street.**

> **A RATE SHEET FOR GROUND NOBODY HAS CLAIMED. MADE BY I. STENN, FREIGHT CLERK, OUT OF THE FOURTH COLUMN, ON A FORTNIGHT PROMISED IN A COUNTING ROOM ON THE THIRTEENTH OF THIS MONTH. PUBLISHED THE TWENTY-EIGHTH.**
>
> **FIRST LINE. A RATE SHEET IS A DOCUMENT THAT SAYS A PLACE IS A PLACE. THE LINES UNDER IT ARE ABOUT MONEY AND THE FIRST LINE IS NOT, AND THE REASON THE FIRST LINE IS ON THE SHEET AT ALL IS THAT THIRTY-ONE PEOPLE WERE ON A BANK NINE MILES WEST ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THIS MONTH AND WERE COUNTED AS FOUR HOUSES, AND THE COUNT WAS WRONG AND THE FORM WAS WRONG AND NEITHER WAS STRUCK.**
>
> **THE NINTH PLACE. CALLED IN THE LINE BOOK BY THE FIGURE ONE MILE AND THREE QUARTERS AND BY NOTHING ELSE. FOUR HOUSES OCCUPIED ELEVEN, SEVEN, NINE, FOUR. A CHAPEL WITH A BELL THAT IS NOT RUNG. THIRTY-ONE PEOPLE. NO ROAD UP TO IT BUT THE ONE THEY USE.**
>
> **THE TWO BLOCKS WEST OF THE DEPOT. TWENTY-TWO PEOPLE. ELEVEN ROOMS, SIX OCCUPIED.**

And then the columns, and the middle one is empty in the way the middle one has been empty three times in nine days, and this time it is empty on a sheet that is about money.

> **WHAT THE PLACE IS WORTH AT THIS MARKET'S RATE, FIFTY COPPERS A QUARTER ON EVERY HOUSEHOLD OF TWO ADULTS AND ABOVE: THE NINTH PLACE, FOUR HOUSEHOLDS, TWO HUNDRED COPPERS A QUARTER. THE TWO BLOCKS, SIX HOUSEHOLDS, THREE HUNDRED COPPERS A QUARTER. THE WHOLE OF THE GROUND NOBODY HAS CLAIMED IS FIVE HUNDRED COPPERS A QUARTER.**
>
> **WHAT IT COSTS TO BE A PLACE IN A BOOK, TO THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF THIS MONTH, IN FIGURES THIS MARKET HAS ACTUALLY PAID OR REFUSED TO PAY: NINE COPPERS A QUARTER FOR A LAMP AND A BUSHEL OF CHALK. FOUR DAYS A WEEK AT ELEVEN COPPERS A QUARTER FOR A READER WHO HAS WALKED NINE MILES ON IT AND TOLD NOBODY. ELEVEN COPPERS A WEEK FOR A SECOND LAMP ON A RIVER STAIR, UNPAID AND NOT AS NIL. AND NOTHING ELSE, BECAUSE NOTHING ELSE HAS BEEN ASKED FOR.**
>
> **AND THE FINDING, WHICH IS THE FIRST LINE OF THE SECOND HALF OF THIS SHEET, AND IT IS THE REASON THE SHEET IS LONGER THAN THE OTHER ONE:**
>
> **THE NINTH PLACE IS WORTH TWO HUNDRED COPPERS A QUARTER AND THE COST OF HAVING BEEN ASKED TWICE IS ALREADY AT TWO HUNDRED AND FOUR COPPERS. A PLACE THAT CANNOT PAY FOR ITS OWN INSTRUMENTS CANNOT BE PUT ON A SHEET WITHOUT MAKING THE SHEET WRONG. A MARKET CANNOT LEVY A HOUSEHOLD IT HAS NOT MET AND IT ALSO CANNOT RUN AN INSTRUMENT AT A PLACE THAT CANNOT CARRY IT, AND THE SECOND OF THOSE THINGS IS NEW AND IT IS THE ONE THAT MADE THIRTY-ONE PEOPLE INVISIBLE, AND IT IS NOT PREJUDICE. PREJUDICE WOULD HAVE BEEN CHEAPER.**

---

**And the meter book is the fourth book and it is in the file, and nobody asked for it, and it is two columns of figures on thirty-six pages, and it is the reason the second half of that sheet is as long as it is.**

The column on the left is a date, one per quarter, nine years of them, thirty-six. The column on the right is a reading off a brass plate on a concrete base on the best ground for two miles, and the plate is stamped with the name of the Needle Road House and under the name, in smaller letters, a number.

"**Four coppers a quarter,**" said Stenn, at about four in the afternoon on the twenty-third, to the ward market's own second clerk, who had come in because a man in a counting room cannot work out a sum alone and is not ashamed of it. "Nine years. Thirty-six readings. **Thirty-six coppers.**"

"That is the price of a bushel of seed," said Wren Ostler.

"**That is the price of four coppers, and this market has charged four coppers a quarter off every household in this city for eleven years for public notice and printed matter, and it has printed nothing since the flood year, and the paper levy at forty-one was collected for eleven years on a promise of a sheet and no sheet was ever bought.**" He put his finger on the two columns. "**And here is a well. On a village's best ground. Drawn off twice a week by four men a week for nine years by men who send a barrow and fill it and stand on the hard ground and go, and the whole price of it is the same four coppers, and this market cannot tell the difference between the four coppers for nothing and the four coppers for a well, because there is no line on any sheet in this district that a well can be put on.**"

"So which of the two do you put on the sheet?"

"**Neither. I put both, and the second is the finding, and the finding is that a rate sheet in this district is a list of things that can be charged to a house, and a meter is a thing that can be charged to a house, and that is the only reason it works, and the reason it does not work is that nobody has ever charged anything to a village.**"

---

**And then the chandler came in on the twenty-fourth, because the chandler comes in on the twenty-fourth, and she is a woman of about fifty named Hessa Krell and she has a stall and she has voted in every rate this market has taken for six years and she has objected in every one.**

"You have made a sheet for a village," she said.

"I have made a sheet for two pieces of ground and one of them is nine miles west."

"**You have made a sheet for a village, and you have done it out of the fourth column, and the fourth column is the one everybody stopped looking at three weeks ago because a bushel of chalk went into it, and now it has a village in it, and the village is worth two hundred coppers a quarter.**" She put her hand flat on the table, which she does, and which is why her trade has never once been accused of being friendly. "**Do you know what two hundred coppers is? It is a quarter of what one of my eleven houses pays to have four coppers of oil that does not arrive. It is the whole of what a widow with a room takes in a quarter to keep the room. And you have gone nine miles and come back and written it on a sheet, and now there is a figure in this city for what a village is worth, and it is a small one, and it is going to be in a book.**"

"Yes."

"**Then say the other half, Mr. Stenn, because you have been the man in this room who says the other half since the eighth of this month and I have watched you do it four times.**"

"**The other half is that the figure is small because nobody has ever said what the place is for,**" said Stenn, "**and if I put two hundred coppers on a sheet in a book and nothing else, then in four years a stranger will take that book cold and read that a village of thirty-one people is worth two hundred coppers a quarter, and he will be right about the arithmetic and he will be wrong about everything else, and there will be no column anywhere in this district that he can check it against, and that is the fault of this sheet and not of the man who wrote it, and I am writing it anyway because a sheet with a hole in it and a date on it is worth more to a stranger than no sheet.**"

"And when does the money arrive?"

"It does not, and I want that printed on it and not said out loud in a room, so that nobody can be surprised in a quarter."

"**Print it,**" said Hessa Krell.

---

**And on the walk out on the twenty-third there was a boy of seventeen with a copy of the line book in a bag, and he was paid nothing then and he is paid nothing still, and he came because a man in a counting room said *I am going to have to walk onto the ground and count and I do not know how to ask, and you are the only person in this district who has ever asked a question out loud and got an answer he did not like.***

He did the thing he does, which is to put two books side by side on a bench and work down them without speaking, and he had a sheet of his own under his coat and he did not take it out.

**The line book at the east end is indexed to nine hundred and six movements since the flood year, of which one hundred and forty-one name the ninth place as the origin held, and the tenth hand has two hundred and eleven of the nine hundred and six, and the keeper's own hut book has sixty-one and four of those in a second ink because she found them wrong and would not rub out her own.** All of that went onto the rate sheet, in a column headed *what this place is in a book about trains*, which is not a column that has ever existed on a rate sheet in this district and which Stenn headed himself and regrets nothing about.

And the boy put his finger on two of the columns and said the thing he has been carrying in a coat for ten days and which he has said out loud to exactly three people, and Stenn was the third, and it is in the rate sheet with a line under it saying that the two things are in two lines and are not to be joined.

"You and Sef's four are not the same four," said Orren Pike.

"I do not know Sef Anyard's column," said Stenn.

"**It is the column headed ORIGIN HELD and she has kept it eleven years and about four of the entries in it are not places, and one of them is spelled out in full in nine words, and it is the name of an office and it is not a guild and it is not in any register anybody in this basin can reach.** And yours has four that are not places and it is not the same four, so there is a fifth in a column of sixty-one that the line book has not got."

Stenn looked at it for about four seconds.

"Then it is two lines," he said, "**and not one, and the reason is that a book that joins two things because they are the same shape is a book that has made a claim about something it cannot check, and a rate sheet is the first document in this district that a stranger is going to take cold and use.**"

"That is not my reason. My reason is in a public book and the registrar agreed to keep them apart."

"Then I will use your reason and cite you," said Ivo Stenn, "**and if you ever want citing in a rate sheet you will have to be paid, and that is a different conversation and I am having it in about a month.**"

---

**And on the twenty-eighth the sheet was published, and the first line of it was read out at the counter by Rutt at six in the morning and again at nine and again at noon, in the words people did not bring with them, and about two hundred and forty people heard it in two days, and one line of it was read out four times because a man at the back asked for it four times.**

> **AND FIVE HUNDRED COPPERS A QUARTER IS ENTERED AND IS NOT COLLECTED, AND THE REASON IS DATED AND IS NOT A REASON ABOUT A PERSON: THE SHEET WAS MADE ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF THIS MONTH AND THE QUARTER BEGAN ON THE FIRST, AND A RATE IS COLLECTED IN THE QUARTER IN WHICH IT IS VOTED, AND A MARKET CANNOT GO BACK, AND THEREFORE THE NINTH PLACE'S TWO HUNDRED COPPERS ARRIVES IN THE NEXT QUARTER AND NOT BEFORE, AND THE TWO BLOCKS' THREE HUNDRED ARRIVES WITH IT, AND NOBODY HAS BEEN PAID ANYTHING YET AGAIN.**

And it was still true on the twenty-eighth that nobody had been paid anything, and the two lines about it are in the book four feet apart, and the one at the foot of the eighteenth of this month is Adrian Vale's and the one at the foot of the twenty-eighth is a freight clerk's, and **the tenth hand wrote under the pair of them the only thing on the page that he wanted written, and it is not a number and it is not an instrument.**

> *Two entries, nineteen days apart, both of them saying the same thing in different hands, and the hands belong to the reader and to the clerk, and neither of them is the hand that is owed anything. I have got a page of my own for numbers I cannot put anywhere. This is a pair of them and I have not got a box for a pair.*
