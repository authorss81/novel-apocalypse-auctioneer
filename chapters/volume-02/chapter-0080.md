# Chapter 80: The Twenty-Ninth of Last Month

At about seven in the morning on the first of this month a man of about forty-six stood in front of a sheet of paper on a board in Alder Reach and asked a stranger what it meant now, and the sentence he was asking about was the third line from the bottom and it read *held by two named people until the twenty-ninth of this month*, and it had been printed on the third of last month in a hand set by a man of seventy, and on the first of this month it was four weeks true and nobody had touched it.

The second sheet is on a tram arch a street away and it says MONDAY THE FIFTEENTH OF THIS MONTH across the top in the same size of type as the price. It is the fifteenth of last month. Both of them are in last month now. **A stranger can take either of them cold and neither of them is wrong, and one of them was a lie and one of them was a truth, and it is not on the paper which is which, and that is the first month anybody in this district has ever gone through with a date on a wall.**

---

**And the board published its fifteenth part on the first of this month at about eleven in the morning with all four seats named aloud, and it is the shortest part it has ever published and it is in two halves, and the two halves are the same west end.**

The register, the half about a line of chalk sixty yards west of a water column, with a keeper and a woman who is the water, renewable only by asking them both again in a room with the asking and the hour and the answer entered whatever the answer is. And the half about a hut, with a signalman of a river office, one month, renewed by the office, nobody asked.

> **15. THE WEST END OF THE NEEDLE LINE. HELD IN TWO HALVES. ASKED IN A ROOM AND NOT ASKED. BOTH FOR ONE MONTH. THE ASKING, THE HOUR, THE ANSWERS AND THE READING-BACK ARE IN THE BOOK AND THE SHEET IS IN A SLEEVE AND THE ROOM IS NINE MILES AWAY AND NOTHING IN THIS ENTRY HAS BEEN SEEN BY A MEMBER OF THIS BOARD.**
>
> **IT CONFERS NO USE. IT AUTHORISES NOBODY TO MOVE ANYTHING. IT SEATS NOTHING. AND THE BOARD HAS NO POWER TO ASK A PERSON WHETHER THEY WILL HOLD A LINE, AND TWO PEOPLE WERE ASKED BY A SIGNALMAN WHO IS NOT A MEMBER OF THIS BOARD AND CANNOT BE PAID, AND THE ASKING IS IN THE BOOK.**

And under the two halves, all four of the standing failures, gathered for the first time onto one page, dated, and **not struck.**

> **STANDING FAILURES, ENTERED AND NOT STRUCK.**
>
> **ONE. THE LINE OF CHALK CANNOT BE SEEN FROM THE WEST END OF THE SIDING, WHICH IS THE END IT IS FOR. THE FIXING IS ONE BUSHEL OF WHITE PAINT AND ABOUT A HUNDRED AND FORTY YARDS OF IT. THE RATE VOTED ON THE SECOND OF LAST MONTH BOUGHT FOURTEEN PINTS. FOURTEEN PINTS IS NOT A BUSHEL. FOUND ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF LAST MONTH BY A WOMAN CARRYING A PAIL, GOING DOWN.**
>
> **TWO. THE WORD IS A WORD ABOUT PEOPLE AND NOT ABOUT A TRAIN AND HAS BEEN SINCE THE SIXTH OF LAST MONTH, AND THE TWO WOMEN WHO HOLD IT HAVE NOT BEEN ASKED ABOUT THAT AND ARE NOT GOING TO BE, BECAUSE A WORD DOES NOT NEED ITS AUTHORS TO AGREE WITH IT. THEIR WORDS, ENTERED AT THEIR REQUEST.**
>
> **THREE. THE LINE DOES NOT KNOW IT HAS STOPPED WORKING. A LINE IS NOT A PERSON AND THERE IS NOBODY STANDING AT THE END OF IT TO FIND OUT. THE NEAREST THING TO A PERSON AT THIS END OF IT IS A WOMAN OF FIFTY-ONE WHO KNEW ON THE TWELFTH OF LAST MONTH AND SAID SO OUT LOUD.**
>
> **FOUR. THE HUT IS HELD BY AN OFFICE THAT RENEWS ITSELF AND ASKS NOBODY, AND THE OFFICE CONSIDERS THE PLACE UNSAFE, AND THE OFFICE LOST A VOTE BY SEVEN TO THREE WITH ONE ABSTENTION IN MARCH.**

And the sentence the registrar wrote under the four, at about half past eleven in the morning on the first of this month, in her own hand, and she entered that it is hers and worse and is the fifth time she has written a worse reason under her own findings, and it is nine words long.

> **A FAILURE THAT IS ENTERED IS NOT FIXED. IT IS KNOWN.**

---

At ten o'clock on the first of this month, at the ward market counter, a reader read the whole of the twenty-ninth of last month out loud in the ordinary voice, and it took eleven minutes, and about seventy people stood there, and it was the first time that counter has read out a set of answers instead of a list of refusals, and the finding is in her hand and it is four lines long.**

"**It is harder,**" said Rutt, afterwards, when she was asked about it and had not expected to be. "**A refusal is a thing somebody did. I can read nineteen of them out in four minutes and I have done it and they are all the same shape. An answer is a thing somebody gave, and giving does not have a shape, and about half of it was a woman explaining how a place works to four people who have been wrong about it for a month, and I read that out in a clerk's voice in a market and about nine people did not look at the sheet at all, and the ninth was a boy of fifteen who had read it before I did.**"

The sheet she read had both answers on it and the request on it, and the request was one line, and the request was *read that back on the first, at that counter, out loud, in the ordinary voice, with both my answers under it, and I want the words the line cannot be seen from the west end in the reading-back, because that is the sentence that is going to be true on the day somebody needs it*, and the words were in the reading-back, in her place, in the correct order, and the finding is that **a person asked to be read back can name the sentence they need repeated, and nobody in this district had thought to ask anybody that, and it is the fourth time in eleven weeks that a person has corrected a document by saying what they needed in it, and three of the four are women.**

---

**And on the morning of the second of this month two figures entered the ward market's book that had never been in it, and the second of them is the reason a man who has spent six years saying no has started a column, and the column has one line in it and the line is four coppers of an old gap carried for the fourth time.**

Ivo Stenn asked the question himself, in the counting room, at about ten, and he had asked her on the sixteenth of last month and been told that she would rather be asked, and he had not asked again for a fortnight, and the minute says that the reason he waited a fortnight is that he had to work out whether it was a rate.

She is called Hessa Krell and she has a stall, and she is the woman who took the ward market's run of five hundred on her own trade three times, on the same figure each time, and the figure in the gap between the price of a run of five hundred and the twelve coppers a quarter the printed-matter column buys is **eight hundred and ninety-four coppers**, carried on a trade in the gap, unpaid and entered and not as nil.

"**It has been carried three times,**" he said. "**I want to ask you the thing I asked you on the sixteenth and I have thought about it for a fortnight and here it is. Is it a rate.**"

"**Three times is a favour,**" said Hessa Krell. "**I did it three times because a man of seventy cannot read a market's minute and a market's minute is not going to be read to him, and I have done it three times and I have put my stall's name at the bottom of all three in type the size of the date, and I have not once been thanked, and I did not want to be thanked, and I am not being thanked now.**"

"That is not the question I asked."

"**It is a rate,**" she said. "**And I have known that since the second one and I have been waiting a fortnight for a clerk to come and ask me, and I would like it in your book that a woman of about fifty with a stall has been doing a rate for three months for nothing and has been waiting fourteen days for somebody to give her a column, and that I am not going to chase anybody up a street about it, and if nobody had asked me on the sixteenth I would have carried it a fourth time and a fifth and it would still have been a rate, because a rate is not a thing you are thanked for. A rate is a thing that is on you.**"

And the column was opened on the second of this month in the counting room of the ward market, and it is headed by four words, and there is one line in it, and the line is the fourth carry, and the figure on it is **eight hundred and ninety-four coppers, unpaid, carried four times, and the entry under it says that a printing rate of six coppers a quarter buys one sheet a year and one sheet costs nine hundred and six, and that the market has known this since the second of last month, and that the column was opened on the second of this month and not before because a rate is not a rate until somebody who is owed it says so.**

And the reason the whole of that is in the book on the second is that a chandler on the lower terrace, on a viaduct, on the eighteenth of last month, wrote a line in a pocket book about a man of seventeen reading his own questions out of a book, and a rate for a railway came out of a stall in Alder Reach six weeks after a man in a good coat printed a date on a sheet, and neither of them knows the other exists.

---

---

The ninth can of oil in the fortnight went up the bank on the first of this month, and it is entered, and the entry is the last line in the twenty-ninth of April's sheet and it was put there by the trader and not by a clerk.**

Nine cans, at seven coppers, is sixty-three coppers, and it went on a cart belonging to a firm that gave up a lease on the thirtieth of last month and is standing in a shed off a yard in a city nine miles east, and it went on the trade of a stall on the lower terrace that now carries a column with one line in it, and the trader put her own name against the sixty-three and did not ask for it to be put anywhere else, and the finding under her line is nine words long and it is hers.

> **HE HAS GOT FOUR WEEKS OF OIL. NOBODY HAS ASKED HIM ABOUT THE FIFTH.**

The engine at the Harrow end is nine years old and it is paid for out of a river office's works rate at a hundred and eighty coppers a quarter and the office has been paying that for nine years out of a return it sent to a market that read it every quarter, and the oil has never been in that column, and the man who puts his hand on the pipe at six every evening is sixty-eight and is given what is left in a tin, and the whole cost of the west end of that railway for nine years is twenty-one thousand two hundred and twenty-two coppers in two books in two rooms in two hands, and not one document in this district contains that figure, and the figure exists now because a man of seventeen asked a question in the wrong order in a room with a fire in it.

---

**And the key to the case of Lot 17 turned three times in this month, and the sentence on the notice board under the two lapses has three lines under it now, and nobody took the sentence down, and the third line is the only thing anybody expected to happen, and it happened.**

A rotation is only real when it turns. A board that asks at the end of a term has not asked in time. A board that cannot ask at all and leaves it to a crowd is a board whose rotation is a weather.

Underneath, in a fair hand, the third line is dated the first of this month and it is nine words long, and the reason it is nine words long is that it took a chandler of about fifty-six four minutes in a yard and would not let anybody improve it.

> **A YARD ASKED AND A YARD GOT A YES, AND A YARD IS NOT A BOARD.**

**And the thing this district has not got is still not got, and it is in the book on the first of this month in two lines that are not joined, and the registrar has been asked to keep them apart and has agreed, and the reason she gives is the reason she gives every time.**

A licensed valuer of the Valuers' Guild, twelve years in, two hundred and forty lots, nine of them wrong, wrote on the fourteenth of last month: **the lot is clean and cannot be sold to a village**, and gave the reason, which is that nineteen takings, one object, and no instrument in this basin that can name the place being charged, because a document that cannot name what is being sold cannot be given to anybody.

A market printed on the third of last month a column at full width in the middle of a page with nothing in it, and printed the reason inside it, and then on the seventeenth of last month printed a second line under it with a date and an hour on it, and the second line says the column is empty and will stay empty until somebody in this district can state the amount of an origin, and nobody can, and that is not a secret and it is a gap.

> **THE COLUMN IS STILL EMPTY. THE WORD IS STILL THE WORD, AND IT IS NOT CROSS-DISTRICT PASSAGE, AND IT IS NOT A GUILD, AND NOBODY IN THIS BASIN CAN FIND THAT OFFICE IN ANY REGISTER THEY CAN REACH, AND THE NAME IS SPELLED OUT IN FOUR ENTRIES IN A COLUMN OF SIXTY-ONE THAT THE LINE BOOK HAS NOT GOT, AND THE BOY WHO FOUND THE FIFTH ONE WROTE IT DOWN AND THEN STRUCK IT AND WROTE IT ON A SHEET, AND THE SHEET IS IN A SLEEVE AT 411, AND THE REGISTRAR WAS TOLD IN MARCH AND HAS AGREED TO KEEP IT IN ONE LINE, AND IT IS NOT ON A WALL THIS MONTH, AND THAT IS ALSO ENTERED.**

The finding under it, in the tenth hand's hand, is that **the page he keeps in a coat for numbers he cannot put anywhere now has four shapes of the same hole on it and he has stopped trying to name them, and this is a change in him and is entered as a change, and it is the only thing in this month's book that is about the man rather than about the thing, and he asked for it not to be published and it was published.**

---

**And on the second of this month, at a quarter to seven in the morning, before the table opened, in a kitchen under the Ward Nine tram line, Adrian Vale and Mara Quill passed a slate, and neither of them said one word about anything that has happened since the twenty-ninth of last month, and the exchange's bank is not in the book and has never been in the book, and there is still no account in any file in this district of what was on it.**

There is one difference, and it is in a reader's hand, in the counter's own book of read-alouds, and Rutt entered it herself on the second and nobody asked her to.

"**A slate went past in that kitchen on the morning of the second of this month at a quarter to seven,**" she wrote, "**and I was not in the room and I know it was a slate because the woman at the counter next to me watched it go past and said so, and I have written this down because four people have now told me that they saw a slate go past in that kitchen in seven weeks and every one of them told me about it after the fact and about nine days late, and I am the fifth person to be told and the first to write it down.**

"**AND I AM NOT GOING TO ASK. I would like it entered that I am not going to ask, and that I know exactly why I am not going to ask, and that the reason is that the answer would be in a room and not in a book, and that this district has spent seven weeks insisting that a thing which is not in a book does not exist, and that I have stopped insisting, and that this is a change in me and is not entered in a column.**"

And he noticed that she had written it, and he did not ask, and the entry says that he noticed and did not ask, and that **not asked is in the count, and the count is the only reason any of the rest of it is honest.**

---

The last two things in this month's book are a number and a morning, and they are on the third of this month, and the first is a train and the second is a word.**

The train on the siding arrived on the eleventh of February and on the third of this month it had stood **eighty-one days**, and the figure is in the index in a boy's hand, and beside it there are now three dates on one line, and nobody has put them in a column.

The twenty-ninth of April, asked, in a room, by a signalman, with the hour and both answers entered.

The twenty-fifth of May, when a hut in an office's name comes to the end of a month that an office renewed by itself.

The twenty-ninth of May, when somebody has to go up a bank again, and the term says they have to ask, and the only thing this district has learned in nine weeks that is worth carrying up a bank is that a term is not a promise, it is a question somebody has to remember to ask.

And nobody in Alder Reach has been given the job of going up that bank on the twenty-ninth of this month. That is the finding of the third of this month and it is the shortest entry in the book and the twentieth of its kind in nine weeks, and it is not a complaint, and it is the condition the term was written under: renewable by nobody except by asking both of them again, in a room, with the asking and the hour and the answer entered, whatever the answer is. **Nobody has to do it. That is the entire difference between a boundary and an office, and this district found it out on the twenty-ninth of last month in a front room with a pail by the door, and it is going to find out whether anybody can do it on the twenty-ninth of this month, and there are twenty-six days in which to find out.**

And Orren Pike is seventeen and he is paid four coppers a quarter and he is not anybody's apprentice, and the index he is paid for has twelve stalls in it and the line book has eleven, and the difference is ninety-four yards of embankment with no signal in it, and the reason the number moved is not a walk and not a count.

It is that on the morning of the twenty-sixth of last month a signalman of a river office was told to walk the section twice a day, and did it, and had a tape in his pocket because that is what a signalman has, and measured a thing that nobody in this district had ever measured because nobody in this district had ever been paid four coppers a quarter to know where it was, and the money did not fix it and the money did not find it, and what found it was a man's ordinary work on his second morning, and **the finding is that a thing in this district gets done when it is somebody else's ordinary work, and that this district has now had that happen three times in eleven weeks and has costed none of them.**

And the last thing is at about six in the morning on the third of this month in a hut nine miles west, and there is nobody in Alder Reach who knows about it and it is not in a book and that is the condition.

A man of thirty-nine said a nine-word sentence, in a room with a woman of fifty standing in it holding a lamp, and she said it again, and he said it again, and it took four goes, and on the fifth he said it the way she said it, which is a way that has nothing in it at all, and she put the lamp down on the bench and said one sentence.

"**That is a person,**" she said. "**And I told him I would not tell you, so I am not telling you, and I am telling the reader of this, and the reader of this is anybody who comes up this bank, and that is now the only way anybody finds out, and I have decided to allow it, and it is a thing with no end on it and I have four of them.**"

And on a bench in that hut the casting still has a clean pale rectangle in the grease and the rust where the iron stood for five years, and a mark on the corner of the frame about the size of a thumbnail where it caught, and a crack in the lime putty about four feet long, and a floor eleven inches out of level at the left-hand end, and the frame is empty, and the iron is on another bench four hours up the bank with a boy of nineteen asleep beside it who is not anybody's keeper, and between the two of them there is no signal in the ground and no instrument that can report anything, and a line is an order and not a length, and **the fifth thing is still unnamed, and the column is still empty, and the word is still good, and the term runs to the twenty-ninth of this month, and the woman who was asked about it said yes, and she said it in a room, and somebody has to go up there again.**
