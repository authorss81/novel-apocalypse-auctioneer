# Chapter 88: The Count Each Trade Carried

At about ten in the morning on the twenty-sixth of this month a sheet went onto the counting-room table with eleven rows on it and a column at the right-hand end that had never been read out in that room, and the column is the number of households each trade carries, and the reason it had never been read out is that on the twenty-fifth of March this market voted on a document that is not a rate, and there is no count of trades in a minute about a document.

The omission was entered in the minute that day, in about eleven words, and the eleven words are the reason a room of about ninety people was full at ten in the morning instead of a third of it.

> **THE COUNT EACH TRADE CARRIED WAS NOT READ OUT AND THE OMISSION IS ENTERED BECAUSE A STRANGER IS OWED THE COUNT.**

The counts were read out at about a quarter past ten by the reader at the ward market counter, who is paid eleven coppers a quarter for four days a week and whose term has no clause for a shoulder, and who was asked to do it and said yes before she was asked, and the finding is that the reason she said yes is that a refusal made in a crowd is not heard by a crowd, and an omission made in a minute is not seen by a stranger, and both of those are her trade.

> **ONE. BAKERS. FOUR HUNDRED AND TWELVE.**
> **TWO. BUTCHERS AND FISHMONGERS. THREE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-SIX.**
> **THREE. CORDWAINERS. TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-NINE.**
> **FOUR. SMITHS, WHEELWRIGHTS AND FITTERS. ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOUR.**
> **FIVE. WEAVERS, TAILORS AND STAYERS. FOUR HUNDRED AND THIRTY-ONE.**
> **SIX. CHANDLERS AND OILMEN. TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHT.**
> **SEVEN. HAULERS AND CARTERS. SIX HUNDRED AND TWENTY-TWO.**
> **EIGHT. BREWERS AND VICTUALLERS. FIVE HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN.**
> **NINE. UNDERTAKERS, LIMNERS AND SCRIVENERS. NINETY-SIX.**
> **TEN. LAMPERS AND OSTLERS. THREE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX.**
> **ELEVEN. PUBLIC CARRIERS. FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY.**
>
> **FOUR THOUSAND AND ONE. THAT IS THE LEVY. ELEVEN TRADES. EVERY HOUSEHOLD IN THIS DISTRICT IS IN ONE OF THOSE ELEVEN ROWS OR IS IN NO ROW, AND ABOUT NINE HUNDRED ARE IN NO ROW, AND THE NINE HUNDRED ARE NOT COUNTED ANYWHERE IN THIS DISTRICT INCLUDING ON THE SHEET THAT COLLECTS THE FOUR THOUSAND AND ONE.**

It took about eleven minutes to read and there was one interruption, from a man of about fifty in the third row who said that the nine hundred were his trade, and the reader read the last four lines again, and the man said thank you and did not say anything else, and the minute says that he said thank you.

---

Nobody drew a new sheet, and the reason is on the record from the second of April, when the same market was asked to produce a figure for a railway and a freight clerk of six years refused in front of about six hundred people: **I have never once put a figure on a wall that I did not intend to defend line by line, and I am not going to start on a day when I would have to defend a figure I did not write.**

So the paper is the paper of the thirteenth of March in five columns at fifty coppers over four thousand and one households, and the paper of the twenty-second of April with a rate of fourteen on it that nobody proposed, and the paper of the twenty-fifth of March with a decision on it that has never been costed, and on the twenty-sixth of this month somebody put the counts on the back of it in a hand that is not the secretary's.

There are about ninety people in that room and eleven of them are trades and the rest are the wives and sons and daughters of trades and four people who are in no trade at all and are not on a roll and got in because somebody held a door, and one of those four is a woman of about sixty with a stick who has located a hole in this district's constitution in three separate rooms since the eighth of March and has never once been wrong about where it was.

The minute names none of the four. It says four people, and it says that not asked is in the count, and it says that the reason the four are not named is that naming them would make them a category, and a category is what this district has been doing to people for eleven weeks.

---

And the public carriers abstained, and nobody in the room could say which trade they were without looking, and they have abstained twice before on the same question, and the reason has been the same both times, and the reason is the reason the market wrote down in March when it wrote it down for the first time.

A public carrier is a person who carries a thing for a fee in this city for whoever pays. A household carries its own. **The people the road is for, the district's own minute says, are people who do not get carried because they can pay and people who do not get counted because they are not in a household, and those are the same people, and a public carrier's customer is neither of them.**

"We are not a trade for the people the road is for," the reader read out, "and we have said it on the thirteenth of March and on the twenty-fifth of March and we are saying it now, and we would like it entered a third time and we would like somebody to do something about it, because a trade that abstains three times is not a trade exercising a right, it is a hole with a name on it."

The minute says that nothing was done about it, and that the abstention stands, and that the fifth hundred and fifty households are not represented in either question, and that this is the ninth entry of its kind in nine weeks and that the count of them has been closed since the fourth week of March at a number this district will not print in a public book.

---

**And the meeting sat twice, and the first question took forty-one minutes and the second took nine, and the reason is that the first one was about a room and the second one was about a sheet, and this district has been arguing about rooms for nine weeks and about sheets since the third of April.**

The standing order of the ninth. Eight posts at eleven coppers a quarter is eighty-eight coppers a quarter, against a take of two hundred thousand and fifty. The eighth of the eight places on page eleven is Harrow. The same office has been paying one hundred and eighty coppers a quarter on the Harrow works for nine years, six thousand four hundred and eighty, paid, not arrears, not deferred, out of a return this market has read every quarter for nine years. **The figure was read out and it is the only thing in nine years that has been entered against the decision this market made, and the reason it was entered is that a figure that goes against a decision is the only thing that keeps a decision from becoming a feeling.**

The arguments took forty-one minutes and there were four of them and three of them were correct.

The first was that eighty-eight coppers a quarter is nothing. A man made that point for four minutes and it is in the minute, and the answer to it was given by a freight clerk of six years and it is eleven words long: **the market refused eighty-eight a quarter for eight posts. A river office paid six thousand four hundred and eighty in nine years. Neither asked the other if the thing existed.**

The second was that the market cannot refuse a standing order because the market is not a body that can revoke it. That is correct and it has been in the book since the twenty-fifth of March, and the reason it is correct is that the last page of that document says it is not subject to revocation by any body existing at the date of it, and the body which may revoke it is created by it, and it consists of the administrator and two persons named by him who hold no office, and it may be dissolved only by the body which may revoke it. **A document that cannot be ended by anybody, and that creates its own ending.**

The third was the technical objection nobody had made in nine years, and it was made in nine words by a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and who came to the meeting for the first time in his life: the eighth of the eight places on page eleven is Harrow, and Harrow is under nine feet of water, and the market cannot amend it, because a body that amends a document which may be dissolved only by itself is the body that revokes it.

"So we are voting on a document about eight posts," he said, "one of which is a place we cannot get to, and we cannot change the document to say so, and if we do change it then we are the people who killed it, and if we do not change it then we have refused to pay for a post in nine feet of water for the ninth year. **I would like it entered that both of those are correct and that this is a vote about which of two correct things we would rather be.**"

The fourth was from the women who hold the west end, and it came up the bank on the twentieth of this month and was in the room on the twenty-sixth, and it is in the minute in their own words, and it is that neither of them asked for a post and neither of them has applied for one and neither of them intends to.

> **THE STANDING ORDER OF THE NINTH. AGAINST: FIVE TRADES. SMITHS, WHEELWRIGHTS AND FITTERS. CHANDLERS AND OILMEN. HAULERS AND CARTERS. BREWERS AND VICTUALLERS. UNDERTAKERS, LIMNERS AND SCRIVENERS. SIXTEEN HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN HOUSEHOLDS.**
>
> **FOR: FIVE TRADES. BAKERS. BUTCHERS AND FISHMONGERS. CORDWAINERS. WEAVERS, TAILORS AND STAYERS. LAMPERS AND OSTLERS. EIGHTEEN HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FOUR HOUSEHOLDS.**
>
> **ABSTAINING: ONE TRADE. PUBLIC CARRIERS. FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY HOUSEHOLDS.**

**The office lost, and it lost five to five, and it lost on the market's own rule, which is by trade and not by household, and the reason the rule is by trade was read out at about twenty past eleven and it is the reason the count is on the sheet at all: a trade is a thing that can be asked and a household is a thing that is counted.**

And the count is what decided it, and every person in that room knew it before the vote and nobody has said so, and the entry says that a market which had read the counts on the twenty-fifth of March would have known on the twenty-fifth of March that the office was going to lose on a tie, and this district spent two months finding out by the slow method, and the room was told that by a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, who said it out loud, and who was not thanked and did not expect to be.

"The public carriers have abstained for the third time on the same question and given the same reason," said the reader, "and the reason is the reason they gave in March, which is: we are not a trade for the people the road is for. And the fact that the reason has not changed is the most useful thing anybody has said in that room in two months."

---

**And the second question took nine minutes and the timetable carried, six to four, and the six is the smallest majority this market has carried on anything that is not a rate, and the finding is that the four who voted against it are the four trades whose people are the ones the line runs through, and nobody in the room had ever asked them that.**

> **THE TIMETABLE. FOR: SIX TRADES. BAKERS. BUTCHERS AND FISHMONGERS. CORDWAINERS. WEAVERS, TAILORS AND STAYERS. HAULERS AND CARTERS. LAMPERS AND OSTLERS. TWENTY-FOUR HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SIX HOUSEHOLDS.**
>
> **AGAINST: FOUR TRADES. SMITHS, WHEELWRIGHTS AND FITTERS. CHANDLERS AND OILMEN. BREWERS AND VICTUALLERS. UNDERTAKERS, LIMNERS AND SCRIVENERS. NINE HUNDRED AND NINETY-FIVE HOUSEHOLDS.**
>
> **ABSTAINING: PUBLIC CARRIERS. FIVE HUNDRED AND FIFTY.**

The market's answer, and it is the answer the market gave in March, and it is in the minute for the second time: **a rate buys a service and does not buy a seat. A seat that may be filled and cannot be emptied is a condition and not a service. And this market has spent eleven years paying for a condition and calling it a service.**

And it is still true, and the market still cannot amend the standing order, and both halves of that are correct and cannot both be acted on, and this district entered it in March and it is still entered and it is still a loss and not a refusal.

The vote was for a **sheet**. It was not a vote for a wage, and the finding entered on the twenty-seventh is that the market funded a thing that can be bought and refused to fund the person who keeps it, and the difference between a sheet and a wage is a person, and the person is a reader at a counter who is paid eleven coppers a quarter for four days a week, and the sheet will therefore go out of date and the market has entered that it will go out of date and has not voted a date.

---

And at about half past four in the afternoon, after both questions, a woman of about sixty with a stick stood up at the back of that room and asked one question, and the secretary gave her the sheet, and she read the counts and the two results on it, and then she asked the second thing, and it is the finding of the twenty-seventh of this month in a different hand.

"You have read out the count of every trade in this room," she said, "and there is a number on your sheet about nine hundred households that are in no row. And nobody has asked what they are. And the ninth place is four households and thirty-one people and it has been in a row since the twenty-eighth of March, and on the seventh of this month it paid this market two hundred coppers out of a pail of change.

"So there is a row now. Good. That is a good thing and I am not here to argue with it. **And the four rows on that sheet are the only four rows anybody in this district has ever made about anybody, and every one of them was made by somebody walking onto a piece of ground and counting what was standing on it, and not one of them was made by asking anybody.**"

Nobody in the room argued with her and nobody told her she was wrong, and the secretary entered her question and entered the answer, which is that the count rule says a person is accounted for by a count and a destination and a remedy and is not accounted for by a household, and that the count rule was written by a man in a kitchen in the second week of March and approved in a yard, and that it turned a village of thirty-one into four households in the week it was written, and that nobody has struck the count rule, because a count rule that has been wrong once is a rule and a count rule that has never been wrong is a habit.

---

**And the money came last and it is the part of the meeting that will be in the book longest, and it is that the market has voted to buy a thing it cannot pay for and has entered the difference on a column belonging to a woman of about fifty with a stall.**

The rate is fifty coppers a quarter. Fourteen of the fifty is the line, six is printed matter, **and thirty of the fifty has never been assigned to anything by anybody and has been sitting in a column with no heading in it since the thirteenth of March.** The market has voted to take the sheet out of the unassigned thirty.

A run of five hundred sheets costs nine hundred and six coppers, quoted on the fourteenth of March: four for stock, two for setting, nine hundred quoted, and the market has been quoted that figure three times and has never once argued with it. Printing has been taking **twelve coppers a quarter** out of this market for longer than the rate has existed, and that twelve is a line in a printer's book and is not the six of the fifty on the rate sheet, and the two have never been in the same column. Nine hundred and six less twelve is eight hundred and ninety-four, and the sheet is being paid for out of the thirty coppers a quarter that nobody ever assigned to anything. **The market has voted to spend eight hundred and ninety-four coppers it does not have, and the difference is the same gap that has been carried on a trade on the lower terrace four times, and the column it goes on was opened on the second of this month at the trader's own asking, and the trader was asked about it in the room.**

"I am not carrying it a fifth time," said Hessa Krell. "Three times is a favour. Four times is a rate. I said that in this room on the second of this month and I have said it since and I am not going to unsay it in a fortnight. A rate is a thing that is on you and a debt is a thing that is on somebody else, and I will carry a rate for the rest of my life and I will not carry this market's arithmetic.

"Print your sheet. And put the difference on it. That is all I am asking and it is the only thing I have ever asked anybody for, and a sheet that says what it cost and what it has not got is worth more than a sheet that says four coppers, and I have proved that four times at my own expense and I am not going to prove it a fifth."

And the market agreed, and the agreement is in the minute, and the minute also says that a market which has voted to spend money it has not found has entered the reason in the same line as the vote and has not called the vote a mistake.

The only press in this district that can do a run of five hundred in a day belongs to Halloway Reed, who is right about four things and wrong about one, and the market bought the run from him, and the toll column was set by the market and not by him, and the day it comes off the press the count is read out at the ward market counter in the ordinary voice, and he asked for it entered that he was paid and was paid, and nobody thanked him in a street, and he said he would not have thanked them either.

---

**And the finding of the whole of the twenty-seventh of this month is in the secretary's hand and it is about a rule and not about a railway, and it is the fourth time this district has had it in a different shape and the first time it has cost an office a seat.**

> **THE DISTRICT COULD NOT AGREE ABOUT AN OFFICE AND AGREED EXACTLY AND INVERSELY ABOUT A SHEET. FIVE TO FIVE BOTH TIMES, THE SAME FIVE ON OPPOSITE SIDES. THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE TWO QUESTIONS IS NOT MONEY AND IT IS NOT PRINCIPLE. IT IS THAT ONE OF THEM CAN BE ENDED AND THE OTHER ONE CANNOT.**
