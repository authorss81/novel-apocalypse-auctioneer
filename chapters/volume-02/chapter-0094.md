# Chapter 94: The Fourth Bearer Is Not a Slot

At about ten past eight in the morning on the fourteenth of this month three people met in the signal cabin at the east end of the line with a sheet from the tenth of May on the table, and the sheet had been in the public lot book in Alder Reach for five weeks, and the fourth line of it was the only one that had never been filled in.

The fourth line is short. It says that the fourth bearer is a person who ends the use, and is not a slot, and is to be named by the three people who hold the weeks, and that Mr Vale has no part in naming them.

The three people are a woman of fifty-one who is the keeper of the only pair on that line, a woman of about fifty-one who keeps a board in this cabin and is not a signal hand, and a man of sixty-eight at a pumping engine eleven miles west who put his name on a card at a chandler's in Alder Reach on the third of this month after saying no for nine minutes.

Two hours and forty minutes, and no fourth line, **and the reason no fourth line is that the first thing they did was find a mistake in their own document, and the mistake had been in it since the tenth of May, and none of the three had found it because none of the three had ever asked whether a board keeper can hold a pair.**

"A keeper is not somebody you go to," said the woman of the board, in about four seconds. Then she said the thing that had been written on the boards in May, and she said it because she had said it in a room in May and had not been asked about it since.

"**A keeper is somebody who is there. I said in May that I am not a signal hand and I never will be and I do not know what a pair is, and that sentence is on a board at the fourth place in my own words.** I put my name in the fourth line of a charter in May and nobody in that room asked me whether I could hold a pair, and the standing of the iron is a person who can hold a signal."

"Write it down," said the woman of fifty-one. "**Write down that we found it ourselves and that we found it in two hours and forty minutes and that it had been in the public book for five weeks and that four hundred people could have taken that sheet cold and read it.**"

---

The finding of the fourteenth of this month is that **a document can be wrong in a way that everybody in the room agreed with, and that agreement is the reason it survives, and that the district has now had this twice.**

The first time was the count rule, which turned a village of thirty-one into four households in the week it was written.

The second time is a board keeper in the fourth line of a rotating charter, and the mistake survived five weeks and two votes and a print. It survived because a clerk who designs things for a living, a woman who is the water, and a mechanic who had argued for a different clause for four months all said yes to it, and nobody said *can she hold a pair*.

**The reason nobody said it is that a person in a room who has said yes to something is very hard to look at properly, and this district has learned that four times in eleven weeks in four different rooms and has not learned it in any of them.**

The line was struck on the fourteenth of this month at about ten past ten in the morning and the strike is in the minute with the name of the person who struck it, and her name is on it, and she said she wanted it on.

"You are striking yourself," said the man of sixty-eight.

"**I am striking myself in front of three people and I am going to be the one who says why, because if I wait for somebody else to say it then in about four years the story is that a man at a pumping engine found a woman's mistake, and he did not, and I did, and I said yes to it in a room in May because I had a queue at a counter and I wanted it to be finished.**"

---

They were three and then they were two, because a board keeper is not a bearer and the east end of that line is a cabin with a lever frame in it and a man in the district's own signal crew who has a pair in his hand on a Tuesday, or would have, if anybody had ever asked him.

The finding on the fifteenth is in the tenth hand's hand and it is four lines long.

The east end is eleven minutes' walk from the ward market counter. **Nobody had ever asked who holds the frame at the east end because a board went up in front of the cabin in May and a board is a thing people look at, and the frame is behind the door, and the district built an instrument for looking at a thing and forgot that there is a thing behind it.**

"He will say yes," said the woman of fifty-one. "He has said yes four times in eleven weeks and he has never once been asked about his own hand, and if you ask him he will tell you the standing objection before you have finished the question and he will be right."

He did. His name is not on this page because he is not the fourth bearer and his name is in the fourth line of the sheet, and **he is the second of the three, and the first week on the rotation is still the keeper's, and the order of the weeks was settled in May on the back of a mechanic's pencil on a trade's invoice and has not been touched since.**

He is a man of about thirty-one and he is a signalman of the district's own crew and he walked up the bank four times between the fourteenth and the seventeenth to be in on this, and he was not paid for any of it and did not ask.

"**Say the whole thing again, and then say it to the two of them, and then let me say no, because I am going to say no, and I have thought about it for two days, and I am going to say no, and you are going to write it down, and I would like it written down that I said no before you read it to me rather than after.**"

The clerk wrote it down. He said no for about eleven seconds.

"The reason is that a week at the east end ends on the twenty-ninth," he said. "**I have said yes to a boundary on the twenty-ninth twice. I have walked that bank in April and in May. And if the third asking is on the twenty-ninth of this month then a week at the east end is a week that ends on the same day I am two and a half miles up a bank saying what a question was, and I am not going to be a bearer of a thing and a man who reads back somebody else's asking on the same day. Ask me for any other week.**"

And the entry on the seventeenth of this month says that the three holders took the week he named, and that he said yes to that one in about four seconds, and that the finding is that a rotation is only real when it turns **for a reason, and that the first reason anybody in this district has ever given for not holding a week is that the week was the wrong shape.**

---

And the fourth bearer was found on the fifteenth of this month, and not by anybody who was looking for one. **The finding attached to it is that the district has spent nine weeks unable to name a person and has had a person eleven steps away from it for the whole of that time.**

It is the twelfth place. It is two miles west of the ninth place, on the embankment, on the far side of the drainage cut, and there is a ground frame in a hut about eight feet by nine with a lamp and a stool and a lever, and the frame has no bracket and never had one, and the iron does not fit it.

There is nothing at the twelfth place. There is no board, no rate, no toll, no siding, and no reason for anybody in this district to walk two miles off a bank to look at it, and about forty people in nine weeks have said that a place with nothing at it is not a place anybody can be sent to.

There is a man there. **He is there twice a month because a frame that is not used has to be turned by hand or it binds, and he has been going for nineteen years, and he has never been asked anything by anybody, and he is not on a roll, and he is not in the count of trades, and he is not in anybody's rate sheet, and he is not on any board.**

The woman of about thirty-eight with the hook found him, and she found him because the fourth of her four houses is at a gate two hundred yards from the frame hut and she has been buying paper at that house for nine years, and on the fifteenth of this month she stood at the gate for about a quarter of an hour and asked a question about his book.

She did not ask him whether he would end a railway. **She asked him what the lever was for, because her whole trade is other people's documents and a man with a lever and a book is a man with a document she has never seen, and she had nine words about it afterwards and they are in the minute in her own words.**

"I asked him what the lever was for. He said it turns the points at the eleventh and twelfth so that a thing nobody sends does not go into the flood. I asked him who sends the thing. He said nobody has sent one since the fourth year. I asked him what he does about that. He said he comes twice a month. **I did not ask him anything else and I did not tell him anything and I did not know what I had found until the sixteenth.**"

His name is Elias Rooke and he is sixty-one and he has held a pair on this line for thirty-four years and has kept that frame for nineteen. He is not in an office. He has never carried anything for anybody. He was not travelling when he was found, he was in a hut, and the room at Alder Reach recorded both of those facts before anybody asked him anything, because it had learned them in May.

The keeper of the only pair on that line has the twelfth place in her own book, in her own hand, and she produced it in a room in Alder Reach on the sixteenth of this month at about half past four in the afternoon, and the entry is nineteen years old and it is four words long, and the four words are **FRAME TURNED. NOTHING SENT.**

"And I have copied that line out about nine hundred times since," she said, "**and I have never once asked what the frame turns, and I have never once asked who turns it, and I have written nothing after those four words in nineteen years, because there was nothing to write and because I did not know that there was a person.**"

Nobody in that room said anything for about four seconds.

"You knew the frame was there," said the tenth hand.

"I knew the frame was there the way I know the drainage cut is there," she said. "**It is on the line. It is between two things. I have never had a reason to go to it and I have never had a reason to ask and I have been the keeper of the only pair on this line for eleven years and I have never once been curious, and I am putting that in the book in my own hand because I would like somebody in four years to know that it was me and not the water at the column.**"

---

And the thing the three holders could not do for two days was say what a bearer actually does, and it came out of a conversation about a lever in a hut two miles off a bank. **It is the plainest description of the job this district has ever produced.**

It is nine words long and the woman of fifty-one wrote it on the back of a mechanic's invoice on the sixteenth of this month, because there was nothing else to write on, and the invoice went into the minute and the nine words went into the public lot book.

**YOU STAND WHERE THE USE IS AND YOU STOP IT OUT LOUD.**

Nobody was satisfied with it. The man of sixty-eight asked for about four minutes alone with it, which nobody has ever been granted at a meeting in this district, and he came back and said that it is correct and that it is useless, and that a person cannot stop a use out loud if a use is nine vehicles and a lever and a flood, and that what he had meant for thirty years by standing where a thing is and stopping it out loud was the thing she had just written down, and that it had taken a woman of sixty-one two miles off a bank to say it.

---

He was asked on the sixteenth of this month in a room above a chandler's shop, in the five things, in about nine minutes, and he gave the first thing in his own words and it is longer than anybody else's has been.

"You want to know what the job is," he said. "**The job is that a use stops. Not a train, not a road, not a thing. A use. And nobody in nine years has ever come up this bank and told me that there is a use, and I have been coming up here twice a month for nineteen years to turn a lever for a thing that nobody sends.**

"So I am going to say what I want and I am going to say it once. **I will end a use. I will not end a railway, I will not close a line, and I will not stand in a place and stop people.** I will put my hand on a lever and reverse whatever is running and I will say the reason out loud in a room with two other people in it, and if the reason is not good enough then somebody can put the points back, and that is their job and not mine.

"And I have been on my own for nineteen years, **and I would like it recorded that this is the first offer anybody has made me, and that I said yes to it in about four seconds, and that I did not want it to be longer.**"

---

And the sheet they wrote on the seventeenth of this month is the fourth line of the tenth of May filled in. **It is four lines long, and the third of them is the reason the whole of this volume has been able to finish.**

> **THE FOURTH BEARER IS ELIAS ROOKE, SIXTY-ONE, KEEPER OF THE FRAME AT THE TWELFTH PLACE, WHO HAS HELD A PAIR ON THIS LINE FOR THIRTY-FOUR YEARS AND HAS KEPT THAT FRAME FOR NINETEEN AND HAS BEEN ASKED NOTHING BY ANYBODY. HE IS NAMED BY THE THREE PEOPLE WHO HOLD THE WEEKS. MR VALE WAS NOT IN THE ROOM AND HAS NO PART IN NAMING HIM AND WAS TOLD THE NAME AT THE SAME TIME AS EVERYBODY ELSE, ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF JUNE, IN A COUNTING ROOM, IN THE ORDINARY VOICE, BY A CLERK.**
>
> **HE IS NOT A SLOT AND HE IS NOT A SUBSTITUTE. HE HOLDS NO WEEK AND NO PLACE. HIS ONLY STANDING IS THAT HE ENDS THE USE, AND HE ENDS IT BY REVERSING WHAT IS RUNNING AND SAYING THE REASON OUT LOUD IN A ROOM WITH TWO OTHER PEOPLE IN IT, AND THE REASON GOES IN A PUBLIC BOOK.**
>
> **HE IS NOT AN OFFICER AND HE IS NOT IN A ROOM WITH A DOOR ON IT AND HE IS NOT THE MAN WHO DRAFTED ANYTHING. HE LIVES ELEVEN MILES FROM THE NEAREST FRAME THAT ANYBODY IN THIS DISTRICT CAN POINT AT, AND HE HAS BEEN THERE NINETEEN YEARS, AND THE FINDING IS THAT THE DISTRICT COULD NOT NAME HIM BECAUSE NOBODY HAD EVER WALKED TWO MILES.**
>
> **AND THE MAN WHO POSTED THE SECURITY THIS CHARTER IS FOR IS A PERSON THE FOURTH BEARER HAS NEVER MET.**

---

Nobody in that room thanked the paperer, and she was not thanked, and she said beforehand that she did not want to be, and the minute enters that she was not thanked and that she asked for it to be entered that she was not thanked.

And the finding of the eighteenth of this month is the one the room took an hour over.

**The person who ends a use is the only one of the four who is not anybody's substitute.** This district has spent nine weeks building a thing in which every job could be filled in later, and the one job in it that cannot be filled in later is the one that matters.

**A person who cannot be replaced is not the same as a person who is permanent, and the difference is that a permanent person has to be paid and a person who cannot be replaced is paid out of nothing and stays because nobody has thought to ask them to stop.**

A boy of seventeen was in the room and was not asked and said one thing, and the clerk wrote it down.

"He is the second person in eleven weeks who has been found by somebody doing a different job," he said. "The first was me and I was copying a book. **And the finding is not that this district is short of people. It is that this district cannot send anybody anywhere without a document, and the fourth bearer had no document, and the reason he had no document is that nobody has ever written one about him, and a person with no document is a person nobody can be sent to, and that is why he is the right one.**"

And the registrar refused to enter the last half of that, in about four seconds, and the boy asked why, and she gave her reason, and it is the reason she gives every time.

"Because you have joined two things that arrived separately," she said. "**A person nobody can be sent to and the right person for a job are not the same statement, and the difference between them is the whole of what anybody in this district has ever been able to say about a mark.**"

He was not argued with. He is seventeen and has been wrong in public four times in eleven weeks and has written all four down, and this is the fifth, and the minute says it is the fifth and that it happened in a room with a reader in it.

---

And on the eighteenth of this month a clerk of the ward market read a name out at a counter in Alder Reach at about eleven o'clock in the morning in the ordinary voice, and about forty people stood there. **The man it was about was nine miles west and did not hear it.**

The name went out with the sentence that it had been found in nine minutes on the fifteenth of this month by a person doing a different job, and with the sentence that it was the fourth line of the tenth of May filled in, and with the sentence that the person named is not a slot and holds no week and has no part in any of the other three.

Adrian Vale was in the room. He had been told in advance that a name was coming and he had said in advance that he had no part in it, and the clerk said the sentence about his having no part in the middle of the reading, because she read it in the order it was written and not in the order anybody wanted.

"**Say what you are going to do with it,**" said the man of about thirty who sells nothing and has a use.

"I am going to write it in my own book," he said, "under the date, with the time I heard it and the fact that I was in this room, and then I am going to go and do the only part of this that is mine, which is put four pounds of cast iron in a bracket, and **I would like it read out that I did not ask for this and that I would have said no if I had been asked and that saying no is not in anybody's hands any more.**"

"And the finding is that you cannot close it."

"**The finding is that I cannot close it and that is not why I would have said no.** I would have said no because a man who writes the fourth line of a document and gets to name the person who can stop him is a man with a document and a man, and this is a document and a man, and it took a woman with a hook and a question about a lever to find the one person in eleven miles who could not be reached by either of those things."

And the registrar entered it, and entered that he said the second half of it in front of about forty people, **and entered that the phrase *if I had been asked* has been in this district's public books eleven times in nine weeks and that this is the first time it has been entered as a thing he would have done and not a thing somebody else did.**

---

**And then a mechanic of forty-three who had not been in the room said one thing on the evening of the eighteenth that four people wrote down, and it is in the book because it is the reason a security gets spent in the next two days and the district is not surprised by it.**

"You have posted four things in May," he said, "and three of them are about work and one of them is a piece of ground that this district owes nothing on, and a man at a pumping engine and a firm that gave up a lease and a market and a village."

"The ground."

"**The ground is the best one and you are all about to spend it. I posted it so that a man could walk up that bank and stand on it, and on the twenty-seventh of this month there is going to be a platform on it.** A security is a thing somebody else can go and look at, and a platform is the easiest thing in this district to look at, and I posted mine for exactly one purpose, and the purpose is about to be used up in public, and nobody is going to be able to tell in August whether the ground was ever anybody's to look at."

"So what do you want."

"**I want it entered now that the fourth security is going to be spent, and I want it entered before it is spent, and I want the entry to say it was spent and not lost, and I do not want it back.**"
