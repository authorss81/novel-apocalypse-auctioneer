# Chapter 827: The One Hundred And Eleventh Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Woman Came Down That Lane With A Page In Her Hand For The First Time In Twenty Mornings And A Boy Of Seventeen Said A Figure Out Loud Before A Clerk's Hand Moved And Was Right And Said Five Sentences About A Channel And A Roof And A Dead Man And A Woman Who Keeps A Scale Read One Figure Off The Page And A Man Of Sixty-One Was Not In That Yard At About Eleven

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and sixty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and twenty-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and forty-one days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and fifty-five days. Seven hundred and sixteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and thirty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and thirty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and forty-four days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and eighty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and seventy marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a stranger came down that lane with a page in her hand for the first time in twenty mornings and the count of strangers moved for the first time in twenty mornings, and a thing was made in this yard this morning by a woman who is not from here, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and forty-one mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and forty. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for ninety-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and eleven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-six and it moved this morning for the first time in twenty mornings. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-seven lines on it and none of the twenty-seven is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and fifty-second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them had been up that hill and four miles past it and back inside about four days.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and sixty-sixth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and sixty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the fifty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a woman who had walked up that hill and four miles past it was about four hundred yards off at the time and did not hear either of them, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood twenty-six days, and the count of strangers who have come down that lane is twenty-six and it moved this morning for the first time in twenty mornings, and the page arrived at the bottom of that bank before the figure was said at those boards, which is the order it has to happen in.

---

She came down that lane at about ten with a page in her hand.

Nobody in this yard has ever stopped anybody coming down that lane and nobody stopped her. The sheet a man of about forty-one had brought up on the one hundred and sixth morning had gone back down that hill in his coat on the previous afternoon at about ten to four, and there had been nothing on the boards of the second table since. She got to the bottom of the bank and stood there for a moment with her hand flat on the page, the way everybody in this district stands at the bottom of that bank, and then she came up the eleven feet, which is the steepest thing between here and anywhere and she came up it without putting the page down.

She was not old. She had a coat on that had been walked thirteen miles in.

---

She put the page on the boards of the second table and squared it against the edge.

"Twenty-six," said Orren Pike.

He said it out loud at the boards, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet turned, and Mara Quill had her hand about four inches from the page and had not touched it.

---

"She has not even said anything," said Mavis Dorr.

"No," said the boy. "It moves when a stranger comes down that hill with a page in her hand. That is what it counts and it has counted that since the seventy-sixth morning and I have said that figure out loud at these boards every morning since before the wall changed, and I was going to stop saying it, and I have not stopped saying it because I have just watched it move."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say one word, and about four of them looked at the boy, and the boy saw them looking and put his hands behind the boards.

"Fourteen mornings ago," he said, "at about one in the afternoon, I stood at these boards and said that if this figure moves every time a page reaches them then in about four years it is going to be a count of how many times this yard was handed something, and that is a number about us and not a number about a person. And a woman with a beam said that was the first time in nine years somebody had refused to say a number, and about nine people looked at her."

He stopped.

"It moved this morning because a stranger came down that hill with a page in her hand," he said. "So I was right, and I would like it noticed, and I would like about nine people in this yard to notice that I would like it noticed and that nobody is going to."

---

Nobody noticed it.

Orren Pike went round the back of the boards and stood there with his sheet against his chest and did not say one word for about two minutes, and about four people at the top of eleven feet could have said one word and did not say it, and one of those four said later, privately, to a man who keeps a tally, that it was the first thing he had wanted to say out loud in that yard in nine years.

---

The woman put both hands on the boards about four inches from the page and did not open it.

"I am from the eleventh place on the Salt Verge," she said. "It is four miles past the ninth mile and I walked it this morning because there was a woman with a key up that hill who said there was a yard at the bottom of it that could not produce a person, and I have never heard of this yard, and I came to see what it was like."

"And what is it like," said Sabra Holt.

"It is like a yard," said the woman. "I have five sentences and then I am going to walk thirteen miles back and I am not going to be asked for six."

---

"First. The channel is holding. There is a roof on two of the huts that was not there before the winter and nobody has left."

"Second. Nobody has come and asked us for a second time. About nine of us wrote one sentence in about an afternoon and that is all that has happened and we have been waiting about four days for the second asking and it has not come."

"Third. Our own council gave up the channel for about four days a year, on purpose, because of what happened in the water two summers ago, and nobody in this basin asked us to and nobody has given us anything for it. We did it ourselves and we are not asking anybody for anything and I want that said before anybody in this yard gets pleased about it."

---

"Fourth," said the woman, "we sent a form back with a name on it and the name is the name of a man who died in the winter and he is still on our list because nobody has come to take him off it. We sent it because the form wanted a name and that is the name we had. Nobody has been to ask us whether it is the right name and we are not going to say one word about it to anybody, and that is the fifth thing and it is the last thing I came down here for."

She took her hands off the boards.

"You can count that if you want to," she said. "I have just given up the right to say no about a channel and the right to say no about a name and there is nothing in this basin that has given me either of them back, and I am not complaining, and I have been walking since before it was light and I would like to be allowed to sit down."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet had said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other every morning since the wall changed, and not one of them had thought about that this morning while a woman was telling them about a channel, and about four of them had arrived, independently, at the same place, which is that the fifth of the five things this district does not have was sitting on a stool at the near end of the first table with a coat on that had been walked thirteen miles in, and that it is not paid, and that nobody in this district has ever offered it anything.

---

The woman who keeps a scale came up about four feet of the bank, which she has not done since the morning she carried the beam up in both hands, and she stood about four feet off the page.

"Do not touch that page," said Mavis Dorr.

"I am not going to touch it," said the woman who keeps a scale, "I have been weighing things for nine years and I know better than to touch a page that has been walked thirteen miles in."

She looked down the side of it.

"There are about nine places. The third line from the bottom is this yard and it says four." Her finger stopped. "And the second line from the bottom is a place four miles past the ninth mile and it says nine."

---

"Nine," said Mavis Dorr.

"Nine, and I do not know what it was last week, because there is a line for that place on the other sheet, the one that came down that hill five mornings ago, and there was a figure beside that line then and it was not nine." The woman who keeps a scale took her finger off it. "And I am not going to tell you what nine means because I do not know, and I have been at the foot of that bank for nine years and I have never read anything off a page before and I have just read two figures off one and one of them is about this yard and it is four and I have been in that yard every morning of the four."

---

At about four the man of sixty-one came up the eleven feet for the fourth time in nine years.

He had not been in that yard at about eleven and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were certain of it, because the woman who keeps a scale had watched the whole morning from about forty feet off and had not seen him once.

He came along the boards and put his finger on the second line from the bottom and did not take it off for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall. He read the figure. He read the mark beside it that meant it was a place in this basin. He did not read anything out loud and he did not look at the woman who was sitting on a stool at the near end of the first table and he did not look at about nine people.

Then he took his finger off it and went back down the eleven feet and went down the lane without turning round.

---

Mara Quill read it out in her own voice and then wrote it, and it is the twenty-eighth line on the page at the back of the lot book and none of the twenty-eight is in the lot book, and it said that a stranger came down that lane at about ten in the morning with a page in her hand and said five sentences at the boards of the second table at about eleven, and that she was entering the fact that the stranger said five sentences and not what the stranger was owed, because nothing about what the stranger was owed had been said in that yard by anybody including the stranger, and because a clerk enters what a yard was told and not what it owes the person who told it.

---

The light went off the boards of the second table at about half past four and the woman got up off the stool and put her coat on and picked up her page and did not leave it on the boards, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stood about as far back from the boards as they usually stand and let her get to the top of the bank first.

Adrian Vale stood four feet off the near end of the first table and did not go and see her off and did not offer to carry anything, and he found out about four minutes later that he was not going to be able to think about anything else for the rest of the morning, and that the reason was not the five sentences.

It was the third one. A council of about nine people had given up the use of a channel for four days a year because of what happened in the water two summers ago, and nobody had asked them to, and nobody had given them anything for it, and a woman had walked thirteen miles to say so in a yard where the fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid and the count of it is still five.
