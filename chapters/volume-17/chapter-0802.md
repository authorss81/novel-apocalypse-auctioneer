# Chapter 802: The Eighty-Sixth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Bid Was Appended To One Permanent Record Between About Ten And About Eleven In The Morning By Somebody Nine Miles Up That Hill Who Did Not Come Down The Lane And It Was Not Run And Nobody In That Yard Was Asked And The Wall Was Eleven Feet Below Them And Had Four Figures On It Nobody Had Said Out Loud

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and forty-one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and sixteen days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and thirty days. Six hundred and ninety-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open this morning. Four hundred and five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and ten days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and eighty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and eighty-three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and nineteen days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and fifty-nine marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and forty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the bid that stood open in this district for four hundred and fifty days went into a permanent record between about ten and about eleven this morning without anybody in this yard being asked.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and sixteen mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and fifteen. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for sixty-six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is eighty-six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-four and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and there is no more in it this morning than there was yesterday morning, and the record that has taken the bid is not in this book and is not in this district.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and twenty-seventh of those mornings, and the four he has been reading since the middle of the winter are still in him and have not been said out loud. The record has stood one day, and it is a record and not a thing in this district, and nobody in this yard can be asked about the standing of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and forty-first night of that run, having slept on four hundred and forty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the thirty-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and about nine feet above them and eleven feet below the top of the bank a man of thirty-eight was walking the length of the wall with his hands behind his back and had been since about eleven.

---

Sabra Holt had her own book out on the boards at about a quarter to eight and had not opened it, and she said the thing she had been carrying up and down that lane for two mornings before she sat down.

"I am going to read it again in this yard, at about ten, in the ordinary voice, and I am going to read the whole of it this time, including the part about the form of words, and the reason I have not done it before is that I have read it four times on my own and I did not want to arrive at a sentence in it by myself.

"If I read it in front of about nineteen people and then somebody in this yard finds a sentence in it, that sentence is a sentence nineteen people heard a stranger's paper say, out loud, in the sun, before anybody in this district had a view on it. That is the only piece of machinery I have got and I am going to spend it this morning and I am not going to be careful with it."

---

At about ten she read it, standing at the near end of the second table, and she read it all the way through, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the whole of it.

The part about the form of words is eleven words long and it says that the matter is held, and that the holder may not be questioned on the holding of it, and that no term is set and no end is entered.

"I am going to say the eleven words out loud again by myself," said Sabra Holt, "and I am not going to explain them, because if I explain them I have made them mine and I am not going to be the person in this basin who explains a form of words to about nineteen people in a yard."

She said them again. They were the same eleven words.

---

The man of fifty-six was at the wall at about ten and a half when the wall did not change, and he said afterwards that he felt it, which is a thing about nine people in that yard believed and about nine did not, and that the clerk entered nothing about it because a clerk does not enter what a person felt at a wall.

What changed, at about ten and about six minutes past, was that a line appeared on the first table.

---

It was a line on a strip of paper about the length of a hand, and it had been under the boards of the first table at the near end, and nobody in that yard had put it there and nobody in that yard had seen it put there, and the man of fifty-six was eleven feet off the ground and looking at the wall and the man at the end of the second table was four feet off the boards and the only person facing that table at about ten and six was the clerk, and she said afterwards that she had been looking at the near end of the boards and had not seen a hand.

The strip said four things and it had a heading, and the heading was about a matter, and the four things were: the district, the thing, the figure four hundred and fifty, and the words *held and not questioned*.

---

Nobody touched it for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

---

"Do not," said Adrian.

He said it from about nine feet off the near end of the boards, and he had not meant to say it, and he came down the bank about four steps and stopped.

"I am not going to touch it either, and I want to be exact about why, because in about four years somebody is going to write down that a man of thirty-eight stood in a yard and told a clerk not to pick up a piece of paper.

"If I touch it, that is a person handling it. If Mara Quill touches it, that is a clerk entering it. If any one of about nineteen people in this yard touches it, that is this district holding the matter, and a district that holds a matter cannot be asked about the holding of it, and I do not know that to be true and I am not going to find out on this morning by watching somebody pick it up."

---

Sabra Holt said, "I am going to touch it," and she came down the eleven feet of the bank and picked it up off the boards of the first table at the near end and carried it up and put it down on the second table about four feet along from where she had been standing, and that is the whole of what she did with it.

"I have held the third of the nine oldest keys for nineteen years," she said, "and I have never once used it on a piece of paper, and I have been turning that over since yesterday afternoon and I have got to a place where the reason I have never used it on a piece of paper is that I have been saving it for a piece of paper, and there is not going to be a better one than this, and if I do not use it on this then I have had it for nineteen years for nothing and I would rather be wrong about that than right about it for nineteen more."

---

"You have just made this district the holder," said the man of fifty-six, from about eleven feet off the ground.

"I have just made this district the holder," Sabra Holt agreed, "and I did it in front of about nineteen people in the sun, and I am not going to pretend to you that I did it in order to be asked about it, because I did not, and I want the yard to hear the order of those two things."

She put her own hand flat on the boards next to the strip.

"I have held a key for nineteen years that nobody has ever asked me the use of, and my name has never been on a page in that corridor in nineteen years, and I told this yard what I wanted on the eighty-first morning and I have not repeated it since because I have been working on it. That is the whole of my reason and it is a bad one and I would have used a better one if there was a better one."

---

Adrian said, "It is not a bad one, and I want to say why, and I am saying it to her and not to the yard.

"You are the only person in this basin who has ever been able to put a key on a piece of paper. I have been in that yard one hundred and seven mornings and I have watched about nine people in it go at a wall and a book and a table and a set of figures and a cart wheel and a road, and not one of them has ever been able to do that. Sabra Holt has been able to do it for nineteen years and has not done it once, and she has done it this morning for the worst possible reason and it has still cost her something, because about four people in this yard have just worked out that they could not have done it either and did not know that until this morning."

---

"I could not have done it," said Mavis Dorr, from the boards, "and I have known that since the middle of the winter and I did not know that I knew it."

---

At about a quarter past ten Iven Tallow said one thing from the stones, about four feet below the top of that bank, in the ordinary voice, and he had been on those stones for thirty-one afternoons running, and nobody had asked him anything and nobody expected anything and about four people in that yard heard him and stopped what they were doing.

"You have about four days before anybody nine miles up that hill has to ask anybody anything," he said. "That is not a warning and it is not advice and I am not trying to be a man with a warning in a yard at eleven in the morning. I am telling you what the thing is so that you do not spend the four days working out what it is, because I have been on these stones for a month and there is a great deal of that going on, and because a strip of paper came off a board at about ten and six this morning with nobody standing near that board, and about nine of you looked at nine feet of air when it did."

---

Everybody in that yard turned round.

---

"Say the rest of it," said the man of the end of the second table.

"There is no rest of it," said Iven Tallow. "I have said the one true thing I know about this morning and I have said it because I have a bad habit of saying one true thing and then stopping, and I have had it since the day I came up this lane, and the whole of what has happened in this yard since I came up it is that about four of you kept asking me for the second half and I kept not giving it to you.

"What I will say is that you have just become the only district in this basin that can be asked about what it is holding, and every other district that has had this done to it in the last two mornings is a district that nobody can ask. You are not in a worse position than they are. You are in the position of the only one in the basin with a key in its hand at eleven o'clock, and I would like about four of you to work out what to do with that before the hour is out, and I am not going to help, and that is not modesty. If I help, you will be able to say afterwards that a man who has been the custodian of the thing for seventeen years told you what to do about it, and then it is his and not yours, and that is the exact shape of the last seventeen years and I have had it twice."

---

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about four o'clock and got all four of them, and he had said the old four to nobody all day, and the strip of paper was on the boards of the second table where a woman had put it at about ten and six, and nobody had covered it, and the boards of the second table were bare where the stone had been, and the stone is about four miles off.

At about half past five Adrian came down the eleven feet of that bank and went off down the lane about four hundred yards and stopped and came back, which is a thing he has done twice in a hundred and seven mornings and about four people in that yard have noticed both times.

He has not been given a bed, a wage or an office. He has not asked for any of the three. There is a length of fencing wire in the pocket of his coat about four miles from a gap of a mile and a half that he has not been to, and the man at the end of the second table came up that lane at about a quarter past six and asked him, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nine people's hearing, whether he was going to go, and Adrian said that he did not know, and the man at the end of the second table said that this was the answer off that step every morning for nine years and that he had got a different figure out of it this morning and would not say what the figure was.
