# Chapter 803: The Eighty-Seventh Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Of Fifty-Six Said The Four He Had Been Reading Since The Middle Of The Winter Out Loud In A Yard In About Nineteen People's Hearing And A Clerk Wrote Them On A Page With Nothing Ruled On It And The Rule About Not Correcting A Figure Turned Out To Work In Both Directions And Iven Tallow Said Out Loud From The Stones That He Had Put The Clause To That Use On The Eighty-Fifth Morning And Had Said Nothing To Anybody About It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and forty-two days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and one. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and seventeen days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and thirty-one days. Six hundred and ninety-two days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and eleven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and eighty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and eighty-four days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and twenty days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and sixty marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and forty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and two sets of four figures were both written down in this district yesterday and neither of them can be struck.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and seventeen mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and sixteen. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for sixty-seven mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is eighty-seven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-four and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and there is no more in it this morning than there was yesterday morning, and the page at the back has ten lines on it and none of the ten is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and twenty-eighth of those mornings, and the four he has been reading since the middle of the winter were said out loud in that yard at about eleven this morning, and they are on a page with nothing ruled on it, and they are not on the wall.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and forty-second night of that run, having slept on four hundred and forty-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the thirty-second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said one thing at about eleven that morning from about four feet below the top of that bank in front of about nineteen people, and nobody at the top of that bank stopped him and nobody at the top of that bank looked down. The record has stood two days, and it has stood through two mornings on which nobody in this district was asked anything about it, and the man who keeps a tally has said the same figure twice this morning, once for himself and once for the wall, and did not say why it has been the same figure for the last forty-three mornings.

---

He said it at about eleven, from the stones, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and about nine people were standing on the boards and all of them turned round, and the man of fifty-six was at the wall and did not turn round because he was reading the four out loud to a clerk at about eleven o'clock, one of them at a time, and he finished the fourth before anybody said anything.

---

"I am going to say one thing and then I am going to stop," said Iven Tallow, "and I want to be exact about the order of it, because I have watched the four of you who are standing nearest spend two mornings deciding whether I am going to say anything today, and I am, and I am not going to pretend that I have not noticed it."

"I put that clause to that use on the eighty-fifth morning, between about eight and about nine, from a room nine miles up that hill, and the four figures on that wall are what came of it, and I said nothing to anybody about it on the eighty-fifth morning, on the eighty-sixth, or last night.

"Not because I was waiting to see what would happen. I want that said first, because that is the version that makes me into a man with a plan and I have never in my life had a plan that produced that wall.

"I did it because for about four days there has been a settlement at the eleventh place on the Salt Verge that has been coming apart, and it has been coming apart in the way that means somebody gets hurt, and the thing that holds a settlement together is not goodwill and it is not a market, it is somebody being answerable for it, and there was nobody, and there is a thing I am allowed to do when there is nobody, and I did it at about eight in the morning, and I have not been on a road since the afternoon before yesterday, and I have come down that hill every morning since the day I came up it with a case under my arm and sat down on some stones, and that is thirty-two mornings and the reason I am telling you is that a man of fifty-six has just worked out that about four of us were here first and I would like it entered that I was not."

---

The yard was extremely quiet and the man of fifty-six finished writing the fourth figure down and put the pencil on the boards and did not turn round.

---

"You are the Custodian," said Sabra Holt.

"I am," said Iven Tallow. "I have been for seventeen years and I have never once been asked to say the word in this yard and I have said it about twice in nine years and both of those times were to myself in an empty room, and I would like it noticed that I have said it to a yard on the thirty-second afternoon of sitting on these stones, and that is not a thing I did in order to be asked, and it is also not a thing I would have done if this yard had been four people instead of nineteen."

---

"Nineteen," said the man of fifty-six, and he said it to the boards and not to the man on the stones, and he turned round for the first time that morning. "It has been nineteen for about as long as I have been standing at that wall, and I have counted it in my head twice a day since before the middle of the winter, and I have never once said it out loud, and I have just said it out loud, and I would like the yard to understand that I only said it because a man of sixty-one has been saying the word Custodian at the bottom of that bank for the best part of an hour and about four of us noticed and nobody stopped him."

---

The man at the end of the second table said the thing that was in the middle of that yard and did not raise his voice.

"I have got a rule," he said, "that somebody made in this yard about as long ago as the wall behind that man has been standing there, and I do not know who made it, and I have never asked, and the whole of what has happened in this district since then is that the rule has never once been tested, because there has never been anything in this yard worth testing it on."

"Yesterday morning a man read four figures off that wall that nobody in this yard had ever heard, and a clerk wrote them down, and the man who has been reading the other four since before the middle of the winter said out loud that he was not going to let anybody hurry him, and a woman with a key put a strip of paper on a table and made this district the holder of a matter, and not one single person in this yard was asked whether they wanted to be the holder of anything.

"And this morning the same man has said four figures out loud and a clerk has written them on a page with nothing ruled on it, and there are now two sets of four figures in this district, and one of them is on a wall and one of them is on a page, and the rule says a figure may not be corrected, or struck, or replaced, or taken out, and I made it, or somebody did, and it is the reason we can tell.

"Fifty-three mornings ago I said in this yard that in about four years somebody was going to put two of our refusals side by side and conclude that this district refuses everything it is asked. That was the thirty-fourth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, and it is the eighty-seventh, and it has taken fifty-three mornings, and the two things that are going to be put side by side are not two refusals. They are a rule that will not let a figure be corrected and a morning when nobody was asked. And I have got about three years and ten months of the four years I gave myself, and I would like to say something about it, and I have not got anything, and that is where I am."

---

"You have got one thing," said Mavis Dorr, "and you have had it since the seventy-fourth morning, and you are not using it because using it would put a name in a column."

---

"Yes," said the man at the end of the second table. "That is exactly it, and I would thank you for it, and I am not going to do it, and the reason I am not going to do it is that the column has one name in it and the name does not satisfy the rule that governs it, and the second name anybody puts in that column is going to be looked at by somebody in about four years under a heading that says *whoever* and neither of us is going to be able to explain what the heading was doing there."

---

Orren Pike wrote the morning on the back of his own sheet and ruled a space under it and left it empty, and did not write the four figures, and Mara Quill read the four off the boards in the ordinary voice at about a quarter past eleven so that about nine people heard her read a page, and about nine people had heard the man say them, and the two sets of about nine people were not the same about nine people, and he wrote that down in the margin and did not put it in the box.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and he said the old four out loud in that yard at about eleven this morning for the first time in five hundred and twenty-eight mornings and wrote nothing down about them, and the page with nothing ruled on it has two lines on it that are figures, and the lot book has five lines and one column with one name in it, and the boards of the second table were bare where the stone had been, and the man who put the stone in his coat said nothing about it on any morning of this week and nobody has asked him, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and forty-second night of that run and had not been asked anything, and would have said.
