# Chapter 819: The One Hundred And Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Keeps Every Road In This Basin Came Up That Lane A Second Time In Nine Years And Stopped And Said That Two Places In This Basin Have Said No Out Loud And That One Of Them Has Had Nothing Done To It And That One Has Said Yes And Cannot Produce A Person And A Man At The End Of The Second Table Said Out Loud That He Has Been In A Room Nine Miles Up That Hill And A Man Of Fifty-Six Said At About Two In The Afternoon That He Wanted The Third Way And Then Did Not Take It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and fifty-eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and seventeen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and thirty-three days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and forty-seven days. Seven hundred and eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and twenty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and twenty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and ninety-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and thirty-six days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and seventy-six marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and sixty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of fifty-six said out loud in a yard at about two this afternoon that he wanted the third way and did not take it, and a man wanting a thing is not a thing this district made and the count did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and thirty-three mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and thirty-two. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for eighty-three mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and three, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-three lines on it and none of the twenty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and forty-fourth of those mornings, and at about two in the afternoon he said one thing out loud in about nineteen people's hearing and it was not the four off that wall, and the four were said twice that day and not three times, and he has never counted an afternoon.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and fifty-eighth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and fifty-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the forty-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of about forty-three who keeps every road in this basin came up that lane at about ten to eight, stopped for the second time in nine years, and went back down it at about ten past eight without putting the book on the boards.

The record has stood eighteen days, and three places in this basin have now answered a form and about four people at the top of eleven feet can say what all three answers are and about nine of them heard it said out loud for the first time this morning, and the number of places where the three lines can be read is still four and did not move.

---

The road keeper said it standing up this time, with the book in his coat, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and about four of them had already worked out about four of the words.

"I said a week ago that this district cannot produce a person. I have been wrong about one thing and I want the yard to have that before the rest of it."

"A place on the north road sent the form back with nothing in it and a line under the space that said they would not. They wrote it out. They wrote a whole sentence and they sent it down nine miles of road."

"And nothing has happened to them. I have been up that road twice in four days and there are men working outside a building and the channel is holding and nobody has been up there asking them for a second time. That is the only thing in this basin that has happened this week that anybody can call a thing going right, and it happened because about nine people in a place four miles off sat down and wrote one sentence."

"A place past the water sent somebody to sit in the room. That somebody sat in it from the second day and wrote nothing at all, and they stopped being there on about the fourth day, and nobody told anybody why, and I went past that room on about the sixth day and it is a room with a table in it."

"And the eleventh place on the Salt Verge sent a form back with a name on it that is not the name of anybody living there and has not been asked whether it is the right name."

He put his hand on the closed book and did not take it off.

"So. Two places said no and one place said yes and cannot. That is three answers and they are three shapes and here is the thing I have been carrying down four miles of road for four days and I am a man of about forty-three and I keep roads and not things."

"From that room all three are a piece of paper with a space on it. Nobody nine miles up that hill has any way of telling a place that wrote a sentence it would not write from a place where the sentence cannot be got up to, and they are not going to look, and they are not being lazy. They cannot. There is about four hundred yards of hill between this yard and any room in this basin and every single thing any of us has ever said out loud in this yard has died about eleven feet down that bank the moment it stopped."

"And I said on the ninety-fifth morning that I would carry the news down about half past four whether this district produced a person or not. I am carrying it down about half past four. You did not produce a person and the reason you did not is not a reason anybody can write down."

---

He went down the eleven feet at about ten past eight and the lane count is twenty-five and did not move.

---

At about eleven the man at the end of the second table put both hands flat on the boards and said the thing he had been not saying for about nine mornings, and he said the words out loud before he said the thing, which is a habit of that yard and not of him.

"I am going to say three things and then I am going to stop and about four people have been waiting since the ninety-first morning and about nine people have not."

"They asked me three times in nine years to be the person. I said no twice. The third time I said nothing at all, and nothing is not the same as no, and I have known that since about the fourth year of this table and I have never once said it out loud in this yard."

"The nothing is the one in the room."

---

Mavis Dorr said, "You have been in a room nine miles up that hill."

---

"I have been in a room nine miles up that hill four times in nine years," said the man at the end of the second table, "and three of those were about the surface of the ground and one of them was not, and it was about seven years ago, and I have never once said so in this yard, and I said nothing that day when I was asked and I have been asked two or three times since and I have said no each time and the yes to the nothing is still sitting in a room nine miles off with my name nowhere on it."

"And I am saying it now because a man of about forty-three stood at that bank this morning and told about nineteen people that there is no way of telling a place that wrote a sentence from a place where the sentence cannot be got up to, and I have been the sentence that could not be got up to, for about seven years, and it has cost me nothing, because nothing costs nothing. It cost about four years of wanting anything at all and I have said that out loud in this yard twice and about four people know it."

"And the price of saying it here is that in about four years a person can say *ask him, he was in the room*, and about four people have just heard me say it and about nine people had already."

---

Orren Pike wrote the morning in the box on the back of his own sheet and ruled a space under it and did not leave it empty, and wrote under it that a man had said in that yard that he had been in a room nine miles up that hill, and that nobody had entered it anywhere, and that he had not read it out and that he was not going to.

---

"Do not," said the clerk.

---

"I am not going to," said the boy. "The box is for mornings and this is a morning and that is the whole of why it goes in the box and the whole of why it does not go anywhere else, and if it goes in a column then in about four years it is a line about a man, and I am not doing that to him on the morning he has just told us."

---

At about two the man of fifty-six came up off the stone and came about four feet along the wall and stood where about nineteen people could see him from the top of eleven feet, which he has not done once in this run of mornings, and about nine of them stopped what they were doing.

"Three ways," he said. "And about four people have said all three of them out loud in this yard in about three mornings and I have heard every one of them from about eleven feet below where I stand, and a man of thirty-eight is standing at about four feet off the near end of the tables and has not asked me for one of them either."

"I want the third one. I am going to say that out loud in the ordinary voice before I say why I am not going to take it, because about four people have taught me to say the wanting first and about nine people have not."

"One morning a year, named beforehand, when a figure moves and nobody in this district can say no to it. That is what I want. I am fifty-six and I have read four figures off that wall twice a morning for five hundred and forty-four mornings and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have heard me and not one of them has ever once asked me what any of the four was for, and I would like one morning a year when those four can move in the open and be looked at and go on moving."

"And I am not going to take it."

---

"Why," said Sabra Holt.

---

"Because on the eighty-fifth morning those four changed," said the man of fifty-six, "and there is no way on this earth anybody in this basin can prove it except me, and the reason they cannot is that a rule in this yard will not let a figure move, and I have read that wall five hundred and forty-four mornings and got them every time and not one figure in any of them has moved, and that is the only reason anybody in this basin can tell which ones are new."

"And one morning a year when a figure moves in the open is that rule with a hole in it the shape of a morning, and the hole is in the same wall."

"I have been asked to put four figures into a column and I said no on the ninety-third morning and gave the reason out loud in full, and I am not going to take a thing that hands the same four figures a way out that a column would have handed them, because the day that happens the four on that wall belong to whoever decided which morning, and I have got them here and I would like to keep them in the same place."

He put his hand flat on the stone and took it off again.

"I have said no out loud twice in about ten days and I have given a reason for both, and the man at the end of that table has said no out loud on more mornings this week than I have and has not given a reason for one of them, and I would like about nine people to notice that the reason is the whole of the difference between him and me and that he is a better writer than I am and that I have had five hundred mornings to find that out."

---

The woman who keeps a scale said one thing from about forty feet off.

"I have been weighing things at the foot of that bank for nine years and I said on the ninety-eighth morning that there is no way on a beam to tell a heavy thing that came by itself from a heavy thing somebody brought you, and I have been proved right twice this morning by two men who have never spoken to each other, and neither of them knew the other one existed until about four days ago."

"And he has just said no to that morning for the reason I have not been able to give away, and the man at the end of that table said the same morning to a room nine miles off about seven years ago and said nothing at all, and those are not the same act and I am not going to pretend they are, and about nine people at the top of that bank have just heard the same morning refused twice from two different directions and I would like to say that is a coincidence and I do not believe it and I have not got anything better than a beam."

---

Mara Quill read the line out and then wrote it, and it is the twenty-fourth line on the page at the back of the lot book and none of the twenty-four is in the lot book, and it said that a man of fifty-six had said out loud at about two in the afternoon that he wanted one morning a year on which a figure could move, and had said in the same place why he was not going to take it, and that she was entering the fact of both halves of it because he had said them in that order on purpose and had told the yard in advance that he was going to, and that she was not entering the meaning of either half.

---

One hundred and twenty-four was written in the box on the back of a sheet four years old at about half past four and was not read out, and at about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine of those nine had heard him refuse something at two o'clock and about four of them had also heard him say that he wanted it, and the boards of the second table were bare where the wire had been and bare where the stone had been, and four hundred and eleven has been on the sheet at that gatepost every morning of this run of mornings and its size of error has not been computed, and the fifth of the five is not paid, and the column has one name in it and the heading over it still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-four lines on it and none of the twenty-four is in the lot book.
