# Chapter 814: The Ninety-Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man At The End Of The Second Table Asked In A Yard In Front Of About Nineteen People What This District Would Offer For The Right To Administer The Tally And A Woman Of Fifty-Two Named One Of The Ways It Is Done And The Man At The End Of The Second Table Named Another One And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Said Out Loud What This District Has And Refused In The Ordinary Voice To Call It An Offer

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and fifty-three days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and twelve. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and twenty-eight days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and forty-two days. Seven hundred and three days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and seventeen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and twenty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and ninety-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and ninety-five days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and thirty-one days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and seventy-one marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and fifty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and four mornings ago in this yard a question was asked for the first time in twenty mornings that anybody in this district could answer, and it has not been answered, and the count did not move.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and twenty-eight mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and twenty-seven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for seventy-eight mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is ninety-eight, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty lines on it and none of the twenty is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and thirty-ninth of those mornings, and a rule about a figure not being able to be corrected or struck or replaced or taken out has stood in that yard for four hundred and twenty-two days and was described out loud in that yard for the first time this morning as something this district has got, and nobody has said it out loud before today in the whole of this run of mornings.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and fifty-third night of that run, having slept on four hundred and fifty-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the forty-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said two sentences this afternoon that the man of about sixty-four did not answer and about four feet of stone does not carry a voice, and this is the fourth time in forty-three afternoons that a man on those stones has said more than two words and it is the first time in this run of mornings that anybody at the top of eleven feet has known there was more said at all.

The record has stood thirteen days, and there are two mornings between this one and the hundredth, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have begun saying the number out loud in the ordinary voice when they are talking to each other about something else, and about nine have not.

---

He said the words out loud before he asked, the way he has said every question in that yard for ten mornings, and he said them at about eleven at the near end of the boards with both hands flat on them.

"I am going to ask this yard a question and I am going to ask it badly, and I am asking it badly on purpose, and I want to say before anybody answers that the last nine mornings of questions in this yard have all been about one person at one end of this table."

"What does this district offer for the right to administer the tally. Not what does it want. Not what does it refuse. What does it have, that it could put on a page in a room nine miles up that hill, in exchange for one mouth for the number and the end of every other count in this basin."

"And I am going to say before anybody answers that I have got about three years and four months of the four years I gave myself, and that I am spending this morning on it, and that whatever is said next is going in a yard and not in a book, and I would like about nine people to notice that this is the first question I have ever put in this yard where the answer is about us and not about a person."

---

Nobody said anything for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

---

"There are three ways I have heard of doing it," said Sabra Holt, "and I am going to say all three and then I am going to stop, because there is nothing in this yard about the first one and about four of us have been standing here for about ten days waiting to see which one we had."

"A man of about forty-one told us the first one two mornings ago at about one and I am not going to say it in his words because it is not his any more, it is ours. One mouth for the number. One person says the tally out loud on a morning and everybody else in this basin stops counting anything at all, and the boards stay up and the marks stay on them and nobody is rude to anybody."

"The second one I was told by a woman with a key, in writing, as a Council and not as a kindness, and she asked me whether this district would take it, and I said I would put it to the yard and I have not done it in four days and I am doing it now. In the second one the right to administer the tally does not go to a person in this district. It goes to a person who is not going to be in this district. The key stays with the room and the person does the counting in a corridor and comes down here on a morning and asks you for your figures and you give them to her and she gives them to the room, and the room does not have to be able to see you to do that."

---

She stopped for about four seconds.

"I have carried the second of those up and down that hill for about four days and I have not been able to put it down, and I want to say why, and the reason is not that I think it is worse than the first one. The reason is that it is easier. And a thing that is easier is a thing that gets offered second, and this yard has about nineteen people in it and about nine of them are tired, and I would like it said out loud that I am one of the nine and that is the reason I can hear it better than the man at the end of that table can."

---

"There is a third one," said the man at the end of the second table, "and I am not going to say it, and then I am going to say it."

"I have been at this table for fifteen years. I have watched about four thousand mornings go past these boards. And the whole of what this district has ever done that anybody anywhere could use is one rule, and the rule is that a figure may not be corrected, or struck, or replaced, or taken out, and I did not make it and I have never asked who did."

"Somebody nine miles up that hill has been offering that rule back to this yard. Not as a favour. As a term. Whoever administers the tally would be allowed one morning a year when a figure moves, in the open, on a morning that is named beforehand, and nobody in this district would be able to say no to it on that morning, because the whole point of the rule is that nobody can say no to it on any morning, and there would be one."

"That is the third one. I did not get it from a woman with a key and I did not get it from a man of forty-one. I have had it since the ninety-first morning and I have not said it because saying it in this yard is me offering this district's one thing for a job for a man of forty-one, and I have known that since the ninety-first morning and I have said nothing for seven mornings, and that is the worst thing I have done in this run of mornings and there have been about ten of them."

---

He put his hands off the boards.

"There. Now about nine people have it and about nine people did not, and I am not going to take it back, and I would like the yard to decide for itself whether I should have said it before somebody came down that lane with a form."

---

Adrian Vale had been at about four feet off the near end of the first table since about nine and he came about four feet further in and stopped, and it was the first time in four mornings that he had moved towards anybody.

"You have all three of them now," he said, "and about nine people have been waiting since yesterday for somebody to say what this district has, and I am going to say it and I am going to say it as a fact about a place and not as an offer, and I am not going to be asked twice."

"This district has a wall. It is nine years old and about eleven feet below the top of that bank and there are four figures on it and nobody in this basin has ever asked anybody what any of the four is for."

"It has a man who reads those four off it twice a morning, and has got them right every morning since before the middle of the winter, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have heard him do it five hundred and thirty-nine times, and one man in this district read four figures out loud at about eleven in the morning nine days ago and a clerk wrote them on a page with nothing ruled on it."

"And it has a rule that will not let a figure be moved, and about four of us arrived at the same thing on the eighty-seventh morning, which is that that rule is the only reason anybody can prove the four on that wall changed, because a thing that cannot be changed cannot be quietly changed."

"That is what this district has got. It is not an offer. It is a man, a wall and a hole in the record, and I want to say the last part out loud because about four people have been carrying it and about nine people have not."

"The hole is that the man is on no page and the wall is on no page and the page with the old four on it has nothing ruled on it, and neither of those two things has been carried up that hill by anybody from this district, because there is nothing in this district that carries anything."

---

The man at the end of the second table said, "That is a description of a district that cannot produce a person," and he said it to the boards and not to Adrian.

"Yes," said Adrian. "I have been walking for a day and a night and I know what the difference is from this side, and the difference is that a district that will not say no can say no, and it costs it something, and it can be found out who paid."

"And we have a wall with four figures on it that nobody has ever asked about, and a rule that says a figure may not be corrected, and a boy of seventeen with a sheet four years old that nobody in this basin can get hold of, and about nineteen people who would have to be asked one at a time from this morning to the hundredth, and that is three mornings if you count this one and two if you do not, and I have counted both in my head since about eleven and got a different answer twice."

"From the other side it is one form with a space on it. And a form with a space on it is not a refusal. It is a thing that could be a house that burned down or a road that is not there in the winter or nineteen people who did not want to be in a room, and I have been on the road side of that distinction for about a day and a night and I could not tell you which one we are and I am not going to guess in a yard."

---

The woman who keeps a scale said one thing from about forty feet off and it carried the way a voice carries when everything else has stopped.

"I have been weighing things at the foot of that bank for nine years," she said, "and the two heaviest things I have ever weighed were a sack of something in the second month of the year before last and a man of fifty-six, once, because somebody put a beam on him and he let them and he asked what it was for afterwards. And there is no way on that beam to tell a heavy thing that came by itself from a heavy thing somebody brought you."

"And that is the whole of what I know and I am not going to give any of it away free, and about four people have been standing in this yard for about four days waiting for one of you to say the thing that makes it stop, and about nine people have not, and I would like the nine people to know that the four people have been perfectly correct this whole morning and that being correct has never once cost anybody anything."

---

She read one line out and then wrote it, and it is the twenty-first line on the page at the back of the lot book and none of the twenty-one is in the lot book, and it said that a question had been asked in that yard at about eleven in the morning about what this district would offer, and that three ways of doing it had been described out loud in about nineteen people's hearing by three different people, and that no offer had been made by anybody in that yard, and that she was entering the fact of the three descriptions and not of the third one, which a man at the end of the second table said in this yard on the ninety-first morning and had not said again until this morning.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and nobody at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at all on the ninety-fifth, ninety-sixth or ninety-seventh morning, and about four of the nine who did not say it this morning have said since that they did not say it because of something a boy said at about one yesterday afternoon about a figure being true on every page, and the box on the back of the boy's sheet had the morning in it and an empty space ruled under the morning and he did not rule a line under that, and under the morning he had written in pencil that two mornings were left and that he was not going to write the number in the box and that the box is for mornings, and the boards of the second table were bare where the wire had been and bare where the stone had been and about nine people at the top of eleven feet could tell you without being asked what the third way was, and four hundred and eleven has been on the sheet at that gatepost every morning of this run of mornings and its size of error has not been computed, and the fifth of the five is not paid, and the column has one name in it and the heading over it still says whoever.
