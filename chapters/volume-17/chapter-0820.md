# Chapter 820: The One Hundred And Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of Thirty-Eight Stood Up At About One In The Afternoon After Sitting At About Four Feet Off The Near End Of The First Table Saying Nothing Since About Nine And Said Out Loud In A Yard In About Nineteen People's Hearing That A Woman On Working List Forty-One Has Not Been Told That Her Name Is On A Page And Was Not Fine And Did Not Want To Be Told And Had Asked Him A Question In Return And He Was Not Going To Answer It In A Yard And A Man At The End Of The Second Table Asked To Come With Him And Was Told He Could Not Because He Is Now On No Page And Is On One

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and fifty-nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eighteen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and thirty-four days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and forty-eight days. Seven hundred and nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and twenty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and twenty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and ninety-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and thirty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and seventy-seven marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and sixty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of thirty-eight came back up that bank at about one this afternoon with nothing in either pocket and a thing in his head, and a thing in a man's head is not a thing this district made and the count did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and thirty-four mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and thirty-three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for eighty-four mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-four lines on it and none of the twenty-four is in the lot book, and there is nothing on that page about the hundredth morning and nothing on it about this one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and forty-fifth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had heard him five hundred and forty-five times and about four of them have said since that the morning he finds out what they are for is the morning somebody in this yard says so, and nothing of the kind has happened in this run of mornings.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and fifty-ninth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and fifty-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the forty-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and neither of them said anything else, and about four feet of stone and a bank did not carry it, and the whole of what has passed between those two men in forty-nine afternoons is two words, and neither of them has said a third.

The record has stood nineteen days, and a man of thirty-eight was in that record's country on the ninety-fifth morning and came up eleven feet at about one this afternoon with nothing in either pocket, and the lane count is twenty-five and did not move, and does not move for a person and is not a page.

---

He came up at about one with nothing in either pocket and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had not heard one word out of him about that woman since about ten past eight in the evening on the ninety-fourth morning, and about four of them had watched him cross the yard at about ten past five on the ninety-fifth without stopping at the boards, and had said nothing about that on any of the mornings since, and he walked the length of the yard and stopped about four feet off the near end of the first table and stood there.

"One hundred and twenty-five," said Orren Pike.

He said it to the boards, out loud, and then he said the rest of it before anybody had turned round.

"That is the number of mornings a man of thirty-eight has stood in this yard. It was one hundred and sixteen on the ninety-fifth morning and it has gone up one every morning since and it is one hundred and twenty-five this morning, and I counted the ninety-fifth morning because he came up that lane at about ten past five and stood at the top of it, and I did not see him do it and a man who keeps roads saw him do it."

"And I have not put it in the box this morning because the box is going to be empty this morning and I am going to say why out loud at about half past two, and a figure is a fact about a morning and that one is a fact about him."

---

Adrian looked at the boy for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and then he said the thing he had come up the bank to say.

"I have been asked nothing in about a day and a night," he said, "so I am going to say the whole of it in order and then somebody can ask me one thing and I will answer it, and I would like the asking to come from about four people and not from about nine people, and I know that is a preference and not a right."

"She has not been told. That is the first thing. Her name went onto a page nine miles up that hill on the eighty-ninth morning in a hand that is not hers and nobody asked her, and I went up that lane on the ninety-fourth morning at about ten past eight in the evening to find out whether anybody had told her since, and nobody had. She is on working list forty-one. She holds the sixth of the nine coppers. She is about fifty. She walked two miles of that corridor to come and stand at these boards on the eightieth morning because a woman with a key wrote her four sentences and asked her to be here at about two, and she has never once been asked anything by anybody, and I know that last part from the man who drives the cart and from a man of about forty-three who keeps roads, and both of those men told me it without being asked and I did not ask either of them, and I want that said first because about four people have spent ten days telling me that the asking is the whole of it."

---

Nobody said anything.

---

"She was not fine."

He said it in the ordinary voice and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and nobody said anything.

"I am not going to say what she is. She asked me not to say what she is and she asked me that before she asked me anything else and I am going to keep it, and I would like about nine people to notice that a woman I have spoken to exactly four sentences to in a corridor nine miles off has told me what to do with the rest of my life in about nine days, and I am doing it, and that is the first time anybody has told me what to do in about a hundred and twenty-five mornings and I have not liked it and I am going to do it."

"And she did not want to be told. That is the part I would like about nine people to hold on to and the part that about four people are already holding. She said that being told was going to make it so that somebody would have to do something about it, and that she was not going to have somebody do something about it, and that if it was never told then it was a page and if it was told then it was a thing that happened to her."

"And she is right. I have been in this yard a hundred and twenty-five mornings and I have watched this district work out how a thing is not a thing about a person, and she worked it out in about four seconds, standing up."

---

"What did she ask you," said Mavis Dorr.

---

"She asked me what the district has got," said Adrian.

---

Nobody at the top of eleven feet said anything for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and about four of them looked at the boards instead of at him.

"She asked me what the district has got, in a corridor, at about eleven at night, having not been asked anything by anybody in about nine years. And she did not ask it as a person who has been named. She asked it the way you would ask about the weather on a place you have to walk to."

"And I have not got an answer. That is what I went up there to get and that is what I have got, and I am going to say what I have got, and I am not going to answer it, and I would like the yard to understand that those two clauses are the same clause and that if I answer it here then I have answered it in about nineteen places at once and she is nine miles off and it is hers."

"What I have got is that this district has a wall with four figures on it and about nineteen people and a book with one name in a column and a rule that will not let a figure be moved and a boy of seventeen who has said a figure out loud twice and held it back once and a man of fifty-six who has wanted one morning a year to himself since yesterday afternoon, and a man of about forty-one who has made himself the only mouth for a number in this basin and a woman who has a key for a room she has not been in."

"And about half of that is a way of not doing the thing, and the other half is about nine people, and she asked me what the district has got and she asked it in about nine words and I have been walking about nine days with those nine words and I am not going to say them at these boards."

---

The man at the end of the second table said, "I would like to come with you when you go back up there."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet turned round, because nobody had said anything like that in this yard in this run of mornings, and the man at the end of the second table had not said anything like it in about four years.

---

"You cannot," said Adrian.

---

"I have been at this table for fifteen years and I know what is nine miles up that hill better than anybody in this yard and I have been in a room there four times in nine years and I said so out loud in this yard yesterday morning and about nine people heard it."

"And that is exactly it," said Adrian. "You said it out loud in the sun in front of about nineteen people on the hundred and third morning because a man of about forty-three told us that a room nine miles off cannot tell a place that wrote a sentence from a place where the sentence cannot be got up to. And now you are on a page."

"Not a page anybody can walk up to and read. A page. The second space on a form, under a heading that says whoever. You have been findable for about a day and a half and you got findable by telling the truth in a yard, and I am not saying that was wrong, and I am saying that if I ask you to walk nine miles with me this afternoon then about four people in this basin and at least one woman in a corridor are going to see two men go up that hill together, and this morning the whole of what this yard said out loud died about eleven feet down that bank."

"You are the only person in this district I would want to walk up there with and you are the only person in this district I cannot have, and I have been here a hundred and twenty-five mornings and that is the first thing I have wanted out loud and been told no to, and I am not going to insult you by pretending I am not saying that it is because of yesterday."

---

"Do not ask me why I asked," said the man at the end of the second table.

---

"You asked because you have been in a room and you know what the answer would be like in there, and I know that, and I am not going to make you say it."

---

The turner had been standing about four feet off the near end of the second table since about ten to eight and he had not said anything since about eleven, and now he said two sentences.

"I am the only mouth for the number in this basin from a morning that gets named, and nobody has named the morning yet, and I want to say something about that at about two o'clock and I am not going to say it now because a man of thirty-eight has just come up that bank after two days and about four of us have not asked him anything and I am not going to be the fifth person this morning who does that to somebody."

---

Mara Quill did not open the book. She said so, at about half past one, out loud, in about nine people's hearing, and she said that the hundredth morning had taught her something and that she was not going to explain what it was, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at her and about nine did not.

---

Orren Pike did not write anything in the box on the back of his own sheet, and at about half past two he said why out loud to the boards, which is the only place anything he writes can end up being said.

"I am not writing this morning in the box because the box is for a thing that was said out loud in that yard and was not entered anywhere, and I have had about nine mornings of deciding what a morning is, and a man came back up that bank at about one and said four things that nobody is going to enter anywhere, and it is a morning, and if I write it then it is in a sheet a boy of seventeen has kept for four years and that is the same place as a column with a heading over it, only smaller and with me in it."

"So I am leaving the space empty and I am going to write *nothing today* in it, and that is not the same as the four other mornings when I left a space empty, because on those mornings nothing had been said that I wanted to keep and this morning something has been said that I want to keep and I am not going to be the one who keeps it."

"And if this yard wants a record of the hundred and fourth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month it will have to be somebody who is not me, and there is not anybody, and that is the answer to the road keeper and I am seventeen and I have said it and it is said."

---

At about half past two the turner said his two sentences and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard them and about four of them have said since that the second one is the reason a man of thirty-eight came back up that bank.

"The morning has not been named. I have been the one mouth for the number in this basin since about a quarter past eleven on the one hundred and first morning and about four days and nobody has named the morning I start, and I have found out that I do not want to name it, because the day I name it is the day I begin, and for about four days I have been a man who has agreed to something and has not yet got to the part where it is his hands."

"And I am telling this yard that at about four o'clock this afternoon I am going to go down that lane and do it, because I have carried it about four days and I am not a man who carries things, I am a man who reads walls, and I have been at the end of this lane for nine years and I have been reading a wall in it for six of them and this morning there are about nineteen people in that yard who want to hear a number and I am going to go and give them one and it will be the first one and it will be wrong within about four years and I would like it said in this yard that I said that."

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and a man of about forty-one went down the eleven feet at about ten to four to go and be the only mouth for a number in this basin, and the lane count is twenty-five and did not move and does not move for a person and is not a page.

The boards of the second table were bare where the wire had been and bare where the stone had been and the light went off them at about half past four, and four hundred and eleven has been on the sheet at that gatepost every morning of this run of mornings and its size of error has not been computed and is not going to be computed by anybody in this basin, and the fifth of the five is not paid and the count of it is still five, and the column has one name in it and the heading over it still says whoever and nobody in this district knows who ruled it, and the clause has been extended and not ended and the condition on the seat is still unnamed and a man of sixty-one is sitting on some stones at the foot of that bank saying good afternoon to a man who has said it back every afternoon for forty-nine afternoons, and about nine miles up that hill a woman on working list forty-one has not been told, and a man of thirty-eight is going to go and try to answer her, and the page at the back of the lot book has twenty-four lines on it and none of the twenty-four is in the lot book.
