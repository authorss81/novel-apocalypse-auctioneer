# Chapter 823: The One Hundred And Seventh Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Boy Of Seventeen Said A Figure Out Loud And A Man At The End Of The Second Table Asked A Yard In About Nineteen People's Hearing What This District's One Rule Is For Now That A Room Nine Miles Off Has Said Out Loud What It Pays For And Nobody Answered Him And A Man Of Fifty-Six Told A Man Of Thirty-Eight To Ask Him When He Had Got It Ready

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and sixty-two days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and twenty-one. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and thirty-seven days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and fifty-one days. Seven hundred and twelve days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and twenty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and thirty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and four days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and forty days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and eighty marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and sixty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet read a heading off a sheet yesterday morning and about four of them have not said one word about it since, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and thirty-seven mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and thirty-six. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for eighty-seven mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-six lines on it and none of the twenty-six is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and forty-eighth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and not one of them has ever asked him what any of the four is for and that has been true since before the middle of the winter.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and sixty-second night of that run, having slept on four hundred and sixty-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the fifty-second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said two words and stopped, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood twenty-two days, and the clerk entered one line on the back of that book yesterday morning and read it out before she wrote it, which is the twenty-sixth, and she entered nothing this morning and gave no reason for it.

---

"One hundred and twenty-eight," said Orren Pike, to the boards, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were already standing there because word travels about four hundred yards in this district faster than it travels up a hill.

He did not add anything to it, and he put the pencil back down behind the boards, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said out loud what the boy had said yesterday and the day before that, which is a thing that has started happening in that yard this week and which nobody there has decided to stop.

---

The man of about forty-one had gone down that lane again at about ten to four the previous afternoon and had not come up it, and Mara Quill had opened the lot book at about nine and looked at the page at the back of it and closed it again, and about four people saw her do it.

"You have not written anything this morning," said Mavis Dorr.

"No," said the clerk.

"You wrote one yesterday morning and you read it out before you wrote it."

"Yes."

"That is two mornings in a row that a woman has had a pen in her hand in this yard and not used it, and I would like to know what the second one is."

"You would like to know," said the clerk, "and I am not going to tell you, and I have not told you the first one either, and I would like about nine people to notice that the two of those are the same sentence and that I have said it twice and only once."

---

At about eleven the man at the end of the second table put both hands flat on the boards the way everybody in that yard puts its hands flat on the boards, and he said the words out loud before he asked, which is a habit that yard has and not a habit of his.

"I am going to ask a question and about four people have been waiting since yesterday and about nine have not."

He put his hands down off the boards and left them at his sides.

"A sheet came down that hill yesterday morning with a heading over it. The heading says mornings on which a number was said out loud in a place and somebody in that place heard it and can be asked about it separately. And a room nine miles off that hill has been deciding for seventeen years what it pays for, and it has never once been in this yard to say so, and yesterday morning it said so by accident and through a man of about forty-one."

About nine people at the top of eleven feet were very still.

"So I would like to ask what this district's one rule is for. Not what it is for. It is a figure may not be corrected, or struck, or replaced, or taken out, and I have said that sentence out loud in this yard about four thousand times in fifteen years and it has never once been asked what it is for. I am asking it this morning because I have found out that somebody nine miles off has been paying for a thing this yard has been refusing to do for nine years, and I would like to know which of the two of us has been right."

---

Nobody said anything.

The yard does that. About nine people at the top of eleven feet had watched a man refuse a column and refuse a morning in about ten days, and had watched a man at the end of a table say nothing at all twice, and every one of those had been about a person, and this one was not.

"You are not going to answer that," he said, to the boards.

"No," said Adrian Vale.

"I did not expect to be answered. That is four times in about four years that I have said a thing in this yard and been let it stand, and there is a figure in this yard about how much of the four years I gave myself is left, and I said that figure out loud once and I am not going to say it a second time, because the third time I say a number like that in this yard it stops being a thing I am giving up and becomes a thing I am measuring."

---

Adrian went down the eleven feet at about half past twelve and went along the wall about four feet and stood about four feet off the man of fifty-six with his back to the stone, the way a man stands who has read figures off a copy for six years and is not going to pretend to read them off the wall.

The man of fifty-six said the four and got all four of them.

"Have you ever been asked anything," said Adrian.

"No."

"About any of the four."

"No."

"Not by anybody since before the middle of the winter."

"Not by anybody since before the middle of the winter," said the man of fifty-six, "and I want to say the next part before you get to it, because I have watched you carry something about four days and it is on your face and I have never seen a thing carry a man so badly."

"I have not asked yet."

"You have not asked yet," said the man of fifty-six, "and I know what it is, because it is the only question anybody has ever got out of me, and you are not going to get it out of you in a morning by asking it at eleven feet below a bank where about nine people cannot hear you."

"Then when," said Adrian.

"When you have got it ready," said the man of fifty-six. "That is all. And I will tell you now so that you do not spend three days deciding whether to say it. You have been in this yard a hundred and twenty-eight mornings and you have not asked me anything and I have noticed that, and I have noticed it about four days and I have not said anything about it either, because a man who has been right about four figures for five hundred and forty-eight mornings does not get asked for a favour by a man who has been standing at the top of a bank for four days."

---

He put his hand flat on the stone and took it off again, and Adrian Vale stood there for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and found that he had been given leave and that being given leave was heavier than being refused.

---

At about two Sabra Holt was at the boards with her hands in her sleeves, which is where she keeps them.

"You have not been up that lane," she said.

"No."

"You said on the one hundred and fourth morning that you were going to answer a question."

"I have not got an answer."

"That is not what I asked you," said Sabra Holt. "I asked whether you have been up that lane, and you have not, and I have been an appointed administrator for nineteen years and I know exactly what a person looks like on the third morning of not doing a thing they said in a yard they were going to do."

---

"And a woman nine miles off asked me what this district has got," said Adrian, "in about nine words, in a corridor, at about eleven at night, and I am not going to say those words at these boards, and I have been walking about nine days with them."

"Then you will have to do a different thing," said Sabra Holt, "and I do not know what it is, and I have thought about it for about a day and a night, which is longer than I have thought about anything in about nineteen years."

She took her hands out of her sleeves and put them flat on the boards for about a second and took them back.

"And I am not going to help you find it. I want to be very clear about that, because I have noticed that a woman who has a key gets asked for the difficult half and I have done it twice now and both times I have found out afterwards that the difficult half was not the part anybody wanted."

---

Orren Pike came round the near end of the boards with his sheet under his arm and stopped about four feet off them.

"I have got a question," he said, "and it is about the sheet and not about a morning, and I have been keeping it since yesterday and I am saying it at about two in the afternoon and not in the box because the box is for mornings."

Nobody said anything, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet waited, and the boy of seventeen found that waiting was the worst part and said the rest of it anyway.

"That page came down that hill yesterday morning with about nine places on it and not one name anywhere on it. And I have got a sheet I have kept for four years and there is a name in it and there is a morning in it and there is a figure in it that a man wrote and did not read out. And that page says a room nine miles off does not want anybody's name. And I have been four years thinking my sheet was the only copy of anything, and it turns out the only copy of anything is a sheet with nobody's name on it, and I would like somebody to tell me what I have been keeping."

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine of those nine had been at those boards at about two when a boy of seventeen asked a yard a question about a sheet he has kept for four years, and not one of them has answered him, and nobody was entered on the back of that book this morning and the woman who enters things gave no reason for it, and four hundred and eleven has been on the sheet at that gatepost every morning of this run of mornings and its size of error has not been computed, and the fifth of the five is not paid, and the column has one name in it and the heading over it still says whoever.
