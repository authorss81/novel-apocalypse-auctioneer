# Chapter 805: The Eighty-Ninth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Woman Who Is On Working List Forty-One In A Corridor Nine Miles Up That Hill Was Named In A Permanent Record Nine Miles Up That Hill Before She Was Asked And A Clerk In This District Entered That She Had Been Named And Did Not Enter Why It Felt Like Being Named And A Man Of About Twenty-Nine Who Drives The Cart Said One Sentence About It That He Had Been Not Saying For Nine Mornings

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and forty-four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and nineteen days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and thirty-three days. Six hundred and ninety-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and thirteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and eighty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and eighty-six days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and twenty-two days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and sixty-two marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and forty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the fifth of the five is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a record nine miles up that hill named a person who is not in a household this morning without asking her, and the count did not move and the fifth of the five is still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and nineteen mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and eighteen. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for sixty-nine mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is eighty-nine, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-four and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and a name that is not in any of them went into a permanent record this morning.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and thirtieth of those mornings, and at about a quarter to eleven this morning a woman with a key put a sheet of four lines on the boards of the second table and read them twice, and about nine people heard the reading of the second line of it a third time, and there is a man of about sixty-four at the foot of that wall who has never in his life been handed a page and who was not handed one this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and forty-fourth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and forty-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the thirty-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and neither of them knew that a woman who has been in about four of these mornings had a page in her own coat, and neither of them would have wanted to know.

The record has stood four days, and a person in this basin was entered in it this morning who has never been asked anything by anybody in her life, and the lane count is twenty-four and does not move for a thing that happened nine miles up a hill.

---

Sabra Holt had the sheet on the boards at about a quarter to eleven and she read it twice before she read it out, which is a thing she has done in this yard about four times, and the second time she read it slower.

It is a sheet of four lines and it is headed about a thing. The first line says that the open matter of a district in respect of persons not in a household has been taken into one permanent record. The second line gives a form of words for that. The third line gives a figure. The fourth line names her.

---

"Nell Cray," said Sabra Holt, and stopped, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at the boards.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart said the next part, and he had not said a whole sentence in that yard in nine mornings, and everybody standing near him noticed it before he had got to the end of it.

"She is not in that room," he said. "Nobody has asked her. She is nine miles up that hill in a corridor with a door and she walks two miles of it to come here, and she is on a working list, and the working list has been on that side of that hill since before any of us could read, and there is a woman at the end of that corridor who has never once been told anything about anybody on it.

"And that sheet has got her name on it in a hand that is not hers, and she has not been asked, and by the time anybody in this basin finds out about it she will have been in that record for about four days, and the four days will be the reason nobody can do anything about it. That is what a record is. It is not that it hides things. It is that it is already true by the time you get to it."

---

"Do not say her name again," said the clerk, from the near end of the second table. "Not in that order. I am not refusing to write it. I have written what a person was named four times this week in a book with nothing ruled on it and I will write it a fifth time, and I am refusing to have her name be the fourth thing that happened in about an hour in that order.

"It is not her name that has gone nine miles. It is that nobody asked. And if I write her name first then about nine people in this yard will remember this morning as the morning a woman got put in a record, and in about four years that is what it will be, and it will be the right shape and it will be the wrong morning, because the morning is that this district has now been the holder of a matter for three days and has not read the terms of it, and a person got named in the middle of that and about nine of us read on."

---

"What are the terms of it," said Adrian.

---

Nobody in that yard had asked him anything since the first morning he came up that lane and about four people in it turned round.

---

"Four lines," said Sabra Holt. "I have read it twice. The first line is what it is. The second line is eleven words and I read them out loud in this yard on the eighty-sixth morning and I am not going to read them again today, and I read them twice on the morning they came and once on the eighty-sixth, and I have said them once this morning, and that is four times and that is the number I have given myself and I am not going past it.

"The third line is a figure and I am not going to say the figure in this yard. I have said four figures out of a page this week and I am not going to be the person in this district who reads a number off a paper in the sun and cannot say where it came from, and there are about four people in this yard who have been saying numbers out loud all morning and every one of them has said where it came from.

"The fourth line names her. And I want to say one thing about the fourth line and then I am going to stop and let somebody else have this yard for a minute, because I have been carrying four things up that hill for about eleven mornings and refusing a fifth, and this morning the hill has sent something back with a person's name at the bottom of it in a hand that is not hers, and I have not been asked about that, and I am not going to pretend that being asked about it would have been better."

---

Mara Quill entered two lines on the page at the back of the lot book with nothing ruled on it and read them out before she wrote them, and they are the twelfth and thirteenth of the lines on that page and none of the thirteen is in the lot book.

The first line said that a person had been named in a record held elsewhere and that this district did not know when and had not been asked. The second line said that the clerk was entering the fact of the naming and was not entering the effect of it on the person named, on the ground that she was not that person's clerk and would not be one.

---

Mavis Dorr said, "You are hers. You are the only person in this basin she has ever spoken four sentences to in writing, and if you are not her clerk then who is."

---

"Then I am hers in a way I did not agree to and cannot get out of," said the clerk, "and I would like the yard to understand the size of that. I have been a clerk in this yard for about four mornings and I have been in this district for fifteen years, and those are two different jobs, and four of us in this yard have been treating them as one job since the first morning, and a person got named in a record this morning because we did."

---

"Do not do that," said Adrian. "You did not name her. Somebody nine miles up a hill named her and you entered a fact about it and that is a different act and it is the only act any of us could have done this morning and I want to be the last person in this yard to say it and I am going to be a late one about it."

---

"You are going to be," said the man at the end of the second table. "You are a man of thirty-eight who has been in this yard a hundred and ten mornings and has not been given a bed, a wage or an office, and there is a length of fencing wire in your coat about four miles from a gap of a mile and a half that you have not been to, and four of us in this yard have watched you not go for about ninety mornings, and today there is a woman two miles up that lane with her name on a page and you are going to be the man who was talking about clerks."

"I am not saying that to be cruel. I am saying it because it is the arrangement, and I made a line about the arrangement when I was about twenty-two, and this morning somebody used it, and I would like to hear from one person in this yard what the arrangement is for, and I have asked, and nobody has said, and I have got about three years and nine months."

---

Adrian did not answer it and about four people in that yard noticed and about nine did not, and it is the third morning running he had not answered the man at the end of the second table, because on the eighty-sixth he had answered him in seven words and the man at the end of the second table had noticed that too and had said nothing about it since, which is a thing about him that about four people in that yard have learned in about nine years.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and the clerk's pencil went into a book, and about four people said *as sent* and about nine did not, and Orren Pike wrote the morning in the box on the back of his own sheet and under it he wrote a name and then he put the pencil down and picked it up again and left the name where it was, because the box is for mornings and a name is a person.

The boards of the second table were bare where the stone had been, and the man of about sixty-four said good afternoon at about half past three to a man on some stones and neither of them said anything else, and the whole of what has passed between those two men in thirty-four afternoons still fits in two words, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet noticed on the eighty-ninth morning that neither of them had looked up at the bank.
