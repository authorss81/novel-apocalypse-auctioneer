# Chapter 818: The One Hundred And Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Woman Asked A Woman Of Fifty-Two In A Yard In Front Of About Nineteen People What She Had Done With A Key And The Woman Of Fifty-Two Put A Small Iron Key On The Boards And Said That She Had Wanted To Find Out Whether Anybody Would Come And Ask And Was Not Comforted And At About Half Past Three A Man Of About Sixty-Four Said One Sentence At The Foot Of That Low Wall To A Man Of Fifty-Six And About Nine People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Never Heard It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and fifty-seven days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and sixteen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and thirty-two days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and forty-six days. Seven hundred and seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and twenty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and twenty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and ninety-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and ninety-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and thirty-five days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and seventy-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and sixty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a key went onto the boards of that table at about eleven this morning and came off them again before the light went off them, and a key is not a thing this district made and the count did not move.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and thirty-two mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and thirty-one. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for eighty-two mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-two lines on it and none of the twenty-two is in the lot book, and three of them are about a thing that happened nine miles up that hill and one of them was never read out.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about half past three and got them both times, and he said them a third time at about four this afternoon and got them again, and that is the five hundred and forty-third of those mornings, and at about half past three this afternoon he was four feet off that wall with his back against it and a man of about sixty-four came along and said one sentence and he did not answer it, and nobody at the top of eleven feet has any idea that either of those two things happened.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and fifty-seventh night of that run, having slept on four hundred and fifty-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the forty-seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of about forty-one came down that lane at about half past two with his bag over his shoulder and went past the wall and did not stop and did not look at it.

The record has stood seventeen days, and a man of about forty-one said yes in this yard at about a quarter past eleven yesterday morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they have not worked out whether the yes was a thing this district did or a thing that was going to happen to this district whether anybody in it said anything, and the clerk entered neither.

---

Mavis Dorr did not make a speech about it. She said the words out loud before she asked, the way the people in that yard had learned to wait for, and she asked it at about eleven with about nineteen people at the top of eleven feet.

"I am going to ask Sabra Holt what she did the key for, and I have had about sixteen mornings to think about it and I have decided that I am not going to ask her whether she is all right and I am not going to tell her afterwards that she was brave, because she said on the eighty-sixth morning that the reason was a bad one and nobody in that yard has asked her for the reason since and about four people have noticed that and about nine people have not."

---

"Yes," said Sabra Holt.

---

She took a chain out from inside her coat and there was a small iron key on the end of it and she put it on the boards of the second table in the middle, about a foot from the near edge, and she did not let go of it for about a second and then she did.

"It is the third of nine. I have held it for nineteen years. It is not a key to this yard and it is not a key to that bank and it is not a key to anything anybody in this district can get into except one room, and I have not been in that room since the morning I used it."

"You all know what I used it on. A strip of paper about the length of a hand. On the eighty-sixth morning, in front of about nineteen people, and I put a key next to a piece of paper so that this district would be the only holder of a matter in this basin that anybody could be asked about, and I read the four lines out twice, and I said the reason out loud and I said the reason was a bad one, and nobody has asked me for the reason in about sixteen mornings and I have been waiting every morning since about ten to eight for one of you to ask me for the reason."

---

Mavis Dorr said, "That is the reason."

---

"That is the thing I did," said Sabra Holt. "The reason is worse and I am going to say it and I am going to say it once."

"I have been an appointed administrator for nineteen years. In nineteen years I have signed about eleven hundred things and administered one thing every morning and I have never once been asked what any of it was for, and I have never once been asked whether I wanted to do it. Nobody in this basin has ever handed me a piece of paper and said *put your name to this*."

"So I put a key on a piece of paper. That is the whole of it. I wanted to find out whether anybody in this basin would come and ask me what I had done and why I had done it. Not whether they would approve. Whether they would come."

"Four people came down that lane in about sixteen days. About nine of them came to find out what a record was. A boy of seventeen came to say a figure out loud before a clerk could. A man of about thirty-eight came to ask whether a woman had been told about her own name. And every single one of them asked me what was in the paper, and not one of them asked me why I had put a key next to it."

"And I am fifty-two years old and I have had a key on a chain for nineteen years and this morning it is on a table in front of about nineteen people because somebody in this yard decided that sixteen mornings was long enough."

---

She picked the key up and put it back inside her coat.

"Do not say anything to me now," she said. "I am not going to be comforted in this yard and I am not going to be comforted outside it either, and if about four people want to tell me that I did a difficult thing today then I would like you to notice that a difficult thing is a thing that was difficult and not a thing that was right, and that I have been the third of nine oldest keys in this basin for nineteen years and not one of the other eight has ever been asked a question about a key, and I would have liked one of them to have been asked a question in about four years. That is all."

---

Adrian Vale had been at about four feet off the near end of the first table since about nine and he had said nothing all morning and he said one thing now.

"I am going to say the part that is mine and not yours, and then I am going to go and stand somewhere else."

"Nineteen years is a long time to administer one thing a morning and never be asked what it is for. It is longer than the wall is old. And you did the only thing available to you, which was to put something down where a person would have to walk past it, and about four people in this yard have spent sixteen days walking past it, and you have been right about all of that and none of it is any use to you this morning."

"And the part that is mine is that a person who waits sixteen mornings to be asked a question has been doing the same thing as the rest of us, only louder, and I have never once asked anybody in this yard what they were for."

---

At about one the man at the end of the second table asked her a second question, which was not the same question and which she permitted.

"Who has the other eight," he said.

---

"Rooms," said Sabra Holt. "A room with a table in it on the far side of that hill and nobody sitting in it. About four women in this basin who are not older than me, and two of us who are, and not one of the nine of us has ever been asked a question about a key, and one key is on a chain in a room nine miles up that hill belonging to a woman who has been dead for about six years and the room is not locked."

---

Nobody in that yard had heard that before, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at each other and about nine looked at the boards.

---

One line went on to the page at the back of the lot book and she read it out before she wrote it, and it is the twenty-third line on that page and none of the twenty-three is in the lot book, and it said that a woman of fifty-two had put a key on the boards of the second table at about eleven in the morning and taken it off again before the light went off them, and that she had said out loud in about nineteen people's hearing that she had put it there to find out whether anybody would ask her why, and that she was entering the fact of the saying and not the reason, and that the reason had been given and belonged to the woman who gave it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of fifty-six was four feet off it with his back against the stone, and the two of them had said good afternoon and then the man of about sixty-four said one sentence.

"It is at the turn. It is not at the culvert. It is about four hundred yards from where a man of thirty-eight put it and about four miles from the top of this bank, and I have been down there four mornings. I have not put it back and I am not going to put it back."

---

The man of fifty-six did not say anything.

He stood with his back against that stone for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and then he said the four off that wall, which is not what that wall is for, and got all four of them, and the man of about sixty-four was not there when he finished them.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them for the third time that day, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine of those nine did not know that the same four figures had been said out loud once already at about half past three at about eleven feet below where they were standing with no one within about four hundred yards of him, and there was a ruled space under the morning in the box on the back of the boy's sheet and nothing at all in it and nothing written beside it either, and the boards of the second table were bare where the wire had been and bare where the stone had been, and four hundred and eleven has been on the sheet at that gatepost every morning of this run of mornings and its size of error has not been computed, and the fifth of the five is not paid, and the column has one name in it and the heading over it still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-three lines on it and none of the twenty-three is in the lot book.
