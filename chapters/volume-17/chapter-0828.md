# Chapter 828: The One Hundred And Twelfth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Forty-One Came Up The Bank At About Ten And Said That The Second Space On That Form Is Still Empty And Is Going To Stay Empty Until Somebody In This Yard Asks A Question And At About Two A Man Of Thirty-Eight Asked A Man Of Fifty-Six What The Four Figures On That Wall Are For And The Man Of Fifty-Six Said He Did Not Know And Said He Had Nobody To Hand It To

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and sixty-seven days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and twenty-six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and forty-two days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and fifty-six days. Seven hundred and seventeen days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and thirty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and thirty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and forty-five days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and eighty-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and seventy-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of thirty-eight asked a question out loud in that yard at about two this afternoon and about nine people heard it and the clerk wrote it down and read it out before she wrote it, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and forty-two mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and forty-one. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for ninety-two mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and twelve, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-six and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-eight lines on it and none of the twenty-eight is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and fifty-third of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of those nine had been at those boards at about two when a man of thirty-eight asked a question in the ordinary voice, and not one of those nine has said out loud that the two of those things were about different men.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and sixty-seventh night of that run, having slept on four hundred and sixty-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the fifty-seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had asked each other in the ordinary voice what a heading says, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood twenty-seven days, and the count of strangers who have come down that lane is twenty-six and did not move, and a question was asked in that yard at about two this afternoon and about nine people heard it and about four of them have said since that they can remember it exactly.

---

"One hundred and thirty-three," said Orren Pike, and then, to about nine people who did not turn round, "He said on the one hundred and ninth that he was going to do it in about three days and it is the one hundred and twelfth and he has not come out from behind the first table yet and I am not saying anything about it."

---

"It is the third morning you have said no to me about it," said the man at the end of the second table, "and I am going to go on mentioning it, and I am mentioning it in front of about nine people and not to you, and you are not to thank me for it and you are not to say anything about it, because a man of thirty-eight standing in a yard saying no to himself is not the same as a man of thirty-eight standing in a yard saying no to somebody else and I have had about four years of knowing the difference."

---

The man of about forty-one came up the eleven feet at about ten and went straight to the boards and put the form down flat, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at it and it was the same piece of paper that had gone down that lane with two empty spaces on it.

"They have asked me twice more," he said.

---

"About what," said Sabra Holt.

"Whether this district has a person in it who can be asked about a figure." He put one finger on the second space and did not write in it. "And I have not answered either time, and I am going to carry this down that lane with the two spaces on it the way it came up, and the second one has somebody else's heading over it and the first one has mine."

"You could put your own name in the first space," said Mavis Dorr.

"I could, and about four years ago the answer to that was that nobody in this yard had ever asked me, and now there is a heading over the other one that says whoever, and if I put my name in the first space then a room nine miles off gets a page that says this district produced a person, and it does not get a page that says this district has not been asked, and this district has not been asked."

---

"Then it is going to stay empty," said the man at the end of the second table.

"It is going to stay empty," said the turner, "and I want it said out loud in that yard that the second space on that form is going to stay empty until somebody in this yard asks a question, and that I have known that since the hundredth morning and that I have been the reason it is still empty, and that I am not going to carry it up there and have anybody say in about four years that this district declined."

He put the form back in the inside of his coat.

"It has not declined. It has not been asked. There is a difference and it is the only difference this district has at the moment and I would like it entered somewhere, and nothing is going to be entered anywhere, so I have said it in a yard and about nine people heard it."

---

At about two Adrian Vale came round the near end of the first table and stood about four feet off the boards of the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stopped.

"I said on the one hundred and ninth morning that I was going to ask a question and that it was not a figure," he said. "I am going to say the question out loud before I ask it, because that is what this yard does and I have got used to it in about nine days."

He put his hands flat on the boards.

"The four figures on that wall are for what."

---

Nobody said anything for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

"I am not asking which of them is wrong," said Adrian. "I am not asking you to tell me what is on that wall now. I am not asking you to put anything in a column and I am not asking anybody in this yard to put your name anywhere. I have read the heading on that sheet and I know what the room nine miles off pays for and this is not it, and I am going to do it anyway, and I said in this yard on the one hundred and ninth that I would say so before I did it."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet looked down about eleven feet.

The man of fifty-six was on the flat stone at the foot of the low wall. He came about four feet along it, which he has not done in this run of mornings, so that he could be seen from the top of the bank, and he stood there and put his hands loose at his sides.

"Who is asking," he said.

"A man of thirty-eight who has stood in this yard one hundred and thirty-three mornings."

"That is not the second half of it."

"And why now," said Adrian Vale, "is because a man of sixty-one came up these eleven feet seven mornings ago and said five things that nobody in this yard could check, and one of them was that the figure that decides what this basin is worth is not going to be on that sheet. And I have found out since then that a room nine miles off that hill pays for mornings on which somebody is asked, and that everything I have been doing since the eighty-fifth morning has been the wrong shape for it, and I would rather ask you a question that is not going to be paid than carry a page nine miles that is going to be paid and be a man who carries pages."

---

The yard was extremely quiet.

"I am going to answer it," said the man of fifty-six, "and I am going to ask for one thing first and I am asking for it out loud in a yard where about nine people can hear me, because I have been asked for nothing since before the middle of the winter and I am not going to be asked for anything and let it go out of the yard like everything else does."

"Ask."

"Put it down that you asked me." He put one hand flat on the stone. "Not what I say. Put it down that I was asked, in that yard, on a morning, and about nine people heard it. That is all I want. I have been right five hundred and fifty-three mornings running and about four people in this basin know it and none of them can prove which ones were new, and the only reason anybody can prove which ones were new is that a rule in this yard will not let a figure move, and if you ask me a question in that yard and about nine people hear the asking then there is going to be one thing in this basin that happened this morning and anybody can be asked about it separately."

"It will be entered," said Adrian.

---

"I do not know," said the man of fifty-six.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not make a sound.

"That is the whole of my answer and it has taken me about four seconds and I have been getting it ready since before the middle of the winter." He took his hand off the stone. "I did not know what those four figures were for at the beginning of it either and I thought I would, and I have not known since about the middle of it. And I have said them out loud at about a quarter to eight and about four for five hundred and fifty-three mornings to about nine people who have never once asked me, and about four feet of stone and eleven feet of bank have carried every one of them, and not one of them went anywhere."

---

"And that is all," said Adrian.

"That is all of the first part," said the man of fifty-six. "There is a second part and I am going to say it now because you asked it in a yard and not at the foot of a wall, and because a man of fifty-six has been asked one thing since before the middle of the winter and he is not going to be asked one thing and then go back to the same morning he has been having."

He turned round and faced the top of the bank.

"This afternoon, after I have said the four, I am going to say out loud that I do not know what any of them is for. And I am going to say it in the ordinary voice, every morning, at about four, and anybody in this yard who wants to ask me about that can ask me about it at these boards on any morning, and I will answer it the same way every time, which is that I do not know."

---

Mara Quill read the line out first, which is what she does, and then entered it, and it is the twenty-ninth line on the page at the back of the lot book and none of the twenty-nine is in the lot book, and it said that a man of thirty-eight had asked a question at the boards of the second table at about two in the afternoon and that a man had answered it in about nineteen people's hearing, and that she was entering the fact of the asking and the fact of the answering and not what either of them meant, and that neither of the two men had given a name and that nobody in that yard had asked either of them for one.

---

"Put it in the book," said Mavis Dorr. "Put it in the book. It has been five lines for fifteen years, and I am not going to say your name in front of these people, because you have spent about nine days not having one said in front of them."

"No."

"It is the first thing anybody has wanted in that book since the fifth line."

"I know what it is," said the clerk. "I have been its clerk for fifteen years and I know exactly what that book has been, and it has been five lines and one column and a page at the back that has never once gone anywhere, and I said on the one hundred and first morning that this page was going to have about four lines on it that nobody heard me read and that there is no way on this earth for anybody to know which four, and I have written one more line this morning and that is all I am going to do about it."

---

"Then say it to the yard," said Mavis Dorr, "and not to me, because I have asked you one question this morning and I am not going to be the person you say it to."

---

"The reason I will not put it in that book," said the clerk, to about nine people at the top of eleven feet, "is that it is the only book in this district and if a thing about a man of fifty-six goes in it then in about four years there is a page in this basin that says this district asked a man what his figures were for, and on that page there will not be one word about the twenty-nine lines at the back of it, and not one word about the mornings a man of about forty-eight has been keeping a number in his head and putting it out loud into about four hundred yards of air, and not one word about the boy, and the page will be true and it will be the whole of it and it will be about four lines long and it will have cost this district more than anything else it has ever done."

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he did not know what any of them was for, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard that as well, and about four of them said *that* and not *as sent*, and about four of them have said since that they had not expected the second part and had not expected it to be true.
