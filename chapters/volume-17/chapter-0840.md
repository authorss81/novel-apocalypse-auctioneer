# Chapter 840: The One Hundred And Twenty-Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Boy Of Seventeen Said A Figure Out Loud And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Said Out Loud In That Yard That He Was Staying And Did Not Ask For A Bed Or A Wage Or An Office And A Man Of About Forty-Eight Took A Bell Off A Nail And Rang It Once And Nobody Said What It Was

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and seventy-nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and thirty-eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and fifty-four days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and sixty-eight days. Seven hundred and twenty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and forty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and forty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and eighteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and twenty-one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and fifty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and ninety-seven marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and eighty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of about forty-one came up that hill and said as sent out loud in that yard for the first time in nine years, and a man of about forty-eight took a bell off a nail and rang it once, and nothing was entered on the back of that book this morning and nothing is going to be, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and fifty-four mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and fifty-three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and four mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and twenty-four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and sixty-fifth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a tenth voice said it from the top of that bank about as long afterwards and nobody turned round.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and seventy-ninth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and seventy-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the sixty-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of about sixty-four said good afternoon and nothing else, because that is not a question, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood thirty-nine days, and a bell went once in that yard at about four in the afternoon and nobody said what it was and nothing about it has been entered anywhere, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

"One hundred and forty-five," said Orren Pike.

He said it at the near end of the first table at about ten to eight and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him, out of step with each other, which they have never done about that figure, and about nine people noticed and none of them said one word.

---

At about a quarter past eleven a man of about forty-one came up the eleven feet.

He came up it in the ordinary way and he got to the top of that bank and he stood about four feet off the near end of the first table and waited until about nine people had stopped what they were doing, and then he said as sent.

That is the first time in nine years he has said it in that yard.

---

He had said it in that room twice now, at about ten to ten on two mornings running, and about four hundred yards of air had carried it out of this yard and no further, and the only other people who had ever heard that man say those two words were about three people in a room nine miles up that hill who were not in this yard this morning.

---

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the number.

He said it into the middle of that yard in the ordinary voice and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and he went and stood about four feet off the boards with his hands behind his back, and about four feet of stone and about eleven feet of bank and about four hundred yards of air carried it exactly as far as they have carried it every morning of this run of mornings.

---

At about half past two Adrian Vale stood up off the rail at about four feet off the near end of the first table and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stopped, because he had done that on the one hundred and ninth morning and had said he was going to ask a question, and on the one hundred and fourteenth morning and had said he was not going up that lane, and about four people at the top of eleven feet had been waiting about nine mornings to find out what he would stand up for.

---

"I am staying," he said.



Nobody said anything.

---

"That is the whole of it," said Adrian. "I am staying in this yard. I have no bed and I have no wage and I have no office and I have not asked for any of the three this morning and I am not going to ask for any of the three this afternoon, and I would like it said out loud that I have not asked for them, because about nine people in this yard have noticed that I have not asked and about four of them have been waiting about nine mornings for me to ask and I am not going to, and I would rather be asked about not asking than be congratulated on something."

---

Mavis Dorr opened her mouth and Adrian Vale held up one hand about four inches off the boards, which is the signal he has used in that yard about nine mornings of this run, and she closed it.

---

"You are not going to be told it is a good thing," said Sabra Holt, "and I am going to say that out loud so that nobody else has to, and it is not a kindness. It is a fact about that yard. There is nothing in that yard that can be given to you this afternoon that you have not already been asked for and refused, and if I say a thing is good then somebody in about four years can say a man of thirty-eight was told his staying was good, and a man of thirty-eight who has never been told anything is the only person in this basin who can still be asked what he wanted."

---

"Thank you," said Adrian Vale.

---

"I have been on a page," said Sabra Holt, "in a hand that is not mine, since the ninety-first morning, and nobody has ever asked me why, and a room nine miles up that hill has spent the last two days asking this district for a name and it did not send for me, and I am not going to be comforted about any part of that, and I have said so four times in about twenty-two mornings and it has got less true every time, and I am saying it a fifth time this afternoon in a yard where a man of thirty-eight has just said he is staying and nobody said that was a good thing, and I would like about four people at the top of eleven feet to notice that I said it anyway."

---

At about three Mara Quill put her hand on the page at the back of the lot book and did not open it.

"Nothing this morning," she said.

---

"You said it was the last one on the one hundred and twenty-second morning at about a quarter past twelve."

"I said on the one hundred and nineteenth morning that it was the last one I was going to write and then I wrote one, and I said on the one hundred and twenty-second morning that it was the last one and then I wrote one," said the clerk, "and I would like that noticed, because it means the last one was on the one hundred and twenty-second morning and there has been nothing on that page since, and this morning is a different thing, and there is no reason for this morning and I am not going to give one."

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other. About nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and they said it too, and a man of about forty-one said it from the top of the bank about as long afterwards and nobody turned round.

---

Then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people heard it for the thirteenth time running.

---

About two minutes afterwards the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally went into his hut and came out with a hand bell in his hand.

It came off that nail at about four this afternoon, and it is not a number, and there is nothing to enter and nobody is going to enter anything.

He walked about four feet off the near end of the first table and stopped and he rang it once.

---

It is not a long sound. It is about as long as a man of fifty-six takes to say one figure off a wall and it is not a figure and there was no number attached to it and nothing was written down and nothing is going to be.

About four hundred yards of air carried it four hundred yards.

---

Nobody said what it was.

Mavis Dorr asked him what it was and he looked at her and said that it was on a nail and it was off the nail now and he was not going to put it back on this afternoon and he was not going to say anything else about it, and Mavis Dorr did not ask him a second time.

---

At the foot of that wall a man of fifty-six heard it.

He heard it the way he hears four figures off a stone twice a morning, which is that he heard it and knew exactly what had made it and did not put his hands on anything. He had been at the foot of that wall every morning of this run of mornings and about four hundred yards of air had carried everything anybody in that yard had ever said, and a hand bell was not one of those things.

He has never been asked what he thought it was and he was not asked this afternoon.

---

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall at about half past three on his four hundred and seventy-ninth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and seventy-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the sixty-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and the man of about sixty-one said nothing else, and about four o'clock a hand bell went on the top of that bank, before the man of about sixty-four had got himself together to go out on nine miles of road, and both of them heard it and neither of them said one word about it, because there was nothing anybody in that yard had said yet to answer.

---

Adrian Vale was still standing at about four feet off the near end of the first table when the light went off the boards.

He has been standing in that yard one hundred and forty-five mornings and he has said out loud in about nineteen people's hearing that he is staying, and nobody in that yard has told him that it is a good thing, and nobody has offered him a bed or a wage or an office, and he has not asked for any of the three and he said in that yard this afternoon that he is not going to.

---

Orren Pike put his hand flat on the boards of the second table and left it there, and he had been up that hill and back and his sheet was in a folder on a table nine miles off with the time written on the corner of it in pencil, and thirteen folders and four hundred yards of air were between that yard and the two men at the foot of the wall, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other, and the clerk did not open the book, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid and the count of it is still five, and four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and nobody in this basin has computed by how much it is out.
