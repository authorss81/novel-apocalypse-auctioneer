# Chapter 832: The One Hundred And Sixteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Woman Came Down That Lane With A Page In Her Hand And Stood At The Bottom Of That Bank Before She Came Up The Eleven Feet And A Boy Of Seventeen Said Twenty-Seven Out Loud At Those Boards Before A Clerk's Hand Had Moved And A Woman Who Keeps A Scale Read A Figure Off That Page Which Was Not Four And Would Not Say What It Was For

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and seventy-one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and thirty. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and forty-six days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and sixty days. Seven hundred and twenty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and thirty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and forty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and ten days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and thirteen days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and forty-nine days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and eighty-nine marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and seventy-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a stranger came down that lane with a page in her hand and the lane count moved for the fourth time in this run of mornings, five mornings after it last moved, and nothing was entered on the back of that book this morning, and a page came down that hill and went on those boards and that is a thing that was done to this yard and not a thing this yard made, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and forty-six mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and forty-five. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for ninety-six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and sixteen, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and it moved this morning. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-one lines on it and none of the thirty-one is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and fifty-seventh of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a woman who keeps a scale read a figure off that page which was not four and would not say what it was for.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and seventy-first night of that run, having slept on four hundred and seventy of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the sixty-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a woman who keeps a scale read a figure off a page at those boards and would not say what it was for, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood thirty-one days, and the count of strangers who have come down that lane is twenty-seven and it moved this morning for the fourth time in this run of mornings, five mornings after it last moved, and the page reached the bottom of that bank before the figure was said at those boards and the figure was said before the clerk's hand moved.

"One hundred and thirty-seven," said Orren Pike.

He said it and then he said a second thing, which was a number, and both of them came out of him before anybody in that yard had stopped what they were doing.

"Twenty-six until about ten minutes ago," he said, "and twenty-seven now, and I have not said it in front of her and I said it the moment the page was on the boards and not before, because that is the order it happens in and I have been saying that figure at these boards since before the wall changed."

---

She had been standing at the bottom of that bank for about four minutes with her hand flat on the page before anybody at the top of eleven feet knew she was there.

That is the whole of what a lane is in this district. A woman comes down it, and she stops at the bottom of the bank the way everybody in this district stops at the bottom of that bank, and about nine people at the top of it are looking at about four hundred yards of air, and the air does not carry a person.

Then she came up the eleven feet, which is the steepest thing between here and anywhere, and she came up it with the page in her hand and did not put it down.

---

Mara Quill's hand was on the page at the back of the lot book, four inches away from it, and had not touched it.

"Nobody has said anything about it," said Mavis Dorr.

"Nobody is going to," said the woman. "I have been told to put it down and go back up the hill and I am going to do both of those things and I am not going to do anything else. I have three sentences and after the third one I am going to pick it up again."

---

"First. It is not about this yard. It is a page with about nine places on it and every one of the nine has a figure beside it and none of the nine has a name on it."

"Second. I am not from the eleventh place on the Salt Verge and I am not from the north road and I have never heard of this yard."

"Third," she said, "the woman who gave it to me said that if anybody in this district wanted to say that a figure beside their own line was wrong, they were to say it out loud in this yard this morning and not to send anybody up the hill, and that is what I came down for and it has not happened."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet said nothing at all for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

"You did not come down for a person," said Mavis Dorr. "You came down for a figure."

"I came down because she said that if it could be said out loud here then it could be said out loud here," said the woman, "and she has been saying a thing like that for about four years and nobody has ever taken her up on it, and I am not the person to take her up on it. I am a person with a page."

---

The woman who keeps a scale came up about four feet of the bank, which she has not done since the morning she carried the beam up in both hands, and she stood about four feet off the page and looked down the side of it.

"There are about nine places," she said. "The third line from the bottom is this yard and it says four. It said four the last time that page came down this hill and it says four now and there is no difference in that line that I can see with my hand at this distance."

Mavis Dorr did not say anything.

"There is a line under it," said the woman who keeps a scale, "and the figure under it is not four. I am not going to say what the figure under it is and I am not going to say what it is for, and about nine people in this yard are going to hear me not say it."

---

"Why not," said Adrian Vale.

The woman who keeps a scale turned round and looked at him, which she had not done in about nine years.

"Because there is one person in this district who could say what a figure is for," she said, "and he said he did not know at about two in the afternoon on the one hundred and twelfth morning and about nineteen people heard him say it, and I read two figures off a page at these boards on the one hundred and eleventh morning and I did not know what either of them was for then and I know now that I did not know, and about four mornings have gone by and nobody has asked me about it, and I am not going to start today by saying that I do not know what a number is for."

She turned the beam a quarter of a round on her knees and did not look up.

"There is a difference between the two of us and he knows what it is and I know what it is," she said, "and the difference is that he said it where anybody could be asked about it separately and I am about to go back down that bank and be forty feet off again."

---

The woman picked the page up at about eleven and squared it against the edge of the boards before she lifted it, and she did not leave it, and she went back down the eleven feet and down the lane without turning round.

Nothing was entered on the back of that book this morning and Mara Quill did not open it.

---

"It was not four under this yard," said the man at the end of the second table, to nobody, about a minute afterwards.

"It was four under this yard," said Mavis Dorr. "You heard the woman with the beam say it. Third line from the bottom, four, and it said four last time as well."

"It was four under this yard," he said. "What is not four is the line under it, and I have looked at that page twice and I am about thirty-seven years old and I have been at this table for fifteen years, and what I want to say out loud this morning is that there was nothing under this yard on that page five mornings ago and there is a figure under it now, and that neither of us has seen anybody put it there, and I do not know what a room writes on a page."

He stopped.

"I am not saying a figure is wrong," he said. "I am saying there is a line under this one and there is not supposed to be a line under this one, and if somebody in that room has put a line under this one then somebody in that room knows something about this yard that nobody in this yard has said out loud, and I am not going to be the one who says what it is."

---

Adrian Vale stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table for a long time with his hands behind him.

There was a number said out loud in that yard this morning and nobody in that yard would repeat it. About nine people had it and about nine people can be asked about it separately tomorrow and none of them can be asked what it means, because the one woman who read it off that page said three times out loud that she was not going to say, and she has never in nine years refused to say a thing in that yard until this morning. And the number the man of about forty-eight puts into the air every morning went into the same air about two hours before she did, and the one person who might have said something about it had been at the foot of a wall eleven feet down since before any of it started and had said he did not know.

And it was about four o'clock, and in about half an hour the man at the foot of that wall was going to say the four out loud again and then say the other thing, and about nine people would hear both, and neither of them would be about the figure under this yard.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and seventy-first night of that run, having slept on four hundred and seventy of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the sixty-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and about four miles of lane between here and the place that page came from carried nothing in either direction.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for.

About nine people heard it for the fifth time running.

And about four people at the top of eleven feet had heard a different figure said out loud in that yard that morning in front of the same nine people, and not one of the nine connected the two of them out loud, and the clerk did not write either of them down.
