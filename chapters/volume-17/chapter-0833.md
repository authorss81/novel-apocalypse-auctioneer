# Chapter 833: The One Hundred And Seventeenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of Sixty-One Came Up The Eleven Feet For The Fifth Time In Nine Years At About Two And Said That He Was Going To Use Once The Thing He Holds And Would Not Say What It Was For And Said That The Ground At The Foot Of That Bank Was Public Lot From This Afternoon And Named A Toll Of Ninety-Eight And Nobody Asked Him What It Was For

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and seventy-two days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and thirty-one. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and forty-seven days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and sixty-one days. Seven hundred and twenty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and thirty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and forty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and eleven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and fourteen days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and fifty days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and ninety marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and seventy-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of sixty-one came up the eleven feet at about two and said that he was going to use the thing he holds once and would not say what it is for, and said that the ground at the foot of that bank is public lot from this afternoon, and nothing was entered on the back of that book this morning, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and forty-seven mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and forty-six. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for ninety-seven mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seventeen, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-one lines on it and none of the thirty-one is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and fifty-eighth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and the man at the foot of that wall was not in that yard at about two and nobody in that yard could say where he was.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and seventy-second night of that run, having slept on four hundred and seventy-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the sixty-second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one came up the eleven feet at about two and gave away a piece of ground and got nothing for it, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood thirty-two days, and a man of sixty-one came up the eleven feet at about two and said that the ground at the foot of that bank is public lot from this afternoon and named a toll of ninety-eight, and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said he would not put it on his number.

A boy of seventeen stood at the near end of the second table from about ten to eight until about two and said no figure at all, which he has not done since the hundredth morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed and none of them said one word about it.

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the number at about one.

He said it into the middle of that yard in the ordinary voice, the way he has said it every morning of this run of mornings, and about nine people heard it, and nobody wrote it down, and he went and stood about four feet off the boards with his hands behind his back and looked at the ground.

---

At about two a man of sixty-one came up the eleven feet.

It is the fifth time in nine years. He came up it without stopping and he went past the near end of the first table without looking at it, which is what he did on the one hundred and fifth morning and is not what anybody in that yard does.

---

"I am going to use it once," he said.

He said it to the boards and he said it in the ordinary voice, before his reasons, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stopped what they were doing, and there were about nineteen people in that yard and every one of them heard the first two words of it.

"I have been offered a way out of this twice and refused it and I have said no out loud in that yard three times, and I am not going to end it and I am not going to say a word about the condition on the seat and anybody who wants to write down that I did not say it can write down that I did not say it. I am going to use the thing I hold once, on a piece of ground, and after this afternoon I am not going to have it and that is the whole of what I am going to say about what it is for."

---

"What ground," said Mavis Dorr.

---

"The ground at the foot of that bank," said the man of sixty-one.

He put one hand out sideways without looking down.

"From the stones to the flat stone at the foot of that wall and about four feet off it. From this afternoon it is public lot. Any of about nineteen people in this district may stand on it at any hour of any day. No rule in this yard may put a person off it. It cannot be taken back by anybody, including me, and I am not going to ask any of the nineteen of you whether you would rather have it, because if I ask you that then it is not lot, it is a favour, and I have been on that ground for about as long as I have held the thing I hold and I am not going to hand it out as a favour in the last month of it."

---

Nobody said anything at all for about as long as a man of fifty-six takes to get four figures off a wall.

"And the toll," said Sabra Holt.

"There is a toll," said the man of sixty-one. "It is one figure. It is taken once, and it is not taken this afternoon. It will be taken in this yard, out of the number the man of about forty-eight says out loud every morning, and there is no other number in this district that anybody nine miles up that hill has ever paid for."

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally turned round.

"That is my number."

"That is your number," said the man of sixty-one.

"Then I will not put it on it."

He said it in the ordinary voice and about nine people heard it and nobody in that yard had ever heard him refuse anything at all.

---

"Will you say the figure," said Mavis Dorr, "so that about nine people have heard you say it."

The man of about forty-eight looked at her for a while.

"Ninety-eight," he said.

He said it into the middle of the yard the way he says the other one, and it went into about four hundred yards of air and about nine people heard it and two of them looked at each other.

"I have been keeping that number since before any of this began," he said, "and I could not tell you which morning I started it on and nobody has ever asked me, and I am not going to put ninety-eight on it this afternoon. My number is a number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank and it is a hundred and seventeen this morning and it is one a morning every morning, and if a man of sixty-one takes ninety-eight out of it in a yard in front of about nineteen people then it stops being a number of mornings and it starts being a thing that was spent, and it has not been spent, and it is the only thing in this district I have ever had that nobody could take."

---

"Then it comes out of nothing," said the man at the end of the second table.

"It comes out of my number or it does not come out of anything," said the man of about forty-eight, "and I have said the figure and I have said where it comes from and I am not going to say one word more about it this afternoon, and you are going to stop mentioning that I said it."

---

The man of sixty-one took his hand off the air.

"I am not taking it this afternoon," he said. "It is taken at about two on the day after tomorrow and it is taken in this yard and about nineteen people watch it and the woman who keeps a scale does the sum out loud, and I am going to say the rest of it in about four minutes and then I am going down the eleven feet and that is all."

He stopped for a moment.

"The condition on the seat is not the toll," he said. "I want that said out loud. I am not going to name the condition and I am not going to say what it is and the toll is not the condition and the toll is the only part of this that anybody in this yard is going to get a figure out of, and I would like that noticed, because a man of sixty-one is the last person anybody in this basin should be trusted with the shape of a thing."

---

Sabra Holt came about four feet in from where she had been standing.

"What are you giving up," she said.

---

He did not answer that for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

"That is not a question you get to ask me twice," he said, "and I am going to answer it once and then I am going down that bank and I am not going to say anything else this afternoon."

He put his hand out sideways again, at the same height.

"The ground the clause sits on," he said. "It has been mine to hold or not hold for seventeen years and I have not put my name on it once in any of the seventeen and I have never met one of the people it is holding. From this afternoon it is the lot of about nineteen people at the foot of a bank and not the seat of a man, and I get nothing, and I have said so out loud in this yard before somebody gave me a figure for it and I am saying it again with a figure in the middle of it, and I would like the nineteen of you to notice that I have just given away the last thing I stood on and got nothing for it, and that the count of the five things this district does not have is still five, and that a man giving a thing away is not one of those five being paid, and I am not going to stand in this yard and let anybody say that it is."

---

Nobody wrote one word of that down and nobody asked him a second question.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and seventy-second night of that run, having slept on four hundred and seventy-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the sixty-second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and neither of them said anything else.

The man of fifty-six was not in that yard at about two.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet noticed that he was not in that yard at about two and about four of them looked at each other about it, and nobody said out loud where he had been, and he was on the flat stone at the foot of that wall again at about a quarter to eight the next morning and got all four of them.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and then he said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for.

About nine people heard it for the sixth time running.

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet had heard a man of sixty-one say *lot* out loud at about two that afternoon, and not one of the nine could say afterwards whether the man of fifty-six had been in that yard when he said it.
