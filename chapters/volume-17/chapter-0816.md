# Chapter 816: The One Hundredth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Forty-One Put A Form On The Boards Of The Second Table At About Eleven With The Space For A Name Empty And A Second Space Under It With A Heading That Says Whoever And A Clerk Of Thirty-Four Who Had Not Written Anything For Two Mornings Looked At The Two Spaces For About As Long As It Takes A Man Of Fifty-Six To Say Four Figures Off A Wall And Wrote Nothing In Either And Said Out Loud That There Would Be No Page About This Morning And It Would Be The First In Eleven And It Would Be Hers

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and fifty-five days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and fourteen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and thirty days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and forty-four days. Seven hundred and five days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and nineteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and twenty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and ninety-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and ninety-seven days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and thirty-three days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and seventy-three marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and fifty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a form with two spaces on it went into a room nine miles up that hill at about one this afternoon with both of them empty, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and thirty mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and twenty-nine. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for eighty mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered, and nobody in that yard marked it out loud and about four people have noticed that nobody marked it out loud. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-one lines on it and none of the twenty-one is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and forty-first of those mornings, and on the hundredth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about nine of them were also thinking about two empty spaces on a sheet of paper, and about four of them were not.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and fifty-fifth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and fifty-four of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the forty-fifth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and both of them said it, and about four feet of stone and a bank did not carry either of them.

The record has stood fifteen days, and a form went into a room nine miles up that hill this afternoon with nothing in it and not one word under it, and there is no page anywhere in this basin about the hundredth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month except the back of a sheet a boy of seventeen has kept for four years.

---

He put it on the boards at about eleven and squared it against the edge the way everybody in that yard squares everything, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at it and about four of them had never seen a piece of paper that was not one of theirs.

"It came to me at the eleventh place," he said. "It came to me with a woman standing next to the man who asked me to be the person, and nobody said anything about it, and I have had it for three days and I have not brought it up, and I am bringing it up at eleven on the morning it goes in and not before, because if I bring it up early it looks like an answer and it is not an answer, it is a piece of paper with two spaces on it."

---

It was about the length of two hands and it had a heading that was about a form and not about a matter, and it had two spaces on it and one line of writing under each space.

The first space was for the name of a person.

The second space was under a line and the line said *whoever enters this*.

---

Nobody at the top of eleven feet said anything for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said a word about eleven, and it was *whoever*, and nobody finished it.

---

"That is our heading," said Mavis Dorr.

---

"That is our heading," said the man at the end of the second table, "and it is not our hand and it is not our ruling and it is not nine years old, and I have been at that table for fifteen years and about four people have seen that heading on a piece of paper about four thousand times."

"And I am going to say the part I would like somebody else to say, because I have been about two days not saying it and I am tired of it."

"Somebody nine miles up that hill knows the heading over a column in this district. Not what it says. Knows it. And the only two people on that hill who could have put it there are a man of about forty-one, who has been at the end of this lane for nine years and has never once been in a room nine miles up that hill that anybody in this yard knows of, and a woman of fifty-two who has been an appointed administrator of nineteen years and has carried a key up and down that hill for about eleven days."

---

"I did not put it there," said Sabra Holt.

---

"I did not say you did," said the man at the end of the second table. "I said you were one of two people it could have come through and I have said it out loud in about nineteen people's hearing, and I would like about nine people to hold on to that sentence, because in about four years somebody is going to work out which of the two of us it was and about four people are going to remember that this morning I said it out loud in the sun in front of everybody before anybody worked it out."

---

The clerk had her hand flat on the cover of the lot book and she was not looking at it. She was looking at the form.

She looked at the two spaces for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it and did not say anything, and then she took her hand off the lot book and did not open it and did not take a pencil out.

"I am not writing in it," she said.

---

"Read it to me," said the man at about twenty-nine who drives the cart, from the far end, "and I will put a mark beside it and I am not anybody either and that would be a joke and I would rather have a joke in that room than nothing."

---

"It is not a joke," said the clerk, "and I am going to say why, and then I am going to stop, and then somebody is going to hand me a pencil in about four minutes and I am going to have to do something with it, which is a thing I said out loud in this yard on the eighty-eighth morning and I am saying again because it has not got better."

"The second space is under a heading that says whoever enters this. That is not this district's wording. That is four words long and a heading, and it is asking for a person, and it is not asking for the person who would be answerable for the number, it is asking for the person who would write the name down."

"And about six days ago a man of fifty-six was asked to write four figures into a column in this yard and said no out loud and gave a reason out loud in full, and I entered the asking and the answer and the reason, and the reason was that a column is for somebody being asked and he had not been asked once in five hundred and thirty-four mornings."

"And if I put my name in that space this afternoon then in about four years there is a page somewhere that says a district in this basin produced a person for the second space on a form, and the person is a woman who enters things, and anybody who holds that page is going to take it that this district put a name on a form, and this district will not have."

---

"Then enter that you did not write in it," said Mara Quill, from about four feet off the near end, in the ordinary voice, and about four people at the top of eleven feet turned round.

---

"There is nowhere to enter it," said the clerk. "That is the part I have had for about two days and I have not said it out loud until now and I am saying it now because a woman asked me a question and I would rather answer it than not."

"There are twenty-one lines on the page at the back of that book and every one of them is a thing that happened in this yard. The hundredth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month happened in this yard. There was a form on the boards and two empty spaces and a man of thirty-four said no to writing in it out loud in front of everybody, and yesterday morning a man of sixty-one was asked a question in this yard and did not answer it."

"And if I put one line on that page about this morning then the room nine miles up that hill gets a form with nothing in it and this yard gets a page with one line on it, and the page says that this district had a morning. And in about four years somebody is going to hold both of them and conclude that this district declined."

"This district did not decline. This district was not asked. I have said that out loud in this yard about four times and about four people have agreed with me and about nine people have looked at the boards."

"So it is going to be blank. The form goes down that lane with nothing in it and the page stays at twenty-one lines and there will be no page anywhere in this basin about the hundredth morning except the back of a sheet that a boy of seventeen has kept for four years, and I have decided that if that is what has to happen then I will be the one who lets it happen and I will not be the one who made it nicer."

---

Nobody said anything for a while and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at the boards and about four of them looked at the man of about forty-one, who had not said anything at all since eleven o'clock and who had a form on the boards in front of him with his own name the obvious answer to the first space.

---

"You did not ask me," he said.

---

"Ask you what," said the man at the end of the second table.

---

"Whether I was going to put my name in it," said the turner. "That is the question this yard has spent four days not asking and you have all been extremely good at it and I have watched every one of you not ask me and I have watched about four people work very hard at not asking me, and this morning a piece of paper came down that lane with my name written on it already in a room nine miles off, in a hand that is not mine, and not one of you has said one word to me about it."

---

The yard was extremely quiet.

---

"Whatever is on that page," said Adrian Vale, from about four feet off the near end of the first table, "was written before any of us knew this district had a form. Nobody in this yard put your name there and about nine of us could prove that we were not in the room."

"And it is worse than if we had. If we had said nothing and you had said yes, then there is a name and a reason and the two of them go together and somebody can be asked. And if we had said no and you had said no, then there are two no's and somebody can be asked about both of them separately."

"And right now there is a name in a room that none of us put there and there is nobody here who has said anything at all, and that is the only version of this morning where there is no person in it to be asked."

---

"Ask me," said the turner.

---

"Not this morning," said Adrian. "You have got the rest of this morning and about four miles and I am not going to spend the time we have left asking you a question at these boards when you have said out loud twice that a question asked here is a different animal from the same question in a corridor. Go and be asked in the room and then come up and tell us."

---

"I have been asked in the room," said the turner.

---

"Then do not go and be asked again," said Adrian, "and come up this bank and tell us whatever the answer turns out to be."

---

Mara Quill closed the lot book and put her hand flat on it and said that she had been entered and she had said it out loud and that the form was going down that lane at about one with nothing in it and that she had nothing to add to it and that she would like everybody to go back to what they were doing.

---

The turner put the form in the inside of his coat at about ten past one and went down the eleven feet with it and the lane count is twenty-five and did not move, because a person went up that hill with a page in his hand and not down it, and at about half past two the man at the end of the second table watched him go up the lane and said one thing to nobody.

---

"There is going to be no page," he said, "and there is going to be a page, and there are going to be two versions of this morning and one of them is going to be in a boy's hand, and on the seventy-seventh morning a woman with a key ruled in this yard that a thing was not to happen twice, and about twenty-three mornings later it has happened twice, and I have not worked out whether the rule is smaller than the morning or the morning is smaller than the rule."

---

Orren Pike wrote the hundredth morning in the box on the back of his own sheet, and it is the first morning he has written in that box with a number on it, and he wrote *100* under the words, and then he ruled a space under that and did not leave it empty.

He wrote: *a form with two spaces. both empty. a woman said no out loud to writing in it and said why. nobody in the yard asked a man of forty-one anything. and there is nobody else in the basin who can be asked. that is the whole of it and I am seventeen and this is the only place in this basin it is written down and if this sheet is lost then the hundredth morning never happened and there are about nineteen people who will say it did.*

---

He read that out loud to the boards because he had to, because he said it out loud or it was not said out loud, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen read out the end of a district's ability to say no and nobody in that yard answered him.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and the boards of the second table were bare where the wire had been and bare where the stone had been and a form had gone down that lane at about ten past one with two empty spaces on it, and four hundred and eleven has been on the sheet at that gatepost every morning of this run of mornings and its size of error has not been computed, and the fifth of the five is not paid, and the column has one name in it and the heading over it still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-one lines on it and none of the twenty-one is in the lot book, and there is no more in that yard this afternoon than there was yesterday afternoon, except a boy with a sheet.
