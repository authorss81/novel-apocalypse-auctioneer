# Chapter 821: The One Hundred And Fifth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Boy Of Seventeen Said A Figure Out Loud And A Man Of Sixty-One Came Up The Eleven Feet For The Second Time In Nine Years And Said Five Things That Nobody In That Yard Can Check Any Part Of

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and sixty days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and nineteen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and thirty-five days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and forty-nine days. Seven hundred and ten days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and twenty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and twenty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and ninety-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and two days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and thirty-eight days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and seventy-eight marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and sixty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of sixty-one came up eleven feet at about ten this morning for the second time in nine years and said a thing out loud that about nine people heard and not one of them can check any part of it, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and thirty-five mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and thirty-four. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for eighty-five mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and five, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-four lines on it and none of the twenty-four is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and forty-sixth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about four of them have said since that a man who has said no out loud twice in about ten days and has been right once about something else is now the only person in this basin anybody could be asked about, and not one of those four wants to ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and sixtieth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and fifty-nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the fiftieth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and about four feet of stone and a bank did not carry a voice that had gone up the eleven feet about five and a half hours before, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood twenty days, and a man of about forty-one went down that lane at about ten to four yesterday afternoon to begin being the only mouth for a number in this basin and has not come up it yet, and the lane count is twenty-five and did not move and does not move for a person and is not a page.

---

"One hundred and twenty-six," said Orren Pike.

He said it to the boards, the way he said everything, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had been standing there since about ten to eight.

"That is the number of mornings a man of thirty-eight has stood in this yard," he said. "It was one hundred and sixteen on the ninety-fifth morning and it has gone up one every morning since and it is one hundred and twenty-six this morning, and he is standing at about four feet off the near end of the first table while I say it, and I have not had to go and look."

Adrian Vale did not move and did not thank him.

---

The man of about forty-one had gone down that lane at about ten to four the previous afternoon to be the only mouth for a number in this basin, and had not come up it, and nobody in that yard knew what that meant yet, and about four people had stopped saying his name out loud because a name said out loud three times a morning turns into a thing people are waiting for.

"He has been gone one morning," said Mavis Dorr. "That is all that is true."

"That is all that is true," said Adrian.

---

At about ten a man came up the eleven feet from the bottom of that bank carrying nothing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet turned round, because a man of sixty-one had come up those steps once in nine years and that was on the ninety-ninth morning.

He was not carrying anything. He put both hands flat on the boards of the second table and squared them against the edge the way everybody in that yard squared everything, and he stayed there.

"I am going to say five things," he said. "I am not going to say good morning and I am not going to ask anybody anything. I have come up eleven feet to say five things and then I am going to go back down."

---

"The first one. Before the middle of the next month a stranger will come down that hill with a page in her hand, and the page will have a figure on it for about nine places, and one of the nine will be a place four miles past the ninth mile, and the figure beside that one will have gone up since I last said anything about it."

Somebody said something at the far end and it was not loud.

"The second one. This yard will be on that page, and it will be a small figure, and it will be the smallest figure on the page that anybody in this basin would call wrong."

---

"You have not been to the eleventh place on the Salt Verge," said Mavis Dorr.

"No," said the man of sixty-one.

"You have never met one of the people in it."

"No," he said. "I have never been four miles past the ninth mile in my life. I look at a list of places in a room and it has about nine of them on it on a good morning and I have looked at it for seventeen years."

---

"The third one," he said. "When that page is on those boards the man of fifty-six will still be on no page."

He put one finger on the boards and left it there.

"The fourth one. The figure that decides what this basin is worth will not be on that sheet. It will be on it in about four years, and it will not be his, and nobody in this yard will have chosen it."

Adrian Vale had not moved from about four feet off the near end of the first table and he found that his hands had gone cold and that he did not know what to do with the rest of his morning.

---

"The fifth one," said the man of sixty-one, "is that I am telling you now, five and a half hours before I am on those stones again, so that when it happens nobody in this yard can stand at these boards and say a man of sixty-one told us."

He took his hands off the boards and went back down the eleven feet without saying anything else, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stood there for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

---

"That is not a prediction," said Mavis Dorr. "That is a man who has been sitting in a room for seventeen years saying what he would like."

"It is both," said the man at the end of the second table, from about four feet off the near end, "and I have been in a room four times in nine years and I can tell you that a man who says what he would like in a room for seventeen years is not a man who is only saying what he would like."

Nobody answered him, and about nine people looked at the boards, and he had said on the hundredth morning that about nine people would look at the boards.

---

Adrian said the thing he had been standing there for.

"You can name the morning," he said. "You cannot name the figure."

"No," said the man of sixty-one, from the bottom of the bank, where he had stopped with one hand on the turf.

"You have said in this yard that you have never been wrong about anything that could be checked, and I have believed you since the ninety-ninth morning. And you have just come up eleven feet to tell me you can see a page arriving and you cannot see what is on it."

"I can see a page arriving," said the man of sixty-one. "I have been able to see that for about four days. I do not know how and I am not going to stand in a yard and pretend I do."

---

He went down the lane and the light went off the boards of the second table at about half past four, and Adrian Vale was still standing four feet off the near end of the first table with a whole morning gone and nothing put down anywhere.

That was the part that had gone wrong. Twenty mornings of putting figures in front of the person who stood nearest to them, and a page with nothing ruled on it with four figures on it in a clerk's hand, and a man of thirty-eight who had walked about nine days with nine words in his head that he was not allowed to say at these boards, and about four people in that yard who had told him it was the best work anybody had done in ninety mornings.

And at about ten o'clock on the one hundred and fifth morning a man of sixty-one had come up eleven feet and said that the thing that decides what this basin is worth is not going to be on the sheet, and had not said what it was, and had been right about everything anybody could check.

---

Twenty-five," said Mara Quill. "And twenty-five is the first line on that page this morning and I am going to say what is on it before I put it there, because that is what I do, and because I have not done anything else for twelve mornings that anybody could check.

She entered one line on the page at the back of the lot book and it is the twenty-fifth line on that page and none of the twenty-five is in the lot book, and it said that a man of sixty-one had come up eleven feet at about ten in the morning and had said five things out loud in about nineteen people's hearing, and that she was entering the fact that he came up and said five things and not what any of the five were, because a clerk enters what happened in a yard and not what a man of sixty-one thinks is going to happen in about four weeks.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine of those nine had heard a man of sixty-one say five things that nobody could check and about four of them had been standing at those boards since about ten to eight waiting to see whether anybody would ask him about one of them, and nobody did, and a man of thirty-eight stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table and wrote nothing down, and the boards of the second table were bare where the wire had been and bare where the stone had been, and four hundred and eleven has been on the sheet at that gatepost every morning of this run of mornings and its size of error has not been computed, and the fifth of the five is not paid, and the column has one name in it and the heading over it still says whoever.
