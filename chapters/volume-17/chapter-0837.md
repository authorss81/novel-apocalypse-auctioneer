# Chapter 837: The One Hundred And Twenty-First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Forty-One Came Up That Hill At About Half Past Three And Said Out Loud In That Yard That A Room Nine Miles Off Has Asked For The Name Of The Person Who Can Be Asked About A Figure And Would Not Put It To Anybody Himself And A Woman Told The Man At The Foot Of That Wall At About Ten Past Four In Front Of About Nineteen People

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and seventy-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and thirty-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and fifty-one days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and sixty-five days. Seven hundred and twenty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and forty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and forty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and fifteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and eighteen days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and fifty-four days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and ninety-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and eighty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of about forty-one carried a question up that hill in his coat and would not put it to anybody in that yard, and nothing was entered on the back of that book this morning, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and fifty-one mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and fifty. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and twenty-one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-two lines on it and none of the thirty-two is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and sixty-second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about four of those nine had been in that yard at about half past three this afternoon when a man of about forty-one said out loud what a room nine miles off has asked for.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and seventy-sixth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and seventy-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the sixty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of about forty-one came up that hill at about half past three and said out loud in that yard that a room nine miles off has asked for the name of the person who can be asked about a figure, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood thirty-six days, and a room nine miles up that hill has asked for a name, and about nineteen people in that yard know it, and the man it is about was told at about ten past four in front of about nineteen people, and nothing was entered on the back of that book this morning.

A boy of seventeen stood at the near end of the second table with his sheet against his chest and said no figure at all and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him for one, and one of the nine, privately, to the man who keeps a tally, said that she had been expecting it every morning for about four mornings and had stopped expecting it and that this was the first morning she had been wrong about what she was expecting.

---

At about half past three the turner came up the eleven feet and went straight to the boards and put nothing down.

"I have not brought anything," he said, before anybody asked. "I went down that lane at about six this morning and a room asked me a question and I brought it up in my head and I am not going to put it in front of these boards, and I want that said first because about four people at the top of eleven feet have been looking at these boards every morning this week and there is nothing on them from me and there is not going to be."

---

"Say what they asked," said Mavis Dorr.



"They have asked for the name of the person who can be asked about a figure."

He said it in the ordinary voice and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stopped.

---

"Will you put it to him," said Adrian Vale.

"No."

"Why not."

---

"Because I was asked to carry a question up a hill and not to put it to a man," said the turner. "Those are two different objects and if I do both then in about four years somebody in that room can say that a man of about forty-one put a question to a person in this district in a yard of about nineteen people, and that is not what happened and I am not going to be the instrument of it happening."

He kept his hands behind his back.

"And because of the second space," he said. "It is still empty and it is going to stay empty until somebody in this yard asks a question, and a question was asked on the one hundred and twelfth morning and the space is still empty, and I am not going to be the man who says that the reason it is empty is that a room wanted a name in it."

---

The yard was extremely quiet for about four seconds.

---

"So they will get it from here," said Sabra Holt.

"They will get it from here," said the turner. "The woman who asked me said that the district knows its own person and that she has nine years of a district not producing one and one morning of a district producing one, and that she would rather have it said out loud in the yard that knows him than carried up a hill by a man with a coat on."

---

The man at the end of the second table said something to the boards that nobody heard, and then said it again.



"Say it louder," said Mavis Dorr.

---

"I have been telling this yard for about four years that it does not work that way," he said. "I have said no three times to being a person and nothing once, and the nothing is in a room nine miles up that hill with my name nowhere on it, and in about four years a person can walk into that room and say *ask him, he was in the room*. And I have been wrong about the shape of it for about four years and I have found out this morning that a room does not need me at all, because a room does not ask the person it wants. It asks the yard. It has always asked the yard."

He stopped.

"I am not going to ask anybody anything," he said. "I decided on the one hundred and fourteenth morning that I was not going to ask to go up that lane again and I have not asked, and this morning is not going to make me, and if a room finds out what I said at those boards by asking somebody else about it then it can have that for free and I will not have asked."

---

Nothing was entered on the back of that book this morning.

Mara Quill had it open in front of her with her hand on the corner of it and about nine people watched her not write anything down, and about four of them would have given her a line if she had asked them for one.

---

At about ten past four Mavis Dorr went down the eleven feet.

She went down it badly, the way everybody goes down it, and she came the rest of the way on the stones and stood about four feet off a man who was on the flat stone at the foot of that wall with his hands loose at his sides.

---

"They have asked for your name," she said.

She said it in the ordinary voice, in about nine people's hearing, at about ten past four, and she did not soften it and she did not put anything in front of it.

---

The man of fifty-six did not move for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.



"Who asked," he said.



"A room. Nine miles up that hill. About a quarter past eleven this morning."

---

"Have they got it."

"No," said Mavis Dorr. "They have got a man of about forty-one telling them there is one, and they have asked this yard for it, and nobody in this yard has given them anything, and I have come down here to tell you a thing about yourself in front of about nineteen people because that is the only way a thing about yourself gets to be a thing anybody else can be asked about separately tomorrow."

---

He put one hand flat on the stone.

"Then I am not going to give it to them," he said, and about eleven feet of bank carried it up and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard a man who has been asked one question in the whole of this run of mornings refuse a second one out loud and in the ordinary voice.

---

"You do not have to say why," said Mavis Dorr.

"I am going to say why and it is one sentence," he said. "I asked to be asked on the one hundred and twelfth morning and you put down that I was asked and about nine people heard the asking and that is the only reason anybody in this basin can prove what my four figures were. A name is a thing that goes on a page and I have never been on one and I am not going to start at about ten past four in the afternoon because a room nine miles up that hill has run out of ways of asking."

---

Then he said the four, out loud, all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and after them the second thing, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people heard it for the tenth time running, and about four of them heard a refusal in front of the same nine people about two minutes before the same refusal of a different kind.

---

Adrian Vale stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table and understood about a quarter of what had happened in that yard and all of what had happened to the man at the foot of that wall, and the quarter he did not understand was whether a man who refuses a name in a yard is safer or less safe than a man who gives one, and he had no way of asking, and he has been carrying nine words he is not allowed to say at those boards since the one hundred and second morning and he did not say them.

---

Sabra Holt was standing about four feet in from where she had been standing and she had not said one word.

"I have been on a page myself," she said. "On the ninety-first morning. In a hand that is not mine. And nobody has ever asked me what any of it was for, and I have held the third of the nine oldest keys for nineteen years and used it once, and there is a woman nine miles off from this yard who has just been told that this district has a person in it who can be asked about a figure, and she did not send for me."

Nobody answered her and she did not appear to want an answer.

"That is all I have," she said. "I am not going to be comforted about it and I have said that twice in about nineteen mornings and it has got less true, and I have said it in two yards about four miles apart and it has got less true in both."
