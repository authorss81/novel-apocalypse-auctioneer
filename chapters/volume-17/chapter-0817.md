# Chapter 817: The One Hundred And First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Forty-One Came Up The Eleven Feet At About Eleven And Said Out Loud In A Yard In About Nineteen People's Hearing That He Was Going To Say Yes To Being The Person A Number Is Checked Against And That He Was Saying It Here First And A Woman Who Keeps A Scale Said From About Forty Feet Off What He Had Just Done And A Clerk Wrote A Line On The Page At The Back Of The Lot Book And Did Not Read It Out And About Nine People Noticed

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and fifty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and fifteen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and thirty-one days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and forty-five days. Seven hundred and six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and twenty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and twenty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and ninety-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and ninety-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and thirty-four days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and seventy-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and sixty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of about forty-one said yes to a job in a yard in about nineteen people's hearing this morning and a man is not a payment and the count did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and thirty-one mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and thirty. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for eighty-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-one lines on it and none of the twenty-one is in the lot book, and there are three lines on that page that have been entered by anybody in this district about a thing that happened nine miles up that hill.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and forty-second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have heard him and about nine of them heard him say four figures out loud at about eleven on the eighty-seventh morning before any of this had started, and about four of those nine have worked out what the man of about forty-one has just done by standing on a beam and thinking about weights.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and fifty-sixth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and fifty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the forty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of about forty-three who keeps every road in this basin came down the lane at about half past four with a book in his coat and did not stop and went back down it.

The record has stood sixteen days, and a woman said no to writing her name on a form at about eleven yesterday morning and a man of about forty-one said yes to a room at about eleven this morning, and the two of those happened about four feet apart and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have not worked out which of them cost more.

---

He came up at about eleven with the bag over his shoulder and did not put the bag down, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had been standing at those boards since about ten to eight because word travels about four hundred yards in this district faster than it travels up a hill.

"I am going to say it here first," he said, "because a man of thirty-eight stood at those boards yesterday afternoon and told me not to go and be asked again and to come up here and tell you whatever the answer turned out to be, and I did not do that on purpose, I did it because it was the first sensible thing anybody has said to me in about four days."

"I am going to say yes. To the first way. One mouth for the number, from a morning that gets named, and nobody else in this basin counts anything."

"And I want about nine people to hear the reason before the yes, because I have been carrying the reason for about four days and the reason is not the job. The reason is that on the ninety-second morning I found out I had been right about four figures for six years and that it did not help me, and about four people in this yard have watched me not be helped by it, and a woman with a beam has watched that figure move three times and stand still once in twenty mornings and has told me in about four sentences what each of those was, and not one of those four sentences was about me."

"And one mouth for the number means that on the next morning after they choose it, whatever I read off those boards will be the only number in this basin. And I will be a person that other people can check, because there will be nothing left in this district to check me against, and that is not a job. I have been at the end of this lane for nine years and I have been reading a wall in it for six of them, with nobody else in the lane, and I am extremely good at it, and I have never once been asked."

---

He said the yes out loud at about a quarter past eleven and it took him about two seconds and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

---

"You have made yourself the person a number is checked against," said the woman who keeps a scale, from about forty feet off, without coming up the bank, "and I am going to say the next part out loud because I am the only person on that step who can tell you the two halves of it go together and nobody at those boards can."

"I have watched that figure for nine years. It has moved three times since the seventy-sixth morning and it stood still once, on the ninety-seventh, and that is twenty-one mornings, and a boy of seventeen said why out loud in front of about nineteen people and he said if it moves every time a page reaches those boards then in about four years it is a count of how many times this yard was handed something, and I said then that he was the first person in nine years who had refused to say a number, and about four people looked at me and about nine did not."

"The man of about forty-one has just given that figure a person."

"From this morning the wall has a reader in it and the reader is not the man of fifty-six, and the reader has agreed to be the only one, and about four years from now the only way anybody in this basin will be able to tell whether a figure is right is by asking that man, and he will have been asked."

"That is what I weigh things for. Not for the weight. For the shape. And I have been watching the shape of this for nine years and this is the first morning I have watched a person in this district put himself under something."

---

The man at the end of the second table did not say anything for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

"Then there will be two people," he said.

---

"There will be two people," said the turner.

---

"And one of them is a man of fifty-six who reads four figures off a wall twice a morning and has never been asked for one, and the other is a man of about forty-one who has agreed in a yard to be the only mouth for the number in this basin, and I am not going to ask you to give up the wall and I am not going to ask you to give up anything, because there is nothing on the wall that is yours to give up and you have known that for nine years and so have I."

"And I would like the yard to notice that this district has just acquired the thing it has been missing since I made a rule about a thing not happening twice, and that it acquired it because a man of about forty-one walked four miles and came up a bank and said the reason out loud first, and that nobody in this yard made him do any of that."

---

Adrian said, from about four feet off the near end of the first table, "He is right about you and he is wrong about what it costs."

"You are going to be asked every morning and every morning you are going to be able to give an answer, and about four years from now a person who has never met you is going to hold a page that says the number and nothing else, and there is going to be no wall to put beside it, because by then you will have said the word in about nine mornings' time that nobody in this basin will remember saying out loud, and the wall will still be there with four figures on it that you and a man of fifty-six have got different readings of, and there will be no way to do that about it twice."

---

"I know," said the turner.

---

"You do not know," said Adrian, "because you have known for about four days and it is still the most interesting morning of your life and in about four years it is going to be a job and you are going to be about forty-five and there will be about nine people in this basin who can check you and about nine of them will be too frightened to say so."

---

"Do not tell a man of forty-one what he does not know on the morning he has said yes," said Mavis Dorr, sharply, and then she said, "I am sorry. That was four words too many from me and I have had four mornings of carrying a question and I did not need to use it on him. Do not listen to me this morning."

---

"I am listening," said the turner. "About four people are not and I have been at the end of this lane for nine years and I know exactly what it looks like when about four people are not."

---

The clerk wrote a line on the page at the back of the lot book and did not read it out.

She wrote it in her own hand, at about a quarter to twelve, in about the ordinary time, and about four people at the top of eleven feet saw her do it and about nine did not and about nine heard the silence where a reading should have been and looked over.

---

"She did not read it out," said Orren Pike.

---

"No," said the clerk.

---

"You have read every one of them out before you wrote it. All of them. Twenty-one of them."

---

"Yes."

---

"Why," said the boy.

---

And the clerk stopped with her hand on the page and looked at him for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stood still and did not make a sound.

---

"You are going to ask me why out loud in that yard," she said, "because you are seventeen and you have asked me why out loud four times in three mornings and every one of the four times I have answered you."

"I am not going to answer this one, and I am going to tell you that I am not answering it in a book either, and I am going to tell you exactly what I am going to do with it and I am not going to tell you why."

"I am going to leave that line where it is and I am not going to read it out and I am not going to take it back, and I am going to have to write about nine more of them before this is over, and I would like about nine people to notice that in about four days there is going to be a page in this district with about twenty-eight lines on it and about four of those lines are going to be ones that nobody heard me read, and there is no way on this earth for anybody to work out which four."

---

The turner went back down the eleven feet at about half past twelve and the lane count is twenty-five and did not move, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at the boards and about nine of them did not, and one hundred and twenty-two was written in the box on the back of a sheet four years old and was not read out, and the boards of the second table were bare where the wire had been and bare where the stone had been.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and a man of about forty-one said one thing at about two o'clock in the afternoon from about four feet off the boards and did not raise his voice and nine people heard it and about four of them have said since that it was the first thing he had said since he came up the bank that was not about the job, and it was that a man who has been right about four figures for six years has never once been told what the four figures on that wall are for, that he has been at the end of that lane every morning for nine years and cannot get it out of anybody including the man who reads them, and that he is going to go and ask him in the room, and about four people in that yard heard him say it and about nine did not say one word to him about it afterwards, and four hundred and eleven has been on the sheet at that gatepost every morning of this run of mornings and its size of error has not been computed, and the fifth of the five is not paid, and the column has one name in it and the heading over it still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-two lines on it and none of the twenty-two is in the lot book.
