# Chapter 822: The One Hundred And Sixth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Forty-One Came Up The Eleven Feet At About Eleven With A Sheet He Was Given In A Room Nine Miles Off And Read The Heading Over The Second Column Of It Out Loud At The Boards And The Figure Beside This District On It Is Four

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and sixty-one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and twenty. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and thirty-six days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and fifty days. Seven hundred and eleven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and twenty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and thirty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and thirty-nine days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and seventy-nine marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and sixty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of about forty-one came up the eleven feet at about eleven with a sheet he was given in a room nine miles off and put it flat on the boards and about nine people looked at a heading instead of at a figure, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and thirty-six mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and thirty-five. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for eighty-six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has twenty-five lines on it and none of the twenty-five is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and forty-seventh of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them had spent the whole morning not thinking about him.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and sixty-first night of that run, having slept on four hundred and sixty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the fifty-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and neither of them has said anything else on any afternoon of this run of mornings, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood twenty-one days, and a sheet came up that lane this morning in the inside of a man's coat, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet can say what is written over the second column of it and about four of them know what that is going to cost this yard.

---

He came up at about eleven with the sheet in the inside of his coat and he did not fold it again when it was out, and he put it flat on the boards of the second table and put both hands flat on it about four inches from the edge.

"I am not going to read you the figures," he said. "There are about nine of them and I have been in the room where they came from for about two days and I do not think one of those numbers is mine to say out loud in this yard at these boards, and if I say one of them out loud then this yard has said it, and that is a different thing from my having read it."

"You have read a lot of figures off a wall in your life," said Mavis Dorr.

"I have read them off a wall at the end of a lane with nobody in it," he said, "and that is not the same as saying one at these boards, and I have been careful about the difference for nine years and I would like to keep being careful about it for about four more."

---

"Then what are you going to read out," said the man at the end of the second table.

"The heading," said the turner.

He put his finger above the second column and left it there, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stood and looked at it, and nobody read it before he did.

"It says this." He read it the way he read figures off a copy of a wall, one part at a time, and he got all of it. "Mornings on which a number was said out loud in a place. And somebody in that place heard it. And can be asked about it separately."

He stopped for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

"That is the whole of it. That is the heading and there is nothing under it and nothing above it."

---

"There are no names on it," said the woman who keeps a scale, from about forty feet off.

She had not come up the bank. She was on her own step and her voice carried the way a voice carries when everything else has stopped.

"There are nine places down the side of that thing and a figure beside every one of them and not one name anywhere on it, and I have been weighing things at the foot of that bank for nine years and I have never once seen a place write down where anything came from."

"That is what I noticed too," said the turner. "I have been in a room where everything has a column with a heading over it and the heading says whoever and it has been the same heading my whole life. And that page has got no column on it at all for a person. It wants a place and it wants a number and it does not want to know anybody's name."

---

Adrian Vale read the figures down the side of that sheet and they were not about a wall.

He read them again and the smallest figure on the page was the one in the second line from the bottom, and it said four, and beside it there was nothing that meant anything he could put a name to, only a mark that had been made by whoever ruled the sheet to say that it was a place in this basin.

"Four," he said.

He said it out loud and about nine people turned, and he had not meant to say it out loud.

---

He went back through nineteen mornings and put his finger on the boards as he went and he did not hurry.

"Nineteen mornings ago," he said, "a man of fifty-six said four figures out loud at about eleven and a clerk wrote them on a page, and about nine people heard him and one of them wrote them down. That is one."

"Five mornings ago," he said, "a man of about forty-one stood at these boards and said yes out loud in about nineteen people's hearing and nine of them heard the yes. That is two."

"Three mornings ago a man at the end of this table stood up and said out loud that he had been in a room nine miles up that hill and had said nothing at all in it, and about nine people heard both halves of that. That is three."

"Two mornings ago I stood four feet off the near end of that table and said four things out loud and about nine people heard all four of them. That is four."

---

He put his hand flat on the sheet and looked at the four for a while.

"That is all of them," he said. "That is the whole of what this yard has done in nineteen mornings that a room nine miles up that hill is willing to put a number beside, and I have been in that yard for one hundred and twenty-seven mornings and I have got four."

---

"A man of fifty-six has said four figures off that wall twice a morning for five hundred and forty-seven mornings," said the turner.

He said it without raising his voice and he was looking at the sheet and not at the man of fifty-six, who was eleven feet below and about four hundred yards back from the top of the bank and could not hear a word of it.

"He has said them at about a quarter to eight and about four. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have heard him, and not one of them has ever asked him what any of the four was for. And the third part of that heading is *and can be asked about it separately*, and there is no separate. There is a man and there is a wall and there is nine people who can all remember him saying it and not one of them who could stand in front of anybody and say that anybody had asked him what it meant."

---

"That is absurd," said Mavis Dorr. "He has not been wrong in a single morning since before the middle of the winter. Not one. I would put him against anybody in this basin and the figure would come out the same."

"I know," said the turner. "I have read a copy of that wall for six years in a lane with nobody in it and I have never been wrong either and I have been exactly as useful as he has been, and I have been doing it for six years, and the heading on that sheet is not asking whether a figure is right."

He turned round and looked at the yard.

"It is asking whether anybody was asked about it. And a figure that cannot be asked about is not a figure. It is a habit with a number in it. And I am not saying that about him. I am saying it about me and I would rather say it about me in front of nine people than have somebody else say it about him in about four years."

---

Nobody at the top of eleven feet said anything for about four seconds.

Adrian Vale stood there and found that he was not thinking about the four figures on a wall at all, and that the thing he had carried up that hill two days running, and had carried across a room, and had carried in a corridor at about eleven at night and had not been allowed to put down at these boards, was a page with nothing ruled on it with four figures on it in a clerk's hand.

And that a sheet with nine places on it and not one name anywhere on it had come nine miles down a hill that morning and put a figure beside this yard of four.

---

"The thing you have been doing since the eighty-fifth morning," said the woman who keeps a scale, without coming up the bank, "is getting figures in front of the person who stands nearest to them. And you have done it better than anybody who has ever stood in that yard. And that sheet came down that hill this morning and told nine places what they are worth, and not one of the nine of them is a place where somebody stood nearest to a figure."

"I know," said Adrian.

"Say that you know it twice," she said, "because I have watched about four people in that yard come back from a corridor with something and put it down and take it up again, and I have been weighing heavy things for nine years and I know what a thing looks like when a person has not put it down."

---

Mara Quill got a pencil and said the line out before she wrote it, and it is the twenty-sixth line on the page at the back of the lot book and none of the twenty-six is in the lot book, and it said that a sheet had come up that lane in the inside of a man's coat and that a heading had been read out loud at the boards of the second table at about eleven in the morning, and that she was entering the fact that the heading had been read and not what the heading said, because what the heading said was not hers and she is a clerk of a book with five lines on it and has been for fifteen years.

---

The light went off the boards of the second table at about half past four, and the sheet was still lying on the boards of the second table with about nine places and not one name on it, and the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about four and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other.

Adrian Vale was still four feet off the near end of the first table when about half of them went down the eleven feet, and he stayed where he was, and the yard got emptier around him the way it had every morning for twenty mornings, except that this morning there was a figure on the boards of the second table that said four, and every one of the four was something he had heard out loud, and not one of them was the best thing he had ever done.
