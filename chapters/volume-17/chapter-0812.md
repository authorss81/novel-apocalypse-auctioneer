# Chapter 812: The Ninety-Sixth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Forty-One Came Up The Eleven Feet At About One In The Afternoon Which Is Three Mornings After He Said He Would Come Up And Not Do It Again And Said That He Had Been Asked In A Room Nine Miles Off To Be The Person A Number Is Checked Against And Had Not Answered And Had Come Up To Say So Out Loud Before He Answered Anywhere Else And A Woman Of Fifty-Two Asked Him A Question She Had Decided In Advance She Did Not Want The Answer To

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and fifty-one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and ten. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and twenty-six days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and forty days. Seven hundred and one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and fifteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and twenty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and ninety days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and ninety-three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and twenty-nine days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and sixty-nine marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and fifty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of about forty-one said in this yard at about one this afternoon that he had been asked to be the person a number is checked against, and a man of about forty-one is not a thing this district made and the count did not move.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and twenty-six mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and twenty-five. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for seventy-six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is ninety-six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has seventeen lines on it and none of the seventeen is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and thirty-seventh of those mornings, and a man of thirty-eight stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table from about nine o'clock without saying one word about where he had been or how long for, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed that and about nine did not.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and fifty-first night of that run, having slept on four hundred and fifty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the forty-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and the man of about sixty-four said three words about where he had put a length of fencing wire and would not say a fourth, and the man of sixty-one did not ask, and the whole of what has passed between those two men in forty-one afternoons came to three words this afternoon.

The record has stood eleven days, and a man who keeps every road in this basin went up that hill yesterday morning and said out loud in about nineteen people's hearing that this district cannot produce a person, and he went back down the surface, and nobody in that yard has said one word to the man of fifty-six about the description that was handed over at ten to eight yesterday, and he read the four off that wall twice yesterday and twice this morning and got them four times.

---

Adrian Vale came up the eleven feet at about nine in the morning and stopped at the top of it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had not seen him since about ten to eight on the ninety-fourth morning, and about four of them had already decided without being asked that they were not going to be told what the nine miles was for, and he put his hand on the boards and gave none of them the chance to ask him, and stood about four feet off the near end of the first table for about an hour saying nothing at all.

---

"One hundred and seventeen," said Orren Pike, at about half past nine, to the boards, and he wrote it in the box on the back of his own sheet under the morning and did not read it out, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said the figure back to each other afterwards in the ordinary voice and not in a whisper and not to him.

---

At about one the man of about forty-one came up the eleven feet with his bag in his hand and did not put the bag down, which is what he had done on the ninety-second morning, and he was three mornings late and said so before anybody asked him.

"I said I would come up tomorrow at about one and I am coming up at about one and it is three mornings later and I am going to say why in the wrong order, because the wrong order is the one that is true and I have had two days of thinking about the right order and the right order is a lie."

"I did not come because I went to the eleventh place on the Salt Verge and back. It is four miles further than the ninth mile from this bank and I have walked it in a day and a night and I did not tell anybody I was going, and I want that said, because I have spent four mornings being a man who says things out loud before he does them and I broke it on a thing about four miles and I would rather have that on the page than have somebody find it out."

---

"You have not broken anything," said the man at the end of the second table. "I made a rule about a thing not happening twice and you are the second person to have come up this bank and asked to be allowed to say the thing you came up for, and the rule has held both times, and I would like it noticed that it held on the turner this morning for about four minutes and cost him three mornings of walking, and I do not know yet whether that is the rule working or the rule being convenient."

---

"Which is why I came up."

---

Sabra Holt came out from the near end of the second table, and about four people in that yard had not seen her move all morning.

"I am going to ask you one question," she said, "and I said the question out loud before I asked it on the eighty-sixth morning and I am not going to start explaining myself about it now, and I am going to tell you before I ask it what I have decided I do not want, because I have had four mornings and I am not going to walk into it blind."

"You said on the ninety-second morning that you would come up at about one and not do it again. You did not come at about one on the ninety-third, or the ninety-fourth, or the ninety-fifth. And I have sat on those boards for four mornings with two questions about it and I have decided that I do not want to know whether you did not come because you did not want to, or because somebody told you not to. I want to know the third thing."

---

The turner stood about four feet off the boards with his bag in his hand and looked at her for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

"The third thing is that you asked," he said, "and that is all there is, and I have been three mornings late because I went and found out, and because I did not want to come up here and be asked what I was going to say before I had said it anywhere that was not this yard, and because I got what I went for and it took two days, and I have been carrying it up and down four miles for two days and about nine people in this yard have been asking each other where I went and not one of them has asked me, and I noticed that this morning and I want it in a yard that it was noticed."

---

"Then say what you went for," said Mavis Dorr.

---

"I was asked in a room nine miles off, on the lane, at about half past two on the second day, to be the person a number gets checked against," said the turner. "That is the phrase. I have used their phrase twice now because it is the shortest one and because when I put it in my own words I make it sound like a job."

"And I have not answered. That is the whole of why I came up. I have not said yes and I have not said no and I have come up a bank to say so in about nineteen people's hearing before I answer in a room, and I would like somebody here to understand what that costs me, because I was asked in that room about four days ago to come up this lane and say a thing out loud before I did it, and I did it, and I have been eating on it for four days, and the reason I am eating on it is that I have found out that doing it once is cheap and doing it twice is not, and there is no third time in this yard that is going to be free."

---

He put the bag down on the ground, which he had not done on the ninety-second morning.

"They told me the terms in the ordinary voice and not off a paper, and I am going to say them in the ordinary voice and I am going to say them exactly, because if I put them in my own words they become something I could agree to."

"The first one is one mouth for the number. One person in this basin says the tally out loud, once, on a morning, and from that morning nobody else in this basin counts anything. Not is stopped. Counts nothing. The boards stay up and the marks stay on them and the wall stays where it is and every other person in this basin goes back to what they were doing."

"And the woman who told me that did not say it as a favour and she did not say it as a threat. She said it the way a woman says a thing that has been true for a long time and has never had to be said. That is what I have been carrying up and down four miles."

---

The yard was extremely quiet and about nineteen people at the top of eleven feet were all standing still and not one of them had heard the two halves of that sentence before.

---

"Do you want it?" said Mavis Dorr.

---

The turner looked at the boards for a while.

"I want to be able to say a number and have somebody else say it after me," he said. "That is what I want and it is about nine words and I have been at the end of this lane for nine years and I have been reading four figures off a copy of that wall for six of them in a lane with nobody in it, and on the ninety-second morning a man read four figures out loud in a yard and I found out that I had been right for six years, and I have not been able to put that down since, and about four people in this yard watched a man of forty-one find out that he was not wrong and saw that it did not help him, and they have not worked out how those two things go together and neither have I."

"And there is a room nine miles off that will give me a job where being right is the whole of what I am for. I am not going to stand in this yard and tell anybody I do not want it, because I do want it, and I have wanted it since about four days ago, and the only thing standing between me and it is that I have not said the word out loud yet in a place where about nineteen people could be asked about it separately."

---

Adrian said, from about four feet off the near end of the first table, in a voice that had not been used all morning.

"You have about nine minutes before the light goes off that table," he said, "and you have been walking for two days and you have not slept and I have been walking for about a day and a night and I have got nothing to say about any of it and I am not going to pretend to."

"I am going to say one thing and then I am going to stop and it is not advice and I have no standing here and I know that."

"You can take it. If you take it you will be the only mouth for the number in this basin and about nine people will find out in about four years that they cannot check you, because there is nobody left counting the same thing that you are counting. That is not a thing I have worked out. That is a thing I have watched happen to a person on the ninety-second morning, and it happened to a man of forty-one who was not wrong."

---

Mara Quill entered one line on the page at the back of the lot book and read it out before she wrote it, and it is the eighteenth line on that page and none of the eighteen is in the lot book, and it said that a man of about forty-one had come up the eleven feet at about one in the afternoon, three mornings later than he had said he would come, and had said out loud in about nineteen people's hearing that he had been asked in a room nine miles off to be the person a number gets checked against and had not answered, and that she was entering the fact of the asking and not what he was going to do about it.

---

"I am not going to give you advice," said Adrian again, "and I have given you one thing and I am aware that one thing was advice and I would like it noticed that I gave it anyway and that nobody in that yard stopped me."

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and on the back of the boy's own sheet the box had the morning in it and a ruled space under the morning with nothing in it, and the nothing stayed, because a thing had been said out loud in that yard that morning and had been entered and a thing had been said out loud in that yard that afternoon that had not been entered, and the box takes one of those and not the other and he has never worked out which and did not write it down, and the boards of the second table were bare where the wire had been and bare where the stone had been, and a bag was on the ground at about four feet off the near end of the boards and was still on the ground when the light went off the boards, and four hundred and eleven has been on the sheet at that gatepost every morning of this run of mornings and its size of error has not been computed, and the fifth of the five is not paid, and the column has one name in it and the heading over it still says whoever.
