# Chapter 810: The Ninety-Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Sixty-Four Came Back Up The Eleven Feet At About A Quarter Past Six With A Length Of Fencing Wire In His Hand And Put It On The Boards Of The Second Table In Front Of About Nineteen People And Said Three Things And One Of Them Was That He Had Said He Was Not Going To Take It Down And Was Going To And Was Wrong And Adrian Vale Went Down That Lane At About Ten Past Eight In The Evening And Did Not Come Back Before The Light Went Off Those Boards

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and forty-nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and twenty-four days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and thirty-eight days. Six hundred and ninety-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and thirteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and eighteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and eighty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and ninety-one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and twenty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and sixty-seven marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and fifty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the count of the five did not move on any of these ten mornings including the morning a man brought a length of fencing wire back up that bank in his hand and put it on a table, and a wire is not a payment and is not the fifteenth of anything.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and twenty-four mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and twenty-three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for seventy-four mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is ninety-four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move and does not move for a person and is not a page. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and there is a strip of paper about the length of a hand on the boards of the first table at the near end that was under the boards on the eighty-sixth morning and nobody in this yard put it there.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and thirty-fifth of those mornings, and the four he has been reading since the middle of the winter are on a page with nothing ruled on it and are not on the wall, and nobody in this district has said which of the four on the wall is wrong and nobody is going to be asked.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and forty-ninth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and forty-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the thirty-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of about sixty-four went off at about half past four and came back up that lane at about a quarter past six with about eleven feet of fencing wire in his hand and the two of them said nothing at all about it.

The record has stood nine days, and nobody in this district has been asked anything about the standing of it on any of those nine days, and two pages came down that lane inside eleven mornings and neither of them has been carried up it, and the lane count is twenty-five and did not move this morning because a man came up that lane with a piece of wire and not with a page.

---

He came up the eleven feet at about a quarter past six with it in his right hand, held in the middle the way a man holds a thing he has picked up off the ground and not out of a coat, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had been in that yard since about a quarter to eight, and he came to the near end of the second table and put it down on the boards and squared it about nine inches and put his hand flat on it the way a man of fifty-six puts his hand on a stone.

---

"I told you on the ninetieth morning, out loud, at a culvert, that there was a thing about it I was not going to explain to anybody in a yard in about four mornings' time," he said, to about nineteen people. "It has been four mornings and I have had every one of them. I am fifty-nine years old or I am sixty-four and I have never once said which and I am not going to start on the boards of a table in front of about nineteen people.

"What I am going to say is three things and I am going to say them at once because I have got about a quarter past six and I have got to be at the bottom of this bank at half past six for nine years and I have not missed one and I am not missing this.

"One. I am taking it down. I said I was not going to be the man who takes it down and I have been wrong and I would like that said before anything else, because I said it out loud in the open at a culvert and about nine people heard me and I have had four days of it and I have not slept properly and the reason is the second thing.

"Two. It is not mine to leave. That is what I could not explain in four mornings and it is about eleven words at the front of it and I have got them. A thing at a place where a thing cannot be refused is not a thing anybody put there. It is a thing the place has. And there is about a mile and a half of nothing down there with a turn and a culvert on it and about nine years ago there was nothing on either side of it and now there is a wire at the culvert and I walk past it twice a day and I have had four days of deciding that somebody has to be the man who takes it down, and there is not anybody, and the reason there is not anybody is that it is not mine and it is not the wire's and it is not the man who put it there any more than it is mine.

"Three. The man who put it there went back up that lane at about half past eleven this morning and said out loud on the boards, in front of about nineteen people, at about a quarter past two, that he did not know what it was for and that everybody in that yard knew it was not going to work that afternoon. He said it so that they would all know it at the same time. And then he went down four miles of road and did the thing anyway, and I have had four days of a sentence going round in my head and the sentence is that a man who says out loud in a yard that he does not know what a thing is for is the only kind of man in this basin whose word you can do anything with at all, and everybody in that yard heard him say it and not one of them has said one word about it to me in four mornings, including the man at the end of the table who said on the eighty-third morning that he was not going to ask a man of thirty-eight what it was for, and who has not asked him about it since, and who I have watched come about four inches from asking him twice in four days and turn round both times."

---

Adrian said, "I have not been in this yard for about thirty hours and I came back at about a quarter to six and I am not going to have this be the morning I am late to a thing that is about me."

---

"You are on time," said the man of about sixty-four, without looking round, "because I would not have come up that bank before half past six for anybody in this yard and I came up at a quarter past six, and that is the only thing I have got and I have spent it, and you can have the rest of the morning."

---

He put both hands flat on the wire on the boards and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at about eleven feet of fencing wire lying on a table in a yard where about a quarter of a mile of it is a rail and a man who fitted the rail has never once been thanked, and nobody said anything, and about four people in that yard looked at the wire for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall and then about nine of them looked at the man of about sixty-four and not at the wire, and one of the four who looked at the wire first said afterwards that it was the first thing anybody had put down in that yard that she could not describe, and one of the nine who looked at the man said afterwards that she had looked at him because the wire was not the thing that had been put down.

---

The clerk entered one line on the page at the back of the lot book and read it out before she wrote it, and it is the seventeenth line on that page and none of the seventeen is in the lot book, and it said that a man had brought a length of fencing wire back up that bank and put it on the second table and had said three things out loud in about nineteen people's hearing, and that she was entering the fact of the three things and not what any of them meant, and that she had been entering the fact and not the meaning for nine days and that this was the seventeenth time.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and a man who has said as sent about four times in nine years did not say it this morning, and Orren Pike wrote the morning in the box on the back of his own sheet and wrote the two line references under it and nothing else, and the boards of the second table were bare where the stone had been and were not bare where the wire was, and the strip of paper about the length of a hand was on the boards of the second table about four feet along from where a woman had put it eight mornings ago, and it had not been covered, and it had not been moved, and a rule in this yard says that is not a thing anybody can do twice. and the bid was not run, and the fifth of the five is not paid, and the column has one name in it and the heading over it still says whoever, and four hundred and eleven has been on the sheet at that gatepost every morning of these ten and its size of error has not been computed and was not computed by anybody in that yard and is not going to be computed in the next four days.

Adrian Vale said one thing at the top of that bank at about ten past eight in the evening, out loud, to about nine people who were still in that yard, before he went down it with nothing in either pocket.

"I am going two miles up that lane," he said, "and then nine, and I am going to find out whether a woman on a working list in a corridor has been told that her name is on a page, and I am going to ask her, and not a woman with a key, and not this yard, and I am going to go on the road and not up it, and I have not got a fence wire any more and I am not going to be able to put a thing down at a place where a thing cannot be refused.

"And I am not telling this yard what she will say and I am not telling this yard what she is owed. I have been in it a hundred and fifteen mornings and I have watched about four of the five things it does not have get described three times by three people who were not asking me, and I am not going to describe it to her.

"It will be about nine miles and it will be dark before I am back and I am going anyway, and if anybody in this yard wants to come with me they have got about a minute to say so, and I am not going to wait longer than a minute, and I am not going to be angry with anybody who does not come."

About nine people in that yard did not say anything, and the man at the end of the second table looked at the lane for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and did not come, and did not say why, and has not been asked.

Adrian Vale went down that lane, and the lane count is twenty-five and did not move, and does not move for a person, and there is nobody in this district who can say what he found at the end of nine miles or whether he got back before the light went off those boards, and nothing in that yard was made this morning by anybody in it.
