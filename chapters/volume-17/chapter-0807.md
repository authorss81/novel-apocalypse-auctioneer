# Chapter 807: The Ninety-First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Stranger Came Down That Lane With A Second Page In Her Right Hand And A Boy Of Seventeen Said Twenty-Five Out Loud From The Boards Before The Clerk's Hand Moved And The Page Said That A Clause Nine Miles Up That Hill Had Been Extended And Not Ended And A Man Of Sixty-One Said Out Loud In A Yard That He Had Not Ended It And Had Been Going To And Had Not

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and forty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and twenty-one days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and thirty-five days. Six hundred and ninety-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and ten days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and fifteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and eighty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and eighty-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and twenty-four days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and sixty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and fifty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a condition with no end on it was extended nine miles up that hill between the eighty-ninth morning and this one and the count of them is still four and did not move, because extending one is not making a new one.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and twenty-one mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and twenty. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for seventy-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is ninety-one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and it moved this morning, and a page came down that lane in a stranger's right hand and a boy of seventeen said the figure out loud from the boards before the clerk had the cover off. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and there is no more in it this morning than there was yesterday morning.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and thirty-second of those mornings, and at about a quarter to ten this morning a woman stood at the bottom of that bank with a page in her hand and would not come up the eleven feet, for the second time in eleven mornings, and said so before anybody asked her.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and forty-sixth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and forty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the thirty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and the man of sixty-one did not say anything else that afternoon, and the man of about sixty-four said two words about the wire at a culvert about four miles down that lane, and would not say a third.

The record has stood six days, and three pages have come down that lane inside eleven mornings, on the eighty-first, on the eighty-fifth and on this one, and not one of the three has been carried up it by anybody in this district.

---

Orren Pike said it from the boards before Mara Quill had the book open.

"Twenty-five."

He said it out loud, in the ordinary voice, and about nine people turned, and the clerk's hand was still on the cover of the lot book, and the woman at the bottom of that bank had not finished getting her breath back from coming down four hundred yards of hill in about eleven minutes.

---

"It moved," said the woman who keeps a scale, from about forty feet off, without coming up the bank. "I have watched that figure every morning since the seventy-sixth and it has moved three times in sixteen mornings, and the first time was the eighty-first, the second was the eighty-fifth and the third was a minute ago, and all three times it moved because a person came down that hill with a page in her hand and a boy got to the figure before a clerk did, and I am the only person on this step who can tell you those are the same kind of thing, and I am telling you because nobody on those boards can."

---

The stranger did not come up the bank and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had to go down it to hear her, which is a thing that has happened twice in eleven mornings now, and Sabra Holt went down it first and stood about four feet off her at the bottom.

"You did not come up last time either."

"No," said the stranger, "and I have thought about nothing else for two days, and I have decided that I am not coming up, and I would like it said out loud at the bottom of this bank in front of about nine people that I have decided it, so that when somebody writes it down in about four years there is a morning in it and not just a shape."

---

"Read it out," said Sabra Holt. "From here, in the ordinary voice, and everybody will hear the same words and I will not be standing in front of any of them."

It is four lines long. It has a heading that is about a thing. The first line says that the matter held under the founder's clause has been extended. The second line gives a form of words for that and it is the same eleven words that were read out in this yard on the eighty-sixth morning, in the same order and with the same punctuation. The third line gives a figure for the whole of it and it is a figure with a term on it. The fourth line gives a name and it is the name of the person who filed the application in respect of this district, and it is the name Sabra Holt has said out loud in this yard four times in three mornings, and it is a name in a hand that is not hers, and she has not been asked about it.

---

The yard was extremely quiet and about four people at the bottom of that bank and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were both standing still and neither set of them knew about the other.

---

"Say the second line again," said Adrian, from four feet off the boards.

The stranger read it again. It was the same eleven words and she read it at the same speed and about nine people at the bottom of that bank heard the words *may not be questioned on the holding of it* come out of a stranger's mouth for the second time inside eleven days.

---

"It is the same eleven words," said Sabra Holt.

"It is the same eleven words," said the stranger, "and I asked for them in writing, and I was told that was the fixed form, and I read them out at the bottom of this bank twice because about nine people are standing here and I have never read anything twice in my life."

---

Iven Tallow said nothing at all for about the length of time it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and then he said one thing, and he said it to the stones.

"I have been going to end it for about four days and I have not, and I am not going to, and I would like the yard to have that from me this morning rather than work it out."

---

"Going to," said Mavis Dorr, from about four feet off the boards. "Not went to. Going to."

---

"Going to," said Iven Tallow. "I said going to and I meant going to and I am not going to take it back to be tidy. I have been the holder of that thing for seventeen years and on the eighty-fifth morning I used it and I have not stopped and I am not going to, and in about four days somebody is going to come up this lane and offer me a way out of the gap where two witnesses should have been, and I am telling this yard now that I have thought about it and I am not going to take it, and I said the same thing on the eighty-fourth morning and I have said it twice and it has not got less true the second time, which I did not expect and which is the only thing I have learned this week."

---

"You have not been asked anything," said the man at the end of the second table, "and I am the one who has been doing the asking in this yard for about four mornings, and I would like it entered that a man of sixty-one has now said in this yard, twice, on his own account, that he is not going to do a thing he said on the eighty-fourth morning he was not going to do, and that nobody asked him on either morning, and that I am the man who has been not asking him about it for about four days and I have had about nine mornings of it and I am going to stop."

He put both hands flat on the boards.

"I have got about three years and eight months," he said, "and I gave a morning of that away on the eighty-fourth for a sentence about a man of thirty-eight and a length of wire, and I gave about four minutes of this morning to a man of sixty-one on a bank he has not come up for nine years, and I would like somebody to tell me what I have left it for."

---

Nobody told him and the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about four and got all four of them, and about four people said *as sent* and about nine did not, and Mara Quill entered one line on the page at the back of the lot book with nothing ruled on it and read it out before she wrote it, and it is the fourteenth line on that page and none of the fourteen is in the lot book.

The stranger went back up the lane at about half past two and the lane count is twenty-five and did not move after that, and eleven feet of fencing wire was across a mile and a half of nothing at the culvert about four miles down that lane and had been there for about a day, and the boards of the second table were bare where the stone had been, and nobody in that yard had gone down that lane to look at it and about four people in that yard had thought about going and had not.
