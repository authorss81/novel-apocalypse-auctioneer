# Chapter 813: The Ninety-Seventh Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Forty-One Came Up The Eleven Feet At About One With A Page He Was Given In A Room Nine Miles Off And Read It Out At The Boards And The Page Said That The Forms Go Into A Room Before The Hundredth Morning And That This District Has Not Put A Name On One And That Two Places In This Basin Have Said No And That One Has Said Yes And Cannot Produce A Person And A Boy Of Seventeen Did Not Say A Figure Out Loud And A Woman Who Keeps A Scale Said From About Forty Feet Off That She Had Been Waiting Nine Years For It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and fifty-two days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eleven. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and twenty-seven days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and forty-one days. Seven hundred and two days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and sixteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and twenty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and ninety-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and ninety-four days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and thirty days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and seventy marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and fifty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a form went into a room nine miles off with no name on it and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and twenty-seven mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and twenty-six. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for seventy-seven mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is ninety-seven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move and a page reached the boards of the second table at about one this afternoon. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has eighteen lines on it and none of the eighteen is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and thirty-eighth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about nine of those nine had heard him do it five hundred and thirty-eight mornings running and had never once heard him say what any of the four was for.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and fifty-second night of that run, having slept on four hundred and fifty-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the forty-second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and the man of about sixty-four said nothing at all about the wire this afternoon and neither of them mentioned it, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood twelve days, and a page came up that lane in the hand of a man of about forty-one this morning and it did not come down it, and the lane count is twenty-five and did not move, and the first time since the seventy-sixth morning that a page has reached that yard the count has stood still, and nobody in that yard has worked out yet whether that is a fact about the page or a fact about the boy.

---

He came up at about one with a sheet folded in the inside of his coat and he did not fold it again, and he put it flat on the boards of the second table and put both hands flat on it about four inches from the edge, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stood about as far back from the boards as they usually stand.

"I have had this since the second day," he said, "and I have not read it out of a room, and I am going to read it out here because I have been four days learning what it is like to hold a thing and not say it, and because about four people in this yard have taught me that a thing said at the boards on a morning is a different animal from the same thing said in a corridor, and I am not going to say it in a corridor and leave it here to be described afterwards."

"It is four lines. I am going to read all four and then I am going to stop, and I am not going to read it a second time, because the second time is a thing I do for a woman with a key and she is standing about four feet off me and she has read it once already and she has not asked me to read it again."

---

Sabra Holt said, quietly, "I have not read it."

---

"Then I am reading it twice," said the turner, "once for me and once for that, and I am telling you I have done it and why."

---

He read it out.

"The first line says that the forms for this basin go into a room nine miles up that hill before the end of the hundredth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, and there are three mornings between that morning and this one."

"The second line says that the form asks for the name of a person, and that a form with nothing on it goes into the room the same as a form with a name on it, and that nobody nine miles up that hill is going to come down here and find out which is which."

"The third line says that this district has not put a name on one."

"And the fourth line is about three places and it is the one I have read eleven times. A place on the north road sent the form back with nothing on it and a line under the space that said they would not. A place past the water sent somebody to sit in the room and that somebody has been sitting there since the second day and has not written anything. And the eleventh place on the Salt Verge sent a form back with a name on it, and the name is not the name of anybody living there, and they have not been asked whether it is the right name, and they are not going to be, because the form does not ask them."

---

He stopped and put his hand flat on the sheet again and did not say anything else for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

---

"Twenty-five."

---

It was not the boy who said it. It was a woman who had been standing about four feet off the near end of the boards since about ten to eight and who had not moved, and she said it in the ordinary voice, and about nine people turned.

"I am not the boy," she said. "I keep a scale and I have a beam and I have stood on that step about forty feet off the foot of that bank for nine years and I watch this yard because I weigh things that come out of it and there is not a great deal else to do at that hour."

"That figure has moved three times since the seventy-sixth morning. It moved on the eighty-first. It moved on the eighty-fifth. It moved on the ninety-first, about a minute before the boy got to it this time, which he will not thank me for. And all three times it moved because a person came down that hill with a page in her hand and a boy got to the figure before a clerk did."

"It has not moved this morning and a page has reached those boards. I am the only person on that step who can tell you that those are two different kinds of thing, and I have been waiting nine years for one of them."

---

"Then it should have moved," said Mavis Dorr.

---

"No," said the woman who keeps a scale. "It should have moved and it did not, and that is not the same as the other way round, and there is a word for the other way round and there is not a word for this yet. I have been thinking about it since about one and I have got about as far as a woman with a beam gets and I am going to say what I have got and then I am going to stop."

---

Orren Pike came out from behind the boards with his sheet against his chest and about four people at the top of eleven feet said something at once and one of them said *he is seventeen* and nobody took any notice of it.

"I did not say it," said the boy, when he could.

---

"You have not said it," said the woman who keeps a scale, "and I want to say to about nineteen people that I have watched that figure every morning for nine years and a figure that does not get said is not the same as a figure that does not move, and the second one is a person and the first one is a decision, and about four people know the difference and about nine people do not, and I have wanted to say that in this yard since before any of you were standing at these boards."

"Go on then. Say it now, if you are going to. Or do not. I would rather it was either than the third thing, which is neither of us waiting about four feet apart for about four minutes pretending it is a morning like the other ones."

---

The boy said it. He said it in the ordinary voice and it took him about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

"No," he said. "And I am going to say why and then somebody is going to tell me I am wrong and they can and I would like it entered in the box and not in the columns, and a man who walks nine miles has not asked me for anything either, so we are both of us doing it."""

"A man of thirty-eight has been at about four feet off the near end of the first table since about nine and he has not said one word this morning and he has not asked me for one number either, and I want it said that he has not asked me, and that a man of thirty-eight who walks nine miles and comes back is the last person anybody in this yard should have to ask."

"That figure is a count of strangers who came down that hill with a page in their hand. Not a count of pages. Not a count of mornings this yard was told something. A count of people who were not from here and came down on foot and gave something to us and went back up."

"And a man from the end of this lane came up it this morning with a page in his coat and it is not the same thing and if I say twenty-six this afternoon then in about four years that number is going to be a count of how many times this yard was handed something, and that is a number about us and not a number about a person, and it is the first number in this district that would be true on every page and mean nothing on any of them, and I am seventeen and I have kept that sheet for four years and I have never had a figure in it that I could not get back by asking somebody who was standing there."

---

The turner stood about four feet off the boards and did not say anything for a while.

"That is the first time in four days," he said, "that anybody has told me what I was carrying."

---

Mara Quill entered two lines on the page at the back of the lot book and read them both out loud before she wrote them, and they are the nineteenth and the twentieth lines on that page and none of the twenty is in the lot book, and the first said that a page had reached the boards of the second table at about one in the afternoon and that four lines on it had been read out, and the second said that the count of strangers who had come down that lane had not moved on the morning a page reached those boards for the first time since the seventy-sixth morning, and that she was entering the fact of the figure standing still and not the reason for it, because the reason had been given by a boy of seventeen and she was not his clerk and would not be one.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine of those nine had heard a boy of seventeen say a figure would be a worse number than no number, and about four of them had not been in the yard at one o'clock and had found out about it from the boards, and Orren Pike wrote the morning in the box on the back of his own sheet and ruled a space under it and did not leave it empty, and wrote under it in pencil that the lane count did not move and that he had not said it and why, and that the box is for mornings and a decision is not a morning and he had put it in the wrong place on purpose, and the boards of the second table were bare where the wire had been and bare where the stone had been and the sheet of paper was off them and folded in a coat again, and four hundred and eleven has been on the sheet at that gatepost every morning of this run of mornings and its size of error has not been computed, and the fifth of the five is not paid, and the column has one name in it and the heading over it still says whoever, and there are three mornings between this one and the hundredth.
