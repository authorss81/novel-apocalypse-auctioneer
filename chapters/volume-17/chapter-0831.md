# Chapter 831: The One Hundred And Fifteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Forty-One Came Up The Eleven Feet At About Ten And Said Out Loud In That Yard That He Had Answered A Room Nine Miles Up That Hill And Had Said There Is One And Had Not Said Who And A Woman Who Keeps A Scale Said From About Forty Feet Off That There Are About Nine People In That Yard Who Heard A Man Answer And That A Room Nine Miles Off Does Not Know About The Nine

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and seventy days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and twenty-nine. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and forty-five days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and fifty-nine days. Seven hundred and twenty days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and thirty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and thirty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and twelve days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and forty-eight days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and eighty-eight marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and seventy-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of about forty-one came up that bank at about ten and said out loud that he had answered a room nine miles up that hill, and the clerk entered a line on the back of that book and read it out before she wrote it, which is the thirty-first, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and forty-five mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and forty-four. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for ninety-five mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and fifteen, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-six and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty lines on it and none of the thirty is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and fifty-sixth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about four of those nine have not been told that a room nine miles up that hill was told about a person in this yard this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and seventieth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and sixty-nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the sixtieth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of thirty-eight stood at the top of that bank and did not go down the eleven feet, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood thirty days, and a man of about forty-one came up that bank at about ten and said that there is one person in this district who can be asked about a figure, and the man at the foot of that wall was not told, and the thirty-first line on the page at the back of that book was read out before it was written.

"One hundred and thirty-six," said Orren Pike.

He said it and put the pencil down behind the boards, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at him and waited for the rest of it, and there was no rest of it.

"I have said that figure at these boards since the hundredth morning," he said, "and I have not said it on every morning since and I am not going to pretend that I have, and this morning I am not going to add one word to it, and I would like about nine people to notice that I did not add one word, and I would like about four of them to notice that I have been standing at these boards since about ten to eight with my hands flat on them waiting for somebody to come up that bank."

---

Nobody came up that bank until about ten.

The yard did what it does. The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart got a cart out of a gateway he has been getting it out of every morning for about nine years. The woman who keeps a scale was on her own step about forty feet off with a beam across her knees doing nothing to it. Mara Quill had the lot book open and her hand on the page at the back of it and had not put anything on either.

Adrian Vale stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table with his hands behind him and thought about nothing at all for about four minutes, which was the first time in this run of mornings he had managed it.

---

The turner came up the eleven feet at about ten with his coat open and no sheet in his hand.

He had not come up that bank with nothing in his hands before.

---

"They have asked me a third time," he said.

He said it to the boards and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stopped what they were doing, and the woman who keeps a scale looked up from her step without moving her knees.

"It was not the same question," said the turner. "The first two were whether this district has a person in it who can be asked about a figure. That is a question about a room and I gave it nothing twice. This morning at about ten to four yesterday they asked me whether there is one. Yes or no."

---

"And what did you say," said Mavis Dorr.

"I said yes."

Nobody said anything for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

"I took about two days to be able to say it," said the turner. "That is the truth and it is not a good look on me. I went down that lane on the one hundred and fourth morning to begin and I have been down it and up it twice since and this is the first time anybody in this yard has heard me say a word about it."

---

"Why," said Mavis Dorr.

"Because there is one."

He said it without any weight on it at all, the way a man says a thing he has checked twice.

"And because I am not going to be a man who has said no in that room more times than he can count," he said, "and because they did not ask me a name and I did not give them one, and because the woman who asked it said that it was all right that I would not describe him and that they would take it from the district."

---

The yard was extremely quiet.

"Take it from the district," said Sabra Holt.

"Take it from the district," said the turner. "That is her words and I am not going to improve on them."

---

Adrian Vale had been standing at about four feet off the near end of the first table with his hands behind him and he took them out and put them flat on the boards.

Something had come down that hill about the man at the foot of the wall. Not about him. About a man in this yard who stood on a flat stone at about a quarter to eight every morning and about four every afternoon and had been asked one question in the whole of that, on the one hundred and twelfth morning, and had answered it in four seconds.

And it had not come as a page and it had not come as a form. It had come as a word in a mouth in a room, and about nine people had heard the word and could be asked about it separately tomorrow, one at a time, and none of them had been asked about it this morning.

---

From about forty feet off, without coming up the bank at all, the woman who keeps a scale said one thing.

"There is one person a room nine miles off can ask about a figure," she said. "And there are about nine people in this yard who heard that man answer one on the one hundred and twelfth morning, and every one of those nine can be asked about it separately tomorrow, and the room does not know about the nine."

She put her hand flat on the beam.

"That count is mine," she said. "It has been mine since I came to that step and I have not given it to anybody and nobody has asked me for it in about nine years and I am not going to give it to anybody this morning either, and I would like it noticed that I said a figure out loud in that yard this morning, in about nine people's hearing, and that what I said was not for me, and that on the one hundred and eleventh morning I read two figures off a stranger's page at these boards and nobody has asked me about either of them since."

---

At about eleven Mara Quill put her hand on the page at the back of the lot book and read out what she was going to write, and then wrote it.

"I am going to write that a man of about forty-one came up that bank at about ten this morning and said out loud in about nineteen people's hearing that he had answered a room nine miles up that hill, and that he had said yes, and that he had not given a name."

She put the pencil down.

"I gave you a reason on the one hundred and twelfth morning and it was about a person asking me, and I gave you a reason on the one hundred and fourteenth morning and it was about me, and this one is duller than either of them and I am going to say it anyway. It happened in this yard. That is the whole of my office. A thing that happened in this yard goes on that page and a thing that happened nine miles up a hill does not, and I have got one of the second this morning in my coat pocket and I am not going to write it."

---

It is the thirty-first line on the page at the back of that book and none of the thirty-one is in the lot book.

---

"Are you going to tell him," said Mavis Dorr.

She was looking at Adrian Vale and she was not asking the yard.

"No," said Adrian.

"That is the whole of your answer?"

"It is not the whole of my answer." He took his hands off the boards. "I am not going to tell him because a man who has said out loud at about four on about four mornings running that he does not know what any of them is for cannot also be handed the fact that a room has been told about him without becoming a different thing in this yard by about four o'clock, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet would be able to tell the difference and he would be the only one of the three of us who would not be able to tell it from the outside."

---

"Then somebody will," said Mavis Dorr.

"Somebody will," said Adrian Vale, "and it will not be me, and I have known that since about half past ten this morning and it is the first thing I have decided in this yard that I have not decided out loud to nine people before I did it."

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four came round the end of the low wall at the foot of that bank on his four hundred and seventieth night of that run and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and neither of them said anything else.

The man of fifty-six was not there at about half past three.

He is there at about a quarter to eight and at about four and about four feet of stone and eleven feet of bank and about four hundred yards of air are what he has to work with, and on this morning, at about ten, he was eleven feet below the top of that yard and there was a man at the top of it who had just said a word in a room about him and had not said who, and nobody had gone down.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, the second thing, which is that he does not know what any of them is for.

About nine people heard it for the fourth time running.

And about four feet of stone and eleven feet of bank did not carry one word of it to the man of about sixty-four, who had already gone out at about half past four, and the man at the foot of the wall did not know that a room nine miles up that hill had been told that there is one of him in this district, and there was nobody at the top of that bank who was going to be the one to tell him, and Adrian Vale stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table and had made that decision out loud at about half past eleven to about nine people and would not be moved on it by anybody.
