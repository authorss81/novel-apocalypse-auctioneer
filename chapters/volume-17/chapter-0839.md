# Chapter 839: The One Hundred And Twenty-Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Boy Of Seventeen Went Up That Lane At About Six In The Morning With A Sheet He Has Kept For Four Years And Was Back At The Top Of That Bank At About Eleven And Said That A Woman Had Read It And Asked Him What The Man Is Called And That He Does Not Know And Nobody In That Yard Could Tell Him

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and seventy-eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and thirty-seven. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and fifty-three days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and sixty-seven days. Seven hundred and twenty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and forty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and forty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and seventeen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and twenty days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and fifty-six days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and ninety-six marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and eighty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a boy of seventeen went up that hill at about six in the morning with a sheet he has kept for four years and came back at about eleven and said that a woman nine miles off had read it and asked him what the man is called, and nothing was entered on the back of that book this morning, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and fifty-three mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and fifty-two. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and three mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and twenty-three, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and sixty-fourth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not know until about eleven that he had been up that hill and back.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and seventy-eighth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and seventy-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the sixty-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a boy of seventeen went up that hill at about six in the morning and was back at the top of that bank at about eleven, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood thirty-eight days, and a sheet went up that hill this morning and came back and nothing came down it, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and three of the thirty-three are ones nobody heard read and that count is not going to be lower.

Orren Pike was not in that yard at about ten to eight on the one hundred and twenty-third morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet noticed his absence at about eleven and not before.

---

He came up the eleven feet at about eleven with the sheet in his coat and about nine people at the top of eleven feet turned round and one of them said the morning number out loud for him before he had got to the top, which is the only time in this run of mornings anybody has said it for him.

---

"I have been up that hill and back," he said. "I went at about six. It is nine miles. I got there at about ten to eight and I was outside a door for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and then a woman read it."

---

"Did she read it out loud," said Mavis Dorr.

"She read it out loud to me and she read the asking and not the answer, and she read it twice, and the second time she had a finger on the part where a man of fifty-six says he does not know."

---

"And then," said Orren Pike.



"And then she asked me one question back," he said. "She asked me what the man is called."



About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not make a sound.

---

"I said I did not know," said the boy. "And she waited, and I said it was not that I had forgotten it. I have been in this yard four years. He has never given a name in this yard. Not once. Not to me, not to anybody. There is a book on these boards with a column in it and a heading over that column that says whoever, and there is one name in that column and the name in it is not his, and I have read that column about four hundred times."

---

"You could have made one up," said Mavis Dorr.



"Yes," said the boy.



Nobody asked him anything else about it for about four seconds and then Sabra Holt asked him one thing.



"What did her hands do," she said, "before she read it."

---

Orren Pike stopped and thought about it, which none of the about nine people expected.

"She put both hands flat on the table," he said, "about four inches apart, and she did not touch the sheet, and she looked at it for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and then she picked it up with two fingers and she did not straighten it. It was straight. It had come out of a coat."

---

"Thank you," said Sabra Holt, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard her say it and about four of them have said since that they cannot remember her thanking a person at those boards before, and that the person she thanked was seventeen and had walked nine miles that morning.

---

The turner came up the eleven feet at about half past two.

He had gone down that lane at about half past five and he had said the number in a room nine miles up that hill at about ten to ten and then he had walked nine miles back, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had been looking down that lane since about eleven.

"I said the number at about ten to ten," he said. "In that room. Out loud. And there were three people in it and two of them wrote it down and one of them did not, and I watched the one who did not, and she had a page in front of her with about nine lines on it and she did not put my number on any of the nine, and she said one thing to me on the way out."

---

"What did she say," said Adrian Vale.



"She said, is that the only name this district has sent us this year," said the turner.



Nobody at the top of eleven feet made a sound.

---

"I said yes," he said, "and she said thank you, and I came up that bank at about half past two and I have not said a word to anybody since and I did not want to say it in that room and I am saying it here because about nine people heard me say in this yard on the one hundred and twenty-second morning that I would begin, and about nine people can be asked about that separately tomorrow."

---

"That is not a fair thing to carry up that hill," said Adrian Vale.

"No," said the turner.

"Then do not carry it."

"I have carried four figures off a copy of that wall for six years with nobody in the lane," said the turner, "and I am not going to be told what is fair by a man who has been standing at those boards every morning of this run of mornings and has never once been asked what his own number was for, and I know that was unkind, and I said it because I have been in a room with three people in it this morning and one of them has a page with nine lines on it."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard that and about four of them said nothing at all and Adrian Vale said nothing at all, which is a thing he has done in that yard before and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed him doing it again and about four of them have said since that they would rather he had said something.

---

The man at the end of the second table said nothing about it either, and about nine people looked at him and about nine of them did not ask, and that was the second time in two mornings that a thing was named in that yard and he made no note of it on the side of his own hand.

---

Mara Quill had the page at the back of the lot book open and she did not write anything on it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet saw her not write anything and about nine heard the silence.

"Thirty-three lines," she said, to nobody, at about half past eleven. "Three of them are ones nobody heard me read and I told you the names of two of them were the boy's to find out about and one of them was not, and the boy's has found out what was in his and it was not a name, and I am not going to write down this morning either and I am not going to say the reason and about nine people in this yard have stopped asking me for the reason."

---

Adrian Vale asked the boy one question and it was not about the name.



"What did she do with it when she had finished," he said.

---

"Put it back in the folder and wrote the time on the corner in pencil," said Orren Pike, "and there were thirteen folders on that table and nine of them had got a corner written on and four had not, and I counted them twice on the way down because I did not believe it the first time, and I am seventeen and I have got four hours of walking in me and nothing else to do."

---

"Thirteen," said Adrian Vale.

---

"Thirteen," said the boy. "I counted them twice on the way down because I did not believe it the first time and I am telling you that I did not believe it and that I am a person who can be wrong about a count and I was not wrong about that one."

---

Adrian Vale stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table for a while with his hands behind him.

Thirteen folders and about nine lines and four hundred yards of air and nine words he is not allowed to say at those boards, and he had spent every morning since the eighty-fifth morning finding out that a figure is a promise that somebody counted something once, a long time ago, in a place where a person could be found, and that the whole of what he had brought to that yard had gone up a hill this morning and come down as a question about a man's name, and the man had no name in that yard, and there was nothing on the boards about any part of it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and seventy-eighth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and seventy-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the sixty-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and neither of them said anything else.

About nine people were standing on that ground because it was public lot and about four of them were standing on it for no reason at all, and the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about four with about nine people about four feet off him and about four hundred yards of air above them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and then he said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people heard it for the twelfth time running.
