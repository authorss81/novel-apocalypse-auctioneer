# Chapter 808: The Ninety-Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Turner Who Comes Up That Lane Twice A Day Asked A Woman With A Key What A Number In A Record Is For And Was Told That It Is The Age Of A Figure And That The Figure Is Four Hundred And Eleven And That Nobody In This Basin Knows What That Figure Is The Age Of And That Is The First Time In The History Of This District That Two People Have Said Out Loud In A Yard That They Do Not Know The Same Thing And Both Of Them Said It On The Same Morning

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and forty-seven days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and twenty-two days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and thirty-six days. Six hundred and ninety-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eleven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and sixteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and eighty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and eighty-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and twenty-five days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and sixty-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and fifty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a figure with an age and the figure itself are two numbers in one book and nobody in this district has worked out what to do with that, and nobody has ever asked.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and twenty-two mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and twenty-one. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for seventy-two mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is ninety-two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-five and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and there is no more in it this morning than there was yesterday morning, and the page at the back has fourteen lines on it and none of the fourteen is in the lot book, and it is the fifteenth morning of this run of mornings and the fifteenth of them has not been entered in the lot book either.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and thirty-third of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have heard him say four figures twice a day for five hundred and thirty-three mornings, off a wall that is nine years old, and about four of those nine have thought about what the figures are at least once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and forty-seventh night of that run, having slept on four hundred and forty-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the thirty-seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and the man of about sixty-four said three words about the wire at a culvert about four miles down that lane, and would not say a fourth, and the man of sixty-one did not ask.

The record has stood seven days, and a man of about forty-one who comes up that lane twice a day asked a question in that yard at about eleven this morning and has not been in a yard in about nine years, and the lane count is twenty-five and does not move for a person and is not a page and does not move, and a woman who is on working list forty-one in a corridor nine miles up that hill was named in a permanent record on the eighty-ninth morning and has not been told, and nobody has asked her, and the lane count would not have moved for her either.

---

He came up the eleven feet at about eleven with his bag in his hand and stood about four feet off the boards and did not put the bag down, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had not seen him at the top of it in about nine years and about four of them had known he was on the lane twice a day and the other five had not known anything about him at all.

---

"Ask her," he said, "and let me stay where I am, and if she says no I will go back down and I will come up tomorrow at one and I will not do this again."

---

Sabra Holt said, "I have not asked you for anything."

"Then ask me nothing and let me say the thing I came up here for," said the turner. "There is a copy of that wall at the far end of this lane where the boards come to an end, and I have read the four off it for about six years with nobody in the lane, and I got them right by myself and I had no way of knowing. On the seventy-seventh morning I asked the man at the wall in this yard to say them to me and he said them and a woman with a key ruled that it was not to happen twice.

"On the eighty-fifth morning the four changed. I was at the bottom of that bank at about a quarter to eight on the eighty-fifth morning, at the place where I stop, and I heard a man read four figures and I heard about nine people not react, and I knew on the spot that they were not the four I had been reading at the end of that lane for six years, because I have read those four about four thousand times by myself and I know what they sound like in the mouth of a person who has read them since before I started.

"And then in about eleven days a record took three pages about this district and I have read all three of them and not one figure in any of them is a figure I can match to anything, and there is one number on a sheet at a gatepost about nine miles down that road that is four hundred and eleven and it has been four hundred and eleven every morning of this run of mornings and there is a figure about how old it is and I have never known what that figure is the age of.

"So that is my question and it is not for her. I have come up this bank to stand next to a man of fifty-six while somebody else says it, and I am not going to be the man who says the first half of it."

---

"What is the number on the sheet at that gatepost the age of," said the man at the end of the second table.

Nobody answered him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at about four other people, and the clerk looked at the boards.

"It is four hundred and eleven," said Sabra Holt, "and it has been four hundred and eleven since before I came up this lane, and there is a figure beside it for the age of it, and I have carried both of them up and down that hill for about eleven mornings and I have said both of them out loud in this yard and I have never once said what the age is the age of, because nobody nine miles up that hill has ever told me, and I have asked, twice, in writing, as a Council and not as a kindness, and both times I was told that it was not a matter the form went into.

"And I am going to say the other half of it now, out loud, in this yard, because a man of about forty-one has come up a bank he has not stood on in nine years to hear me not know something, and the man at the end of the second table asked that question in this yard at about eleven this morning and got no answer either, and about four people heard both of those happen and I am not going to let it be the first time.

"Nobody in this basin knows what four hundred and eleven is the age of. It is not the age of the sheet. I have had the sheet. It is not the age of a print. I have asked. It is not the age of a body, because a body four hundred miles off is about four hundred and twenty-five days past a printing it did not make and that is a different number and it has been a different number every morning of this run of mornings.

"And that is all of it, and I have held it for eleven mornings and I have not put it in a book, and I am putting it in a book this morning at my own asking and nobody else's, and I would like the man of about forty-one to hear it enter."

---

The turner was still standing about four feet off the boards with his bag in his hand and he had not put it down and he had not said anything.

"Read them to me," he said, to the man of fifty-six. "The ones on the wall. Not for a record and not for a book and not because a woman with a key has ruled it is not to happen twice. Because I have been wrong for six years and I am not going to find out about it from a page, and I have been standing on this bank for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall and I am not going to be the reason that gets entered anywhere."

---

Nobody moved and the clerk had her hand flat on the boards and did not lift it and did not open the book.

"Read them," said the turner. "I have been about nine years asking for a thing and getting nothing, and I have not asked this morning and I have not got a rule and I do not have a book to put it in and I have not got a column, and I am asking anyway, and I am asking out loud, and about nineteen people have heard me, and I would like that entered, and I know that nobody in this yard is able to enter it."

---

The man of fifty-six took his hand off the stone.

He said the four off that wall, at the speed he says them, and he got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard a man of about forty-one hear the four he had been reading in a lane with nobody in it, and the turner stood still and did not write and did not move and did not say anything at all, and about four people in that yard have said since that they watched a man of about forty-one find out that he was not wrong and it did not help him and that they have not worked out how those two things go together.

---

"I was right," said the turner, at last. "I have been right for six years and I would like it entered that I was right, and I know it cannot be, and I am going to go back down that bank and come up it tomorrow at one with my bag and I am not going to do this again, and I would like the yard to notice that I said that in the present tense and I have not got a reason for it."

---

He went back down the eleven feet and the lane count is twenty-five and did not move and does not move for a person and is not a page, and the clerk entered on the page at the back of the lot book that two people had said out loud in that yard that they did not know the same thing, and that she was entering the fact of the two sayings and not the fact of the not-knowing, and that on the ninety-second morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month two people in this district said out loud that they did not know what four hundred and eleven was the age of, and that one of them had carried the number up and down a hill for eleven mornings without asking and the other had been reading four figures off a wall in a lane with nobody in it for six years, and that a woman with a key said out loud that she did not know, and that a man of about forty-one came up that bank to hear her say so, and that neither of them knew the other one was in that yard at the same time this morning.

It is the fifteenth line on that page and none of the fifteen is in the lot book.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and the turner was at the bottom of that bank at about half past four with his bag in his hand and was going up the lane at about one o'clock every morning and about half past four every afternoon and had been for nine years, and eleven feet of fencing wire was still across a mile and a half of nothing at the culvert about four miles down that lane and nobody had taken it down and nobody in that yard had asked anybody whether it should stay, and the boards of the second table were bare where the stone had been, and a man of about forty-one was at the bottom of that bank at about half past four with his bag in his hand and had said that he was right about four figures for six years and had not known what to do with it and was going to come up the eleven feet tomorrow at about one.
