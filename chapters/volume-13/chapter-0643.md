# Chapter 643: A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Mends Fencing Said Out Loud That He Was Not Going To Pretend He Did Not Know What His Own Yard Was For

The boards of that second table were pale and dry by about seven on the nineteenth of the sixth month and had begun to crack along the grain in two places where the sun had been on them since eight, and the dust that had come off that bank in the night was lying in the gap between the two tables in a fine pale line that nobody had walked into, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and forty-one mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and eighty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seven and did not move on its forty-third morning, and entered that the dust in the gap between those two tables is not entered as a figure about the tables, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-eighth of those mornings. About four of them have said that the fourth of those four figures has gone up by one again this morning, and that about four of them have said that a man has read it three hundred and sixty-eight times and has never once asked himself what it is counting from, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard is going to ask him.

"**The board carries five hundred and forty-one, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and fifty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seventy-one, and five hundred and thirty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about ten with nothing in his own hands at all, which nobody at that low wall could remember him doing before, and stood at the near end of that second table and put his own right hand flat on the boards and said one thing out loud, in about 38 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to stand in my own yard for another morning and pretend that I do not know what it is for. It is for keeping a number. That is the whole of what it has ever been for, and about four of you have worked out that I have stood at the near end of one of these two tables for forty-three mornings and have not said that out loud one morning in all that time, and I am saying it this morning, and nobody in this yard has asked me about it, and I am not going to wait until somebody does.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has not been asked about it and is not going to wait to be, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past ten with the boards dry to the knee of his own right leg and a skin of dried mud on his own shin that came off in flakes when he put his foot on the boards, and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man at the near end of that table has said out loud this morning what that yard is for and he said it is for keeping a number, and about four of you have worked out that that is the first time in forty-three mornings that anybody in this yard has said what any part of this is for, and about four of you have worked out that I have been at the far end of it for the same forty-three mornings and have not said it, and I am not going to say it after him this morning, because a man who says a thing out loud after another man has said it out loud is not saying it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not going to say a thing after another man has said it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eighteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have entered what that man said this morning, and I have not entered it as a reason, and about four of you have worked out that a reason is a thing a person gives out loud before a hand moves, and that what he said this morning came after forty-three mornings of nothing happening, and that I cannot put a reason in front of a thing that has already been done, and about four of you have worked out that this is the first time in nineteen years that a person in this yard has told me what to enter, and that nobody told me, and that he is not a clerk and would not be one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she entered a thing this morning and did not enter it as a reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the near end of that second table this morning with nothing in his own hands and said out loud what that yard is for, and that about four of them have said that about nine people were in that yard when he said it, and that about four of them have said that a man at the far end of the same table said out loud afterwards that he was not going to say it after him, and that about four of them have said that a clerk said out loud after both of those that this is the first time in nineteen years anybody in that yard has told her what to enter, and that nobody told her.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man said out loud this morning that that yard is for keeping a number, and that the road keeper came up that lane afterwards and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that a man who keeps a number on a wall every morning came up a lane this morning and nobody asked him anything about any of the four of them.

The cart came up at about three and the near wheel was still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down and stayed there until about four.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said out loud this morning what that yard is for, and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing in two hundred and eighty-two nights, and that about four of them have said that whatever that yard is for, nobody has asked the man on those stones what he thinks it is for either, and that about four of them have said that he was in that yard before the first of those two tables was carried up it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and eighty-second night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and eighty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and ninety-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and forty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fifty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and twenty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and twenty-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and sixty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and eighty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying out loud what his own yard is for is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that what a yard is for is a thing a man says and not a thing a district has, and the count of the things this district has made has not moved on any morning of this month including today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and thirty-two.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and forty-one mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and a man saying out loud what his own yard is for this morning did not move it, because a condition with no end on it is a thing a document carries and a yard is not a document. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and forty mornings in that yard did not advance it and neither did a man saying what the yard is for, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that the man who said what this yard is for this morning is not the man at the end of that line and did not say no to anybody this morning or any other. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who said what his own yard is for is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud what his own yard is for is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said out loud this morning what that yard is for, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six with the dust off that bank coming up behind her and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and that about four of them have said that a man has now said out loud in that yard what it is for and that the one person in that yard who comes past it every single evening has not said a word about it, and that about four of them have said that she is the only one there who has not.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-eighth morning, and that a man said out loud this morning that that yard is for keeping a number, and that a man who reads four figures off that wall twice a morning and gets all four of them was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that a man has been keeping a number off that wall for three hundred and sixty-eight mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever stood up and told him what the number is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the nineteenth of the sixth month that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud at about ten that he was not going to pretend he did not know what his own yard was for, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seven and did not move, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about half past ten and had said out loud that he was not going to say it after him.
