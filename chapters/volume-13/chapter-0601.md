# Chapter 601: A Book In That Yard Told A Stranger Where A Man Was And On The First Morning After It Nobody Came Down That Lane

The stone lying face up on that second table had about a quarter of an inch of water standing in the hollow in the middle of it by about seven on the eighth of the fifth month, and the boards under the near end of that table were dark along the south edge and pale along the north one where a copy had been sitting, and a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had put his own thumb into that hollow at about ten past seven and taken it out and looked at his thumb and wiped it down his own leg, and about four people standing at the near end of that first table had watched him do it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for ninety-nine mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and forty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move, and entered that she is entering it as a count of mornings and not as a count of pages, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and twenty-sixth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a fifth line went onto a book in that yard on the sixth of this month and that two mornings have gone by since and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is zero.

"**The board carries four hundred and ninety-nine, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and fifteen days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and twenty-nine, and four hundred and ninety days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eight, in about 29 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have entered a new figure this morning and it is the first one I have entered in about two volumes that is not about a page, and it is a figure about a lane and about a person walking down it, and it is zero, and I want it said out loud in this yard that a count of zero is not the same as a count of nobody and I am not entering it as one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count is entered as a count of mornings, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds had his bundle of reeds over his shoulder at the low end of that yard and came and stood at the near end of that first table and said one thing out loud, in about 27 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been standing at the bottom of that lane at a different hour every morning this week and I have counted nine people going down it and not one of them had a page in his hand, and about four of you have been counting the top of that lane and about four of you have been right to count it because that is where the road keeper comes up.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering nine as a figure about anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about nine and set a rail he had cut the ends off down against the low side of that second table and stood at the near end of it with his right hand flat on the boards and his left hand in the cloth it has been in since the eleventh of the June, and said one thing out loud, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I asked one man one question in this yard on the second of this month and I have not asked anybody anything since, and six mornings have gone by, and about four of you have been standing in this yard since before eight every morning waiting to see whether a page does anything, and I would like it entered that I asked it and that I have not asked it twice and that I do not know yet what it did.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had come and gone twice behind that second table since seven and said one thing out loud on his way past the second time, in about 23 seconds, to about four people near enough to hear, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put that table up in about eleven minutes on a morning nobody asked me and I have not been thanked for it once in two volumes, and this yard has now got a book on it that tells a man where to walk to, and I have been thinking about that all morning, and about four of us have not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not entering the figure about a table, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen had been standing at the far end of that second table since before the clerk had finished and said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 27 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted the whole of this yard this morning and it is the biggest number I have counted in three volumes and I am not going to say what it is a number of until somebody else says it, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is empty and I read that fifth line out loud twice last week and I am not going in there.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered that she is not entering him in the column, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank for about a quarter of an hour after that, and about nine people at the near end of that first table have said since that this is the first morning in about a month on which nobody in that yard has come up with something to put down, and that about four of them have said that is not the same as a morning on which nobody had anything.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a count of people coming down that lane is zero this morning and that the road keeper comes up it twice a day and has never once been asked which end of it this district is counting. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank without stopping for anybody in that yard on that day, and the near wheel was still dragging when it stopped at the top. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a book in that yard told a man where to walk to two mornings ago and that the man on those stones was not told anything about it on the morning it went on. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fortieth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and thirty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and forty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and seventy-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and eighty-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and eighteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and fifty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and forty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a count of a lane is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a lane is not a thing a district made and that count is in a different book. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was touched this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and ninety.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for ninety-nine mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and counting a lane did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason out loud, which is that a count of a lane is not a reading of anything. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a count of a lane is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a book in that yard has five lines on it and that the woman who has read three of them every evening for about four months read three this evening and did not read the fourth or the fifth, and that about four of them have said that is not a failing. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man has read four numbers off that wall two hundred and forty times and that on the two mornings since a line went onto a book in that yard telling a man where to walk to he has read them exactly as he read them the two hundred and thirty-eight mornings before that.

The clerk of nineteen years entered at about half past eight in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the eighth of the fifth month that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about nine with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
