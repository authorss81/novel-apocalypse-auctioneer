# Chapter 608: A Man Of About Forty-Eight Who Keeps A Tally Said One Thing Out Loud In That Yard And What He Said Was Not A Name

The stone lying face up on that second table had a skin of water back in the hollow in the middle of it by about a quarter to seven on the fifteenth of the fifth month, about a twentieth of an inch deep and nothing at either end, and a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had looked at it and said nothing and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone and stood at the top of that lane and had come back down it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and six mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and forty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move on its eighth morning, and entered that four people went to the top of that lane before eight this morning and that she is not entering that as a figure about the lane because it is the wrong end of it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and thirty-third of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man who keeps a tally came to the near end of that first table this morning instead of the east end of that yard, and that he has not done that on any morning in about two volumes.

"**The board carries five hundred and six, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and twenty-two days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and thirty-six, and four hundred and ninety-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came up that bank at about half past eight with the flat book open under his left arm and stopped at the near end of that first table instead of at the east end of that yard and said one thing out loud, in about 42 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and eleven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I keep a book. It is not a book anybody in this parish has ever asked to look at and I have never offered it to anybody and I have said that out loud in this yard before. I am going to say one more thing and then I am going to shut it. If a person comes down that lane with a page in his hand, and asks me whether it came out of this yard, I am going to tell him I cannot tell, and I am going to tell him to go and ask the man by the water, and I am not going to write it down.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not entering his reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter to ten, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has just said out loud in that yard that he will send a stranger to the man the fifth line names, and he is not going to write it down, and I am not going to enter it as a decision, and about four of you have noticed that two men in this parish have now told a stranger where to walk to without either of them being asked and I am the only person who has been asked about it and I have said no every time.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she has said no every time, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about ten and put a rail down against the low side of that second table and stood at the near end of it with his right hand flat on the boards and said one thing out loud, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have not asked one person one question in eleven mornings and a man who keeps a tally has just said in this yard that he is going to answer one, and about four of you have worked out that the man who asked the question and the man who will answer it are not going to be in a room, and I would like it entered that I have known that since the morning I asked it and that I said it anyway.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has known it since the morning he asked the question, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 25 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have counted the count of that lane every morning for eight mornings and it is zero eight times and I have also counted how many people in this yard have said out loud this week where a stranger should walk to, and that is two, and about four of you would like me to compare the two numbers and I am not going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered that she is not asking him to compare them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about eleven with her own door open behind her and said one thing out loud, in about 29 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**If a man I have never met walks into my shop and asks me where a man is digging, I am going to tell him, and I have told two of them already this week and I am going to tell the next one too, and I would like it entered that I said it before anybody asked me to and that I am not going to be thanked for it by anybody in this yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open behind her, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that two people in that yard said out loud this morning that they would tell a stranger where the man is, and that about four of them have said that neither of them was asked and that about four of them have said that a woman has been telling strangers for a week without being asked and that about four of them have said that the only person in that yard who has been asked anything about any of it is a clerk and she has said no every time, and that about four of them have said that this is the correct way round and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard knows what the correct way round is.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man stood at a gate at the ninth mile four mornings this week and that a man keeps a tally at the east end of this yard and has said out loud that he will send a stranger on to the man by the water, and that about four of them have said that this district has now got three places a stranger can be told to go and none of them is in a book. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and the near wheel was still dragging when it stopped at the top. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nineteen people in that yard have spent this morning on where a stranger would be told to walk to and that the man on those stones has not been asked where he would tell a stranger to walk to, and that about four of them have said that is not an oversight anybody made. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and forty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and forty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Two hundred and fifty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and eleven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and sixteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and eighty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and eighty-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and twenty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and sixty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and fifty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and three places a stranger can be told to go to is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a place is not a thing a district makes and a man telling a stranger a direction is not a place. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and ninety-seven.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and six mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and two people saying out loud that they would tell a stranger where to walk did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and nobody in that yard has asked him and nobody is going to. About five things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and three people telling a stranger a direction is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about nine people in that yard said out loud this morning where a stranger should walk to and that the woman who has read three lines in that book every evening for about four months did not say one word this morning and has not said one word on any of the eight mornings since a fifth line went onto it, and that about four of them have said that a person who reads a page every day and does not say anything about it is not the same as a person who has not read it. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that the fourth of the four figures on that wall came to five hundred and two days this morning and that nobody in that yard has said one word about it, and that about four of them have said that the third of the four is a day out and that the fourth of the four is a year out and that neither of those is going to be said out loud again by anybody in that yard, and that about four of them have said that this is the fifth week that has gone by without either thing being said and that it will keep going.

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fifteenth of the fifth month about what he would do if a stranger came down that lane with a page in his hand, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is zero and did not move on its eighth morning, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about ten with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
