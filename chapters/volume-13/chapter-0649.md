# Chapter 649: A Stranger Asked The Man Who Digs Loam A Thing That Was Not About A Page And He Said No And Would Not Take It Back

It had rained in the night and stopped about six, and the boards of that second table were running with it by about seven on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month, and the whole of that bank was the colour of a wet road, and there was a rail in the fence at the far end of that second table with the drop off the top of it, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and forty-seven mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and eighty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is nine and did not move on its forty-ninth morning, and entered that she had told herself at about a quarter to seven standing at that table that she was going to enter whatever was said in that yard this morning, and entered that the man who stood in that yard yesterday came down that lane again at about nine and is not a ninth person counted twice, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-fourth of those mornings. About four of them have said that the fourth of those four is out by a whole year and that he has said it in the ordinary voice three hundred and seventy-four times.

"**The board carries five hundred and forty-seven, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and sixty-three days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seventy-seven, and five hundred and thirty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-three who is not from this parish came down that lane at about nine and came up that bank and stood at the near end of that second table with his own hands in his own coat and said nothing at all to anybody until the man who digs loam was at the far end of it, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past ten with the black of the ditch running off the back of his own right leg and down onto the boards, and he set his own spade up against the leg of that second table and went to the rail in the fence at the far end of it and put his own two hands on the rail.

The man of about thirty-three who is not from this parish said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about 5 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**What do you want.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything at all for about nine seconds, and the water came off the boards at the low side of that second table while it went on.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam turned round at the rail, in about 51 seconds, and said one thing out loud to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. You did not come down that lane for that. That line of mine names a place and it names me, and that is all it does, and I have stood at this end of this table every morning of this month and nobody in this yard has asked me one question, and you stood in this yard yesterday for a whole day and you did not ask me one either, and about four of you have worked out that I have noticed that. I am not taking it back. I am going to go on standing at this end of this table in the morning and saying things out loud now and then, and I am not going to be a person that a line in a book points at.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was asked one thing out loud in that yard and entered the question and the answer, and entered that the answer began with the word no, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was standing about eight feet off the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man at the far end of that table has said no out loud in this yard this morning and then he has said out loud that he is not taking it back, and about four of you have worked out that those two sentences have never been in the same morning before in this yard, and about four of you have worked out that a man who says both of them on one morning has made a thing this district has no word for, and I have been cutting reeds on that water since I was about eleven years old and I have got a word for a good many things and I have not got one for that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that this district has no word for what a man said this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-three who is not from this parish turned round to the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 4 seconds, to a man about eight feet away from him, and the boy counted what he said and got six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**What do you think about it.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud, in about 40 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put that line on that book. I asked the question in this yard on the second of the fifth month and I did not ask whether a stranger might come down that lane with something else, and I knew the answer to that at the time and I said it out loud anyway, and I am the only person in this yard who can be asked that this morning. I am not to be thanked for this morning. Nobody should thank me for this morning, and I would like that entered before anything else anybody says about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was asked one thing out loud in that yard and entered the question and the answer, and entered that he said out loud that he should not be thanked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about 68 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and sixty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to enter a no on that page this afternoon, and it is the first time in nineteen years that a thing in this yard has been a person refusing something rather than a figure going down, and I am entering the reason with it, which is that a person said no in this yard this morning in the ordinary voice and that a page is where a person says a thing when it is going to be said twice, and about four of you have worked out that a clerk is the only person here who can make a no permanent, and I am not going to strike it out tomorrow, and about four of you have worked out that I have been right about a great many things this month and that a clerk who is never wrong is not a clerk, and I am not going to work out in front of all of you which ones I was not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is going to enter a no this afternoon, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a stranger asked a man at the far end of that second table a thing that was not about a page this morning, and that the man said no and then said out loud that he was not taking it back, and that the man who put that line on the book was asked what he thought by the same stranger at about half past eleven and answered out loud about himself.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man said no in that yard this morning, and that a man who walks that road twice a day came up a lane with a man in it saying no and did not stop at the top of the bank.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four and did not turn round towards that yard once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said no in that yard this morning and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing in two hundred and eighty-eight nights, and that a man saying no in a yard and a man saying nothing on a stone for two hundred and eighty-eight nights are not the same thing, and that nobody in that yard has ever entered one of them against the other. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and eighty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and eighty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and ninety-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and fifty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fifty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and twenty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and thirty days back, it is open, no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and sixty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and ninety-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying no is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a thing a person refuses is a thing a person carries and the page it went down on was already there. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and thirty-eight.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on that wood for a hundred and forty-seven mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and a man saying no at about eleven this morning did not move it, because a condition with no end on it is a thing a document carries and a man refusing a thing is a thing a man carries, and that fifth line is still on that book and has not been touched. The reading of that lot was the fourth of the five when this day began, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and at about eleven this morning a person said no in that yard, and a clerk entered that no at about four in the afternoon, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth. About six things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who said no this morning is not in it, and a stranger who asked him is not in it, and a man who answered a stranger about himself is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying no out loud in that yard this morning is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and a clerk entering that no on a page this afternoon is not a payment either.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said no in that yard this morning and that the fifth line on that book is the fifth of the five now, and that she was standing up about four feet off it at about ten to six and did not look at the fourth one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-fourth morning, and that a man who said no in that yard this morning is standing at the far end of that second table now with his own two hands on the rail in the fence, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was asked one thing out loud in that yard at about eleven and said no and said out loud that he was not taking it back, and that she entered a no on her own page for the first time in nineteen years and entered the reason with it, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is nine and did not move, and that the fifth line of that lot book has not been touched.
