# Chapter 619: The Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Mends Fencing Said Out Loud In That Yard On The Twenty-Sixth Of The Fifth Month What His Own Question Did And Did Not Say Why He Had Put It Where He Had Put It

The boards of that second table had a skin of wet on them again by about seven on the twenty-sixth of the fifth month, thinner than the last. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had turned the stone lying face up on that table over onto its other face, which he does about twice a month. Had come back about a minute later and turned it back again, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and seventeen mornings.

What a clerk of nineteen years entered at about a quarter to eight was that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that three hundred and fifty-eight days is the age of that figure, and that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is two and did not move on its nineteenth morning, and that a stone was turned over and turned back, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and forty-fourth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has not been asked a single question this month and that about four of them have said that a clerk has entered four figures off that wall every morning for twenty mornings and has never entered that nobody asked where they came from.

"**The board carries five hundred and seventeen, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and thirty-three days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and forty-seven, and five hundred and eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about nine, in about 30 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said yesterday that my figure is a figure of hands, and I have been sitting here two mornings trying to work out what a figure of hands is for, and I have not worked it out, and I have written the word no in the margin of my own page twice this morning and cut it out both times, and about four of you have noticed the cutting and about four of you have not noticed the word.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she wrote a word and cut it out, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came up that bank at about ten with a flat book under his own arm and set it on the far end of that first table and said one thing out loud, in about 42 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and ten and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**On the eighth of this month I said out loud in that yard that I would send a stranger to the man by the water, and I have not sent anybody, and I am not going to, and I have told about four people in this yard that I am not going to and I have not told a clerk, and about four of you have worked out that the man who digs loam is not the only person in this parish who has said a thing out loud and then not done it, and I have worked that out this morning and I have not decided what it means.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the flat book he set down on her table, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven with nothing over his own shoulder for the first time this month and stood at the near end of that second table with his right hand flat on the boards and said one thing out loud, in about 55 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I asked a man one question in this yard on the second of this month and I am going to say what it was and what it did, in the plain words this yard uses, because I have been asked by nobody and I have been asked by about four people, and here it is. I asked him whether a stranger with a page in his hand might come down that lane and ask him whether it came out of this yard, and I asked it in front of all of you, and he said a thing back at me and then he said he did not ask to be written down, and I wrote it down anyway, and two people have come down that lane since with a page in their hand and not one of them has asked him that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the question as he gave it, and entered that he wrote a thing down after a man said he did not ask to be written down, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then nobody in that yard said anything at all for about a minute and a half, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that a man has said out loud in an open yard that he put a man on a page after being told not to, and that about four of them have said that the last thing he said was the part about the two people, and that about four of them have said that he said it in the ordinary voice and not the way he said the first part, and that about four of them have said that whoever wrote this district's figures would have put that man on the wall in a different order.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the knee of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 35 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has just told this yard he wrote me down after I said not to, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and that is not why I said it, and I am not going to help anybody turn that into an argument about owing me something, because a man can be owed a thing and still be standing in a yard on his own account.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not want it made into an argument, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about twelve, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has stood at that end of that table and told this yard what he did on the second of this month and it is the first morning this month that anybody has said what a man in this yard did, and about four of you have worked out that I have counted about a hundred and sixty things said out loud in this yard this month and I could not tell you what any one of them was for, and that today was the first one I could.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about forty-three was sitting on the low wall at the far end of that yard at about half past twelve with the page still folded in four in her own lap and had been there since about eleven, and nobody offered her anything and nobody asked her anything, and she said one thing out loud, in about 36 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in this parish since the twenty-fifth and I have not opened it and I have not gone down that lane, and about four of you have noticed that I am the only person in this yard who has come the whole way here and has not done the one thing that page says to do, and I have not worked out yet whether that is because I am careful or because I am frightened, and I am not asking anybody in this yard to help me work it out.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering her name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man said out loud in that yard this morning that he wrote a man down after being told not to and that the road keeper has come up that lane twice a day for the whole of that and has not been asked about any part of it. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and looked down at the yard without speaking to anybody in it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a woman sat on that wall from about eleven and was offered nothing and asked nothing. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fifty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and fifty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and sixty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and twenty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and twenty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and ninety-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and thirty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and seventy-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and sixty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying out loud what he did is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man saying a thing is a thing a man does and that this district has entered a great many of them this month and is not going to start entering them as things it has made. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and eight.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and seventeen mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man saying out loud that he wrote a man down after being told not to did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that the fourth of the five is a person and a person who has been written down against his wishes is still a person and is still not a remedy, and that this is the second time in thirteen volumes that the same man has been the subject of a page and that both times it was somebody else's hand. About six things were said out loud in that yard today, one of them by a person who is not from this parish, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and the man who said what he did this morning is not entered in it either.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who has said out loud that he wrote a man down after being told not to has not paid it and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a woman sat on that wall for about an hour and a half and was not asked one question and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same wall at about ten to six and looked straight at her for about a second and then went on up. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man said out loud in that yard today what he did on the second of this month and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not told about it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-sixth of the fifth month that a man said out loud in that yard what his own question did, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is two and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and his own hand still on them a minute and a half afterwards.
