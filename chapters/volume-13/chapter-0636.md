# Chapter 636: A Man Of About Thirty-One Who Digs Loam Said Out Loud On The Twelfth Of The Sixth Month That What He Minds About The Fifth Line Is That It Is The Only One Anybody Reads And A Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Mends Fencing Said He Was Not Going To Agree With Him About It

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the twelfth of the sixth month and there was about a shovelful of grey grit standing in the gap between the two tables that had been there since before the cart came up that bank, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and thirty-four mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and seventy-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is six and did not move on its thirty-sixth morning, and entered that a man was asked nothing in that yard yesterday and that she entered that he did not ask, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-first of those mornings. About four of them have said that the gap between the two tables has been standing full of grit since before this month and that the man who puts tables up went down that bank with a shovelful of it at about eight this morning and that about four of them have said that nobody has entered that anywhere and that about four of them have said that he entered it.

"**The board carries five hundred and thirty-four, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and fifty days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and sixty-four, and five hundred and twenty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went down that bank at about eight with a shovelful of that grit in a barrow and was gone about four minutes and came back up it and put the barrow against the low wall at the top of that bank, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man took grit out of a gap between two tables and down that bank with it, and entered that nobody asked him to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 49 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man in that yard is going to say out loud this morning what he minds about a line on that book, and about four of you have worked out that this is the second morning running that a person in this yard has said a thing about what he wants out of a page, and about four of you have worked out that I have entered both of those and entered neither of them in the column, and about four of you have worked out that a column with a name in it is a column for one thing, and that nobody in this yard has worked out what the other thing would go in, and I have not ruled a line and I am not going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she has not ruled a line, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and a puddle of it coming off the back of his own heel onto the boards, and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 41 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That line of mine is the only one of the five on that book that is not a number and not a date and not a name, and I have stood at this end of this table every morning this month reading a page that is four-fifths other people's work, and nobody in this yard has asked me one question about whether I minded, and what I mind is that it is the only one anybody reads, and I am saying that out loud this morning because I would rather be a man who says what he minds than a man that somebody has been standing about waiting on.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that what he minds about that line is that it is the only one anybody reads, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about twelve and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man at that table has said out loud what he minds about a line, and about four of you have worked out that in five weeks in that yard that is the first sentence anybody has said about a thing that was put there on purpose, and I put that line there, and about four of you have worked out that I have been standing at this end of this table five weeks waiting for somebody to say one word about it, and I have got what I have got this morning, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to stand here and agree with him about it, because agreeing with a man about what he minds is a thing I have no standing to do, and I have had five weeks to work that out and this morning is the fifth week.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not going to agree with the man it was said to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man at the far end of that second table said out loud this morning what he minds about a line on that book, and that about four of them have said that the man who put that line on the book stood at the near end of that table at about twelve and said out loud that he was not going to agree with him about it, and that about four of them have said that about nine people in that yard have now heard two men say two different things about the same line inside about half an hour and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has entered the difference.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man said out loud this morning what he minds about a line, and that the road keeper came up that lane afterwards and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that he reads the four figures off that wall on his way past and has never once asked what any of them is for.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four with his own back to that yard. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that two men in that yard said two things about one line this morning and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing about any of it, and that about four of them have said that he is now two hundred and seventy-four nights into a run and that nobody has counted him out loud once. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and seventy-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and seventy-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and eighty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and thirty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and forty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and fourteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and seventeen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and fifty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and ninety-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and seventy-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying out loud what he minds about a line is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that what a man minds is a thing a man carries and not a thing this district has made, and the count of the things this district has made has not moved on any morning of this month including today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and a clerk said out loud at about eleven that she is still not going to say in a yard how far either of them is out, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and twenty-five.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and thirty-four mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and a clerk entered the reason with the count on the ninth of this month and has not entered it since and nobody in that yard said so out loud this morning. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man saying out loud what he minds about a line did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that the man who said what he minds this morning has still said no to nobody, and that what a man minds about a line and what a line is owed are two different sizes of thing. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who said what he minds is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud what he minds about a line is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said out loud this morning what he minds about one line of that book, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six with eleven buckets on her hip and did not stop and did not look, and that about four of them have said that she reads the first three lines and that what a man minds about the fifth is not one of the three.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-first morning, and that two men in that yard said two things about one line this morning and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall twice and was not told about either of the two.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twelfth of the sixth month that a man at the far end of that second table said out loud what he minds about a line on that book, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is six and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about twelve saying out loud that he was not going to agree with the man it was said to.
