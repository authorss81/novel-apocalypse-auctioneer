# Chapter 607: About Four People In That Yard Said Out Loud That A Count Of People Coming Down A Lane May Be A Figure About The Wrong End Of A Lane And The Number Of People Who Have Come Down That Lane Was Zero And Did Not Move

There was no light on that yard before about seven on the fourteenth of the fifth month and the boards of that second table were wet along the whole of their length at about a quarter to seven and dry along the whole of it by about eight, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the shop up that bank and had come back again, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and five mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and forty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move on its seventh morning, and that seven is a figure about mornings and about nobody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and thirty-second of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that about four people in that yard went to a shop up that bank before eight this morning and came back again and that nobody has counted what happened in a shop at that hour on any morning in about two volumes.

"**The board carries five hundred and five, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and twenty-one days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and thirty-five, and four hundred and ninety-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past eight, in about 27 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Seven mornings is zero seven times and about four of you have said that is a fact about a lane and I have been up that bank at the top about half an hour every morning this week and I have counted the other end twice and it is also zero, and that means the lane is empty at both ends and not that anybody is standing at the wrong one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted both ends of that lane and that one of them is the wrong end, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about nine with her own door open behind her and said one thing out loud, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Two of them came into my shop before I had the door open this morning and neither of them asked me about a page and one of them asked me for a candle and paid for it, and I am telling that yard because about four of you have been standing here for a week waiting to be told that something is wrong with a lane and the thing that is wrong with it is that about nine people a day walk down it and about four of them come into my shop on the way back.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open behind her, and entered that she is not entering nine as a figure about a lane, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 35 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said in this yard four days ago that this district counted the wrong end of that lane and about four of you have been quoting it at me ever since, and I would like it entered that a man who has been wrong out loud in a yard for four days in a row is a thing this district has never had, and that about four of you have worked out that it is harder to be wrong about a figure than it is to be wrong about a table.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has been wrong out loud for four mornings, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about ten and went and stood at the top of that lane for about two minutes and came back down it and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been standing at that table for ten mornings with my hand on it and I have not asked one person one thing and about four of you have worked out that the reason I am not asking is that I do not know what I would do with the answer, and that is the first reason I have given in this yard for anything and I have given it out loud this morning before about four of you got there.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man went to the top of that lane and came back, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to keep that count at zero every morning until somebody comes down that lane and it is the only figure in this book that I enter without knowing what it is a figure about, and I am not going to take it out, and about four of you have been waiting since the sixth of this month for me to enter something that goes up and I would like it entered that I am not going to enter anything that goes up because it goes.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not taking the count out, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud in that yard this morning that she is going to enter a figure every morning that she does not know what it is a figure about, and that about four of them have said that this is the first figure any person in that yard has entered on purpose without knowing what it is for, and that about four of them have said that a district which has spent six volumes refusing to correct a figure is now entering one it cannot check, and that about four of them have said those two facts have nothing to do with each other and that this district has not worked out what it is supposed to mean.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that two strangers came into a shop up that bank before eight this morning and that neither of them asked about a page and that about four of them have said that the count of that lane may not be a count of strangers at all and may be a count of people who have already read that fifth line somewhere else, and that nobody in that yard knows where anybody reads anything. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stood there for about a minute and a half. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nineteen people in that yard have spent a morning on a figure of zero and that the man on those stones has not been given one. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and forty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and forty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and fifty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and ten days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fifteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and eighty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and eighty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and twenty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and sixty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and fifty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a figure nobody can check is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that entering a figure is not making a thing and that this is the fourth time in a month that the two have been confused in that yard. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and ninety-six.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and five mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a woman saying out loud that two strangers came into her shop and neither of them asked about a page did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a stranger who buys a candle has not had a reading of anything. About five things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who is findable on a page and is not paid is the same sentence as it was on the sixth of this month and it has not moved, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about nine people a day walk down that lane and about four of them come into a shop at the top of it on the way back and that the woman who reads three lines every evening walks up it with eleven buckets and is not one of the nine and has not asked to be. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went to the top of that lane and came back this morning, and that about four of them have said that he is the first person in that yard to have done it, and that about four of them have said that the man of about fifty-six has not gone to the top of that lane once in about two volumes and reads four figures off a wall about nine feet from it instead.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fourteenth of the fifth month about a lane being empty at both ends, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is zero and did not move on its seventh morning, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about ten with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
