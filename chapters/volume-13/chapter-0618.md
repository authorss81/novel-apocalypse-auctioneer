# Chapter 618: A Second Person Came Down That Lane On The Twenty-Fifth Of The Fifth Month And The Number Of People Who Have Come Down It Moved From One To Two And A Clerk Entered A Reason Before She Entered The Figure

The boards of that second table were dry the whole length of them by about seven on the twenty-fifth of the fifth month and the hollow in the middle of the stone lying face up on that table was dry with them. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had swept the boards of that first table from the far end to the near end with a besom he had brought down that bank on his own shoulder. About four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane in ones and twos, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and sixteen mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and fifty-seven days as the age of that figure. Entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is one at about a quarter to eight on its eighteenth morning. Entered that she entered what she said out loud yesterday morning she would enter if that figure moved. Entered that the figure is two and that it moved later in the day with a person in the room and the reason in the room before the hand. Entered that she swept her own table this morning and that she is not entering that as a figure about the tables, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and forty-third of those mornings.

"**The board carries five hundred and sixteen, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and thirty-two days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and forty-six, and five hundred and seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter to nine, before anything else had happened that morning, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Yesterday morning a man in this yard told me that my figure is a figure of hands and not of a lane, and I have thought about that all night, and I am going to say what I am going to write down before it happens and not after, and it is this. If a person comes down that lane this morning I am going to write down that they came down it and that they had a page in their hand and the hour, and I am not going to write their name and I am not going to write what they ask, and about four of you have noticed that I have stopped writing what they ask and that I had been writing it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she said it before it happened, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then a woman of about forty-three came down that lane at about ten with a page in her hand, and about nine people at the top of that lane turned round, and she came up that bank and stopped about nine feet off the near end of that second table instead of about six feet off it, and stood there a moment with the page folded in four, and said one thing out loud, in about 36 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have walked two days to get here and I have a page and I have not opened it since the fourth of this month, and I am not going to tell this yard what is in it, and I am not going to tell you what I am going to ask the man at the end of that lane, and about four of you have worked out that I am going to say something out loud today and that I have not said it yet, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman came down that lane with a page in her hand at about ten on the twenty-fifth of the fifth month and entered that she came down that lane and that she had a page in her hand and the hour, and entered that she is not entering her name, and entered that she is not entering what she asks, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is two and moved this morning, and the reason was in the room before the figure moved, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 29 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is two, and it has been one for six mornings and zero for twelve before that, and I have said out loud twice now what I will not write and about four of you have worked out that a figure nobody can check is still a figure and that I have been writing one for nineteen mornings out of a lane I could stand at the top of and see the whole length of.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her figure is two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven with his own hands empty and stood at the near end of that second table about six feet off that woman and said one thing out loud, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A person has come down that lane on a page and I put the line on the book that sent her and I have not said one word to her and I am not going to go over there, and about four of you have worked out why, and I am going to say the reason out loud because I said a reason out loud in this yard on the morning before and it turned out to be the right thing to do.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not go over to her, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the knee of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 26 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a woman standing about six feet off me who I have never seen before and who has a page in her hand, and I have read that fifth line about nine times and it says a man and it does not say how many, and about four of you have noticed that I have just said how many and that I did not have to think about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he said a number he had not had to think about, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about twelve, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have counted that yard four times this morning and I have got the same number every time and I have not counted a woman in it at all, because I did not have her counted before she got to the top of the bank, and about four of you have worked out that a boy who counts a yard is not counting the yard, he is counting the people who were already in it, and that is the second time this month I have found that out from somebody else and not from me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted her late, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a woman of about forty-three came down that lane at about ten with a page folded in four and stopped about nine feet off the near end of that second table instead of about six, and that about four of them have said that the man who digs loam looked at her and said nothing for about a second and then said a number.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a woman came down that lane in the morning and that the road keeper has walked that lane twice a day for longer than anybody can remember and that nobody has ever seen anybody come down it and go back up it. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and stopped at the top of it for about two minutes looking down that lane. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a woman came down that lane this morning and stood about six feet off a man and would not say what she had come for. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fifty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and fifty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and sixty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and twenty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and twenty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and ninety-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and ninety-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and thirty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and seventy-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and sixty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and about four of those marks were put on the underside of those boards before about nine by a man who was not asked to put them there. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a figure going from one to two is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a count moving is a fact about a person and not a thing a district makes, and that the reason was in the room an hour before the figure moved. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and seven.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and sixteen mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. A page carried down a lane by a person is a page a person brought and is not a fifth document this district does not own, and a clerk entered that reason before the figure moved. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a second person coming down that lane did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and a woman who would not say what she came to ask has not said anything yet. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a woman stood in that yard this morning about six feet off a man with a page folded in four, and that the woman who reads three lines every evening came up that bank at about ten to six with eleven buckets on her hip and stopped about four feet short of the yard and looked at the two tables for about two seconds and then went on up, and that about four of them have said that in about four months she has never once stopped at those two tables and that about four of them have said nobody in that yard has worked out why she stopped this evening. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a woman came down that lane this morning and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was about nine feet from her at the second one and neither of them looked at the other.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fifth of the fifth month that a second person came down that lane with a page in her hand and that the number of them is two, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his own hands empty and about three feet further from the end of it than he has stood all month.
