# Chapter 604: A Woman Who Keeps A Scale Said Out Loud In That Yard That Four Strangers Came Into Her Shop This Week And Asked Her Whether The Page On Her Counter Was Theirs And The Number Of People Who Have Come Down That Lane Was Zero And Did Not Move

The wind came up that bank from the south-west about seven on the eleventh of the fifth month and took the last of the water off the near end of that second table in about ten minutes and left the boards there dry and pale and about nine people in that yard had come up it before eight, which is the most that has come up it before eight on any morning since the count of the third month, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and two mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and forty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move, and entered that a woman said a thing about her own shop in that yard yesterday and that she is not entering it as a figure about this yard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and twenty-ninth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a book in that yard has told a man where to walk to for five mornings and that nobody has come down that lane, and that about four of them have said that a woman who keeps a shop up that bank has been asked the question the fifth line was written to answer, and by somebody else, and four days before.

"**The board carries five hundred and two, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and eighteen days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and thirty-two, and four hundred and ninety-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came up that bank at about ten past nine with her own door open behind her and a cloth in her hand and said one thing out loud, in about 36 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Four of them came into my shop between the eighth of this month and the tenth and every one of them asked me the same thing, which is whether the page on my counter is theirs, and I told all four of them I cannot tell, and I have told about four of you in this yard the same thing three times in three volumes and I am not going to change it, and I would like it entered that I said it and that I am not going to be thanked for saying it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open behind her, and entered that she is not entering four as a figure about this yard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about half past nine and went to the far end of that second table and stood there about nine feet off the woman with the scale and said one thing out loud, in about 30 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A woman has just told this yard that the question a page was written to answer has been asked four times in a shop in four days, and that the answer was the same every time, and about four of you have worked out what that means and I am the one who put it there and I am saying it before anybody else does because I have been in this yard two volumes saying things after other people.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the person, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds had come up that bank at about eight and had not gone to the low end of that yard at all, and said one thing out loud at about ten, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been down that lane every morning this week at half past six because that is the hour a man who is not from here would come down it, and there is a gate at the ninth mile and a man stood at that gate at about half past six for four mornings and he did not go down the lane and he did not come up it either and I did not ask him one word about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody in that yard asked the man at that gate anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 26 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted the people who came into that shop this week the way I count everything, and I counted four, and about four of you are going to tell me that is the same as four people coming down that lane and it is not and I do not know how to say why it is not and I have been thinking about it since the eighth of this month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered that she cannot say whether the two counts are the same count, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about 41 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to enter that a woman was asked a question four times in a shop and that I have not entered it as a figure, and I want it entered that the reason is not that a shop is not a place, because four of you have worked out that it is, and the reason is that a person standing in a shop is asked a thing and a person standing in that yard is asked a thing in front of about nineteen people and those are two different things and I am the one who has to decide which of them goes in my book.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud in that yard this morning that the reason she has not entered a woman's count is not that a shop is not a place, and that about four of them have said that is the first time in about two volumes that she has given a reason for not entering something that was about a person and not about a page.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man stood at a gate at the ninth mile four mornings this week and did not go down that lane, and that the road keeper comes up that lane twice a day and has never once said a word about that gate to anybody in that yard. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nine people came up that bank before eight this morning and that the man on those stones was one of the four who has come up that bank before eight every morning for about two volumes, and that about four of them have said he is not in that number this morning either. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and forty-third night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and forty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and fifty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and twelve days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and eighty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and eighty-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and twenty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and sixty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and forty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and four strangers in a shop is not a fourteenth thing and did not become one by being said out loud in that yard, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a person is not a thing a district makes however many of them walk in. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and ninety-three.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and two mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a woman saying out loud that she cannot tell did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a person saying she cannot tell is not a reading of anything and is not a remedy either. About five things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a woman who says she cannot tell four times in four days is not paid and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a woman who keeps a shop up that bank was asked a question four times this week and a woman who reads three lines every evening was not asked one thing, and that about four of them have said that the second of those two women was at the foot of that bank at half past five this evening and the first of them was not, and that about four of them have said that is not a comparison anybody set out to make. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that the fourth of the four figures on that wall came nearer to a round number this morning than it has been in about a volume and that nobody in that yard has mentioned it.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the eleventh of the fifth month about four strangers who came into her shop and asked her whether a page on her counter was theirs, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is zero and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the far end of that second table at about half past nine with his right hand hanging at his side and not on the boards and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
