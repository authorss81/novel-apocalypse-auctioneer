# Chapter 602: The Number Of People Who Have Come Down That Lane Was Zero On The Second Morning After A Page Went On A Book And A Clerk Said Out Loud That A Thing Which Has Not Been Used Is Not A Thing That Has Failed

The boards of that second table had dried on both edges by about seven on the ninth of the fifth month and the hollow in the middle of the stone lying face up on it had gone down to about a twentieth of an inch of water and no further, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane and stood there looking down it, and had not gone any further, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and forty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move, and that two mornings is not a figure about anybody and she is not entering it as one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and twenty-seventh of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that about four people went and stood at the top of that lane before eight this morning and looked down it and came back without going down it.

"**The board carries five hundred, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and sixteen days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and thirty, and four hundred and ninety-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eight, in about 38 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to say a thing this morning that I have not said in this yard in about two volumes and I would like it entered that I have said it once and I am not going to say it every morning, and here it is. A thing which has not been used is not a thing that has failed. That count of mine is zero and it is zero on its second morning and a count of zero is a fact about two mornings and about nobody, and I am not going to enter it as anything else.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not saying it a second time this month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about nine with his binder shut under his arm and stopped at the low end of that yard and said one thing out loud, in about 29 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nine people went down that lane yesterday and I have counted every one of them from the low end of this yard and not one of them was holding anything, and one of them was carrying a sack and one of them was carrying nothing at all and holding a hand, and about four of you have been standing at the top of that lane for two mornings looking for somebody with a page in his hand.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering nine as a figure about a page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came across that yard at about half past nine with nothing in his hands at all, which has not happened on any morning of a month, went to the near end of that second table and stood there with his right hand hanging at his side and not on the boards, and said one thing out loud, in about 24 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have not asked anybody anything for seven mornings and I have not put a hand on that table for two of them, and I want it entered that I noticed the second one about halfway through this morning and not before it, and that I have not worked out what the two things have to do with each other.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering why he noticed it halfway through the morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came and stood against the low side of that second table with his straight edge in his hand and said one thing out loud, in about 21 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have set tables for about twenty years and the only thing I know about a road is which end of it people come in from, and this yard has been counting the end they come in from for two mornings, and I would like somebody to say out loud what the other end is for.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody in that yard answered him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen had turned his own page over at about ten and had his own finger on it, and said one thing out loud, in about 23 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been counting the people who come up that bank before eight and I have got a number for that too and I am not going to say it out loud this morning either, because about four of you would use it against the other count and it is not the same count and I do not want it to be.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered that she is not entering his figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud in that yard this morning that a thing which has not been used is not a thing that has failed, and that about four of them have said that this district has said the opposite of that out loud in that yard for about six volumes, and that about four of them have said that a page a stranger has not asked about yet is not a page that has failed either, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked a page whether it had.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk counted the top of that lane this morning at about eight and about four people went and stood at it before she had finished and that the road keeper comes up it twice a day and has not been asked one question in about two volumes. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it, and nobody in that yard asked him anything and he said nothing to anybody. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nineteen people in that yard have spent two mornings watching one end of a lane and that the man on those stones has not been watched at all by anybody in that yard, and that about four of them have said that is not a comparison anybody asked for. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and forty-first night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and forty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.
