# Chapter 624: The Thirty-First Of The Fifth Month Came And Went And A Clerk Entered That The Month Ended And That Nobody In That Yard Has Counted It And A Man Of About Thirty-Two Who Puts Tables Up Moved That Stone A Foot Along That Second Table

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the thirty-first of the fifth month and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had got both hands under the stone that lies face up on the far end of that table and had walked it a foot along the boards, one board at a time, and had stopped when it came to rest against the chalk, and had looked at the chalk for about a minute without touching it, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and twenty-two mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years wrote at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight and entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and sixty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is three and did not move on its twenty-fourth morning, and entered that a stone was moved a foot along that second table, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and forty-ninth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man has been at the foot of that low wall every morning of that month and that today is the last one, and that about four of them have said that he will be at that wall in the morning and that nobody has asked him about that either.

"**The board carries five hundred and twenty-two, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and thirty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and fifty-two, and five hundred and thirteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up stood at the far end of that second table at about nine with both hands still on the stone he had moved and said one thing out loud, in about 29 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have moved that stone a foot and I did it because the boards under it are hollow and the ones under the chalk are sound, and I did it before the month went, and about four of you have worked out that I have never done a job in this yard at the end of a month before, and that I do not know why I did it today and that I have not told a clerk.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did a job at the end of a month and has not told her why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A month ends this morning, and a month that has ended can be counted off a board in daylight in front of anybody, and I counted one in this yard on the twelfth of this month and it took me about a minute, and this one I am not going to count and I am entering this morning that I have decided not to, and the reason is that a month a person is standing in is not a month, it is a month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a month ended this morning and that she has decided not to count it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 41 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk has said out loud that she has decided not to count a month, and about four of you have worked out that is the first time in thirteen volumes a clerk has entered a thing she has decided not to do, and about four of you have worked out that I asked a man a question on the second of this month and never asked him whether anybody might come with something else, and that those two are the same kind of entry and that I have had a month to think about the second one and about four of you have not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not ask a question about somebody else coming, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the knee of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table about three feet off the stone the man of about thirty-two had moved and said one thing out loud, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That stone has been on that table longer than I have been digging and a man moved it a foot this morning before the month went, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard has ever moved a thing on that table and that about four of you have worked out that it was me who said nothing about it being moved, so I am saying something about it now: I do not mind, and I would have said so on any other morning, and I am saying it on this one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a stone was moved and that he does not mind, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about twelve, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a clerk in that yard who has counted four months in her life and a man who has been at that wall every morning of the last one, and about four of you have worked out that between those two of them this month has been counted once and not counted once, and that I have counted that yard every morning of it and it is still the same number, and I do not know which of those three is right and nobody asked me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he does not know which of three is right, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a month ended in that yard this morning and that nobody in that yard counted it, and that about four of them have said that a clerk who has entered a figure for every morning of a month entered this morning that she was not going to enter one about the month, and that about four of them have said that a man moved a stone a foot along a table before the month went and that nobody in that yard asked him why and that he did not tell a clerk.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane this morning as he has every morning of that month and that a month in this parish now ends on a morning where a clerk writes down that she is not going to count it. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that this is the last morning of that month and that about four of them have said that the month went without a figure in it and that about four of them have said that a man at that wall has been in every morning of it and that is the only account anybody has of it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and sixty-third night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and sixty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and seventy-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and twenty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and thirty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and forty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and eighty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and sixty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and a stone was moved a foot along those boards before about nine. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a month ending is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a month is a thing a person is in and that this district has never counted one of those and is not going to start at the end of a morning when it has already decided not to. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and thirteen.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and twenty-two mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a stone moving a foot did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that a man saying he would have said he did not mind on any other morning is not that person saying anything. Five things were said out loud in that yard today, one of them a decision, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud stood ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who has been at that low wall every morning of a month and been given nothing on the last of them is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a clerk in that yard entered this morning that a month ended and that nobody counted it, and that about four of them have said that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six and did not look at them and that about four of them have said that she has read that book four hundred mornings and has not once said she was going to burn it. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and forty-ninth morning and that a month in that yard ended without a figure in it and that about four of them have said that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall twice in a day that the year does not have a figure in, and that about four of them have said that neither of them knows that and that neither of them is going to be told.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirty-first of the fifth month that the month ended and that she has decided not to count it and entered the reason, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is three and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and his own fingers about an inch apart, which is how he holds them when he is not holding anything.
