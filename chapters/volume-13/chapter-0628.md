# Chapter 628: The Man Who Digs Loam Asked The Man Of About Thirty-Four Who Mends Fencing On The Fourth Of The Sixth Month What He Thought And The Man Said A Thing Out Loud About Himself

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had the grey fan of grit from the far end of that second table swept into a heap at the foot of it by about seven on the fourth of the sixth month and was standing over that heap with a besom in his own hand deciding whether to put it in the water or in the gap between the two tables, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and twenty-six mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and sixty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is four and did not move on its twenty-eighth morning, and entered that a man has a heap of grit at the foot of that table and has not decided what to do with it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fifty-third of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a clerk entered a heap of grit this morning and did not enter it as a figure about anything.

"**The board carries five hundred and twenty-six, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and forty-two days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and fifty-six, and five hundred and seventeen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about nine, in about 42 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twelve and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Two mornings ago a man answered a question in that yard and said out loud that there is no question, and yesterday a road keeper answered a question in that yard and said out loud that about a mile and a half of road has gone, and this morning I have been asked by nobody what I think of either of those, and about four of you have worked out that there is a person in that yard who everybody in it would ask and that she has not been asked yet, and that is me, and I would like it entered that I have noticed it myself before anybody said it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she would be asked and had not been, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up put the besom down against the leg of that first table at about half past nine and said one thing out loud, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been putting tables up since I was thirteen and I have never once been sent anywhere by anything I wrote down, and on the sixth of this month somebody wrote four lines on a page in a clerk's hand and a stranger walked two days and stood in that yard, and about four of you have worked out that I have been in this yard every one of those mornings and that I was not sent, and that is the whole of what I have thought about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he was not sent, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about ten with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 36 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man of about thirty-two has just said out loud in that yard that he has never been sent anywhere by anything he wrote down, and about four of you have worked out that I have been sent, and about four of you have worked out that I have not been sent once, and that every man who has come down that lane this month has been sent by four lines on a page, and I am going to stand here this morning and not say anything else until I have thought about that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not saying anything else until he has thought about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen came down that lane at about eleven and said one thing out loud, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I was at the top of that lane from about six this morning and I did not count it, and a man of about fifty told this yard on the third of this month that he could see me from about nine hundred yards off, and about four of you have worked out that I have been standing where a person could see me from nine hundred yards for a month and never once wondered whether anybody was, and that is the second thing this month I have found out about myself from a mouth and not from counting.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not count that lane this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about half past eleven and stood at the near end of that second table with his right hand flat on the boards and said one thing out loud, in about 40 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk has said out loud in that yard this morning that she would be asked what she thinks and has not been, and a boy has said out loud that he has been standing where anybody could see him for a month, and about four of you have worked out that both of those people are telling this yard they would rather be asked, and about four of you have worked out that I asked one man one question on the second of this month and asked him nothing else since, and that I have not been asked anything either, and that is not a complaint.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that it is not a complaint, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then at about twelve the man of about thirty-one who digs loam came the length of that second table and stopped about nine feet off the man of about thirty-four and asked him one thing out loud, in about 3 seconds, to a man about nine feet away from him, and the boy counted what he said and got four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**What do you think.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing took his right hand off the boards and said one thing out loud, in about 35 seconds, to about nineteen people and to one man about nine feet away from him, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I think I should not be asked and I am going to answer anyway, and about four of you have worked out that the man who should be asked is standing at the top of that lane, and I think I have known since the second of this month what I would say if a person in this yard asked me that, and I have said it out loud twice since, and this is the third time and I am saying it to the man the page is about and not to you.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he said it to one man and not to the yard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that the man who digs loam asked a man what he thought and the man answered, and that about four of them have said that the man who asked it has not answered a question in a month, and that about four of them have said that the man who answered it has not been asked anything since the second of this month, and that about four of them have said that those two facts have been true at the same time in that yard for four days.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he was asked a question in that yard on the morning before and that he has come up that lane twice a day since and that about four of them have said that a man who has answered one question in thirteen volumes has now had somebody ask him a second thing, and that the second thing is whether it was the frost. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and put the shafts down across the track at the top of it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man was asked what he thought in that yard this morning and answered, and that the man on those stones has not been asked what he thinks in about two volumes, and that about four of them have said that he is the only person in that yard nobody has ever needed an answer from. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and sixty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and sixty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and seventy-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and thirty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and thirty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. It is three hundred and six days since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and forty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and eighty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and seventy-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man being asked what he thinks is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a person being asked is a thing a person does and that this district has four columns of not asking and no column for an answer. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and seventeen.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and twenty-six mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man saying what he thinks out loud did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that the man in that yard has still said no to nobody, and that about four people at that wall have said since that the fourth of the five has been the same man since a page in this yard first carried one. About six things were said out loud in that yard today, one of them a question and one of them the answer to it, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and the man who asked what he thought is not entered in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who digs loam being asked a question in that yard this morning is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man in that yard was asked what he thinks and answered out loud, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six and did not stop and did not look, and that about four of them have said that there is now a man in that yard who has said what he thinks and a woman who reads three lines every evening about a book and has never once said what she thinks. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man was asked what he thinks in that yard and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fourth of the sixth month that a man asked a man what he thought and that the man answered, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is four and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about ten past twelve with his right hand off the boards for the first time this month.
