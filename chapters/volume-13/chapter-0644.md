# Chapter 644: The Count Rose To Eight On The Twentieth Because A Person Came Down That Lane And Said A Thing Out Loud In That Yard

There was no cloud on that bank by about seven on the twentieth of the sixth month and the boards of that second table had gone the colour of a bone, and there was a smell coming off the low wall where the sun had been on the stones since eight that about four people said they could get at the top of the lane, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and forty-two mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and eighty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seven at a quarter to eight on its forty-fourth morning, and entered that she is going to enter it again this afternoon and that a person and not a clock is what will decide the figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-ninth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he got all four of them again this morning with the sun straight on that wall, and that about four of them have said that two of the four have been out for longer than anybody in that yard has been counting, and that about four of them have said that nobody has worked out which two.

"**The board carries five hundred and forty-two, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and fifty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seventy-two, and five hundred and thirty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had been at the near end of that second table since a quarter to eight with his own two hands in his own pockets, and he said one thing out loud at about ten, in about 38 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There has been a person standing at the top of that lane for about twenty minutes and has not come down it and has not gone back up it either, and I have not gone up there to look, and about four of you have worked out that that is the first morning in this month that I have watched the top of that lane instead of the boards, and about four of you have worked out that I have watched it because I could hear somebody standing on the stones at the top of it and not moving, and I do not know what a person stands at the top of a lane for.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not go up to the top of that lane to look, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then a man of about forty-one came down that lane at about ten with a folded page in his own right hand, and about nine people at the top of that lane turned round, and he came up that bank and stopped about five feet short of the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 42 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twelve and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been at the top of that lane about twenty minutes and I have got a page in my hand and I have not opened it since I came over that rise, and I have been reading what is on it in my own head for two days, and I came down this lane because it told me to come down this lane, and I have not come down it to find out whether the page came out of this yard, and I have not come down it to hand it to anybody, and I have come down it because it says there is a man at the end of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person came down that lane at about ten on the twentieth of the sixth month with a page in their hand and entered that they came down that lane and that they had a page in their hand and the hour, and entered that she is not entering his name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That figure is eight, and it was seven at a quarter to eight this morning, and I am entering the reason now and the reason is that a person said a thing out loud in this yard, and that is the whole of why it moved, and about four of you have worked out that I could not have written the eight down before he said it and that I was not going to try, and about four of you have worked out that a page a person has carried down a lane in his own hand is not a fifth of the four documents this district does not own, and I am entering that in the same breath because somebody in this yard will ask me for it this week.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eight and moved this morning with a person in the room and the reason in the room before the hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man of about forty-one came down that lane at about ten with a page in his own hand and said out loud that he had not come down it to find out whether the page came out of this yard, and that about four of them have said that a man has said that in that yard and that about four of them have said that every person who has come down that lane in a month has said some version of it, and that about four of them have said that the clerk entered the figure before she entered the reason and that the reason says the figure moved because somebody spoke, and that about four of them have said that no figure in that yard has ever moved because of that before.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man stood at the top of that lane for about twenty minutes this morning and that the road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and went past the same place and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that about a mile and a half of that road has gone since the frost and that nobody in this yard has ever asked him which night it went on.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and went on up that bank and did not stop at the top of it and did not look at anybody standing in that yard.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man came down that lane this morning with a page in his hand and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing in two hundred and eighty-three nights, and that about four of them have said that a person who comes down a lane with a page in his hand gets a figure entered against him in an afternoon and that the man on those stones has not had a figure entered against him in two hundred and eighty-three nights, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with that difference. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and eighty-third night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and eighty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and ninety-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and forty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fifty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and twenty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and twenty-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and sixty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and eighty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a person coming down that lane is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a person who comes down a lane is a person and not a thing a district has made, and that the count of the things this district has made has not moved on any morning of this month including today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and thirty-three.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and a page carried down a lane in a man's own hand is not a fifth of them, and a clerk entered that with the figure. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and forty-two mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and a person saying out loud that a page told him to come down a lane did not move it, because a condition with no end on it is a thing a document carries and a person carrying a page down a bank is a thing a person carries. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and the count of that lane moving to eight this morning did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that nobody has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for on any morning of this month, and that a person saying out loud that he has come down a lane because a page said so is not that thing. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man of about forty-one who came down that lane this morning is not in it and is not a resident.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and eight people who have come down a lane with a page in their hand and been answered by a man this district cannot pay is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man came down that lane this morning with a page in his hand and said out loud that he had not come to hand it to anybody, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and that about four of them have said that a person came down that bank this morning holding the page the whole way and that she has never once held anything in her hands at that book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-ninth morning, and that a figure in that yard moved this morning for the first time in three days, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two, and that about four of them have said that he has never once asked why a figure in that yard moved or stood still.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twentieth of the sixth month that a man of about forty-one came down that lane at about ten with a page in his hand and said one thing out loud in that yard, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eight and moved this morning, and that the reason it moved is that a person said a thing out loud in that yard, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about ten and had said out loud that he had watched the top of that lane instead of the boards.
