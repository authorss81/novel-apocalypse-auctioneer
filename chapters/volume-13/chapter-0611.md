# Chapter 611: The Fourth Of The Four Figures On That Wall Came To Five Hundred Days And About Four People Noticed And None Of Them Said Why And The Number Of People Who Have Come Down That Lane Was Zero And Did Not Move On Its Eleventh Morning

The boards of that second table were cold enough at about a quarter to seven on the eighteenth of the fifth month that about four people put a hand flat on them and took it off again, and the hollow in the middle of the stone lying face up on that table was dry and had been dry for two days, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane and had stood there and had not gone down it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and nine mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and fifty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move on its eleventh morning, and entered that the fourth of the four figures on that wall is five hundred days this morning and that she is entering it as a figure and is not entering anything else, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and thirty-sixth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that the fourth of the four figures on that wall came to five hundred days this morning and that all four of them noticed and that none of them said one word about it to anybody, including the clerk, and that about four of them have said that the man who said them said the four and then said them again at about four and got them again.

"**The board carries five hundred and nine, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and twenty-five days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and thirty-nine, and five hundred days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about half past eight and went to that wall and stood in front of it and read the four off it there, about nine feet from where he reads them every morning, and said one thing out loud, in about 23 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read those four off that wall about nine feet from it every morning for eleven days and this morning I walked over and read them off it from about two feet and I got the same four and I want it entered that I did that on purpose and that I do not know what I was checking.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering what he was checking, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter to ten, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The fourth of the four figures on that wall is five hundred days this morning and I entered it and I am not entering the size of anything and about four of you have been waiting about three weeks for me to say one word about that figure and I am not going to and this morning I am saying why and the reason is that a clerk who says it in a yard is a clerk who has said it twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the size of the error in the fourth of the four figures on that wall, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book shut under his left arm and said one thing out loud at about ten, in about 39 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said in that yard three mornings ago that I would tell a stranger to go and ask the man by the water and I have shut that book and I have not written it down and I am telling you this morning that I have changed my mind about saying that out loud again, and about four of you have worked out that I am doing it because a clerk has just said out loud that saying a thing once in a yard is a thing she cannot do twice, and that is the reason and not the other one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about eleven with her own door open behind her and said one thing out loud, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have told nine people where that man is this fortnight and I have been wrong about it every single time because I have not seen him, and I have been standing in my own shop saying a thing about a man nine feet away from a man I do not know, and I am going to keep saying it and I am not going to be right about it and I am not going to be thanked for that either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the door of that shop was open behind her the whole time she said it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about 23 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Four hundred and ninety-nine yesterday and five hundred today and that is the first figure I have watched cross anything in about two volumes, and about four of you looked at that wall this morning and about four of you did not say anything and I counted that too and it is not a number I am going to say out loud.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he said he counted before anybody in that yard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud in that yard this morning that she is not going to say the size of the error in the fourth of the four figures on that wall and gave the reason, which is that a clerk who says it in a yard is a clerk who has said it twice, and that about four of them have said that is the first time in this district's history that anybody has given a reason for not saying a figure out loud that was not about a name.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that the fourth of those four figures came to five hundred this morning and that the road keeper said all four of them as he went past without stopping and without looking at the wall, and that about four of them have said that he has read that figure off that wall about three hundred and thirty-six times and that this is the first round one of them has ever come to. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a figure on that wall came to five hundred this morning and that the man on those stones has a number too and that about four of them have said it is two hundred and forty-nine nights and that nobody has ever asked him what it is a number of and neither has anybody else. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fiftieth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and forty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and fifty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and fourteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and nineteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and eighty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and ninety-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and twenty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and sixty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and fifty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a figure coming to a round number is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with it, which is that a figure crossing nothing is a figure crossing nothing. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and nine mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a clerk saying out loud that she is not going to say a figure twice did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is not about a clerk. About five things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a woman saying out loud that she has been wrong nine times and will go on being wrong is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man read the four off that wall from two feet this morning instead of nine and got the same four, and that the woman who reads three lines every evening read three this evening from about a foot away and got the same three, and that about four of them have said that about four people in this district read the same thing every morning from different distances and none of them has said so. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that the fourth of those four figures came to five hundred days this morning and that he read it twice as he reads it every morning and that nobody asked him anything about it and he has not been asked twice about anything by anybody in that yard in about three weeks.

The fourth of the four figures on that wall came to five hundred days on the eighteenth of the fifth month and about four people in that yard noticed and none of them said why, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is zero and did not move on its eleventh morning, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at that wall at about half past eight with his right hand not on it and not on either table and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
