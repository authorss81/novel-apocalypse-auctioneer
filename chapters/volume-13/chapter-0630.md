# Chapter 630: The Block Of Fifteen Mornings Ended On The Sixth Of The Sixth Month With The Fifth Line Working Exactly As It Was Written And With Nobody In That Yard Having Asked The Man It Names Anything About What He Is To Be Used For

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the sixth of the sixth month and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had cleared the gap between the two tables of the grit that had been standing in it for three days and had gone to the bottom of that bank with it, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and twenty-eight mornings, and nobody in that yard put a hand on either of them at any hour of that day.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and sixty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is five and did not move on its thirtieth morning, and entered that a man said out loud in that yard yesterday that he was never told what a line would be used for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fifty-fifth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has stopped him on any of them, and that about four of them have said that a clerk has entered five of the last six days as days on which a person came or was found out, and that this is the first morning this month that nothing in that yard has been entered but figures.

"**The board carries five hundred and twenty-eight, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and forty-four days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and fifty-eight, and five hundred and nineteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about nine, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A month ago this morning the count of that lane was zero and I entered it at a quarter to eight and said nothing out loud about it, and this morning it is five and I have entered five, and about four of you have worked out that five is a number this district can hold in its head, and about four of you have worked out that I said out loud yesterday morning that I had decided two figures are not the same thing, and that is the only thing I have decided in a month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her count is five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 30 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Five people have come down that lane with a page in their hand this month and there are five lines on that book and nobody has ever asked me whether those are the same five, and about four of you have worked out that I have wanted to ask that all month and have not, and that is the same thing a boy said in that yard about three weeks ago, and I have not been asked about that either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has wanted to ask a question and has not, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 44 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fourteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Five people have come down that lane this month and every one of them has been answered by a man who has not been asked what he is for, and about four of you have worked out that a man who has been answered five times is still a man who has not been asked, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth line in that book says ask that man and not ask anybody else, and that it does not say what about, and it has not been altered this morning and I have not touched it and nobody in this yard has put a hand on it this month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody in that yard has put a hand on that line this month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 39 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been standing at this end of this table every morning of this month and about four of you have been waiting for one of you to ask me what I am to be used for so that I could say no to it, and nobody has, and I am not going to help anybody along by saying something that would make it easier, and about four of you have worked out that a man who has said no to nobody is not a man who is owed an answer, and that is what I am and it has been all month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody asked him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about twelve, in about 44 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and seventeen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted that yard four times this morning and got the same number four times, and the count of that lane has been five since yesterday, and I have said out loud in that yard on two mornings this month that I would stop standing in the same place at the same hour, and I am standing in the same place at the same hour, and about four of you have worked out that I told you I would do a thing and I did not do it, and that about four of you have worked out that this is the first time in a month that I have not been able to give you a reason.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is standing in the same place at the same hour, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man said out loud in that yard this morning that nobody has asked him what he is to be used for, and that about four of them have said that five people have come down that lane this month and that not one of them asked him that, and that about four of them have said that the fifth line in that book has done exactly what it says and that the reason it has is that it does not say anything else.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he was asked one question in that yard on the third of this month and that he has come up that lane twice a day since, and that about four of them have said that about a mile and a half of that road has gone since the frost and that the man who mends fencing has still not been up it. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four with his back to the yard. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man in that yard has said a great deal out loud this month and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing on any of the mornings of it, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has counted one of the mornings of that run of his and that about four of them have said that a clerk enters his nights at about four of the afternoon and that nobody has ever asked her to. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and sixty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and sixty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and seventy-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and thirty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and thirty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and eleven days back, which is the same figure as the one on the sheet at that gatepost, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk wrote that same figure on two lines of her own book this morning and that about four of them have said that a figure is not a figure because it agrees with another figure. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and forty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and eighty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and seventy-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying that nobody has asked him anything is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man saying he has not been asked is a thing a man says and that the count of the things this district has made has not moved on any morning of this month including today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and nineteen.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and twenty-eight mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open. The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and nobody in that yard said so out loud this morning. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and five people coming down a lane and a man answering them all did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that the man in that yard has not said no to anybody on any morning of this month. About six things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who has said a great deal out loud this month is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who is findable on a page and has been answered five times and has said no to nobody is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man in that yard said out loud this morning that nobody has asked him what he is to be used for, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six with eleven buckets on her hip and did not stop and did not look, and that about four of them have said that she has read that book four hundred mornings and that a book with five lines on it has been in that yard all of them, and that about four of them have said that she reads three of the five and that the two she does not read are the fourth and the fifth, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked her about either of them. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fifty-fifth morning, and that about four of them have said that a clerk will enter this afternoon that the man of fifty-six has said four figures off that wall three hundred and fifty-five times, and that a clerk will not enter that he has said them out loud, because nobody has asked her to.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the sixth of the sixth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that table for a month working exactly as it was written and that nobody in that yard has asked the man it names anything about what he is to be used for, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is five, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and his other hand at his side.
