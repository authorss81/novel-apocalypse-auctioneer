# Chapter 645: A Clerk Of Nineteen Years Said The Word Remedy Out Loud In That Yard On The Twenty-First And Nothing In That Yard Moved

The sun was off that bank by about seven on the twenty-first of the sixth month and there was low grey cloud sitting on the far side of the water, and the boards of that second table had gone cold and dark the colour of a wet slate and about four people in that yard had a wet coat on by half past eight, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and forty-three mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and eighty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eight and did not move on its forty-fifth morning, and entered that she was going to say one word out loud in that yard this morning and had entered that before she said it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventieth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he said the fourth one on a morning when the sun was not on that wall at all, and that about four of them have said that nobody has ever checked that a man can do it in the dark as well as in the light, and that about four of them have said that checking him would be asking him something.

"**The board carries five hundred and forty-three, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and fifty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seventy-three, and five hundred and thirty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been writing the word remedy in that book every morning of this month and I have never once said it out loud in this yard, and I am saying it out loud this morning, and a remedy is the fifth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and it is unpaid, and it has been unpaid on every morning that this book has stood on this table, and what that fifth line does is tell a person with a page in his hand where to go and who to ask, and that is the whole of what it does, and I have said the word and nothing in this yard has moved, and that is what I wanted to find out, and I am entering that I wanted to find it out and not that I found it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she said the word remedy out loud for the first time this month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about ten with the wet of the ditch dried back off his own boots and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 30 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That book has five lines on it and I have read four of them about four hundred times and I have not read the fifth one this month once, because it is mine and I know what it says, and about four of you have worked out that a man who wrote one line out of five knows that line better than anybody in this yard does and has still not read it out loud to himself, and I have not read it to myself and I am not going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has not read the fifth line of that book this month and is not going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about half past ten with his own right hand still in a cold coat pocket and said one thing out loud, in about 53 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk has said out loud this morning that a remedy is the fifth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and about four of you have worked out that I have heard that word off the end of her book every morning of this month and have never once heard it in a mouth, and about four of you have worked out that a word is a different size of thing in a book than it is in a mouth, and I am not going to say out loud this morning which of the two I think is the true one, and about four of you have worked out that that is the first thing I have said in this yard in a fortnight that I have not said the end of.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not finish it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk stood at the near end of that first table this morning and said the word remedy out loud in that yard for the first time this month, and that about four of them have said that the count of that lane did not move, and that about four of them have said that the count of the things this district has made did not move, and that about four of them have said that the count of conditions with no end on it did not move, and that about four of them have said that a woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six yesterday and read three lines of that book standing up and said nothing to anybody and that about four of them have said that the word remedy has now been said out loud in that yard and that the one person in that yard who reads that book every evening did not hear it because she was not there.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk said a word out loud in that yard this morning for the first time this month, and that the road keeper came up that lane afterwards and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that he read the four figures off that wall as he went past with no sun on it and got all four of them, and that about four of them have said that a man who gets a figure right in the dark has never been asked how.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four with his own back to that yard and his own right hand on the near shaft.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a word was said out loud in that yard this morning that has been written in a book at the end of that table every morning for forty-five mornings, and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing in two hundred and eighty-four nights, and that about four of them have said that a man who is given nothing and asked nothing is the only person in that yard who could have been asked about a word and was not, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever put those two facts next to each other in a mouth. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and eighty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and eighty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and ninety-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and forty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fifty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and twenty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and twenty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and sixty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and eighty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk saying the word remedy out loud in that yard is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a word is a thing a person says and a thing this district has made is a thing four people have argued about for six volumes, and the count of the things this district has made has not moved on any morning of this month including today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and thirty-four.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and forty-three mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a clerk saying the word out loud did not pay it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that the word being said in that yard this morning is not the same thing as the thing being done in that yard, and that the man at the end of that line has said no to nobody. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a clerk is not in it for saying a word she has been writing for forty-five mornings.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk saying the fifth of the five out loud in that yard is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a clerk said the word remedy out loud this morning in that yard, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six in the wet and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and that about four of them have said that there are five lines in that book and that she has read three of them standing up for about four hundred mornings, and that about four of them have said that a clerk has now said a word out loud in that yard about what the fourth of those five is and that nobody has said a word to the woman about any of the five.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventieth morning, and that a word was said out loud in that yard this morning which has been in a book at the end of that table every morning for forty-five mornings, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four with no sun on it and got all four, and that about four of them have said that he is the only person in that yard who reads a thing out loud every morning that nobody has ever asked him to read.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-first of the sixth month that a clerk of nineteen years said the word remedy out loud in that yard at about half past nine and that nothing moved, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eight and did not move, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about half past ten and had not finished what he was saying.
