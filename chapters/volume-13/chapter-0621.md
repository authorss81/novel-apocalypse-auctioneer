# Chapter 621: A Clerk Of Nineteen Years Said Out Loud On The Twenty-Eighth Of The Fifth Month One Thing About The Column For The Name Of Whoever Read A Thing Out Loud That She Had Not Said In This Volume And Entered That She Had Thought About Entering Something And Had Not

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the twenty-eighth of the fifth month and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was under that table with a wedge of wood in his own teeth working at the near leg, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and nineteen mornings. The table moved about half an inch when the man under it drove the wedge home and both pages went with it and neither of them slid.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about a quarter to eight, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. Entered three hundred and sixty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move. Entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is two and did not move on its twenty-first morning. Entered that a table in that yard moved half an inch this morning and that a figure is not a figure of the yard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and forty-sixth of those mornings. About four of them have said that a table moved half an inch this morning and that nothing on it moved with it. About four of them have said that is the first morning this month that two things in that yard happened at once and neither one of them was a person saying anything.

"**The board carries five hundred and nineteen, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and thirty-five days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and forty-nine, and five hundred and ten days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about nine, before she had written anything else that morning, in about 38 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have said out loud in this volume that the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is something a person looks down at, and that I have no standing over a man who is not from this parish, and I have never once said out loud why I keep it ruled and empty on the mornings when it would be easy to fill, and the reason is that I have been keeping it empty for thirteen volumes on purpose and the purpose is older than the standing and I do not have words for the purpose.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she keeps a column empty on purpose, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came out from under that second table at about ten with the wedge in his own hand and put it in the pocket of his own coat and said one thing out loud, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been under that table about forty minutes and I could see about half of that yard from down there and what I could see was a clerk's elbow and about nine people's boots, and I am going to say out loud that a clerk who keeps a column empty for thirteen volumes on purpose is not a person with a reason, she is a person with a habit, and that nobody in that yard has ever told those two things apart.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he was under her own table, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about forty-three was at the far end of that yard at about half past ten with the page still folded in four and said one thing out loud, in about 40 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody in this yard has asked me one question and I have been in this parish three days and a man asked me nothing in that shop on the morning before and a woman in that shop told a man the same thing six times and did not tell me anything, and about four of you have noticed that I am the only person in this yard that everybody has decided not to ask, and I have not worked out yet whether that is a kindness or whether it is a thing this district does with a person it has no standing over.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody in that yard has asked her a question, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven and stood at the near end of that second table with his own hands in his own pockets and said one thing out loud, in about 45 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and nineteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk has said out loud that she has kept a column empty for thirteen volumes on purpose and has not got words for the purpose, and I am going to say the thing that nobody in this yard is going to say, which is that I have watched that column every morning this month and there is a morning coming when somebody is going to read a thing out loud in that yard and the column is going to be the right place for their name, and about four of you have worked out that I have not said one word to her about it and I have not said one word to anybody else about it either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he said nothing to her, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the knee of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 43 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has just told this yard that a morning is coming when somebody will say a thing out loud and a clerk will have a place to put their name in, and about four of you have worked out that he said that to a woman who has told us she has no words for why she keeps it empty, and about four of you have worked out that he did not say it to me and that I am the only person in that yard that a name could go beside, and I am not going to say a word about that and I want it entered that I did not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he said nothing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about twelve, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk has said out loud that she keeps a column empty on purpose and a man has said out loud that a morning is coming, and about four of you have worked out that those are the only two things said out loud in this yard this month that are about something that has not happened yet, and that before that it was one, and I have been counting since the first of this month and I did not know that until this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then a clerk of nineteen years entered, and what she entered was this, that on the morning of the twenty-eighth of the fifth month she had thought about entering a page in the column for documents this district does not own, and had not done it, and entered the reason, which is that a person brought it up that lane and there is a person and a reason in the room, and that entering it would have been saying that this district is holding a thing it has not been asked to hold, and that nobody in that yard has asked her to hold it, and that a page lying folded in four in a woman's own lap is not a thing anybody in this parish has agreed to anything about, and about four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk has said out loud that she does not have words for what she has been doing for thirteen volumes and that a man has said out loud that a morning is coming when somebody will need it.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and went on up that lane past the wall. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk has said out loud this morning that she has no words for why she does a thing she has done for thirteen volumes and that the man on those stones has not needed a reason for anything in about two volumes. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and sixtieth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and fifty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and sixty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and twenty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and twenty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and ninety-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and thirty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and seventy-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and sixty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and the table itself moved about half an inch at about ten and both pages went with it and neither page slid. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk saying out loud that she has not got words for something is not a fourteenth thing. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and ten.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and a page folded in four in a woman's own lap is not a fifth of them and a clerk entered that reason with the figure, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and nineteen mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a clerk saying out loud that she has no words for a purpose did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that a clerk saying she does not know why she does a thing is not that person and is not anybody's no. About six things were said out loud in that yard today and one thing was entered that was not said, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who has said out loud in that yard this month that a morning is coming when a name will go in it is not entered in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk who has kept a column empty on purpose for thirteen volumes is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was named in that yard this morning by a man and by a clerk and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past it at about ten to six and said nothing to anybody about it and did not stop. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that she has been keeping a column empty on purpose and has no words for it and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-eighth of the fifth month that she had thought about entering a page and had not and entered the reason, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is two and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his own hands in his own pockets and about four feet further back from the end of it than he stood on the two mornings before.
