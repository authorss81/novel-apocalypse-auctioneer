# Chapter 632: A Man Of About Thirty-Two Who Puts Tables Up Put One Question In That Yard On The Eighth Of The Sixth Month And The Man It Was Put To Answered It And A Clerk Entered A Question For The First Time In Nineteen Years

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the eighth of the sixth month and there was about a shovelful of grey grit standing in the gap between the two tables where it has been standing since the cart came up that bank, and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had looked down into that gap twice before eight and had not touched it. About four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and thirty mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and seventy-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move. Entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is five at about a quarter to eight on its thirty-second morning, and entered that it did not move. Entered that a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said out loud in that yard yesterday morning that he had one question and had been carrying it about a week, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fifty-seventh of those mornings. About four of them have said that a clerk of nineteen years entered a question and an answer on her own page for the first time this morning and that about four of them have said that a page which has carried a record of what was not asked for nineteen years now carries two things that were asked, and that nobody has decided what to call that.

"**The board carries five hundred and thirty, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and forty-six days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and sixty, and five hundred and twenty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had the loose leg of that first table wedged with a piece of slate at about eight and got a piece of chalk out of his own pocket and put a small mark on the slate, and then at about ten he said one thing out loud to a man standing at the far end of that second table with his back to about nine people, in about 3 seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**What do you say to one of them.**"

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam turned round at the far end of that table with mud dried to a crust on the thigh of his own right leg, and said one thing out loud, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Whatever is true at the time. This month that has been the same eleven words five times, and I have not enjoyed saying it and I have not minded it either, and about four of you have worked out that a man who says the same thing five times has not had five conversations, he has had one, and that I am the one having it, and about four of you have worked out that I would rather it was a bad conversation than a fifth of nothing.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was asked one thing out loud in that yard and entered the question and entered that a man answered it and entered the answer, and entered that she has entered both of them on her own page in her own hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nineteen years of entries on that page in front of me have been a record of what people did not say, and this morning there are two things on it that people did say, and about four of you have worked out that I have not decided what to do with that, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to decide it out loud before the next person in this yard has had a turn at it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she has not decided what a page with a question on it is, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven with his own pincers in his own hand and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 35 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man put a question in that yard this morning and the man he put it to answered it in about half a minute, and about four of you have worked out that the question was not what I would have asked, and that I have had five weeks to work out what I would have asked, and about four of you have worked out that what I would have asked is a worse question and that I have known that since the first morning I stood at this end of this table.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he would have asked a worse question, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came down that lane at about half past eleven and stood at the top of that bank with his binder shut and looked at the two tables for about a minute and said nothing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man stood at the top of that bank for about a minute and entered that she is not entering what he made of a question, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up carried a question up and down that bank for a week and put it in that yard at about ten this morning, and that about four of them have said that the question was four words and that the answer was longer than anybody in that yard expected, and that about four of them have said that a man who puts tables up has never in nineteen years asked one question out loud in front of a crowd and that about four of them have said that he put a chalk mark on a piece of slate under a loose leg at about eight this morning and that nobody has asked him what the mark is for.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one with his hat in his own hand and read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a question was asked in that yard this morning and that the road keeper came up that lane afterwards and was not asked one, and that about four of them have said that he read the four figures off that wall as he went by the way he does and that about four of them have said that there is a question in that yard now and that it was not his.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stood there until about four looking down that lane. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a question was put and answered in that yard this morning in front of about nineteen people, and that the man on those stones was not there for it and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever told him there was a question. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and seventy-first night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and seventy of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and eighty days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and thirty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and forty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and ten days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and thirteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named, and about four people near that wall have said since that it is a figure this district will have to keep reading out loud for a long time and that about four of them have said that nobody has said a night. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and forty-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and eighty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and seventy-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a question put in a yard is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a question is a thing a person says and that this district has had questions in it before a clerk came and that the count of the things this district has made has not moved on any morning of this month including today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and twenty-one.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and thirty mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open and the woman who keeps the scale under it, and nobody in that yard asked her one thing this morning about any of the three of them.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move on the thirty-second morning, and nobody in that yard has said it out loud on any morning of this month. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a question and an answer in a yard did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that the man who was asked a question this morning has not said no to the man who asked it. About four things were said out loud in that yard today, one of them a question and one of them an answer, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who carried a question about in his own pocket for a week is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who has been asked one question in that yard this month and has answered it is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up asked a man who digs loam a question in that yard this morning and that the answer was that he has said the same eleven words five times, and that about four of them have said that a man who has said the same eleven words five mornings running is a man who has not had anybody ask him a second question yet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and that about four of them have said that a clerk entered a question and an answer on that page this morning for the first time, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall twice and was not told about either of the two.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the eighth of the sixth month that a man was asked a thing in that yard and answered it, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is five and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his own pincers shut in his own hand.
