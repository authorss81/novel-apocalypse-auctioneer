# Chapter 615: A Man With A Page In His Hand Said A Thing Out Loud In That Yard And A Clerk Entered That He Said It And Entered That She Is Not Entering Him In The Column For The Name Of Whoever Read A Thing Out Loud

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the twenty-second of the fifth month for the first time in four days and the hollow in the middle of the stone lying face up on that table was dry with it, and a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had put his own thumb in it and found nothing and wiped his thumb down his own leg and stood looking down that lane for about a minute, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane and had not come back down it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and thirteen mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and fifty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is one and did not move, and entered that a man did not answer a question in that yard yesterday and that she is not entering a figure about that, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fortieth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that the fourth of the four figures on that wall came to five hundred days four mornings ago and that four people in that yard have walked past that wall every morning since and looked at it and said nothing, and that about four of them have said that a person who has been right about four figures three hundred and forty times running is the last person anybody in that yard should be expecting to hear a word from about anything.

"**The board carries five hundred and thirteen, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and twenty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and forty-three, and five hundred and four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man with the page came up that bank at about ten and stood about six feet short of that second table in the same place he has stood twice and said one thing out loud, in about 36 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to say this once in that yard and then I am going to take that page down that lane and put it in the water and go home, and I have not worked out whether I am saying it because it is true or because I cannot carry it any more, and I would like somebody in that yard to tell me which of those it is and about four of you have been standing here for a fortnight and one of you put that line on that book.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering his name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 41 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man said a thing out loud in that yard this morning and I have entered it, and I am going to say the other thing now and I have thought about it since about eight. I am not entering him in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the reason is that column is something a person looks down and that man is not from this parish and I have no standing over him at all, and about four of you have been waiting for me to give a reason and that is the first one that is about me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the reason, and entered that the column was ruled and empty at about six on this morning as it has been on every morning of this volume, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven and went to the near end of that second table and stood there with his right hand not on anything and said one thing out loud, in about 35 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has just told this yard that he is going to put a page in the water and go home and that he does not know whether he is saying it because it is true or because he cannot carry it, and about four of you have worked out that those are the same thing and that this district has spent six volumes saying they are not, and I am not going to say anything this morning because I do not know which of them he is asking.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering what he does not know, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to say yes and I am not going to say no and about four of you have been waiting sixteen days for me to say one or the other and I have read that page every morning since it went on and I am telling you this morning that reading it has not told me anything, and if a man asks me tomorrow I am going to tell him that, and that is what I have.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that it is a thing a man would say to a stranger, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about twelve, in about 36 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted the yard before the clerk had her book open and it is the same number I have got for four weeks and I am telling you this morning that I am going to stop, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to stop counting and I am going to stop standing in the same place at the same hour to do it, and I am not going to say why, and I have never not said why before and about four of you have noticed.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered that she is not entering why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then nobody in that yard said one word for about two minutes, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that the longest anybody in that yard has stood in since a man asked a man a question on the second of this month was about two minutes, and that the man who has been standing in the middle of it did not look at anybody, and that about four of them have said that he has done that three times in sixteen days, and that the third of those was the morning he asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man with a page in his hand has been in that yard three times this week and that the road keeper comes up that lane twice a day and has not been asked one question about any of it and has not asked one about it either. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stood there about a minute. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man with a page in his hand stood in that yard this morning and said he was going to put it in the water and go home, and that the man on those stones has been at that low wall every morning of that week, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has offered either of them anything and that about four of them have said that is the correct way round. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fifty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and fifty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and sixty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and eighteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and twenty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and ninety-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and ninety-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and thirty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and seventy-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and fifty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying he will put a page in the water is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a man deciding what to do with a sheet of paper is a thing a man does and this district has never entered one and is not going to start this month. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and four.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and thirteen mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man saying he will put a page in the water did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and a man saying he is going to walk away is not that person saying anything. About six things were said out loud in that yard today, one of them by a man who is not from this parish, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a stranger who says a thing out loud in that yard is not entered in it, and about four people in that yard have worked out that this column is now emptier than it was in the first week of this volume and that that is what this column does and that about four of them have said so out loud in that yard.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who is findable on a page and has been asked to confirm that he is findable and has not said yes is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said in that yard this morning that he was going to put a page in the water and go home and that the woman who has read three lines in that book every evening for about four months walked up past that yard at about ten to six with eleven buckets on her hip and did not stop and did not look, and that about four of them have said that she has read that book four hundred mornings and has not once said she was going to burn it. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fortieth morning and that a man with a page in his hand has been in that yard three times and that the man of fifty-six has read four figures off that wall on every one of the three and has not spoken to him on any of them and that about four of them have said that neither of them is going to be spoken to and that about four of them have said that this is the first pair of people in that yard in about two volumes who have both been told about the other and have both said nothing.

A man with a page in his hand said a thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-second of the fifth month and said he would say it once and then take that page down that lane and put it in the water, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and entered that she is not entering him in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is one and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his right hand not on anything at all.
