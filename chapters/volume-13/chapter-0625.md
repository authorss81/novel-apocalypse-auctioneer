# Chapter 625: A Man Who Cuts Reeds Asked A Question In That Yard On The First Of The Sixth Month At About Eleven That Nobody In This District Has Asked In Thirteen Volumes And Nobody Answered It For Eleven Seconds And Then A Man Answered It Out Loud

The boards of that second table had a skin of water on them again by about seven on the first of the sixth month, and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had walked the stone that lies face up on the far end of that table back the foot he had moved it on the morning before, and had got both hands under it and had set it down against the chalk and had left it there, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and twenty-three mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and sixty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is three and did not move on its twenty-fifth morning, and entered that a month went yesterday and that she entered no figure about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fiftieth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that he has now said four figures off that wall three hundred and fifty times without a mistake, and that about four of them have said that a month in that yard ended yesterday without a figure in it and that the man of fifty-six did not notice and that about four of them have said that neither of those two things is a thing to notice.

"**The board carries five hundred and twenty-three, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and thirty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and fifty-three, and five hundred and fourteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to nine with his own binder shut in front of him and both his hands flat on it and said one thing out loud, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I stood at that gate at the ninth mile at about six every morning this month and this morning I am in this yard instead, and I have not been to that gate this morning and I have not sent anybody to it, and I am not going to say why, and about four of you have worked out that a man who stood at one end of a road for a month has come to this end of it and has brought nothing with him and has not been asked a single question about it by anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he was not at that gate this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about nine, in about 40 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I did not count that month yesterday and I have been asked about it by nobody, and this morning I have entered that a month went and no figure, and I am going to say the thing I have not said and then I am going to do the rest of the day, and it is this. A month that has ended is the only kind of month in this district that is the same to everybody in it, and it is the only kind I have ever been able to enter, and there is one of them a month and one of them is not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she has not been asked about the month by anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about ten with his own hands empty and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has moved himself from one end of a road to the other in a night and has brought nothing and has not been asked, and I have been at this end of that road every morning this month and have asked nobody one question since the second, and about four of you have worked out that between the two of us this yard has two men in it who have said nothing to anybody for a month, and that about four of you have worked out that I put one of them there.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has asked nobody one question since the second of this month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past ten with mud to the knee of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 42 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and ten and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A month went yesterday and nobody in this yard counted it and I have been asked about that by nobody, and I am going to say the one thing I have been carrying up that bank for a month and then I am going to stand here, and it is this. I did not ask to be on a page and I said so out loud in this yard and I am not taking it back, and about four of you have worked out that a man can say the same thing twice and mean less the second time, and I am not going to argue with you about that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not taking anything back, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then nobody in that yard said anything at all for about two minutes, and the man who cuts reeds stood at the near end of that first table with both his hands on his binder and did not look at anybody, and the boy of about nineteen came down that lane and stood in the middle of that yard, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that the longest anybody in that yard has gone without speaking is the next thing that man does.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about 4 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ten and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**What is a man who comes down that lane for?**"

And then nobody in that yard answered it.

Nobody answered it. The man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall without turning round, and the road keeper was a hundred yards up that lane, and about four people at that low wall have said since that it was eleven seconds and that about four of them have said that the boy counted them.

And then the man of about thirty-one who digs loam said one thing out loud, in about 44 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He comes down that lane because a page told him where I am and not one word of that page told him what he was to ask me, and I have never in my life had a page in my hand, and about four of you have been standing in that yard a month waiting for me to be the answer to a question, and there is no question, and there has never been one, and the three of you who came down that lane are carrying three things this yard did not write and I am not the answer to any of them, and I am still standing here at the end of it.**"

And then nobody in that yard said anything at all for about a minute, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who has had no question put to him in that yard since the seventeenth of this month, which is eight days, answered one in about three quarters of a minute this morning and that the answer was not one word.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, and what she entered was that a man asked a thing out loud in that yard and that nobody answered it for eleven seconds and that a man then answered it out loud, and entered the answer, and entered that she is not entering the name of the man who asked it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in about 29 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted eleven seconds this morning and nobody in that yard has ever asked me to count one before, and about four of you have worked out that I counted it because there was nothing else to count, and about four of you have worked out that I have been counting things out loud in that yard for a month to find out what they were for and that this morning the thing was the not answering.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted a silence and that nobody asked him to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man asked a question in that yard at about eleven and got no answer for eleven seconds and that the road keeper has come up that lane twice a day through all of it. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and turned round at the top of it and came back down and went up again. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and sixty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and sixty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and seventy-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and twenty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and thirty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and forty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and eighty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and sixty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a question being asked in that yard is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a person asking a question is a thing a person does. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and fourteen.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and twenty-three mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man answering a question out loud in that yard did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that a man has now said a great deal out loud in that yard and has said no to nothing. About seven things were said out loud in that yard today, one of them a question, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who has answered a question out loud in that yard this morning is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man asked a question in that yard at about eleven and got nothing for eleven seconds and that the woman who reads three lines every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six and stopped. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a question was asked in that yard at about eleven and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall in the middle of the eleven seconds without turning round, and that about four of them have said that is the only thing he did in that yard today that anybody in it has seen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the first of the sixth month that a man asked a question in that yard and that a man answered it out loud after eleven seconds, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is three and did not move, and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about half past twelve with his hands at his sides.
