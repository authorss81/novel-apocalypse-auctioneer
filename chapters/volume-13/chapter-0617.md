# Chapter 617: The Yard Worked Out On The Twenty-Fourth Of The Fifth Month That A Page In A Hand Is The Only Thing Making A Stranger And A Man Asked The Man Who Digs Loam About A Question And He Did Not Answer It

The boards of that second table had dried in patches by about seven on the twenty-fourth of the fifth month and the near end of them was still dark where the eave came down. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had knelt down at the far end of that table and run the back of his own hand along the underside of the boards and said one word out loud to nobody, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and fifteen mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years wrote at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight. Entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. Entered three hundred and fifty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move. Entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is one and did not move on its seventeenth morning. Entered that a man stood at a post on that lane at about five yesterday and told this yard yesterday that he had, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and forty-second of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has got them right for a second day running in wet and that about four of them have said that there was nothing in that to be right about this morning, and that a clerk looked at the same four figures afterwards and did not write anything down.

"**The board carries five hundred and fifteen, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and thirty-one days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and forty-five, and five hundred and six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up got up off his knees at about nine and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been standing at that bank since about half past seven looking at hands, and about forty people have gone up that bank past me this morning and I have seen one sheet of paper in any of those hands, and I want it said out loud that I have been doing this since yesterday morning and that I did not tell anybody I was going to, and that I have not been asked about it by a single person.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody in that yard has asked him why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about ten, in about 36 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have stood at the top of that lane every morning this month and I have counted what came up it and I have never once counted what went down it, and about four of you have worked out this morning that the only difference between about nine people a day and one is that one of them had a page in his hand, and about four of you have worked out that I have had a figure in my own head for seventeen mornings and it has been a figure of the wrong thing.**"

What the clerk entered that morning, and what she entered afterward, was that a man had looked at about forty hands at the foot of that bank and that a boy had said out loud what the looking had found, and that the two of those were the same finding and that one of them was made with his own two eyes and the other one with a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 26 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have entered that figure at a quarter to eight every morning for eighteen mornings and this morning two people in this yard have told me what it is a figure of without either of them saying so to me, and I am going to say it myself, and it is this. It is not a figure of that lane. It is a figure of the hands.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her figure is a figure of hands and not of a lane, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about eleven with mud to the knee of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man told this yard yesterday that I was about nine feet off him and standing still and that I did not turn round, and I have been thinking about that all night and about four of you have worked out that I have not once said whether it is true, and I am not going to say whether it is true, and I am going to say that it is a strange thing to be nine feet off a man who is thinking about nothing at all.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not say whether it was true, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about half past eleven, went the length of that second table, and asked the man who digs loam one thing out loud, in about 9 seconds, to a man about nine feet away from him, and the boy counted what he said and got twenty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Did a man come down that lane yesterday with a page in his hand and ask you whether it came out of this yard.**"

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam did not answer it. He looked at the boards about four feet in front of his own boots, and then he took his own right hand off the rail and wiped it down the side of his own leg, and he said nothing at all to anybody for about fifteen seconds, and about nine people in that yard said nothing either, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was asked a thing out loud in that yard and entered that he did not answer it and entered that nobody in that yard asked him why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about twelve without moving off the far end of that table, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I asked him that question because I put the line on the book that sent that man to him and because I wanted to know whether the two of them had ever stood in the same place, and he did not answer me, and I am not going to ask him again this week, and about four of you have worked out that I asked him in front of all of you and that is the opposite of asking a man a question and I did it anyway.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not going to ask him again this week, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man asked the man who digs loam whether a stranger had come to him and that the man did not answer and that the man who asked him is the same man who put the line on that book, and that about four of them have said that there is now a question in that yard that the man who wrote it cannot take back and cannot get an answer to, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard is going to say anything about that either.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man who looks at hands was at the foot of that bank from about half past seven and that the road keeper came up at half past one and went past him and that neither of them said one word. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop at the top of it for anybody. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man did not answer a question in that yard this morning for about fifteen seconds and that the man on those stones has said nothing at all to anybody in that yard for about two volumes, and that about four of them have said that the first silence cost the man who was asked and the second has cost nobody at all. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fifty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and fifty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and sixty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and twenty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and twenty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and ninety-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and ninety-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and thirty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and seventy-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and sixty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and two people working out the same thing by different means is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a district has been counting a lane for eighteen mornings and has this morning counted what the lane is for, and that finding out what your own figure is about is not making anything. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and six.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and fifteen mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man not answering a question did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that the fourth of the five is a person and that a person who is asked and says nothing has still not said anything, and that this district has been counting that as one thing since a book in this yard first carried a name. About six things were said out loud in that yard today, one of them a question and not an answer, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who asks a question in that yard is not entered in it either.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man asked a question this morning by a person he has known for thirteen volumes and answered nothing is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man stood at the near end of that yard this morning looking at about forty pairs of hands and that the woman who reads three lines every evening came up past him at about ten to six with eleven buckets on her hip and looked at her own hands for about a second and then went on, and that about four of them have said that she has never once said she had a page. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man asked a man a question in that yard this morning and got nothing and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about that either, and that about four of them have said that a man can be right about four figures three hundred and forty-two mornings running and still be the only person in that yard nobody has thought to stop.

The clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fourth of the fifth month that a man asked the man who digs loam a question in that yard and that he did not answer it, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is one and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the far end of that second table at about twelve with his right hand off the rail for the first time this month.
