# Chapter 610: A Man Who Digs Loam Said Out Loud In That Yard That He Had Never Been Asked In Thirteen Volumes To Look At A Page And That He Had Started Doing It On His Own And That Was New

The stone lying face up on that second table had gone dry again in the hollow in the middle of it before seven on the seventeenth of the fifth month and a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had put his own thumb in it at about ten past seven and found nothing and wiped his thumb down his own leg as he has on about two hundred mornings, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had not gone to the top of that lane and about four of them had gone to the shop up that bank, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and eight mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and forty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move on its tenth morning, and entered that a man said in that yard yesterday that he has never been asked in his life to look at a page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and thirty-fifth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing has had his right hand in his own pocket at that table for three mornings and that he has not been asked about it and has not said anything about it.

"**The board carries five hundred and eight, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and twenty-four days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and thirty-eight, and four hundred and ninety-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about nine, earlier than he has on any morning of a month, and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 28 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody in this yard has ever asked me to look at a page. Not once in thirteen volumes. On the morning that line went on I asked to look at it and I have looked at it every morning since and I am telling you this morning that I have started doing it on my own and that is new and I have not told anybody why and I am not going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he asked to look at a page on his own account, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has just told me in that yard that he has started doing a thing on his own account and has not told anybody why, and about four of you have noticed that that is the second time in four days a person in that yard has said out loud that they are not going to be thanked for it, and I am the only person in this yard who is paid nothing and entered everything and I am not going to say anything about that this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to say anything else about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about ten and took his own right hand out of his pocket and put it flat on the boards of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 30 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He read it. He read a line that is about him, on his own, ten mornings, and he has not told anybody why, and about four of you have just worked out what that means and I am not going to say it because it is his and not mine and I have been putting my hand on that table for ten mornings without a reason and he has had a reason since the sixth of this month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I went down to that gate at the ninth mile again this morning and there is a man at it who has not been standing there on any of the four mornings I stood at it, and I did not ask him one word and I want it entered that I wanted to ask him one word and that I did not, and that the reason is that I have been in this yard three days being a person who stands at the wrong end of a road and I have not worked out what the right one is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he wanted to ask a question and did not, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about 27 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted the yard again this morning before the clerk had her book open and I have got the same number for the fourth week running and about four of you have worked out that I have been standing in the same place every morning at about a quarter to eight counting the same room, and I am not going to say what I would count instead because I do not know.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he had been counting the same room at the same hour for four weeks, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who digs loam said out loud in that yard this morning that he has started reading a page about himself on his own account and has not told anybody why, and that about four of them have said that about four of the nineteen people standing in that yard read that book every morning and one of them has said it out loud and thirteen of them have not, and that about four of them have said that is a count of readers and this district has a column for that and the column is empty.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man who mends fencing put his hand flat on that table this morning after three mornings of keeping it in his pocket, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard asked him why and that about four of them have said that a man who digs loam said out loud this morning that he has a reason and it is not for that yard to have. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man read a page this morning for the tenth morning running and that the man on those stones has not been asked to read anything on any morning of about two volumes and is not going to be asked this morning. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and forty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and forty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and fifty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and thirteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and eighteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and eighty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and ninety-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and twenty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and sixty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and fifty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man starting to read a page about himself on his own account is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a person deciding to read is a thing a person does. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and ninety-nine.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and eight mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man reading a page about himself did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that the fourth of the five has been a person since a page in this yard first carried one and a man looking at himself in one has not moved it. About five things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man reading a page about himself for the tenth morning is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said out loud this morning that he has started reading a page about himself on his own account and that the woman who has read three lines in that book every evening for about four months was not asked to say one word about it, and that about four of them have said that she has never been asked and is not going to be. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that the days from the second of January came to four hundred and ninety-nine on that wall this morning, which is the last figure below five hundred, and that about four of them have said that it will be five hundred tomorrow and that about four of them have said that nothing is going to happen when it is.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the seventeenth of the fifth month about having started to look at a page about himself on his own account, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is zero and did not move on its tenth morning, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about ten with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
