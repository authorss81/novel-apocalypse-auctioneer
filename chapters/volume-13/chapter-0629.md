# Chapter 629: The Man Who Digs Loam Said Out Loud On The Fifth Of The Sixth Month That He Was Never Told What The Fifth Line Would Be Used For And That He Put It There For One Question And A Fifth Person Came Down That Lane At About Ten

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the fifth of the sixth month and the gap between those two tables had about a shovelful of grey grit in it that nobody had come back for, and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had looked at that gap twice and had not touched it, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and twenty-seven mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and sixty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is four at about a quarter to eight on its twenty-ninth morning, and entered that she entered what she said out loud yesterday morning she would enter if that figure moved, and entered that the figure is five and that it moved later in the day with a person in the room and the reason in the room before the hand, and entered that a shovelful of grit is standing in a gap between two tables and that she is not entering that as a figure about the tables, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fifty-fourth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man was asked in that yard yesterday what he thinks and answered out loud and that a clerk has entered no figure about it, and that about four of them have said that a man who answers a question out loud in that yard is not a thing this district has ever entered.

"**The board carries five hundred and twenty-seven, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and forty-three days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and fifty-seven, and five hundred and eighteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

And then a woman of about thirty-one came down that lane at about ten with a page in her hand and about nine people at the top of that lane turned round, and she came up that bank and stopped about four feet short of that second table, which is nearer than anybody has stood this month, and said one thing out loud, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have had this for a fortnight and I have carried it down that lane once and I did not go past the gate, and I came back up here instead and stood about six feet off that table for about ten minutes this morning before I came in, and about four of you have worked out that I chose where to stand, and that is the first thing anybody has done in that yard this month that was chosen on purpose by somebody who is not from this parish.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person came down that lane with a page in their hand at about ten on the fifth of the sixth month and entered that they came down that lane and that they had a page in their hand and the hour, and entered that she is not entering her name, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is five and moved this morning with a person in the room and the reason in the room, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 35 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is five, and it was zero on the sixth of this month, and I have entered every one of them, and about four of you have worked out that five is the same figure as the fifth of the five things this district does not have, and about four of you have worked out that I noticed that this morning and that I am entering the two separately and that they are not the same thing, and I have decided that they are not the same thing and that is all I have.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she entered two figures separately and decided they are not the same thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk has said out loud that she has decided that a figure of people and a figure of things are not the same thing, and about four of you have worked out that deciding is the whole of what this yard does, and I am going to be asked something in this yard before the middle of the day, and about four of you have worked out that I am telling you that this morning instead of waiting until it happens.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he told the yard before he did it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then the man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I was never told what that line would be used for. I said I would put it there and I said I would put it there again, and nobody has ever told me what it is for, and about four of you have worked out that I put it there for one question, and about four of you have worked out that a person who wrote down one question did not write down that it would be the only one, and I have been at that end of this table for a month saying the first of those things and not saying the second, and I am saying the second one now and it took me a month and I am not going to take it back.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he was never told what that line would be used for, and entered that he put it there for one question and that nobody wrote down that it would be the only one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood at the top of that lane until about twelve and then came down it and said one thing out loud, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has said out loud in that yard that a person wrote down one question and did not write down that it would be the only one, and I have counted about two hundred things said out loud in that yard this month and that is the first one I could have told you what it was for before he said it, and I am not going to say that makes it better and about four of you have noticed that I said it and did not take it back.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he found it out from a mouth and not from counting, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man said out loud in that yard that he was never told what that line would be used for and that a person who wrote down one question did not write down that it would be the only one, and that about four of them have said that about four people in that yard have been waiting a month for one of those two sentences.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he was asked a question in that yard three mornings ago and answered it and that about four of them have said that about a mile and a half of that road has gone since the frost. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop and did not look at anybody standing in that yard. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man in that yard said out loud that he was never told what a line would be used for and that the man on those stones has been at that wall through every morning anybody in that yard has been saying things, and that about four of them have said that is the sixth morning of that run and that he is still the only person in that yard nobody has asked what he thinks. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and sixty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and sixty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.
