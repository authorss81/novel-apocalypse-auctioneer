# Chapter 623: A Man Of About Sixty-Four Was At The Foot Of That Low Wall On The Morning Of The Thirtieth Of The Fifth Month And Three People Came Down That Lane This Month And Nobody In That Yard Has Asked Him Anything

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the thirtieth of the fifth month and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had found a nail head standing about the thickness of a finger proud of the boards of that first table at the far end and had driven it back with the heel of his own hand and looked at it for a while, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and twenty-one mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and sixty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is three and did not move on its twenty-third morning, and entered that a nail head came back into a board, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and forty-eighth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man has been at that low wall every morning of that month and that this is the second to last morning of that month, and that about four of them have said that he will be there tomorrow and that nobody in that yard has asked him and that nobody in that yard is going to.

"**The board carries five hundred and twenty-one, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and thirty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and fifty-one, and five hundred and twelve days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up sat down on the low rail at the far end of that first table at about nine and said one thing out loud, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been coming down that bank every morning since the sixth of this month and I have not once been asked what I come down it for, and about four of you have worked out that I come down it because the boards in this yard are the only boards anybody in this parish has to stand on, and that nobody has ever put that in a book, and that I am not going to start it now with a nail head.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he came down that bank and why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 41 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Three people have come down that lane in twenty-four mornings and I have entered all three, and a boy was found out for walking it, and a man was asked a question by another man and did not answer it, and I have entered all of those too, and about four of you have worked out that this yard has had more happen in it this month than in the three months before it, and that about four of you have worked out that every one of those things happened to somebody who came here on purpose, and that is what I wanted to write down this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that everything in that yard this month happened to somebody who came on purpose, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven with a rail over his own shoulder and put it down against the leg of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk has just said out loud that everything in that yard this month happened to somebody who came here on purpose, and about four of you have worked out that I have not come on purpose once this month, and that I came down that bank on the second of this month to ask a man a question and have not had an answer since, and that I am the only person in that yard who is here by accident and about four of you have not worked out why that is my own fault.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is there by accident, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the ankle of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 27 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has told this yard he is here by accident and I have been here every morning of my life and neither of us has said one word to the other about it in thirteen volumes, and I am going to say one word about it this morning and then I am going to stop, and the word is that I would rather be here by accident than be found.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he would rather be here by accident, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about twelve, in about 27 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted that yard five times this morning and got the same number five times and about four of you have worked out that I do that now before anybody has said anything, and that I used to do it after, and that the difference is that this month I have been standing in that yard waiting for a morning to happen instead of writing one down after it has happened.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has said for six weeks that he was going to stop and has said nothing about why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he has done that about six hundred times and that about four of them have said that there is a man in that yard who has now been at that same wall every morning of a month and that nobody has asked either of them a single question in that time. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and stopped at the top of it and stood there about a minute without turning round. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that he has been at that wall every morning of that month and that this is the second to last morning of it and that about four of them have said that a clerk entered a figure about a nail head this morning and did not enter a figure about him, and that about four of them have said that is the correct way round. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and sixty-second night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and sixty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and seventy-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the second to last morning of a month came up past that yard with eleven buckets on her hip and did not stop, and that about four of them have said that she will be up past it again in the morning and that nobody in that yard has worked out how anybody would put a figure on that. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and forty-eighth morning and that he read four figures off that wall twice and did not look at a stranger all day, and that about four of them have said that a month in this yard is now a thing a person is in rather than a thing a clerk has counted.
