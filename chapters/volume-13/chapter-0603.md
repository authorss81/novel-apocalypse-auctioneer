# Chapter 603: A Man Who Puts Tables Up Asked Out Loud What The Other End Of That Lane Is For And Nobody In That Yard Answered Him

The near end of that second table had a wet ring on it about the size of a saucer by about seven on the tenth of the fifth month where a page had been sitting and had been lifted, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone straight to the top of that lane without stopping at either table, and one of them had come back about ten minutes later and gone down it again, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and one mornings.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and twenty-eighth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that about nine people went to the top of that lane before eight this morning and that one of them came back and went down it and that the count of the bottom of that lane is still zero.

"**The board carries five hundred and one, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and seventeen days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and thirty-one, and four hundred and ninety-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was already at the top of that lane when the clerk had finished her entry and came back down it at about a quarter to nine with his straight edge under his arm, and he said one thing out loud at the near end of that first table, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I asked a question in this yard yesterday about what the other end of that lane is for and nobody in this yard has answered me in a day, and I am going to say the answer I have got and it is that this district put an instruction on a book that tells a man to walk down that lane, and then counted the top of it for two mornings, and that is not a mistake anybody made. That is a thing we all did.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a question he asked in that yard yesterday was not answered in that yard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 39 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I want to say the same thing he said and I want it entered that I am saying it second and that he said it first, and the reason I am saying it is that I wrote the count and the count was of the wrong end of that lane before anybody said so out loud this morning, and I have known it since about ten past eight yesterday and I did not say it because I did not want to be the person who found out that the day I entered a new figure it was the wrong figure.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she had known since the morning before, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about ten carrying a length of wire in his right hand and set it against the low side of that second table and stood at the near end of it and said one thing out loud, in about 36 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I asked a man on the second of this month whether a stranger with a page in his hand might come up this bank and ask him about it, and I did not ask him a second thing, and this yard has spent three mornings counting the wrong end of a lane, and about four of you have worked out that those two facts are the same fact, and I have not said so in this yard because I have not worked out how to say it without it being about me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the reason he has not said it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came up that bank at about half past ten with the flat book shut under his left arm and stopped at the east end of that yard and said nothing at all for about four minutes, and then said one thing out loud, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got a figure in this book that has been on a ladder for two volumes and that nobody in this parish has ever asked me for, and this morning I have got another one, and I am not going to say either of them out loud in this yard, and the reason I am not is that about four of you would put whichever one you liked on that book tomorrow and it would be true for about a day.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering either of the two figures, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood at the far end of that second table with his own page face down and said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about 26 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have counted every man who has come up that bank before eight for three mornings and the number has not gone up or down by one and that is the first count anybody in this yard has kept steady in about two volumes, and I am not going to say the number and I am not going to say what it is a number of.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud in that yard this morning that she had known since the morning before that she had entered a figure about the wrong end of a lane and had not said so, and that about four of them have said that a person who enters a figure every morning of her life ought to be the first one to say when one of them is wrong, and that about four of them have said that saying it second, after a man who puts tables up said it first, is the correct way round and not a failure.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that about nine people in that yard went to the top of that lane before eight this morning and that the road keeper came up it at half past one and went on up the bank without stopping at either table, and that about four of them have said that he has never once been one of the nine and that nobody has ever asked him what he makes of it. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and stopped at the top of it for about a minute looking down that yard and then went on, and nobody in that yard said one word to him and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man looked at that yard and entered that she is not entering what he made of it, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a yard said out loud this morning that it had counted the wrong end of a lane for two days and that the man on those stones has not been counted wrongly or rightly by anybody in that yard in about two volumes. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and forty-second night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and forty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a clerk was wrong yesterday and right this morning about a lane, and that a man at that wall has been right about four figures every morning for three hundred and twenty-eight mornings and has not been told about either thing.
