# Chapter 648: A Man Came Down That Lane On The Words Of The Fifth Line And Stood In That Yard Until The Sun Came Off It

The boards of that second table were dry and pale by about seven on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month and by about two in the afternoon the shadow of that low wall had come across them and gone off them again, and a man who was not from this parish stood in it and watched it do that, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and forty-six mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and eighty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eight at a quarter to eight on its forty-eighth morning, and entered that a page in a man's own hand going down onto those boards and coming back up off them is not a figure and is not a fifth of the four, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-third of those mornings. About four of them have said that he said the four of them at about a quarter to eight and at about four with a stranger standing about four feet off the end of that table the whole day between the two, and that about four of them have said that the stranger was about nine feet off him both times and that he did not look round either time.

"**The board carries five hundred and forty-six, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and sixty-two days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seventy-six, and five hundred and thirty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A man of about thirty-three came down that lane at about ten with a page folded twice in his own coat and came up that bank and stopped about four feet off the end of that second table and put his own back to the low wall and said one thing out loud, in about 43 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I came down that lane because there is a line in a book that tells me to come down that lane, and I have read it twice and I have understood all of it, and about four of you have worked out that a person can understand every word of a thing and still not know what it is for, and about four of you have worked out that I am going to stand here for a while, and I am not going to bother anybody, and if anybody wants to say anything to me they know where I am.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person came down that lane at about ten on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month with a page in their hand and entered that they came down that lane and that they had a page in their hand and the hour, and entered that she is not entering his name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past ten with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 39 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and eighteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a man in this yard who has been standing about four feet off that stone for about half an hour and has not been asked one thing by anybody, and about four of you have worked out that I have watched him stand there from this end of this table the whole time, and about four of you have worked out that I have not gone over, and about four of you have worked out that a man who digs loam a mile off is not a man to walk up and introduce himself to somebody, and I have stood at this end of this table for forty-eight mornings and have not introduced myself to anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has not introduced himself to anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-three who is not from this parish said one thing out loud at about one, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have stood in this yard about three hours and nobody has asked me one thing, and I am going to put this page down on the end of that table now, and I am telling about nineteen people why before I do it, and the reason is that it came out of my own pocket this morning and it is going back into my own pocket before I go up that bank, and about four of you have worked out that I am doing that so that I cannot say afterwards that I came down a lane and stood in a yard and never once put the thing down, and about four of you have worked out that that is the only reason anybody would need for it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the reason came before the hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then he put the page down flat on the end of that second table about a foot off the near copy and stood looking at the four of them on it for about four minutes, and then he picked it up and put it back inside his own coat, and about nine people in that yard said nothing at all while he did both of those things.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past two, in about 45 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That figure is nine, and it was eight at a quarter to eight this morning, and it moved because a person said two things out loud in this yard, and I am entering that a person can come down a lane on the words of a line in a book and stand in a yard for a whole day and be asked nothing, and that this district has no count for that and is not going to get one out of me this afternoon, and I am entering that a page a person puts down on a table and takes off it again is not a fifth of the four documents this district does not own, because he brought it and he took it and I watched him do both.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is nine and moved this morning with a person in the room and the reason in the room before the hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man of about thirty-three came down that lane at about ten and stood in that yard until the sun came off it and was not asked one thing by anybody, and that about four of them have said that he said two things out loud in that yard and was asked nothing after either of them, and that about nine people watched a man carry a page down a bank, put it down, pick it up and put it away, and that not one of the nine said one word to him at any point in it.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a stranger stood in that yard from about ten until about three and was not asked one thing, and that the road keeper came up that lane in the middle of all of it and was not asked one thing either.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four with his own back to that yard.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a stranger stood in that yard all day and was asked nothing, and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing in two hundred and eighty-seven nights, and that about four of them have said that a person can stand in a yard for a day and go away not knowing anything, and that the man on those stones has not gone away in two hundred and eighty-seven nights, and that nobody in that yard has worked out which of those two is the harder thing to be doing. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and eighty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and eighty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and ninety-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and fifty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fifty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and twenty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and twenty-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and sixty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and ninety-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The number of pages on the end of that second table went to three for about four minutes in the middle of the day and came back to two before the light went, and a person was in the room and the reason was in the room before the hand both times, and a page a person carries down a lane and takes back up it is not a figure. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a stranger standing in that yard is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a person who is not from this parish is not a thing this district has. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and thirty-seven.

The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and forty-six mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and a man standing in that yard all day on the words of that line did not move it, because a condition with no end on it is a thing a document carries and a man standing in a yard is not a document. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and the count of that lane moving to nine did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that a person standing in a yard for a whole day asking nothing of anybody has not got a no in it, and that the man at the end of that line has said no to nobody on any morning of this month. About four things were said out loud in that yard today, two of them by a person who is not from this parish, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who stood in that yard all day is not in it and is not a resident.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and nine people who have come down a lane with a page in their hand and have stood in that yard without being asked one thing is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man of about thirty-three went up that bank at about three after standing in that yard all day, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables an hour and a half later and read the same three lines standing up, and that two people read that book standing up on the same evening and that nobody in this yard has asked either of them what the other two lines say.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-third morning, and that a man stood in that yard from about ten until about three and was not asked one thing, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month that a man of about thirty-three came down that lane at about ten on the words of the fifth line and stood in that yard until about three and was not asked one thing by anybody, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is nine and moved this morning, and that a page went down onto the end of that second table for about four minutes and came back up off it again with the reason in the room before the hand both times.
