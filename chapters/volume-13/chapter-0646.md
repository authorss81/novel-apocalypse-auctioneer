# Chapter 646: A Man Said The Reason Out Loud And Then Took Up Half An Inch Of The Footing Of That Low Leg With A Wedge Of His Own

The cloud thinned about seven on the twenty-second of the sixth month and the near end of the boards of that second table began to come back up out of the damp while the far end stayed dark, and there was a heel of mud dried hard on the boards about a foot in from the low leg that nobody had scraped off, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and forty-four mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and eighty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eight and did not move on its forty-sixth morning, and entered that a heel of dried mud on those boards is not entered as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-first of those mornings. About four of them have said that he said the four of them with his own back to that wall this morning, and that about four of them have said that he has said them with his back to that wall on most mornings this month, and that about four of them have said that he turned round about ten minutes ago for the first time and then turned back, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has worked out what he went back for.

"**The board carries five hundred and forty-four, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and sixty days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seventy-four, and five hundred and thirty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put his own right hand flat on the near end of that second table at about nine and rocked his own weight onto it and took it off again, and then put a folded coat of his own down at the low leg and pushed at the corner with the heel of his own hand and watched the corner move, and then said one thing out loud, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to take up about half an inch of the footing of that low leg and put a wedge of my own under it, and I am saying that out loud before I touch it, and nobody in this yard has asked me to, and I am not doing it for that book and I am not doing it for the two tables, I am doing it because a table that rocks cannot have anything written on it, and I would not want anything written on it in a yard like this one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the reason came before the hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then he got his own right knee on that bank at about ten, and worked a flat bar of his own under the corner of that leg and levered the footing up about half an inch, and drove a wedge of his own in under it with the heel of his own hand, and got up off the bank and put his own palm flat on the boards and pushed, and the corner did not move, and about nine people in that yard watched him do all of that and said nothing to him at any point in it.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 55 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has taken up half an inch of the footing of that low leg and has put a wedge of his own under it with his own hands, and I entered the job, and I did not ask him why, and about four of you have worked out that he said the reason out loud before he touched it and that he is the only person in this yard who does that, and about four of you have worked out that a repair is not a making and that the count of the things this district has made did not move when he got his knee on that bank, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has offered him one thing for it and that I am entering that too.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she entered a job and did not ask why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and a cut of his own hand across the back of his own left wrist that had stopped bleeding, and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have watched a man this morning fix a thing I could not fix in a fortnight, and I am not going to say thank you to him for it, and about four of you have worked out that that is not because I am hard on him, and about four of you have worked out that it is because I do not know what a man is paid in, and about four of you have worked out that I have got a figure for that in a clerk's book every morning of this month and that a figure is not a way of paying anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not going to say thank you to a man who mends fencing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man got his own knee on that bank at about ten and levered half an inch of footing out from under the low leg of that second table with a bar of his own, and that about four of them have said that a man who puts tables up has stood at that low corner for most of a month with a straight edge and has not once lifted it, and that about four of them have said that about nine people in that yard watched a man of about thirty-four do it in about four minutes and that not one of them said anything, and that about four of them have said that a clerk entered the job and entered that she did not ask him why, and that about four of them have said that this is the second time this month a man has done work in that yard that nobody asked him to do and that both times he said the reason out loud first.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a table in that yard stopped rocking this morning, and that the road keeper came up that lane afterwards and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that he read the four figures off that wall as he went past and that the fourth of them is the one nobody in that yard will say anything about.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down and did not stay.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man got his knee on that bank this morning and did four minutes of work nobody asked him for, and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing in two hundred and eighty-five nights, and that about four of them have said that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing has now been at the near end of that table for forty-six mornings and has not been paid for one item of anything, and that about four of them have said that the man on those stones has not been offered one item of anything, and that about four of them have said that those are two different wrongs and that nobody in that yard has worked out which one this district started first. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and eighty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and eighty-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and ninety-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and forty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fifty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and twenty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and twenty-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and sixty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and eighty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man levering a leg of a table with a bar of his own is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a table was already a thing this district had made and that a leg of it going into the ground is a thing a man does to a thing this district has made, and the count of the things this district has made has not moved on any morning of this month including today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and thirty-five.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and forty-four mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and a man getting his knee on that bank did not move it. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man saying out loud that he does not know what a man is paid in did not pay it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that a man who has said no to nobody in forty-six mornings did not say no this morning either, and that a table that does not rock is not a remedy for anything. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who got his knee on that bank is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud in that yard that he does not know what a man is paid in is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man levered a leg of that second table up this morning, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six and put her own hand flat on the near corner of them on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and that about four of them have said that she has put her own hand on those tables every evening for about four hundred mornings, and that about four of them have said that the corner she put her hand on this morning is the corner a man of about thirty-four has been working on all morning.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-first morning, and that the second table in that yard stopped rocking this morning with a wedge of a man's own under the low leg of it, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two, and that about four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud three hundred and seventy-one times and that not one of those four hundred and forty figures has ever gone anywhere.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-second of the sixth month that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the reason out loud at about nine and then took up half an inch of the footing of that low leg with a bar and a wedge of his own, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eight and did not move, and that she entered the job and entered that she did not ask him why.
