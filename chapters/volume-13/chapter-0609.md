# Chapter 609: The Boy Of About Nineteen Counted The Whole Of That Yard Again And Did Not Say What It Was A Number Of And The Number Of People Who Have Come Down That Lane Was Zero And Did Not Move On Its Ninth Morning

The rain came in off that bank at about ten past six on the sixteenth of the fifth month and went off it again by about seven and left the boards of that second table darker along the whole of their length than they had been at six, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had not gone to the top of that lane and had gone to the shop up that bank instead, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and seven mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and forty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move on its ninth morning, and entered that nine is the fourth figure on that wall this morning and that she is entering both of them and joining nothing to anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and thirty-fourth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a clerk has entered a figure about a lane and a figure off that wall in the same entry this morning and has said out loud that she is joining nothing to anything, and that about four of them have said that is the first time anybody in that yard has written two numbers next to each other and then said so.

"**The board carries five hundred and seven, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and twenty-three days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and thirty-seven, and four hundred and ninety-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past eight, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted the whole of this yard before the clerk had her book squared and I have got a number and it is the same one I got last week and the week before, and about four of you have been waiting for me to say what it is a number of and I am not going to, and the reason I am not is that every time I have said what a number is a number of it has turned out to be a number about me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted the yard before she had her book open, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about nine and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 35 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in that ditch every morning since the day that line went on and I have not come up that bank on purpose once, and I want it said out loud in this yard that about four of the nineteen people standing in it are here because of a page and about fifteen of them are here because of a boy counting, and both of those are reasons and I am not going to be told which one is the right reason to be in a yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering fifteen as a figure about anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 43 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twelve and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to say something this morning and I have said it in my own head for two days and I am saying it here because that is what this yard is for. There is a figure on that wall that has been out for a year and a figure on that wall that has been out for a day, and this morning I entered a figure about a lane and it is out by nothing and it is still out by eight mornings, and about four of you have noticed that I said nothing about that and I am saying it out loud so that it is on a page.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a figure about a lane is out by eight mornings, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven with his own coat open and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk has just said out loud that a figure she has entered is out by eight mornings and that the way to put that right is to say it out loud, and about four of you have worked out that she cannot enter what the figure ought to say and that this morning she has said the next best thing out loud instead, and I have been in this yard eleven mornings not asking anybody anything and I have not done one thing as good as that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering what she said about him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about 27 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been wrong out loud in this yard for five mornings and nobody has sent me home and about four of you have started saying my name when you repeat it, and I am not going to complain about that because I do not know what else to do with it, and I would like it entered that I have no idea what to do with it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering that about four people have begun to use his name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud in that yard this morning that a figure about a lane is out by eight mornings and that a figure may not be corrected and that she said the next thing out loud instead, and that about four of them have said that in about two volumes of that yard nobody has ever said a thing out loud on the grounds that they could not write it, and that about four of them have said that this is the same reason she has given for not entering a name for about two volumes and that about four of them have said that a clerk who has spent a month refusing to write a person's name has just found a figure she can refuse in the same way.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that the fourth of the four figures on that wall is four hundred and ninety-eight days this morning and that a clerk has entered a figure of nine beside it in the same line and has said out loud that she is joining nothing to anything. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and said one thing out loud to nobody in particular, in about 3 seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Two hundred and twenty-five days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering what the figure is a figure of, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said a number out loud at the top of that bank this afternoon and that it is a figure this yard has been printing for about two volumes and that about four of them have said that he said it to nobody in particular and that about four of them have said that is the only man in this yard who has ever done that. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and forty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and forty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and fifty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and twelve days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and seventeen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and eighty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and ninety days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and twenty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and sixty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and fifty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and saying out loud that a figure is out by eight mornings is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with it, which is that a figure being out is a fact about a figure and this district has been printing two of them every morning for about a year. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and ninety-eight.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and seven mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a clerk saying out loud that a figure of hers is out did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and a figure being out is a thing that happens to a board. About six things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a figure about a lane being out by eight mornings is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that two figures on that wall are out and one of them has been out for a year and that the woman who reads three lines every evening was not told and was not asked and has not said one word. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a clerk said out loud in that yard this morning that one of his four figures has been out for a year, and that he read all four this morning and at about four, and that nobody asked him about it and he was not asked twice, and that about four of them have said that this is the second time in about three weeks that this has been said in that yard and that about four of them have said it will not be said a third time.

The boy of about nineteen counted the whole of that yard again on the sixteenth of the fifth month and did not say what the number was a number of, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is zero and did not move on its ninth morning in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his right hand in his own pocket and not on the boards and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
