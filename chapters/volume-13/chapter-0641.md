# Chapter 641: The Count Stood At Seven And Did Not Move And A Man Who Keeps A Tally Said One Thing Out Loud

The boards of that second table were wet through by about seven on the seventeenth of the sixth month and there was a standing puddle under the low side of them that had not been there the day before, and the grit that had been standing in the gap between the two tables for most of this month had gone soft in the night and was gone, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and thirty-nine mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and eighty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seven and did not move on its forty-first morning, and entered that the standing water under the low side of that second table is a thing the rain did and is not entered as a figure about the tables, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-sixth of those mornings. About four of them have said that a man who has read four figures off that wall three hundred and sixty-six times has still not been asked what one of them is for, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard is going to ask him this month.

"**The board carries five hundred and thirty-nine, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and fifty-five days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and sixty-nine, and five hundred and thirty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had been in that yard before seven and had got his own right shoulder under the low corner of that second table and had put his weight onto it, and the boards had come up about a quarter of an inch and had gone back down when he took his own shoulder off them. He did that four times and then stood with his own two hands on his own hips and looked at the leg of it, and about four people at the top of that bank said afterwards that he said nothing at all and asked nobody for anything and that the boards went down again exactly where they had been.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 38 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to enter that figure again this afternoon whether it moves or not, and the reason is that a figure is what I write in the morning and a fact is what I write in the afternoon, and a number that has stood all morning without anybody in this yard saying anything that would move it is a figure and not a fact, and I have got nine of those this month and I have not had to put one of them out, and nobody in this yard has ever asked me how I would know which one was which.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is going to enter the same figure again this afternoon whether it moves or not, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came up that bank at about ten with the flat book shut under his left arm and stopped at the east end of that yard, where he has stood for about thirty years, and said one thing out loud, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got a figure for every month in this book and I have never showed one of them to anybody, and on the ninth of this month a clerk counted a month off that board with a finger on the wood in the open with about nineteen people at that wall, and I stood about nine feet off her with this shut under my arm and I did not open it, and I have not opened it since either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally did not open a book on the morning a month was counted in the open, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past ten with the wet of the ditch running off the back of his own right heel onto the boards and stood at the far end of that second table and said nothing to anybody, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was at the far end of that second table and said nothing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen came down that lane at about twelve and stood about four feet off the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 29 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have counted the people who came down that lane three ways this month and I have not got the same number out of any two of them, and nobody in this yard counts that lane but her, and about four of you have worked out that I do not know which of the three is right, and I have not asked her, and about four of you have worked out that that is the only thing I have wanted off that count since the sixth of the fifth month and I have not wanted it out loud.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has got three ways of counting that lane and has not got the same number out of two of them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard this morning and said out loud that he did not open a book on the morning a clerk counted a month in the open, and that about four of them have said that he has been at that east end of that yard for about thirty years and has said a great deal in it, and that about four of them have said that a man who has a figure for every month and does not open the book is not a man anybody in that yard has a word for, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has said a word about him this month except this morning.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that about a mile and a half of that road has gone since the frost and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him which half of it went or in which month.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and did not stay.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that the rain has been coming into that yard since before seven and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing in two hundred and eighty nights, and that about four of them have said that there is one question standing in that yard this morning that nobody has asked and that it does not belong to anybody. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and eightieth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and seventy-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and eighty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and forty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and forty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and nineteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and twenty-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and fifty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and ninety-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and eighty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying out loud that he did not open a book is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a book a man does not open is a thing a man carries and not a thing this district has made, and the count of the things this district has made has not moved on any morning of this month including today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and thirty.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and thirty-nine mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and the yard may say so out loud once on a named day and nobody in that yard has said it out loud this month, and the count did not move on any of the ten mornings before this one, because a condition with no end on it is a thing a document carries and the fifth line is a thing a man carries. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and forty mornings in that yard did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that the man at the end of that line has said no to nobody. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who said out loud this morning that he did not open a book is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and about nineteen people have said a great deal in that yard over a morning and none of it is a payment, and a man saying out loud that he did not open a book under his own arm is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said out loud this morning that he did not open a book, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six in the wet and did not stop and did not look, and that about four of them have said that she has read those three lines standing up for about four hundred mornings and that about four of them have said that nobody in this yard has ever asked her what the other two say.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-sixth morning, and that a clerk said out loud this morning that she was going to enter the same figure again this afternoon whether it moved or not, and that about four of them have said that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the seventeenth of the sixth month that a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard at about ten and had said out loud that he did not open a book on the morning a month was counted, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seven and did not move, and that a clerk said out loud at about half past nine that a figure is a thing entered in the morning and a fact is a thing entered in the afternoon.
