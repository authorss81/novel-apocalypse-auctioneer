# Chapter 614: A Man With A Page In His Hand Was In That Yard Again And Asked The Man Who Digs Loam A Thing And The Man Did Not Answer It And Nobody In That Yard Asked Him Why

There was about a half inch of rain on the boards of that second table by about seven on the twenty-first of the fifth month and it had not come off them by about eight and a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had laid his own coat over the near end of that table at about ten past eight and taken it off again at about half past and put it back, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane and about four of them had come back down it before nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and twelve mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and fifty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is one and did not move, and entered that a man said in that yard yesterday that he has never in his life been able to tell whether a page came from a yard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and thirty-ninth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that about four people came back down that lane before nine this morning and that the man with the page came up it again at about ten and that nobody in that yard went to the top of it to see whether anybody was following him.

"**The board carries five hundred and twelve, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and twenty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and forty-two, and five hundred and three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man with the page came up that bank at about ten and stopped about six feet short of that second table in the same place he stopped yesterday and said nothing at all to anybody for about a minute, and then said one thing out loud, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to stand at that wall again. I went back down that lane last night to the water and there is a man digging in it and he is about nine feet off me and he did not look up and I asked him the same thing I asked in that shop and he did not say anything at all, and that was yesterday, and I have come back up here because I do not know what else to do with it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not entering his name, and entered that a man at that wall said nothing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He has just told this yard that he went to the water yesterday and asked the man the fifth line names and the man said nothing, and about four of you have noticed that this happened on the sixth of this month as well and that we have entered a number for it and did not enter him in it, and I am not going to change that entry this morning and I am going to say out loud that I have thought about changing it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she thought about changing an entry and did not, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven with a rail over his own shoulder and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 40 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I asked a man one question on the second of this month about whether a stranger with a page in his hand might come up that bank and ask him about it, and a stranger with a page in his hand came down that lane yesterday, and this morning that same stranger has told this yard that he asked him a second question at the water and the man said nothing, and about four of you have worked out that the question I asked did not cover that and I have been awake about half the night working out that it did not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering that he was awake, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven and stood at the far end of that second table about nine feet off that first table with his own right arm not above the level of his own shoulder, and a man with a page in his hand went to him and asked him one thing out loud, in about 3 seconds, to a man about nine feet away from him, and the boy counted what he said and got nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Is this the man the book sends people to.**"

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam did not answer it. He looked at the page and then at the man and then at the ground about four feet in front of his own boots, and said nothing at all to anybody for about twenty seconds, and about nine people in that yard said nothing either, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was asked a thing out loud in that yard and entered that he did not answer it and entered that nobody in that yard asked him why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about twelve, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted the yard while that man was standing there not saying anything and it was the biggest it has been since I started counting and I am not going to say what it is a number of and about four of you have worked out that it is a number of people standing in a yard watching a man not say a word, and I am not going to say it out loud because nobody asked me what it is a number of.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted while a man was standing there not saying anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man with a page in his hand asked a man who digs loam this morning whether he is the man the book sends people to and that the man did not answer it and that nobody in that yard asked him why, and that about four of them have said that the fifth line on that book says go and ask that man and not ask anybody else, and that about four of them have said that a man who has been findable on a public page for thirteen days has this morning found out what being findable is, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard watched him find it out and that about four of them have said that is not the same as nobody watching.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man with a page in his hand has been in that yard twice this week and that the road keeper comes up that lane twice a day and has not been asked one question about either visit. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man in that yard said nothing at all to a stranger for about twenty seconds this morning and that the man on those stones has said nothing at all to anybody in that yard for about two volumes, and that about four of them have said those are not the same silence and that about four of them have said nobody in that yard has worked out why they are not the same. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fifty-third night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and fifty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and sixty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and seventeen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and twenty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and ninety-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and ninety-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and thirty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and seventy-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and fifty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and about four marks went on the underside of those boards before about nine. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man not answering a stranger is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud at about half past ten, which is that not answering is not making anything and this district has spent thirteen volumes refusing to enter it and is not going to start this afternoon. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and three.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and twelve mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man not answering a stranger did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason out loud at about half past ten, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and a man saying nothing is not the same as a man saying no and this district has not worked out the difference and is not going to work it out today. About six things were said out loud in that yard today, one of them by a man who is not from this parish and one of them a question and not an answer, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a stranger who says a thing out loud in that yard is not entered in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who is findable and does not answer is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man did not answer a stranger in that yard this morning for about twenty seconds and that the woman who reads three lines every evening did not look up when it happened and did not say one word about it afterwards, and that about four of them have said that she was not in that yard when it happened and that about four of them have said she would not have said anything if she had been. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man with a page in his hand was in that yard for about two hours this morning and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was nine feet from a stranger the whole of the time and neither of them looked at the other, and that about four of them have said that this district has two people in it who are on a page and that about four of them have said one of them is on a page and the other one is not.

A man with a page in his hand was in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-first of the fifth month and asked the man who digs loam whether he is the man the book sends people to, and the man did not answer it and nobody in that yard asked him why, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is one and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
