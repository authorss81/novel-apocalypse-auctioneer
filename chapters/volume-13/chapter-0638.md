# Chapter 638: A Woman Of About Twenty-Six Came Down That Lane At About Ten On The Fourteenth Of The Sixth Month With A Page In Their Hand And Said A Thing Out Loud In That Yard That Was Not About The Page And A Clerk Entered That And Did Not Enter Her In The Column

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the fourteenth of the sixth month and the wind had got up that bank in the night and had put about a hand's worth of grit along the far end of that second table and blown it into the corner where that first table meets it, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and thirty-six mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and seventy-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is six at about a quarter to eight on its thirty-eighth morning, and entered that it moved later in the day with a person in the room and the reason in the room before the hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-third of those mornings. About four of them have said that a clerk of nineteen years put a question to a woman in that yard three mornings ago and that nothing was said to it, and that about four of them have said that she has entered it as a fact about a morning and not as a reading, and that about four of them have said that about nine people in that yard said nothing at all at the time.

"**The board carries five hundred and thirty-six, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and fifty-two days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and sixty-six, and five hundred and twenty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

A woman of about twenty-six came down that lane at about ten with a page in their hand and about nine people at the top of that lane turned round, and she came up that bank and stopped about six feet off the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I got to the water at about two in the afternoon on the second day and a man in a ditch asked me if I wanted a hand with it, and about four of you have worked out that I shook my head at him, and about four of you have worked out that I have not spoken above a murmur since that morning except to the two men at that gate, and about four of you have worked out that this is the first thing I have said in a voice anybody could hear from nine feet off, and that I did not expect to say it in a yard, and about four of you have worked out that I have still not opened it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person came down that lane with a page in their hand at about ten on the fourteenth of this month and entered that they came down that lane and that they had a page in their hand and the hour, and entered that she is not entering her name, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seven and moved this morning with a person in the room and the reason in the room before the hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 51 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A person has just said one thing out loud in that yard and it was not about the page she was carrying, and I entered it, and about four of you have worked out that I am not entering her in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and about four of you have worked out that she is not from this parish and that I have never had to decide that about a person who was standing in front of me in that yard before, and about four of you have worked out that the six of them before her all said something about the page and this one did not, and I have entered that too, and I am not going to say what I make of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering a person in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 51 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A woman has come down that lane with a page in her hand this month and has said one thing out loud in that yard and it was not about the page, and about four of you have worked out that the six before her all said something about the page, and about four of you have worked out that there is a thing about that lane which is not the page and that nobody in this district has ever written down or counted, and about four of you have worked out that I am the man who put the line that sent her down it, and about four of you have worked out that I have got no figure at all for that, and I have had a month to get one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has no figure for a thing that is not the page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A woman came down that lane this morning with a page in her hand and said one thing out loud in that yard and it was not about the page, and about four of you have worked out that six people have come down that lane with a page in their hand and every one of them came down it on that page, and about four of you have worked out that I have stood at this end of this table and answered six of them about a page, and about four of you have worked out that a man who answers about a page does not get asked about anything else, and I have been on this bank about a month working that out, and about four of them have said that about four of you have noticed.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who answers about a page does not get asked about anything else, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a person came down that lane at about ten this morning and said one thing out loud in that yard and it was not about the page she was carrying, and that about four of them have said that she said she had not opened it, and that about four of them have said that six people have been down that lane this month and a clerk has entered all six of them and has entered that one of the six did not come down it about a page, and that about four of them have said that a clerk has said out loud that she is not entering that person in a column and that about four of them have said that nobody has been in that column since before this district began counting anything.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a person came down that lane this morning and was not asked about anything when she got here, and that the road keeper came up that lane afterwards and was not asked one thing about her either.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and went on up that lane past the wall and was gone about a minute and came back down it and stopped at the top of it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a person said one thing out loud in that yard this morning that was not about a page, and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing about a page or about anything else, and that about four of them have said that about nineteen people were in that yard when she said it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and seventy-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and seventy-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and eighty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and forty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and forty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and sixteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and nineteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and fifty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and ninety-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and eighty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a person saying one thing out loud in a yard that is not about a page is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a person is a thing a person is and not a thing this district has made. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and twenty-seven.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the page in that woman's own hand this morning is not a fifth of them, and a clerk entered that reason with the figure. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and thirty-six mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and a person came down that lane this morning with a page in their hand and that is not a condition with no end on it either. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and seven people down a lane did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that the man at the end of that line has been answered seven times and has said no to nobody. About four things were said out loud in that yard today and one of them was not about a page, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a woman who came down that lane this morning is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and seven people who have come down a lane and been answered by a man this district has no way to pay is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a woman of about twenty-six came down that lane this morning and said one thing out loud in that yard, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and that about four of them have said that a stranger and a woman of four hundred mornings have now both been asked nothing and answered nothing and said one thing, and that about four of them have said that the two of them are not the same and that nobody has worked out what they are.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-third morning, and that a person said a thing in that yard this morning that was not about a page and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not told about either of the two.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fourteenth of the sixth month that a person came down that lane at about ten with a page in their hand and said one thing out loud in that yard that was not about the page, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seven and moved this morning, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with no figure at all for a thing that is not a page.
