# Chapter 616: The Man With The Page Came Up That Lane Again And Said Out Loud In That Yard Why He Was Not Going To Put It In The Water And Had Not Said Why To Anybody

There was about a quarter of an inch of rain standing in the chalk along the edge of that second table by about seven on the twenty-third of the fifth month and the mark at the far end of that run of chalk had come off the wood altogether in the night and left a bare patch about the width of two fingers. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had rubbed at that patch with his own thumb for about a minute and got nothing back onto it and had put his own thumb in the hollow in the middle of the stone instead and found about a sixteenth of an inch in that. About four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane and had not come back down it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and fourteen mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years had her own book open on the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight and entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. Entered three hundred and fifty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move. Entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is one and did not move on its sixteenth morning. Entered that the mark at the far end of that chalk has come off the boards and that she is not entering that as a figure about the boards, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once. At about four he said them again and got them again. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and forty-first of those mornings. About four of them have said that the fourth of the four figures on that wall came to five hundred days on the eleventh of this month and that a clerk said out loud on that morning that she was not going to say it a second time. About four of them have said that a person keeping a figure back in her own mouth for twelve days is a different thing from a person who has never had it, and that nobody in that yard has worked out which of the two that clerk is.

"**The board carries five hundred and fourteen, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and thirty days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and forty-four, and five hundred and five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man with the page came up that bank at about ten from the far end of that lane and stopped about six feet short of that second table in the same place he has stopped twice and said one thing out loud, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I got up at five and I went down that lane with this in my hand and I got to the fourth post and I stood at that post about a quarter of an hour and I did not go on to the water, and the reason is that a page that says go and ask that man and not ask anybody else does not say where the water is, and the water is past the man, and so I could not get to it without going past the place it sent me to, and I am telling this yard that I did not do it and that I have come back up that bank twice since and have not said so to anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not entering his name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years stood up off the low seat she has carried down that bank every morning since the sixth of this month and said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 38 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That man has now been in this yard three times in four days and my figure for that lane has not moved on one of those mornings and has not moved this morning, and I am not going to change what the figure is about so that it fits what I can see from a table, and about four of you have worked out that there are two ways of writing a morning in this yard and that I have had one of them for seventeen mornings and about four of you have not worked out which one it is.**"

Where the clerk had been sitting there was a wet print about the size of a book on the boards of that first table, and about four people at that low wall have said since that she has not once wiped it off and about four of them have said that is the second morning running she has sat down on the same wet place and that neither of them has been to a clerk about it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven with a rail over his own shoulder and set the rail down against the leg of that second table and stood with his right hand flat on the boards and said one thing out loud, in about 41 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has just told this yard that he could not get to the water because the page sent him past the water, and I put that line on that book, and I did not know there was a fourth post on that lane and I have walked it twice and not counted them, and I am not going to say anything else about it this morning because I have not worked out the rest of it and about four of you have noticed that I have said the first half of it out loud in an open yard and have not said the second half anywhere.**"

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he set a rail down against the leg of that table, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the ankle of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I had my back to that lane from about six and a man went past the end of my ditch and stopped about nine feet off me and stood there about a quarter of an hour and never came to the edge, and I did not know he was there at all until he went, and I am telling this yard that I am not going to turn round next time and that is the whole of what I am going to say about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man stood about nine feet off him for about a quarter of an hour, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about twelve, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted that yard at about ten and I got the same number I have got every morning this month, and then I counted it again at about eleven and I got the same number again, and about four of you have worked out that I count it twice on the mornings a man is standing in it, and about four of you have worked out that I have not counted that lane once all month and that the lane is the only thing in this district that has actually changed.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted the yard twice and the lane not at all, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then about four people at that low wall said, not to each other and not to anybody in the middle of it, that a man who said out loud on the twenty-second of this month that he was going to put a page in the water had come back up that bank twice since and had told this yard about a fourth post instead, and that this is not a rule anybody in this district wrote down and that it has been true twice.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that there is a man in that yard who has now been asked four questions in four days and that none of the four were about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stood there about a minute looking down at the yard. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that nobody in that yard has offered either that man or a man with a page in his hand anything this week, and that about four of them have said that is the correct way round. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fifty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and fifty-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Two hundred and sixty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and nineteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and twenty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and ninety-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and ninety-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and thirty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and seventy-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and fifty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and the mark at the far end of that chalk came off in the night and has not been put back and nothing was said about it in a mouth. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man standing at a fourth post about a quarter of an hour is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a man standing in one place is a thing a man does and this district has never entered one and is not going to start this month. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and five.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and fourteen mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man not being able to reach the water did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and a man standing at a fourth post is not that person saying anything. About five things were said out loud in that yard today, one of them by a man who is not from this parish, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a stranger who says a thing out loud in that yard is not entered in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who has come up that bank three times with a page in his hand and has not been paid for any of the three is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man stood at a fourth post on that lane at about five this morning and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening walked up past the same post at about ten to six with eleven buckets on her hip and did not stop and did not look, and that about four of them have said that the fourth post is about nine feet short of the end of the ditch and that she knows exactly where it is. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and forty-first morning and that a man with a page in his hand has been in that yard three times in four days and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about any of it, and that about four of them have said that he has now been at that wall for three hundred and forty-one mornings without one person in that yard stopping him and that about four of them have said that nobody has ever thought to stop him.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-third of the fifth month that a man with a page in his hand came up that lane for the third time in four days and said out loud why he had not put it in the water, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is one and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with a rail against the leg of it and his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
