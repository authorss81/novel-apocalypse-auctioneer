# Chapter 647: A Woman Of About Fifty-Eight Stood In That Yard On The Twenty-Third And A Clerk Did Not Ask Her Anything And Entered The Reason

There was a thin rain on that bank by about seven on the twenty-third of the sixth month that was not quite rain and did not soak anything, and the boards of that second table held a film of it along the low side that nobody wiped off, and the wedge the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had driven under that low leg was dark all along its own length, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and forty-five mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and eighty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eight and did not move on its forty-seventh morning, and entered that the film of water along the low side of those boards is a thing the weather did and is not entered as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-second of those mornings. About four of them have said that he said the four of them in a thin rain without the coat on and got all four of them, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever offered that man a coat, and that about four of them have said that he has never once looked like a man who wanted one.

"**The board carries five hundred and forty-five, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and sixty-one days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seventy-five, and five hundred and thirty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about eleven with an empty pail in her own right hand and stopped at the near end of that second table, and she had not stopped there in the whole of the time about nine people at that low wall could remember, and she stood there for about twenty-five minutes and read three lines in that lot book standing up with her own shoulder about four inches off the boards, and she said nothing at all to anybody, and about nine people in that yard said nothing at all to her.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about 49 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That woman is standing at that table and I am not going to ask her one thing, and I am going to say out loud why before this afternoon, and the reason is that I asked her one question in this yard earlier this month and she said nothing to anybody, and a person who does not answer a question the first time is not asked it a second time, and I told about nineteen people in this yard beforehand that I was going to ask it and I asked it and I got nothing, and I entered that I got nothing, and I am not going to do it to her twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to ask that woman one thing, and entered the reason before she did not do it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the near end of that second table at about twelve about four feet off that woman and said one thing out loud, in about 46 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A woman has been standing at that table for about twenty-five minutes reading three lines of a book and nobody in this yard has said one word to her, and about four of you have worked out that I have stood at this end of this table for forty-seven mornings saying things out loud about ten feet off a man who has not answered one word of any of them, and about four of you have worked out that that is the same as what is happening over there and that neither of us has asked the other one what it is, and I am not going to move off this board and give her more room than she has taken.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not move off that board, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past twelve with the boards wet through to the thigh of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk has said out loud this morning that she asked a woman a question in this yard in this month and got nothing, and about four of you have worked out that I was in this yard that morning, and about four of you have worked out that I have been asked nothing this month, and about four of you have worked out that a man being asked nothing and a woman not answering a question are not the same thing, and that this district has never once put them in the same count, and neither have I.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has been asked nothing this month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a woman of about fifty-eight stood at the near end of that second table this morning for about twenty-five minutes and said nothing to anybody, and that about four of them have said that she has come up past those tables at about ten to six on about four hundred evenings and has read three lines standing up and has gone on, and that about four of them have said that a clerk said out loud at about half past eleven that she is not going to ask her one thing, and that about four of them have said that the first time she asked, she told about nineteen people in that yard beforehand, and that about four of them have said that about nine people in that yard said nothing to her this morning and that it took four of them about ten minutes to manage it.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a woman stood in that yard this morning and a clerk did not ask her anything, and that the road keeper came up that lane afterwards and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that about a mile and a half of that road has gone since the frost and that a man who walks a road that is going does not usually stop in a yard to be asked what he thinks about it, and that about four of them have said that he did not stop this morning.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it, and then stood a while at the top of that bank and did not come back down it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that she is not going to ask a woman one thing a second time, and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing in two hundred and eighty-six nights, and that about four of them have said that both of those are a person being left alone in a yard, and that about four of them have said that one of them chose it this morning and that nobody in that yard has worked out which one chose theirs. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and eighty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and eighty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and ninety-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and fifty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fifty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and twenty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and twenty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and sixty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and ninety marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk saying out loud that she is not going to ask a woman one thing is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a decision not to ask is a thing a clerk does and a clerk is a person and not a district, and the count of the things this district has made has not moved on any morning of this month including today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and thirty-six.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and forty-five mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and a clerk not asking a question did not move it. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a woman standing at that table this morning did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that a woman reading three lines of a book standing up in the rain is not that person and did not say no to anybody. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a woman of about fifty-eight who stood at that table for about twenty-five minutes is not in it and is not a resident.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a woman who stood in that yard this morning on her own errand is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight did not come up past those tables at about ten to six, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that she is not going to ask that woman one thing a second time, and that about four of them have said that she has come up past those tables at about ten to six on about four hundred evenings and did not come up past them this evening, and that about four of them have said that she went back down that bank at about half past twelve with an empty pail in her own hand, and that about four of them have said that nobody in this yard has worked out what she did with the rest of that day.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-second morning, and that a woman stood in that yard for about twenty-five minutes in a thin rain and that nobody in that yard said one word to her, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two, and that about four of them have said that he was about nine feet off that woman for twenty-five minutes and that he did not look at her once and that about four of them have said that she was not looking at him either.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-third of the sixth month that a woman of about fifty-eight stood at the near end of that second table at about eleven for about twenty-five minutes and said nothing to anybody, and that she was not asked one thing and that the reason she was not asked was entered out loud at about half past eleven before she was not asked, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eight and did not move, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not move off that board.
