# Chapter 605: A Clerk Of Nineteen Years Counted The Fourth Month Off A Board With A Finger On The Wood In The Open In Daylight With About Nineteen People At The Wall And Got Thirty

Word had got round that parish before seven on the twelfth of the fifth month that a clerk of nineteen years was going to count a month off a board in the open, and about nineteen people were at that wall by about a quarter to eight, which has happened in that yard twice before in about a year and once in a fortnight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and three mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and forty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move, and that the fourth month of this year is thirty days, counted this morning off a board with a finger on the wood in the open in daylight with about nineteen people at the wall, and that it ended twenty-seven mornings ago, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and thirtieth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a clerk counted a month off a board this morning for the third time in this district's history and that two of the three counts have come out the way the dates on the pages already said they would, and that about four of them have said that a count that comes out the way the page said is not a small thing and is not a large one either.

"**The board carries five hundred and three, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and nineteen days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and thirty-three, and four hundred and ninety-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years counted the fourth month of this year off a board with a finger on the wood, in that yard, in the open, in daylight, with about nineteen people at that wall, and it took her about eleven minutes, and the boy of about nineteen stood at her own shoulder and counted the marks with his own finger an inch behind hers and did not say one number out loud the whole time, and when she had finished she said one thing out loud, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Thirty. That is the fourth month and it is thirty days and it ended twenty-seven mornings ago and the pages in this yard have been printing the first of this month since the morning after it ended, and nothing on any page in this district has to be corrected, and I am going to say that out loud because about four of you have been waiting since the last time I counted a month for somebody to say it and I have not said it because I did not want to be the one who says it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of counted months is four, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had not come up that bank until about ten and came and stood at the near end of that first table and said one thing out loud, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in this yard two volumes and a bit waiting for a figure on a page to come back different from what it said, and this morning a woman counted thirty marks off a board and got thirty and told us that we have been printing the right dates all along, and about four of you have gone quiet, and I want it entered that I have not been quiet and that I do not know what I expected the number to be.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering what he expected, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about a quarter past ten, in about 24 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put my finger behind hers and I counted the same marks and I got the same number, and that is the first time two people in this yard have counted the same thing and got the same answer on the same morning, and I am not going in the column for it and I want it said that I counted.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering him in the column, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book open and said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been counting months off that board in my own head for eleven years and I have got thirty for this one and I have got a different figure for the month before that and I have never said it out loud in this yard, and this morning a clerk of nineteen years beat me to it in front of about nineteen people and I am not resentful of her and I would like it entered that I am not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the figure he has for the month before that one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had his own thumb in the hollow in the middle of the stone lying face up on that second table at about eleven and said one thing out loud from there, in about 26 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Four months now that somebody in this yard has counted off a board and the length of this month is not one of them, and about four of you have worked out that this month ends while I am standing here and that nobody is going to count it, and I am going to be here when it ends and I am not going to count it either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to count a month a person is living in, and the reason, which is that she gave it on the morning of the last one and the reason has not changed, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven and stood at the far end of that second table and read the fifth line of that book from the near end of that first table with his forefinger an inch off it from the beginning to the end and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was in that yard and entered that she is not entering what he made of a month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk counted a month off a board this morning at about a quarter to eight with about nineteen people at the wall and got thirty and that no date on any page in this district has to be corrected, and that about four of them have said that the man of about fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked one thing about a month he has been printing the right answer for three hundred and thirty mornings, and that about four of them have said that is the fourth time in this district's history and that nobody has ever worked out what to do about it.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk counted a month off a board at that wall this morning and that a man has stood at that wall for about two volumes reading four figures off it and that about four of them have said the two men have never once said a word to each other about either the figures or the month. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nineteen people in that yard spent eleven minutes this morning counting marks off a board and that the man on those stones has not been counted this morning and has not been counted on any morning. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and forty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and forty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and fifty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and thirteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and eighty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and eighty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and twenty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and sixty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and forty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and counting a month is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud this morning with the count itself, which is that a month is not a thing a district makes and that count is in a different book. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and ninety-four.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and three mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and counting a month did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a reading of a lot does not move because somebody counted a month. Nothing was read out of anything into that book today, a month was counted off a board at about a quarter to eight, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk counting a month off a board did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, after all of it, and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about nineteen people in that yard spent this morning counting a month off a board and that the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months was the only person at that wall who counted nothing today, and that about four of them have said that is not a failing. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a clerk counted a month off a board under his own eyes at about a quarter to eight and that he read his four figures at about the same moment and was not asked one thing about either, and that about four of them have said that a man who has said four numbers out loud in that yard three hundred and thirty mornings has still never been asked what one of them is for.

The clerk of nineteen years counted the fourth month of this year off a board with a finger on the wood in the open in daylight in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twelfth of the fifth month and got thirty, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is zero and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that first table at about ten with his right hand flat on the boards of it and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
