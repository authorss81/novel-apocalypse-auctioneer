# Chapter 642: The Count Did Not Move On The Eighteenth And A Clerk Asked A Man One Thing About A Table Leg And He Answered It About Himself

The boards of that second table had a grey skin on them by about seven on the eighteenth of the sixth month where the rain had come off them in the night and left the grit of themselves behind, and the gap between the two tables was clear for the first time in most of a month, because whatever had been standing in it had gone down that bank in the water, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and forty mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and eighty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seven and did not move on its forty-second morning, and entered that the grit that was standing in the gap between those two tables has gone down that bank in the water and is not entered as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-seventh of those mornings. About four of them have said that two of those four figures are out and that nobody in that yard has worked out which two, and that about four of them have said that the man who read them knows and has not said.

"**The board carries five hundred and forty, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and fifty-six days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seventy, and five hundred and thirty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had a straight edge of his own making under the near end of that second table at about eight and had his own left thumb on the high end of it and had gone along the boards with his own eye, and about nine people in that yard watched him do that for about ten minutes and none of them said anything to him about it.

A clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 3 seconds, to a man about four feet away from her, and the boy counted what she said and got seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**What you are doing to that leg.**"

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said one thing out loud, in about 36 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I set these two tables up myself. I set the first one and I came back eleven days after it and set the second one, and I have not been able to make the two of them level on one single morning since, and I have come up this bank about every morning to see whether that has changed, and it has not changed, and nobody in this yard ever asked me to do any of it, and I am not going to ask anybody in this yard to help me do it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was asked one thing out loud in that yard and entered the question and entered that he answered it and entered the answer, and entered that the answer was about himself and not about a page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about ten with his own coil of wire over his own left shoulder and set it down at the foot of that low wall and said one thing out loud, in about 29 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man at the near end of that table has said out loud this morning that nobody asked him to do any of it, and about four of you have worked out that is the third man in this yard this month to stand at his own work and say a thing like that, and about four of you have worked out that none of the three of us has said it to the other two, and I am not going to be the one who does.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not going to be the one who says it to the other two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past ten with the boards wet to the knee of his own right leg where the water had come off him in the wind, and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 26 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That ditch I am in is a mile off and it is full today, and there is a man from up past the water standing at the top of it watching it come up, and I am not going to tell him to get out of it, and I have not told him anything yet, and about four of you have worked out that a man who says he is not going to tell a man to get out of a ditch is a man who has thought about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not going to tell a man in a ditch to get out of it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said out loud this morning that he has never made the two of those tables level, and that about four of them have said that he has been coming up that bank to look at the low corner of a table for most of a month and that about four of them have said that nobody knew that until this morning, and that about four of them have said that a clerk asked him one question about it and that he answered it and that neither of them entered the word page in either the question or the answer.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man was asked one thing in that yard this morning about a table leg and that about four of them have said that the answer he gave was about himself and that about four of them have said that a person can be asked a question and can answer it about himself and that this district has not got a column for that either.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stood there with his own back to that yard until about four.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man at the near end of that second table said out loud this morning that he is not going to ask anybody to help him, and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing in two hundred and eighty-one nights, and that about four of them have said that a man who will not ask anybody for help and a man who will not ask anybody anything are two different men and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever said which one of them has been here longer. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and eighty-first night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and eighty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and ninety days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and forty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fifty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and twenty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and twenty-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and fifty-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and ninety-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and eighty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying out loud that he cannot make two tables level is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a table is a thing this district has made and a man being unable to level it is a thing a man carries, and the count of the things this district has made has not moved on any morning of this month including today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and a man who has read them three hundred and sixty-seven times was not asked about either of the two, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and thirty-one.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and forty mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and nobody in that yard said so out loud this morning, and a clerk asking a man about a table leg did not advance the count, because a question about a leg is a question a person carries and the fifth line is a thing a man carries and the two are not the same size of thing. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man being asked about a table leg this morning did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that the man at the end of that line was not the man who was asked. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who was asked a question about a table leg at about half past nine is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who has not asked anybody to help him with two tables is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man at the near end of that second table said out loud this morning that he is not going to ask anybody to help him, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and that about four of them have said that about four people have now said out loud in that yard this month that they are not going to ask anybody for something, and that about four of them have said that she has not asked anybody for anything for about thirty years and has not said so out loud once.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-seventh morning, and that two of those four figures are out, and that a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said out loud this morning that he set those two tables up himself, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall twice and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked about the man who put the tables up either.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the eighteenth of the sixth month that a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was asked one thing out loud at about half past nine and answered it about himself and not about a page, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seven and did not move, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about ten and had a coil of his own wire at the foot of that wall.
