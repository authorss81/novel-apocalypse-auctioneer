# Chapter 650: On The Twenty-Sixth Of The Sixth Month A Man Who Said No The Morning Before Stood At The Far End Of That Second Table

There was a hard white rime on the boards of that second table by about seven on the twenty-sixth of the sixth month, which was the hundred and seventy-seventh morning after the count, and the whole of that bank was dry and hard and had gone the colour of a road that has not been touched since the frost, and the fifth line of that lot book was still on that book, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and forty-eight mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not a figure off that other figure and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on either of them on any morning of this month.

The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and eighty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is nine and did not move on its fiftieth morning, and entered that the rime on those boards is a thing the night did and is not entered as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-fifth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and fifty times this month, and that nobody in this yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that about four of them have said that two of the four have been out for longer than anybody in that yard has been standing at that wall.

"**The board carries five hundred and forty-eight, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and sixty-four days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seventy-eight, and five hundred and thirty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about half past ten with nothing in his own hands and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 46 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twelve and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said out loud in that yard earlier this month what that yard is for and I am not going to say it twice, and about four of you have worked out that I have not been back down to that ditch since that morning, and about four of you have worked out that I am going back down to it after this, and that nobody has told me what I am going to be asked down there, and about four of you have worked out that nobody told me the last time either, and I am going down there anyway, and that is the only sentence I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is going back down to that ditch, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about eleven with his spade over his own right shoulder and mud to the thigh of his own right leg, and set the spade up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it, and went the three steps to the rail in the fence and put his own two hands on it and stayed there. He said one thing out loud, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and eleven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk entered a thing about me yesterday and she did not put it in that book, and about four of you have worked out that I have not asked her to, and about four of you have worked out that I came up this bank this morning before she had that book open, and that I have come up this bank every morning this month, and that is all I have got and I am not going to make it into more than that, and about four of you have worked out that there is a man standing in this yard who is still paid nothing and I am him.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is still paid nothing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who said no out loud in that yard on the morning before came up that bank again this morning at about eleven and set his own spade against the leg of that second table and put his own two hands on the rail, and that about four of them have said that the fifth line of that book is on that book and has not been touched and that nobody has asked that man one question about it this morning, and that about four of them have said that about nineteen people in that yard have watched a man stand at the far end of a table for fifty mornings and that the only morning any of them have seen somebody else speak to him about anything at all was the morning before this one.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man said no in that yard yesterday morning, and that the road keeper came up that lane this morning and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that about a mile and a half of that road has gone since the frost and that nobody in this yard has ever asked him which part of it is left.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said no in that yard yesterday and is standing at the far end of that second table this afternoon with his own two hands on a rail, and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing in two hundred and eighty-nine nights and has slept on two hundred and eighty-eight of them, and that about four of them have said that those two sentences have now been in the same afternoon twice, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has put them together in a page. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and eighty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and eighty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and ninety-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and fifty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fifty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and twenty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and thirty-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and sixty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and ninety-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying no in that yard is not a fourteenth thing and a remedy being paid is not a fifteenth, because a remedy is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that the page it went down on was already there. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and thirty-nine.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and forty-eight mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this month and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the morning before this one by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and a clerk entered the reason with it, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that the page is still on the table this morning and the fifth line is untouched and the man is still findable in the morning and is still not paid. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who said no yesterday is not in it and a man who said what he is still not paid is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud in that yard that he is still paid nothing is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and a clerk who entered a no on her own page yesterday is not a payment either.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said no in that yard yesterday morning, and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-fifth morning, and that a man who said no in that yard yesterday morning is standing at the far end of that second table now with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-sixth of the sixth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that nobody in that yard has asked the man it names one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is nine and did not move, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail.
