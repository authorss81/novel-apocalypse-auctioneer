# Chapter 606: A Man Who Cuts Reeds Said Out Loud In That Yard That He Had Stood At A Gate At The Ninth Mile Four Mornings This Week Waiting For A Man With A Page In His Hand And That Nobody Had Asked Him One Word About It

The hollow in the middle of the stone lying face up on that second table was dry for the first time since the eighth of the third month, and a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had put his own thumb in it and found nothing in it and looked at his thumb and said nothing, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had not gone to the top of that lane at all this morning, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and four mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and forty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move, and entered that four is the number of counted months in this district and that the fourth of them was counted on the morning before, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and thirty-first of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that the stone on that second table was dry this morning for the first time in two months and that nobody in that yard has said one word about the water in it either.

"**The board carries five hundred and four, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and twenty days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and thirty-four, and four hundred and ninety-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to nine with his binder open on the boards beside his own hand and said one thing out loud, in about 39 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I stood at the gate at the ninth mile at half past six on four mornings this week and I watched that lane and not one man came down it with a page in his hand, and I got nothing out of it that anybody could pay me for and I am not going to be thanked, and what I want said is that the man standing at that gate every morning of my life has now been stood at by a man who cuts reeds and nobody in this yard has ever asked him whether that is a thing he minds.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody in that yard has asked the man at that gate anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 38 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have entered a count of people coming down that lane and it has been zero for six mornings, and this morning a man who cuts reeds has been standing at the ninth mile at half past six for four of them, and I am going to say out loud that I did not ask him to and I am not going to enter it and about four of you have already worked out that there are now two people in this parish waiting at two ends of the same road and neither of them was sent.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she did not ask him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about ten with a coil of wire over his own shoulder and set it down and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Eight mornings ago I asked a man one question in this yard and I have not asked one since, and this morning a man who cuts reeds has been standing at a gate four mornings without being asked and a clerk has just said out loud that she did not ask him, and about four of you have noticed that those are the same sentence with two different men in it and about four of you have not asked me whether I noticed.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody in that yard asked him whether he had noticed, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past ten and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read that fifth line about nine times since the morning it went on and every time I have read it I have been reading about where I am and not about what a man is to do, and I want that said out loud in this yard this morning because I have been up that bank about four times this week waiting for somebody to ask me what it is for, and about four of you have just worked out that nobody is going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man read a line of that book on his own account and not on a clerk's, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about 25 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The count of that lane is zero on its sixth morning and the man at the bottom of it has been standing there four mornings and I have counted both of those and I have got two numbers and one of them has a person in it and I am not going to say which one and I am not going to be asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered that she is not asking him which one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who digs loam said out loud in that yard this morning that he has read a line of that book about nine times and that every time he has read it he has read about where he is, and that about four of them have said that a line which says go and ask that man is the first line in that book that has ever been read by the man it is about, and that about four of them have said that nobody has ever checked that before.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man who cuts reeds has been at the ninth mile four mornings and that the road keeper has not been asked about the eleven miles on any morning this month and has not been asked about the gate either. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and went on up the lane past the wall without stopping. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who cuts reeds has been standing at a gate at the ninth mile since half past six this morning and that the man on those stones has been at that low wall since before seven and that nobody has asked either of them one question about it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and forty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and forty-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and fifty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fourteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and eighty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and eighty-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and twenty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and sixty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and forty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man standing at a gate that nobody sent him to is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a person waiting is not a thing a district makes. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and ninety-five.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and four mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man reading a line of that book on his own account did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a man reading what is said about him is not a remedy and a remedy is not a reading. About five things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who is now findable on a public page is not paid and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the man who digs loam said out loud at that yard this morning that he has read that fifth line about nine times and that he read it about where he was, and that the woman who reads three of them every evening read the same three this evening, and that about four of them have said that there are now two people in this parish reading that book about themselves and neither of them is in the column for it. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that the third of the four figures on that wall is a day out and has been since the first morning of a volume two back and that about four of them have said that this morning the second of them crossed five hundred days and the third of them did not, and that nobody has worked out what that means and that about four of them have said it is not for them to work out.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirteenth of the fifth month about what he has read on a page about himself and not about what a man is to do, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is zero and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about ten with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
