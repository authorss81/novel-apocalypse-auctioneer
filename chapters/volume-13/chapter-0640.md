# Chapter 640: The Block Of Ten Mornings Ended On The Sixteenth Of The Sixth Month With The Fifth Line Working Exactly As It Was Written And With Nobody In That Yard Having Asked The Man It Names Anything About What He Is To Be Used For And With The Remedy Unpaid

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the sixteenth of the sixth month and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and thirty-eight mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and seventy-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is seven and did not move on its fortieth morning. Entered that the fifth month of this year was counted off that board on the ninth of this month and that the count of counted months is five, and entered that no figure about a month is on any page of hers except that one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-fifth of those mornings. About four of them have said that a clerk said out loud yesterday morning that a name is not a figure, and that about four of them have said that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud yesterday morning that he had been standing about, and that about four of them have said that a man who has read four figures off that wall three hundred and sixty-five times has still not been asked what one of them is for.

"**The board carries five hundred and thirty-eight, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and fifty-four days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and sixty-eight, and five hundred and twenty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Seven people have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month and there are five lines in that book and the fifth of them has been working for about a month, and about four of you have worked out that I have entered a figure about a person and put a question to a person and taken a word off a count in the last ten mornings, and about four of you have worked out that not one of those three is a thing this district has made, and about four of you have worked out that the count of the things this district has made is thirteen and has been thirteen on every morning of this month, and I would like that entered by somebody, and nobody has asked me to enter it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she has entered three things in ten mornings that are not a making, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 49 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been at this end of this table at about half past eleven on most mornings this month and I have said one thing out loud on almost every one of them, and about four of you have worked out that a man who says one thing at the same hour on the same morning is a man who is waiting, and I have worked that out about myself, and I am not going to stop, and about four of you have worked out that that is the first thing I have said out loud in that yard that is about me and not about that table, and about four of you have worked out that I said it on a morning when nobody asked me one question.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has worked out that he is waiting and that he is not going to stop, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about twelve and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 63 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man at that table has said out loud this morning that he has worked out a thing about himself and that he is not going to stop, and about four of you have worked out that a man who says that in front of about nineteen people has stopped being able to go on the way he was going, and about four of you have worked out that I have put two staples in a leg of that table and carried a coil of wire up that bank four mornings this month and taken a barrow of grit down it, and about four of you have worked out that I have not been asked for one item of that and have not been paid for one item of that, and about four of you have worked out that I have counted every morning of that off that wall and have not told anybody that I do it, and a clerk found out yesterday.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has not been paid for one item of what he has done this month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came down that lane at about half past twelve and stood about eight feet off the near end of that second table for about twenty minutes and said nothing to anybody, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man stood in that yard for about twenty minutes and said nothing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man at the far end of that second table has said out loud this morning that he has worked out that he is waiting, and that about four of them have said that this is the fortieth morning of that table being in that yard, and that about four of them have said that in forty mornings about nineteen people have said a great deal in that yard and that nobody has asked the man at the end of that line what he is to be used for, and that about four of them have said that a man who is waiting for something is not a man who has been asked it.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years has entered three things in ten mornings that are not a making and that the road keeper came up that lane twice a day through all ten of them and was not asked about one of the three.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and did not stay, and about four people at that wall have said since that he has not stopped at the top of that bank on any morning of this month and that about four of them have said that he did not stop this morning either. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man in that yard has said out loud that he is waiting, and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing in two hundred and seventy-nine nights, and that about four of them have said that he is two hundred and seventy-nine nights into a run and has slept on two hundred and seventy-eight of them and that about four of them have said that no person in this district has ever asked him which of the two he intends to break. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and seventy-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and seventy-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and eighty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and forty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and forty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and eighteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and twenty-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and fifty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and ninety-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and eighty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying out loud that he has worked out that he is waiting is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that what a man is waiting for is a thing a man carries and not a thing this district has made, and the count of the things this district has made has not moved on any morning of this month including today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and twenty-nine.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and thirty-eight mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and the yard may say so out loud once on a named day and nobody in that yard has said it out loud this month, and the count did not move on any of the ten mornings of it, because a condition with no end on it is a thing a document carries and the fifth line is a thing a man carries. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and ten mornings in that yard did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no, and that in forty mornings about nineteen people have said a great deal in that yard and that the man at the end of that line has said no to nobody. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who said out loud this morning that he is waiting is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and about nineteen people have said a great deal in that yard over ten mornings and none of it is a payment, and a man saying out loud this morning that he has worked out that he is waiting is not a payment either, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said out loud this morning that he is waiting, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and that about four of them have said that about nineteen people in that yard have now watched that same figure come up that bank about four hundred mornings and go down it without stopping, and that about four of them have said that she was not asked this month either.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-fifth morning, and that a clerk will enter this afternoon that a man of about fifty-six has said four figures off that wall three hundred and sixty-five times, and that a clerk will not enter that he has said them out loud, because nobody has asked her to, and that about four of them have said that no person in that yard has ever asked that man to stop, and that about four of them have said that nobody has.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the sixteenth of the sixth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that table for about a month working exactly as it was written, and that nobody in that yard has asked the man it names anything about what he is to be used for, and the reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, and the count of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is seven, and the count of counted months is five, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about twelve with his right hand flat on the boards and a hammer in his own hand.
