# Chapter 626: On The Second Of The Sixth Month A Man Of About Thirty-Two Who Puts Tables Up And A Man Of About Forty-Eight Who Keeps A Tally Said Two Things To Each Other Over A Table And A Person Put A Page Down On That Table And Took It Away Again

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the second of the sixth month and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had got a scraper in his own hand and was taking the last of the grey off the boards at the far end of that table where they had been wet all the week. The grit off them was going across the boards in a grey fan about the size of a hand, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and twenty-four mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and sixty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move. Entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is three and did not move on its twenty-sixth morning. Entered that a man answered a question in that yard yesterday and that she is not entering what a person said before he answered it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fifty-first of those mornings. About four of them have said that yesterday was the only morning this month when nothing in that yard was a question. About four of them have said that a man who has said four figures off that wall three hundred and fifty times running was not waiting for anything at about eleven yesterday and that about four of them have said that is the first morning anybody has been able to say that about him.

"**The board carries five hundred and twenty-four, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and forty days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and fifty-four, and five hundred and fifteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

A person of about twenty-nine with a page in their hand came down that lane at about a quarter to nine and came up that bank and went to the near end of that second table and put that page down face down on the boards about four inches from the end of it and stood there with both hands behind their back looking at it, and about nine people in that yard looked at it and said nothing at all, and about a minute afterwards the same person picked it up again and put it inside their own coat and went up that bank, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a page was put down on the end of that second table at about a quarter to nine and was taken off it at about ten to nine, and entered the reason, which is that the person who put it down said out loud that they wanted to see what it looked like on the same boards as the other one, and that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about 36 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A person put a page down on my table this morning and took it off it again and said a reason out loud while it was down there, and about four of you have worked out that that is the whole of the rule and that nobody in this yard has tested it since before that man went to that gate, and about four of you have worked out that a person put it down face down, and that I have not been able to work out why that is, and I have not asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she did not ask why a page was put down face down, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 39 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man asked a question in that yard yesterday at about eleven and a man answered it and the answer was that there is no question, and about four of you have worked out that that is the end of a thing this district has been doing since the sixth of this month, and about four of you have worked out that I put that line on that book and that what it does not say is now the thing that yard is talking about, and I am going to be asked about it by somebody and I would rather be asked than not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he would rather be asked than not, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the knee of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A person put a page on those boards this morning face down and took it off again in about a minute and about nine people looked at it and nobody said anything, and I have been told about it three times since I came up that bank, and about four of you have worked out that I am the only person in that yard who is not allowed to go over and look at it, and I am not going to say who that is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has never seen a page laid next to another one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about twelve, in about 35 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted that yard twice this morning and got the same number twice and a page went down on those boards at about a quarter to nine and nobody counted that, and about four of you have worked out that I have counted about a hundred and ninety things said out loud in that yard this month and one page laid on a table, and that the page is the first thing in a month I have not been able to put a number to afterwards, and I am not going to say why.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he could not put a number to a page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who puts tables up and a man who keeps a tally spent about forty minutes at the far end of that second table this morning with a scraper and a cross rail and said two things to each other in the ordinary voice that nobody in that yard wrote down. The man who puts tables up said that a page this yard wrote is the only thing in this parish that can send a man who has never set foot in it up a bank he has never climbed, and that it does that by not saying what it is for, and that every way of saying what it is for would have cost them the sending. The man who keeps a tally said back, without stopping, that he has kept a tally for nineteen years and that nothing he has ever written down has sent anybody anywhere either, and that on the sixth of this month somebody wrote four lines on a page in a clerk's hand and a stranger walked two days and stood in that yard with it, and that a clerk entered all three of those and entered neither of these two. And then neither of them said anything else about it and the scraping went on until about half past eleven.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a question was asked in that yard yesterday and none was asked this morning, and that the road keeper has come up that lane twice a day through both. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and went on up that lane past the wall without stopping. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man in that yard has answered a question and a man in that yard has said a thing about a page and that the man on those stones has done neither, and that about four of them have said that this is what a month of mornings does to a place. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and sixty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and sixty-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and seventy-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and twenty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and thirty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and forty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and eighty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and sixty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a page being put down on a table and taken off it is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a page is a thing a person carries and that the boards it lands on are the same boards whichever way up it goes. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and fifteen.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and a page carried into that yard and put down and taken up again is not a fifth of them and a clerk entered that reason with the figure, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and twenty-four mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man answering a question out loud yesterday did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that a man who says there is no question has not said no to anybody in that yard. About four things were said out loud in that yard today and two more were said over a table at the far end of it and neither of the two was entered, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who puts tables up and a man who keeps a tally saying two things to each other over a table is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a person laid a page on that table this morning and a woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six and did not stop and did not look, and that about four of them have said that she has read that book four hundred mornings and has not once said she was going to burn it. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that two men said two things over a table this morning that a clerk did not enter and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall twice and was not told about either of them, and that about four of them have said that is now the fifth morning this month that something has been said in that yard which is not on a page, and that about four of them have said that nobody has decided whether that is a good thing or not.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the second of the sixth month that a page was put down on the end of that second table and taken off it again, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is three and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven saying out loud that he would rather be asked than not.
