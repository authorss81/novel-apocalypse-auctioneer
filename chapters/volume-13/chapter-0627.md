# Chapter 627: A Man Asked The Road Keeper About The Eleven Miles On The Third Of The Sixth Month And The Road Keeper Answered Him And A Fourth Person Came Down That Lane At About Ten With A Page In Their Hand

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the third of the sixth month and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had left the grey fan of grit from yesterday's scraping on the boards at the far end of that table and had not swept it up. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds stood at the near end of that first table with his binder shut and his hands flat on it and said nothing to anybody between about eight and about ten. About four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and twenty-five mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and sixty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is three at about a quarter to eight on its twenty-seventh morning, and entered that she entered what she said out loud yesterday morning she would enter if that figure moved. Entered that the figure is four and that it moved later in the day with a person in the room and the reason in the room before the hand. Entered that a man stood in that yard for two hours and said nothing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fifty-second of those mornings. About four of them have said that a man has now been in that yard for a full month without saying one word out loud. About four of them have said that the fifth line in that book does not have his name in it and does not have the name of the man who digs loam in it either.

"**The board carries five hundred and twenty-five, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and forty-one days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and fifty-five, and five hundred and sixteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

And then a man of about fifty came down that lane at about ten with a page in his hand and about nine people at the top of that lane turned round, and he came up that bank and stopped at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have come down that lane four mornings this month and I have turned round at the gate three times, and this morning I did not, and I am going to say why and then I am going to stand here, and it is this. On the second morning there was a boy of about nineteen standing at the top of that lane at about six in the morning, and I could see him from about nine hundred yards off, and I did not go down.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person came down that lane with a page in their hand at about ten on the third of the sixth month and entered that they came down that lane and that they had a page in their hand and the hour, and entered that she is not entering his name, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is four and moved this morning with a person in the room and the reason in the room, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have written on this page every morning for nineteen years that a man came up that lane and that I did not put a question to him, and this morning I am going to enter that somebody did, and I am not going to strike out what I wrote yesterday, and about four of you have worked out that this is the first time in thirteen volumes that a sentence I have written every morning has been true and untrue on consecutive mornings, and about four of you have worked out that it is still on the page.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she did not strike out an entry, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 41 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has just told this yard that he did not go down that lane on the second morning because he could see a boy standing at the top of it, and about four of you have worked out that the boy went down it at six and the man was watching from about nine hundred yards off, and that a boy of nineteen has been standing at the top of that lane every morning this month and has never once been asked whether anybody was at the far end of it looking at him, and I am going to go and find out whether anybody was.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is going to find out whether anybody was watching, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then the road keeper came up that lane at about half past eleven instead of at half past one and stopped at the low wall, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him one thing out loud, in about 2 seconds, to a man about six feet away from him, and the boy counted what he said and got six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**How far is the eleven miles.**"

The road keeper took his own cap off with both hands and said one thing out loud, in about 41 seconds, to about nineteen people and the man who asked him, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is eleven miles and it has been eleven miles all my life and about a mile and a half of it has gone since the frost, and I have told that council twice about the mile and a half and been told twice, and that is my answer and I have not got another one, and about four of you have just worked out that I have been walking that road twice a day for nineteen years and that nobody in this yard has ever asked me that, and I am not asking you for anything, I am telling you what I came up this lane to say.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the road keeper was asked a thing out loud in that yard and entered that he answered it and entered the answer, and entered that he came up that lane at about half past eleven instead of half past one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about twelve with mud to the knee of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man asked the road keeper a question this morning and the road keeper answered it and about four of you have worked out that I have watched that man come up that lane for thirteen volumes, and about four of you have worked out that I never once thought to ask him a thing, and that is about the two of us and not about him, and he is owed an apology by me and not by anybody else in this yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he owes the road keeper an apology, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in about 40 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man of about fifty has told this yard that he could see me at the top of that lane from about nine hundred yards off on the second morning, and I have been at the top of that lane every morning this month about an hour before that, and about four of you have worked out that I did not know, and that about four of you have worked out that I counted that yard this morning eleven times and I have not counted that lane once, and that is not going to change and I am not going to say that it will.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted the yard eleven times, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man was asked a question in that yard this morning and answered it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop at the top of it, and stood at the top of that lane for about a minute afterwards. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man was asked a thing in that yard this morning and answered it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and sixty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and sixty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and seventy-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and thirty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and thirty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and forty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and eighty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and seventy marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a mile and a half of road going is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a road is a thing the weather is on and that this district makes things in a yard and has never counted a road. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and sixteen.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and twenty-five mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and the road keeper answering a question did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and that the man the fifth line sends people to has still said no to nobody. About six things were said out loud in that yard today, one of them a question and one of them an answer, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and the road keeper is not entered in it and the man who asked him is not entered in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a road keeper who has walked eleven miles twice a day for nineteen years and told a council twice and been told twice is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man at the ninth mile said out loud in that yard this morning that about a mile and a half of the road has gone, and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening came up past the same tables at about ten to six and said nothing to anybody and did not stop. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man was asked a question in that yard this morning and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about the road.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the third of the sixth month that the road keeper was asked a thing in that yard and answered it, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is four and moved this morning, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with a rail against the leg of it and his right hand flat on the boards.
