# Chapter 637: A Clerk Of Nineteen Years Said Out Loud Before She Did It On The Thirteenth Of The Sixth Month That She Was Going To Ask A Person In That Yard A Question And A Woman Of About Fifty-Eight Was Asked About One Of The Two Lines She Does Not Read And Said Nothing To Anybody

The boards of that second table were dry by about seven on the thirteenth of the sixth month and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and thirty-five mornings.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and seventy-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is six and did not move on its thirty-seventh morning. Entered that she said out loud in that yard at about a quarter to eight that she was going to ask a person in that yard one question about a line in that book before eleven, and entered the reason out loud with it, which is that she has been asked four times this month by people at that wall whether anybody in this district had ever asked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixty-second of those mornings. About four of them have said that a clerk told that yard at about a quarter to eight that she was going to ask somebody a question before eleven, and that about four of them have said that a clerk of nineteen years has said out loud this month that she was going to do a thing and then gone and done it, and that about four of them have said that that is twice this month and that nobody at that wall noticed the first time.

"**The board carries five hundred and thirty-five, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and fifty-one days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and sixty-five, and five hundred and twenty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter to eight, in about 35 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to ask a person in this yard a question about one line in that book before eleven this morning, and I am telling you that now, and about four of you have worked out that four people at that wall have asked me this month whether anybody in this district had ever asked, and that I have said no four times, and about four of you have worked out that I have never said no to anything else, and that today I am going to find out whether I was right.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she has said no four times to a question about whether anybody had ever asked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about eleven with an empty pail in her own hand and stopped about three feet off the near end of that first table to put the pail down, and the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about 2 seconds, to a woman about three feet away from her, and the boy counted what she said and got six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**What is on the fourth line.**"

The woman of about fifty-eight did not answer and did not move and picked her pail up again and went down that bank, and about nine people in that yard said nothing at all, and about four of them have said since that a clerk asked a woman a question in that yard at about eleven and that the woman said nothing to anybody, and that about four of them have said that this is the first morning this month that anybody in this district has asked anybody in this yard anything, and that about four of them have said that it is the first time in four hundred mornings that she has been asked. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she asked a person in that yard a question about a line in that book and entered the question, and entered that nothing was said to it, and entered that a clerk may not enter nothing said as an answer, and entered that she is entering it as a fact about a morning and not as a reading, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about half past eleven and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk asked a woman in that yard a question about the fourth line of that book this morning and the woman said nothing, and about four of you have worked out that the fourth line is the one that says he is not named here, and about four of you have worked out that a woman who has read three lines of that book every evening for about four months has just been asked about the fourth one by a clerk and not by me, and about four of you have worked out that I am the man who put a line on that book and that nobody has asked me one thing about the fourth line this morning, and that I would not have known what to do with it if they had.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody put a question to him about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about twelve with mud to the knee of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 62 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk asked a woman a question about the fourth line of that book this morning and the woman said nothing to anybody, and about four of you have worked out that she has been asked one question in about four hundred mornings and that a clerk entered it, and about four of you have worked out that I have been standing at this end of this table for about an hour working out whether I would have said something, and about four of you have worked out that I would have said something, and that is the whole difference between her and me and about four of you have worked out that I have got nothing on her and about four of them have said that is the first time anybody in this yard has said that about me and that about four of them have said that it was said on a morning when I had said a great deal yesterday.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he would have said something, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that five people in that yard have said nothing to anybody on one morning each this month, being the woman of about fifty-eight, the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds, the boy of about nineteen, the man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart, and the man of about sixty-four, and that this morning is the first time one of the five has been asked, and that about four of them have said that a clerk asked the question and not a person of about thirty-four, and that about four of them have said that about nine people in that yard said nothing at all when it was put.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk asked a woman a question in that yard at about eleven this morning and that the road keeper came up that lane afterwards and was not asked one thing about it, and that about four of them have said that he has walked that road twice a day through a morning when somebody in that yard was asked something for the first time in four hundred mornings.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and stood at the top of it for about a minute with his own face turned back down at that yard. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a woman in that yard was asked one question this morning and that this is the one of the five who has sat at the foot of that wall for two hundred and seventy-six nights, and that about four of them have said that he was not asked it and that about four of them have said that nobody thought to ask him and that about four of them have said that those are two different things and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever worked out which of the two it would be. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and seventy-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and seventy-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.
