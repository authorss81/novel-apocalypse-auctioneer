# Chapter 620: A Man Went Up That Bank On The Twenty-Seventh Of The Fifth Month And Asked The Woman Who Keeps A Scale A Second Time And She Said The Same Thing And It Was The Sixth Time She Had Said It And She Is Still Right

There was a skin of water on the boards of that second table by about seven on the twenty-seventh of the fifth month and the hollow in the middle of the stone lying face up on that table had about a sixteenth of an inch standing in it, and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had put his own coat over the near end of that table and left it there and gone up that bank, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table under the corner of that coat and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and eighteen mornings.

The clerk of nineteen years entered at about a quarter to eight, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and fifty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is two and did not move on its twentieth morning, and entered that a coat of a man who puts tables up was lying across two pages on her own table, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and forty-fifth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man went up that bank before eight.

"**The board carries five hundred and eighteen, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and thirty-four days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and forty-eight, and five hundred and nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went up that bank at about a quarter to nine and into the shop with the door propped open and stood at the end of that counter and asked the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale one thing out loud, in about 26 seconds, to a woman about four feet away from him, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am the second person to ask you this and I am not going to tell you what the first one was asked and I am not going to tell you who asked me to, and I am going to ask you the same thing in the same words, and if it is the wrong thing to ask you then I would rather know that now than in a month.**"

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale put her own hand flat on the beam of her own scale to stop it moving and said one thing out loud, in about 37 seconds, to about four people at her own door, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have never in my life been able to tell whether a page came out of a yard, and that is what I said the five times before this one and it is what I am saying now, and about four of you have worked out that a woman who says the same thing six times is not repeating herself, she is finding out whether it was true the first time, and about four of you have worked out that it was, and I am not going to take anything back off a counter to prove it.**"

There were about four people at that door and none of them came in, and one of them was the boy of about nineteen who had come up that bank on his own and had not gone to the top of that lane, and he stood on the step with his own back to the yard and counted what was said inside without saying the number out loud, and about four people who were not at that door have said since that a boy who has counted a yard every morning for a month stood in a shop on the twenty-seventh of the fifth month and counted two people in a room, and that about four of them have said he did not count himself.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in a shop and entered the thing, and entered that a woman said a thing out loud in a shop, and entered that she did not ask him who had asked him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came back down that bank at about eleven and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I asked a woman up that bank a thing this morning and she gave me the same answer she has given five other people and she gave it to me before I had finished asking, and about four of you have worked out that a person who answers you early is telling you something about the question and not about the answer, and I have been up that bank and down it again and I have not worked out what.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he went up that bank and came back, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about 31 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man went into a shop this morning and asked a question there and I have entered that he did and I have not entered what he asked, and about four of you have noticed that I enter everything else he says in that yard down to the foot of the rail he leans the rail on, and that the one thing I have not entered this month is the only thing anybody has gone up that bank to find out.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she did not enter the question, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about a quarter to twelve with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 33 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man has asked somebody a question this morning and got the same answer five other people have got, and about four of you have worked out that this is the only thing anybody in this parish has done twice this month and got the same thing, and about four of you have worked out that I do not know which of us he asked and I would like it said that I do not know and that I am not going to go and find out.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not know who had been asked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen came back down that bank at about twelve and said one thing out loud, in about 34 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I was in that shop this morning and I counted two people in there and I have not counted that shop before in my life, and about four of you have worked out that I have been counting a yard with about nineteen people in it for a month and this morning I counted a room with two people in it and the number was smaller and it took longer, and I am not going to say what I think that means and nobody has asked me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted a shop, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a woman of about thirty-six told a man this morning that she has never in her life been able to tell whether a page came out of a yard.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man went into that shop at about a quarter to nine and came out at about eleven and that the road keeper has not been asked one question in that shop in as long as it has been open. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a woman in a shop up that bank has said the same thing six times and that the man on those stones has not said one thing at all for about two volumes, and that about four of them have said that those are the only two people in this parish whose answer has not changed this month and that one of them changed hers on purpose. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fifty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and fifty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and sixty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and twenty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and twenty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and ninety-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and thirty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and seventy-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and sixty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a woman saying the same thing for the sixth time is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that the count of the five things this district does not have has a woman in it this morning and that a woman saying a thing out loud six times has not paid any of them. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and nine.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and a coat was lying across both of them for about an hour and neither page was wet and neither clerk nor man said one word about it, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and eighteen mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open. A shop counter is a place and it is not on that list of four and was not added to it this morning, and a clerk entered the reason. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a woman who has said the same thing six times did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens when that person says no and a woman with a beam on her counter has said no to a question and not to a person, and that this district has not worked out the difference and is not going to work it out today. About six things were said out loud today, two of them in a shop and four in that yard, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a woman who has answered six times in a shop is not entered in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a woman who has answered the same question six times in a shop with a door propped open is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a woman of about thirty-six has been asked the same thing six times and that the woman who reads three lines in that book every evening has been asked nothing at all, and that about four of them have said that one of those two women answers questions and one of them has not got a question, and that about four of them have said that this district has three pages on a shop counter with a door propped open and has never once asked the woman they sit beside what they are. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man went into that shop and came out two hours later having been told the same thing a woman has told five other people, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not told about that either.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-seventh of the fifth month that a man was asked a thing in a shop and that a woman said the same thing she has said five times before, and the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is two and did not move, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with a wet coat of another man's across the two pages at the end of it.
