# Chapter 613: A Man Came Down That Lane At About Half Past Ten With A Page In His Hand And The Number Of People Who Have Come Down That Lane Moved From Zero To One With A Person And A Reason In The Room

The stone lying face up on that second table had about a quarter of an inch of water standing in the hollow in the middle of it by about seven on the twentieth of the fifth month and a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had put his own thumb in it and wiped it down his own leg without saying one word, which he has not done on any morning in about a month, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone to the top of that lane and had stayed there in different positions from each other, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and eleven mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and fifty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of this month is zero and did not move on its thirteenth morning, and entered that about four people went to the top of that lane before eight and that she is not entering that as a figure about the lane, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and thirty-eighth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that about four people have been standing at the top of that lane before eight every morning this fortnight and that two of them had moved about four feet apart by about ten to eight and had not said one word to each other about it.

"**The board carries five hundred and eleven, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and twenty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and forty-one, and five hundred and two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eight, in about 43 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have entered that number at a quarter to eight every morning for thirteen mornings and I enter it before anything else and I have never once said out loud what I would do if it went to one, and I am going to say it now and then I am going to do it, and it is this. If a man comes down that lane with a page in his hand I am going to write down that he came down that lane and that he had a page in his hand and the hour, and I am not going to write his name and I am not going to write where he goes.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering his name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about nine and went to the top of that lane and stood there about four minutes and came back down it and stood at the near end of that second table with his right hand flat on the boards and said one thing out loud, in about 28 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk has said out loud what she will write down if that number goes to one and she has said it before it went, and I asked a man a question on the second of this month and did not ask it again, and I want it entered that the two of those are the same kind of thing and that I have only worked that out this morning and not before.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he went to the top of that lane and came back, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about ten, in about 30 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have stood at the top of that lane every morning this fortnight and about nine people have walked down it past me and not one of them looked at me, and that is nine, and about four of you have worked out that I could have spoken to any of them and did not, and about four of you have worked out that I did not because I did not know what I was going to say.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted at the top of that lane and not in the middle of it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then a man came down that lane at about half past ten with a page in his hand, and about nine people at the top of that lane turned round, and the boy of about nineteen did not turn round for about four seconds and then did, and the man came up that bank and stopped about six feet short of that second table with the page out in his own hand and said one thing out loud, in about 25 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have had this about nine days and I have carried it down that lane and up it twice and I did not know whether I was to come up that bank or go on to the water, and somebody has read it to me in a shop and told me to go down there, and I am asking whether it came from this yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man came down that lane with a page in his hand at about half past ten on the twentieth of the fifth month and entered that he came down that lane and that he had a page in his hand and that he asked whether it came from that yard, and entered that she is not entering his name and entered that she is not entering where he goes, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is one and moved this morning with a person and a reason in the room, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter to eleven, in about 32 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is one. I entered what I said this morning I would enter and I entered it, and he has not asked me a single question since he came up that bank and about four of you have worked out that he is standing here waiting to be asked the way the page says to ask, and about four of you have noticed that he came from the far end and not from the water and that the fifth line was not what brought him here.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he came from the far end, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the knee of his own right leg and stood about nine feet off that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 25 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have not seen that page and I am not going to take it off him, and about four of you have been standing in that yard for thirteen mornings waiting for me to be the answer and I would like it said out loud that I have never in my life been able to tell whether a page came from a yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not take a page off a man, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man came down that lane at about half past ten with a page in his hand and stopped about six feet short of that second table and asked whether it came from that yard, and about four of them have said that the fifth line on that book tells a man to go down that lane and past the gate to the water, and that the man who came down that lane this morning came from the far end and not from the water, and that about four of them have said that this district spent thirteen mornings getting a line ready for a man and a man came from the other direction and asked a question the line was not written to answer.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man came down that lane at about half past ten and that the road keeper comes up it at half past one and did not see him and that about four of them have said that if the road keeper had come down that lane an hour earlier he would have. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and stopped at the top of it for about two minutes. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man came down that lane this morning and asked whether a page came from that yard and that the man on those stones has not been asked one thing this morning. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fifty-second night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and fifty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and sixty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and sixteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and twenty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and ninety-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and ninety-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and thirty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and seventy marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and fifty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and one is not a fourteenth thing and did not become one by moving, and a clerk entered the reason out loud at about a quarter to eleven with the count itself, which is that a count moving is not a thing a district makes and this district has never worked out the difference and is not going to work it out today. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and two.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and eleven mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and one man coming down that lane did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that the fourth of the five has been a person since a page in this yard first carried one and a stranger carrying one up a bank does not make him the answer. About six things were said out loud in that yard today, one by a man not from this parish, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a stranger who says a thing out loud in that yard is not entered in it at all.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a stranger coming down a lane to be answered by a person this district has no way to pay is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man came down that lane with a page in his hand and the woman who reads three lines every evening walked up past him with eleven buckets on her hip and did not stop, and that about four of them have said that about four people in that yard have now been told out loud that they are waiting to be asked and that nobody has asked him one question this morning. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man came down that lane this morning and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about the stranger and neither was he about anything else, and that about four of them have said that he has now been at that wall for three hundred and thirty-eight mornings without a single person in that yard stopping him and that nobody has ever thought to stop him.

The clerk of nineteen years entered at about half past eleven in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twentieth of the fifth month that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is one and moved this morning with a person and a reason in the room, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on either of the two pages.
