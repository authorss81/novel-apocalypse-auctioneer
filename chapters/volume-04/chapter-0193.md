# Chapter 193: What The Minute Did

---

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning on the fifteenth of the third month unasked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that no one in this district sent him and that she is not going to ask him for a reason.

He said that a man with a slate had come to the end of the lane off the lower terrace on the afternoon of the fourteenth and had asked for him by what he was and not by his name, and that he had said no, and that the man had not gone away.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the man with the slate had said after that, and the man of about twenty-three gave the answer himself, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**He said there was a book that says a man in a yard read a sheet out loud in four lines on the twenty-second of the second month, and he said the book does not have your name in it and does not have to.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything for a moment. A man of fifty-six said that the four lines of the twenty-second of the second month are in a page in a book, and that a page in a book is a page a stranger may read, **and that a page a stranger may read is a half an instrument, and that the half this district is short is not the half that goes out of the room, it is the half that goes out of the district.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the reading of the arithmetic on the twenty-second of the second month is in a page in the minute, and that a minute is a document a person has decided to keep, **and that a document a person has decided to keep is the opposite of a document nobody owns, and that the count of documents nobody owns is three and did not move on the fifteenth of the third month and a page in a minute is not a fourth.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half of that, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The district's best instrument is a book that works if a person comes to the yard. That is what makes it strong and it is exactly what made this happen, and a thing that is strong at one end is the thing that lets a man in at the other.**"

It was not contradicted. A man of fifty-six said that this district has spent four months saying out loud that a page nobody owns is the only reason it has ever been able to prove a thing against a house, and that a page nobody owns cannot be read by a stranger and therefore cannot be found by one, **and that a man who copies a page and gives the copy to a building with a door on it has changed a document nobody owns into a document somebody owns, and that the somebody is an office and the office is hers and the office is not this district's.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that no one in that yard has said a word about her office and that a man of fifty-six said it and that he is not going to be asked about it, **and that the rule against the office's name is not a rule and is not a column and the office is in five places and in no register and was not gone near on the fifteenth of the third month.**

**AND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A MAN WITH A SLATE CAME TO THE END OF A LANE AND ASKED FOR THE MAN WHO COPIES, AND HE SAID THAT A BOOK IN THIS DISTRICT DESCRIBES HIM AND DOES NOT HAVE TO HAVE HIS NAME IN IT.**

---

Then the finding of the day, and it was given by the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, and he gave it standing up with his hand flat on the table, and about nine people were in that yard and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him for it.

"**Four months. Every fact this district has proved about a house has been carried into a building this district does not own, by a person this district does not pay, and two of the people who carried them were not in a household and were not asked and were not on anything. The yard published the method. The method is a room with a door on it, and the door has a name over it.**"

Nobody argued with it. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether the yard had published anything, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said no and said the rest of it before he was asked for it, **and that a yard that has published nothing and still got a man found at the end of a lane has found out that the district's instrument is not the disclosure and is the room, and that the disclosure was the excuse.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the word published was used and that nothing has been published in this district in four months, **and that a man who says the word about his own yard in front of nine people is a man and not a rule and is not written down and she was not asked why he said it.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half of the finding, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked to write the two halves in one line and did and that they are not joined, **and that a man who copies for nothing is not a security and is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not a party and is not an instrument, and the count of six did not move on the fifteenth of the third month.**

The man of about twenty-three said the other half of that himself, and it was not about himself, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-eight.

"**A house is not owed a list of what it has done. A house is owed nothing at all until somebody asks it for something, and this district has spent a year making lists for houses that have not asked. That is the work that is not paid.**"

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a man who copies for nothing has just named the fifth of the five things this district does not have, **and that the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and that the work of making a list for a house that has not asked is done by about six people in this district and is not paid to any of them.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked how many people, and a man of fifty-six said that he does not know and that the district has no way of knowing and has not asked, **and that a count of six came out of a room once in the second month and was a count of a room and is not a column and is not a rate and is not going to be entered as one.**

**AND THE FINDING OF THE FIFTEENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH IS THAT THE YARD PUBLISHED NOTHING, AND A MAN WAS FOUND AT THE END OF A LANE ANYWAY, AND THE INSTRUMENT IS THE ROOM AND NOT THE DISCLOSURE, AND THE WORK OF THE UNPAID LISTS IS THE FIFTH OF THE FIVE THINGS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE.**

---

He came back up the bank on the sixteenth of the third month at about half past eleven in the morning with the copy flat against his chest under his coat and about four people in that yard, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him and that she is not going to ask him for the copy.

He put it on the trestle table face up this time and it was not the same gesture as the one on the twenty-second of the second month, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a page put down face up on a table in front of people is a page a person has decided to do something with, **and that a page put down face up is not a copy and is not a held thing and is not a document nobody owns and is not a fourth of anything.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, what he wanted done with it, and he said that he wanted it in the records office and that he wanted it read once and that he wanted to be in the room while it was read.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked a question and answered it and that nobody put a second thing to him, **and that a man who asks for a page to be read once in a room he is standing in is a man who wants to have been in the room, and that is not a consent and is not a release and is not a security and cannot be entered as any of the three.**

The registrar of this district's records office was in that yard and had not been sent for, and she read it once, and it took her about four minutes, and she did not read it out and nobody asked her to.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the copy went into a drawer in a building with a door on it, and that a drawer is not a keeper and is not a bearer and is not a holder, **and that a page in a drawer in a building is the same shape as a page in a drawer in a building thirty-four miles east, and that the two of those are not joined and have not been joined and are not going to be.**

A man of fifty-six said that the registrar read the sheet and the copy is now the only copy in this district and it is in a building a stranger can walk into and ask for, **and that the count of documents nobody owns is three and the copy is not a fourth, and that a man who owned a page has given it to a room and is now a man who gave something away, and that those are two men and not one.**

It was not picked up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man who gave a page to a room is a man who is findable in a room and a man with a page under his coat is a man who is findable in a lane, **and that the district has moved him from one kind of findable to another and did not ask him which he would rather have, and that is on us and is entered as it stands.**

The man of about twenty-three said the thing he had come for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she wrote it in her own words at his request and that he asked for it in a yard and was not asked why.

"**I gave it up. Nobody took it. Put both of those down and put down which one of them is true.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything about it for a moment. A man of fifty-six said that both of them are true and that the distinction is the whole of what a person can have when there is no money in the district, **and that a rule said out loud by the man it costs him is not a rule and is not written down and he would not have it written down.**

The registrar of this district's records office said the other thing then, and she said it in the ordinary voice of a person who has thought about a thing for a day, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-seven, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it is in the minute in her own words.

"**My instrument is a book that works if a person comes to the yard, and I have said that three times in four months, and the fourth time is today and there is a reason for it.**"

Nobody argued with that. A man of fifty-six said that a registrar who says her own instrument sentence a fourth time in four months has put a reason on the face of it, **and that a reason is not a column and is not a rate and is not going to be asked for a second time, and that the two of those are the same promise and only one of them is hers.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she had been asked, in the six things on the sixteenth of the third month, whether the page with the four lines in it was going to be taken out of the book, and that the registrar said no and gave no reason, **and that a removal refused with no reason is a fourth refusal with no reason in this district's business and four are four and are not a column and are not added to the thirteen.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the page is staying in a book and the copy is in a drawer in the same office and that between them this district has now got a document it can produce and a document it cannot, **and that those are two halves of a thing and neither half is the half the house was looking for and that is the only good news in the afternoon.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked who the man with the slate was, and the man of about twenty-three said what he had been told and gave it in one sentence that a man of about nineteen counted and got twenty-eight.

"**A man of about thirty-four with a slate and a string, at the head of the lane, and he said four coppers for a sheet about one thing.**"

Nobody picked that up. A man of fifty-six said that a rate is a rate and that a man who says a rate out loud at the end of a lane is quoting somebody, **and that the two figures anybody in this district knows for a sheet are the two the house prints it for and they are not the same two, and that a man quoting a rate he did not set is a man with a house behind him and not a man in a lane.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure was said and that it was not entered as a price and that nothing in this district has been printed this month, **and that a printed sheet with a rate on it is a thing a house does and this is not this district and the count of six did not move on the sixteenth of the third month.**

**AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH THE ONLY COPY OF A HOUSE'S SHEET IN THIS DISTRICT WENT INTO A DRAWER IN A BUILDING WITH A DOOR ON IT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE SAID THAT HE GAVE IT UP AND THAT NOBODY TOOK IT, AND THE COUNT OF DOCUMENTS NOBODY OWNS IS THREE.**

---

The rest of the sixteenth was short and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the only question anybody asked that afternoon, and he asked it in the six things at about half past three in the afternoon and nobody answered it.

He asked whether a district that has just found out that its instrument is a room with a door on it was going to say so on a board, and a clerk of nineteen years said no in about two seconds and gave no reason at all, and nobody asked her for one.

A man of fifty-six said that the board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for eighty-two days and that it has not been washed, **and that the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty and that it has been ruled and empty since the second of January, and that a finding does not go on a board because a finding is not a date and four other people set the four that are on it.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the question about the tank and the boy's name is a day and a half old now and has not been answered, **and that the district has now got a second reason not to answer it, and that the second reason is better than the first and neither of them is going to be in the minute as a reason.**

Then the other instrument, and it came out of a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asking whether anybody knew what else in this district could find a man the way that slate did, and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said the answer before anybody had finished the question.

She said that she goes into about forty houses a week and that a man with a slate and a string does not have to ask a person in a yard, he has to ask a person with a board, **and that there are about three people in this district a house could ask and two of them have already been asked this month and one of them is sitting in this yard with a copy of the sheet on the table in front of him.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she said the second half before she was asked for it and that she is not going to put a number on it, **and that a person who goes into about forty houses a week is an instrument this district has and has entered none of and is not going to, and that the count of six did not move on the sixteenth of the third month.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that the district has three instruments that work without anybody coming to a yard, which are a bucket, a basket and a barrow, and that on the fourteenth of the third month a fourth of them came up a bank with his hands empty, **and that a fourth instrument that can be found by a man with a slate is a worse thing than three instruments that cannot, and that this is the first time in four months that one of the four has been a reason to shut one of the four.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that nobody in that yard is going to ask her to stop going into forty houses, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said that in front of about nine people and that she was not asked to agree with it and did not, **and that a woman who takes in washing is not a column and is not a not-asking and is not a protected thing and is not one of the five and is not going to be a sixth of the five.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether the man with the slate had asked her, and the registrar said she did not know and that she was not going to find out and was not going to write to anybody, **and that a records office which writes to a house to ask whether a house has been asking is a records office doing the house's work, and that this office is not going to do that and did not do it on the fourteenth and will not do it on the seventeenth.**

The interval was given out loud at about half past four in the afternoon by the man of fifty-six, and a clerk of nineteen years stood near enough to take it, and the sum went underneath the figure the way it goes in this district.

"**The thirty-first of March is fifteen days off this day and the first of April is sixteen, and the first of April is the last day a person may put their hand to a notice, and a notice in this district costs nobody anything to give and everybody a year to have given.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody wrote on a board on the sixteenth of the third month and that the count of boards is five and the count of lines is six, **and that the train on the siding had stood three hundred and ninety-eight days on this day, and that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is a figure a clerk keeps and does not read out and is not a column.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against walked down to the end of the lane at about five in the afternoon and stood in the ruts with his hand in one of them, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam went with him, and neither of them said anything for about a quarter of an hour.

The lane is not metalled for about a mile and a half and the last half mile of it is where the cart ruts are, and a man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing had stood in them a month ago and put the flat of his hand into one and told this district what hour a cart could get through. Nobody said that out loud. The man who digs loam said the other half of it out loud, which is that a lane is a lane and that two men have now stood in the same ruts in the same month and one of them read a sheet out loud and one of them did not.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the thing he had come down for, and he said it to a rut and not to the man beside him, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-two.

"**A page is the only thing I have ever made that works. Today I found out that working is what it does to the people who carry it, and I am not going to say I did not know, because I did.**"

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man who says that in a lane is a man who has said the second half before the asking again, and that a clerk would not put a number on it and was not there, **and that a man who admits a thing in a lane instead of a yard is a man who has worked out that a yard takes it and makes it a rule, and that the difference between those is the whole of what this district has learned this month.**

Then they walked back up the hill in the wet and neither of them said anything else, and there was no clerk on the lane and nothing was entered on the way and both of them said so at the top of it, **and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against had come down that lane four times in ten days and had carried a bucket up a hill four times and had stood on a bank a mile from a sluice for eight hours, and none of it is in a book and the count of six did not move on the sixteenth of the third month.**

**AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM WALKED DOWN TO THE END OF A LANE WITH THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID ANYTHING, AND A QUESTION ASKED ON THE TENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH IS A DAY AND A HALF OLD AND HAS NOT BEEN ANSWERED.**
