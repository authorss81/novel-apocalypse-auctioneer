# Chapter 166: The Name Is Not Copied

---

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row came into the yard of Lot Seventeen at about four in the afternoon on the twenty-ninth of January, and he had walked back on the twenty-first with the paper in his coat and had kept it eight days in a drawer in his forge, and he said that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had been out at the forge on the twenty-eighth and had said out loud in the yard that a receipt in a drawer is a receipt nobody has read, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him.**

He had two things and he put them both on the trestle table. The first was the clean sheet of about nine inches by six, and it had nothing written on it, and he said that a man about forty behind a counter had looked at it and given it back.

The second was a piece of paper about the size of his own hand with a house's name printed at the top of it, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a house's name is on it and that the house is thirty-four miles east and that nobody asked him which house and he said it out loud without being asked.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JANUARY THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY AT A FORGE END OFF SALT ROW CAME INTO THE YARD WITH A CLEAN SHEET WITH NOTHING ON IT AND A PIECE OF PAPER WITH A HOUSE'S NAME PRINTED AT THE TOP OF IT, AND HE SAID THE NAME OUT LOUD BEFORE ANYBODY ASKED HIM FOR IT, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT HE HAD CARRIED IT EIGHT DAYS.**

---

A man of fifty-six read it out and then read it out again, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong both times, and the paper is about the size of a man's hand and there are four lines on it.

> **RECEIVED: ONE DRAY AND ITS TWO SHAFTS, IN THE FOURTEENTH YEAR, AT THIS COUNTER.**
>
> **THE FIGURE GIVEN FOR IT IS ENTERED AGAINST THE FIGURE OWED.**
>
> **THE PERSON GIVING THE SECURITY IS DISCHARGED WITH THE THING, AND THE NAME IS NOT COPIED.**
>
> **A STRANGER MAY READ THIS AND MAY NOT TAKE IT AWAY.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds and a man of fifty-six said the finding out loud in the ordinary voice, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in her own words.

"**A discharged security is only discharged if somebody received it, and somebody received it in the fourteenth year, and this district has had the man on a page for nine years, and the two figures are five years and nine years, and the two are not joined and neither of them can be got off the other.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five posted securities is a man and that the fourth of the five is a piece of paper about the size of his hand, **and that the district's figure and the house's figure are two figures about one man and that the two are not joined, and that a figure a person can only be got out of by another party's paper is not a figure this district can strike, and a figure this district cannot strike is a figure the man is still standing behind.**

Nobody took it up for about nine seconds. A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace said that the third of the four lines says the name is not copied, and that the line is the reason nobody in this district could find it, and that a house that discharges a man and does not write his name down is a house that has made the discharge unarguable, **and that it is the only sentence about a person this district has found written by somebody who did not have to be asked to write it.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JANUARY A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY BEHIND A COUNTER THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST SHOWED A RECEIPT WHOSE THIRD LINE SAYS THE PERSON GIVING THE SECURITY IS DISCHARGED WITH THE THING AND THAT THE NAME IS NOT COPIED, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE READ IT OUT TWICE IN A YARD, AND THE HOUSE'S FIGURE IS FIVE YEARS AND THIS DISTRICT'S IS NINE, AND THE TWO ARE NOT JOINED.**

---

Then the cost, and it came out of the room in about nine seconds, and the man of about thirty-one said it to the man the figure of twenty-one years is against and not to the yard, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it to him and that she was not asked to leave and did.**

"**I have been in a forge for nine years and I never did a season of cartage, and the thing I was given for is a season of cartage, and nobody has ever asked me for it and nobody ever will.**"

Nobody took it up. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said the second half of that before anybody asked him for it and that she is not going to put a number on it, **and that a man who was given as a security for a season's work and has not done the season's work is a figure standing against a person and that the two are not the same and that the figure is not struck.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him about the ninth year, in the six things, at about half past four in the afternoon, and the man of about thirty-one answered it, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is forty-eight words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got forty-eight and said it out loud.

"**A man came to my forge in the ninth year and asked me for the dray and said he would take it for a season's cartage and gave me a paper with three lines on it, and I signed the third, and I did not read the second.**"

Nobody argued with it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked in the six things and answered and was not asked why he did not read the second line, **and that a person who signs a line and has not read the line is not a person who has refused it and is not a person who has consented to it, and that the record on that is not asked and must say not asked.**

A man of fifty-six said that the second line of a bond of the ninth year says the said dray is discharged upon sale, and that the man signed the third, and that the second half of the ninth year is the one nobody in this district has ever read out loud, **and that a house which discharges a man with the thing and does not copy his name did it five years ago without anybody in this district being told, and that this district has been asking for it for nine and has been asking the wrong people.**

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide was in the yard and said one thing about that, and it is in the minute in her own words and it is forty-one words, and the man of about nineteen counted them and got forty-one and said it out loud.

"**There are two houses in this and one of them will not write a boy's name in a column and the other will not write a man's name down after it has paid him. Neither of them has ever asked him.**"

Nobody took that up and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two are a house thirty-four miles east and a counter two days that way, and that the two are not joined, **and that a sentence said twice by two houses is two sentences and that a clerk of nineteen years was asked to make it one and said no.**

A man of fifty-six said that the figure is entered against the figure owed and that the line on the receipt says so, and that a house that enters a figure against a figure is a house that has a rate, **and that there is no rate in this basin that turns one year into coppers and there never has been and there is one on that piece of paper and neither of them can read it.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that the figure is on the receipt and that the receipt may be read by a stranger and may not be taken away, **and that a man who cannot take a page out of a building cannot do arithmetic on it in a yard, and that this district has spent eleven weeks insisting on a stranger being able to go and look at a thing and has not once insisted on being able to carry it.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a security a stranger can look at and cannot carry is half an instrument, and that the district's own sentence in the counter's hand does not have a half in it, **and that a half of the district's method is a finding and the other half is the thing the finding is about and they are not joined.**

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in the yard at about half past four and was not asked for anything, and she said that the four dates on her bucket are the first of the second month and the sixteenth of the second month and the twenty-fourth of the third month and the last day of the third month, and that the first of them is tomorrow.

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that a woman who does not read figures has said out loud in a yard the day before the district's nearest date came, and that a district whose nearest date is said by a woman carrying water is a district being told its own dates by somebody who is not in a room, **and that a bucket is a better register than a board because nobody can wash a bucket, and that sentence has been in this district since the twelfth of January and is not going to be said a third time.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of not-askings about the woman of fifty-eight is not a column and carries no number on it, and that she is not to be asked again about her own not-asking, **and that the first of the second month is one day from the thirtieth of January, and that the district's nearest date came on a day nobody in this district had named, and that the bucket had it in it in the fourth month of the year.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, what he wanted written down, and the man of about thirty-one said that he wanted the ninth year written down, and gave no reason, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked and answered and was not asked why.**

**AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY AT A FORGE END OFF SALT ROW SAID THAT HE HAS BEEN IN A FORGE FOR NINE YEARS AND NEVER DID A SEASON OF CARTAGE, AND THAT NOBODY HAS EVER ASKED HIM FOR IT AND NOBODY EVER WILL, AND HE ASKED FOR THE NINTH YEAR TO BE WRITTEN DOWN AND GAVE NO REASON.**

---

The thirtieth of January was the day the list was not written for the fourth time in a month, and a man of fifty-six asked about it in the yard and got the same answer he got in the second week, and the answer was longer this time and a clerk of nineteen years entered all of it.

"**Four of the five I can say. The fifth is a man a house says was discharged in the fourteenth year and the paper is in a building I have not been in, and my own page says nine years and does not have a figure to take off him. A list a stranger can walk up and read is the whole of the method and this one has a line in it that says a stranger may read this and may not take it away.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a line like that is the fourth thing this district cannot get round and that the three of them it has got round are a page, a chalk line and a bucket, **and that a page works if a person comes to the yard and a chalk line works if a man does not wash it and a bucket works if a woman carries it, and that the three of those depend on a person and the fourth depends on a house.**

The room did not argue and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who cannot list what he has posted cannot pool a single toll, and that the Underwriter's power is not begun in this district, **and that the reason has changed its shape since the second of January and has not moved, and that the reason is entered and the reason is not a delay.**

A man of fifty-six said that is the first thing in four months that has changed shape without moving, and that he is not going to call it progress, **and that a list which cannot be written is a list this district does not have, and that a district which has not written its list has never had its securities in writing, and that a man a house says was discharged five years ago is still a man in a bond in a box in this district.**

**AND ON THE THIRTIETH OF JANUARY THE LIST WAS NOT WRITTEN FOR THE FOURTH TIME IN A MONTH, AND A MAN A HOUSE SAYS WAS DISCHARGED IN THE FOURTEENTH YEAR IS STILL A MAN IN A BOND IN A BOX IN THIS DISTRICT, AND THE UNDERWRITER'S POWER IS NOT BEGUN IN THIS DISTRICT AND THE REASON HAS CHANGED ITS SHAPE AND HAS NOT MOVED.**

---

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households at the sluice end of the loams came down the bank at about half past four and said that the man who lives in a cart has been asked to sign the same sheet and has not signed, and that he was not asked twice and was not asked why.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sixth household on his board is a man who lives in a cart and has no roof, and that his name is written under the sixth heading with a roof drawn beside it in charcoal, **and that a man with no household cannot be given a sheet of this shape because the sheet is given to a family, and that this is the only person in this district's business that a sheet of this kind cannot touch, and that a person a sheet cannot touch is not a protected thing and is not a defence and is a shape.**

A man of fifty-six said that about nine hundred households in this district are in no row of anything and that nineteen people at the ninth place are not in a household, and that a man in a cart is one of them, **and that a district which has spent four months arguing about the people it can name has one person it cannot reach and that person is safe by accident and not by design and the two are not the same.**

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a sheet is given to a family and that a family is a thing with a name on it, and that the whole of the trouble in this district's business is that a sheet is given to a family and takes a person out of one, **and that a man with no family cannot be taken out of anything and that nobody in this district has ever said that out loud before this afternoon.**

Nobody took that up and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of things this district does not have is five and that a way to pay a person who is not in a household is one of the five, **and that a sheet a person cannot be given is not one of the five and is not entered, and that the two are not joined and the count did not move on the thirtieth of January.**

---

The registrar of this district's records office was in the yard at about five o'clock and was asked, in the five things, by a man of fifty-six, whether her office was going to enter a discharge, and she said no in four seconds and gave the reason in a sentence.

"**I will not enter a discharge I have not seen. I have not seen it. That is the whole of my reason and there is no second half of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked and answered and was not asked why, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a records office which enters only what it has seen is a records office and not a newspaper, and that this district has one of those and has never said so out loud until the thirtieth of January.**

A man of fifty-six said that a discharge a person has not seen is not a discharge this district can rely on and is not one it can refuse, and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came back into this yard on the eighteenth of January with a page and did not take it to the ward market counter at all, **and that a man who had a document and did not enter it has made the same choice the woman of about forty-four made about a name and is not going to be told he is the same as her.**

Nobody argued. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the discharge was for if nobody was going to write it down, and a man of fifty-six said that it was for the man, and that the man had it, **and that a thing done for a person is not a document and this district has no column for things done for a person and is not going to make one.**

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said that he read the third line of the receipt twice and that the third line is the shortest sentence anybody has ever read out in this yard, and that it does not say the man was paid, and it does not say the man is forgiven, **and that it says the man is discharged, which is a word about a figure and not about a person, and that a person discharged by a word about a figure is a person and is a person and the two are not the same.**

Nobody argued with that and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a word about a figure applied to a person is the same shape as a name put on a figure, **and that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, and that the third line of that receipt takes a figure off a man and not a name, and that the two are two rules and only one of them can be used on a person and the one that can is the one a house uses.**

The man of about fifty-six walked about nine yards out from the middle of the yard and stopped there while all of that was going on, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he stopped and that she was not asked about it and was not going to be.

---

The last of it was the clean sheet, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten asked him why he had brought it back, and he said that he had been asked to take it and that taking it was the only part of the day he had understood.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a sheet a person takes away from a counter is the only proof anybody ever has and that he had left his proof in a yard, **and that a man who has been a security for nine years on one side of this basin and five years on the other and has brought his proof back to a yard has done the one thing in this month's business that was not a question.**

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a printed gap is the cheapest thing a house can build, and that this house has printed no gap at all, **and that a man who is handed a clean sheet by a house and given it back and cannot think what to do with it has been handed the only thing in this district's business that costs a person nothing to refuse.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about thirty-one whether he would have the ninth year written down in a book somewhere a stranger could read it, and he said no in four seconds and gave the reason in a sentence, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the reason in his own words and it is thirty-one words.

"**That is for a room. If it goes in a book I am a man in a book and I have had nine years of being a thing in a box.**"

The yard did not argue with that. A man of fifty-six said that a man who will not be entered in a book is a man exercising the district's own sentence in the direction it was written, **and that the sentence says a security is a thing a stranger can go and look at, and that a man who has been looked at for nine years and has been told by a house that he was discharged five years ago and will not be looked at again has said the second half of it out loud and nobody asked him for the first half.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of not-askings about the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row is not a column and carries no number on it, **and that a man who says the second half before he is asked is a fact and not a count and that she is not going to give it one, and that the count of the second half said before being asked has not been looked at since the twenty-ninth of December.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a man in a box is in a box whatever a house says, and that a man in a box with a bond in it that says discharged on sale and a receipt thirty-four miles away that says the person is discharged with the thing and a man saying yes to that outside a forge end off Salt Row are three pieces of one page, **and that the two halves are in two places nine hundred yards apart and that neither half can see the other and that this district is the only thing in this basin that has ever known about both.**

The train on the siding had stood three hundred and fifty-three days on the thirtieth of January, and a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and beside the index there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date.

**AND ON THE THIRTIETH OF JANUARY A MAN WHO HAD BEEN A SECURITY FOR NINE YEARS ON A PAGE IN THIS DISTRICT AND FIVE YEARS ON A RECEIPT THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST CAME BACK INTO A YARD WITH A CLEAN SHEET HE HAD BEEN GIVEN AND GIVEN BACK, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WHO DEALS IN SECOND-HAND PAPER SAID THAT LEAVING HIS PROOF IN A YARD IS THE ONE THING IN THIS MONTH'S BUSINESS THAT WAS NOT A QUESTION.**
