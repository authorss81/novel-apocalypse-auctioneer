# Chapter 161: Seventy-Six Days and Seventy-Seven

---

The room met for the third time with nothing on the board that said it was going to meet, and the board outside its door had carried the twenty-fourth of December for twenty-one days, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said at about ten in the morning that a board which has carried a spent date for three weeks is not a board and is a sign.

A clerk of nineteen years said that nobody had asked her to write a fifth date and that she had not offered, **and entered that she has now been asked nothing about it twice and is not keeping a count of that, because a count of it would have been a column and a column is a thing a person is paid for.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a sign tells a person what is there and a board tells a person when.

The fourth of the four was the only date on that plank anybody could argue with, and a room which washes the last date off has told every stranger who comes into the yard that there is no date, **and that a stranger cannot tell the difference between a room that has no date and a room that has ended, and the two look the same to anybody standing in the yard.**

Then the man who mends fencing asked for a fifth date, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and the clerk said no in four seconds and gave the reason in a sentence, and the sentence is in the minute in her own words and it is thirty-one words.

"**A fifth date is a date somebody is expecting to arrive. This board has never once carried a date anybody was expecting and I am not going to give it one.**"

**AND ON THE FOURTEENTH OF JANUARY THE BOARD OUTSIDE THE ROOM HAD CARRIED THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF DECEMBER FOR TWENTY-ONE DAYS AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED FOR A FIFTH DATE OUT LOUD AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS AND GAVE AS HER REASON THAT A FIFTH DATE IS A DATE SOMEBODY IS EXPECTING TO ARRIVE.**

---

The finding of the morning was not about the board. A man of fifty-six put it on the table at about half past ten and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-two words, and a clerk of nineteen years had it copied out and did not count it, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted it afterwards and got thirty-two and said so.

"**This district has two dates in it and neither of them is a day it has to attend to, and it has been carrying them like a man carrying somebody else's box.**"

Nobody argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that the two are the thirty-first of March and the first of April, and that they are named in three rooms in this district and carried on two forms and entered in one book with a line under them, **and that the thirty-first of March is the day the name comes off a figure in a ledger and that the first of April is the last day a person may put their hand to a notice, and that the two are not to be joined.**

The three rooms are this one, the ward market counter where a reader who is about fifty reads a page out at about ten in the morning, and the room at the end of a lane off the lower terrace where a stove is lit and a page with about forty names on it lies under a folded blanket.

A date in a room is a date a person can walk into and a date on a form is a date a person can hold, **and this district has one of the two and has never had the other, and the one it has is the one that can be entered.**

Nobody had named a fourth room. A man of fifty-six said that a district can name the rooms it is in and cannot name the ones it is not in, **and that thirty-four miles east there is a room where figures are held and this district has never stood in it and cannot say what is on the walls.**

The two forms are the third form of the notice of a withdrawal and a line in the address of a public lot book.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten wrote the third form in four hours in the second month of last year, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he wrote it and that he is a clerk of nothing, **and that the two forms are in two rooms and that neither of them has the other's date on it and that a person holding one of them cannot find out what day the other one is.**

Then the interval, and a man of fifty-six did it out loud in the yard after lunch with a clerk of nineteen years standing close enough to write it down, and the sum was put under the figure the way it always is.

"**Seventeen days left in January after the fourteenth, then the whole of February, then the whole of March to its last day. Seventeen and twenty-eight and thirty-one is seventy-six.**"

"**And to the first of April it is seventeen and twenty-eight and thirty-one and one, which is seventy-seven.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the two figures are a day apart and mean two different things, and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against has had the seventy-seven out of a book and never the seventy-six, and that a district which hands a person a figure without saying which interval it is for has handed that person a figure and not an interval, **and that this district has done that eleven times in four months and has never once said which one it was.**

It was not argued with and a man of fifty-six said that the thirty-first of March is in a ledger in this yard and the first of April is on a form in a room off the lower terrace, and that a date in a ledger is a thing a clerk can enter and a date on a form is a thing a person can act on, and that only one of those belongs to anybody.

**AND ON THE FOURTEENTH OF JANUARY A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS SEVENTY-SIX DAYS FROM THAT DAY AND THE FIRST OF APRIL IS SEVENTY-SEVEN, AND THAT THE TWO FIGURES ARE ONE DAY APART AND MEAN TWO DIFFERENT THINGS, AND THAT A DISTRICT WHICH HANDS A PERSON A FIGURE WITHOUT SAYING WHICH INTERVAL IT IS FOR HAS HANDED THE PERSON A FIGURE.**

---

The third form was read out because a man of fifty-six asked for it, and nobody asked for it twice, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong.

She did not read the whole of it. She read the four lines under the three things the second form had, and the second of the four is the one the room came for, and it has been on the face of the paper since the second month of last year.

> **A PERSON MAY ON ANY DAY BEFORE THE FIRST OF APRIL SAY THEY WILL NOT STAND IN THAT FRAME AGAIN. THE NOTICE IS NOT A RELEASE. IT IS NOT A CONSENT. IT DOES NOT GIVE BACK A YEAR, AND NOBODY IS TO ASK THE PERSON WHY.**

Nobody argued with it. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that this is the only date in the whole of this district's business a person can do something about on the day it comes round.

Everything else the district holds is a day something happens to somebody, **and that a date a person can act on and a date a person is acted on are two things, and that the form is the only one of the two this district has ever printed.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in the yard and said that he wrote the four lines in a shed in four hours and that he took the sentence about not asking the person off the district's own argument and not off any form, and that he has thought about the sentence every day since and has not improved it, **and that a form a man writes in four hours can be argued with for four years and he is not going to be asked to write another one.**

Nobody took that up and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was entered before he spoke as not speaking for the room, and that this is a count of not-speaking and not a count of not-askings, and that the two are not added to each other.

Then the address, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the line in the lot book was in his own hand and that he would not have written it, and a man of fifty-six read it out of the book.

> **ADDRESS: THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AND THE LOT BOOK AT THE WARD MARKET. THIS ADDRESS IS GOOD UNTIL THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH, AND AFTER THAT DAY IT IS A PLACE AND NOT AN ADDRESS.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that an address is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not a witness and is not a responsible party, and that the line in front of them had been read out and was none of those four things, **and that an address which runs out on the thirty-first of March leaves a place behind it, and that a place is not a holder either, and that a person cannot be found at a place.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace was in the yard and said one thing about that, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is twenty-four words, and the man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-four and said it out loud.

"**I have sold paper for thirty years and I have never once sold a person a place to go and be looked for at.**"

The room let it stand and a man of fifty-six said that a man who sells paper has been saying for thirty years the thing a Binder says for eleven weeks, and that the two arrived at it from opposite ends of the same road, and that neither of them had the other until the fourteenth of January.

**AND THE THIRD FORM SAYS THAT A PERSON MAY ON ANY DAY BEFORE THE FIRST OF APRIL SAY THEY WILL NOT STAND IN THAT FRAME AGAIN, AND THE ADDRESS IN THE LOT BOOK IS GOOD UNTIL THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH AND AFTER THAT DAY IS A PLACE AND NOT AN ADDRESS, AND AN ADDRESS IS NOT A BEARER AND A PLACE IS NOT A HOLDER AND A PERSON CANNOT BE FOUND AT A PLACE.**

---

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came in at about half past one in the afternoon and said the thing about the hand, and she said it before anybody asked her, and nobody in the yard wrote it down at the time and it is in the minute in her own words afterwards and it is twenty-seven words.

"**The first three of them I could not read and the fourth one anybody could read, and you have all spent a month on the fourth one.**"

A man of fifty-six said that she is right and that the three she wrote cannot be argued with and the fourth can, and that a room which can only be argued with on the date a person can read is a room that has one argument left in it, **and that she has never been made a clerk of anything and has refused to be one twice and that the two occasions are not a column.**

The room did not argue. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in the six things, whether she would write a fifth date in a hand nobody could read, and she said no in four seconds and gave no reason at all, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked and answered and was not asked why, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

**AND ON THE FOURTEENTH OF JANUARY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO TAKES IN WASHING AT THE SECOND CHANNEL WHETHER SHE WOULD WRITE A FIFTH DATE IN A HAND NOBODY COULD READ, AND SHE SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS AND GAVE NO REASON AT ALL, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT SHE WAS ASKED AND WAS NOT ASKED WHY.**

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide was in the yard and said one thing about the address, and it is in the minute in her own words and it is twenty-nine words, and the man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-nine and said it out loud.

"**You have had a place for eleven weeks and you have not once asked whether the place is yours, and a person has to ask before somebody takes it.**"

The room let it stand and a man of fifty-six said that nobody is going to ask her why she said it in a yard.

She is not the only one in this yard who has said a thing before anybody asked her for it, and he is not going to give that a number either, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said so and that a number a man of fifty-six declines to give is not a column and carries no figure on it.**

Then the ledger, and this is the fourth time a clerk of nineteen years has entered the two dates on one line, and she wrote them and ruled a line under them herself and did not write anything under the line.

"**Thirty-first of March. First of April. Seventeen weeks and a day is not a figure and the two are not the same and they are ruled together because they are two and a clerk rules what is on the page.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a clerk ruling a line under two things is the oldest thing this district does and that nobody has ever argued with it, **and that a line under two things is not a joining and that the two of them come off that page on their own dates and not on each other's.**

---

Tarin Kest came through the yard at about two in the afternoon with a horse that was not his and said the thing about the week before anybody asked him for it, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is twenty-eight words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-eight and said it out loud.

"**The water comes in the first week of the second month and a week is a week, and a district cannot send anybody a letter about a week.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that a man who says the second half of a thing before he is asked for it is a man who has been thinking about it on a road, and that the count of that is not a number anybody keeps.

Then the fourth of the nine holdings, which he said out loud in a yard with a dozen people in it and gave no reason for saying it in a yard.

"**The fourth is a farm and the farm's owner has not been at his house in about four years, and there is nobody in this district who is the farm, and a column with nobody on it is not a column that has gone quiet. It is a column with nobody on it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fourth of the nine holdings is a farm four miles out past the loams with a house and four fields and a sluice of its own, and that the person named in the entry of the registry of the western tide is the owner, and that the register does not say the man is dead and nobody in this district is going to say it for him.

Nobody answered it and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a register is a register and not a mystery, and that a farm with an owner who has not been seen in four years has an owner, and that the finding of the fourteenth of January is not about the owner at all.

---

The registrar of this district's records office was in the yard from about half past two and she was not asked for anything, and a man of fifty-six said that a woman who comes to a yard without paper has now done it twice and that he has noticed it both times, and that he is not going to say what he thinks it means.

She said one thing about the address line, and it is in the minute in her own words and it is thirty-six words, and a clerk of nineteen years had it copied out and the man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-six.

"**My office keeps an address the way a person keeps a hat. It is where a thing is sent. On the thirty-first of March the hat is not the head, and nobody has told the head.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that the sentence is a figure a person said about herself and is not a figure this district enters, and that a records office that keeps an address the way a person keeps a hat is a records office that has been keeping a place for eleven weeks and did not know it had one.

She and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against were in the same yard for about an hour and neither of them said anything to the other, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that they were in the same room at the same hour on purpose for the third time since the eighteenth of December, **and entered that the two of them being in a room is not a conversation about the two of them and that she is not going to be the person who writes it down as one.**

**AND THE REGISTRAR OF THIS DISTRICT'S RECORDS OFFICE SAID IN THIRTY-SIX WORDS THAT HER OFFICE KEEPS AN ADDRESS THE WAY A PERSON KEEPS A HAT, AND THAT ON THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH THE HAT IS NOT THE HEAD AND NOBODY HAS TOLD THE HEAD, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT SHE WAS IN THE SAME YARD AS THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST FOR ABOUT AN HOUR AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID ANYTHING TO THE OTHER.**

---

The question a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked in the yard at about half past three was not answered and it went into the minute with nobody's name against it, and it is in his own words and it is thirty words.

"**If a house has not written to anybody since the twenty-ninth of December, what does this district do on the morning it does, and who is it that gets up?**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that the question is not the same as the one this room asked on the twenty-seventh of December, and that the two are two questions and he is not going to have them joined, and that a question which is not answered is not a question that has been put into a column.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the twenty-ninth of December came and went and the claim did not pass and that the only reason it did not pass is that nobody did anything, **and entered that a house that has not written to anybody can write tomorrow, and that a district cannot wake a house up, and that a day on which somebody else decides what this district does is not a day this district has an instrument for.**

Nobody argued with that. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that he has dug loam nine days in ten for about eleven years and that in eleven years he has never once had to be told to be somewhere by a house, **and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against put a man in a bond in the ninth year and did not ask him, and that those two are two things and he is the second one and not the first.**

Nobody took that up and a man of fifty-six said that a man who says a thing about himself that another man did to him has said a finding and not an opinion, and that the finding is not entered against anybody today.

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a person who is given a thing by a house in bad weather is not going to hand it back, and that this is true of a bed and a cart and a dray, **and that it is not true of a question, and that a question a house will not answer out loud is the one thing in this basin that cannot be taken instead of an answer,** **and that a question a house cannot answer out loud is the only thing anybody in this district cannot be given instead of an answer.**

The room let that stand and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was said in a yard and not in a room, and that the two are two things, and that the sentence about a question is in the minute in his own words and has not been copied anywhere.

A question a house cannot answer out loud is the only thing in this district that cannot be given to a person instead of an answer, **and a man who sells paper for a living has known that for thirty years and a man who posts things for a living has had it for eleven weeks and neither of them has a column for it.**

---

The last of the day was the board and the man who cannot get near it, and nothing came into the yard for about an hour, and a man of fifty-six walked about nine yards out from the middle of the yard and stopped there, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he stopped and entered that she was not going to ask about it.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that in January that man said a reason out loud in this yard for why he stops where he stops and refused to have it written down, and that it is the only thing he has said in eleven weeks that nobody has entered, and that it is still not entered this afternoon, **and that nobody in this yard is going to ask him a second time and that this is not a refusal, because there is nothing on the page to strike.**

The yard did not argue with that and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked a second time and entered that a reason a man gives against his own advantage is not a rule and is not written down, and that the sentence stands in the same place whether it is said in a room or in a yard.

A man of fifty-six said that nobody is going to make him a page about it and that the yard has enough pages.

He is the reader of the market's own book and of every figure anybody in this yard has said out loud since the eighth of March, and this is the first thing he has said in that time that has not gone into a page, **and he is a man who is not a clerk of anything and is not paid for reading.**

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in the yard and was not asked for anything, and she said that the two dates on the ledger page are not her dates and that hers are on a bucket, and that a bucket has four dates and a ledger has two, and that the two of them are not added.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is fifty-one and is a count of days and not a column, **and that the board carries four dates and no fifth, and that the count of protected things is five and the count of conditions with no end on it is four, and that neither of those two counts moved on the fourteenth of January.**

And the register's standing question was not said out loud on the fourteenth of January and nobody asked it, **AND A ROOM MET FOR THE THIRD TIME WITH NO INSTRUMENT ON IT, AND THE COUNT OF INSTRUMENTS THIS DISTRICT HAS BUILT AND NOT NAMED IS SIX, AND THE COUNT OF THINGS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE IS FIVE, AND THE COUNT OF DOCUMENTS NOBODY OWNS IS THREE, AND NONE OF THE THREE COUNTS MOVED ON THE FOURTEENTH OF JANUARY.**
