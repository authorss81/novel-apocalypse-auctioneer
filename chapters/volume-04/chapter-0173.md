# Chapter 173: The Man in the Cart

---

The eleventh of the second month was the day a second household at the sluice end of the loams said no, and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households came down the bank with the tally under his arm at about ten in the morning, and there was no clerk on the road and no minute, and what came back was a page in his own hand which a clerk of nineteen years read out in the yard at about two on the twelfth because a page is read out.

The tally is a board of about eighteen inches by twelve and it is a tally and not a board, and it has six headings on it and four or five names under each in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and there is no column anywhere on it for a person who is not in a household.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that one household has signed and that the district has not asked which one, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a household which has signed and a household which has not are two facts in a yard and neither of them is a column, and that the count of sheets in this district that have been put in front of a family is a figure a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave in the fourth week of January and has not been added to since.**

The second household said no on the eleventh of the second month and said it in four seconds, and it said the reason itself, and the reason is in the page in the man of about forty-eight's own hand and it is twenty-three words.

"**We will take the water. We will not put a name in a column for a season we have not been in yet.**"

Before anybody argued with it, the man of about forty-eight who keeps the tally said the other half of where the sheet comes from, and it is on the page in his own hand, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam asked him for it in the five things and was answered.

He said that the sheets come down at the end of a month on a cart with the seed and a man puts them down at the head of the lane and goes, and that nobody stays, and that in nine weeks he has walked them to five of the six households himself.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who puts a sheet down at the head of a lane and goes has not asked anybody anything, **and that the shape of that arrangement is the shape of a counter thirty-four miles east where a column gets filled in about eleven seconds, and that the two are not the same arrangement and are not joined.**

A man of fifty-six said that a man who carries a sheet to five of six households and is not paid for carrying it has been made into the last link in a chain he cannot see, and that the district put him there by asking him to, and that a person who is used to carry a thing is not a person who has agreed to carry it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about forty-eight who keeps the tally is not one of the five near-identical figures at the west end of this line and is not merged with any of them, **and that a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale at the same end of the same loams and has stood at it nine years is a second person and not a first, and that the two of them are about two hundred yards apart and are not joined.**

So the sheet arrives the way the water did not, on a cart, at the end of a month, **and a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally has carried it to five of the six himself and is not paid for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who is used to carrying a thing is not a man who has agreed to carry it.**

---

Nobody argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that the second of the four printed things says the named person shall not be below fifteen and shall not be asked, and that a household has just found the other half of that sentence, which is that a household may also not be asked, **and that a household which has refused in four seconds has not been asked a second time and has not been asked why, and that a refusal with a reason in it is a refusal and not a document.**

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the reason is the first sentence anybody in this district has given for refusing that has a season in it, **and that a season is not a figure and that a household refusing a season it has not been in is a person refusing a thing that has not happened yet, and that the two are not the same and are not joined.**

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A SECOND HOUSEHOLD AT THE SLUICE END OF THE LOAMS SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS, AND ITS REASON WAS THAT IT WILL TAKE THE WATER AND WILL NOT PUT A NAME IN A COLUMN FOR A SEASON IT HAS NOT BEEN IN YET.**

---

Then the part of it that cost something, and it came out of the man the figure of twenty-one years is against and not out of the yard.

He said that the water came in the night of the thirty-first of January and filled the tank at the shelter for the first time in nine years, and that it came before the sheet was put in front of anybody, and that the sheet asks for the labour of a family after the season and the season has not started, and that this district has spent a month reading a sheet it has never seen a copy of.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the tank filled on the first of the second month and that the sheet was put in front of the households in the fourth week of January, **and entered the two facts in one line and did not join them, and that the order of two facts is not a finding and that she is not going to say which of them caused the other.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a house which gives water before the season and takes the labour after it is a house that has designed the order of those two things on purpose, and that the district has been reading the sheet as though the order in it were a description and not an instruction.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the district does not have a copy of the sheet and has only a copy kept by a man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing and would not have it read out in a market, **and that a district arguing about the order of two things on a document it has never read is a district arguing about a document it has not got, and that a man who copies for nothing and owns nothing is the reason the district has not got it, and that the two are joined and both of them are the same fact.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of documents nobody owns is three and did not move on the twelfth of the second month, **and that a copy kept by a man who is not paid and does not own it is a copy and not a fourth, and that a document nobody owns is not the same kind of thing as a document nobody can read and the two are not joined.**

**AND THE WATER CAME IN THE NIGHT OF THE THIRTY-FIRST OF JANUARY AND WENT TO THE TANK FOUR MILES DOWN A LANE, AND THE SHEET WAS PUT IN FRONT OF SIX HOUSEHOLDS IN THE FOURTH WEEK OF JANUARY, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THE TWO FACTS IN ONE LINE AND DID NOT JOIN THEM.**

---

Then the sixth household, and he is a man who lives in a cart and has no roof, and his name is written under the sixth heading with a roof drawn beside it in charcoal by the man of about forty-eight who keeps the tally, and he has been asked to sign the same sheet and has not and was not asked twice and was not asked why.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him a question in the six things at about half past eleven in the morning, and it was the first question anybody in this district's business has asked him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was asked and that she was not asked why it was asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

"**Can a sheet like that find a man with no roof.**"

The man of about forty-eight who keeps the tally said no in four seconds and gave the reason himself, and it is in the page in his own hand and it is thirty-four words.

"**The sheet is given to a family and a family is a thing with a name on it, and he has not got one and is not going to be given one by me.**"

Then the man in the cart said the other half before anybody asked him for it, and it is in the page in his own hand and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted it and got forty-four and said it out loud.

"**I have had nine years of not being on a piece of paper and I have not made anything out of it and I am not going to, and a man who wants writing down for being left off is asking for a column.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said the second half before anybody asked him for it and that she is not going to put a number on it, **and that a person a sheet cannot touch is not a protected thing and is not a defence and is a shape, and that the count of protected things is five and did not move on the twelfth of the second month, and that a man in a cart who is safe by accident is not safe by design and that the two are not the same.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that this district has spent four months arguing about the people it can name and has one person it cannot reach, and that the one person it cannot reach is the only one who has just declined to be made an exception, **and that a man who declines to be an exception is a person and not a category, and that the five protected things in this district are a passage twice, a loss once, a failure of a remedy once and a refusal to be asked again once, and that none of the five is him.**

A man of fifty-six said that the four conditions with no end on it are not protected things and are never to be entered in that tally, **and that a condition with no end on it is a different class of thing from a protected thing and that the two counts have not been added to each other in eleven weeks and are not going to be added by a man in a cart.**

**AND THE SIXTH HOUSEHOLD ON A TALLY UNDER A MAN'S ARM IS A MAN WHO LIVES IN A CART, AND HE SAID THAT HE HAS HAD NINE YEARS OF NOT BEING ON A PIECE OF PAPER AND IS NOT GOING TO ASK FOR WRITING DOWN FOR IT, AND HE IS NOT ONE OF THE FIVE PROTECTED THINGS.**

---

Then the page that was not written. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether the six households could be put in the ward market's own book on the page after the page the five terms went into on the twentieth of January, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said he could, in four seconds, and then did not do it.

He said the reason himself before anybody asked him for it, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is forty-one words.

"**A page in that book says which book it is in, and a household that has not signed would be on it as a household that has not signed, and a house can walk to a market counter and read it.**"

The room let that stand. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who can write a page and has not written it has said the same thing a man of fifty-six said in a yard in the second week of January, which is that a district which says nothing protects nobody, **and that a district which says everything hands a house a list, and that the two halves of that sentence are both true and that this yard has only ever put one of them on a wall.**

Nobody took it up and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a man who has decided on his own account not to write a page has done the first thing this district's method has ever cost anybody, **and that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage is not a rule and is not written down and he would not have it written down.**

A man of fifty-six said that the second half of that has been said in this yard in the fourth month of a year and is not going to be said again, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing entered in the fourteenth of January that nobody in this yard is going to ask him a second time and that this is not a refusal because there is nothing on the page to strike.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a household which has not been asked twice is not a household that has refused, and that four of the six on a tally under a man's arm are in that state, and that a page would turn four not-askings into four refusals on a morning, **and that a record which changes what it says about a person because somebody wrote it down is a record and not a witness.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way and that the record says not asked, **and that the count of not-askings about anybody in particular in this district is not a bucket and she is not going to make it one, and that a page in a market book is a bucket with a heading on it.**

**AND A MAN WHO COULD PUT SIX HOUSEHOLDS IN A PUBLIC BOOK IN FOUR SECONDS DID NOT, AND SAID THAT A HOUSE CAN WALK TO A MARKET COUNTER AND READ WHAT IS WRITTEN THERE, AND A PAGE WOULD HAVE TURNED FOUR NOT-ASKINGS INTO FOUR REFUSALS ON A MORNING.**

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps the tally was asked, in the five things, at about half past two in the afternoon, whether the tally could go in a book, and he said no in four seconds and gave the reason in a sentence that is on the page in his own hand.

"**This is a thing under my arm and I can lose it, and a book is a thing a house can ask to have.**"

The yard let it stand. A man of fifty-six said that a man who has been asked twice in three weeks whether his own page can be put somewhere better has answered no twice, and that the two refusals are not a column and are not added to the thirteen, **and that a man who keeps a tally for six households and is not paid for it and will not have it in a book is a man this district has no instrument for and has never claimed one.**

A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale at the sluice end of the loams and has stood at it nine years came up the bank and looked at the six headings on the tally and did not touch it, and she said that a scale has a pan and a weight and a man and the three of them are three, and that a tally has six headings and about nine hundred names and no column for anybody outside a household, and that the two are not the same shape and are not joined.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two women and the one man at the sluice end of the loams are three people in three trades about two hundred yards apart and that they are not one of the five near-identical figures at the west end and are not merged with any of them.

Nobody argued. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that the sixth heading is a roof drawn in charcoal and that a roof drawn in charcoal is not a mark, because a mark is cut and that is laid, **and that a line of stones is not a mark for the same reason, and that a man who has drawn a roof is not a bearer of anything and has not been asked to be.**

A man of fifty-six said that the district's rule about marks was built around the rule that a mark may not carry a job and not in defiance of it, and that a charcoal roof on a tally carries no job and has been refused entry as an instrument and that the count of six did not move.

And so a charcoal roof drawn beside a name is not a mark, and a line of stones is not a mark for the same reason, **and a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally has now refused twice in three weeks to have it put in a book, and the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and did not move on the twelfth of the second month.**

---

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures came up the bank at about half past three and was not asked for anything, and she said that one of the four households Tarin Kest had been talking about is on her round, and that she has carried water to it for nine years, and that she is not going to say which of the four it is.

Nobody asked her why she would not say which one it is, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a woman who has carried water to about ninety houses for nine years has been the only person in this district who has physically been in all four of them, and that nobody has ever asked her for a list and she is not going to be asked for one.**

A man of fifty-six said that the district's nearest instrument is a book that works if a person comes to the yard, and that a woman carrying water to ninety houses is an instrument that works if nobody comes anywhere, **and that the two are the only two instruments this district has that reach a person who is not in a household, and that neither of them is on any list and that a list of the two is not going to be made.**

Tarin Kest came down the lane at about four in the afternoon and said the other half of a thing about the four households, and it is in the minute in his own words and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted it and got thirty-two and said it out loud.

"**Those four are the ones the water went to when the fourth holding did not take, and two of them have asked me what they owe the water and one has not.**"

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a household which has asked a route keeper what it owes the water has asked a man who keeps a road, and that a route keeper is a Binder's counterparty and not an apprentice, **and that a figure said by a man who keeps the road in a yard is a price put on it by the man who keeps it, and that this district has known that since the ninth of February and has not found a way round it.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the four households have water they did not have and a sheet they have not signed, and that one of the four has signed, and that the three states a district can be in with a sheet are three, **and that a household which has signed and a household which has refused and a household which has not been asked again are three things and none of them has a figure against it in any book in this basin.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a household which has not refused and has not signed has not been asked and cannot be counted either way, and that the record says not asked, **and that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is eighty and is a count of days and not a column, and that the day was not written in a space and was not put on a board.**

**AND ON THE TWELFTH OF THE SECOND MONTH ONE HOUSEHOLD HAD SIGNED AND ONE HAD REFUSED IN FOUR SECONDS AND FOUR HAD NOT BEEN ASKED TWICE, AND A MAN WHO LIVES IN A CART IS THE ONLY PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT'S BUSINESS A SHEET OF THIS SHAPE CANNOT TOUCH, AND THAT IS BY ACCIDENT.**
