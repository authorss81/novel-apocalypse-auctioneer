# Chapter 200: The First Public Debt Court

---

The thirty-first of the third month was cold and clear and the yard of Lot Seventeen had about nine people in it at about eight in the morning, and nobody had come to say anything about the twenty-ninth and nobody was going to.

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray was asked a question in the six things before he had got his coat off, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him whether anybody knew when a train came, and he gave the whole of it out loud in a yard and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down, and he did it in two runs of words with the sums under the figures, and the first of the two runs was sixty-eight words and the second was eighty-seven.

"**The train came on the eleventh of February. There are seventeen days left in that month after the eleventh, and March and April and May and June and July and August are thirty-one and thirty and thirty-one and thirty and thirty-one and thirty-one, and that is one hundred and eighty-four, and a hundred and eighty-four and seventeen is two hundred and one at the last day of August.**"

He was not interrupted. A man of fifty-six said that the tail of it had been said out loud eight times in four months by a man who does the counting for a living and that a man who has kept nine years of days in his head has just done it differently from every one of those eight, **and that a figure said twice by two people in two ways is two figures and is not a check and that the train is not coming and nobody has said it is.**

"**September is thirty and October is thirty-one and November is thirty and December is thirty-one, and that is a hundred and twenty-two, and two hundred and one and a hundred and twenty-two is three hundred and twenty-three at the last day of December, and the first of January is three hundred and twenty-four, and January is thirty-one and February is twenty-eight and March is thirty-one, and that is ninety, and three hundred and twenty-four and ninety is four hundred and thirteen at the last day of March.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the train on the siding had stood four hundred and thirteen days on this day and that the figure was made out of the day the train came and not carried, **and that the days since the twenty-fourth of November that nobody has entered stand at one hundred and twenty-seven today against one hundred and twenty on the twenty-fourth, and that she does not say the figure out loud and is not going to and it is not a rate.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing, out in the open, about the two days before this one, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I am not going to be a man who says he did not know what a page is. I have known what a page is since the fifteenth of the second month and I put my hand on one anyway.**"

It was not disputed. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man who says that in front of nine people on a cold morning has said the second half of it before the asking and that a clerk is not going to put a number on it, **and that the registrar of this district's records office said one thing about the two of them on the thirtieth and did not say a second thing and has not been asked for a second thing and is not going to be.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the part that was being said around him without being said to him, and he said it to the man who digs loam and not to the yard. He said that two people were in a room on the twenty-eighth and a sheet went up on a wall on the thirtieth and a page of a man's hand went into a drawer in between, and that the whole of it took two days and one sentence, and that a thing which takes two days and one sentence can be undone in two days and one sentence and has not been.

A man of fifty-six entered afterwards that nothing was said to that and that the fence man was not asked where he had heard it and is not going to be, **and that a room with a door on it is still a room with a door on it and is not an instrument and is not a seventh of the six.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half of it, and he said it before anybody asked him for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the two halves in the order they came and that she is not going to put a number on that order.

He said that the registrar is the only person in this district whose instrument is a room, and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against is the only person in this district who has put his own name at the back of one, and that those two facts came together on the twenty-eighth of this month.

A man of fifty-six said that a man who says that and then says he would rather be carrying a yoke has said the whole of what has happened in this district in a month, **and that a man who says a thing out loud and then offers to go and do a job instead of arguing about it is a man and not a rule and is not written down and he would not have it written down.**

**AND ON THE THIRTY-FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE SAID THE WHOLE OF THE DAYS OUT OF THE DAY THE TRAIN CAME IN TWO RUNS OF WORDS WITH THE SUMS UNDER THE FIGURES, AND THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD FOUR HUNDRED AND THIRTEEN DAYS, AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS THE DAY.**

---

The court was made on the twenty-ninth of the third month and it was made the way the ledger was made and the charter was made, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the shape of it and entered that about nineteen people were in a yard and that a clerk of the ward market took it down and that nine hands copied it and that it was read out three times at a counter at the ward market on the thirtieth.

The man of fifty-six read it out on the thirty-first at about ten in the morning with about nineteen people round him and a clerk of nineteen years read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **A PUBLIC COURT FOR DEBTS IN THIS DISTRICT. THE FIRST DAY IS THE THIRTY-FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH AND AFTER THAT ON THE DAYS A CLERK OF THE WARD MARKET NAMES.**
>
> **A FIGURE A HOUSE OWES AND HAS NOT PAID MAY BE BROUGHT TO IT. A FIGURE A PERSON OWES MAY BE BROUGHT TO IT AND THE PERSON MAY BE HEARD ONCE AND MAY ANSWER ONCE.**
>
> **THE COURT MAY WRITE DOWN WHAT A PERSON SAYS. THE COURT MAY NOT TAKE A YEAR OFF A PERSON AND THE COURT MAY NOT GIVE ONE BACK, AND NOBODY HAS ESTABLISHED WHAT A WITHDRAWAL COSTS.**
>
> **THE COURT IS A ROOM AND A DAY AND A PERSON WHO READS IT OUT. A ROOM AND A DAY AND A PERSON WHO READS IT OUT ARE NOT AN INSTRUMENT AND ARE NOT A SEVENTH OF THE SIX.**
>
> **THE TERMS OF A HOUSE THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST ARE VALID UNTIL THE PEOPLE INSIDE THEM CAN BE HEARD. THE PEOPLE INSIDE THEM HAVE NOT BEEN HEARD.**

Nothing was said to it for a moment. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether a person can be made to come, and a man of fifty-six said no and gave the reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty.

"**A figure a person owes may be brought and the person may be heard once. A person who does not come is not a person who has refused and cannot be counted either way and the record says not asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the third line of the second sheet refuses to send for anybody, and that the two counts of not-joining stand at thirteen and at twelve and that neither of them moved on any of the days from the twenty-fourth of the third month to the thirtieth of it, **and that a court which cannot fetch a person is not a court that can end anything and that this district has not got the second of those and did not get it on the twenty-ninth and has not entered it as a delay.**

Nobody picked it up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the fifth line is the only line in five boards and nine copies and eleven hands that anybody in this district is going to be able to use in a year, **and that it is a door and not a remedy and that a house's terms being valid until the people inside them can be heard is a thing a house is owed and not a thing a person is given.**

**AND ON THE THIRTY-FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH THE FIRST PUBLIC COURT FOR DEBTS IN THIS DISTRICT READ ITSELF OUT IN A YARD, AND ITS FIFTH LINE SAYS THAT THE TERMS OF A HOUSE THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST ARE VALID UNTIL THE PEOPLE INSIDE THEM CAN BE HEARD, AND THE PEOPLE INSIDE THEM HAVE NOT BEEN HEARD.**

---

The first thing brought to it was the four sheets of the eighteenth of December and the man of fifty-six read all four of them out in one run and refused nothing in them and changed nothing in them.

The third one is the withdrawal notice in its third form with twenty-nine words on its face and the season in the top line of it that has not started, and the fourth one carries the figure of twenty-one years in the same line as one name, and the entry runs to this date.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about thirty-four who digs loam, in the six things, what the court was going to do with a charter that cannot be certified, and a man of about nineteen counted the answer and got forty-eight.

"**The court can hear a figure and write it down and that is all it can do, and the four sheets cannot be argued with because there is nothing in them to argue at, and a thing nobody can argue with is a thing a court cannot touch.**"

Nobody contradicted him. A man of fifty-six said that a guild inspector of forty-four sent those four sheets back thirty-four miles with nine lines in a letter and that the word certified is on none of the four, **and that a court established on the twenty-ninth of this month cannot take a year off a person and cannot give one back and that the hole in the third form of the notice is the same hole and is still in it and is the reason the third line of the court's second sheet is the line it is.**

The food agreement of six sheets was the second thing brought to it and the two witnesses to it were not in the room and were not sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in this district asked either of them anything on the thirty-first of the third month and is not going to, **and that the sheet says on its own face that the two witnesses were not in the room when the reason was read out and that the court is not going to strike a line off a sheet and has not struck one.**

The two buildings about four hundred yards apart at the sluice end were both named out loud on the morning of the thirty-first and neither of them sent anybody, and the man of about fifty-five who keeps one of them and the woman of about thirty-eight who keeps the other were in the same yard at different ends of it for about an hour and did not speak, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that neither was asked what the other had said.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam asked the woman of about thirty-eight, in the six things, in front of about fourteen people, whether the claim that passed on the twenty-fourth was against her place, and she said that she had been told it was a place at the sluice and had not been told it was hers, and that she was not going to be asked a second thing about it in a yard on the morning the court was sitting.

A man of fifty-six said that a woman who keeps a building and has one place in it has learned in four years what a sheet of four printed things does, **and that a sheet which will not name a place leaves the person who keeps it holding a claim she cannot see, and that this district is not going to make her a party to a claim in order to find out about it.**

The two households in the lane were not before the court and nobody sent for them. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a court which cannot fetch a person cannot hear a person either, and that the four of them in that lane have not been asked and have not refused and cannot be counted either way, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the record says not asked and that four people are not four addresses.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the fifth thing was, and the man of fifty-six said that the fourth line of an offer which has been on a table three times was not before the court and was not going to be read out in a yard by anybody, and gave no reason, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked once and gave no reason, and that the refusals to read are nine.

**AND THE FOUR SHEETS OF THE EIGHTEENTH OF DECEMBER AND THE SIX SHEETS OF THE SEVENTH OF DECEMBER WERE BOTH BEFORE A COURT THAT WAS ESTABLISHED THE DAY BEFORE YESTERDAY, AND THE COURT WROTE DOWN WHAT WAS SAID ABOUT BOTH OF THEM AND COULD DO NOTHING ELSE WITH EITHER.**

---

The figure came off at about two in the afternoon and a clerk of nineteen years read the line out because a line is read out, and about fourteen people were standing there and nobody had asked for it.

The entry ran to the thirty-first of March and the name came off in the same line as the date it came off on, and the line is the fourth line of the fourth sheet, and it is the line that says the figure of twenty-one years against one name and an entry that runs to this date, and it is in the ward market's own book and a copy of it is on a board of eleven in a yard.

A man of fifty-six said that nobody there did anything at all and that the whole of what happened was a clerk reading a line, **and that the name did not come off because a man asked and did not come off because anybody was let out of anything and came off because a date arrived, and that a date is the last day somebody can do something and this was the last day of it.**

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray said the thing he had come for and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-six.

"**My name was on a page in this district for nine years and it has come off in the same line as the date it was to come off on, and I did not ask for it and I could not have stopped it, and I am not going to say thank you for a calendar.**"

It stood without anything said against it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that nothing was written in the place where the figure had been, **and that a blank at the top of a form is a place for the next person to write and that the next person is always a stranger, and that no figure can be entered against a man in this district on the thirty-first of the third month because no figure has been established and a figure that has not been established is not a blank, it is the absence of one.**

Then the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing said his piece unasked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody sent him and that nobody asked him a second thing.

"**The court is here and there is still no money in this district that anybody can pay anybody with, and I am not going to ask you in a room, and I am going to keep asking, and I have said that before and I am saying it again.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the same reason behind a second refusal is not a new refusal and is not added to anything and is not a column and is not a rate, **and that the eleventh coppers a week were refused twice in the second month and this afternoon is the third time the same reason has been given and the man is owed nothing and is owed a figure for what he does.**

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat at the third house up the lane from the scale was standing there and had not been sent for and nobody asked her a second thing, and she said that she would be in it on the second of the next month as well and that a yoke-load is a yoke-load whoever is standing at the back of the book, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked once and answered once and that a person who has said she will be somewhere on a day has not signed anything.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the counts of this district on the thirty-first of the third month, and entered that she entered them once and at the end of the month, **and that the figure of entries carrying a finding in them since the eighteenth of December is not stated and has not been looked at.**

She entered that things this district does not have number five, being a way of saying what a toll lands on a place, a column that is a place, an office, a station with nobody at it, and a way to pay a person who is not in a household.

She entered that instruments this district has built and not named number six, and that documents nobody owns number three, **and that the three of those are three different lists and are not added to each other and that a court is not in any of the three.**

She entered that the protected things number five and that no sixth was added in this month or in any month, being a passage twice, a loss once, a failure of a remedy once, and a refusal to be asked again once, **and that the conditions with no end on it number four and are a different class of thing and are never entered in that tally, and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and neither of them changed on any of the ten days from the twenty-fourth of the third month to the thirty-first of the third month.**

She entered that five boards and six full lines stand in this district and that the seventh at the ninth place is ruled and has nothing on it, and that nobody put a line on a board on any of the seven days from the twenty-fourth of the third month to the thirtieth of it.

She entered that the board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for ninety-seven days and nobody has washed it, **and that a list of five written on the back of a page in a yard is not a board and a sheet nailed to a wall is not a board and a page in a drawer in a building is not a board.**

She entered that the fifth thing this district does not have is still the fifth and has not been named by anybody in ten days, and that a day written in a space in a page in a building is not a seventh instrument and was refused entry as one in this yard in the month before last.

Then the stub came out of his coat and he put it on the table face up and it is about the size of a coin and it has been folded and unfolded so often that it is going to come apart at the folds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked where he had got it and is not going to be.

He said the thing out loud in front of about fourteen people and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**You told her in the second month and you came back with nothing in your hands, and this is the whole of it and it is two days of road and there is no clerk on it, and the next time the counter is open is the second of the next month.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the counter a house keeps at the end of a road takes a name off a sheet and will not ask why and costs nobody anything, **and that this district said so out loud in a yard on the twenty-second of the second month and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said it himself in a room and came back with nothing, and that the two of those are the same act done twice and the second time it is in a book.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said that he is going on the second of the next month, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked why and is not going to be, **and that the stub does not carry the twenty-ninth of December and that the day which decides the claim is on a sheet in a pocket two days' walk away and the entry of it on the twenty-second of the second month is unchanged.**

**AND ON THE THIRTY-FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS CAME OFF A LINE IN THE WARD MARKET'S OWN BOOK IN THE SAME LINE AS THE DATE, AND NOTHING WAS WRITTEN IN ITS PLACE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE PUT A STUB ABOUT THE SIZE OF A COIN ON A TABLE AND SAID WHAT THE OTHER HALF OF IT WAS.**

---

The tank at the back of the building four miles down a lane was within a hand's breadth of the lip at about half past four in the afternoon and it is the first time since the first of the second month, and the crack is still in it and is still weeping and the man of about fifty-five has not put a fifth mend in it and is not going to.

Eleven days have gone into the fifth page in that book since the twenty-first of the third month, under a column a finger wide with nothing written above it, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the ruling is the best in the district and would not call that a finding or a rate, **and that the counting of days in a column in a book in a building was turned down as a seventh instrument in this yard in the month before last, and that the six this district built is the six it built on the second of January, and that this month did not add to it.**

The woman of fifty-eight came up the bank at about half past four with a yoke and two buckets and nobody sent for her, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked a first thing.

She put the yoke down on the stones at the top of the hill and she did not pick it up again, and the man of about fifty-five came out and looked at it and neither of them said anything, and there were about four people on the lane and nobody asked either of them what the yoke was doing there.

A man of fifty-six said that a woman who has carried four yoke-loads a day up a hill for nine years and put the yoke down on the stones is a woman whose round ended on the twenty-fourth of the third month and who has not got to tomorrow, **and that a district which has taken a woman's work out of her hands in a wet week and has got nothing to give her in place of it has not solved anything and has moved the bill, and that he said that on the twenty-first of the second month in a lane four miles down from a lower terrace and it has not stopped being true.**

Nobody in that lane said anything to it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the five things this district does not have are the same five and that a yoke on a stone is not one of them and is not a sixth, **and that the count of boards and of full lines is the same count it was on the eleventh of the third month, that the seventh at the ninth place is ruled with nothing on it, and that the board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for ninety-seven days without being washed.**

**AND ON THE THIRTY-FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH A TANK FOUR MILES DOWN A LANE WAS WITHIN A HAND'S BREADTH OF THE LIP FOR THE FIRST TIME SINCE THE FIRST OF THE SECOND MONTH, THE CRACK WAS STILL IN IT AND STILL WEEPING, AND A YOKE WAS PUT DOWN ON THE STONES AT THE TOP OF A HILL AND WAS NOT PICKED UP AGAIN, AND NOBODY ASKED EITHER OF THEM WHY.**
