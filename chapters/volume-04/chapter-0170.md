# Chapter 170: Nobody Has Ever Posted the Train

---

The ninth of February was the day a man of fifty-six said out loud in the yard that nobody in this district has ever posted the train, and he said it before anybody asked him for it and gave no reason, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said the second half before he was asked and that she is not going to put a number on it.

The train on the siding had stood three hundred and sixty-three days, and a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and beside the index there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a train is a thing a stranger can go and look at, and that it is the only large thing in this district anybody can walk up to, **and that a thing a stranger can look at and that nobody has ever posted is the exact shape of the first of the five and has been standing there for three hundred and sixty-three days.**

Nobody argued. A man of fifty-six said that a train is not a lot and has no bearer and no toll and no term and no release and no remedy, **and that the rule which came into this yard on the second of January says that the only remedy for a thing that cannot be inspected is to take it down, and that this district is not going to take down a train, and that this is the first time the rule has been applied to itself and has come out the other side.**

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-THREE DAYS, AND A TRAIN IS NOT A LOT AND HAS NO BEARER AND NO TOLL AND NO TERM, AND NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAS EVER POSTED IT, AND THE ONLY REMEDY THE DISTRICT'S OWN RULE OFFERS FOR A THING THAT CANNOT BE INSPECTED IS TO TAKE IT DOWN.**

---

The interval, and a man of fifty-six did it in the yard at about eleven in the morning, and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down, and the sum was put under the figure.

"**Off the eleventh of February, and there are two days to it, so the eleventh of the second month is three hundred and sixty-five, and the ninth of the second month is three hundred and sixty-three, and the thirty-first of March is fifty days off this one.**"

"**And the first of April is fifty-one, and the seventy-seven is on the interval to the first of April out of the fourteenth of January and is also the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November as of today, and the two are not the same figure and are on two pages.**"

The room let it stand and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a number written in two places meaning two different things is the oldest thing this district does, **and that a clerk of nineteen years has handed people the wrong interval for it eleven times in four months and that the figure of eleven is a count and not a column and is not added to the sixty-five minutes of the six askings that carry one.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is seventy-seven and is a count of days and not a column, and that the days are not added to the askings and the askings are not added to the days, **and that the refusals to read are nine, the departure is unspent, and there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading.**

Nobody took that up and a man of fifty-six said that the eleventh of the second month is a day the train came and a day a house has not named, and that it is two days off, and that a district does not get to make a date mean two things because it likes one of them, **and that the water came on a day nobody named in the first week and that the tank filled and that the fourth holding did not take, and that the two unnamed days are not the same unnamed day and are not joined.**

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE ELEVENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH IS TWO DAYS OFF AND IS THE DAY THE TRAIN CAME AND A DAY A HOUSE HAS NOT NAMED, AND THE WATER CAME ON A DAY NOBODY NAMED IN THE FIRST WEEK, AND THE TWO UNNAMED DAYS ARE NOT THE SAME UNNAMED DAY.**

---

They went east on the ninth. The man the figure of twenty-one years is against and the registrar of this district's records office, together, at about eight in the morning, and nobody sent for them and a clerk of nineteen years entered that they were not asked to and that she was not asked to write it down as anything.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the yard, in the six things, what they were going east for, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said that they were going to ask a house what a sluice is on a sheet, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a house is not a person, and the man said yes and said he knew.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two of them said yes in the same yard at the same hour on purpose for the sixth time since the eighteenth of December, **and entered that the arranging is not a conversation about the two of them and is not to be read as one, and that she is not going to be the person who reads it as one, and that the slate went past the kitchen in Alder Reach for the tenth time on the sixth and neither of them wrote on it.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that the fourth line of an offer that has been on a table three times has still not been read out loud in this district, **and that two people are going thirty-four miles east on the ninth of February and that he is not going to write in a minute that they are going to read it, and that a clerk of nineteen years is not going to enter it, and that if it is read out loud it will be entered then and not before.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that, and entered that a clerk is a person and not a rule, **and that the fourth line is a line and that a line that is read out loud in a room is a document and that a document nobody has read is not a refusal and is not a yes and cannot be counted either way.**

Nobody took it up and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a woman whose name is in no register in the west bank has offered four lines three times and that nothing has been accepted and nothing has been refused, **and that a woman who has offered a thing three times and has not had it taken is a person and not a house and is not joined to a house and the two are not to be.**

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST AND THE REGISTRAR OF THIS DISTRICT'S RECORDS OFFICE WENT THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING, AND THE FOURTH LINE OF AN OFFER THAT HAS BEEN ON A TABLE THREE TIMES HAS STILL NOT BEEN READ OUT LOUD IN THIS DISTRICT.**

---

Tarin Kest came into the yard at about half past eleven in the morning on the ninth of February and said the thing about the road before anybody asked him for it, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is fifty-one words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got fifty-one and said it out loud.

"**There is a mile and a bit of head race with water standing in it and no term on it and no bearer on it, and I know what that is worth to four households, and I am not going to say the figure in a yard in the second month.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who knows a figure and will not say it in a yard has not refused the figure, and that the two are not the same, **and that the two people in this yard who will not say a figure are a man who sells on a tram arch and a route keeper and that the two of them are not joined and neither is a column.**

A man of fifty-six said that a route keeper is a Binder's counterparty and not an apprentice and that a man who keeps a road is not a man who may be asked what a road is worth by a yard full of people, **and that a figure said in a yard by a man who keeps the road would be a price put on it by the man who owns the keeping of it, and that this district has spent four months finding out what that does.**

Nobody argued and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and was not asked to be, **and that about nine hundred households in this district are in no row of anything and that a figure for four of them is a figure for four and not a figure for the rest, and that the two are not the same and are not added.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said that a sluice with no term on it is a thing that will be open in the fourth month and closed in the fifth and open again, and that a man who says that is a man describing a season and not a lot, **and that a season is not a figure and that a year is not a figure and that a year and a half is not a figure and that this district has never had a rate that turns one into coppers.**

---

The hundred and eighty-three coppers was read out at the ward market counter at about noon on the ninth of February for the third time in four weeks, and it is a page in the ward market's own book with a heading and no name against it, and a reader who is about fifty read it out and read the sixth column first, as she always does.

Nobody refused. A man of fifty-six said that the figure is not to be struck and that the answer is not to be struck either, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a page with a heading and no name against it is a page in that book and that a page in a ward market's own book with a heading and four entries on it and two unruled inches at the foot of it is a different page, **and that the two are two pages and are not a column and are not a pattern and have not been one since the ninth of January.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace said that a figure nobody can claim is a figure that stays in a book, and that a figure a person can claim is a figure that leaves, **and that this district has one of each in the same book on the same afternoon and that the one which can be claimed left and the one which cannot did not.**

Nobody took that up and a man of fifty-six said that the page with four entries on it has a heading and about two inches at the foot of it that were not ruled, and that a clerk of nineteen years was asked to rule them and said no in four seconds, **and that a clerk has now been asked to look at two pages in one book on two different afternoons and has said no twice and that the two refusals are not a column and are not added to the thirteen.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked again about the two inches and that she is not going to rule them, and that the reason is the same reason and that saying the same reason twice is not a rule, **and that a rule a clerk says out loud against her own convenience is a person and not a rule and that this is the fourth time in eleven weeks she has been asked nothing about a thing she is keeping.**

---

The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace came up the bank at about two in the afternoon and said that there were three days in the spaces now and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him and that the figure of three was said by him and has not been checked.

The yard let it stand. A man of fifty-six said that three days on a page kept by a person in a building is the first thing in this district's whole business that anybody can count and nobody has paid for, **and that the count of protected things is five and did not move and that three days in a page is not one of the five and is not a new condition with no end on it, and that the count of those is four and did not move either.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a page with three days in it is a page that has started and that the man who keeps it is not a keeper of anything and is not a clerk and is not paid, **and that the six instruments this district has built and not named are a rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security, and that a day in a finger's width of space in a building is not a seventh and was refused entry in this yard in the month before last.**

Nobody took that up and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a day in a space was refused entry as a seventh instrument on the second of the second month and that the count of six did not move, **and that a thing this district cannot name is not automatically an instrument, and that the woman of about fifty-eight said in January that a bucket is not a register and that a day in a page is not a register either.**

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in the yard and said that the tank is still full and that she has not had to fill it since the first of the second month, **and that the nearest of the four dates on her bucket is the sixteenth of the second month and that it is seven days off and that seven is a figure and a week is a week.**

Nobody took that up and a man of fifty-six said that a week is a week and that a claim on a place in a building cannot be worked out in a week, and that the third of the four printed things says a claim passes if a family does not pay on the day, and that no document in this district has ever named the day, **and that the man of about fifty-five has now started writing days in a page and that the days he writes start on the fourth of the second month and that the days before the fourth are absent and cannot be given to anybody.**

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THERE ARE THREE DAYS IN THE SPACES ON THE FOURTH PAGE OF A BOOK IN A FLOOD SHELTER, AND THE DAYS BEFORE THE FOURTH OF THE SECOND MONTH ARE ABSENT AND CANNOT BE GIVEN TO ANYBODY, AND THE COUNT OF PROTECTED THINGS IS FIVE AND THE COUNT OF CONDITIONS WITH NO END ON IT IS FOUR AND NEITHER MOVED.**

---

The board outside the room carried the twenty-fourth of December and had carried it for forty-seven days, and a man of about fifty-six said at about four in the afternoon that a room which meets because a person tells about nine other people where to stand is a room that has about nine people in it, and that a room with a date on the board has about a dozen.

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A clerk of nineteen years said that she has not been asked to write a fifth date and has not offered, **and entered that she is not going to be asked about the twenty-fourth of December and is not going to be asked on the first of March, and that a clerk is a person and not a rule and the two facts are in one line on purpose and she wanted them on two lines and did not do it.**

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide was in the yard at about half past four and said that she is going to the counter on the second day of the next month whatever happens, **and that the carter will stand in her yard on the second day of every month whether the sheet is in her pocket or not, and that a person who is owed a place in a building has nine years of a carter to look at and that is not a security and is not nothing.**

Nobody argued and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the yard behind the Road House on the tide is entered in this district's books as not a security at the keeper's own request, **and that the request was made in December and was unpaid and unasked and that the entry is still in a book and that a person who prefers debt to hunger has two things in a book this month and neither of them is a claim.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the finding of the ninth of February is not a finding and that the room has had no finding in it for four days and that a room can go four days without one, **and that the count of entries carrying a finding since the eighteenth of December was last entered on the thirty-first of December and has not been looked at since, and that she is not going to look at it now.**

A man of fifty-six walked about nine yards out from the middle of the yard and stopped there, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he stopped and entered that she was not asked about it and was not going to be, **and that the reason he gave for it in a yard in January was not written down and is not written down today and is not going to be, and that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage is not a rule whether it is said in a room or in a yard.**

A man of fifty-six said the standing question out loud at about half past four in the afternoon and it was not answered, and the question is the one this district has carried since the thirtieth of December, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in her own words and entered that it has now been said six times in this district and answered none of them.

Nobody took it up. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a district which cannot certify a document and cannot argue with it has to be able to do both, **and that a document a district cannot argue with is a document the district has and is not a thing the district lacks, and that the two halves of that sentence are both true and this district has only ever put one of them on a wall.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a document that cannot be argued with and cannot be certified is a bill, **and that a bill in this basin is the only thing a person can read and cannot change and that this district has four of them in two rooms and thirty-four miles away and has never put two of them on the same table.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that four documents are not a column and that a clerk was not asked to count them and did not, **and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and that a bill is not one of the six and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve and neither moved on the ninth of February.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the ninth of February is a day with no instrument on it and that a day with no instrument on it is a day a person has to come and find out about, and that about nine people came.

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A ROOM MET WITH NO INSTRUMENT ON IT, AND THREE DAYS WERE IN THE SPACES ON A PAGE IN A BUILDING, AND THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-THREE DAYS, AND NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAS EVER POSTED IT, AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS FIFTY DAYS OFF AND THE FIRST OF APRIL IS FIFTY-ONE.**
