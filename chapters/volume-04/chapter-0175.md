# Chapter 175: Nobody Is Going to Publish a Person

---

The fifteenth of the second month was the day the man the figure of twenty-one years is against asked the registrar of this district's records office to put a sentence in a public book, and she said no in four seconds, and the two of them were in the same room at the same hour on purpose for the seventh time since the eighteenth of December, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked to write the arranging down as anything and that she is not going to be.

He asked in the six things at about eleven in the morning, in the yard, and he asked for the page after the page the five terms went into on the twentieth of January, and he said the reason himself before she had answered.

"**A woman at a counter has said a house has not named a day. If that sentence is in your book, any person can walk up to a market counter and read that a house has not named a day.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said the second half of that before he was asked and that she is not going to put a number on it. Nobody took that up and a man of fifty-six said that the sentence is the best argument anybody has made in this yard for putting a thing in a public book and that the argument is made by the man whose own method it is.

"**No. I will not.**"

She said it in four seconds and then she gave the reason, and nobody had asked her for the reason, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked for it and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was. It is in the minute in her own words and it is fifty-four words.

"**She said it once, at a counter, to a room with two people in it, and she was not asked for it. If I put it in that book it stops being a thing she said and it starts being a record of my office and I did not ask her for that either.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the district's whole argument since the eighth of March has been that a thing a stranger can go and look at is worth something, and that this is the first time a person has run into the other end of it.

A man of fifty-six said that a person who says a thing once at a counter and is then printed has had the saying taken off her and put in a book, and that the district was built on a rule about security and not on a rule about sentences, **and that the two rules have never been in the same room until this morning and that when they were, the older one lost.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a records office which enters only what it has seen is a records office and not a newspaper, and that this district has one of those and said so out loud on the thirtieth of January, **and that a sentence is not a thing an office has seen and that the office has a page for what a person was owed and has no page for what a person said.**

**AND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST ASKED THE REGISTRAR OF THIS DISTRICT'S RECORDS OFFICE TO PUT A SENTENCE SPOKEN AT A COUNTER THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST INTO THE WARD MARKET'S OWN BOOK, AND SHE SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS, AND THE REASON WAS GIVEN BEFORE ANYBODY ASKED FOR IT.**

---

Before they went on with it, a man of fifty-six went down to the ward market counter at about half past eleven in the morning and asked the clerk of the ward market whether a sentence could go into the market's own book without a records office standing behind it, and the clerk of the ward market said she had never been asked that and that she would have to ask somebody above her.

Nobody argued. A man of fifty-six came back up the bank and entered in the yard that a book in a market is a book with a keeper and a keeper has somebody above her, **and that a sentence a person said at a counter has no keeper and no one above the keeper, and that a thing with no keeper cannot be put in a book in this district and that is not a rule and is a wall.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the fifth of the five terms says the house is not a person and that this district has now found the other side of that sentence, which is that a records office is not a person either, **and that two institutions that are not people are the two most powerful things in this basin and that the finding of the fifteenth of the second month is that neither of them can be asked to hold a sentence.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the word institution is not a word this district uses and that she entered the sentence instead, and that a clerk of the ward market was not asked a second question and did not have to be, **and that the count of documents nobody owns is three and did not move, and that a sentence a person said is not a document and is not a fourth.**

Then they went on with it, and nobody in the yard interrupted for about half an hour, and this is the record of that half hour and it is in the minute because a clerk of nineteen years wrote all of it down and did not shorten it.

He said that the sentence is the only thing anybody in this district has got that the house thirty-four miles east cannot take back, and that a house can write to a household and can fill a column and can stand at a sluice and write nothing, and cannot unsay a thing a person said at its own counter.

She said that a house does not have to unsay it, and that the sentence will be in a book in a market for as long as the book stands, and that a man who is a figure of twenty-one years is against has just described exactly the thing this district has spent four months refusing to build, which is a place where a person's own words are held on to after they have stopped.

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a house's sentence is the house's own and that this district's sentence is the district's own, and that neither of them can be quoted back at the person who said it without their permission, **and that a rule which is good on a sheet and bad in a book is a rule that has been moved and not a rule that has been broken.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a sentence a person said and a copy of it are two things and that only one of them is hers, and that he has been copying things for thirty years and has never once had a person ask him to keep a copy of what he said.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man who deals in second-hand paper is entered before he speaks as not speaking for the room, and that this is a count of not-speaking and is not a count of not-askings, and that the two are not added.

The rule arrived in the yard at about half past twelve in the afternoon and nobody standing in that yard had asked for it, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not ask for it and is the only person in that yard who could have got one by asking.

> **A THING A STRANGER MAY READ AND NOT CARRY IS HALF AN INSTRUMENT, AND THE HALF THIS DISTRICT IS SHORT IS THE HALF THAT GOES.**
>
> **A SENTENCE SAID AT A COUNTER BY A PERSON WHO WAS NOT ASKED IS A SENTENCE AND NOT A DOCUMENT. A DOCUMENT IS A THING A PERSON HAS DECIDED TO KEEP.**
>
> **TO PUT A SENTENCE IN A PUBLIC BOOK IS TO MAKE IT A THING YOUR OFFICE STANDS BEHIND. YOU MAY DO THAT TO A HOUSE. YOU MAY NOT DO IT TO A PERSON.**
>
> **A DISTRICT THAT CAN ONLY ACT ON WHAT A PERSON CARRIES HOME CANNOT ACT ON WHAT A PERSON SAID. THAT IS A LIMIT AND NOT A FINDING, AND A LIMIT IS STILL A THING TO WORK IN.**
>
> **YOU MAY NOT ASK HER A SECOND THING. THAT IS NOT A CHOICE YOU GET TO MAKE LATER.**

A man of fifty-six said that the fourth of the five is a rule and not a rule this district has had before, and that the first of the five is the sentence in the counter's hand and that the district has been building on that sentence for a month.

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the second of the five is the one the whole of this morning is about, **and that a district that spent a month teaching people to insist on a thing they can go and read has just been told what the reading costs, and that the cost is a person, and that the person is not in the room and was not asked.**

Nobody answered that. A man of fifty-six said that a man who can post a security and cannot print a sentence is a man whose instrument is a document and that this district has known that since the eighth of March and has not had it put in a yard before.

**AND THE RULE ARRIVED IN A YARD AT ABOUT HALF PAST TWELVE IN THE AFTERNOON ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH AND NOBODY ASKED FOR IT, AND ITS LAST LINE IS THAT YOU MAY NOT ASK HER A SECOND THING, AND THAT IS NOT A CHOICE YOU GET TO MAKE LATER.**

---

What it cost was counted out loud in the yard afterwards and nobody argued with the counting.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the sentence a woman said at a counter is now the only thing in this district's business that is true, is not in a book, cannot be carried away, cannot be given in evidence, cannot be read by a stranger, and cannot be got back, and that all five of those are the district's own rules applied to it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the five of those are not a column and that she was not asked to make one, **and that a sentence which cannot be carried is not a security and is not nothing, and that a thing a stranger can go and look at that nobody goes and looks at is a third thing, and that this district has not got a word for the third thing and said so on the sixth of the second month and has not got one today.**

A man of fifty-six said that the third thing has now got a second instance, and that two is not a rate and is not a column, and that the two instances are a page in a lane four miles down a hill nobody has gone to look at, and a sentence at a counter thirty-four miles east, **and that the two are not joined and that joining them would be the first time this district had joined a thing in a building to a thing in a house and it would have been wrong.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against has now gone four times since the eighth of March to a thing he cannot bring back, and that a man who keeps going is not a man who is learning and is a man who has not worked out what he is collecting.

A man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row was in the yard and said one thing about that, in the six things, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty words.

"**You cannot take a thing home off a counter, and you cannot take a year back, and those are the same size and neither of them fits in a coat.**"

The yard let it stand. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five posted securities said the second half of that before he was asked for it and that she is not going to put a number on it, **and that a year is not an asset and is not a security and nobody can post one against it, and that the two of those are a thing he has said before in this yard and are not to be joined to what he said today.**

**AND A SENTENCE SPOKEN AT A COUNTER IS THE ONLY TRUE THING IN THIS DISTRICT'S BUSINESS THAT IS NOT IN A BOOK, AND A DISTRICT THAT HAS SPENT A MONTH INSISTING ON THINGS STRANGERS CAN GO AND READ HAS FOUND OUT WHAT THE READING COSTS, AND THE COST IS A PERSON WHO IS NOT IN THE ROOM.**

---

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam asked, in the six things, at about half past one in the afternoon, what the fourth line of the offer actually is, and a man of fifty-six said that nobody in this district knows and has never known, and that it is on a table three hundred and thirty miles or thirty-four miles away and has never been read out loud here.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fourth line is not entered, and that a thing nobody in this district can state is not a thing this district can carry, **and that a woman whose name is in no register in the west bank has offered four lines three times and has had none of them taken and none of them refused, and that a person who has offered a thing three times and not had it taken is a person and not a house and is not joined to a house.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a woman of about thirty-five at a counter said one thing on the ninth that was not offered, and that a person who says one unasked thing has not made an offer, **and that an offer is a thing three times asked for and once refused, and that nobody in this district has refused anything and that not refusing is not a kind of refusing and is not a column.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a woman who has offered a thing three times to a district that has not taken it has learned what the district will not do, and that a house which learns what a district will not do is further ahead than a house which knows what a district cannot do, **and that the difference between the two is about eleven weeks and both of them are this district's own fault.**

The man of about fifty-five who keeps a flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace walked up into the yard at about twenty past two in the afternoon on his own account, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the fact that nobody had sent for him and that nobody had told him he was coming.

He said that the days are going into the spaces faster than he can write them, and that there are eleven of them and that the page has about forty spaces and that a page is not a thing that ends when a man runs out of it.

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a man who fills a page has got a fourth thing this district did not have two weeks ago, which is the end of a page, **and that the end of a page is the only kind of ending in this district's business that a person can bring about by doing the right thing, and that a season ends on the thirty-first of March and a day ends when it is dark and a page ends when it is full.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, what he is going to do about the forty names already on the page with nothing against them, and the man of about fifty-five said that he is not going to do anything about them, and gave the reason in a sentence that a clerk of nineteen years entered in his own words.

"**They came before the fourth and I did not write it and I am not going to go back and invent it for a page.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has started a record and will not fill in the part he missed has done the thing the district's own rule arrived in a lane on the second of the second month told him to do, **and that a day which is absent is not a day of zero and cannot be given to anybody, and that about forty names on a page in a building are forty absences and that the count of forty is a figure a man said about another man's page and is not a column.**

Nobody argued. A man of fifty-six said that a page which is filling up is a page a stranger can go and look at, and that nobody has gone and looked at it, **and that the district put five terms in a public book on the twentieth of January and a day in a space on the fourth of the second month and neither of the two has been read by anybody who was not sent, and that two of those is not a rate.**

The rest of it was the lane, and the slate went past the kitchen in Alder Reach for the eleventh time on the evening of the fifteenth of February, and the two of them stopped in the lane again and neither wrote on it, and a clerk of nineteen years was not there and what the lane holds is a page in nobody's book.

She said one thing in the lane and it is in the minute in her own words and it is twenty-seven words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road was about nine paces behind them and did not hear it and was not asked.

"**I am not going to ask her about the fourth line, and nobody in this yard is going to either, including the man who posted the man.**"

Nobody asked her why and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked her why and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

He said that he is not going to ask her about the fourth line either, and that the fourth line of an offer that has been on a table three times has still not been read out loud in this district, and that a clerk of nineteen years is not going to enter that they are going to read it and is not going to be asked to.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that, and entered that a clerk is a person and not a rule, **and that two people have now said out loud in a lane that they are not going to do a thing, and that a thing two people have said they will not do is not a rule and is not a column and is not written down.**

A man of fifty-six said that a sentence a man says out loud against his own advantage is not a rule whether it is said in a room or in a lane, and that this district has three of those now and that a man of fifty-six has said one of them and is not going to say it again.

Nobody took it up. A man of fifty-six said the interval out loud at about six in the evening and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down, and the sum was put under the figure.

"**The thirty-first of March is forty-four days off today and the first of April is forty-five, and the eleventh of the second month was four days ago, and a figure with no day on it is not an interval.**"

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in the yard at about half past four and was not asked for anything, and she said that the nearest of the four dates on her bucket is the sixteenth of the second month and that it is tomorrow, **and that a bucket has four dates on it and a book has two and a man has three hundred and sixty-five in an index, and that the three of them are not added to each other anywhere in this district.**

Nobody took that up and a man of fifty-six said that the district has three registers and will not join them and that the not-joining is the only rule the district has that has never once been broken, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that a rule this district has never broken is not a rule and is a habit, **and that a habit is not entered in a book and is not going to be.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is eighty-three and is a count of days and not a column, **and that the refusals to read are nine, the departure is unspent, and there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading, and that a clerk of nineteen years has now been asked nothing in this district eleven weeks running and is not keeping a count of that because a count of it would be a column.**

**AND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A SENTENCE WAS REFUSED ENTRY IN A PUBLIC BOOK BY THE PERSON WHO HOLDS THE BOOK, AND A SLATE WENT PAST A KITCHEN IN ALDER REACH FOR THE ELEVENTH TIME AND NEITHER OF THE TWO OF THEM WROTE ON IT, AND THE FOURTH LINE OF AN OFFER THAT HAS BEEN ON A TABLE THREE TIMES IS STILL NOT READ OUT LOUD IN THIS DISTRICT.**
