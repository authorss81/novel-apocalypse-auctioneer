# Chapter 153: A Name in a Bearer Column

---

A man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing copied the four printed things onto a page in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-seventh of December, and the original was in a pocket on the tide and did not come up the bank again that week.

He said yes in four seconds and said the second half before he was asked for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both, and that he will keep it and will not be the man whose copies are the record, and that a copy is the document and the original is a room, and that the man who wrote that sentence in November is in this yard and has not said it again and does not need to.

**A clerk of nineteen years entered that the copy is on one page and is not in a book and is not in the ward market's book and is not in the records office, and that the count of documents nobody owns is three and did not move, and that a copy a man of about twenty-three keeps is a copy and not a fourth.**

The copy went out twice at the counter that afternoon and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and a reader who is about fifty read the fourth of the four first and then read the first, and said that she does that because people read the top of a page and the fourth is at the foot and the fourth is the only one anybody will act on.

> **THE FAMILY SHALL NAME ONE PERSON IN THE BEARER COLUMN. THE NAMED PERSON SHALL NOT BE BELOW FIFTEEN AND SHALL NOT BE ASKED.**
>
> **IF THE FAMILY SHALL NOT PAY ON THE DAY, THE CLAIM OF THE FAMILY TO ONE PLACE IN THE FLOOD SHELTER AT THE SLUICE PASSES TO THIS HOUSE.**
>
> **THE HOUSE SHALL NOT ASK THE NAMED PERSON. THE FAMILY SHALL ANSWER FOR THE NAMED PERSON.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the room left the first of the four off the copy, and that the first of the four says the house gives water and seed before the season and takes the labour of the family after the season, and that the room left it off because a room in this district knows what a labour is and does not know what a house gives.

Nobody argued with that and a man of fifty-six said that it is the first time in eleven weeks a room has left a line off a copy, and that the line it left off is the only one anybody in the room would have benefited from, and that the two facts are in one line and are not joined.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a copy with a line off it is a document about a district and not about a house, and that the district has just corrected a stranger's paper and put its own figure in the place of his, **and that a copy is the document and the original is a room and that neither of the two has been in the room the sheet came from.**

---

The argument went on from about two in the afternoon until the light went, and it is in a minute of eleven lines and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was the longest single sitting since the eighteenth of December and that nobody left it, and that the man who digs loam sent word that he was not coming and that he was not asked.

The question a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put at about half past two was whether the house can do anything with the claim once it has passed to them, and the room found that it cannot answer it, and that the sheet does not say, and that a sheet which does not say is a sheet whose silence belongs to whoever wrote it.

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the third of the four says the house shall not ask the named person, and that the fourth says the family shall answer for the named person, and that between those two sentences there is a gap the width of a boy of nine and that the gap is not an accident and that a printed gap is the cheapest thing a house can build.

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that a printed gap is cheaper than a hidden clause, and that this sheet has no hidden clause and that the room has been praising it for that for two days, **and that a sheet with a gap in it is more dangerous than a sheet with a clause in it, because a clause can be argued with and a gap can only be filled in by whoever fills it in first.**

A man of fifty-six said the other half and it is in the minute in his own words, and it is thirty-four words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got thirty-four and did not count them again.

"**A document says what it does not know and that is honest. A document says what it will not say and that is a door, and a door is a thing you wait beside.**"

Nobody argued and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the district's own third form of a notice has a hole on its face and that the hole says nobody has established what a withdrawal costs and nobody is going to establish it by asking the person, **and that the hole in that form is a thing four people agreed to leave and the gap in this sheet is a thing nobody in this room can close, and that the two are two things and are not joined.**

A man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace was in the yard for the whole afternoon and was asked, in the five things, what a house could do with a claim to one place in his building, and he gave an answer in a sentence, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in his own words.

"**They would send me a line with a name on it and I would have to put a mark beside the name and there is no line on my page for a mark.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked in the five things and answered and that he was not asked what his page does have lines for and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a man who keeps a book of names for nine years has said that his page has no line for a mark, and that a mark may not carry a job in this district and that the two are two things and are not joined.**

And a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a page with no line for a mark is a page that has been asked the wrong question for nine years, **and that the question it has never been asked is who is allowed to write on it, and that a page a person can write on is a page a person can argue with, and that the district found that about a board in the middle of the year and has not gone back to it.**

Nobody asked him to finish and he finished. He said that a book of forty names in nine years is forty claims and that a claim is not a name, and that a man who cannot tell the difference will do whatever the last letter tells him to do, and that nobody has sent him a letter and that this is the first time anybody has come near it.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF DECEMBER A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE WHO KEEPS THE FLOOD SHELTER AT THE SLUICE END OF A LANE OFF THE LOWER TERRACE SAID THAT A HOUSE WOULD SEND HIM A LINE WITH A NAME ON IT AND HE WOULD HAVE TO PUT A MARK BESIDE THE NAME AND THERE IS NO LINE ON HIS PAGE FOR A MARK, AND THE FINDING OF THE AFTERNOON IS THAT A DOCUMENT WHICH SAYS WHAT IT DOES NOT KNOW IS HONEST AND A DOCUMENT WHICH SAYS WHAT IT WILL NOT SAY IS A DOOR.**

---

Then the thing a clerk of nineteen years said at about four in the afternoon, and she said it standing up, and a man of fifty-six asked her afterwards whether she wanted it in the minute and she said she did.

She said that a name in a bearer column is what makes a row a row, and that in December a book came into a room from a house that guarantees things and it had nineteen rows and four columns and three of the rows had a name in the bearer column and sixteen did not, and that the sixteen have no word in any book anybody in this district has read.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the two books are not joined and that they are not to be joined, **and that the sixteen are a third thing and that the third thing has no word, and that a name in a column is the only difference between the third thing and a thing a person can be held to, and that this district found the third thing in December and has now been handed the other half of it on a piece of paper.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two books are not joined and that the two are two things, and that she had said the thing standing up because she is the clerk of the page and the page is the one in this district with a column in it for a person, **and that the column is the sixth one and the sixth one says what this district asked and not what a house took, and that the two columns are not the same column and are not to be added.**

Nobody took it up for about nine seconds. Then a man of fifty-six said that the finding of the twenty-seventh of December is that a name in a bearer column looks exactly like a claim, and that the only thing standing between a family's boy and a row in a book a stranger can buy is a person asking the question in a room, and that this district has been doing that for eleven weeks and has not once done it for a family that came in with a sheet.

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide was not in the yard and was sent for and came at about half past four, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was sent for by a man of fifty-six and not by the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, and that the reason is in the minute in his own words, and that it is twenty-seven words.

"**We want to ask you one thing and we want to ask it once, and we would rather do it in a room than at a counter.**"

She said she would rather be asked in a room, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of times a person has been asked in this district to be asked in a room rather than at a counter is two, **and that the two are this one and the ninth household in December, and that the two are not joined and that neither is a column.**

And on the twenty-seventh of december **A NAME IN A BEARER COLUMN IS WHAT MAKES A ROW A ROW, AND THREE OF NINETEEN ROWS IN A LEDGER OF A HOUSE THAT GUARANTEES THINGS HAVE A NAME IN THAT COLUMN AND SIXTEEN DO NOT, AND THE SIXTEEN HAVE NO WORD IN ANY BOOK ANYBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAS READ, AND THE TWO BOOKS ARE NOT JOINED.**

---

The question they asked her was one question, and a man of fifty-six said it out loud in the room and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in his own words and the minute says a boy of nine is in the bearer column of a sheet his mother signed at a counter two days' walk away on the twenty-second of December.

The room let it stand and the woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said yes, and said the second half before anybody asked her for it, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said the second half before she was asked and that the count of that is five and is not a column, and that the five are not joined to each other.**

"**His name is in one place and it is not in your books and I would like it to stay in one place.**"

Nobody argued with it and a man of fifty-six said that the boy's name is not in any book in this district and that the room has not written it down and did not write it while she was saying it, **and that a name in a bearer column cannot be taken off the column by the person it is put on, and that the rule is in the market's own book and was entered there in the middle of the year, and that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage is not written down and he would not have it written down.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what happens if the family pays on the day, and the woman said it does not know, and a man of fifty-six said that the sheet does not say either and that a sheet which does not say what a payment does is a sheet that has left the only door open, **and that a house which leaves one door open is a house that expects to be walked through it, and that a room which finds that out in December is a room that has about nine days.**

The twenty-ninth of December is the day, and a man of fifty-six said so and read it off the copy, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the date and entered that no figure is due until that day and that the copy carries the day with its month on it, **and that a day in a document is a thing a room can count and a gap in a document is not, and that this district has spent eleven weeks learning the first and not the second.**

Nobody asked the woman to pay anything and nobody suggested it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody did, and that she was not asked what she intended to do, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF DECEMBER A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD HOUSE ON THE TIDE SAID THAT HER BOY'S NAME IS IN ONE PLACE AND IT IS NOT IN THIS DISTRICT'S BOOKS AND SHE WOULD LIKE IT TO STAY IN ONE PLACE, AND THE BOY'S NAME WAS NOT WRITTEN DOWN WHILE SHE WAS SAYING IT, AND NOBODY ASKED HER WHAT SHE INTENDED TO DO ABOUT THE TWENTY-NINTH OF DECEMBER.**

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against asked her, in the five things, at about a quarter to five in the afternoon, whether the copy could be read out at the ward market counter every morning for a month, and he said the reason out loud before she answered, and the reason is in the minute in his own words.

"**So that a person can come and look at it. That is the whole of the reason and it is the reason this district has used since the eighth of March.**"

She said no in four seconds. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she said no in four seconds and was not asked for a reason, and that she gave one anyway, and that it is in the minute in her own words, and that it is twenty-five words, and that a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-five and said it out loud.

"**You would read his name out in a market every morning for a month and I would be the woman whose boy was read out.**"

The room let it stand and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said yes in four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he accepted it in four seconds and that nobody argued with her and that the two are two things.

A man of fifty-six said that the whole of this district's method is that a document a person can go and look at is worth more than a document that cannot be looked at, and that a person has just refused the method on the ground that it would put a name of nine into a market every morning, **and that the refusal is correct and that the method is not a rule and that a method a person can refuse is not a rule and that this is the first time anybody in eleven weeks has refused the method and not a document.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage in a room is not a rule and is not written down, and that a man who gives up a good instrument because a woman told him what her boy would sound like at a counter is not giving up an instrument and is finding out what it was for.

Then the woman of about forty-four who keeps the Road House on the tide set the limit herself, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she set it and that nobody asked her to and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

"**Read the four lines and not the name. Put it up where a stranger can see it and leave the column out of it.**"

A man of fifty-six asked her how a person was going to know the sheet was a real one with the name left off it, and she said that a sheet with a name left off it is a sheet anybody can copy and that a sheet anybody can copy is worse than one nobody can, **and that she had not thought of that when she said it and that she was saying it anyway, and that this is the first thing she has said in eleven weeks that she did not think of first.**

Nobody argued with her and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the copy now stands with the second of the four lines left out and that the second of the four lines is the one that names a person, **and that a copy of a document with the naming line left off is a copy a stranger can take cold and put in a wrong mouth, and that the woman who allowed it knew that and allowed it, and that the two facts are in one line and are not joined.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF DECEMBER A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD HOUSE ON THE TIDE REFUSED IN FOUR SECONDS TO HAVE A COPY READ OUT AT A COUNTER EVERY MORNING WITH HER BOY'S NAME ON IT, AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST ACCEPTED IT IN FOUR SECONDS, AND SHE THEN ALLOWED THE FOUR LINES TO BE READ WITH THE NAMING LINE LEFT OUT.**

---

The twenty-eighth of December was a day of nothing happening, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that and did not enter a finding in it, **and that the count of entries with a finding in them since the eighteenth of December is two and did not move, and that the count of entries with no finding in them is a column she keeps and does not publish and that she is not going to be asked about it.**

The train on the siding had stood three hundred and nineteen days on the twenty-seventh and three hundred and twenty on the twenty-eighth, and a man of seventeen wrote both figures on one line in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and a clerk of nineteen years read Ivo Stenn's separate sheet out on the twenty-eighth because a minute is read out.

She read it back after the ledger as she reads it every time, **and got twenty as printed and nineteen as called, and entered both figures, and entered that the count of refusals to read is nine and did not move in December and that the departure is unspent and that there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading.**

A man of fifty-six said that the board outside the room has carried four dates for two months and a fifth day has not been written, **and that a room whose only instrument runs out on a date is a room a clerk can end, and that the clerk has not ended it, and that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is thirty-four and is a count of days and not a column.**

A man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row was in the yard on the twenty-eighth and said that a claim to a place in a building is the only security he has ever heard of that a person can be turned out of, and that a wheel and a dray can be taken back by a man with the receipt, and that the receipt is the difference, **and that a receipt is a thing a stranger can go and look at and a claim to a place in a building is not.**

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six entered it in the minute, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row has now said two things in a yard in three days and that the two are in two minutes and are not joined, **and that the count of things this district does not have is five and did not move and that a receipt is not one of the five and that a claim to a place in a building is also not one of the five and that neither of them is going to be.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF DECEMBER A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY AT A FORGE END OFF SALT ROW SAID THAT A WHEEL AND A DRAY CAN BE TAKEN BACK BY A MAN WITH THE RECEIPT AND THAT THE RECEIPT IS THE DIFFERENCE, AND THAT A RECEIPT IS A THING A STRANGER CAN GO AND LOOK AT AND A CLAIM TO A PLACE IN A BUILDING IS NOT, AND THE COUNT OF THINGS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE IS FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE.**
