# Chapter 187: The First of the Third Month

---

The first of the third month came on a wet morning and the month turned on it, and the month before it is the second month and the month before that is the first month, and the year that began on the first of January is the eighteenth after the Long Fracture, and the train on the siding had stood three hundred and eighty-three days.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the turn was said once on the first of the third month and that the two months before it were named with it, and that it is not going to be said again in this district, **and that a turn of a month is not an event and is not a season and is not a term and is not going into any of the three counts and has gone into none of them.**

Nobody argued with that. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that this district has now had three month turns in the year and has done nothing about any of them, and a man of fifty-six said that a district which has had three turns and has done nothing about three of them is a district that has not confused a turn with an event, **and that not confusing a turn with an event is the only thing this district has done about all three and that doing it three times is not a practice and is a coincidence with a date on it.**

Then the board, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not ask for a fifth date on it, and nobody asked her for one either, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not ask and that she was not asked, **and that a board which has carried four dates that four other people set is not a board this district has ever written on and is not going to be written on by a clerk because the month turned.**

The board outside the room had carried the twenty-fourth of December for sixty-seven days and it had not been washed, and beside the index a man of about seventeen sets down the day's figure in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date.

A man of fifty-six said the interval out loud at about ten in the morning and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down, and the sum was put under the figure the way it always is.

"**The thirty-first of March is thirty days off this day and the first of April is thirty-one.**"

The whole of the arithmetic was not done in that yard on the first of the third month and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked for it and that she was not going to ask for it, and the man of about nineteen who walked in from the road had the index in his coat and read the tail of it out by himself at about half past ten.

"**The index says three hundred and twenty-three at the last day of December, and this morning is three hundred and eighty-three, and January is thirty-one days long and February is twenty-eight days long, and one more day is sixty, and three hundred and twenty-three and sixty is three hundred and eighty-three, and that is the check.**"

A man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted the whole of that himself and got fifty-six and said it out loud, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who checks a figure against his own sum is not a clerk of anything and has not been asked to be one, **and that the four lines of arithmetic that were read out loud in this yard on the twenty-second of the second month are not the four lines of this and that the two are not joined and are not a rate and are not a column.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is ninety-seven and is a count of days and not a column, **and that the board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for sixty-seven days and that nobody wrote on it on the first of the third month, and that the count of boards is five and the count of lines is six and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty.**

**AND ON THE FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THREE DAYS, AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS THIRTY DAYS AWAY AND THE FIRST OF APRIL IS THIRTY-ONE.**

---

The cart came at about ten in the morning and it came before eleven, and a man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing had said on the twenty-third of the second month that it would.

It is one cart with two wheels at the front and a tailboard and about eleven sacks on it, and the sacks came off first and the sheets came off after in a bundle of six held with a piece of tarred string, and a man of about forty-four who drives a cart for a house was on the box and was not asked anything by anybody in this district in nine years and was not asked anything today.

The sacks came off the tailboard one at a time and there were about nine people on that lane by the fourth of them, and a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale at the sluice end of the loams had a barrow under the second one and did not put her hand under the third.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a barrow under the second sack and not the third is a woman who has decided what she is carrying and is not going to be told about it, **and that a woman who has stood at a scale for nine years can lift a sack onto a barrow and is not a bearer of the sack and is not a keeper of the barrow, and that the district's own list of things a person is not applies to her and does not apply to what she is holding.**

The cart came before eleven and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud that a man who stood in a rut with his hand in it on the twenty-third of the second month had said a cart gets through before eleven and not after two, **and that the district did not check him then and cannot check him now and has entered after the fact that he was right, and that a figure checked after the thing it was about has happened is not a check and is not going into any of the counts.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure of eleven in the morning and two in the afternoon was said by a man of about twenty-three and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked, **and that a man who is right about a rut is not a keeper of ruts and is not an instrument and the count of six did not move on the first of the third month.**

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households was standing at the head of the lane when it came, and he had said on the twenty-fifth of the second month that he was not walking them round a third time, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was there and that nobody sent for him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, whether he was going to walk them, and the man of about forty-eight said that he had said he was not going to and that he was standing there, and a man of fifty-six said that those two facts are both true and that a man who says he is not going to do a thing and is standing in the place where the thing is going to happen is a man and not a rule and is not written down.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the sheets and the seed came down on one cart and that the two of them are not the same arrangement, and that the seed goes to five doors and the sheets go to five doors, and that the sixth household is a cart and a sheet is given to a family and a family is a thing with a name on it and he has not got one.

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the other half of that himself, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I am not walking the sixth one to anybody. It is not his and it is not going to be left at his door by me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that there are six sheets on that cart and that five of them are for households and one of them is not, and that a sheet which is not for anybody is not a refusal and is not a not-asking and is not a sixth of anything, **and that the man who lives in a cart was out of that lane on the first of the third month and was not sent for and is not in this district's books and is not going into them.**

**AND ON THE FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH A CART CAME UP THE LANE BEFORE ELEVEN IN THE MORNING WITH ELEVEN SACKS AND SIX SHEETS ON IT, AND THE SIXTH HOUSEHOLD IS A CART AND A SHEET IS GIVEN TO A FAMILY AND HE HAS NOT GOT ONE.**

---

The finding of the day came out of a yard at about half past ten and it is the plainest thing this district has found in a month, and a man of fifty-six said it and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in her own words.

The yard decided on the nineteenth of the second month to do nothing about a man's refusal for nine days, and doing nothing about it was entered as a decision, and the nine days ended on the first of the third month, which is the day the sheets come down on a cart with the seed.

"**We decided about a man. The cart does not know that. A cart is not a man and it does not take instructions from a yard, and it came.**"

Nobody in that yard argued with it. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that nine days is nine days and that the district does not go back on a decision before the day it made it, and that this is not going back on it, **and that a district which has decided about a man and is now dealing with a cart is a district that has found out the difference between a decision and a schedule and has done it by accident.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a decision about a man and a schedule about a cart are two things and are not a column, and that a man of fifty-six entered the decision on the nineteenth of the second month and the schedule belongs to a house and neither of them is going to be asked about the other, **and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and the registrar's own count stands at twelve and that neither moved on the first of the third month.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the six households are going to have a second sheet in about nine weeks whether anybody in this yard asked for it or not, and that a household which refused in about four seconds is going to be asked a second time by a man putting a sheet on a step, **and that a household which has not been asked again is going to be asked again, and that the record of the three still says not asked and the record of the three is about to be wrong by the middle of March.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a record of not-askings which is made wrong by a house and a cart and not by anybody in this yard is a record that was never a refusal and has never been a refusal, **and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the sheet on the step is an asking and the asking is not this district's and the asking is not going to be entered as one.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that the yard is not going to write to the six households and tell them a thing has changed, and that a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally has said he is not walking them round, and that those two decisions together mean the sheets are going to sit at the head of a lane and go to whoever comes for them.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about forty-eight, in the six things, what he was going to do about the three that had not been asked again, and the man of about forty-eight said that he had been asked that on the twenty-sixth of the second month and that the answer had not changed, and gave the rest of it himself in a sentence that a man of about nineteen counted and got forty-one.

"**I have walked a board to five doors twice and I do not know what I am supposed to do about the other one and nobody has told me and I am not going to work it out in a yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the question has now been asked twice and that two is a count of a question and is not a column and is not added to the count of not-askings about anybody in particular, **and that a district which asks a household three times in six weeks has turned an offer into a campaign, and that a campaign is a thing a house does and not a thing a district is, and that the yard said that on the nineteenth of the second month and has not gone back on it.**

**AND THE FINDING OF THE FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH IS THAT THIS DISTRICT DECIDED FOR NINE DAYS ABOUT A MAN AND THE CART CAME ANYWAY, AND A DECISION ABOUT A MAN IS NOT A DECISION ABOUT A SCHEDULE.**

---

The seed went down to the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale at the sluice end of the loams and has stood at it nine years, and she weighed about eleven sacks on the first of the third month and did not read anything that came with them, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in the six things, how many of the eleven were for the household that refused a sheet, and she said that she does not know and that she weighs sacks and does not do houses.

Nobody argued with that. A man of fifty-six said that a woman who will not say which of eleven sacks is for which house has just kept six of them out of this district's business for the fifth time, **and that the count of protected things is five and did not move on the first of the third month, and that a woman who does not do houses is not a protected thing and is not a sixth of the five.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that the woman of about thirty-six has stood at a scale for nine years and been paid nothing and is that person, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said the second half of that before he was asked and that she is not going to put a number on it.

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace was in that yard and had not been sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him, **and entered him before he spoke as not speaking for the room, and that a count of not-speaking is not a count of not-askings and the two are not added to each other anywhere in this district's books.**

He said that he has been in the second-hand paper trade for thirty years and that in thirty years about nine people have come and asked him what a page is worth, **and that eight of the nine wanted to know what they could sell it for and the ninth wanted to know whether it was his, and that the ninth is the man of about twenty-three and that one is not a rate and is not a column and is not a figure about anybody.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a page a man owns and a page a house owns are two different problems and that the one this district has is the second of them, **and that a man who bought his own paper for nine weeks and worked out on his own that a page is his has done the only kind of checking anybody has done in this yard in a month, and that he did it in a lane and is not going to be given anything for it.**

The man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing was at the Road House on the tide at about two in the afternoon with the copy in the inside of his coat and had not been sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was there and that nobody sent for him and that he did not take the copy out and did not read it out.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, whether he was going to give it to anybody, and he said not today, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said the second half of it before he was asked and that she is not going to put a number on it.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who has carried a page to a door for nine days and has not taken it out of his coat has done the whole of what a person can do with a page and has done none of what anybody wanted, **and that a man who copies for nothing and has asked for eleven coppers a week and been refused is not a clerk and is not a keeper and is not anybody's.**

**AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE WHO COPIES FOR NOTHING STOOD AT A ROAD HOUSE ON THE TIDE AT ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON WITH A COPY IN THE INSIDE OF HIS COAT AND DID NOT TAKE IT OUT.**

---

The last of the carrying was done by two men and neither of them is paid and one of them has said no three times in a yard in this district's business.

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row carried four of the six sheets down the lane to four doors between about eleven in the morning and about three in the afternoon, and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally carried two, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked by a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing whether he wanted the second one taken off him and that he said no in about two seconds, **and that a man who will not walk them round a third time carried two of them on the first of the third month and that those two facts are entered in one line and are not joined.**

Nobody in that lane said anything about it. A man of fifty-six said that a man who has said he will carry a thing and not be paid and not be in a book has carried four sheets to four doors and has not been in a book, **and that the district has three instruments that work without anybody coming to a yard and has now had a fourth of them carry four sheets in a day, and that four is a count and not a column.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that **a man who carries a thing and is not paid for it and is not entered anywhere is not a bearer and is not a keeper and is not a security, and the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and is the same six it was on the second of January, and a man who carries four sheets is not a seventh of anything.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam carried the fifth sheet down the lane himself at about half past two in the afternoon and it was the first time in nine weeks he had carried a thing for anybody in this district's business, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that on his own account, **and that a man who digs loam nine days in ten carried a sheet and is not a bearer of it and is not a holder of it and is not a security, and that the count of six did not move and a sheet in a hand is not a seventh.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about thirty-one what the four doors said, and the man of about thirty-one said that three of them took the sheet off him and one of them did not come to the door, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the fourth of those is not a refusal and is not a not-asking and that nobody has been asked about the fourth and is not going to be.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a door that does not open to a sheet is not a household refusing a sheet and is a household with a man in it who is not in, **and that the two of those are not a column and are not to be counted, and that a man who is not in a house is not a person who has refused anything and has not been asked anything today.**

So the sheets went to four doors and one door did not open and the sixth sheet is still rolled and tied on a cart at the head of a lane, **and the tank four miles down a lane is down to about three days and a man of about fifty-five put loam in a crack in it for the fourth time on the thirtieth of the second month, and the train on the siding had stood three hundred and eighty-three days.**

**AND ON THE FIRST OF THE THIRD MONTH FOUR SHEETS WENT TO FOUR DOORS AND ONE DOOR DID NOT OPEN AND THE SIXTH SHEET IS STILL ROLLED AND TIED ON A CART AT THE HEAD OF A LANE.**
