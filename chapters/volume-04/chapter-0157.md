# Chapter 157: Nobody Came With a Line

---

The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace came up the bank on the fourth of January before ten in the morning on his own account.

And the first thing he said in the yard of Lot Seventeen was that he had come to tell them that nothing had come, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the order those two things were said in and entered that she did not ask him which of the two he had meant first, **and entered that the twenty-ninth of December is the day the third of the four printed things turns on, and that it went past in a fortnight in which this district argued about a board and a farm and a copy.**

He was asked, in the five things, what he had expected to come, and he said a line, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the word and entered that a line is a thing a person puts on a page and that the page he keeps has four pages and that the fourth page has a column headed for the name of a person who is in the building, **and that a line is not a figure and is not coppers and is not a year, and that nobody in this district can say what a line is worth to a house that has not written it.**

He said the second half of that before he was asked for it and gave the reason without being asked, and the reason is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got thirty and did not count them again.

"**Nine years I have kept that page and nobody has ever put a mark beside a name, and I have assumed somebody would come and tell me what to do.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that a man who has assumed for nine years is a man who has been owed an answer since the ninth year and has not asked for one, **and that the ninth year is the year a dray and its two shafts were given as a security, and that a man who has been owed an answer for nine years does not stop being owed it because the building is warm, and that the two of those are two things and are not joined.**

Then the man of about fifty-five read out the heading of his own page because a man of fifty-six asked him to and nobody asked him twice, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice and he read it out twice and a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong.

> **THE NAMES OF PERSONS IN THIS BUILDING. A PERSON IS ENTERED ON THE DAY THEY ARRIVE AND NOT ON THE DAY THEY LEAVE. NO LINE ON THIS PAGE IS FOR A MARK BESIDE A NAME.**

Nobody argued with it and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the second of the three sentences is the one a building runs on and that a person is entered when they arrive and is never entered when they go, **and that a page which cannot record a leaving cannot show a room who has left it, and that a shelter that cannot show a leaving is a shelter that has about nine hundred households' worth of people still in it in its own book.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was entered before he spoke as not speaking for the room and that this is not a column and carries no number on it, because a number on it would have been a column and a column is a thing a person is paid for, **and that about nine hundred households in this district are in no row of the page this district has been keeping and that a household is a count and a person is a count and the two are not added anywhere in this district's books.**

**AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE WHO KEEPS THE FLOOD SHELTER AT THE SLUICE END OF A LANE OFF THE LOWER TERRACE READ OUT THE HEADING OF HIS OWN PAGE AND IT SAYS THAT A PERSON IS ENTERED ON THE DAY THEY ARRIVE AND NOT ON THE DAY THEY LEAVE, AND THAT NO LINE ON THE PAGE IS FOR A MARK BESIDE A NAME, AND HE HAS KEPT IT NINE YEARS WITH ABOUT FORTY NAMES ON IT.**

---

The finding of the morning came out of a question a man of fifty-six asked and it is in the minute in his own words and it is sixteen words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got sixteen both times and did not say so.

"**What day was it, and where is that day written, and is it on the sheet?**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing came up from the lower terrace with the copy in his hand and put it on the trestle table, and a clerk of nineteen years read the third of the four printed things off it and then read off the corner, and the corner is not one of the four.

"**The twenty-ninth of December. W. J.**"

The room did not argue about it and a man of fifty-six said that the third of the four printed things says that if the family shall not pay on the day the claim passes to the house, and that the four printed things do not name the day, **and that the day that decides whether a family keeps a place in a building is written in a corner in a hand that is not the hand of the woman who signed and is not the hand of the man who filled the column and is not the hand of the house that gave the sheet.**

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide was sent for and came at about half past ten in the morning, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was sent for and that nobody asked her whether she could read the sheet again and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

She said she was told the day by a man behind a counter and that she did not write it down and that she could not have written it down because she cannot read the sheet at all, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that and entered that a figure a person says about herself is not a figure this district enters, **and entered that the person most affected by the day did not write it down and does not know it and is not going to be asked to learn it now.**

Nobody took that up and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a term on the face of a sheet is a thing a person was supposed to have known and that this term is not on the face and that the whole of the district's finding of the twenty-fifth of December holds and is worse, **and that a document which says what it does not know is honest and a document which says what it will not say is a door, and that this one has not even got a sentence in it to be a door in.**

A man of fifty-six asked the woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide what a place in the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace is worth in coppers, and she said she did not know, and gave no reason, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked and answered and not asked why.

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper was in the yard and said one thing about that, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is twenty-six words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-six and said it out loud.

"**I can price a dray because a dray has a maker's mark on it. A place in a building has no mark and cannot be sold.**"

Nobody argued and a man of fifty-six asked him what a house would do with a claim once it has passed to them, and the man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that the house would write it and the building would be told and the building would believe it, and that a thing a person is told by a house and cannot go and look at is not a security and is not nothing, **and that the whole of the law about it is that the building does not sell places, and that he has been selling paper for about thirty years and that is the only sentence he knows about buildings and that there is no other one.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that this is the second time in a fortnight that a person who was not asked has described a thing a person is told by a house and cannot inspect, **and that the first of the two is a rule which came into this yard on the second of January and says the only remedy for a thing that cannot be inspected is to take it down, and that the two occasions are not joined.**

Then a man of fifty-six asked a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road what he was doing on the twenty-ninth of December, and nobody had asked him that before, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked on the fourth of January and that he had not been asked before and that the two are two entries and are not joined, **and that a man who stands in a yard every day and is written on nothing and has been entered as not speaking for a room four times is not a man anybody is keeping and is nobody's keeper and is not to be turned into one.**

He said he was in the yard on the twenty-ninth of December at about ten in the morning and that about a dozen people were and that the whole of the day was about a farm four miles out past the loams, and a man of fifty-six read the four names back to him and he said that was right and that the man who brings a fold of paper was in and out four times.

Nobody argued and the man of about nineteen who walked in from the road said the other half of it on his own account and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-six words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got thirty-six and did not count them again.

"**Nobody said the day out loud in that yard and I would have remembered it, and I have been telling people for four days that I do not know what happened on the twenty-ninth of December.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said the second half before he was asked for it and that she was not going to enter a number on it, because a number on it would have been a column and a column is a thing a person is paid for, and that the count of the second half said before being asked stood where it stood on the twenty-ninth of December and has not been looked at since and that she is not going to look at it now.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the thing he had been holding since the morning of the twenty-seventh of December, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is twenty-three words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got twenty-three.

"**I did nothing on the twenty-ninth of December and I want it written that I did nothing and that I knew the day.**"

The room let it stand. A man of fifty-six said that the room spent the twenty-ninth of December on a farm four miles out past the loams and on a register thirty-four miles away, **and that four days' notice was given to a yard of about a dozen people on the twenty-seventh of December and that the only place the day was written was a page in the pocket of a man who copies for nothing and is not paid and does not own it.**

**AND THE DAY THAT DECIDES IT IS THE TWENTY-NINTH OF DECEMBER AND IT IS IN A CORNER OF THE COPY IN A HAND THAT IS NOT THE WOMAN'S AND NOT THE COUNTER'S AND NOT THE HOUSE'S, AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST SAID IN TWENTY-THREE WORDS THAT HE DID NOTHING ON THAT DAY AND THAT HE KNEW IT, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED IT IN HIS OWN WORDS.**

---

Nobody argued with what he had said and a man of fifty-six asked him what he was going to do about it now, and he said the thing he said on the twenty-seventh in the yard, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is forty-seven words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got forty-seven both times.

"**I cannot take it down. It is in a pocket two days' walk away and I do not have it and I cannot post against it either, because I have five things posted and four of them are on a table and the fifth is a man.**"

The room let that stand for about nine seconds and then a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said one thing, and it is in the minute in her own words and it is thirty-one words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got thirty-one and said it out loud.

"**Then nothing is standing between my boy and that sheet except a house that has not written to anybody, and a house that has not written to anybody can write tomorrow.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that a silence belonging to another party is not a security and is not nothing, and that the rule which came into this yard on the second of January says that a district which posts a thing nobody can inspect is running a lot and not holding a security and that the only remedy for a thing that cannot be inspected is to take it down, **and that the man who can be found has said out loud that he cannot take it down and that the reason is not a rule and is not written down and he would not have it written down.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage in a room is not a rule and is not written down, and that the register's standing question is the one the district has been carrying since the thirtieth of December, and that a district which cannot take down a thing two days' walk away is a district that has to be able to do something else instead and has not found what it is.

Then the man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace said the other half, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is twenty-one words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got twenty-one and did not count it again.

"**Anybody can write on my page. It has been anybody's for nine years and about forty people have never used it.**"

**AND ON THE FOURTH OF JANUARY A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE WHO KEEPS THE FLOOD SHELTER AT THE SLUICE END OF A LANE OFF THE LOWER TERRACE SAID THAT ANYBODY CAN WRITE ON HIS PAGE AND THAT IT HAS BEEN ANYBODY'S FOR NINE YEARS, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID THAT THE DISTRICT FOUND THE SAME THING ABOUT A BOARD IN THE MIDDLE OF THE YEAR AND DID NOT COME BACK TO IT.**

---

They went down the bank together after lunch, the man the figure of twenty-one years is against and the man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace, and the lane is not metalled for about sixty yards and then stops being a lane, and there is no clerk down there and nothing was entered on the way and both of them said so.

The building is one room and a stove and four shelves and the shelves hold about sixty at a time in the cold, and the man of about fifty-five said the figure and said it was not checked and was not asked to be checked, and a woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was at the stove with a kettle and was not asked for anything and said she comes there to sit and that sitting is not the same as being in a shelter and that she has not put her name on the page.

The page is the fourth of four and it is on a shelf under a folded blanket, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood looking at it for a while and did not touch it and was not asked to.

There is a column on it and the column is headed with the name of a person who is in the building, and beside every one of the about forty names there is a space about the width of a finger and nothing is in any of them, and a man of fifty-six was not there and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not there and nobody said anything about the spaces for about nine seconds.

Then the man of about fifty-five said that he wrote the heading himself in the fourth year and that a page with no heading is a page nobody can be asked about, **and that the heading is the only thing on the page he is proud of and that he has never had anybody use the spaces and that a man who keeps a page for nine years and cannot say who bears it is not a man with a page, he is a man with a cupboard.**

Nobody took that up and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said that this district has spent eleven weeks building a page of six columns in a yard and has never once come down a lane and looked at a page that already existed, and that the page of six columns is a better page and that nobody in this district has ever held both of them up together.

The woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures said one thing at the stove and it is in a minute nobody took, and a clerk of nineteen years entered afterwards that the figure is twenty-eight words and that it was said in a lane and not in a yard and that the two are two things.

"**A page is a thing that waits for somebody to be brave on it, and about nine years is not a long wait for a thing like that.**"

Nobody asked her about the nine and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

**AND ON THE FOURTH OF JANUARY THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST STOOD IN A LANE OFF THE LOWER TERRACE AND LOOKED AT A PAGE HE HAD NEVER SEEN AND DID NOT TOUCH IT, AND BESIDE ABOUT FORTY NAMES ON THAT PAGE THERE ARE ABOUT FORTY SPACES THE WIDTH OF A FINGER AND NOTHING IS IN ANY OF THEM, AND THE COLUMN HAS BEEN HEADED NINE YEARS BY A MAN WHO WROTE THE HEADING HIMSELF BECAUSE A PAGE WITH NO HEADING IS A PAGE NOBODY CAN BE ASKED ABOUT.**

---

The rest of the fourth of January was the copy, and a man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing said he would keep it and would not lose it and would not be the man whose copy is the record, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the copy is on one page and is not in a book and that the count of documents nobody owns is three and did not move, **and that a copy a man of about twenty-three keeps is a copy and not a fourth, and that the copy is the only thing in this district that knows on what day a family's claim passes to a house and it is in a coat.**

A man of fifty-six said that a man who says he will keep a thing and will not be the record of it is a man who has said the two sentences this district spent eleven weeks arriving at and that he said them in about nine seconds, and that the district is not going to make him a clerk of anything, **and that a district which needs a man of about twenty-three to hold its one copy of the only page that knows a date is a district that has arranged itself, and that nobody arranged it on purpose and the two are not joined.**

The train on the siding had stood three hundred and twenty-seven days on the fourth of January, and a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and beside the index there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date, and the count of refusals to read is nine and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is forty-one and is a count of days and not a column, **and that the days are not added to the askings and the askings are not added to the days, and that the nearest of the two dates the district is carrying is the thirty-first of March and the distance to it from the fourth of January is eighty-six days, and that twenty-seven days remain in the first month, then the whole of the second month, then the whole of the third month to its last day.**

Nobody argued with the figure and a man of fifty-six said that the count of things this district does not have is five and did not move, and that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, and that the two of those are the whole of the morning, and that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage is not written down and was not written down this morning either.

Then the woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide went back down the bank with the sheet in her pocket for the second time, and nobody asked her what she intended to do and nobody suggested it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody did and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **AND THE TWENTY-NINTH OF DECEMBER CAME AND WENT AND THE CLAIM DID NOT PASS AND THE ONLY REASON IT DID NOT PASS IS THAT NOBODY DID ANYTHING, AND THE SHEET IS IN A POCKET TWO DAYS' WALK AWAY AND A NAME IS IN ONE PLACE AND THE PLACE IS A COLUMN IN A BOOK NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAS READ.**
