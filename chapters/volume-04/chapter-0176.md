# Chapter 176: A Boy in a Column

---

The sixteenth of the second month was the day a boy of nine came into the yard of Lot Seventeen with the woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide, and nobody sent for him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him and that she was not asked to write his name and did not and that the minute says a boy of nine and does not say his name.

He had a wet cap in his hand and he was holding it the way a person holds a thing he has decided not to put down, and he looked at the trestle table for a while and then at the rain and then at the table again, and nobody in that yard said anything to him at all for about a quarter of an hour.

A man of fifty-six said afterwards in the yard that a clerk of nineteen years put the time at eleven minutes, and that the putting of it was not asked for either, and that a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road put the time at eleven as well and was not asked why.

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a boy of nine in a yard is not a finding and is not a column and is not one of the five protected things, and that the protected things are a passage twice, a loss once, a failure of a remedy once and a refusal to be asked again once, **and that a child who is brought into a yard by his mother and stands in it is none of the five and is not going to be entered as one.**

The woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said that he is at the yard because she is at the yard, and that she did not bring him and did not leave him, and that he came along the way he has come along for nine years.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked why she did not leave him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a person who has been asked nothing in eleven weeks running and has a count of it that is not a column has been asked nothing about this either, and that the two are not added.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the boy one question in the six things at about half past ten in the morning, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked to write the answer in a way that put the boy in a column and was not asked at all.

"**What did you think was going to happen.**"

The boy said it in the ordinary voice of a child of nine in a wet yard and it is in the minute in his own words and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-one and said it out loud.

"**I thought there would be a queue. There is no queue. The man at the counter wrote it and mum was crying at the cart and I have not been back.**"

The yard went quiet for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that a child who expected a queue and found a counter has described the whole of this district's business in one sentence, **and that a queue is a thing a person can see and a counter is a thing a person cannot, and that a district that has spent a month insisting on things people can see has now been told what a counter looks like from four feet away.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the boy was asked once and answered once and was not asked a second thing, and that the record of the asking says asked once, **and that a person who has been asked once has not been asked twice, and that the second of the six things was used on a child of nine in a wet yard and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put it there and was not asked about it.**

**AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A BOY OF NINE STOOD IN A YARD FOR ELEVEN MINUTES AND WAS ASKED ONE QUESTION AND ANSWERED IT, AND HIS NAME IS IN ONE PLACE AND THE ONE PLACE IS A COLUMN IN A BUILDING TWO DAYS' WALK AWAY, AND IT IS NOT IN THE MINUTE.**

---

Then the fence posts, and a boy of nine asked a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road how many there are along the north wall, and the man of about nineteen said he did not know because nobody had ever asked him.

They counted them twice and got two numbers, and the boy got thirty-one and the man of about nineteen got thirty, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both numbers and entered that the difference is one and that one is not a rate.

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that two people counting one thing and getting two numbers is the oldest thing in this district and that this district has spent four months not joining things and has just found the smallest possible instance of it, **and that a boy of nine and a man of about nineteen counted a fence and got two answers and neither of the two is a finding and the difference is not a column.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the man of about nineteen is nobody's keeper and is not her keeper and has never been anybody's keeper, and that a man who counts a fence for a boy of nine is not a keeper of a fence, **and that a person who stands somewhere every day and writes nothing down is described by people who have never met and that this district has joined none of them and is not going to join a boy of nine to a fence.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy of nine is not described in any column in this district's books and that a fence is not a lot and carries no toll, **and that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and the count of protected things is five and neither moved on the sixteenth of the second month, and that a boy counting a fence is not one of the four and is not one of the five.**

So two people counted a fence along the north wall and got thirty-one and thirty, and the difference is one, **and one is not a rate and neither number is a finding, and a boy of nine is not in any column in any book in this district.**

---

Then the sheet, and it was not reprinted, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the second of the four printed things next to the third of the five terms of a house thirty-four miles east in different words, and did not reprint either of them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two have been next to each other in a yard before and that a clerk of nineteen years has said the two are two houses and are not joined and that saying it again is not a third time and is not a column.

The second of the four printed things says that the named person shall not be below fifteen, and the boy is nine, and the third of the five terms of a house thirty-four miles east says that the named person will not be asked whether they will stand.

Nobody argued. A man of fifty-six said that this district has had both of those sentences in front of it for a month and has spent the month on the fourth of the four printed things and on the fourth of the five terms, and that the two sentences about a child are the two nobody has read out loud, **and that a sheet which says a named person shall not be below fifteen and carries the name of a person of nine has been broken by the hand that wrote the name and not by the person it names.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man at a counter who fills a column in about eleven seconds without asking a man of forty-five anything has this district's own sentence on the face of the paper and is working from both ends of it at once, **and that a rule which looks like protection and can be turned into a way of not asking anybody is doing the same work from both sides, and that this district wrote that down on the seventeenth of January and has not done anything with it since.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two facts are that the sheet says the named person shall not be below fifteen and that the name in the column is the name of a person of nine, and that the two are in one line and are joined, and that the finding of the sixteenth of the second month is about a house and is not about a boy.

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a finding about a house which is in a building two days' walk away and is not in a book this district has read cannot be shown to the house, **and that the district has proved the breach to itself on a wet afternoon and that proving a breach to yourself is the only kind anybody in this district has yet been able to do.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a person can be shown a document by a stranger and can be shown a finding about their own paper by nobody, and that he has been in the second of those trades for thirty years and it is the worse one.

**AND A SHEET WHICH SAYS A NAMED PERSON SHALL NOT BE BELOW FIFTEEN CARRIES THE NAME OF A PERSON OF NINE, AND THE HOUSE THAT WROTE THE NAME BROKE ITS OWN TERM, AND THE PROOF OF THAT IS IN A YARD FOUR MILES FROM A LOWER TERRACE AND IS IN NO DOCUMENT ANYBODY CAN CARRY.**

---

Before the yard behind the Road House was read out, a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace whether the boy's name could go in the fourth page, and the man of about fifty-five said no in four seconds, and nobody had sent for him and he had come up on his own account.

A man of fifty-six asked him why not, and he gave the reason in a sentence that is in the minute in his own words and it is twenty-seven words, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-seven.

"**That page is for people who are in my building on a night. He is not in my building on a night. He is in a yard.**"

Nobody took that up. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the page records a person in a building on the day they arrive, and that a boy standing in a yard is not a person in a building, **and that a man who has been writing days in his own book since the fourth of the second month and has been asked once to put a name in it has said no, and that the no is entered and is not a refusal of a rule and is not a refusal of the boy.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man keeping a page for nine years with a column and about forty names and a space the width of a finger beside every one of them has just declined the only thing anybody in this district has offered him, **and that the offer was a name, and that the district keeps offering people names as though names were the thing they have been short of.**

A man of fifty-six said that a sheet a stranger can look at is worth something and that a name a person has not asked to have written anywhere is worth more, **and that those two sentences have been in this yard since the eighth of March and have never once been said in the same breath, and that the reason is that one of them was said by this district and the other one was said by a man of about fifty-five in a lane and neither of them has been entered together.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two sentences are not joined, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her to say whether they are the same and she said no in four seconds, **and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve and neither moved on the sixteenth of the second month.**

**AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE WHO KEEPS A FLOOD SHELTER SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS TO A BOY'S NAME GOING IN THE FOURTH PAGE, AND GAVE AS HIS REASON THAT THE PAGE IS FOR PEOPLE WHO ARE IN HIS BUILDING ON A NIGHT.**

---

The yard behind the Road House on the tide was read out at about half past one in the afternoon because a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked for it, and a clerk of nineteen years read the entry out and it is an entry in a book in this district and it says that the yard is not a security, and that the request was made by the keeper herself in December, and that it was unpaid and unasked.

Nobody argued. A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a yard stood in by a carter once a month for nine years without paying and then entered by the person who keeps it as not a security is the only entry in this district's business that a person has talked herself out of, **and that a person who has talked herself out of a claim has done the thing a records office exists to stop and has done it to herself and is not to be thanked for it.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a yard which is entered as not a security is not a lot and is not a place anybody can be found at, and that a person who has entered her own yard as not a security cannot now claim anything on it, **and that the two of those are the price and that the price was paid by the one person in the transaction who was not asked and did not ask.**

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said that the carter will stand in her yard on the second of every month whether the sheet is in her pocket or not, and that a person who prefers debt to hunger has two things in a book this month and neither of them is a claim, **and that the eleven weeks a clerk of nineteen years has been asked nothing in and the nine years a woman has been paying a carter nothing in are two lengths of the same road and are not joined.**

A man of fifty-six said that a person who prefers debt to immediate hunger is not a fool and is not a victim and is a person making a decision with the two things they have, **and that this district has spent four months trying to make that person sign something and has not once asked what the person would have instead.**

Then the thing nobody had offered, and it came out of the yard in about nine seconds, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said it to the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and not to the yard.

"**The house's own counter says a man may take his name off a sheet. That is on the counter in front of a nail. Nobody has ever told this boy that.**"

The yard did not argue with that. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a right a person does not know they have is not a right, and that this district has spent eleven weeks learning the shape of a sheet and has not once handed the shape to the person standing in front of it.

A man of fifty-six said that a boy of nine cannot be given a form and cannot be asked to sign one and cannot be walked thirty-four miles to a counter to be shown a line, and that the only person in this district who could put a sheet in front of that boy is the woman he came with, and that she has not done it and was not asked why.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked why the woman has not done it and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a mother who told a child of nine to put his name in a column is not a woman who has read the second of the four printed things, and that the district has never established that she did not read it and is not going to.**

Nobody took that up. The woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said one thing about it in the yard and it is in the minute in her own words and it is forty words, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty.

"**He asked me if the water was for us and I said yes, and I did not read it, and if you want it read I will read it and you will have to tell me what to do afterwards.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked a second question about the twenty-ninth of December, and that the not-asking is entered, and that a woman who will read a sheet if somebody tells her what to do afterwards is a woman who has been managing a document alone for nine weeks.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, what the man the figure of twenty-one years is against is going to do now, and he said that he is not going to do anything, and gave the reason himself in a sentence that is in the minute in his own words.

"**The name is in a column in a building and the house that wrote it is the only party that can take it out and the house does not ask anybody anything, and that is the whole of what a Binder can do with a piece of paper and it is nothing.**"

The room let that stand. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who can post a security and cannot get a child's name out of a column is a man whose instrument is a document, and that this district has known that since the eighth of March, **and that a document is a thing a person can do to a document and not to a person, and that the two are not the same and a boy of nine is a person.**

**AND A HOUSE'S OWN COUNTER SAYS A MAN MAY TAKE HIS NAME OFF A SHEET, AND NOBODY HAS EVER TOLD A BOY OF NINE THAT, AND A RIGHT A PERSON DOES NOT KNOW THEY HAVE IS NOT A RIGHT.**

---

The man of about twenty-three who keeps the copy was not in the yard and nobody sent for him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not in it and that she was not asked about him.

The day was named at about half past two in the afternoon by the woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide, and she said the twenty-ninth of December had been forty-nine days, and a man of fifty-six said that a figure with a day on it is an interval and that the two of them are not the same thing.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the day is the twenty-ninth of December and that it is forty-nine days before the sixteenth of the second month, and that the claim did not pass, and that the only reason it did not pass is that nobody did anything, **and that a sheet in a pocket is not a book and a man of about twenty-three who keeps the copy is not a record, and that the count of documents nobody owns is three and did not move on the sixteenth of the second month.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the day was on the copy in a corner in a hand that is not the mother's and not the counter's and not the house's, and that the hand has still not been looked at by anybody in this district, **and that a hand in a corner on a copy kept by a man who is not paid and does not own it is the only evidence in this district's business that anybody exists, and that it is a hand and not a person.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a hand is not a person and may not be entered as one, and that the district wrote that down in the ninth week of January about a mark on a page in a book and has not stopped writing it down, **and that the count of things this district does not have is five and did not move, and that a hand in a corner is not one of the five and is not a sixth.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, what happens on the second of the next month at the counter, and the woman of about forty-four said that she is going and that the carter will stand in her yard whether the sheet is in her pocket or not.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the second of the next month is fourteen days off, and that nobody in this district has an instrument for the second of the next month and does not have one for a day either, **and that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is eighty-four and is a count of days and not a column, and that the thirty-first of March is forty-three days off and the first of April is forty-four.**

**AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A BOY OF NINE STOOD IN A YARD AND WAS NOT ASKED A SECOND THING, AND HIS MOTHER SAID SHE WOULD READ THE SHEET IF SOMEBODY TOLD HER WHAT TO DO AFTERWARDS, AND THE TWENTY-NINTH OF DECEMBER WAS FORTY-NINE DAYS AGO.**
