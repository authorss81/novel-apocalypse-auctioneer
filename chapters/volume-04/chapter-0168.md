# Chapter 168: A Sluice Is Not a Line on a Sheet

---

The tank at the back of the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace was full on the morning of the second of February and had been full since the first, and the fourth holding of the nine on a sheet nailed in a shed eleven miles up a road did not take a drop, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both of those facts in one line and did not join them.

The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter said the second half of the question before anybody asked him for it, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-five words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got thirty-five and said it out loud.

"**If the fourth did not take, then something filled this tank, and I have been pouring a bucket into it for nine years and nobody has ever asked me what I was pouring it into.**"

Nobody argued. A man of fifty-six said that the district's nearest date was a week and not a day until the morning of the first of the second month, and that the week has now been a day for a day, **and that a district which has been counting in weeks has been spared the question of where the water came from for eight days, and that the question is here now and nobody in this yard has an instrument for it.**

**AND ON THE SECOND OF THE SECOND MONTH THE TANK AT THE BACK OF THE FLOOD SHELTER AT THE SLUICE END OF A LANE OFF THE LOWER TERRACE WAS FULL, AND THE FOURTH HOLDING OF THE NINE ON A SHEET NAILED IN A SHED ELEVEN MILES UP A ROAD DID NOT TAKE A DROP, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED BOTH FACTS IN ONE LINE AND DID NOT JOIN THEM.**

---

They went out to the farm on the second of February, four miles past the loams where the lane stops being metalled, and there was no clerk on the road and there was no minute, and what came back was a page in a man's own hand which a clerk of nineteen years read out in the yard three days later because a page is read out.

The sluice is at the low end of the second field and it is open and it has been open since before dawn, and there is water standing in the head race above it going down about a foot in a minute, and nobody was at the farm.

The three households that work the farm were at the top of the lane in the yard of the house and were not asked anything, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man the figure of twenty-one years is against whether anybody had been told, and he said that he had not and that a clerk of a house had signed a line in a book eleven miles up a road, **and that a line in a book is a thing a person can look at and a sluice is a thing a person can stand in front of, and that the two are the same water and are not the same instrument.**

**AND ON THE SECOND OF THE SECOND MONTH THE SLUICE AT THE LOW END OF THE SECOND FIELD FOUR MILES PAST THE LOAMS WAS OPEN AND HAD BEEN SINCE BEFORE DAWN, AND NOBODY HAD BEEN AT THE FARM, AND THREE HOUSEHOLDS WORK IT AND NONE OF THEM OWNS IT, AND THERE WAS NO CLERK ON THE ROAD AND NO MINUTE.**

---

A woman of about fifty-two who has the seventh holding at the top of a lane off the Verge was standing at the sluice with a boot full of mud and she had not been asked to be there and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

She said one thing and it is in the minute in her own words and it is thirty-three words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got thirty-three and said it out loud.

"**The water came past my gate at about half past four yesterday and did not stop for me and went on down to this and I have not had to open anything since.**"

Nobody took it up. A man of about fifty-six said that a delivery in the first week of a named month and not on a named day is a sentence on a nail in a shed and that a sluice is not on the sheet and was never on the sheet, **and that a house writes nine holdings and a figure against each of them and a tenth line that is its own, and that a sluice is an eleventh thing on the same piece of water and nobody has ever written it down.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said that he has said since the twenty-ninth of December that a sluice is the only piece of the water in this district that can be moved a mile, **and that a thing which can be moved a mile does not need anybody's column to be moved, and that a person who opens a gate is a person who has been asked, and a sluice that is open is a sluice that has not been asked about.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a sluice bought with a farm in it is a seventh instrument and was refused entry in this district in the month before last, **and that a sluice standing open in a field with nobody on the farm is not an instrument and is a sluice, and that the count of six did not move on the second of the second month and that the two are not joined.**

**AND ON THE SECOND OF THE SECOND MONTH A SLUICE FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS WAS OPEN AND HAD BEEN SINCE BEFORE DAWN, AND A SLUICE IS NOT ONE OF THE NINE HOLDINGS AND IS NOT THE TENTH LINE AND IS NOT ANY LINE ON A SHEET NAILED IN A SHED, AND THE COUNT OF INSTRUMENTS THIS DISTRICT HAS BUILT AND NOT NAMED IS SIX AND DID NOT MOVE.**

---

On the third of February a clerk of the Underwriters of the Upland came up the road to the farm and stood at the sluice for about half an hour and wrote nothing down, and Tarin Kest saw him and said so in the yard on the fourth and a clerk of nineteen years entered it and entered that nobody sent for Tarin Kest and that he was not asked to be there and that he was there.

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a clerk of a house has stood at a sluice and written nothing, **and that writing nothing at a sluice is a thing a house does and is not a thing this district does, and that the count of instruments built and not named is six and a clerk who writes nothing is not a seventh.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who goes to look at a thing and writes nothing down has admitted in his own hand that the thing is not his to write about, **and that a house has five sentences on a counter and will show a receipt to a stranger and has now sent a man to a sluice to write nothing, and that the three of those are the same house and the district is not entitled to any of them.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a clerk who looks at a thing and writes nothing is worth more to a house than a clerk who writes something, **and that a man who sells paper for thirty years has been on the other end of that for thirty years and has never once been able to make anybody pay for the looking.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that none of that is a finding, and that the three of them are three men saying a thing and that the three are not joined, **and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve and that neither moved on the third or the fourth of February.**

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against tried to do something on the fourth of February and could not, and the trying took about nine minutes and there were about nine people in the yard and a clerk of nineteen years entered all of it.

He said that he could go to the counting house and ask what a sluice is on a house's sheet, and a man of fifty-six said that a house is not a person and cannot be asked, and that a house's clerk is a person and a clerk of a house is not a party to a sluice.

He said that he could write the fourth holding down in a public book, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a rota is a list of places and that a person cannot be a place, and that writing a place where a person should be is what this district has spent four months refusing to do, **and that the fourth column on that sheet is signed by a clerk of the house and that the clerk is not asked and that the clerk has a name and the column does not want it.**

He said that he could go to the farm and ask the three households, and a man of about fifty-six said that the three households work it and none of them owns it and that asking a household whether it wants a sluice opened is asking a household to be an owner and that none of them has agreed to be one and has not been asked.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said that a sluice that has been open since before dawn on the second of February has been open about four days by the fifth, and that a sluice does not have a term, and that the only terms anybody in this district has for water are the term on a charter that ends on the thirty-first of March and the first week of a month on a nail in a shed.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the first week of the second month is a week and not a day and that a sluice is not a week and is not a day, **and that a thing with no term is not a lot and is not an instrument and is not one of the five things this district does not have, and that the count of five did not move on the sixth of the second month.**

Nobody took that up and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the fourth thing himself and it is in the minute in his own words and it is forty-one words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got forty-one and said it out loud.

"**I can put a page in a book saying a sluice is open, and a page in a book saying a sluice is open does not shut a sluice, and a Binder is a thing you can do to a document.**"

The room let that stand. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a power is a thing you can do to a document and not to a person, **and that the man who can be found said the sentence about his own instrument in a yard in about nine seconds and that the yard did not argue with it and that a man of fifty-six entered it as not speaking for the room.**

**AND ON THE FOURTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST SPENT ABOUT NINE MINUTES TRYING TO DO SOMETHING ABOUT AN OPEN SLUICE FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS AND COULD NOT, AND SAID IN TWENTY-NINE WORDS THAT HE CAN PUT A PAGE IN A BOOK SAYING A SLUICE IS OPEN AND THAT A BINDER IS A THING YOU CAN DO TO A DOCUMENT.**

---

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper asked the man of about fifty-five what the page is going to be when the days start going in, and the man of about fifty-five said that it is going to be a page with a day against a name and nothing else, and the man of about thirty-eight said that a page with a day and a name and nothing else is a bill, **and that a bill is the one thing in this basin that everybody can read and that this district has never once written one about a person who is not being paid.**

It was not argued with and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a bill is not a security and is not a lot and is not one of the six instruments built and not named, **and that a page with a day against a name in a building nobody in this district owns is a fourth thing this district can look at and cannot carry, and that the two of those are the same problem as a receipt thirty-four miles east.**

They went down the bank again in the afternoon and the lane is not metalled for about sixty yards and then stops being a lane, and the man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter had the page out of the drawer and on the table with a stone on it so that the door would not blow it, and a clerk of nineteen years was not there and the page is the record of the second of February.

Nobody said anything for about a minute. A man of fifty-six said later in the yard that a man and a page and a stone on a page in a room off a lane is the thinnest record this district has produced and that it is thinner than a page in a man's own hand, **and that the difference is that the page in a man's hand can be argued with and a page in a building can only be read.**

The woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures said one thing in the lane about the day and it is in a minute nobody took, and a clerk of nineteen years entered afterwards that it is nineteen words and that it was said in a lane and not in a yard and that the two are two things.

"**A day is a thing you carry. You do not put a day down anywhere and then own it.**"

Nobody asked her about the twenty-four and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was. A man of fifty-six said that a woman who carries water to about ninety houses has carried more days than anybody in this district and that she is the only one of them who is paid nothing for it, **and that the two facts are a fact and a wage and the wage is nothing and the two are not added to each other.**

Then the man of about fifty-five said the other half and it is in the minute in his own words and it is forty words, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it afterwards and did not count it and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty.

"**Forty names and a column with a man's name at the top of it and forty spaces beside the names, and I have never once written a day in one of them, and a house is waiting on a day.**"

The rule arrived in the lane at about half past three in the afternoon, and nobody standing in that lane had asked for it, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not ask for it and is the only person in that lane who could have got one by asking.

> **A CLAIM THAT TURNS ON A DAY IS A CLAIM ON A RECORD AND NOT ON A PERSON.**
>
> **A RECORD THAT STARTS TODAY HAS NO DAYS BEFORE TODAY. THOSE DAYS ARE NOT ZERO. THEY ARE ABSENT, AND A THING THAT IS ABSENT CANNOT BE GIVEN TO ANYBODY.**
>
> **A DAY IN A CORNER IN A HAND THAT IS NOT THE HOUSE'S IS NOT A TERM, AND NOBODY MAY BE ASKED TO PAY ON A DAY THAT NO DOCUMENT NAMES.**
>
> **A HOUSE THAT HAS NOT NAMED THE DAY HAS LEFT THE NAMING TO A PERSON WHO CANNOT READ, AND THAT IS THE WHOLE OF WHAT IT DID.**
>
> **A DISTRICT MAY SAY OUT LOUD THAT IT DOES NOT KNOW WHICH DAY IT IS, AND MAY NOT SAY IT FOR ANYBODY ELSE, AND THE SAYING DOES NOT MOVE THE DAY.**

A man of fifty-six said that the second of the five is the one this district has been living inside since the fourth of January without knowing it, **and that a page of six columns and eleven rows is a record that started on a day and that this yard has been reading it as though the days before the first row were zero, and that the first row is the ninth year and that the ninth year is not zero and is not absent, it is nine years of a man's memory standing in a channel.**

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in the yard and was not asked for anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the district arrived at this page from a market, and that the market's own book has a page with four entries on it and about two inches at the foot that are empty and were not ruled, **and that the shelter's page has about forty empty spaces that were ruled, and that the two are the same shape turned round, and that a clerk of nineteen years was asked to say whether that is a pattern and said no in four seconds and gave a reason that is twenty words long.**

The reason is in the minute in her own words and it is twenty words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty and said it out loud.

"**One is a space nobody expected to fill. One is a space somebody did. Those are not the same space.**"

Nobody took that up and a man of fifty-six said that a space somebody ruled is a promise and a space nobody ruled is a gap, and that this district spent the twenty-fifth of December finding that a printed gap is the cheapest thing a house can build, **and that about forty ruled spaces in a page in a building are forty promises nobody kept, and that this is not a finding about the man who ruled them and is not entered as one.**

The lane did not argue with it. A man of fifty-six said that the fourth of the five is the one that costs, and that the fifth is the one the man in the lane has to act on, **and that the man in the lane is not going to be told what to write and has not been asked, and that a rule which ends with what a district may not say for anybody else is a rule and not a choice and has not been one in this district before.**

The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter said that he is going to write the day beside a name from the day after tomorrow, and only for people who come in, and only the day, and he said it before anybody asked him and gave no reason, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said the second half before he was asked and that she is not going to put a number on it.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, what the day goes in, and the man of about fifty-five said that the day goes in the space the width of a finger, and a man of fifty-six read the heading of the page out loud in the lane, and the heading is three sentences long and has been on that page since the fourth year, and it is in the minute of the fourth of January in this district's own hand and is not printed here a second time.

The lane did not argue with it. A man of fifty-six said that the second of the three sentences is the one a building runs on, and that a page which says a person is entered on the day they arrive has been saying for nine years that it wants the day, **and that the space the width of a finger has been in that page since the fourth year and that about forty of them have never had anything in them, and that nobody has ever asked a man what a space is for.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said that a space the width of a finger is a space for a day, and that a mark would not fit in it, **and that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten has just said the second half of a thing and has said it about nine times in eleven weeks and is not a column.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the day starts on the fourth of the second month and goes in the space, and that the about forty names already on the page have no day against them and will not be given one, **and that a day which is absent is not a day of zero and cannot be entered as one, and that the two are not the same and the rule that arrived in that lane an hour ago says so.**

**AND THE RULE ARRIVED IN A LANE OFF THE LOWER TERRACE AT ABOUT HALF PAST THREE IN THE AFTERNOON ON THE SECOND OF THE SECOND MONTH AND NOBODY ASKED FOR IT, AND ITS LAST LINE IS THAT A DISTRICT MAY SAY OUT LOUD THAT IT DOES NOT KNOW WHICH DAY IT IS AND MAY NOT SAY IT FOR ANYBODY ELSE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE SAID HE IS GOING TO WRITE THE DAY BESIDE A NAME FROM THE DAY AFTER TOMORROW AND ONLY FOR PEOPLE WHO COME IN.**
