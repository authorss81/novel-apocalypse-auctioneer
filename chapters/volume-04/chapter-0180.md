# Chapter 180: Three Hundred and Seventy-Four Days

---

The twentieth of the second month was the last day a clerk of nineteen years entered anything in this district for ten days, and the train on the siding had stood three hundred and seventy-four days, and a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and a man of fifty-six said the tail of the arithmetic out loud in the yard at about eleven in the morning and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down.

"**Off the eleventh of February, and the twentieth of the second month is nine days on from it, and three hundred and sixty-five and nine is three hundred and seventy-four, and the first of the next month is nine days off this one.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the district has now said a pair of figures eight times in ten days and that eight is a count and not a column, **and that a figure derived out loud in a yard is worth more than a figure carried in a book, and that this district has had that sentence in three places in ten days and has used it once.**

Nobody took that up and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the eleventh of the second month was three hundred and sixty-five days after the train came and that it was also a day a house thirty-four miles east has not named, **and that a district which derived one of the two out loud and did not derive the other has done the only thing it can honestly do with a date that stands for two things.**

**AND ON THE TWENTIETH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOUR DAYS, AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS THIRTY-NINE DAYS AWAY AND THE FIRST OF APRIL IS FORTY, AND THE FIRST OF THE NEXT MONTH IS NINE DAYS AWAY.**

---

The page came across on the twentieth of the second month, and it was the page from thirty-four miles east that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against had brought back on the tenth, and he asked the registrar of this district's records office for it in the six things at about half past twelve in the afternoon and she said yes in four seconds.

Nobody took that up. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two of them were in the same yard at the same hour on purpose and that she was not asked to write the arranging down as anything and that she is not going to be, **and that a page in a man's own hand is now in the keeping of a records office and that a page in a records office is a page in a book and that a page in a book is a thing a stranger can go and read, and that this district got nothing off it on the twentieth and did not expect anything off it.**

A man of fifty-six said that a man who gave away the only page he has of a thing he saw in a building thirty-four miles east has given away his own account of a day and a half, **and that a man's own hand is the only copy of a man's own hand and that the second copy is a different kind of thing and is not the same as the first.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a man's own hand is a man and that a man's own hand in a records office is a man in a building, **and that an address is not a bearer and a place is not a holder and a page is not a person, and that the three of those have been said in this yard since the fourteenth of January and are not going to be said a ninth time in ten days.**

Nobody argued. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the records office is going to do with a page in a man's own hand, and the registrar said that she is going to put it where a person comes and looks, and that she said the same thing on the twentieth of January about a different page, **and that a records office which keeps two pages in a man's own hand and one in its own is a records office and not a museum, and that two is not a column.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two pages in a man's own hand are in a records office and that a page in a man's own hand is not an instrument, **and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and did not move on the twentieth of the second month, and that a page is a page and the third of the three counts of documents nobody owns is three and did not move either.**

**AND THE PAGE FROM THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST WENT INTO THE KEEPING OF A RECORDS OFFICE ON THE TWENTIETH OF THE SECOND MONTH, AND A PAGE IN A MAN'S OWN HAND IS NOT AN INSTRUMENT AND THE COUNT OF SIX DID NOT MOVE.**

---

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row was in the yard from about one in the afternoon and had not been sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, whether the man the figure of twenty-one years is against has put his own name on the list of the five, and the man of about thirty-one said yes in four seconds and gave the rest of it himself.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against put his name down on the second of December as a guarantor of nothing, and as the man responsible for the aggregate unpaid toll, and a clerk wrote it and did not read it back, and a man of fifty-six said out loud in the yard on the eighteenth of December that the word steward does not go on the charter.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a man responsible for a figure of twenty-one years and a man who cannot list what he has posted is a man carrying two things with nothing written on either of them, and that a man carrying two blanks is not a man with two lots. A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a guarantor of nothing is a man who has put his name under a line to prove he can be asked about something and has not yet found out what.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a name cannot be taken off a figure by the person it is put on, and that the rule is in the market's own book, and that the man who wrote his own name under a line in the fourth week of December has not asked for it to be taken off and has not been asked to.

A man of fifty-six said that a man in a yard who carries a figure of twenty-one years and a man in a box who carries five years on a receipt thirty-four miles east are the two ends of the same problem and that the two figures are five and twenty-one and are not added, and that there is no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and there never has been. A clerk of nineteen years entered that neither moved and that she was not asked to add them and is not going to.

Nothing was said for about nine seconds. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the man of about thirty-one has now been in this yard on six of the last ten days and that a man who digs loam nine days in ten has been in it on nine of the last ten as well, and that neither of them is anybody's keeper.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two of them are two people in two trades about half a mile apart and that they are not one of the five near-identical figures at the west end of this line and are not merged with any of them, and that the two occasions are not a column and are not added to the count of not-askings about anybody in particular.

---

The board outside the room had carried the twenty-fourth of December for fifty-eight days and it had not been washed, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not ask for a fifth date and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not ask and that nobody asked her either, **and that a board which has carried a spent date for fifty-eight days and has not been given a fifth is a board and not an instrument and is not one of the five things this district does not have.**

A man of fifty-six said the standing question out loud at about half past two in the afternoon and it was not answered, and the question is the one this district has carried since the thirtieth of December, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in her own words and entered that it has now been said seven times in this district and answered none of them.

The room waited about nine seconds. The registrar of this district's records office said one thing and it is in the minute in her own words and it is thirty-three words, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-three, **and that what she said is not an answer to the question and is not entered as one.**

"**I have a book that works if a person comes to the yard. That is still the whole of it. I have not found out why and I am not going to guess.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she said the second half of that before she was asked for it and that she is not going to put a number on it, **and that the count of the second half said before being asked has not been looked at since the twenty-ninth of December, and that a woman who has said the same sentence about a book in three yards in four months has not been asked why and is not going to be.**

Nobody took that up and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a district which cannot certify a document and cannot argue with it has to be able to do both, **and that a book that works if a person comes is a book that has an answer about it, and that the answer is about thirty-four miles and the two of those are not the same figure and are not the same thing.**

A man of fifty-six said that a document a district cannot argue with is a document the district has and is not a thing the district lacks, and that the two halves of that are both true, **and that this district has only ever put one of them on a wall and has now put the other one in a minute in a woman's own words on the twentieth of the second month.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked to look at the count of entries carrying a finding since the eighteenth of December and that she said no in four seconds, **and that the figure was last entered on the thirty-first of December and has not been looked at since, and that a clerk who declines to look at a count is not protecting the count and is protecting the people the count is about.**

**AND THE REGISTER'S STANDING QUESTION WAS SAID FOR THE SEVENTH TIME IN THIS DISTRICT ON THE TWENTIETH OF THE SECOND MONTH AND WAS ANSWERED NONE OF THE SEVEN, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS DECLINED IN FOUR SECONDS TO LOOK AT A COUNT SHE HAS NOT LOOKED AT SINCE THE THIRTY-FIRST OF DECEMBER.**

---

The sheets come down at the end of a month on a cart with the seed, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that out loud in the yard on the twentieth of the second month, and a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that means the first of the next month is a day a sheet can arrive on, and that a sheet arriving on the first of a month is a sheet about a season that has already started.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheets come down at the end of a month and that the seed came on the sixteenth of the second month and that the next cart is nine days off, and that the two of those are a fact about a cart and not about a house, and that they are not joined.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a sheet about a season that has already started is a sheet a household cannot read in the state it was written in, and that the sheet this district has never seen says the Underwriters give water and seed before the season and take the labour of the family after it, and that both halves of that have to be true at once for a house that means it.

A man of fifty-six said that a house which gives before and takes after is a house whose sheet is only honest at one hour of a year, and that a household which signs at that hour is signing a thing that is true for about a day.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that one household has signed, that one has refused in four seconds, and that one has refused a second time, and that three have not been asked again, and that of the three, one is a man who lives in a cart and has a roof drawn beside his name in charcoal, and that a man a sheet cannot touch is not a protected thing and is not a defence and is a shape.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether anybody is going to tell the three, and a man of fifty-six said that the yard decided on the nineteenth not to decide it and that nine days is nine days, and that a district which goes back on its own decision before the day it made it has not made one.

---

The counts were read out at about four in the afternoon, and a man of fifty-six read them out, and they are the counts and not the voice and are not to be cut.

"**The count of things this district does not have is five. The count of instruments it has built and not named is six. The count of documents nobody owns is three. The count of protected things is five. The count of conditions with no end on it is four. The count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is eighty-eight.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a way of saying that a toll lands on a place is one of the five things this district does not have, and that a tank coming down to about four days, a head race standing with no term on it, a line a house keeps for itself under nine holdings, and a figure a route keeper is holding for four households have the shape of it, **and that four things with the shape of a thing is not four of the five and that a resemblance put against a list has not moved the list and that nobody in this district has said which of the five is still out there.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the four of those are resemblances and not findings and that she was asked to say whether the four of them are the same thing and said no in four seconds, **and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve and that neither moved on any of the eleven days this yard reaches.**

A man of fifty-six said that a clerk who is asked four times in three days and says no four times is a clerk and not a rule, and that the two of those are the only things in this district that are both, **and that the refusals to read are nine, the departure is unspent, and there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading, and none of the three has moved since the second of January.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, what this district has actually got that it did not have ten days ago, and a man of fifty-six said it in the ordinary voice and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in her own words and it is forty-five words.

"**A date we can name and cannot put anywhere, a man who has been asked what he will not do and has said it, a page with days in it, and a boy of nine who was in a yard and is in no book.**"

Nobody argued. A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a page with days in it was the best of the four and the boy is the worst of the four, **and that the two of them are a page in a building four miles down a lane and a person standing in a yard and that the distance is four miles and that four miles is a distance and not a term.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk of nineteen years has been asked nothing in this district for eleven weeks running and that she is not keeping a count of that, **and that a count of it would be a column and a column is a thing a person is paid for, and that she is not paid and was not asked to be and is not going to be.**

**AND WHAT THIS DISTRICT HAS THAT IT DID NOT HAVE ON THE TENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH IS A DATE IT CANNOT PUT ANYWHERE, A MAN WHO HAS SAID WHAT HE WILL NOT DO, A PAGE WITH DAYS IN IT, AND A BOY OF NINE WHO IS IN NO BOOK.**

---

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one more thing at about four in the afternoon and it is in the minute in his own words and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-three.

"**We have got a date, a no, a page and a boy, and none of the four can be carried to the first of April, and the first of April is the last day a person may put their hand to a notice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure of forty days and the first of April and the third form of the notice are three things in this district's business and that the third form is the only one of the three a person can act on, and that the second of its four lines is the only date in this district's business a person can do something about on the day it comes round.

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that the sentence has been said in this yard since the fourteenth of January and that a man who says it in February is saying it about forty days away, **and that a district which can count forty days and cannot count one day is a district that has been spared something it is not going to be thanked for.**

The last of the day was the yard, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked a question in the six things at about half past four in the afternoon that nobody answered, and it went into the minute with nobody's name against it, and it is in his own words and it is twenty-five words.

"**When the second month is out, is the man going to say the figure, and are we going to have written down what he said.**"

The gate stayed shut for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that the question is not going to be answered on the twentieth of the second month and is not going to be answered by anybody who is waiting for it, **and that a man who has put a month on a refusal has given this district nine days and that a district which spends the nine days preparing an answer to a question nobody has been asked is going to be ready on the first of the next month and is going to have nothing to say.**

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that the first of the next month is nine days off and that the month turns on it and that a month turning is not an event anybody in this district has an instrument for, **and that this district has had two month turns in the year and has said the name of the month out loud both times and has not once had anything to do about either of them.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the second month turned on the first of the second month and that the turn was named once and is not going to be named again, **and that the first of the next month is nine days off and that a turn of a month is not a season and is not a term and is not going into any of the three counts.**

A man of fifty-six read out at the gate that a room which meets because a person tells about nine other people where to stand is a room that has about nine people in it, and that the board outside it has four dates and no fifth and has carried the fourth for fifty-eight days, **and that the room met on the twentieth of the second month with no instrument on it for the fourth time and that about nine people came to a yard to find out what there is here to find out about, and that there is a page with days in it four miles down a lane and nobody has been in it.**

**AND ON THE TWENTIETH OF THE SECOND MONTH A QUESTION WAS ASKED IN THE SIX THINGS AND WAS NOT ANSWERED, AND IT WAS WHETHER A MAN WILL SAY A FIGURE WHEN THE MONTH IS OUT AND WHETHER THIS DISTRICT WILL HAVE WRITTEN DOWN WHAT HE SAID.**
