# Chapter 164: The Fifth of the Five Walks East

---

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row left the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten past eight in the morning on the nineteenth of January, and he walked, and a cart would have been two hours each way, and he said both of those things before anybody asked him for either.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked out to the gate with him and a clerk of nineteen years did not go out of the yard, and the man of fifty-six did not go either and stood about nine yards from the middle of the yard and let him go.

**AND ON THE NINETEENTH OF JANUARY THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY AT A FORGE END OFF SALT ROW WALKED THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST TO STAND AT A COUNTER, AND HE NAMED THE DAY HIMSELF ON THE FIFTEENTH AND NOBODY NAMED IT FOR HIM, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING WALKED OUT TO THE GATE WITH HIM AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS DID NOT.**

---

They gave him two things and a clerk of nineteen years entered both and did not join them, and she entered that neither of them was asked to give him anything and that she was not asked whether the yard should have got anything back.

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace gave him a sheet of about nine inches by six and said that it is clean paper and that a clean sheet is the only thing a man can be asked to sign and that anything else is a document.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten gave him a stub of pencil about two inches long and said that he had had it in a pocket since the ninth year and had never once written anything with it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the yard gave a man going to a counter two objects and neither of them was money, **and that a column is a thing a person is paid for and that nobody in this yard has been paid anything this month and that the two facts are a count and not a payment and are not added to each other.**

Nobody took that up and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a man who has been a thing for nine years and is handed a clean sheet and a pencil on the morning he goes to ask a house what it took off him has been given the two things a person gets when somebody thinks he is a person, **and that a yard which can do that and cannot write a list of what it has posted is the whole of this district in one gesture.**

---

The twentieth of January was the day the counting house's terms came into this district, and they came because the man the figure of twenty-one years is against carried them in his own hand on a page and could not read them out twice without a man of fifty-six reading them, and that is not a reason and he gave a different one.

The registrar of this district's records office was in the yard at about eleven in the morning and asked him for the page, and he gave it to her, and she said yes in four seconds to something she had not been asked.

"**I will put it where a person comes and looks, and it will be there on the morrow and nobody will be harmed by it.**"

Nobody answered it and a man of fifty-six said that is the third time she has said a thing out loud before she was asked for it, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it is the third time in this yard and that the count of it is not a column and carries no number on it.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said yes in four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two of them said it in the same yard at the same hour on purpose for the fifth time since the eighteenth of December, **and entered that the arranging is not a conversation about the two of them and is not to be read as one, and that she is not going to be the person who writes it down as one.**

Nobody took that up. A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide was in the yard and said that a sheet in a book in a ward market is not the same as a sheet read out at a counter every morning, and that she has refused one of those and would refuse it again, **and that what is being written down in the ward market's book is a house's terms and not anybody's name, and that those two are not the same and that she has thought about it for two days and has not told anybody she has thought about it.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked whether she had thought about it and entered that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and entered that a person who has thought about a thing for two days and has said nothing is a person and not a position, and that nobody in this yard is going to make her say which.**

**AND ON THE TWENTIETH OF JANUARY THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST GAVE THE PAGE FROM THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST TO THE REGISTRAR OF THIS DISTRICT'S RECORDS OFFICE, AND SHE SAID IN FOUR SECONDS THAT SHE WOULD PUT IT WHERE A PERSON COMES AND LOOKS, AND HE SAID YES IN FOUR SECONDS, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT THE TWO OF THEM DID IT IN THE SAME YARD AT THE SAME HOUR ON PURPOSE FOR THE FIFTH TIME SINCE THE EIGHTEENTH OF DECEMBER.**

---

The terms went into the ward market's own book at about one in the afternoon on the page after the page with four entries on it, and the page with four entries on it has about two inches at the foot of it that are empty and are not a line and were not ruled, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that they were not ruled this afternoon either.**

The five terms were read out at the counter twice by a man of fifty-six, and the reader who is about fifty read the sixth column out first as she always does, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the sixth column of a page of terms is the house's own address and that a house has an address and an address is good until a day and that this one does not say.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a page of five lines in a public book is the seventh thing this district has put in a book where a stranger can look, **and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and that a page of a house's terms is a page and not a seventh, and that a house is not a person and cannot build anything.**

The yard did not argue with that and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said the thing nobody had said yet, and she said it before she was asked, and it is in the minute in her own words and it is forty words, and the man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got forty and said it out loud.

"**It has no gap in it. There is nothing on that page a person has to go and find out, and it says out loud that it will not ask, and it is the best sheet I have ever read.**"

The room let it stand for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that the second of the four printed things on a sheet in a pocket on the tide says that the named person shall not be asked, and that the third of the five terms thirty-four miles east says the same thing in different words, and that the two sheets are in two places and belong to two houses and are not joined.

**AND THE FIVE TERMS OF A HOUSE THAT GUARANTEES THINGS WERE READ OUT AT A WARD MARKET COUNTER TWICE ON THE TWENTIETH OF JANUARY AND WERE PUT IN THE WARD MARKET'S OWN BOOK, AND THEY HAVE NO GAP IN THEM, AND THE COUNT OF INSTRUMENTS THIS DISTRICT HAS BUILT AND NOT NAMED IS SIX AND DID NOT MOVE.**

---

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked a question in the yard at about half past three and nobody answered it and it went into the minute with nobody's name against it, and it is in his own words and it is thirty-six words.

"**If a house writes all of it on the face and a person signs at a counter, what is left for a person to do that the house has not already agreed to on the sheet?**"

A man of fifty-six said that the answer is on the face of the sheet too, in the second of the five terms, and that a person may take their name off, and that taking your name off a piece of paper is the one thing a person can do in a room and that it costs nothing and gets nothing.

Nobody argued. A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a person who takes their name off a sheet and keeps the sheet has bought the sheet, and that about nine sheets in this basin have been taken off in a room rather than returned, **and that a house that lets a person take the sheet away has given away the only proof it had, and that a house that does not let a person take the sheet away is a house that has kept the proof, and that the two are a door and both of them are printed on the same piece of paper.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that neither of those two things is a finding and that the yard has now had a day with no finding in it, and that the count of entries with a finding in them since the eighteenth of December was last entered on the thirty-first of December and has not been looked at since, and that she is not going to look at it now.

**AND ON THE TWENTIETH OF JANUARY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WHO DEALS IN SECOND-HAND PAPER SAID THAT A HOUSE THAT LETS A PERSON TAKE THE SHEET AWAY HAS GIVEN AWAY ITS ONLY PROOF AND A HOUSE THAT DOES NOT IS A HOUSE THAT HAS KEPT IT, AND THE TWO ARE A DOOR, AND BOTH OF THEM ARE PRINTED ON THE SAME PIECE OF PAPER.**

---

The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace was in the yard at about half past four on the twenty-first of January and he had walked down the bank for a page of another district's terms and had not asked for it.

He read the five terms standing up, twice, and a man of fifty-six read them with him the second time, and then he said one thing about them and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-eight words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got thirty-eight and did not count them again.

"**They have put a person in a column and said they will not ask. I have got about forty names on a page and nothing beside any of them and nobody has ever asked me for a column.**"

The room took no notice of it. A man of fifty-six said that the two buildings are nine hundred yards apart and that one of them keeps a column and has never been asked what it is for and the other one keeps a column and will not ask, **and that a person in a column in the second building can take their name off, and that a person on the first building's page cannot be taken off because there is nothing to take off.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about fifty-five was entered before he spoke as not speaking for the room and that this is a count of not-speaking and not of not-askings, **and that he has not been asked about his book in nine years and that the nine years was said by him and is not a column and that nobody is going to ask him this afternoon.**

---

The slate went past the kitchen in Alder Reach for the ninth time on the evening of the twentieth of January and the registrar of this district's records office stopped in the lane and read the four sentences in charcoal out loud, and she did not write on it, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not ask her what they say.

A man of about nineteen who walked in from the road was about nine paces behind them and did not ask either, and entered afterwards that the two of them stopped, and that nine is a count and a count is not a finding, **and that whether he heard the four sentences is not entered, and that the four sentences have been under a chalk line since the fourth of April and the chalk line is longer than either of them has been in this district's books.**

Nobody asked about the sentences. A stranger at the ward market counter asked the reader who is about fifty, at about two in the afternoon, what the new page was for, and she said it was a house's terms and read them out again, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she read them a third time and got nothing wrong and that the count of readings of a document is not a column and is not a rate.

The stranger wrote the third of the five down in a hand of his own and did not write the second one down, and a man of fifty-six said that a stranger who copies the third of five and leaves the second has done the same thing this district did in the twenty-seventh of December with a corner of a page, **and that the two are not joined and that a district which notices a resemblance on a slipper is a district using the calendar again.**

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten was in the market and said that the second of the five terms is the one to write down, because it is the one that decides what happens to a person, and that he was not going to say why he knows that and nobody asked him.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of not-askings about the man of about thirty-one who digs loam is not a column and has no number on it, **because a number on it would have been a column and a column is a thing a person is paid for, and he is not paid and was not asked whether he would be.**

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide was in the market and did not go up to the counter, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she did not go up and that she was not asked why and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

---

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put it to her in the lane at about half past four and she answered it in the lane, and it is in the minute in her own words and it is thirty-seven words, and the man of about nineteen counted them and got thirty-seven and said it out loud.

She said the second half before he finished asking, which she has done about nine times in eleven weeks and which a clerk of nineteen years has entered every time and will not put a number on.

"**I have been thinking about a house with a counter and a man in a shop, and I do not think I have a different answer, and I am not going to have one in a lane.**"

That went past unargued. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked nothing and answered, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a person who says they have no different answer in a lane is not agreeing with anybody and is not refusing anybody and cannot be counted either way, and that the record on that is not asked.**

"**That sheet says a person will not be asked. This one says a person will not be asked. A house thirty-four miles away and a man behind a counter two days that way have written the same line and neither of them knows the other wrote it.**"

Nobody argued and a man of fifty-six said that a sentence can be carried from a sheet to a sheet with its shape and without its work, and that this district found that in January about a form of its own, **and that a sentence written twice by two houses and once by this district is three and is not a pattern, and that a clerk of nineteen years was asked to look at the three and said she was not going to.**

She gave the reason and it is in the minute in her own words and it is twenty-eight words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-eight and said it out loud.

"**Three sentences in three places is three sentences. If I put them in a column it is a pattern and I have not been asked for a pattern.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a column a clerk refuses to make is not one of the five things this district does not have, **and that the count of protected things is five and the count of conditions with no end on it is four, and that a refusal to make a column is neither of those and never has been.**

---

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households at the sluice end of the loams came down the bank on the twenty-first of January with a board under his arm, and the board is not one of the five boards in this district and is thirty-four miles from the nearest of them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it is a tally and not a board and that the two have never been joined.

He said that a man had been at the sluice end of the loams on the twentieth with a sheet, and that the sheet is the same shape as the one in the ward market's book and not the same size, and that six households out of the six he keeps a tally for have been asked to sign one before the season.

Nobody in the yard said anything. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked how many of the six have signed, and the man of about forty-eight said one, and that the one is a household of five and that the other five households have not signed and have not refused and have not been asked twice.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure of six households is a count and not a column and that a household is a count and a person is a count and the two are not added anywhere in this district's books, **and entered that a man who keeps a tally for six households is a man who has been keeping a list of people and was never asked what it is for and that he is not a clerk of anything and is not paid.**

The man of about forty-eight put the tally on the trestle table and there are six headings on it and under each heading there are four or five names in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, **and there is no column anywhere on it for a person who is not in a household, and the sixth household on his board is a man who lives in a cart and has no roof, and his name is written under the sixth heading with a roof drawn beside it.**

A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale at the sluice end of the loams and has stood at it nine years was in the yard and said that a roof drawn beside a name is a thing a person does with a charcoal and not a thing this district has an instrument for, **and that she has stood at that scale nine years and weighed about four hundred sacks a month and that not one of them has had a name on it and that a sack is not a household and a household is not a sack and the two are two things.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that the fifth of the six households will sign, and that the four of them that sign will be told they had no choice, and that the man in the cart will be told the same, **and that a house which fills a column in front of you and will not ask has an easier job than a house which has to persuade, and that this is not a finding and is what a man who sells paper for thirty years has noticed and not written down.**

---

A man of fifty-six said that a slate that has gone past a kitchen nine times is a public document and that a slate is not a copy and a copy is not a document, **and that the two people who stopped in that lane have stopped more times than a clerk of nineteen years is going to count, and that she has entered every one of them and that stopping is not a conversation and is not to be read as one.**

**AND THE SLATE WENT PAST THE KITCHEN IN ALDER REACH FOR THE NINTH TIME ON THE TWENTIETH OF JANUARY AND THE FOUR SENTENCES IN CHARCOAL WERE READ OUT LOUD IN A LANE AND NEITHER OF THEM WROTE ON IT, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED EITHER OF THEM WHAT THE SENTENCES SAY, AND NINE IS A COUNT AND NOT A FINDING.**
