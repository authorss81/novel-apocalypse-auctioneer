# Chapter 172: A Board Carries One Thing

---

The eleventh of the second month was the day the train on the siding had stood three hundred and sixty-five days, and a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and beside the index there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure was said by a man of seventeen and has not been checked, **and that beside the index there are six dates and a rule under the sixth and that the sixth is the fifth of December and the fifth is the fourth of January and the four before them are the seventh of September, the twenty-eighth of September and the twenty-sixth of October, and that a line of six dates is not a column and is not entered as one.**

The yard did the thing it does and stood about a dozen people in it until late, and the interval was done in the yard at about eleven in the morning because it is done every time there is one, and a man of fifty-six did it out loud and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down, and the sum was put under the figure the way it always is.

"**Take it from the eleventh of February, where the train came, and seventeen days are left in that month after the eleventh, and the third month to the eighth come to thirty-one and thirty and thirty-one and thirty and thirty-one and thirty-one, which is a hundred and eighty-four.**"

"**Seventeen and a hundred and eighty-four is two hundred and one at the thirty-first of August, and September adds thirty and October thirty-one and November thirty and December thirty-one, and that is three hundred and twenty-three at the thirty-first of December.**"

"**The first of January is three hundred and twenty-four and the first of the second month is three hundred and fifty-five, and this day is three hundred and sixty-five, and the thirty-first of March is forty-eight days off it and the first of April is forty-nine.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the district has had a seventy-six and a seventy-seven, an eighty-four and an eighty-five, a fifty-eight and a fifty-nine, a fifty and a fifty-one, and now a forty-eight and a forty-nine, **and that six pairs of figures in five weeks is six pairs and that a pair of figures is the oldest thing this district does and the pair is not a column.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said that forty-eight is the number of days a man has to put a hand to a notice and that forty-nine is the day after the last one, and that the two of them are the two halves of a thing this district has been carrying since December.

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FIVE DAYS, AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS FORTY-EIGHT DAYS FROM THIS DAY AND THE FIRST OF APRIL IS FORTY-NINE, AND NEITHER FIGURE IS A COLUMN.**

---

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row was in the yard from about half past twelve and had not been sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, whether a man behind a counter thirty-four miles east knows what day it is, and he said no in four seconds and gave a reason in a sentence that is in the minute in his own words.

"**I have stood at that counter twice and he has never once said a date, and a man who does not say a date is a man who has been told not to say one.**"

The yard let it stand. A man of fifty-six said that a man who is told not to say a date is a man inside a house that has a date it is not saying, **and that a house that does not name days and a woman at its counter who has said out loud that there is one it has not named are two facts about the same house and are not joined, and that a man of thirty-one and a man of thirty-five are not the same fact and are not a column.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five posted securities has now said the second half of a thing before anybody asked him for it and that she is not going to put a number on it, **and that the count of the second half said before being asked has not been looked at since the twenty-ninth of December and that a man who has been a security since the ninth year is not a column either.**

---

Then the board, and the board outside the room had carried the twenty-fourth of December for forty-nine days, and it was washed on the left-hand side from about two winters ago and not from the twenty-fourth of December.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked for a fifth date out loud in the ordinary voice at about half past eleven in the morning, and a clerk of nineteen years said no in four seconds, and the reason she gave was not the one she gave on the fourteenth of January, and a man of fifty-six entered that it was not the same sentence and that the two sentences are two things and are not a column.

"**A board carries one thing and this day is two things, and one of the two is a day a house has not named, and I will not put a house's silence on a wall and call it a date.**"

Nobody took that up for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that the four dates that board has carried were all set by somebody else, and that three of them were days a thing ended and the fourth was a day this room met, and that this is the first date this district has ever had for itself, **and that it is a date of arrival and not of ending, and that a board which has carried four dates somebody else set has never once carried one this district chose.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace said that a date a district chooses for itself is a date the district is expecting to arrive, and that a clerk of nineteen years said on the fourteenth of January that a date somebody is expecting to arrive is a thing she is not going to give a board, and that the two of those are the same rule pointed two ways and the rule has not moved.

The room let it stand. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked and answered and was not asked why the reason was a different reason, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a clerk is a person and not a rule and the two facts are in one line on purpose and she wanted them on two lines and did not do it, which is the third time she has wanted that and has not done it.**

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED FOR A FIFTH DATE ON THE BOARD OUT LOUD AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS, AND HER REASON WAS NOT THE ONE SHE GAVE ON THE FOURTEENTH OF JANUARY.**

---

The aggregate was asked for at about half past one in the afternoon, and it was asked in the six things, and it was asked of the man the figure of twenty-one years is against by a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and the reason it was asked on this day is that the term ends on the thirty-first of March and the number has to be in some book before then.

He said no in four seconds and then gave a reason himself, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is sixty words.

"**It is four and two and eleven and four, and the four are four different things, and a year and a half sits on a line of its own and is not added to anything, and two people in a column are not known and not asked, and one frame took nothing off anybody because there was nobody in it.**"

A man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted that and got sixty and said it out loud, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she would not count a figure of years and that the counting of a sentence and the adding of a figure are two different acts and only one of them is hers.

Nobody took it up. A man of fifty-six said that four and two and eleven and four is twenty-one, and that twenty-one is a floor and not a total, and that a figure which is a floor is a figure about what is known and not a figure about what is owed, **and that the name comes off on the thirty-first of March in the same line as the name and the entry runs to that date, and that a figure that comes off a page is not a figure anybody is paid.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a year is not an asset and is not a security and nobody can post one against it, and that a year and a half is not a figure, and that he has said the second half of that about nine times in eleven weeks and that a clerk entered it in a lane in the month before last and has not entered it since.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading, **and that a figure of years is not a price and four hundred years in a house thirty-four miles east is not a total and that the two are not added and are not to be.**

---

The rest of it was the printed sheet at the fourth place, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had it in his coat and read it out in the yard at about half past twelve, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong.

> **A TRAIN COMES ONCE A MONTH IN THE DARK AND STOPS WHERE THERE IS A PLATFORM. NOBODY IS KEPT AT THIS PLACE.**
>
> **A PERSON WHO IS HERE WHEN ONE COMES WILL BE CARRIED IF THERE IS ROOM.**
>
> **THERE IS NO CALENDAR IN THIS TERRITORY. THE INTERVAL IS A MONTH AND A MONTH IS NOT COUNTED HERE.**

Nobody argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that a sheet which says a train comes once a month in the dark has had twelve chances in a year to be wrong and has not taken one of them, and that the reason it has not been wrong is that nothing has come and nothing not coming does not prove a thing, **and that a sheet which is right for a year for no reason is a document that has become wrong for a reason nobody caused, and that this district has three of those and the count is three and this is not a fourth.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the third of the three sentences is the only printed sentence in this basin that has ever explained this district's own arithmetic to a stranger, and that a territory with no calendar has no weekday either, and that a district which has four habits about days and no week is not a district that has forgotten a week and is a district that never had one.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet is one of three documents nobody owns and that the other two are a notice about a party of nine that went thirty-four miles east and said nine, and a line in a public book with four names on it and a figure under the four names, **and that the three of them are not the same document and are not joined and are not to be, and that a sheet a man keeps in his coat for nine years is a sheet and not a record.**

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A SHEET AT THE FOURTH PLACE THAT SAYS A TRAIN COMES ONCE A MONTH IN THE DARK WAS READ OUT TWICE IN A YARD, AND IT SAYS ON ITS OWN FACE THAT THERE IS NO CALENDAR IN THIS TERRITORY, AND IT IS ONE OF THREE DOCUMENTS NOBODY OWNS AND IS NOT A FOURTH.**

---

The room met at about two in the afternoon for the fourth time with nothing on the board that said it was going to meet, and about nine people came, and the man of fifty-six said out loud that he had told two of them at the gate that there was nothing to come for and that they had come anyway.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the day in the minute, in her own hand, with the month and the number, **and entered that a date entered in a minute in a book in a yard is a date this district has, and that it is the first one it has ever had that it did not get from a house or a form or a piece of iron, and that a date in a book that works if a person comes to the yard is a date a person can only find out about by walking to a yard.**

A man of fifty-six said that a house thirty-four miles east does not come to this yard, and that a woman at a counter thirty-four miles east has said out loud that the house has not named this day and is not going to, **and that the district is therefore holding a date on one side and a refusal on the other and that the two are about the same day and are not joined, and that a line under two things is not a joining and this yard has known that since the fourteenth of January.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, what a district is supposed to do with a day it cannot put anywhere, and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said the other half of it before he had finished, and it is in the minute in her own words and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted it and got twenty-eight.

"**You have one date you cannot send anybody a letter about, and that is the first useful thing a district has not been able to do since March.**"

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a useful thing is not a good thing and that a man who finds a hole in his own roof and calls it a chimney has not solved the roof, **and that the thirty-first of March is forty-eight days off and the first of April is forty-nine and neither of them can be sent a letter about either, and that three dates this district cannot send a letter about is three and is not a column.**

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THIS DISTRICT HELD A DATE IT COULD NOT PUT ON A BOARD AND COULD NOT PUT IN A LETTER, AND THE FINDING OF THE DAY IS THAT A DATE WHICH STANDS FOR TWO THINGS IS TWO DATES, AND A LINE UNDER TWO THINGS IS NOT A JOINING.**

---

Tarin Kest came through the yard at about four in the afternoon on his own account and had not been asked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

He said that the head race is still standing and has been standing since the first of the second month, and that it is down about a foot in ten days where it was down about a foot in four hours when it filled, and that four households have water they did not have and one of them is the shelter.

The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace came up the bank at about half past two in the afternoon and had not been told, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him.

He said that there are six days in the spaces on the fourth page and that two of them are the same day, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure of six was said by him and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked, **and that a page with six days in it has been writing for a week and that a week is a week and that a day a person came in is not the day they are still in there.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that the page says a person is entered on the day they arrive and not on the day they leave, and that two names on the same day is two people and not one, **and that a man who has begun writing days in a page in a building has begun a thing that will take him four years to catch up with and there is no way to ask him whether he wants it, because asking is the other thing.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether anybody had been to look at the page this week, and the man of about fifty-five said that the woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel had been in the lane on the ninth and had not come in, and that a woman who comes down a lane and does not come in is a person who came to see whether the building was there and not to go into it.

The room did not argue with that and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the about forty names on that page have no day against any of them and will not be given one, and that a day which is absent is not a day of zero, **and that the days before the fourth of the second month are not in the page and are not in any other page and are not in any book in this district, and that the three of those are not joined.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a building that can say who was in it and on what day is a building that can be asked, and that a building that can be asked can be refused, and that nobody in this district has thought about what a building says when it is asked.

So a building four miles down a lane has been writing days for a week, and two of the six are the same day, **and a woman stood at the end of that lane on the ninth and did not go in, and a building that can be asked about a person is a building that can be told no, and this district has not asked it one thing.**

---

Nobody argued. A man of fifty-six said that a head race standing with no term on it and no bearer on it is the same water a man of fifty-six named on the ninth of February and that the two are the same water and not a new finding, **and that the finding of the eleventh of the second month is not about the water at all and is about a day, and that a district which is handed a day it cannot put anywhere has been given the first thing it has ever been given that it cannot hand on.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the sluice four miles out past the loams is still open and that the water going through it is not counted by anybody and that a sluice is not a line on a sheet and is not a lot, and that he has said the second half of that about nine times in eleven weeks and that a clerk entered last time that it is not a column and has not entered it since.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is seventy-nine and is a count of days and not a column, **and that the count of protected things is five and the count of conditions with no end on it is four, and that neither moved on the eleventh of the second month, and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve and neither moved either.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, what happens on the first of the next month, and a man of fifty-six said that the first of the next month is eighteen days off and that this yard has no instrument for it and does not have one for the eleventh either, **and that a district which is caught out twice by a calendar it does not have is a district that has been lucky once and is not going to be lucky twice.**

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE HEAD RACE WAS STILL STANDING, THE SLUICE FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS WAS STILL OPEN, AND A DISTRICT THAT HAS BEEN CAUGHT OUT TWICE BY A CALENDAR IT DOES NOT HAVE IS A DISTRICT THAT HAS BEEN LUCKY ONCE.**
