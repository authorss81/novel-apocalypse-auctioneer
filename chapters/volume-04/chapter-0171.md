# Chapter 171: What the Counter Said

---

The tenth of February was the day a page came back from thirty-four miles east, and it was on a trestle table in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and it was about eight inches by five and it was in a man's own hand, and it was read out twice because the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong both times.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the page is a man's account of a day and a half and not a minute, **and that there was no clerk on the road and nobody sent anybody, and that a page in a man's own hand is worth less than a minute and is not the same thing, and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and did not move on the tenth of the second month.**

The page had three things on it and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against read them out in the order he wrote them and did not put them in a better order, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the three in three lines and did not join them.

The first was that the man behind the counter had answered a question, and the question was what a sluice is on a house's sheet, and the answer is on the page in the man's own hand.

"**The house keeps nine holdings and a tenth line, and there is no eleventh thing on a sheet, and we do not keep a list of things that are not lines.**"

Nobody argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that the sentence is true, and that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said in the second week of January that a sluice is an eleventh thing on the same piece of water, and that the two are the same finding said by two people who have never met, **and that a resemblance between two sentences is not a join, and that a house and a loam-digger are not joined by both of them having said a true thing about the same water.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a house which will tell a stranger that it does not keep a list of things that are not lines has just told this district the shape of its own problem in thirty-one words, **and that the shape is that the whole of what there is to know is what a house has decided to list, and that a printed gap is cheaper than a hidden clause and a house that tells you about the gap has built you one.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a house that says what it does not keep is saying what it does not know, and that a document which says what it does not know is honest, and that this district entered the same finding about a sheet in the fourth week of December, **and that the two findings are about two houses and are not joined, and that the resemblance is entered and is not added to anything.**

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A MAN BEHIND A COUNTER THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST ANSWERED THE QUESTION WHAT A SLUICE IS ON A HOUSE'S SHEET BY SAYING THAT THE HOUSE KEEPS NINE HOLDINGS AND A TENTH LINE AND THAT THERE IS NO ELEVENTH THING ON A SHEET.**

---

Then the second thing on the page, and it was the reason the man had gone, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was the reason and that the first thing was not.

There was a woman at the far end of the counter and she is about thirty-five and she had a book in front of her, and the registrar of this district's records office is thirty-four and is not her, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that once and once only, the way she entered it in the second week of January.

She said one thing, and nobody asked her for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked her for it, and that the record of the asking says not asked, and that not asked is not refused and cannot be counted either way. It is in the page in the man's own words.

"**The eleventh of the second month is a day this house has not named and I am not going to name it for you.**"

The man behind the counter did not stop her and did not agree with her, and he did not look up from the book in front of him, and the page says that he did not look up and that the man who can be found wrote that down because it is a thing he had seen and not a thing he had been told.

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a house is not a person and does not speak through a clerk, and that a person at a counter who says a thing is a person and not the house, and that the two are two things and this district has no word for the second of them, **and that a sentence said by a person at a counter is not a term of the house and is not an offer and is not a refusal, and that the fifth of the five terms says the house is not a person, and that a house which has written that down has been told the same thing by its own counter and has not argued with it.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace said it in the ordinary voice, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**A sentence said in a room is a thing four people heard and a sentence said at a counter is a thing four people heard, and I have been in rooms and at counters and I have never once been able to take either of them home.**"

A man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted that and got forty-seven and said it out loud, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not going to count it a second time and that a count of a sentence is not a column.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the woman was not asked and was not asked twice and was not asked why, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a person who is in no register in the west bank has now said one thing out loud in this district's business and has not been asked a question about it, and that the two facts are two facts and neither is a column.**

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FIVE AT THE FAR END OF A COUNTER THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST SAID THAT THE ELEVENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH IS A DAY THIS HOUSE HAS NOT NAMED AND THAT SHE IS NOT GOING TO NAME IT, AND SHE WAS NOT ASKED FOR IT, AND THE RECORD SAYS NOT ASKED.**

---

The third thing on the page was not a sentence anybody had said to him and he wrote that on the page himself.

He wrote that he asked the man of about thirty at the counter whether the house had ever named the eleventh of the second month in anything, and that he did not ask it in a way that could be refused, and that he asked it in the middle of a conversation about a sluice, and that the man behind the counter answered it, and that the answer took about four seconds.

The page says the answer was that the house does not name days and that days are named by the people who owe them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a house which does not name days is a house which has arranged for somebody else to name them, and that somebody else is not the house, **and that a sheet of four printed things in a pocket on the tide has a day in a corner in a hand that is not the mother's and not the counter's and not the house's, and that the hand has not been looked at by anybody in this district, and that the two are not joined.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who cannot be refused a question is a man who has learned how to ask one, and that this district has spent eleven weeks writing down what a man cannot do and has never once written down what a man has worked out how to do.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that, and entered that she is not going to make a column of what a man has worked out how to do, and that a column is a thing a person is paid for and he is not paid, **and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against wrote three things on a page and one of them is a thing he worked out how to do and he wrote it himself and nobody asked him for it, and that a fact a person wrote about himself is not speaking for the room.**

A man of fifty-six said that the page is the second one this district has built on a road and has not named, and that the first one was the seventeenth of January and this is the ninth of February, and that two is not a rate and is not a column, **and that a page in a man's own hand is a page and not an instrument and is not going to be entered as one, and that the count of six did not move and the count of documents nobody owns is three and a page in a man's hand is not a fourth.**

And so the tenth of the second month ends with a house that does not name days, a day named by the people who owe them, **and nobody in this district who has found out who owes the eleventh of the second month, and the count of documents nobody owns is three and did not move.**

---

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row came into the yard at about half past eleven in the morning and had not been sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him.

He had heard the second thing read out twice and he said one thing about it in the six things and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted it and got twenty-nine and said it out loud.

"**A house that will not name a day has not got one, and a house that says so out loud is telling you the day belongs to somebody else.**"

The yard let it stand. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the man is the fifth of the five posted securities and has been since the ninth year, and that a man who is a security is a man who has been told a house's sentence and not his own, **and that the sentence he has just said is the first one he has said in this yard that is not about his own figure, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked to make a column of it and did not.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked about that and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, and that the fifth of the five is a person and a person is not a list and the reason the Underwriter's power is not begun in this district is entered and is not a delay.

A man of fifty-six said that a man who has been a security for nine years on one side of this basin and five years on the other is the only person in this yard who can hear a house describe a day and understand what it costs, **and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against put him in that position in the ninth year and has never once said so in a room where the man could hear it, and that this is the fourth time a clerk of nineteen years has entered the not-asking and has not been asked about any of the four.**

Nobody argued. A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a man in a box is in a box whatever a house says, and that this sentence has been said in this yard three times and that a sentence said three times is a habit and not a finding.

**AND ON THE TENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY AT A FORGE END OFF SALT ROW SAID THAT A HOUSE THAT WILL NOT NAME A DAY HAS NOT GOT ONE, AND HE IS THE FIFTH OF THE FIVE POSTED SECURITIES AND HAS BEEN SINCE THE NINTH YEAR.**

---

The page was read out a fifth time at about half past two in the afternoon because a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked for it again, and he asked for a different thing each time and the third time he asked whether the page has her name on it.

It does not. The man who can be found wrote on the page that he did not write it twice and that he underlined it once, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who writes a fact once and underlines it once is a man who is not going to go and look for the rest of it, **and that the name is in no register in the west bank and nobody in this district has been asked to find it and nobody is going to be asked, and that a name a house does not copy cannot be entered by anybody.**

Then the fifth thing he asked for was a question, and he asked the registrar of this district's records office, in the six things, and she said no in four seconds, and it went into the minute with nobody's name against it.

"**Is a clerk of a house a clerk of your office, and if he is, whose.**"

Nobody answered it and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the question was not answered, and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve, and that neither moved on the tenth of the second month, **and that a clerk of a house is a clerk of a house and the two are two things and the two of them have never once been on the same page in any book this district has read.**

A man of fifty-six said that he is not going to write in the minute that she was asked that and refused, and that a question nobody has answered is not a question that has been put into a column, and that there is no column for it and none was made. **A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a man who asks a question to find out whether two clerks are the same clerk has found the shape of about nine questions this district has been asking since the second of January, and that the shape is the shape and none of the nine is a column.**

So the fifth reading of the page gave this district a question and no answer, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went back to the forge end off Salt Row at about three in the afternoon, **and the two of them were in the same yard on the same morning and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked about it and is not going to be, and that a clerk is a person and not a rule.**

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the thing he was going to do about the woman's sentence before anybody asked him for it, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is fifty words.

"**I am not going to ask her a second thing. The fourth line of that offer has been on a table three times and nobody in this district has read it out loud, and the only way to get it read out loud is to ask a person for it.**"

The room let it stand. A man of fifty-six said that a woman who has offered a thing three times and has not had it taken is not a person to be asked a fourth time by a yard, **and that the fourth line of the offer is still not read out loud in this district and is not going to be read out loud by anybody in this yard, and that a person who says she is not going to name a day for a man is not refusing him a day and is telling him the day is not hers to give.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said the second half of that before anybody asked him for it, and that she is not going to put a number on it, and that the count of the second half said before being asked has not been looked at since the twenty-ninth of December and that she is not going to look at it now either.

Nobody took that up and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a sentence a person said at a counter can be carried home in a head and cannot be carried home in a hand, **and that a district which spent a month teaching people that a thing a stranger can look at is worth something has now met a thing it cannot look at, and that the two are not the same problem and are not the same size.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the four printed things went into the ward market's own book on the twentieth of January on the page after the page with four entries on it, and that there are two unruled inches at the foot of that page, and that they were not ruled and are not going to be, and that a clerk of nineteen years was asked to rule them and said no twice on two afternoons.

Nobody argued. A man of fifty-six said that the day after tomorrow is the eleventh of the second month and that it is three hundred and sixty-five days since the train came, and that the woman at a counter thirty-four miles east has said out loud that the house has not named it, **and that a district which has spent a month finding out that a house does not write is now in the position of a person who has been told the house is not going to say it either, and that the two are two findings and the second one is worse.**

**AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST SAID THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO ASK HER A SECOND THING, AND THE FOURTH LINE OF AN OFFER THAT HAS BEEN ON A TABLE THREE TIMES IS STILL NOT READ OUT LOUD IN THIS DISTRICT, AND THE ELEVENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH IS TOMORROW.**

---

The count was read out at about four in the afternoon, and a man of fifty-six read it out, and it is a count of days and not a column.

He said that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is seventy-eight, and that the board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for forty-eight days, **and that the train on the siding had stood three hundred and sixty-four days at ten in the morning and that a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and that beside the index there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether anybody was going to do anything about tomorrow, and a man of fifty-six said that tomorrow is the eleventh of the second month and that it is a day two things happen on, and that this yard has not decided what it is going to do about a thing and is not going to decide it tonight, **and that a district which decides a thing at nine o'clock at night about a day it has not stood on yet has decided it about a day that is not there.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody wrote on a board, and that the count of boards is five and the count of lines is six and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty, **and that the count of uses of a board has not moved since the first of the second month and stands at seven in about five weeks, and that a board which has not been written on for a month is a board and not an instrument.**

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in the yard and was not asked for anything, and she said that the nearest of the four dates on her bucket is the sixteenth of the second month and that it is six days off, and that six is a figure she can carry and not a thing she can put in a book. Nobody took that up and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

**AND ON THE TENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE COUNT OF UNENTERED DAYS SINCE THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF NOVEMBER IS SEVENTY-EIGHT, THE BOARD OUTSIDE THE ROOM HAS CARRIED ITS FOURTH DATE FOR FORTY-EIGHT DAYS, AND NOBODY WROTE ON A BOARD.**
