# Chapter 156: Publish All Security

---

The registrar of this district's records office came into the yard of Lot Seventeen on the second of January at about ten in the morning without a paper in her hand, and a man of fifty-six said afterwards that a woman who comes to a yard without paper has come to ask for something and not to give something, and that he had been in this yard eleven weeks and had never once seen her do it.

She said it in about nine seconds and it is in the minute in her own words, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it and did not time it, **and entered that she did not time it because a timing is a figure and this was not asked at a boundary, and that the six askings that carry a minute-figure are six and no seventh was asked in January.**

"**Publish everything you have ever posted as a security. All of it. In one place, where a stranger can walk up and read it.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said yes in four seconds, and that he said the second half before anybody asked him for it, **and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that this is the fifth time in eleven weeks and a fortnight that a person has said the second half before she was asked, and that the five are not joined to each other and are not a column.**

"**My public lot has no hidden terms and never has and I have said that since the eighth of March.**"

The room let it stand. A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide was in the yard and said one thing about that, and it is in the minute in her own words, and it is thirty-six words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got thirty-six and said it out loud.

"**A thing that has never had a hidden term is not the same as a thing somebody can go and look at, and I have been trying to tell you that since the twenty-fifth of December.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that the two sentences are the whole of the twenty-fourth of December and the second of January in one line, **and that a hidden term is a thing a person finds and a term on the face of a sheet is a thing a person was supposed to have known, and that the woman is talking about the second and the man is talking about the first, and that the two are two things.**

Then the man who can be found went to get it. He said he would be back in about an hour, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said an hour and was gone about forty minutes, and that he came back with a bundle of five papers and a wooden box and put the box on the trestle table and did not open it.

A man of fifty-six asked him how many he had and he said five, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure is five and that the five is a count of papers and not a sum of anything, **and that none of the five is a sum of money and that the register of this district has never had a figure of a security on it and that a count of five is not a finding.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the man came back with five papers and did not open the box, and that a man who cannot open a box in a room is a man who has not written a list, **and that a list a stranger can walk up and read is the thing that was asked for and that it is in a box and that the box is the whole of the answer to the question.**

The room let that stand for about nine seconds, and then the rule arrived in the yard at about eleven in the morning, and nobody standing in it had asked for it, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not ask for it and is the only person on that line who could have got one by asking.

> **A SECURITY IS A THING A STRANGER CAN GO AND LOOK AT. IF NOBODY CAN GO AND LOOK AT IT, IT IS NOT A SECURITY AND IT IS ALSO NOT NOTHING.**
>
> **A LIST OF SECURITIES IS ITSELF A SECURITY. A DISTRICT THAT HAS NEVER WRITTEN THE LIST HAS NEVER HAD ITS SECURITIES IN WRITING.**
>
> **A CHILD MAY BE NAMED ON A SHEET. A CHILD MAY NOT HOLD A CLAIM, AND A SHEET THAT NAMES A CHILD IS A CLAIM ON THE HOUSE AND NOT A BEARER COLUMN.**
>
> **NO PERSON MAY BE ASKED TO CONSENT IN ADVANCE OF THE SEASON THE CONSENT IS FOR. A PROMISE TO GIVE SOMEBODY SOMETHING LATER IS NOT A CONSENT, AND A PERSON WHO HAS GIVEN ONE HAS NOT ANSWED ANYTHING.**
>
> **A DISTRICT THAT POSTS A THING NOBODY CAN INSPECT IS RUNNING A LOT AND NOT HOLDING A SECURITY, AND THE ONLY REMEDY FOR A THING THAT CANNOT BE INSPECTED IS TO TAKE IT DOWN.**

The room did not argue with it. A man of fifty-six said that the first of the five is the sentence in the counter's hand and that somebody said it to a stranger yesterday and that the rule has now said it to the man who wrote it, **and that the second of the five is the one that is going to cost, and that a list is a security and the only list in this district is a page of six columns with eleven rows filled on it, and that the page is about a year and not about a security at all.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the last of the five is a choice and not a rule, and that it is the first choice the district has been handed since the right to withdraw, **and that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage in a room is not a rule and is not written down, and that a choice is not written down either and that this one has to be.**

And on the second of January, at about eleven in the morning, **A RULE ARRIVED IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING AND NOBODY ASKED FOR IT, AND ITS LAST LINE IS THAT THE ONLY REMEDY FOR A THING THAT CANNOT BE INSPECTED IS TO TAKE IT DOWN, AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST HAD SAID YES IN FOUR SECONDS TO PUBLISHING EVERYTHING HE HAD POSTED AND HAD COME BACK WITH FIVE PAPERS IN A BOX HE DID NOT OPEN.**

---

The box was opened at about half past eleven in the morning and a clerk of nineteen years entered what was in it and the order it was in, and the order matters and is the finding.

The first is a day a week and three bound copies of the public lot book, given to the ward market in the second year, and it is a paper and it is in the box and a stranger could read it if the market let him, and the market does let him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it can be read and that nobody has ever asked to read it.

The second is the covered bay at Lot Seventeen for the four hours a closing takes, given in the fourth year, and it is a paper, and a man of fifty-six said that a stranger cannot walk into a covered bay and see whether anybody is in it, **and that a thing a stranger cannot see is not a security and is not nothing, and that the rule said so at eleven o'clock this morning and had been true since the fourth year.**

The third is first refusal on a pitch at the ward market, given in the sixth year, and it is a paper and it is in the box and it is a right and not a sum and not a day and not a person.

The fourth is a dray and its two shafts, given in the ninth year and sold in the fourteenth, and a man of fifty-six said that one, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the paper says the security was discharged on the sale and that the man who bought the dray does not know that it was a security and has not been told and was not asked.

A clerk of the ward market was sent for at about midday and was asked, in the five things, whether the day a week and the three bound copies could be read by a stranger who came in and asked, and she said yes in four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said yes in four seconds and that it is the first of the five that can be looked at today without anybody doing anything.

Nobody argued and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the first of the five has been readable for nine years and that nobody has ever read it, and that a security nobody reads is a security that has not been posted, and that the only difference between it and the second of the five is that the first one is a piece of paper and the second one is a door.

A man of fifty-six asked how long the copies have been on the shelf at the ward market, and the clerk of the ward market said that the oldest of the three was bound in the fourth year and the newest in the ninth, and that a man had asked for the third one in the ninth year and had not been told what was in it, **and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked for a copy of a book in the ninth year and was not told what was in it, and that the not-telling is not a refusal and is not entered as one.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper was in the yard and said one thing about the first of the five, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and was not asked to and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.**

"**A posted thing nobody has ever come to look at is worth as much as a thing nobody posted, and a man who sells paper has known that for thirty years.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that the count of documents nobody owns is three and did not move, and that a security nobody has read is not a document nobody owns, **and that the two are two things and that the difference is that a document nobody owns has a person who used to own it and a security nobody has read has nobody at all.**

Nobody took it up. A man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row said that he has worked on two drays in this district and that one of them came off a farm four miles out past the loams, **and that a thing a man has bought and paid for and does not know was ever anybody's security is a thing a man cannot go and look at, and that he would not have minded, and that nobody told him and nobody asked him.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said he would not have minded and that nobody asked him whether he would have minded, **and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, and that a man who is not asked whether he would have minded has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the record says not asked.**

Then the fifth, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not put it on the table, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was in his hand and that he held it against his coat and that she was not asked to take it and did not ask.

The fifth is a man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five is a person and that it was given in the ninth year for a season's cartage and that the man is alive and is in this yard twice a week and has never been told, **and that the district has no word for what a person given as a security is and does not have one on any sheet, and that a man of fifty-six stopped in the middle of that sentence on the twenty-sixth of December and did not finish it and that this is the seventh day since and he has not finished it.**

Nobody argued and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a person is a thing a stranger can go and look at, and that anybody in this yard can go and look at him, and that this is the only one of the five that passes the sentence, **and that it passes it by accident and not by design and that a security nobody designed is still a security and that a man who is a security and does not know it is the worst case the rule in the yard this morning describes.**

**AND ON THE SECOND OF JANUARY THE FIVE SECURITIES WERE PUT ON A TABLE IN THE ORDER THEY WERE GIVEN, AND NONE OF THE FIVE IS A SUM, AND ONE IS A DAY A WEEK AND THREE BOUND COPIES OF A BOOK, AND ONE IS A DRAY AND ITS TWO SHAFTS THAT WAS SOLD IN THE FOURTEENTH YEAR WITHOUT THE BUYER BEING TOLD, AND THE FIFTH IS A PERSON WHO IS ALIVE AND HAS NEVER BEEN TOLD.**

---

The registrar asked, in the six things, at about one in the afternoon, who was going to read the list out, and a man of fifty-six asked her why she was asking, and she said the second half before he finished.

"**Because a list in a box is a box and a list read out at a counter is a document, and I have read documents out at that counter for nineteen years and I know what it does to a room.**"

Nobody took it up and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said yes in four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two of them arranged it in a yard in about nine seconds and that the arranging is not a conversation about the two of them and is not to be read as one, **and that a thing two people have agreed to do in the same room at the same hour on purpose is the first thing either of them has agreed to do since the eighteenth of December.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the registrar of this district's records office is about to read out a man's securities at a ward market counter and that she is not the clerk of that counter and is not a keeper of anything and is a person who can be held to, **and that a reader is a person who can be held to and that she has known that since the sixth of December and said it out loud in a room and that this is the second time she has said a thing before she was asked for it in front of this many people.**

The registrar said that she had said it in a room and that a yard is not a room and that the difference is a door and that she had not noticed the door, and a man of fifty-six said that a yard is a room with the weather in it and that the difference is a thing a person learns by standing in one, **and that a rule said out loud in a room is not written down whatever the weather is doing to it, and that he is not going to have the yard written down either.**

Nobody asked either of them anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that the count of not-askings about the registrar is not a column and carries no number on it, because a number on it would have been a column and a column is a thing a person is paid for and she is paid by nobody in this yard.**

The slate went past the kitchen in Alder Reach for the seventh time on the third of January and neither of them wrote on it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that seven is a count and a count is not a finding, and that two people had arranged to stand in a room at the same hour on purpose and that a slate does not know that and is not a party to it.

**AND ON THE SECOND OF JANUARY THE REGISTRAR OF THIS DISTRICT'S RECORDS OFFICE AGREED TO READ A MAN'S FIVE POSTED SECURITIES OUT AT A WARD MARKET COUNTER, AND HE AGREED TO IT IN FOUR SECONDS, AND THE TWO OF THEM ARRANGED IT IN A YARD IN ABOUT NINE SECONDS, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT THE ARRANGING IS NOT A CONVERSATION ABOUT THE TWO OF THEM AND IS NOT TO BE READ AS ONE.**

---

The registrar read four of the five out at the ward market counter on the third of January at about ten in the morning, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she read them and got nothing wrong and that the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and that the four went out twice.

Nobody refused and a man of fifty-six said that a man who has posted four things for nine years has just had them read out in a market he has walked through for nine years and that nobody in the market stopped, **and that a man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row was in the market and heard the second of the four read out and did not move, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not move and that she was not asked why and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.**

A woman of fifty who is the water at the ninth place was at the counter and said that she had heard the first of the four before and did not know what it was and had not asked, **and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that the not-asking about a woman of fifty who is the water at the ninth place is not a column and carries no number on it, because a number on it would have been a column, and that she was not asked and is not going to be asked twice.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that four things read out in a market is an instrument and that the instrument is named and that the name is the woman's, **and that a person who reads another person's securities out loud in a market is doing a thing no person in this district has done before and that the two of them did it on purpose and that nobody is going to be told what it was for.**

**AND THE REGISTRAR READ FOUR OF THE FIVE POSTED SECURITIES OUT AT THE WARD MARKET COUNTER TWICE AND NOBODY REFUSED, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY AT A FORGE END OFF SALT ROW WAS IN THE MARKET AND HEARD THE SECOND OF THE FOUR READ OUT AND DID NOT MOVE.**

---

The third of January was the day the list was not written, and a man of fifty-six said that is the finding and that a clerk of nineteen years is right to enter it as a finding, **and that a man who has been asked to publish everything he has posted has discovered in one day that four of the five can be written down and the fifth cannot, and that the fifth is a person, and that the rule which arrived in the yard on the second says that a person is a thing a stranger can go and look at and that this one passes by accident.**

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide was asked, in the five things, what should happen to the fifth, and she said that she did not know and gave no reason, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked and that she answered and that she was not asked why and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who is a security and does not know it should be told, and a man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row said that he would rather not be told in a yard, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both answers and entered that the two are two people and are not joined.

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper was asked, in the five things, what a list of five things is worth to a man who buys paper, and he gave the answer in a sentence and the sentence is in the minute in his own words, and it is twenty-seven words.

"**A list is worth what it takes off a thing and a list of five takes five things off a man and leaves him with a face.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a face is what this district has been trading on since the eighth of March, and that a man who sells paper on a lane off the lower terrace is the only person in the yard who has been telling him so, **and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper was entered before he spoke as not speaking for the room and that this is the fifth time he has been entered that way and that the count of it is five and is not a count of not-askings.**

**AND ON THE THIRD OF JANUARY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WHO DEALS IN SECOND-HAND PAPER SAID IN TWENTY-SEVEN WORDS THAT A LIST IS WORTH WHAT IT TAKES OFF A THING AND THAT A LIST OF FIVE TAKES FIVE THINGS OFF A MAN AND LEAVES HIM WITH A FACE, AND NOBODY TOOK IT UP.**

Nobody resolved it and a man of fifty-six said that the district has spent four hundred lines in eleven weeks refusing to decide a thing in a lane and that the fifth of the five is going to be decided in a room on purpose or it is not going to be decided at all, **and that the count of protected things is five and did not move and that a man who is a security is not a protected thing and is not in any of the counts and that is a finding and not a fault.**

And the list was not written on the third of January, **AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY AT A FORGE END OFF SALT ROW IS THE FIFTH OF FIVE POSTED SECURITIES AND IS ALIVE AND HAS NOT BEEN TOLD, AND A DISTRICT THAT POSTS A THING NOBODY CAN INSPECT IS RUNNING A LOT AND NOT HOLDING A SECURITY, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT THE UNDERWRITER'S POWER IS NOT BEGUN IN THIS DISTRICT AND THAT THE REASON IS THAT A MAN WHO CANNOT LIST WHAT HE HAS POSTED CANNOT POOL A SINGLE TOLL, AND THAT THE REASON IS ENTERED AND THE REASON IS NOT A DELAY.**
