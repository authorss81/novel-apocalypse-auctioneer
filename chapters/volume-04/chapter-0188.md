# Chapter 188: Nobody Asked Him

---

The second of the third month was the day after the month turned and about nine people came into the yard of Lot Seventeen before the light went, and the boards were wet, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the day in the minute in her own hand with the month and the number on it.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the standing question out loud at about ten in the morning and a man of fifty-six answered it before it was finished, and the answer was in the minute in his own words and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted it and got twenty-four.

"**We decided about a man and we have nine days of doing nothing left. Ask him now and the nine days are worth nothing.**"

No one said the opposite. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a decision to do nothing about a refusal is the only decision this district's business has made about a date and not about a document, **and that a decision which is about a date and not a document runs out on the date the way a document does not, and that this district found that out on the first of the third month and found it out about a cart.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a term on it is not a refusal of the figure and is not an acceptance of it and cannot be counted either way, **and that the term on it ran out on the first of the third month and that the man has not said anything and that a term running out and a man saying nothing are two things and are not joined.**

She entered that she was asked, in the six things, whether asking a man a question is doing something to his refusal, and that she said no, and that the reason is that a question in the six things is a thing this yard does and not a thing done to a man, **and that a yard which stops asking questions is not being careful and is not holding a decision and is a yard that has run out of things to ask about.**

It was left where it was put down.. A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that she has washing on the board at that hour and was going, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she went and that nobody asked her what she thought about a man who has not been asked, **and that a person who goes into about forty houses a week is the only instrument this district has that reaches a door nobody sends anybody to and is not on a list and is not going to be.**

**AND ON THE SECOND OF THE THIRD MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED IN THE SIX THINGS WHETHER ANYBODY WAS GOING TO ASK A MAN WHOSE REFUSAL RAN OUT ON YESTERDAY, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT THE YARD HAS NINE DAYS OF DOING NOTHING LEFT.**

---

Tarin Kest came through the gate at about eleven in the morning and did not stop at the trestle table, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came on his own account and that nobody sent for him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, straight away and in front of everybody, whether he was going to say the figure now the second month is out, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked to write the word term in beside it and did not and that the record says asked and answered and that the two of those are different and are not to be joined.

He said it in about two seconds and gave the rest of it himself, and it is in the minute in his own words, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-two.

"**I said I would not say it in a yard in the second month. The second month is out. I have not said it and I am not going to say it, and I never said that I would say it after.**"

Nobody in that yard answered him for a moment, and then a man of fifty-six said that this is the plainest finding in nine weeks and that it is that the nine days were never a term on a figure and were a term on his own mouth, **and that a man who has put a month on a refusal has been taken by everybody in this yard to have put a month on the other half of it, and that he did not, and that a term on one half of a thing is not a term on the other half of it.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the man said the second half of that before he was asked for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did and that she is not going to put a number on the count of the second half said before being asked, **and that the figure has not been looked at since the twenty-ninth of December and she is not going to look at it now either, and that a clerk is a person and not a rule.**

Then he said the other thing, and it was the thing he had come for, and nobody had asked him for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked for it and that she is not going to ask him why he came.

"**Two of the four households have asked me again what they owe the water and one of them has asked a second household, and I have told none of them anything and I am not going to.**"

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a household which asks a route keeper what it owes the water is asking the man who keeps the road to price the road, **and that a route keeper is a Binder's counterparty and not an apprentice, and is not a bearer of the road and was not asked to be one and is not going to be asked to be one, and that the fourth of the four has now been asked by two households instead of one and that is not a column.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a household which has asked has not refused and has not signed and has done neither of the two things anybody can enter, **and that the four households Tarin Kest named are not the six households at the sluice end of the loams and that the two have never been on the same page in any book this district has read and are not going to be.**

It was not disputed. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether the man had come to say that he had not been asked, and Tarin Kest said that he had come because he wanted it said out loud in a yard where about nine people could hear it, **and that a man who is not asked and does not come and a man who is not asked and comes are two men and not one, and that the difference between them is about nine people and that nine is a count of a room.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that no second thing was put to him, that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a man who is owed a month by a district and turns up to hand the month back is a person and not a figure, and that the figure he is holding is about four households and is not about nine hundred and the two are not added.**

**AND ON THE SECOND OF THE THIRD MONTH A ROUTE KEEPER SAID THAT THE NINE DAYS WERE NEVER A TERM ON A FIGURE BUT A TERM ON HIS OWN MOUTH, AND HE SAID THE SECOND HALF OF THAT BEFORE ANYBODY ASKED HIM FOR IT.**

---

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up the bank on the third of the third month at about one in the afternoon on his own account and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him, and he had a thing in his coat he did not take out.

A man of fifty-six said that the reason the Underwriter's power is not begun in this district has now changed its shape five times since the second of January and has not moved, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him to say the shape again in different words, and he said it standing up against the trestle table and a clerk of nineteen years wrote it in her own hand.

"**A man who cannot write five things down cannot pool one of them. The fifth of the five is a man who has said no to being posted a second time and no to standing in a gate, and the fourth is a receipt in a building thirty-four miles east that a stranger may read and may not walk off with.**"

Nobody argued with it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the list of the five is not written for the sixth time in a month and that the reason is entered and the reason is not a delay, **and that a shape is not a figure and that five is not a column and that the count of the five has not moved since the second of January and is not going to move because a man has described it again in a yard.**

the man who mends fencing asked what would have to be different for the reason to be a different reason, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that he does not know, **and that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam has said the second half of a thing about nine times in eleven weeks and that not knowing which one this is has not been put in a column and is not going to be.**

Nothing was said to it.. A man of fifty-six said that the answer is not going to come out of a man who digs a channel, and that he is not going to be asked for it again today, and that a man who is asked twice for the same second half is a man who is being used, **and that the district has three instruments that work without anybody coming to a yard and one of them is a man who digs loam and none of the three is on a list.**

the man who mends fencing asked the registrar of this district's records office, who had been in the yard since about half past one and had not been sent for, whether the district's own book could carry a not-asking, and she said that a book can carry anything a person is willing to write down and that that is not the same as the district's book, **and that she said the second half of that before he had finished the question and that a clerk of nineteen years entered it and is not going to put a number on it.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she has been asked nothing in this district for longer than she is going to say out loud, and that she is not keeping a count of it, **and that a count of it would be a column and a column is a thing a person is paid for, and that she is not paid and was not asked to be and is not going to be.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a district which cannot write down what it did not do is a district which is going to find out in about a month that it did nothing on a day it had a chance, **and that the twenty-eighth of the second month is that day and this district has entered nothing about it and the man whose tank it is has not been told.**

**THE UNDERWRITER'S POWER IS STILL NOT BEGUN IN THIS DISTRICT, THE REASON GIVEN FOR IT HAS TAKEN A NEW SHAPE FIVE TIMES SINCE THE SECOND OF JANUARY, AND IT HAS NOT MOVED ON ANY DAY OF THOSE FIVE, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT THE REASON IS NOT A DELAY.**

---

Then the fourth line, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked about it at about half past two in the afternoon in front of about nine people, and the question was whether anybody was going to read the fourth line of an offer out loud now that the month had turned.

A clerk of nineteen years said no and gave the reason and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-seven, and she said it in the ordinary voice of a person reading off a thing she has said before.

"**A man of fifty-six said on the nineteenth of the second month that he is not going to write in a minute that they are going to read it. I am not going to write in a minute that they are not going to read it either.**"

Nobody argued with that. A man of fifty-six said that the two of those are the same refusal and that only one of them is hers and that he said his out loud in a yard in the fourth month of a year, **and that a rule spoken out loud by the man it costs him is not a rule and does not get written down, that he would not have it written down, and that a clerk repeating the same rule a week later has not made a second rule and has not made a column.**

Then a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace said the other half of it, and he was entered before he spoke as not speaking for the room, and a count of not-speaking is not a count of not-askings.

"**A month turning is not a reason. I have been in this trade thirty years and a date has never once made a line on a sheet easier to read, and the ones that are hardest to read are the ones somebody is waiting for a month to go past.**"

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that the fourth line of the offer has been on a table thirty-four miles east three times and has not been read out loud in this district and is not going to be read out loud by anybody in this yard, **and that a line nobody can state cannot be carried by anybody, and that a district which cannot carry a line cannot act on it, and that he said the reason out loud on the nineteenth of the second month and has said it again and is not going to say it a third time.**

the man who mends fencing asked whether the sentence a woman of about thirty-five said at the far end of that counter on the ninth of the second month was the fourth line, and a clerk of nineteen years said no in about two seconds and gave no reason at all, and nobody asked her for one.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sentence at that counter is twenty-four words and that it is on a page in a man's own hand in a records office thirty-four miles from here and is not going to be read out again in this yard, **and that the woman is in no register in the west bank and is not named and is not going to be named, and that the registrar refused to print it in a public book on the fifteenth of the second month and gave the reason before she was asked for it, and that nobody in this yard is going to ask her a second thing.**

Nobody took that up. The man who digs loam said that the sentence and the fourth line are two lines about two houses and that the district has one document about a day and one document about a discharge and no document about anything else, **and that two documents is a district with two documents and is not a finding and is not a rate and is not going into any of the three counts.**

**AND THE FOURTH LINE OF AN OFFER THAT HAS BEEN ON A TABLE THREE TIMES IS STILL NOT READ OUT LOUD IN THIS DISTRICT, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS SAID THAT SHE IS NOT GOING TO WRITE IN A MINUTE THAT THEY ARE NOT GOING TO READ IT EITHER.**

---

The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace came up the bank at about four in the afternoon and had not been sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him.

He said two things and the second of them was the reason he came, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked for either of them and that she was not asked why he had come up a bank in the wet.

He said that the loam he put in the crack on the thirtieth of the second month is still in it, and that it has been there three days, and that the two mends before it came out in three pieces in seven days, **and that three days is the longest that mend has been in and that he is not going to say what that means in front of about nine people and is not going to be asked.**

the man who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, whether the tank was going to be all right, and the man of about fifty-five said that the tank is at about two days and that a mend which has held three days is not a tank, **and that a crack in a stone does not say where a toll lands on a place and is not on the list of the five, and that the count of five is the same five it was on the second of January.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure of two days was said by the man of about fifty-five and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked, **and that a tank at about two days and a fourth holding that has not taken a drop since the first of the second month are two pieces of water and are not one subject and have not been entered together today and are not going to be.**

Nobody argued with that. The man who digs loam said that the man who keeps the building is the only person in this district's business who can mend the thing and is the only one who cannot get anybody to help him, **and that a man who is not going to say what three days means in front of nine people is a man who has found out what saying it in front of one person costs and is not going to pay it twice.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that there are twenty-seven days in the spaces on the fourth page and that the page has about forty of them, **and that a page which is two thirds full in the first week of the third month is a page in a hurry, and that the end of a page is the only kind of ending a person in this district's business can bring about by doing the right thing.**

A man of fifty-six gave the interval out loud at about half past four in the afternoon, and a clerk of nineteen years stood near enough to write it down.

"**The thirty-first of March is twenty-eight days off this day and the first of April is twenty-nine, and the first of the third month was two days ago and a figure with no day on it is not an interval.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody wrote on a board on the second or the third of the third month and that the board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for sixty-nine days, **and that the boards still number five and the lines number six, and that the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty, and that a month turning is not going to be put on a wall because a clerk had a wet morning.**

Nobody in that yard said anything about it. The man who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether a man with a month gone by and a figure not said had done anything, and a man of fifty-six said that he had turned up, and that turning up is a thing a person does and not a thing a district records, **and that the refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading, and that none of the three has moved since the second of January.**

the man who digs loam said that a man who turns up to be told he was not asked is a man who has spent a day of his own on a thing nobody had given him, and that the day is a day of his and not a figure of the district's, **and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in this yard on both days and has not been asked for either of them and has not said anything about either of them.**

**AND ON THE THIRD OF THE THIRD MONTH THE TANK FOUR MILES DOWN A LANE IS AT ABOUT TWO DAYS, A MEND IN A CRACK HAS HELD FOR THREE DAYS FOR THE FIRST TIME, AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS TWENTY-EIGHT DAYS AWAY.**
