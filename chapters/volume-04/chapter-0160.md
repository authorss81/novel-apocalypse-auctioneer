# Chapter 160: The Nearest Date on a Bucket

---

The man of about thirty at the counter at the second reservoir came down to the yard of Lot Seventeen on his own account on the tenth of January and had a book under his arm, and he had walked it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

He opened the book on the trestle table at the fourth page and read out the tenth line and a man of fifty-six asked him to read it out again, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice.

> **THE TENTH LINE IS THE HOUSE'S OWN. THE HOUSE'S OWN IS NOT A HOLDING AND IS NOT ASKED FOR AND IS NOT GIVEN UP.**

Nobody took it up and the man of about thirty at the counter said that the house writes that line itself and has written it every year for four years and that he does not know what happens to it and has never asked, **and that the question has never occurred to him, and that he is about thirty, and that the tenth line is the only line on the page that cannot be got out of and it belongs to the house.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a district whose method is that a person can be looked at has spent eleven weeks arguing with a house about its own line and has got the answer in four sentences from a man who was not asked, **and that the man who cannot be got out of the tenth line is the only party in this district's whole business who cannot be got out of anything, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered the ten and it is not a column.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that it is not a column and that she was not going to enter a number on it and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve and that neither moved on the tenth of January.

Then the man of about thirty at the counter went back up the road and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against walked about nine yards of the yard and then stopped, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he stopped, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road said that the man of fifty-six has stopped walking about nine yards every time something comes into the yard and has never once been asked why.

He was asked on the tenth of January, in the yard, by a man of fifty-six, and he said the reason and a clerk of nineteen years entered it and was then asked by a man of fifty-six whether he wanted it in the minute and said no in four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she asked and that he said no and that the asking is entered and the count is where it was.

"**A man who is walking in a yard cannot hear the yard. He has to stop before he can hear it and about nine yards is as far as I have found that works.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that a man who has found a figure that works for his own ears has said out loud against his own advantage the one thing he has never had written down, and that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage in a room is not a rule and is not written down.

**AND ON THE TENTH OF JANUARY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY AT THE UPLAND READ OUT THE TENTH LINE OF HIS OWN BOOK IN A YARD FOUR MILES DOWN THE ROAD, AND IT SAYS THAT THE HOUSE'S OWN IS NOT A HOLDING AND IS NOT ASKED FOR AND IS NOT GIVEN UP, AND HE SAID HE HAD NEVER ASKED, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT A LINE NOBODY CAN BE GOT OUT OF IS A THING NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAS A WORD FOR AND THAT SHE WAS NOT GOING TO GIVE IT ONE.**

---

The woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in the yard on the twelfth of January and was not asked for anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, and that she carries water to about ninety houses and that the figure of ninety was said by her and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked.

She said the other half of what she said on the twenty-fourth of December, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had said it in December and was saying it in January and that the two occasions are not joined, and that a plank has four dates on it and a bucket has four dates on it and one of them is nearer.

She gave the four out loud and a clerk of nineteen years entered them in her own words, and they are the first of the second month, which is the day the water comes to the sluice, the sixteenth of the second month, which is the day the seed is due at the Road House on the tide, the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is a rent day nobody has ever put on a board, and the last day of the third month.

A man of fifty-six said that the last of the four is the thirty-first of March and that it is in her bucket because it is the day she has been carrying water since the ninth year and nobody has ever told her what is on it, and that two of the four are this district's business and two of them are not, and that a bucket is a better register than a board because nobody can wash a bucket.

Nobody asked her what is on the last day of the third month and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, and that a man of fifty-six said the question out loud in a yard in December and was not asked it then either.

Then a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her what the last day of the third month was, and she said the west end of the Needle went under nine feet on that day nine years ago, and that she was carrying water past it when it happened and that the water went up the lane faster than she could walk.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the flood year is the current year and that the year turned on the first of January and that the eighteenth year after the Long Fracture began on that morning with nothing on it, **and that a woman who does not read figures has known the last day of the third month for nine years without anybody telling her, and that this district has carried that date in a charter for eleven weeks and has not once asked the person who has been carrying it longest.**

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that the nearest of the four is a week and that the furthest of the four is a flood, and that a district whose two instruments are a week and a flood is a district that has been measuring the wrong things since the eighth of March, and that the thirty-first of March is on a charter and on a bucket and is seventy-eight days from the twelfth of January.

Then she said which of the four was nearer and it is in the minute in her own words and it is twenty-eight words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-eight and said it out loud.

"**The first of them is a week and not a day, and a week is nearer than a day and I cannot tell you which day in it.**"

The room did not argue with it and a man of fifty-six said that the sheet on a nail in a shed at the second reservoir says the delivery is made in the first week of a named month

And a district which has spent eleven weeks arguing about a date in a corner of a copy has just been handed a week, **and that a claim on a place in a building cannot be worked out in a week, and that the third of the four printed things says the claim passes if the family does not pay on the day, and that this house has not named the day, and that the two are the same sentence said by two people for the same reason.**

A man of fifty-six said the interval out loud in the yard and a clerk of nineteen years entered it, and a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and beside the index there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date.

"**From the thirty-first of December at three hundred and twenty-three, the first of January is three hundred and twenty-four, and the twelfth is three hundred and thirty-five, and eleven days remain in the first month, then the whole of the second month to the eleventh.**"

**AND ON THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIVE DAYS, AND THE COUNT OF REFUSALS TO READ IS NINE, AND THE DEPARTURE IS UNSPENT, AND THERE IS STILL NO DATE IN THE LEDGER FOR THE NEXT READING, AND THE NEAREST DATE ANYBODY IN THE YARD COULD NAME IS THE ELEVENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH AND IT IS A DAY A MAN AT A COUNTER HAS NEVER NAMED.**

---

Then a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the whole of the three days had come down to, and a man of fifty-six said it out loud in the ordinary voice and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in her own words, and it is twenty-one words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-one and said it out loud.

"**Nine holdings, a tenth line, and a house that writes the four lines on the face of every one of them.**"

Nobody took it up and a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said that the last of the three is the one the room will be saying in a fortnight to somebody with a sheet in a pocket, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said it and that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

Then a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether the four lines in the window frame are the same four the room has a copy of, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said he would not swear to it in a yard and that there was a crack across the third one, **and that a crack across the third one is the reason this district may be arguing with the kindest sheet in the basin, and that nobody has checked the other three and that the not-checking is not entered as a refusal.**

A man of fifty-six said that the two are two things and that a house which writes plainly is still a house, and that the district's finding of the twenty-fifth of December holds and is not repaired by anything anybody said on a road.

Then a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud that the eleventh of the second month is the day the train came, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and that it is true, **and that the eleventh of the second month is a day the train came and a day a house has not named,**

And a district which notices a coincidence on a slipper is a district that has run out of instruments and is using the calendar, **and that a district does not get to make a date mean two things because it likes one of them, and that the train and the water are two things and are not joined, and that the two of them falling on the same day is a thing a person noticed and not a finding.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said that he had not thought of it and that he wished he had and that he is not going to let it be a thing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both of those and entered that a man who says he wishes he had not had an idea is a man doing the work of a column and is not one, **and that the day the train came is three hundred and sixty-five days back from the twelfth of January and the day the water comes is thirty days forward of it, and that the two figures are in two hands and are not added.**

Then the three pages, and a man of fifty-six said it out loud and a clerk of nineteen years entered all three in one line and did not join them, **and that a clerk of nineteen years entering three things in one line and not joining them is the oldest thing in this district's books and the only one that has never been argued with.**

The first is a page of six columns and eleven rows on a board of eleven in a yard, and it is about a year and it is not about a security, and it cannot be certified and it can be argued with, **and a year is not coppers and a year and a half is not a figure and nobody can post one against anything.**

The second is a page in the ward market's own book with four entries on it, and it is about a man and it is not about a year. The third is a page with four pages in it and about forty names on the fourth and no line for a mark, and it is about a building and it is not about either of the other two, **and that a page of six columns, a page with four entries and a page with forty names are three instruments and that an instrument is a thing a person can go and look at, and that none of the three can be looked at from where the water is.**

Nobody took that up and a man of fifty-six said that three instruments in one yard and not one of them about water, and that the water is in the first week of the second month and is a mile and a bit long at the top of a road, **and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and did not move, and that a page is not a sluice and a sluice is not a page, and that a district that has spent eleven weeks naming its own paper has a mile of standing water it cannot see.**

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten came into the yard at about half past three in the afternoon and had sent word in the first of January that he would start again in January, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that he was not asked why he had not come before and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

He said one thing about the four dates on a bucket, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got thirty and did not count them again.

"**A bucket is not a register and a plank is not a calendar and neither of them is a book, and I say that as a man who fills one.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that a man who fills a page for nothing has just told the yard that the two best instruments in this district are not instruments, and that the district has six that it has built and not named and that a bucket is not going to be a seventh because a bucket belongs to a woman, **and that a sheet and a frame and a nail and a bucket and a page are five things in this district that a person can go and look at and that the count of instruments it has built and not named did not move and is six.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of protected things is five and did not move and that a passage twice, a loss once, a failure of a remedy once and a refusal to be asked again once are the five, and that nobody added a sixth in January, and that the man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten is in none of the five and did not go in one by coming into a yard.

**AND THERE ARE THREE PAGES IN THIS DISTRICT AND NOT ONE OF THEM IS ABOUT WATER, AND THE COUNT OF INSTRUMENTS BUILT AND NOT NAMED IS SIX AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE COUNT OF THINGS THE DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE IS FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE, AND A NAIL IN A SHED IS NOT A BOARD AND THE COUNT OF BOARDS IS FIVE AND THE COUNT OF LINES IS SIX AND THE SEVENTH LINE AT THE NINTH PLACE IS RULED AND EMPTY.**

---

On the afternoon of the twelfth of January the registrar of this district's records office went down the lane to the flood shelter first and came back at about half past four, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she went and that she was gone about an hour and that she did not say what she had done in there in the yard and that nobody asked, **and that a person who goes into a building and comes out of it and says nothing has been in a room, and that a yard is not a room and that the difference is a door and that she noticed the door this time.**

She said one thing about it when she came back, and it is in the minute in her own words and it is thirty-four words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got thirty-four and said it out loud.

"**A record kept by a person in a building is a record, and my office has a standing instruction about records kept by persons, and I have never once been told what it is.**"

Nobody took it up and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said it and that nobody asked her why and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and the registrar's own count stands at twelve and that neither moved on the twelfth of January.

Nobody asked her why she holds two lines apart either, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked and that she was not going to enter a number on it, and that a question which is not answered is not a question that has been put into a column and that there is no column for it and none was made.

Then she said that she had to go to Alder Reach about the page in the shelter as well, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said it twice in about an hour and that the two of them are the same errand and are not two, **and that a standing instruction nobody in a room has ever been told the contents of is not an instrument and is not a rule and is not one of the five things this district does not have, and that a column is a thing a person is paid for and an instruction is not a column.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said he would walk with her, and she said yes in four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two of them said it in the same room at the same hour on purpose for the second time since the eighteenth of December, **and that the arranging is still not a conversation about the two of them and is not to be read as one, and that a clerk of nineteen years is not going to be the person who reads it as one.**

The slate went past the kitchen in Alder Reach for the eighth time that afternoon and the registrar of this district's records office stopped in the lane and read the four sentences in charcoal out loud, and she did not write on it, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not ask her what the four sentences say, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road was about nine paces behind them and did not ask either.

He entered afterwards that the two of them stopped, and that he was not asked anything, and that the count of slates is eight and that eight is a count and not a finding, **and that whether he heard the four sentences is not entered, and that the four sentences have been under a chalk line since the fourth of April and that the chalk line has been there longer than either of them has been in this district's books.**

Then the last entry of the twelfth of January, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against goes east on the fifteenth of January and that he was not asked to and that the going is entered and that a going is not a reading, **and that a woman whose name is in no register in the west bank has offered four lines three times and nothing has been accepted and nothing has been refused, and that the fourth of the four has not been read out loud in this district.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked in the yard why he goes east at all if there is nothing on the other side of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked to put that to him and that a man of fifty-six put it to him and that the answer is in the minute in his own words and it is twenty-two words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-two and said it out loud.

"**A question nobody has asked is a question nobody has refused, and I am going east to be the one who asks.**"

**AND ON THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST SAID IN TWENTY-TWO WORDS THAT HE GOES EAST BECAUSE A QUESTION NOBODY HAS ASKED IS A QUESTION NOBODY HAS REFUSED, AND HE GOES ON THE FIFTEENTH OF JANUARY AND HE WAS NOT ASKED TO AND THE FOURTH LINE OF THE OFFER IS STILL NOT READ OUT LOUD IN THIS DISTRICT.**

And the register's standing question was said out loud in the yard at about four in the afternoon and not answered, **AND ON THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY A DISTRICT HELD A PAGE OF SIX COLUMNS ABOUT A YEAR AND A PAGE WITH FOUR ENTRIES ABOUT A MAN AND A PAGE WITH FORTY NAMES AND NO LINE FOR A MARK, AND NONE OF THE THREE IS ABOUT WATER.**

**AND THE WATER IS A MILE AND A BIT LONG AT THE TOP OF A ROAD AND COMES IN THE FIRST WEEK OF THE SECOND MONTH ON A DAY NOBODY HAS NAMED, AND WHAT A DISTRICT DOES WITH A DOCUMENT IT CANNOT CERTIFY AND CANNOT ARGUE WITH IS STILL A QUESTION AND THE ANSWER IS THAT IT HAS TO BE ABLE TO DO BOTH, AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS SEVENTY-EIGHT DAYS FROM THE TWELFTH OF JANUARY.**
