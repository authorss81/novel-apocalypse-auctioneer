# Chapter 189: What To Do Afterwards

---

The sentence the woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the sixteenth of the second month has been in the minute ever since and it has not been entered as a task, and a clerk of nineteen years entered on the fourth of the third month that nobody was sent and that the not-sending is entered and the count is where it was.

She said that she did not read the sheet and would read it if somebody told her what to do afterwards, and a man of fifty-six said in the yard on the nineteenth of the second month that the yard was not going to decide who would tell her in a yard, **and that a district which decides things in a yard about a date has not yet decided anything about a woman on a tide four miles away and has not said that it has not.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, in the six things, on the morning of the fourth of the third month, whether he was going, and he said that he was, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked why and that she is not going to ask.

He went down the lane off the lower terrace and along the road to the tide on foot, and the lane is not metalled for about sixty yards and the road for the first mile and a half of it is not metalled either, **and there was no clerk on that road and nothing was entered on the way and both of them said so, and the man who can be found went alone and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he went alone and that nobody sent for him.**

The first mile and a half of that road is clay under about two inches of gravel and it had been raining since about four in the morning, and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a man who walks that road once in a season is doing a thing and a man who walks it nine times is a road, and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against has now walked it once and it has made him no roads at all.

A clerk of nineteen years entered in the yard at about six in the evening that he came back and that nothing came back with him, **and that a visit which produces no document is not a visit which produced nothing, and that a page in a man's own hand is worth less than a minute and is a different kind of thing and this was not even that.**

That went no further. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a man who goes four miles to a door and comes back with nothing has done a thing and has not made a record, **and that a record a man brings back is worth more than a thing he did, and that the district has spent four months believing the second of those and the first of those is what a man does on a wet road.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace said that a man who comes back with nothing has been the only person in that room who did not come back with a sentence off a counter, **and that four people in this yard have now said something about carrying a page and not one of them has said anything about carrying a question to a door, and that the man who came back up the hill this morning is the only one of the four who has been to the far end of anything.**

**AND ON THE FOURTH OF THE THIRD MONTH THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST WENT FOUR MILES TO A ROAD HOUSE ON THE TIDE TO TELL A WOMAN WHAT TO DO AFTERWARDS, AND HE WENT ALONE, AND HE CAME BACK WITH NOTHING TO PUT ON A TABLE.**

---

The Road House has a door and a shutter and a bar across the inside of it, and the bar was not on because it was about four in the afternoon, and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam said later that a bar that is not on in the afternoon is a thing a person has decided about every morning and the district has no instrument for that.

She was behind the bar with it off and the boy of nine was on a stool by the stove with a piece of paper in his hand, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked a second thing and that his name is not in the minute and that the minute says a boy of nine.

The boy got off the stool and went into the back room and came in again with a piece of folded paper and put it on the table, and nobody asked him where he had got it and nobody asked him anything else, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked nothing further and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, and that a child who puts a piece of paper on a table and is not asked where it came from is a child and not a document.**

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam asked the woman of about forty-four whether the boy knew what was on it, and she said that she did not know and that he had not told her, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a mother who does not know what her child has is not a mother who has refused to know, **and that a house in this district's business is kept by people who do not know what is in it nine days in ten and that the two of those are not a finding about either of them.**

It was not picked up. A man of fifty-six said that a man who came four miles to tell a woman what to do afterwards did not ask her what her son had, and that the not-asking is in the same line and is not joined to the other not-asking, **and that a clerk of nineteen years has four separate columns of not-askings in this district and has entered two of them on one day and is not adding any of them to another and is not going to.**

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a piece of paper in a child's hand and a page in a man's hand are two things and that the one in the child's hand came out of a back room and not out of a counter on the second of the third month, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that both of those are facts and are not joined.

Then he told her, and he told her three things and he did not write any of them down, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not write them down on his own account and was not asked about it.

He told her what the four printed things on that sheet say, and he did not read them out of a copy because he has not got the copy, and he told her that the day which decides the claim is the twenty-ninth of December and is not on the sheet at all, **and that a man who carries a page to a door cannot read out of it and read a document out of it and that those are two different men and he is the first of them.**

A man of fifty-six said in the yard afterwards that the whole of what a Binder can do to a piece of paper in a house is carried in a coat and is not in the district, **and that a man who has said that out loud in a lane has said it against his own advantage and it is not a rule and is not written down and he would not have it written down.**

Nothing was said to it. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether anybody was going to tell the woman of about forty-four that the man had said that, and a man of fifty-six said that she was in the room and did not have to be told, **and that a district which keeps a sentence because keeping it is convenient is the house thirty-four miles east and is not this district and is not going to become it while there is a page left to write on.**

**AND ON THE FOURTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A BOY OF NINE PUT A PIECE OF FOLDED PAPER ON A TABLE AND WAS NOT ASKED WHERE HE HAD GOT IT, AND A MAN TOLD A WOMAN WHAT TO DO AFTERWARDS AND DID NOT WRITE ANY OF IT DOWN.**

---

The paper was about the size of a coin and it had been folded and unfolded so many times that it was going to come apart at the folds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it came out of a back room in a Road House on the tide on the second of the third month and that nobody asked the boy about it and that nobody asked the woman about it either.

the man who digs loam read it out in the yard on the evening of the fourth and a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter, and this is the second document of that shape this district has read out of somebody's hand.

> **THE NINTH PLACE. THE SECOND OF THE THIRD MONTH.**
>
> **ONE NAME IN A BEARER COLUMN. NO FIGURE AGAINST IT.**
>
> **THIS IS NOT A RECEIPT FOR WATER. THIS IS NOT A RECEIPT FOR SEED. IT IS WHAT THE COUNTER GIVES YOU WHEN A NAME GOES IN.**

Nobody said anything for a moment. A man of fifty-six said that the stub carries a date and the sheet does not, and that this district has spent a month arguing about a day that is not written on a sheet, **and that a day on a stub is not a day on a sheet and a stub is not a minute and a receipt is not a consent and neither of the two is the thing the twenty-ninth of December decides.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a stub is a receipt and that a receipt is not a minute, and that a stub a person is holding is a document somebody owns and this district has three documents that nobody owns, **and that the two of those are different kinds of thing and are not the same list and the count of three did not move on the fourth of the third month and a stub is not a fourth.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the woman of about forty-four, in the six things, what happened at the counter on the second of the third month, and she said the whole of it in the ordinary voice and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-seven.

"**I stood at the end of it with eleven other women and the man filled the column in and did not ask me anything and I did not ask him anything, and I have been at that counter on the second of every month for nine weeks.**"

Nothing was said against it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that no second thing was put to her, that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a column filled in about eleven seconds without an asking is a fact this district found on the seventh of February and has now been told again by the person whose name went in, and that the two are not joined and are not a column.**

A man of fifty-six said that a man who fills a column in about eleven seconds without asking a man of forty-five anything has this district's own sentence on the face of the paper, **and that the second of the four printed things says the named person shall not be below fifteen and shall not be asked, and that the counter is working from both halves of that at once and did not know it was doing it.**

the man who digs loam said that the boy of nine is in that column and that the counter man is about thirty and is a clerk of a house and is not a friend of this district, **and that a clerk of a house who fills a column in eleven seconds and is not asked about it is not a person who has refused to ask and is not a person who has consented and is not to be entered as either.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that in thirty years he has bought about nine hundred paper things in this basin and has never once had a person bring him one, **and that a person who brings you a thing they cannot read and asks you to look at it is handing you the whole of it and is not handing you a copy, and that a copy is what she is not going to give this district.**

Nobody took that up. The man who mends fencing asked whether the district could not simply have a copy of the stub, and the man of about thirty-eight said no, and gave the reason in a single sentence, and the minute has it in his own words, and a man of about nineteen counted and got thirty-one.

"**She is not a keeper of it and she is not paid and I am not going to be the man who goes and takes a boy's name off his mother.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who holds a thing is not a keeper of it, **and that a way to pay a person who is not in a household is the fifth of the five things this district does not have, and that a woman of about forty-four keeps a Road House and is in a household and is not that, and that the two are not joined.**

**AND THE STUB THE MOTHER OF A BOY OF NINE HAS GOT SAYS THE SECOND OF THE THIRD MONTH AND ONE NAME IN A BEARER COLUMN, AND IT DOES NOT CARRY THE TWENTY-NINTH OF DECEMBER, AND A STUB A PERSON IS HOLDING IS A DOCUMENT SOMEBODY OWNS AND THE COUNT OF DOCUMENTS NOBODY OWNS IS THREE.**

---

She asked it at about half past four in the afternoon and she asked it once and then she waited, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked it once and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

"**What does the water cost if we say no.**"

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said that he did not know, and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam said afterwards that those are four words and that a man who has come four miles to say four words has had a day, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it on the fourth of the third month and that she is not going to put a number on the count of the second half said before being asked.

A man of fifty-six said in the yard that the figure exists and is being held by a route keeper who keeps a road and is not a bearer of it, and who said in forty-two words on the second of the third month that he had never said he would say it after, **and that nobody in this district is going to ask him to and that the yard decided on the nineteenth of the second month not to do anything about it and has not gone back on that.**

the man who mends fencing asked whether the road was going to keep the water coming anyway, and a man of fifty-six said that he does not know that either, **and that a head race standing with no term on it and no bearer on it is the same water and is not a new finding, and that a man who keeps a road is not a party to what the road does and is not going to be made one.**

Nobody took that up. The man who digs loam said that the figure is about four households and that she is one household and that a figure about four households and a household of one are not the same size and are not added, **and that a household which asks what it costs before it has said yes is a household that is not in this district's records as anything and is not going to be.**

A man of fifty-six said that she did not ask what it costs because she was going to refuse, and that a person who has already decided to say no and then asks the price is asking about the cost of being told no and not about the price of water, **and that a person who is going to say no and still wants to know why the no would cost anything is a person and not a refusal and is not entered as one.**

**AND ON THE FOURTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR ASKED A MAN WHAT THE WATER COSTS IF THEY SAY NO, AND HE SAID THAT HE DID NOT KNOW, AND THE FIGURE IS ABOUT FOUR HOUSEHOLDS AND SHE IS ONE HOUSEHOLD AND THE TWO ARE NOT ADDED.**

---

She said the other thing at about five in the afternoon and she said it while she was putting the bar back on the door, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said it in her own words and that she was not asked for it and that she was not asked why she said it.

"**I want the water. I am not going to put a name in a column for a season I have not been in and he is not going to be asked either, and I am not going to be asked, and that is all of it.**"

Nobody argued with that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody put a second thing to her, that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a person who says out loud that she is not going to be asked has not refused a rule and is not entered as one, and that a woman of about forty-four is not one of the five protected things and is not a sixth of them.**

A man of fifty-six said that a family which prefers debt to immediate hunger is not a fool and is not a victim and is a person making a decision with the two things they have, **and that this district has spent four months trying to make that person sign something and has not once asked what the person would have instead, and that she has now told a man who came four miles the answer in forty-six words and has not been thanked for it.**

the man who mends fencing asked what a person would have instead, and the woman of about forty-four said that she had not been asked that and that the man who had come was the first person to ask her anything at all in nine weeks, **and that a question nobody expected to be asked is not a question anybody in this yard put and the yard is not going to be given the credit for it.**

Nobody took that up. The man who digs loam said that a sentence said to a man on a road is a sentence and not a document, and that the rule about a sentence said at a counter by a person who was not asked arrived in this district in a lane on the second of the second month, **and that this sentence was said to a man who asked, and that the two of them are the same kind of sentence and one of them is in a records office and one of them is in a man.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came back into the yard at about six in the evening with his coat through and sat down by the stove, and that nobody offered him anything and that he was not offered anything because there is nothing in that yard to offer a man, **and that a district which has a man sitting in it at six in the evening in a wet coat and nothing to give him has the fifth of the five things it does not have and has had it since the second of January.**

the man who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, whether he was going to write any of it down, and he said no, and gave the reason himself in a sentence that is in the minute in his own words, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-two.

"**She said it to me on a road and I am not going to put a woman who cannot read a sheet on a page because she trusted me with a sentence.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said the second half of that before he was asked for it and that she is not going to put a number on it, **and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to write a person down has given the reason away and is not going to be thanked for it and has not been thanked.**

Then the four of the nine, because the man who digs loam could not leave it, and the fourth has not taken a drop since the first of the second month and the seventh was given up at the top of an eleven mile road on the twenty-fourth and the eighth is held and the ninth is the one nobody can account for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the four of those are four states of four pieces of paper and are not a column, **and that a house's own delivery note says the house delivers to a holding when the person who holds it opens a gate, and that the fourth has a gate and nobody who can be found to open it and the seventh has a bar and no holder and that the two are not joined and have not been joined.**

A man of fifty-six said that the farm four miles out past the loams is still unsold and that the man of about forty-three who buys standing seed-crop offered two thousand four hundred coppers and a cart of meal for the standing crop and the sluice on the twenty-ninth of December and went west on the thirtieth and has not come back, **and that nobody has sent for him and the not-sending is entered and the count is where it was, and that a gate with a new chain on it and a man who walks the field about twice a month is two facts about a farm and the man has never been seen.**

Nobody took that up. The man who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide was on that list of nine, and a man of fifty-six said that she is not and that the nine are the house's nine and hers is a house of one on a tide and **that a person who prefers debt to immediate hunger has two things in a book this month and neither of them is a claim.**

**AND ON THE FOURTH AND FIFTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A MAN FOUR MILES DOWN A ROAD SAID THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO PUT A WOMAN WHO CANNOT READ A SHEET ON A PAGE BECAUSE SHE TRUSTED HIM WITH A SENTENCE, AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS TWENTY-SIX DAYS AWAY AND THE FIRST OF APRIL IS TWENTY-SEVEN.**
