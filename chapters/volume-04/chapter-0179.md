# Chapter 179: A Refusal With a Month On It

---

Tarin Kest came through the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning on the nineteenth of the second month and had not been sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

He said the other half of a thing before anybody asked him for it, and it is in the minute in his own words and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted it and got forty and said it out loud.

"**I said I would not say it in a yard in the second month. There are nine days left in the second month and I have not said what I will do after that and I am not going to.**"

The yard was quiet for the length of a breath and a half. A man of fifty-six said that a man who has put a month on a refusal has done the only thing this district's business has done for itself in a month, and that it is not a promise and is not a threat, **and that a refusal with a term on it is a different instrument from a refusal without one, and that the difference is the whole of what a term is.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, what happens on the first of the next month, and Tarin Kest said that he did not know and that the man asking him has just said the date out loud in front of about nine people.

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that the finding of the nineteenth of the second month is that this district has one refusal with a term on it and that it is a route keeper's and that the term is a month, **and that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and was not asked to be and is not going to be asked to be, and that the figure he is holding is about four households and not about nine hundred.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a term on it is not a refusal of the figure and is not an acceptance of it, and that it cannot be counted either way, **and that the record of it says not asked, and that a man who says he will not say a thing in a month and is asked in the first of the next month is a man who has been asked and has not yet answered and that is not a no.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a season is a thing a man describes and a month is a thing a man counts, and that a man has just put a count on a season for the first time in this district's business, **and that a count of nine days is a count and not a column and that a district which starts counting a man's refusals is a district that has run out of other things to count.**

**AND ON THE NINETEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH TARIN KEST SAID THAT HE WILL NOT SAY A FIGURE IN A YARD IN THE SECOND MONTH, THAT THERE ARE NINE DAYS LEFT IN THE SECOND MONTH, AND THAT HE HAS NOT SAID WHAT HE WILL DO AFTER THAT.**

---

The four households were asked about at about half past eleven in the morning and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked Tarin Kest in the six things whether the four of them have been told anything, and Tarin Kest said that two of them asked him what they owe the water and one of them has not said a word.

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that two households asking a route keeper what they owe the water is the same two households asking a man to name a price, **and that a route keeper is not a bearer of the road and a man who keeps a road is not the man who owns the keeping of it, and that the difference is the whole of what this district has learned about roads in eleven weeks.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a household which asks what it owes has not refused and has not signed and has done neither of the two things anybody can enter, **and that a household which has done neither has not been asked, and the record says not asked, and a man who is not asked cannot be counted either way and that is the whole of what a clerk can do with him.**

Then the third household, and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households came down the bank at about twenty past twelve and said that the third of the six was asked a second time on the eighteenth of the second month and said no again in four seconds, and that no reason was given the second time either.

Nobody argued with that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that two households have now refused and three have not been asked again, and that one of the three is the man who lives in a cart, and that a household asked twice and refused twice is a refusal and is not a not-asking, **and that two refusals are two and are not a column and are not added to the count of not-askings about anybody in particular, because a person who refuses is not a person anybody is not asking.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether the three who have not been asked again are going to be asked, and a man of fifty-six said that the yard has not decided that and is not going to decide it in a yard, **and that a district which asks a household three times in six weeks has turned an offer into a campaign, and that a campaign is a thing a house does and not a thing a district is.**

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in the yard and was not asked for anything, and she said that the nearest of the four dates on her bucket is the twenty-fourth of the third month and that the last of the four is the last day of that month, **and that three of the four are other people's business and one of them is this district's, and that the one which is this district's is about a month off and a month is a week and a half to a woman carrying water.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the four dates on a bucket are a register and not a column and that nobody has ever asked to see the bucket, **and that a bucket has four dates and a ledger has two and an index has one, and that this district has three registers and will not join them and that the not-joining is the only rule it has never broken.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about fifty-six said that before he was asked for it and that she is not going to put a number on it, **and that the word campaign has not been used in this district's business before today and that a new word is not a new column and is not going into any of the three counts.**

---

Then the fourth line, and nobody asked for it, and a man of fifty-six said out loud in the yard that it has been on a table three times and has not been read out loud in this district and is not going to be read out loud by anybody in this yard.

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a woman of about thirty-five at a counter said one thing on the ninth of the second month that was not offered to anybody, and that a person who says one unasked thing has made no offer and refused nothing, **and that the only offer in this district's business is four lines on a table three hundred and thirty miles or thirty-four miles away and that three of the four have been read out loud and the fourth has not.**

Nobody argued. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the difference is between the third of the four and the fourth of the four, and a man of fifty-six said that he does not know and that nobody in this district knows, **and that a line nobody can state cannot be carried by anybody and that a district which cannot carry a line cannot act on it, and that this is the whole of the reason the fourth line has not been read out loud and it is not a reason anybody has ever said out loud before today.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six said the reason out loud and gave no reason for saying it in a yard, and that she was not asked about it and is not going to be, **and that a man who explains his own silence on the same day he has said he is going to keep it is a man who has given a reason away, and that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage is not a rule and is not written down.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the third of the four printed things is the one about the day and that the district has spent eight days trying to name a day, and that the fourth of the five terms of a house thirty-four miles east is the one about the receipt, and that a district which has one document about a day and one document about a discharge and no document about anything else is a district with two documents.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the four lines of the offer have not been read out loud and that the four printed things on the sheet on the tide were read out in December in a yard by a woman of about forty-four and that the five terms were read out twice at a ward market counter on the twentieth of January, **and that the two of those are the only two instruments anybody in this district has ever published and that neither of them was written by anybody in this district.**

**AND THE FOURTH LINE OF AN OFFER THAT HAS BEEN ON A TABLE THREE TIMES HAS STILL NOT BEEN READ OUT LOUD IN THIS DISTRICT, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO WRITE IT IN A MINUTE, AND THE REASON HE GAVE FOR SAYING THAT WAS GIVEN WITHOUT A REASON BEING ASKED FOR.**

---

The registrar of this district's records office was in the yard at about half past two in the afternoon and was not sent for, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in the six things, whether a page can say who owes the water, and she said no in four seconds and gave the reason in a sentence that a man of about nineteen counted and got twenty-seven.

"**An address is not a bearer. Four households are not four addresses. Write four addresses down and you have written down four places to send something to.**"

Nobody answered that straight away. A man of fifty-six said that a records office has a page for what a person was owed and has no page for what a person said, and that the second of those is what this district has been needing since the tenth of the second month, **and that a page for what a person is owed and a page for what a person said are two different books and this district has one of them and has had it for nine years.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked to write the four households down and was not asked to write the sentence the woman at the counter said, and that the two not-askings are entered and the count is where it was, **and that a records office which enters only what it has seen is a records office and not a newspaper, and that this district has one of those and said so out loud on the thirtieth of January and again on the fifteenth of this month, and that the two occasions are not a column.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a route keeper holding a figure for four households and a records office with no page for what a person said are the two ends of the same gap, **and that the gap is about a figure and nobody has been able to put the figure anywhere and the two ends of it are a man with a road and a woman with a book.**

---

Then the list, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked about it in the six things at about half past one in the afternoon, for the fifth time in a month, and a man of fifty-six gave the answer he has given four times and the answer was longer this time.

"**Four of the five I can say. The fifth is a man who stood in a gate on the seventeenth of this month and said no in four seconds, and the fourth is a piece of paper about the size of his hand that a stranger may read and may not take away, and I have still not written the list.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the list is not written for the fifth time in a month and that the reason is entered and the reason is not a delay, **and that a man who cannot list what he has posted cannot pool a single toll, and that the reason has changed its shape a fourth time since the second of January and has not moved, and that a shape is not a figure and four is not a column.**

Nobody argued. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the reason has changed shape four times without moving, and that a thing which changes shape without moving is the exact description of a thing nobody in this district is looking at, **and that a district which cannot write its own list of securities has never had its securities in writing and has been standing behind them for nine years.**

A man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row was in the yard and said one thing about the fourth of the five, and it is in the minute in his own words and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-one.

"**You have got my name on a list you cannot finish and you have got my no in a book, and those are the two worst places a man can be.**"

The yard let it stand. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it before anybody asked him for it and that she is not going to put a number on it, **and that a man who can be found in two places and cannot be taken out of either is a man who is standing behind a figure he has asked not to stand behind, and that the asking was asked for and given and is not a withdrawal.**

A man of about fifty-six said that a withdrawal reaches forwards and not backwards and that a man who asked not to be in a list in the sixth of the second month has asked a question that a list cannot answer, **and that the district is not going to strike the fifth of the five because a man who put it there said no in four seconds, and that the man who put it there is the only man who can take it off and has not and will not be asked again.**

Nobody took that up and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said in the yard in January that nobody in this yard is going to ask him a second time, and that this is not a refusal because there is nothing on the page to strike, and that the sentence was said in the fourteenth of January and stands.

**AND THE LIST OF FIVE POSTED SECURITIES IS NOT WRITTEN FOR THE FIFTH TIME IN A MONTH, AND THE REASON IS A MAN WHO CANNOT LIST WHAT HE HAS POSTED, AND THE FIFTH OF THE FIVE IS A MAN WHO STOOD IN A GATE ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THIS MONTH AND SAID NO.**

---

The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace came up the bank at about half past three in the afternoon and had not been told, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him.

He said that there are fifteen days in the spaces and that the page has about forty spaces and that he has done the sum twice in his own head and does not want it done again out loud, **and that a man who does a sum in his own head and will not say it out loud is a man who is frightened of the figure, and he is not a clerk and nobody is going to make him one.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that about forty spaces and sixteen days is about half, and that half is not a rate and is not a column, **and that a page which is half full of days is a page which is going to be full in about three weeks, and that three weeks is a season and not a term and that the two are not joined.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the page will be when it is full, and the man of about fifty-five said that it will be another page, and that there are about nine of them, **and that a book with about nine pages and a heading a man wrote himself in the fourth year is a book and not an instrument and is not one of the six and is not going to be a seventh.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure of sixteen was said by him and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked, **and that a page which is half full of days a man started writing on the fourth of the second month is the only record in this district's business that is being kept by a person who was not asked to keep it and has not been asked to stop.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the last of the four sentences a rule put in a lane on the second of this month is that a district may say out loud that it does not know which day it is and may not say it for anybody else, **and that a man with sixteen days in a drawer in a building four miles down a lane now knows which day every one of those sixteen was and that the saying of that does not move the day and the days he does not have were not there to be moved.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two are not joined and that a man in a drawer is not a district and cannot say anything for anybody, **and that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is eighty-seven and the count of days in a page in a building is sixteen, and that the two figures are in two places and are not added.**

**AND THERE ARE FIFTEEN DAYS IN THE SPACES ON THE FOURTH PAGE OF A BOOK IN A BUILDING FOUR MILES DOWN A LANE, AND THE PAGE HAS ABOUT FORTY SPACES, AND A MAN WHO HAS BEEN WRITING SINCE THE FOURTH OF THE SECOND MONTH IS NOT A CLERK OF ANYTHING.**

---

The interval was done at about half past four in the afternoon and a man of fifty-six did it and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down and the sum was put under the figure.

"**The thirty-first of March is forty days off today and the first of April is forty-one, and the eleventh of the second month was eight days ago at forty-eight and forty-nine.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is eighty-seven and is a count of days and not a column, **and that the board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for fifty-seven days and that nobody wrote on it on the nineteenth of the second month, and that the count of boards is five and the count of lines is six and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, what the yard is going to do about a man who has put a month on his refusal, and a man of fifty-six said that the yard is going to do nothing about it for nine days, and that doing nothing about it is a decision and is entered as one, **and that a district which decides in a yard on the nineteenth of the second month to leave a man's refusal alone until the first of the next month has made its first decision in this district's business about a date and not about a document.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the district's first decision about a date has been made about a man who will not say a figure, and that the date is the first of a month nobody has named in a board or a book or a letter, **and that a district which has one date it cannot put anywhere and one man whose refusal runs out on a date it has not put anywhere has two of the same thing and that is not a pattern.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two is not a pattern and that she was asked to say whether it is one and said no in four seconds, and entered the count of refusals to join two things as thirteen and her own as twelve, **and that neither moved on the nineteenth of the second month, and that the refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading.**

**AND ON THE NINETEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE YARD DECIDED TO LEAVE A MAN'S REFUSAL ALONE FOR NINE DAYS, AND A DECISION ABOUT A DATE IS THE FIRST DECISION THIS DISTRICT'S BUSINESS HAS MADE ABOUT ANYTHING THAT IS NOT A DOCUMENT.**
