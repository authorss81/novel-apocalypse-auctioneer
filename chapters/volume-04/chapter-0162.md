# Chapter 162: The Seller's Half

---

He said it at about ten in the morning on the fifteenth of January, in the yard, standing by the trestle table, and it was sixteen words and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in his own words and did not count it and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted it afterwards and got sixteen and said it out loud.

"**I am going east on the fifteenth and I will be back when I am back.**"

Nobody asked him when he would be back and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked, **and entered that a man who says he will be back when he is back has not given a day and that a day this district cannot name is a thing this district is used to, and that it is the same fact as the week at the top of a road and not the same fact.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row was going to be told about the going, and that if the going has anything to do with the fourth of the five then the man is the party to it and not the room. and that a man who can be found has never in eleven weeks put a question to that man and has now sent for him twice.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that this is the first time in this district that a man who can be found has gone and got a person, **and that going and getting a person is not the same as asking a person and is not entered as an asking.**

Then the box, and it was on the trestle table where it had been since the second of January, and a man of fifty-six asked for the fourth of the five and not the fifth, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the order those two things were said in.

**AND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF JANUARY THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST SAID IN SIXTEEN WORDS THAT HE WAS GOING EAST ON THE FIFTEENTH AND WOULD BE BACK WHEN HE WAS BACK, AND HE ASKED FOR THE FOURTH OF THE FIVE OUT OF A BOX AND NOT FOR THE FIFTH, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM WHEN HE WOULD BE BACK.**

---

The paper in his hand was not the bond. It was a second paper, thinner, folded twice, and the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row was in the yard and was not sent for and had come on his own account.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody asked him to, **and that a man who has been in a room four times in six days and came on his own account the fifth time is a man who is not waiting to be sent for and has not said so.**

He said that he had never seen that paper in nine years and that he had seen it once, in the ninth year, on a bench in his own forge, **and that the man who put it there had put it face down and had not said what it was, and that a paper put face down on a bench is a paper a man is not being asked about.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the paper over and read it out, and a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice and a man of fifty-six said that rule is not a law and said it out loud before the second reading.

> **BILL OF SALE, ONE DRAY AND ITS TWO SHAFTS, SOLD IN THE FOURTEENTH YEAR. THIS IS THE SELLER'S HALF. THE BUYER'S HALF IS THE RECEIPT, AND A RECEIPT IS THE BUYER'S, AND WHERE THE RECEIPT IS THE SELLER DOES NOT KNOW.**

There is a fourth line on the paper and it is not a sentence. It is a ruled space about the width of two fingers with the word COUNTER over it and nothing in it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that there is a space on the paper and that nothing is in it, **and that the space was ruled by the man who printed the paper and not by the man who sold the dray, and that a space ruled in advance is a space somebody expects to be filled in, and that this one has been empty for ten years.**

---

Then the man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace was asked, in the five things, what a bill of sale is, and gave the answer in a sentence, and the sentence is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-five words.

"**A bill of sale is two halves. The half the seller keeps proves he sold something. The half the buyer keeps proves he paid. Neither half can prove the other one was ever taken away.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that the fourth of the five is discharged on the face of the paper in the fourteenth year and that a thing a paper says on its face about itself is a thing the paper is allowed to say, **and that a man of fifty-six asked who received the payment on the sale on the ninth of January and this yard could not say, and that the answer is in the buyer's half and the buyer's half is not in this district.**

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row said one thing and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-eight words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got thirty-eight and said it out loud.

"**A man came for it in the fourteenth year and paid me money I did not count and said he was buying it for a house, and I have not thought about that sentence for about nine years.**"

The room let it stand and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said the second half of that before anybody asked him for it, and that she is not going to put a number on it, and that a number on it would have been a column and a column is a thing a person is paid for.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who buys a dray for a house has said where the payment went out loud in a forge and did not know he was saying it, **and that a sentence a person says and forgets is still a sentence and that a house thirty-four miles east is the only party in this that can say what it received in the fourteenth year.**

A house can be asked, a house decides, and a district that goes thirty-four miles to ask is doing the only thing this instrument can do, **which is to stand in front of a thing and be refused in front of witnesses, and that is not a failure and is not a hearing and is not one of the four things Adrian may not do.**

A district's whole method is that a person can be made to answer on the record. A house is not a person and cannot be made and does not have to be, **and this is the first party this district has ever gone to that it has no instrument for, and the district went anyway.**

**AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY AT A FORGE END OFF SALT ROW SAID IN THIRTY-EIGHT WORDS THAT A MAN CAME FOR THE DRAY IN THE FOURTEENTH YEAR, PAID HIM MONEY HE DID NOT COUNT, AND SAID HE WAS BUYING IT FOR A HOUSE, AND HE HAS NOT THOUGHT ABOUT THAT SENTENCE FOR ABOUT NINE YEARS.**

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the question himself, at about half past eleven in the morning, and he put it in the six things and he put it to the man of about thirty-one and not to the room, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the form of the asking because the form of the asking is the whole of the finding.

He asked whether he would go east, and the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row said yes in four seconds, and did not give a reason, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the answer and entered that he was not asked why.

"**A MAN ASKED IN THE SIX THINGS WHETHER HE WILL GO TO A PLACE HAS ANSWERED A QUESTION ABOUT HIS BOOTHS, AND A YES TO A WALK IS NOT A CONSENT TO THE NINTH YEAR AND DOES NOT CLOSE IT, AND ASKING A MAN WHETHER HE WILL BE TOLD SOMETHING IS NOT CONSENT AND IS NOT A RELEASE, AND BOTH OF THOSE ARE IN ONE MINUTE AND ARE NOT JOINED.**"

Nobody in the yard said anything. A man of fifty-six said that the ninth of January was the first time anybody in this district had asked that man anything at all, and that a man who has been asked once and answered once and is being asked a second time is a man who is being used, and that the two occasions are not a column.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who is being used and knows it is a person and not a figure. **The room has no instrument for that either, has not looked for one, and is not going to look for one this week.**

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row said that he has a forge and a boy in it and that a cart would be two hours each way, and that he will go on the nineteenth of January and not before, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the day and entered that he named it himself and that nobody named it for him.

**AND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF JANUARY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY AT A FORGE END OFF SALT ROW IN THE SIX THINGS WHETHER HE WOULD GO EAST, AND HE SAID YES IN FOUR SECONDS, AND HE NAMED THE NINETEENTH OF JANUARY HIMSELF AND NOBODY NAMED IT FOR HIM.**

---

He went out to the forge at about one in the afternoon with the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and neither of them said anything for the first half of it, and a clerk of nineteen years was not there and nothing was entered on the road and a man of fifty-six said so out loud in the yard before they got to the gate.

The forge is at the end of an off-lane off Salt Row and there is a boy in it of about eleven who is working a bellows badly, and the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row took the bellows off him and gave it to him back and then let him have it again, and he said that out loud, and nobody asked him why he said it.

He took the seller's half out of a drawer under a bench and put it on the anvil and did not let go of it, and then he let go of it, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man letting go of a paper on an anvil is a man who has decided something about the paper and not about the day.

He said one thing that he had not said in the yard, and it is on a page in the man the figure of twenty-one years is against's own hand because there was no clerk there, and it is thirty-eight words, and nobody has checked it and it is not to be checked.

"**I did not say I would not mind. I have never said a thing about the ninth year at all, and you have been in my yard every week since the fourth of January saying that I have.**"

Nobody answered him. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards, in the lane, that a man has a right to not have said a thing and that a room which has spent nine weeks telling a man what he feels has not been listening, and that this is the third time this district has done that to one person and the three are not a column.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the second half of that on the road, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it a week later and entered that she was told it and not asked for it, and that a reason a man gives against his own advantage in a lane is not a rule and is not written down.

---

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row had one request left from the ninth of January and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had not made it yet and that nobody had asked him for it.

He made it at about a quarter to twelve in the morning, standing by the trestle table with the seller's half of a bill of sale on it, and he said it in his own words and the last twenty-six of them were the same twenty-six he said on the ninth, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-six and said out loud that he had got thirty-seven on the ninth and twenty-six today.

"**I have not been angry about it for nine years, and I want that written down in words that I have not been angry about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered the request and entered that she was asked to write it and did, **and entered that a record of a man not being angry is not a consent and does not close the ninth year, and that he did not say the first eleven words of what he said on the ninth and that she is not going to be asked why he left them out.**

A man of fifty-six said that the district wrote a man's not-being-angry down on the ninth and has read it out since, and that the reading out was the first time this district has used a person's words as an instrument, and that a person's words read out in a market are a document and the person is not in it.

The room let that stand. A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a man who says a thing twice in a week is not a man who is being asked twice, and that the room has asked him about the ninth year four times and about the fourth of five three times and about the walk once, **and that a clerk of nineteen years has not been asked to say which of those is a not-asking and has not been asked and is not going to be.**

---

The registrar of this district's records office was in the yard when he said he was going, and neither of them said anything about it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that they were in the same room at the same hour on purpose for the fourth time since the eighteenth of December, **and that the two of them going to the same place is not a conversation about the two of them and that she is not going to be the person who writes it down as one.**

Tarin Kest was in the yard and was not asked to go and said so before he was asked, and a man of fifty-six entered it, **and entered that Selik Marne is not on a bank's asking and that the agent of a house that guarantees things did not meet anybody from this district in the fourteenth year and has not met anybody in the fifteenth either, and that the two of those facts are a fact and not a column.**

Then the derivation, and a man of fifty-six did it out loud in the yard at about one in the afternoon because a man was going on a road and a road is where a man says a figure out loud so that somebody else can check it, and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down, and the sum was put under the figure.

"**From the eleventh of February there are seventeen days left in that month, and the months from March to August come to one hundred and eighty-four between them, and seventeen and one hundred and eighty-four is two hundred and one.**"

"**Thirty for September and thirty-one for October and thirty for November and thirty-one for December take that to three hundred and twenty-three at the last day of December, and the first of January is three hundred and twenty-four.**"

"**And the fifteenth of January is three hundred and thirty-eight, and there are sixteen days left in the first month after the fifteenth, and eleven days of the second month to the eleventh, which is twenty-seven days from this one to the day the train came.**"

**AND THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHT DAYS ON THE FIFTEENTH OF JANUARY, AND THE COUNT OF REFUSALS TO READ IS NINE, AND THE DEPARTURE IS UNSPENT, AND THERE IS STILL NO DATE IN THE LEDGER FOR THE NEXT READING.**

---

The woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in the yard in the afternoon and was not asked for anything, and she gave the four dates out loud again because a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her for the nearest one and not for the other three.

She said the first of the second month and then said that it is sixteen days off and that she had been carrying water to about ninety houses since the ninth year and that the tank at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace has about two days in it, and that a tank is not a book and a book is not a tank.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure of ninety was said by her and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked, and that the figure of two days was said by her and has not been checked either, **and that a figure a person says about a tank is not a figure this district enters and that the two figures are not added to each other and not added to anything.**

A man of fifty-six said that the first of the second month is a day and not a week this time because somebody has said it out loud in a yard, and that a day said out loud in a yard by a woman who carries water is not the same as a day on a board, **and that the sheet on a nail at the second reservoir says the first week of a named month and not a named day and that a woman with a bucket and a house with a nail are two instruments and the two do not agree.**

Nobody argued and a clerk of nineteen years entered that they do not agree and that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is fifty-three and is a count of days and not a column.

---

The sixteenth of January was the day the yard was short a man and a man of fifty-six noticed it at about eleven in the morning and said so out loud, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and that she was not asked to write it as a finding and did not.

Without him the yard has a clerk and a man who counts and a man who mends fencing, **and about nine people who stand in it, and none of the three can be asked what a thing is by anybody who is not in it.**

Those three have between them a great many words and not one of them can close a transfer, **and a man who cannot close a transfer is not a man who is standing in the way of one either, and the two are not the same and only the first of them is a fact.**

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide was in the yard and said that the sheet is still in her pocket and that the twenty-ninth of December was seventeen days ago and that a house which has not written to anybody can write tomorrow, **and that tomorrow was the fourteenth of January and that she had said it on the fourth and she is saying it again because the day has changed and the sentence has not.**

Nobody took it up and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked to put it in a column and that the count of things she has said in a yard in one month has no number on it, and that a number on it would have been a column.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten was in the yard and said that a man who goes east to ask a house a question has to be back before the first of the second month, and that a house is a building and a building is not a person and does not keep a week.

He said the second half of that before anybody asked him for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of the second half said before being asked stands where it stood on the twenty-ninth of December, and that she has not looked at it since, **and that she is not going to look at it now on a day when a man is thirty-four miles from here and cannot be asked.**

The register's standing question was said out loud in the yard at about four in the afternoon by a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and it was not answered, and the question is the one this district has carried since the thirtieth of December, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in her own words and entered that it has now been said five times and answered none of them.

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a document a district cannot argue with is a document the district has, and a man of fifty-six said that a document a district cannot argue with is also a document a person can be ruined by, **and that the two halves of that sentence are true and that the district has been carrying one of them since the eighth of March and has never once written the other down.**

**AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF JANUARY THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN HAD NO MAN IN IT WHO COULD BE FOUND, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT A ROOM WITH A MAN WHO CAN BE FOUND IN IT HAS SOMEONE WHO CAN BE ASKED WHAT A THING IS, AND THAT A WEEK IS A WEEK AND A HOUSE IS A BUILDING AND DOES NOT KEEP ONE.**
