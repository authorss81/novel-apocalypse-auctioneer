# Chapter 152: A Yard Lends Once a Month

---

The woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide came up the bank on the twenty-fifth of December with a sheet folded in three, and she came at about ten in the morning because the water is high in the morning. She said that before anybody asked her.

She kept the Road House and there was a yard behind it, and the yard was not hers, and a carter stood in it once a month and paid nothing and she had never been thanked for it in nine years of standing in the doorway. **She asked for it to be entered in a book in this district as not a security, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in her words and did not ask her why she wanted it entered.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a carter standing in a yard once a month and paying nothing is a thing a person does for a neighbour and not a thing a person posts, **and that the distinction she is drawing is the same one the district drew in the middle of September about a mark and a job, and that the two are two things and that the yard is not the sheet.**

The woman said that the yard is not the sheet and that she knows it, and she put the folded sheet on the trestle table in the yard of Lot Seventeen and opened it at the fold and turned it so the room could see it. **A clerk of nineteen years entered that she turned it so the room could see it and that nobody asked her to and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.**

A man of fifty-six said that the sheet is four printed things and that it is one sheet and not four, and asked her, in the five things, whether she could read it. She said no, and said the second half before he finished asking: she can count a carter's load of sacks and she cannot read that, and the two are not the same thing and she has never thought they were.

He said the second half before he was asked for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of that is four and is not a column, **and that the four are a clerk of nineteen years, a man of about nineteen, a man of about thirty-four and a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide, and that the four are not joined to each other.**

A man of fifty-six read the four printed things out in the yard at about half past ten in the morning, twice, and a clerk of nineteen years read them back and got nothing wrong, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice.

> **THE UNDERWRITERS OF THE UPLAND GIVE WATER AND SEED BEFORE THE SEASON AND TAKE THE LABOUR OF THE FAMILY AFTER THE SEASON.**
>
> **THE FAMILY SHALL NAME ONE PERSON IN THE BEARER COLUMN. THE NAMED PERSON SHALL NOT BE BELOW FIFTEEN AND SHALL NOT BE ASKED.**
>
> **IF THE FAMILY SHALL NOT PAY ON THE DAY, THE CLAIM OF THE FAMILY TO ONE PLACE IN THE FLOOD SHELTER AT THE SLUICE PASSES TO THIS HOUSE.**
>
> **THIS SHEET IS GIVEN BY THE UNDERWRITERS OF THE UPLAND. IT IS NOT A PROMISER OF A PERSON. THE SEAL IS NOT A NAME.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. Then a man of fifty-six said that the four things are on the face of it, and that this district has spent eleven weeks insisting that a document which says what it does not know is the first one anybody can hold, and that this sheet says what it is, and that a person can hold it and be holding the whole of it.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the other half, and it is in the minute in his own words, and it is twenty-seven words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got twenty-seven both times and did not say so.

"**A hidden term is a thing a person finds. A term on the face of a sheet is a thing a person was supposed to have known.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that this is the finding of the twenty-fifth of December and that it is not a finding about the house, **and that the district's own third form of a notice says on its face that nobody has established what a withdrawal costs, and that a district which insists on that ought to be able to see what a sheet like this one is doing, and that the two instruments are eleven weeks apart and are not joined.**

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in the yard and was not asked for anything. She looked at the sheet on the table for a while and said that there are four lines on it and that a person could say what four lines are for, and that this is four lines and that nobody in the yard can say what the third one is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, and that she carries water to about ninety houses and that the figure of ninety was said by her and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked.

**AND A SHEET OF FOUR PRINTED THINGS WAS PUT ON A TABLE IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN ON THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF DECEMBER AND READ OUT TWICE, AND THE THIRD OF THE FOUR SAYS THAT IF THE FAMILY SHALL NOT PAY ON THE DAY THE CLAIM OF THE FAMILY TO ONE PLACE IN THE FLOOD SHELTER AT THE SLUICE PASSES TO THIS HOUSE, AND THE SECOND OF THE FOUR SAYS THAT THE NAMED PERSON SHALL NOT BE ASKED, AND THE FINDING OF THE MORNING IS THAT A HIDDEN TERM IS A THING A PERSON FINDS AND A TERM ON THE FACE OF A SHEET IS A THING A PERSON WAS SUPPOSED TO HAVE KNOWN.**

---

Nobody asked her why she took the sheet and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked her, and then she told it anyway, and it is in the minute in her own words, and it is thirty-two words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got thirty-two both times and did not say so.

"**We had eleven weeks in October with no seed and no water and I could not write to you, and a man at a counter said yes to both in one morning.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that the month was October and that it is not the month this room is sitting in, and that a person can be asked about a thing that happened in a month the room has since left behind, and that nobody is going to make her say the month again.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the eleven weeks and the October are in her own words and were not checked, **and that a count of eleven weeks has not been made by anybody and that a figure a person says about herself is not a figure this district enters, and that the district's own eleven weeks is a different eleven weeks and the two are not joined.**

The woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said the other half, and it is in the minute in her own words, and it is twenty-four words.

"**I would rather owe it than go without, and I am not going to be asked to be ashamed of that in a yard.**"

The room let it stand and a man of fifty-six entered it in the minute as not speaking for the room, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide is the fourth person in eleven weeks to say a thing in a room that the room did not ask for, and that the four are not joined and that none of the four is a column.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a person who would rather owe it than go without has just described the whole of the market this district is trying to build, and that the trouble is that the sheet does not describe the market, and that the two are two things and that the room is standing in the middle of the difference with its hands empty.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF DECEMBER A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD HOUSE ON THE TIDE SAID THAT SHE WOULD RATHER OWE IT THAN GO WITHOUT AND THAT SHE IS NOT GOING TO BE ASHAMED OF THAT IN A YARD, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID THAT THE TROUBLE IS THAT THE SHEET DOES NOT DESCRIBE THE MARKET.**

---

The second of the four is the one the room could not get past. A man of fifty-six read it out a third time and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked who fills the bearer column, and the woman who keeps the Road House on the tide said she did, on the twenty-second of December, at a counter in a town two days' walk away, and that a man behind the counter put a name in it and read it back to her and she said yes.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the woman was asked in the five things and answered and that she was not asked what her son's name is and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that the boy's name is not in this district's books and that a name is not to be brought in from a counter two days' walk away and put in a minute.**

The woman's son is nine. A man of fifty-six asked her, in the five things, whether the sheet says the person shall not be below fifteen and she said it does, in the second of the four, and that a man behind a counter read that line to her and she said she had a boy and not a person of fifteen and the man said that was all right and put the name in anyway.

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a line that says a person shall not be below fifteen and a counter that fills the column anyway are two things and that the line is the house's and the counter is the counter's, **and that this district has found that a signature from two days' walk away is a signature and not a person who was in the room, and that the two are two things and that neither of them is being joined to the sheet.**

A man of fifty-six said that the sheet has no column for whether the named person was in the room, and stopped, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had said it and stopped, **and that a guild form with twenty-nine columns has no column for whether a person was in the room and that the district found that in September, and that the sheet has four lines and no columns at all, and that the two are two things and are not joined.**

Nobody asked him to finish the sentence. He said the rest of it himself, in a room with about a dozen people in it, and a man of fifty-six asked him whether he wanted it written down, and he said no in four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she asked and that he said no and that the asking is entered and the count is where it was.

A man of fifty-six said that the third of the four is the line the room came for, and read it out again, and the woman of about forty-four who keeps the Road House on the tide said that she had not known what the third line was for when she signed and that she had asked the man behind the counter and he had said it was about the water.

A man of fifty-six asked her what she thought the third line was about and she said she thought it was about the water, **and that she was told it was about the water, and that a person who is told a thing about water and is given a paper about water has been told the truth about the water and not told the truth about the paper.**

Nobody took that up and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a sheet that says what it is not on its own face is better than a sheet that does not, **and that this one says it is not a promiser of a person and that a seal is not a name, and that the two sentences at the foot are the only part of the sheet this district would have written, and that they are the part nobody will read.**

And on the twenty-fifth of december **THE SECOND OF THE FOUR PRINTED THINGS SAYS THAT THE NAMED PERSON SHALL NOT BE BELOW FIFTEEN AND SHALL NOT BE ASKED, AND A MAN BEHIND A COUNTER TWO DAYS' WALK AWAY PUT A NAME OF NINE IN THE COLUMN ANYWAY AND READ IT BACK AND WAS NOT ASKED WHY, AND THE FAMILY SHALL ANSWER FOR THE NAMED PERSON, AND THE LINE IS THE HOUSE'S AND THE COUNTER IS THE COUNTER'S AND THE TWO ARE NOT JOINED.**

---

A man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row was in the yard and had come for a hoop, and he heard the third line read out twice and said one thing about it. A clerk of nineteen years counted the words in it once and got twenty-nine and did not count them again, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-nine and said it out loud.

"**A claim to a place in a building is not owed to a family. A building is not a sack of meal and cannot be sold back to you.**"

The room let it stand. A man of fifty-six entered it in the minute and did not join it to the hundred and eighty-three coppers, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a page with a heading and no name against it is a page about coppers and that a claim to a place in a shelter is a claim to a place, and that the two are two things and that nobody joined them.**

A man of fifty-six asked the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row whether he meant that a shelter cannot be sold, and the man said he meant that it cannot be sold back, and that the difference is one word and that the word is the whole of the finding, and that he is a man who works on wheels and not on buildings and is not speaking for the shelter.

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a shelter is a roof and a floor and a stove and a list, **and that the list is the part that matters, and that a man of about fifty-five keeps the one at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace and has kept it nine years, and that nobody has asked him what a bearer column is and that he would not know.**

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten was not in the yard and was not asked about any of it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that the count of not-askings about one man who digs loam is not a column and has no number on it, because a number on it would have been a column and a column is a thing a person is paid for.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in the yard for about forty minutes and read the four printed things twice and said nothing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said nothing and that she was not asked to write that down and did, and that a man who says nothing in a yard is a fact and not a finding.

He said one thing at about eleven in the morning and it is in the minute in his own words, and a clerk of nineteen years did not count it.

"**If I take that sheet and put it up where a stranger can see it, they will know which counter it came from. That is the cost of putting it up and it is the reason nobody has.**"

The room let it stand and a woman of about forty-four who keeps the Road House on the tide said that he had said the thing she had come to say and had said it before she had to ask him, **and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had wanted to say it and had not and that this is the thirtieth time and that the two are not joined.**

Then a man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace came into the yard at about a quarter to twelve, having been sent for by a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel, who had told him there was a line about his building on a piece of paper.

He was told the third of the four printed things. He read it himself, twice, with his thumb on the line, and then he said that he keeps a book and that the book has four pages and that the fourth page has a column headed for the name of a person who is in the building, and that the column is nine years old and has about forty names in it and that he has never once been told what to call it.

A man of fifty-six asked him what he calls it and he said that he calls it the page, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the answer, and a man of fifty-six said that a man who keeps a column of names for nine years and calls the column the page is a man who has been doing a thing this district spent four batches naming, and that he has been doing it in a building and not in a yard and nobody has come and looked at it.

Nobody asked him why he had never said. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked, and that she is the fourth person in this district to be entered as not speaking for a room, **and that a man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace has not been asked about his book in nine years and that the nine years was said by him and that it is not a column.**

He said one thing about the third of the four and it is in the minute in his own words, and it is thirty words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got thirty and did not count them again.

"**If a name of mine goes down as a payment, that is a person going out of my door and I am the one who has to look at it.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that a man who keeps a book of names for nine years has just described what a bearer column is, and that he described it in thirty words on the twenty-fifth of December, **and that the district took four batches and a page of six columns and a clerk of nineteen years to arrive at the same sentence and that the two are two things and that neither of them is a finding.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF DECEMBER A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE WHO KEEPS THE FLOOD SHELTER AT THE SLUICE END OF A LANE OFF THE LOWER TERRACE READ THE THIRD OF THE FOUR PRINTED THINGS TWICE AND SAID IN THIRTY WORDS THAT IF A NAME OF HIS GOES DOWN AS A PAYMENT THAT IS A PERSON GOING OUT OF HIS DOOR AND HE IS THE ONE WHO HAS TO LOOK AT IT, AND HE HAS KEPT THE COLUMN FOR NINE YEARS AND HAS NEVER BEEN TOLD WHAT TO CALL IT.**

And on the twenty-fifth of december **THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST SAID THAT A SHEET PUT UP WHERE A STRANGER CAN SEE IT WOULD TELL THE HOUSE WHICH COUNTER IT CAME FROM, AND THAT IS THE COST OF PUTTING IT UP, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD HOUSE ON THE TIDE HAD COME TO SAY IT AND HAD NOT.**

---

The twenty-sixth of December was the day the sheet went back in her pocket, and the day a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked a question in the yard that nobody answered, and the question is in the minute in his own words.

"**How many have you posted?**"

He asked it of the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, and not in the six things, and not at a counter, and in a yard with a dozen people in it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked in a yard and not in the six things and that the two are two things and that the asking is not entered in any column.

The man who can be found said he would find out. A man of fifty-six said that a man who sells on a tram arch has this district's figure on a board somewhere in his own hand with his own price against it and will not say where he got it, **and that the two men have been asked the same question in two yards in two months and neither of them has answered it, and that the two are not joined and that neither answer is owed to anybody.**

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said that she is not asking it for herself, and gave the reason in a sentence, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in her own words.

"**I am asking it because a stranger cannot look at yours and can look at theirs.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that a security in this district is a thing a stranger can go and look at, **and that none of the five this man has ever posted is a sum, and that one of the five was a day a week and three bound copies of a book, and that one of the five was a person, because the district has no word for what that is and does not have one on any sheet.**

He said the first half of that and not the second half, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he stopped, and that she was not asked to write the half he did not say down, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF DECEMBER A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED IN A YARD HOW MANY THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST HAS POSTED, AND HE SAID HE WOULD FIND OUT, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT NONE OF THE FIVE IS A SUM AND THAT ONE OF THE FIVE WAS A DAY A WEEK AND THREE BOUND COPIES OF A BOOK, AND HE STOPPED IN THE MIDDLE OF THE FIFTH.**

---

The train on the siding had stood three hundred and seventeen days on the twenty-fifth of December and three hundred and eighteen on the twenty-sixth, and a man of seventeen wrote both figures on one line in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and beside the line there are now six dates and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date.

A clerk of nineteen years read Ivo Stenn's separate sheet out in the yard on the twenty-sixth because a minute is read out, and read it back after the ledger as she reads it every time, **and got twenty as printed and nineteen as called, and entered both figures, and entered that the count of refusals to read is nine and did not move and that the departure is unspent and that there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading.**

A man of fifty-six said that the fourth of the four days on the board was spent on the twenty-fourth and that the board outside the room was not washed, **and that a room whose only instrument runs out on a date is a room a clerk can end, and that the clerk has said she is not going to, and that a person and not a rule, and that the two are two things and that the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth are two of the days nobody has entered what the room did with.**

Nobody argued and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two days are unentered and that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is thirty-two, **and that the thirty-two is a count of days and not a column and that the days are not added to the askings and the askings are not added to the days.**

And the woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide went back down the bank on the twenty-sixth of December with the sheet in her pocket, **AND THE SHEET IS NOT IN ANY BOOK IN THIS DISTRICT, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT IT IS NOT TO BE ENTERED, AND THAT A SHEET A PERSON CANNOT READ IS A THING A DISTRICT CAN LOOK AT AND CANNOT ACT ON, AND THAT THE COUNT OF THINGS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE IS FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE.**
