# Chapter 198: A Place With Three Claims On It

---

The sluice went up at about six in the morning on the twenty-fourth of the third month and four men had their shoulders under the iron lip of it by about ten past, and the man who digs loam was on the frame side because it is his arm, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing took the other end because he turned up and nobody sent for him, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against had a bucket and was in the channel to the knee inside a quarter of an hour.

Tarin Kest came at about seven and did not put his shoulder under anything. He stood on the bank above the frame with his hands in his coat and watched the water and said nothing at all for the better part of two hours, **and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam said afterwards that a route keeper standing on a bank saying nothing is the only person at a sluice who is not doing the work and is also the only one nobody can pay.**

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray came at about half past seven and stood at the post and did not go past it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him to move and that the not-asking goes in and the count is where it was.

The water came at about eight and took until about two in the afternoon to go down the mile and a bit of channel, and the channel was loud the whole way, and there was no rain on it and the loam under the frame went to a slurry about a foot deep and had to be crossed twice.

It was at the top of the lane at about eleven and a half that the man of about fifty-five found out what a sluice does for a tank.

The tank at the back of the building came up to the shoulder of the stone in about four hours and stopped there with the crack still open in it, and it has been weeping steadily since the fifteenth of the third month, and the water is going out of the bottom of the tank at about the rate it is coming in, and neither figure is a term and neither has anybody standing under it.

He put his hand flat on the stone the way he does and gave the figure out himself without offering it to anybody, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that no check was asked of him and that she is not going to ask him for one. He said in one run of words that a man of about nineteen counted and got forty-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have wanted the water to come out of the ground by myself since the fourth year and it has come down a channel, and I am not going to be a man who is pleased about it in a lane.**"

Nobody in that lane said anything about it for a moment. A man of fifty-six said that a man who has said the same want out loud twice in a fortnight has said it twice and not once, and that a tank at about nine days for a building with about forty people in it is a figure a man said with his hand on a stone, **and that the mend in the crack came out on the fifteenth and has not been put back and is not going to be put back and the reason he gave on the tenth of the third month has not changed and is not going to be asked about a second time.**

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a crack that is weeping with the tank full is a different sentence from a crack that is weeping with the tank at a day and a half, and that the district has been reading the second one for a month, **and that a mend in a stone is not a way of saying where a toll lands on a place and is not the fifth of the five things this district does not have, and the count of five is the same five it was on the second of January.**

The woman of fifty-eight came up the bank at about half past ten with a yoke and two buckets and she was not asked for anything, and she put the yoke down on the stones at the top of the hill, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her why she had put it down, and she said the reason before he had finished the question and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the second half of it came out before the question was finished and that she is not going to count the order.

"**The water is coming down a channel and I am not needed on this hill today and I do not know what I am going to do tomorrow and I have not got to anything about it.**"

Nobody contradicted her. A man of fifty-six said that a woman who has carried four yoke-loads a day up a hill for nine years has just been told by a sluice that her round is over, **and that a woman who is told that on a wet morning by a man she has never spoken to is a person and not a column and is not a protected thing and is not one of the four conditions with no end on it and the count of four did not change.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the woman of fifty-eight was not asked a second question and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a round of about ninety houses that ends on the twenty-fourth of the third month because a water came down a channel is not a figure and is not a rate and nobody has written down what a round is worth on any day it was being done.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A SLUICE FOUR MILES OUT WAS UP FROM ABOUT SIX IN THE MORNING UNTIL ABOUT TWO IN THE AFTERNOON WITH FOUR MEN ON AN IRON LIP AND A ROUTE KEEPER ON A BANK ABOVE IT SAYING NOTHING, AND A TANK FOUR MILES DOWN A LANE CAME UP TO THE SHOULDER OF THE STONE AND STOPPED, AND A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT PUT A YOKE DOWN ON THE STONES AND DID NOT PICK IT UP AGAIN, AND NOBODY ASKED HER WHY.**

---

The man of about thirty at the counter came down the eleven mile road for the ninth time in eight weeks and he came at about one in the afternoon and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him.

A sheet was laid out on the trestle table in the yard of Lot Seventeen with its face upward, and a clerk of nineteen years read it, because a sheet is read out, and it took her the better part of a minute and nobody in that yard interrupted her.

> **THE CLAIM NAMED IN THE THIRD OF THE FOUR PRINTED THINGS HAS PASSED TO THE UNDERWRITERS OF THE UPLAND. THE DAY NAMED AT THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET IS THE TWENTY-NINTH OF DECEMBER AND IT IS THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THE THIRD MONTH.**
>
> **THE WATER NAMED IN THE ROTA DID NOT GO WHERE THE ROTA NAMED IT. THE HOUSE SAYS THAT IS THE WHOLE OF THE REASON AND THE HOUSE WILL NOT GIVE ANOTHER.**
>
> **THE CLAIM IS AGAINST ONE PLACE IN A FLOOD SHELTER AT THE SLUICE. THE SHEET DOES NOT NAME A BUILDING AND DOES NOT NAME A FAMILY.**

The man of about thirty at the counter said the other half of it himself, and he said it in the voice of a man reading off a counter, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-three.

"**The claim has passed. It passed on the day at the foot of the sheet and the water did not go where the rota names it, and I have not come to argue with you about which of those two things caused it.**"

Nothing in that yard picked it up. A man of fifty-six said that a house which puts two facts on a sheet and then declines to say which of them did it has built a thing it cannot be examined on, **and that a claim that cannot be examined is a claim that cannot be refused, and that the district proved the breach on the seventeenth of the third month and has been holding the breach and the claim has both gone past it in a fortnight.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, in front of about nine people, which place the claim is against, and the man of about thirty said he was not going to talk about which place and gave no reason at all.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked once and gave no reason, **and that this is the sixth refusal with no reason a clerk of a house has given in this district's business and that six refusals with no reason are six and are not a column and are not added to the thirteen, and the refusals to join two things are the same thirteen and the registrar's own count is the same twelve.**

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the other half of it, and he said it flatly, and he said it before anybody asked him.

"**We are two buildings about four hundred yards apart and the sheet names neither of them, and the woman of about thirty-eight has one place and it is on the sheet somewhere, and a place with a claim on it she cannot see is a place she cannot defend.**"

The man of about thirty at the counter said that the house does not name a place because a place is not a party and a party is not a building, and that the sheet is correct in every figure on it, and he said the second half of that before he was asked for it, **and that a clerk entered that the second half came out ahead of the asking and that she is not going to put a number on it.**

Nobody said the opposite. A man of fifty-six said that the man of about thirty has now told this district twice what his house is able to look at, which is a column and a day, and that a column and a day do not name a place and never have, **and that the fifth thing this district does not have is a way of saying where a toll lands on a place and a sheet that will not name a place is not a way of saying it and is not a sixth.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was going to do about it, and a man of fifty-six said that the yard was going to do nothing about it in a yard and gave the reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-six.

"**The water came and the tank is at the shoulder of the stone and the claim has passed and both of those happened on the same day in front of about nine people, and a district that cannot do the first one and undo the second has not got a remedy and has got a bill.**"

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THE THIRD MONTH THE CLAIM NAMED IN THE THIRD OF THE FOUR PRINTED THINGS PASSED TO THE UNDERWRITERS OF THE UPLAND, AND THE SHEET DID NOT NAME A BUILDING AND DID NOT NAME A FAMILY, AND THE WATER CAME DOWN A CHANNEL ON THE SAME DAY, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-ONE STANDS AT A POST FOUR MILES OUT AND THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD FOUR HUNDRED AND SIX DAYS.**

---

The question was answered at about four in the afternoon and it was answered by a boy of nine who was in the yard and had not been sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody sent him, and that nobody asked him where the stub came from, and that she is not going to be either.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked it out loud in the six things and in front of about nine people, and he had asked the same question in four yards in a fortnight, and nobody had answered it in any of them.

The boy of nine answered it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Nobody put my name on that board and I would not want them to, and a board is for dates.**"

The gate of the yard stayed shut for about nine seconds and nothing in that yard said anything to it. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a person who has been in a bearer column in a building two days' walk away since the twenty-ninth of December has just been asked a question about a board and has answered it correctly in twenty words, **and that a board is a place for dates and four people have put four dates on this one and the last of them is a spent one, and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty and is going to stay ruled and empty.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the answer was given by a person and that a person who answers a question is not a clerk of anything and is not on a list, **and that a boy of nine who has not been asked a second thing is not a count and is not a column and the asking of him was a thing four people did and the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.**

A man of fifty-six said that the answer was right and that a district which has run out of other kinds of counting has been given the best answer available to it by a person nine years old, **and that the boards number five and the lines number six and a sheet on a trestle table and a page in a book in a building and a list on the back of a page are all things that are not boards, and none of them is a seventh instrument.**

Then the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray said the thing he had come for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked what he had come for and that she is not going to ask him.

"**I have been on a page in this district for nine years and the fifth of five is me, and the boy is right about the board, and I am telling you that a name does not get to be on a board and neither does a person.**"

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THE THIRD MONTH THE QUESTION ASKED IN FOUR YARDS IN A FORTNIGHT WAS ANSWERED BY A BOY OF NINE IN TWENTY WORDS AND THE ANSWER WAS THAT A BOARD IS FOR DATES, AND THE FIFTH LINE WAS NOT WRITTEN ON AND THE SEVENTH AT THE NINTH PLACE IS STILL RULED AND EMPTY.**

---

The twenty-fifth was the butt at the back of the brick building and it was still out and nobody had put anything in it.

The channel four miles out is running and it runs past the back wall of that building in about nine hundred yards of its length, and a butt is a store and not a source, and the water in the channel is about two feet deep in a place where a man of about thirty-one who digs loam can stand with his boots wet.

She was at the hoop of wood with her hands in the water when they came down the four hundred yards and she came out into the yard when she heard the gate and shut the gate behind her, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody knocked and that nobody asked her a first thing and is not going to be asked a second.

She said the thing she had come out for and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-seven.

"**My butt is at nothing and the channel is running past my back wall and I am not going to send a man to a man who digs loam about it. Write that down or do not.**"

Nobody in that yard argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that a woman who has worked out that a store is not a source in four years of carrying water into a building by hand has worked out in one wet week what this district has spent a month not writing down, **and that nobody in this district has entered a single thing she has said in a yard or at a gate as a finding and is not going to.**

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that he emptied that butt himself on the night of the thirteenth of the third month and that the water in it went down the same channel and came back past her wall this week, **and that a man who empties a butt and a woman whose butt it was are two people and the district is the reason they are in the same conversation and that is entered and is not asked about further.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in the six things, in front of about fourteen people, whether she was going to tell the man of about thirty at the counter that the channel was running past her wall, and she said no in about two seconds and gave the reason herself and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-six.

"**He has a sheet about a place and I do not. If I tell him where my wall is then my wall is on his sheet.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the refusal was with a reason and that a refusal with a reason is a different instrument from a refusal without one, **and that the six refusals with no reason are six and were six on the twenty-fourth and are six this afternoon and the count is where it was, and a person who refuses a thing for a reason that is a finding about herself is not a person who has been made into a column.**

The two households were still in the lane. Nobody sent for them and nobody in this district asked either of the four of them anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the four are not four addresses and that a household which has been asked nothing and has said nothing has not refused and cannot be counted either way and the record says not asked.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A BUTT AT THE BACK OF A BRICK BUILDING AT THE SLUICE END WAS AT NOTHING WITH A CHANNEL RUNNING PAST THE BACK WALL, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT REFUSED TO TELL A CLERK OF A HOUSE WHERE HER WALL WAS, AND THE TWO HOUSEHOLDS IN THE LANE WERE ASKED NOTHING BY ANYBODY.**

---

The twenty-sixth was the second day of the schedule a route keeper gave on the nineteenth and he gave two days and did not give a figure, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a schedule is not a figure and that the two are not joined and have never been joined in this district.

The water came down the same channel at about six in the morning and went down it until about two in the afternoon, and the tank at the top of the lane did not rise at all, and the man of about fifty-five said that a tank which does not rise when a sluice is open for eight hours is a tank and a sluice that are not connected by anything anybody can see, and he said the figure of about nine days again and asked nobody to check it.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that the fourth of the nine holdings is the top field of the farm four miles out and has not taken a drop since the first of the second month, **and that two pieces of water four miles apart are two pieces of water and are not a sum, and that the count of the nine holdings is the same four states it was on the tenth of the third month and the ninth of the nine is the one nobody can account for.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, whether anybody was going to go back and ask the man of about thirty at the counter about the ninth holding, and the man of about thirty was still in the district and was not sent for and was not told about a tank.

A man of fifty-six gave the interval out loud at about half past four in the afternoon, in a lane with a butt at the end of it, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote it down, and the sum went under the figure the way it always goes.

"**The thirty-first of March is five days off this day and the first of April is six, and the first of April is the last day a person may put their hand to a notice, and the water came on the twenty-fourth and the twenty-sixth and there are two days behind it and nobody has written down what a day of a water costs anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody wrote on a board on the twenty-fourth, the twenty-fifth or the twenty-sixth, and that the boards number five and the lines number six and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty, **and that the train on the siding had stood four hundred and eight days on this day and that the days since the twenty-fourth of November that nobody has entered are counted by a clerk and are not said out loud and are not a column.**

The fifth page in the book four miles down a lane has four days in it and a ruled column about a finger wide and no heading at all, and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that the ruling is the best in the district and that this is not a finding, **and that a column a man rules for himself and writes no heading over is not an instrument and is not a page and is not a board and the count of six did not change on the twenty-sixth of the third month.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A WATER CAME DOWN THE SAME CHANNEL FOR THE SECOND DAY OF A SCHEDULE OF TWO DAYS AND A TANK DID NOT RISE, AND A FIFTH PAGE IN A BUILDING FOUR MILES DOWN A LANE HAS FOUR DAYS IN IT AND NO HEADING, AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS FIVE DAYS AWAY.**
