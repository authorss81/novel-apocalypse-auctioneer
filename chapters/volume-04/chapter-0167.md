# Chapter 167: The First of the Second Month

---

The month turned on the first of February and the month before the turn was January and the month before that was December, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the three of them in one line and did not join them, and the year that began on the first of January is the eighteenth year after the Long Fracture and did not begin again.

The board outside the room carried the twenty-fourth of December and had carried it for thirty-eight days, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a board which has carried a spent date for thirty-eight days is a thing people have stopped seeing, **and that a thing people have stopped seeing is still on the wall, and that the fourth of the four is the only date on it anybody can argue with and he has not argued with it and is not going to.**

A clerk of nineteen years said that nobody had asked her to write a fifth date on the first of February and that she had not offered, **and entered that she is not going to be asked on a day either and that a day is the sort of thing a person offers and does not have to be given.**

**AND THE MONTH TURNED ONCE AND IT TURNED ON THE FIRST OF THE SECOND MONTH, AND THE MONTH BEFORE IT WAS JANUARY, AND THE YEAR THAT BEGAN ON THE FIRST OF JANUARY IS THE EIGHTEENTH YEAR AFTER THE LONG FRACTURE, AND THE BOARD CARRIES FOUR DATES AND NO FIFTH AND HAS CARRIED THE FOURTH FOR THIRTY-EIGHT DAYS.**

---

The water came in the night and the woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace at about seven in the morning on the first of the second month with a bucket in each hand, and she came up the bank at about half past nine and said that the tank is full and that it is the first time in nine years she has not had to fill it.

Nobody argued. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the tank at the back of the flood shelter was full on the first of the second month and that the figure of nine years was said by her and has not been checked, **and that a tank filling is a fact about water and not a finding about a document, and that the two have never been in one line in any book this district has read.**

A man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace was in the yard at about ten in the morning and he had not been told and had walked up, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him.**

He said that the tank was full and that he does not know whether he is allowed to be pleased about it, and a man of fifty-six asked him why not, and he gave the reason in a sentence and it is in the minute in his own words and it is forty-four words.

"**I have got about sixty people in this room in the cold and nobody has ever written down whose water it is, and I have been nine years being a man who keeps a building and not being a man anything is owed to.**"

**AND ON THE FIRST OF THE SECOND MONTH THE TANK AT THE BACK OF THE FLOOD SHELTER AT THE SLUICE END OF A LANE OFF THE LOWER TERRACE WAS FULL, AND A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT WHO CARRIES WATER SAID IT WAS THE FIRST TIME IN NINE YEARS SHE HAD NOT HAD TO FILL IT, AND NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAD NAMED THE DAY IT HAPPENED ON.**

---

The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace was asked, in the five things, at about eleven in the morning, what he wanted, and he said that he wanted somebody to write down that the tank is his, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked to write it and did not and gave the reason in a sentence, and it is thirty-three words.

"**A tank is a tank. If it is his he can be asked about it and if it is nobody's he cannot be asked about it and he has nine years of nobody's.**"

It was not argued with. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who asks for a thing to be written down and is told it cannot be is a man who has been told the truth about a building for the first time in nine years, **and that a tank is not a place in a building and the building does not sell places, and that a man who owns a tank has a thing and a man who keeps a place has a page, and that the two are two things.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about fifty-five asked to have a tank entered as his and that it is not entered, and that the not-entering is not a refusal of the man and is not a finding against him, **and that a clerk is a person and not a rule and that the two facts are in one line on purpose and that she wanted them on two lines again and did not do it.**

The man of about thirty at the counter at the second reservoir came down the road again on the first of the second month, for the third time, and he had walked it and had his book under his arm, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.**

He opened the book on the trestle table at the ninth holding and read the fourth line out twice, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong.

> **THE FOURTH HOLDING IS A FARM FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS. THE FIGURE AGAINST IT IS ENTERED. THE HAND IS ENTERED. THE HAND IS THE CLERK OF THIS HOUSE AND THE CLERK IS NOT ASKED WHAT IT IS FOR.**

Nobody argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that the fourth line of the ninth holdings in a delivery book eleven miles up a road carries a clerk's hand in a column headed for the person who signs, and that the third of the four sentences on the sheet says that the person who signs is not asked what it is for, **and that a house which signs for its own fourth holding and does not ask its own clerk has found the only arrangement in this basin that nobody can be hurt by, and that the reason nobody can be hurt by it is that the farm has nobody on it.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fourth holding is a farm with a house and four fields and a sluice of its own, and that the person named in the entry of the registry of the western tide is the owner, and that the address of the person named is one house at a crossing, **and that a register is a register and not a mystery, and that a column signed by a clerk for a farm is not a person in that column and is not a bearer and is not a holder.**

Then the question, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked it and it is the only question he asked in three days, and the man of about thirty at the counter refused it in about four seconds and gave no reason at all.

"**Is the fourth a holding, or is it a second copy of the house's own?**"

The man of about thirty at the counter said no and gave nothing else, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked in the yard and not in the six things and that he refused, **and that a refusal with no reason is not a refusal of a rule and is not entered as one, and that the two of those are two things and that this district has a word for the second and not for the first.**

**AND ON THE FIRST OF THE SECOND MONTH THE FOURTH LINE OF THE NINE HOLDINGS IN THE DELIVERY BOOK AT THE SECOND RESERVOIR CARRIES THE HAND OF A CLERK OF THE HOUSE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED WHETHER THE FOURTH IS A HOLDING OR A SECOND COPY OF THE HOUSE'S OWN, AND WAS REFUSED IN FOUR SECONDS WITH NO REASON GIVEN.**

---

Tarin Kest came down the road at about two in the afternoon on the first of the second month and had been up it at about two in the morning, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he went and was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

He said what came down and it is in the minute in his own words and it is forty-five words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got forty-five and said it out loud.

"**A head race a mile and a bit filled from the top in about four hours and went out to eight of the nine holdings and stopped where the farm is, and I stood in the mud at the fourth one until my boots went.**"

Nobody took it up. A woman of about fifty-two who has the seventh holding at the top of a lane off the Verge and has had it since the fourth year came down with him and said that her part of it was full at about half past four the previous afternoon and that she has not had to open a gate since, **and that a woman who has not had to open a gate in nine years says the sentence out loud in a yard because nobody has ever asked her what a delivery is.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the seventh of the nine holdings is a farm at the top of a lane off the Verge and that the person who has had it since the fourth year is a woman of about fifty-two, **and that a delivery is a thing a house's clerk signs for and a thing a person opens a gate for, and that the two have never been on the same page in this district's books and are not joined.**

A woman of about fifty who is the water at the ninth place was in the yard and was not asked for anything, and a man of fifty-six said that the count of uses of a board has now been made seven times in about five weeks and has given seven numbers and that it has not moved since the first of January because nobody has written on a board since the thirty-first of December.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of boards is five and the count of lines is six and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty, **and that a board which has not been written on for a month is a board and not an instrument, and that the woman of about fifty who is the water at the ninth place made the last of the seven counts and is not paid and was not asked to be right.**

A man of fifty-six said that the first of the second month was a week and not a day until the morning of it, and that it has now been a day for about eleven hours, **and that a week is a thing a district can hold and a day is a thing that has already happened, and that a district which has been counting in weeks has been spared eight days of finding out what a day does.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether the eight holdings were signed for, and Tarin Kest said that six of them were and that two were not and that the two are the farm and the seventh, **and that the seventh has been a woman's for nine years and that a house's clerk signs for a woman's farm because the house says it does, and that the two are not joined and that he was not going to say anything else about them.**

Nobody argued and a man of fifty-six entered that two holdings out of nine were not signed for and that the figure of two is the figure the man of about thirty at the counter gave out of his own book, **and that a count of holdings is a count and not a column and that nobody in this district is going to be sent for to sign a column that is not a column.**

---

The interval, and a man of fifty-six did it in the yard at about one in the afternoon and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down, and the sum was put under the figure the way it always is.

"**Twenty-seven days left in the second month after the first, then the whole of the third month to its last day. Twenty-seven and thirty-one is fifty-eight.**"

"**And to the first of April it is twenty-seven and thirty-one and one, which is fifty-nine.**"

That was not argued with and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the district had a seventy-six and a seventy-seven on the fourteenth of January and has a fifty-eight and a fifty-nine on the first of February, and that four figures for two dates in four weeks is four figures, **and that a man who says a figure for the wrong interval has been handed a figure and not an interval, and that nobody in this district has yet handed anybody the right one and said which it was.**

A man of fifty-six said that the thirty-first of March is fifty-eight days from the first of February and the first of April is fifty-nine, and that the two dates are named in three rooms and carried on two forms, and that a clerk of nineteen years has them on a ledger page with a line under them and nothing under the line, **and that the nearest of the two is now a season and not a fortnight, and that a season is not a date and the district has two.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is sixty-nine and is a count of days and not a column, **and that the train on the siding had stood three hundred and fifty-five days on the first of the second month, and that a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and that beside the index there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date.**

**AND ON THE FIRST OF THE SECOND MONTH THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS FIFTY-EIGHT DAYS AWAY AND THE FIRST OF APRIL IS FIFTY-NINE, AND THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIVE DAYS, AND THE COUNT OF REFUSALS TO READ IS NINE, AND THE DEPARTURE IS UNSPENT, AND THERE IS STILL NO DATE IN THE LEDGER FOR THE NEXT READING.**

---

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked a question in the yard at about half past three in the afternoon and nobody answered it and it went into the minute with nobody's name against it, and it is in his own words and it is thirty-three words.

"**A woman carries a bucket for nine years and a man fills a tank for nine years and neither of them has ever been asked whose water it is, and what is that.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that it is a practice and that a practice is not an instrument and that the count of instruments built and not named is six, **and that a bucket and a tank and a yoke and a page are four things a person can go and look at and that none of the four is one of the five things this district does not have and that a clerk of nineteen years has been asked to put them in a column and has not.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked to put them in a column and that a column is a thing a person is paid for, and that a woman of fifty-eight and a man of about fifty-five are not paid and were not asked to be, **and that not asked is in the count and the count is where it was and that the four are not a column and are not added to the count of not-askings about anybody in particular.**

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures said the second of her four dates out loud because a man of about thirty-four asked her for the nearest one, and it is the sixteenth of the second month, **and she said it is the day the seed is due at the Road House on the tide, and that it is fifteen days off and that fifteen is a figure and that a figure is a thing she can carry and not a thing she can put in a book.**

Nobody took that up and a man of fifty-six said that the nearest of the four on the bucket has changed and that the last of the four is the last day of the third month and that the flood was on that day of the ninth year, **and that three of the four are other people's business and one of them is this district's, and that the one which is this district's is the furthest of the four and has not moved since the twelfth of January.**

The last of the day was the shelter and the page, and the man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace said that he had a full tank and four pages and about forty names and nothing in any of the spaces, and that a full tank is not a claim on a place in a building and is not nothing.

He said one more thing at about four in the afternoon and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in his own words and it is thirty-nine words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got thirty-nine and said it out loud.

"**Nine years I have written a man's name on that page when he came in and never once written the day, and I did not know that until a man stood in my lane and read it upside down.**"

Nobody argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that a day a person arrived is not a figure and is not coppers and is not a year, and that a page with forty names on it and no day against any of them cannot say when a building was full, **and that the tank has been full since the first of the second month and the page cannot say whether it was full on the twenty-ninth of December, and that the third of the four printed things turns on a day and the day is in a corner in a hand that is not the house's.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the finding of the first of the second month is that a shelter's page has no days in it and that a claim on a place in that shelter turns on a day, and that the two are a finding and not a remedy, **and that nobody in this district has entered the day of anything on a page kept by a person in a building and that the heading of that page says a person is entered on the day they arrive and not on the day they leave.**

Nobody argued. A man of fifty-six said that a place in a building has no price in this district and cannot be sold because the building does not sell places, **and that a tank is not a place and is not a price and is not a security, and that a man who has a full tank he cannot refuse and about sixty people he cannot turn away has two of the seven parts of a lot and is short of a bearer, and a lot with no bearer is not a lot.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said that a tank that cannot be refused is a toll with no bearer on it, and that this district spent four months in the autumn finding out what happens to a toll with nobody standing under it, **and that nobody has ever applied any of that to a building, and that a building is not a lot and that is the whole of the difference and it is four years old and not eleven weeks.**

Nobody took that up and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a building is not a lot and that the seven parts of a lot were counted in a field in the eighth month of last year by a guild inspector of forty-four, **and that a building has no bearer and a lot has a bearer and the two are not the same instrument, and that this district has never had a page for a building and the count of six did not move on the first of the second month.**

**AND ON THE FIRST OF THE SECOND MONTH A FULL TANK AT THE BACK OF A FLOOD SHELTER IS A TOLL WITH NO BEARER ON IT, AND A BUILDING IS NOT A LOT, AND NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAS EVER WRITTEN DOWN WHO BEARS A SHELTER, AND THE COUNT OF INSTRUMENTS BUILT AND NOT NAMED IS SIX AND THE COUNT OF THINGS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE IS FIVE AND NEITHER MOVED.**
