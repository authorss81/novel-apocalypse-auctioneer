# Chapter 191: Nine Hours

---

He came up the bank at about seven in the morning on the eleventh of the third month and the tank was at about a day and a half, and the man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace had said the day before that he was not going to put loam in the crack a fifth time, and he was not sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody sent for him, that the not-asking is entered, and that the count is where it was.

The mark in charcoal on the board against the wall for the eleventh is a finger below the mark for the tenth, and the man of about fifty-five said the figure himself and asked nobody to check it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked to check it and is not going to ask him again.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam put his hand flat on the stone and said that it is cold all the way down and colder at the top than it was the day before, and that a tank with a cold top on a hill in the third month is a tank with a day and a half in it, **and that the number is a thing you find out with a hand and is not a rate and has nobody standing under it.**

Nothing in the lane said anything about the figure for a moment. A man of fifty-six came down the bank afterwards and said that **a tank down to about a day and a half is a figure and not a term, and that the fourth holding of the nine on a sheet nailed in a shed eleven miles up a road has now not taken a drop of anything for a month and a half.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered the two of those in one line and did not join them, **and entered that she has put both of them in one line more than once and is not going to say how many times, because the number is not a column and a number on it would make it one, and that a tank four miles down a lane and a holding eleven miles up a road are two pieces of water and are not one subject and never have been.**

The man of about fifty-five said the other thing then, and he said it to the man of about thirty-one who digs loam and not to the lane, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him for it and that she is not going to ask him why he said it in a lane at seven in the morning.

"**I do not want a yoke up that hill. I want the water to come out of the ground by itself and I have wanted that since the fourth year and I have never said it out loud.**"

Nobody argued with that. A man of about fifty-six said that a man who has wanted a thing for nine years and has said it once in front of two people on a wet morning has done the whole of what this district is built out of, **and that what the district is built out of is a sentence said out loud in a room, and that a sentence said out loud is not a rule and is not written down and he would not have it written down.**

The man who digs loam said the answer to it himself, and he said it before anybody asked him, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted it and got thirty-four.

"**There is no way for the water to come out of the ground by itself. There is a sluice four miles out past the loams and I can open it in about nine hours.**"

The lane let that stand. A man of fifty-six said that nine hours is a figure a man said with his hand on a stone, **and that a sluice on a farm four miles out with a legal owner who has not been there in about four years is not this district's to open, and that the man who would open it is a man who digs loam nine days in ten and is not a bearer of a farm and is not going to be entered as one.**

It was not contradicted. The man of about fifty-five said that he is not asking this yard for anything and that he is asking the man who digs loam, and that the two of those are different and that the yard is not going to be told it happened.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about fifty-five asked a man in a lane and not a yard, and that nobody in that lane was asked to arrange it, **and that a man who asks another man and not a room is a man and not a decision, and that a decision a yard makes is entered as one and this is not a decision and the count of six did not move on the eleventh of the third month.**

Nobody took that up. The man who digs loam said that he would open it on the night of the thirteenth and shut it at about four in the morning, and that he would go alone, and that if anybody came out to the farm to watch him he would shut it and go home, **and that a man who opens a thing at night and shuts it at night is not a bearer of it in the morning and is not a keeper of it and is not a security and is not going into any of the three counts.**

The man of about fifty-five said the rest of it, and he said it while he was putting the yoke back on its nail, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-two.

"**You will do it and nobody will know and I will not thank you in front of anybody, and that is the arrangement and I am not going to improve on it.**"

The hill did not argue with it. A man of fifty-six said that a man who thanks another man in a lane is a man who has made a record, **and that a man who says in advance that he is not going to thank you is a man who knows what the thanking makes, and that the two of those are the only arrangement available to a district with no money in it.**

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH THE TANK FOUR MILES DOWN A LANE IS AT ABOUT A DAY AND A HALF, A MEND IN A CRACK HAS BEEN IN IT FOR ELEVEN DAYS, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE ASKED A MAN WHO DIGS LOAM AND NOT A YARD.**

---

The woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures came up the hill at about half past seven with a yoke and two buckets and she was not asked for anything, and she said that she has done two and is going to do four and that the fourth is not because anybody asked her and is not because of the tank.

A man of fifty-six entered afterwards that she did the second pair at about eleven and sat on the loam at the top of the hill for about a minute, **and that the only person who saw it was the man of about thirty-one who digs loam, and that nobody in this district was told about it by him and is not going to be, and that the reason is that she would stop coming up the hill.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of fifty-six had been asked about it and had said no, and that she was not asked why.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in the six things, at about half past twelve in the afternoon, whether anybody had told her there was a sluice four miles out, and she said that she had not heard of it and did not want to.

It was not picked up. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a woman who carries four yoke-loads a day up a hill four miles from a yard and does not want to hear about a sluice is a woman with the only opinion about a sluice that has ever been given in this district, **and that an opinion is not a column and is not a not-asking and is not entered as one and she is not to be asked about it again.**

Nobody argued with that. A man of fifty-six said that this district wanted the round to be somebody else's job in the second month and could not have it, and that the round is still hers on the eleventh of the third month and that he said that on the twenty-eighth of the second month and is not going to say it a second time.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman of fifty-eight doing four yoke-loads a day is a supply and not a figure, **and that a supply is not a condition with no end on it and is not one of the four and is not one of the five protected things, and that the count of five did not move on the eleventh of the third month and the count of four did not move either.**

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about fifty-five, in the six things, what he was going to do about the fifth mend, and the man of about fifty-five said that he was not going to do a fifth mend and had said so on the tenth in a yard in front of about nine people, **and that a man who says a thing twice is a man who has a habit and not a finding, and that a habit about a crack in a stone is not a column and is not a rate and is not going into any of the counts in this district's books.**

Then the level, and the man of about fifty-five had gone back to the board against the wall with the charcoal, and the mark for the twelfth was not on it, and he said that he was not going to put a mark for a day he had not had.

Nothing was said to it. A man of fifty-six said that a man who will not put a mark for a day he has not had is a man whose marks are better than anybody's, **and that a board against the back of a building that nobody owns is not one of the five boards in this district and is not a sixth, and that a mark in charcoal on it is a mark that is not cut and is therefore not a mark under this district's own rule, and that a mark may not carry a job.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what a mark on that wall would have to be, and the man of about fifty-five said that it would be a line of charcoal, and that a line of charcoal is drawn and is therefore not a mark, **and that a line of stones is not a mark either, and that he said that on the tenth and is saying it again because nobody has heard it.**

**AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE HAS REFUSED A FIFTH MEND FOR THE SECOND TIME AND HAS LEFT A DAY OFF HIS OWN BOARD, AND A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT DID FOUR YOKE-LOADS AND SAT DOWN ONCE AND WAS TOLD BY NOBODY.**

---

The tank was the rest of the afternoon and it was not a conversation, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nothing was said in that lane for about two hours and that she is not going to write two hours down as a figure.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam went up the bank four times with a yoke because the man who keeps the building could not lift it off the nail, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against went up it with the second bucket, and the man of about fifty-five came out and took the yoke off the hook and carried the second pair himself and did not say anything about it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about fifty-five carrying his own water for about nine hundred yards is not a bearer of the tank and is not a keeper of the building and is not a security, **and that he is a man with a yoke and the count of six did not move on the eleventh of the third month and he is not going to be made a seventh of anything.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a man who has said for nine days that he would rather have a crack than a mend is now carrying his own water and the two of those are the same decision about not being helped, **and that a man who will not be helped in front of a room is a man who has been helped in front of a room, and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was on that hill four times and was not thanked and was not paid and is not in a book.**

A man of fifty-six said the interval out loud at about half past four in the afternoon, and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down, and the sum went under the figure the way it always goes.

"**The thirty-first of March is twenty days off this day and the first of April is twenty-one, and the first of April is the last day a person may put their hand to a notice.**"

The man who digs loam said that the man of fifty-six has now said that sentence in five yards in a month and that a man who says a date out loud once a week is a man with a date, **and that a man with a date is not a man with an instrument and the count of six did not move on the eleventh of the third month and nobody has washed the board outside the room and it has carried the twenty-fourth of December for seventy-seven days.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody wrote on a board on the eleventh of the third month, and that the boards number five and the lines number six, **and that the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty, and that a month is not a reason to put a fifth date on anything and she was not asked for one and did not offer.**

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked one question in the six things at about half past four in the afternoon, and nobody answered it, and it went into the minute with nobody's name against it, and it is in his own words and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-two.

"**The tank is at about a day and a half and a boy's name is in a column, and neither of them is on a board, and the board has four dates on it and the last one is a spent one.**"

The gate of the yard at the head of the lane stayed shut for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that a district which can count twenty days off a date and cannot decide which of two things goes on a board first is a district that has run out of the other kind of counting, **and that the fifth thing this district does not have is a way of saying where a toll lands on a place, and that a tank and a column are two places and are not the fifth thing and are not a sixth.**

The man who digs loam said that the question was asked on the tenth of the third month and nobody has answered it and it is a day old, **and that a day is not long and the district does not have a way of saying that a question is a day old, and that a clerk is a person and not a rule and a person has not been asked to make one.**

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A QUESTION WAS ASKED AGAIN IN THE SIX THINGS AND WAS NOT ANSWED, AND A TANK IS AT ABOUT A DAY AND A HALF, AND THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND NINETY-THREE DAYS.**

---

Then the last of it, and it was not in a yard, and it was at the top of the bank at about five in the afternoon with about four people on the lane and a man of about fifty-five sitting on the stone by the stove pipe with his boots off.

He said that he has been keeping the building nine years and that in nine years he has never once asked anybody for a bucket, and that he asked a man for a sluice on the eleventh of the third month and that is the whole of what has happened in nine years that he wanted.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked about it and that she is not going to ask him what a man wanted.

A man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing came up the bank at about half past four with his hands empty and had not been sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody sent him and that she is not going to ask him for his reason.

He said that he has not been paid eleven coppers a week since the twenty-second of the second month and that he is not going to ask for it again in a yard, and that he wanted to say out loud that he is not going to stop asking for it anywhere, **and that a man who says he is not going to ask in a room is a man who has told the room where he is going to ask, and that he does not know where that is yet and neither does anybody else.**

A man of fifty-six said that there is no money in this district that anybody can pay anybody with and that he said it on the twenty-second of the second month and is not going to be embarrassed about saying it twice, **and that a refusal with the same reason behind it a second time is not a new refusal and is not added to anything and is not a column and is not a rate.**

Nothing was said against that. A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a man who copies for nothing and has just said he is going to keep asking is the only person in this district with a plan, **and that a plan is not an instrument and is not a security and is not a post and the count of six did not move on the eleventh of the third month and he is not going to be made a seventh of anything.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the eleven coppers were refused twice and that the second refusal has the same reason behind it as the first, **and that neither refusal was asked to be refused, and that a person who has not asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way and the record says asked and answered and not asked why.**

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the other half of what the man of about fifty-five had wanted, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A man who keeps a building for nine years and asks nobody is not a man who does not need anybody. He is a man who has found out what asking costs, and the sluice is the first thing he has asked for that anybody can give him.**"

Nobody in that lane argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that a thing a man can be given for nothing is the easiest thing in the world to refuse and the hardest thing to refuse twice, **and that the district refused it once on the twenty-eighth of the second month by deciding to do nothing about a tank, and that a decision to do nothing about a tank is a decision and was entered as one.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the decision was made in a yard on the twenty-eighth of the second month and that the man whose tank it is was not in that yard and was not told, **and that a district which decides about a man's water in a room the man is not in is a district running on a house's terms, and that the second of those is still running and has not been stopped by a man and a yoke and a lane.**

The man of about fifty-five put his boots on and said that he was going to bed and that the man who digs loam knew where the sluice was, and he did not say goodnight to anybody and nobody said it to him, **and that a man of fifty-five going to bed on the eleventh of the third month with his boots off on a stone has not made a request and has not been given a thing and is not the fourth of the nine holdings and is not a protected thing and is not one of the five.**

So the tank at the back of that building is at about a day and a half and a mend that has been in the crack for eleven days is the longest of the four, **and a man is going to open a sluice four miles out on the night of the thirteenth on his own account and shut it at about four in the morning, and the thirty-first of March is twenty days off this day and the first of April is twenty-one.**

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-FIVE SAID THAT HE DID NOT WANT A YOKE UP A HILL AND THAT HE HAD WANTED THE WATER TO COME OUT OF THE GROUND BY ITSELF SINCE THE FOURTH YEAR, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM IS GOING TO OPEN A SLUICE FOUR MILES OUT ON THE NIGHT OF THE THIRTEENTH.**
