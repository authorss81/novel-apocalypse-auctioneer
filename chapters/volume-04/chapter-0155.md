# Chapter 155: The Thirty-First of December

---

The board outside the room had carried the twenty-fourth of December for seven days and the day itself was the thirty-first of December, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said at about ten in the morning that the board was seven days out of date and that nobody had washed it and that the two facts were the same fact.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she had not been asked to wash it and had not offered, **and that the last date on the board was spent in the morning of the twenty-fourth and that the board has now carried a spent date for seven days, and that a board a person can argue with is a board whose last argument has gone out of date, and that the two are two things.**

About nine people came to the room in the morning of the thirty-first of December, and one of them was a woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures, and she was not asked why she came and said she came because she came. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, and that she carries water to about ninety houses and that the figure of ninety was said by her and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked.

Nobody counted the nine out loud. A man of fifty-six said later, in the yard, that a count of what people have come to watch is a column this district does not have and that a count of what people came on a day with no date on the board is a second column this district does not have, **and that the two are two things and that a clerk of nineteen years wanted to enter the first one in November and did not and that he was not going to ask her about it in a yard.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the room met on a day it had no instrument for and that this is the first time in eleven weeks that has happened, **and that a room which meets when it does not have to is a room that is being kept by about nine people and a stove, and that a stove is not a person and that the count of nine is not a finding.**

The finding of the morning is that a day with no instrument on it is a day a person has to come and find out about, and a man of fifty-six said it in the room and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in his own words and read it back and got nothing wrong, **and that the count of entries with a finding in them since the eighteenth of December is three and that the third is his and not hers, and that she entered the count before she wrote the line and not after.**

Nobody argued with the finding and a man of fifty-six said that a day with no date on the board is a day a person has to come and find out about and that a district with two dates left in it and neither of them about a day it has to attend to is a district that has spent its instrument and has not noticed, and that the noticing is the finding and it is two days old.

**AND ON THE THIRTY-FIRST OF DECEMBER THE BOARD OUTSIDE THE ROOM HAD CARRIED A SPENT DATE FOR SEVEN DAYS AND WAS NOT WASHED AND THE ROOM MET ANYWAY, AND THE FINDING OF THE MORNING IS THAT A DAY WITH NO INSTRUMENT ON IT IS A DAY A PERSON HAS TO COME AND FIND OUT ABOUT, AND THE COUNT OF ENTRIES WITH A FINDING IN THEM SINCE THE EIGHTEENTH OF DECEMBER IS THREE.**

---

The carter who stands in the yard behind the Road House on the tide came up the bank at about eleven in the morning on the thirty-first of December, and he is about thirty and he has stood in that yard once a month for nine years, and he came up on his own account and not because anybody sent for him.

He wrote on the back of the second toll board, on a line that was already full, that he stands in a yard once a month and has done for nine years and that nobody has ever written it down, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of lines is six and did not move and that the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty and was not written on, and that a carter standing in a yard is a person and not a line of chalk and that the two are two things.

It worked. A man of fifty-six said that it is the fifth time in about five weeks that a thing written on a board has worked, **and that the four before it were a man losing four days, a woman in a channel, a man whose salt is on nobody's sheet and a woman who is not in a household, and that this fifth one is a carter in a yard, and that the five are five and are not one and are not added to each other.**

A woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide came up on the second day of January and entered him in her own book at the counter of the Road House as a carter who stands in her yard on the second day of every month, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the entry is in her book and not in this district's and that she is not paid for it and was not asked to be and that the instrument is still unnamed and still unowned.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a board is a place a line of chalk goes and that a person who writes on a board five times in about five weeks is not using an instrument and is using a person, **and that a carter who stands in a yard once a month for nine years is a person, and that the fifth use was made by a person and not by an instrument, and that the count of boards is five and did not move.**

Nobody argued and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of uses of a board has now been made seven times in about five weeks and has given seven numbers, and that the woman of fifty who is the water at the ninth place made the last of them and is not paid and was not asked to be right, **and that the seven is not a rate and that a column is a thing a person is paid for and that she is not paid.**

**AND ON THE THIRTY-FIRST OF DECEMBER A CARTER OF ABOUT THIRTY WHO STANDS IN A YARD BEHIND A ROAD HOUSE ON THE TIDE ONCE A MONTH WROTE ON A BOARD THAT HE STANDS IN A YARD ONCE A MONTH AND HAS DONE FOR NINE YEARS AND THAT NOBODY HAS EVER WRITTEN IT DOWN, AND IT WORKED, AND THE COUNT OF LINES IS SIX AND DID NOT MOVE AND THE SEVENTH LINE AT THE NINTH PLACE IS RULED AND EMPTY.**

---

The interval was done twice in the yard after lunch, and a man of fifty-six said both times that the figure of the interval travels with the date it ends on, and that a figure given without an end date is a figure and not an interval, and that the district learned that in the middle of December the hard way.

The first was from the first of January to the thirty-first of March, and a man of fifty-six said the sum out loud and a clerk of nineteen years entered it, and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten was not in the yard and was not asked and had sent word before the month turned that he would start again in January.

"**Thirty days left in the first month after the first, then the whole of the second month, then the whole of the third month to its last day. Thirty and twenty-eight and thirty-one is eighty-nine days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years counted the words in that once and got thirty-six and did not count them again, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got thirty-six and said it out loud and then said it again.

And eighty-nine days is twelve weeks and five days, and a man of fifty-six said that, and then he said the other one, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both and entered that they are two figures and are not joined.

"**Eighty-nine days to the thirty-first of March, and ninety days to the first of April, and the district's own hundred and fifty-one from the first of November in the autumn reaches the first of April and the other three figures in two rooms reach the thirty-first of March.**"

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures said that she calls it three months and two days, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that and entered that the day she counts from is a day in December and is not the first of January, and that a man of fifty-six called the same interval twelve weeks and five days, **and that the two of them are counting from two different days and the two figures are in two places and are not joined, and that a person who is handed a figure for the wrong interval has been handed a figure and not the interval, and that this district has still never handed anybody the right one and told them which it was.**

Nobody took that up and a man of fifty-six said that the thirty-first of March is the day the name comes off the figure and the day the steward's address ends and the day the boundary's term ends and the day the season renews, **and that the four of them are on one line in a charter and are not joined, and that the thirty-first of March is eighty-nine days from the first of January and is a length of time and not a moment, and that a stage is not a moment.**

**AND ON THE THIRTY-FIRST OF DECEMBER A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT EIGHTY-NINE DAYS RUNS FROM THE FIRST OF JANUARY TO THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH AND THAT NINETY DAYS RUNS FROM THE FIRST OF JANUARY TO THE FIRST OF APRIL, AND THAT THIRTY AND TWENTY-EIGHT AND THIRTY-ONE IS EIGHTY-NINE, AND THAT A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT WHO CARRIES WATER AND DOES NOT READ FIGURES CALLS IT THREE MONTHS AND TWO DAYS, AND THE TWO FIGURES ARE IN TWO PLACES AND ARE NOT JOINED.**

---

The slate went past a kitchen in Alder Reach for the sixth time on the thirty-first of December and neither of them wrote on it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that six is a count and that a count of six is not a finding, and that the four sentences in charcoal are under a fifth line in chalk and that the chalk line has been there since the fourth of April, and that she was not told what was in the sentences and did not ask.

Nobody asked her about the sentences. A man of fifty-six said that a slate that has gone past a kitchen six times is a public document and that a slate is not a copy and a copy is not a document and the original is a room, **and that the count of six is the same on the sixth as it was on the fifth and that a count that does not move is a count and not a ladder.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was thirty-four miles east on the second and third of January and has not met the woman whose name is in no register in the west bank, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he is going east and that he was not asked to and that the going is entered and that a going is not a reading and is not entered in the ledger of readings, **and that the count of refusals to read is nine and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading.**

The last entry of the thirty-first of December is eleven lines and it is in the clerk of nineteen years' own hand, and it names the four dates on the board and the fifth use of a board and the interval with its end date and the count of three findings, **and it does not say what the year was, and a man of fifty-six asked her why and she gave the reason in a sentence and the sentence is in the minute in her own words and it is twenty-five words.**

"**A year is not a thing a room decides at midnight and the twelfth hour is a clock and this district has never agreed whose.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the reason is the best one anybody has given for four hundred lines in eleven weeks, and that a clerk who will not close a book on a day the book does not have is a clerk who has understood the instrument she keeps, **and that the finding of the day is that a day with no instrument on it is a day a person has to come and find out about, and that the clerk has not written a date and has not washed a board and has done both on purpose.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the fifteenth year after the Long Fracture is the one this district argues about and that the argument is always about a road and never about the year, and that a year is a thing a room does not have an opinion about, **and that the eighteenth year after the Long Fracture began on the first of January on a morning with nothing on it and about nine people in a yard and a board with a spent date on it, and that that is the only account of it anybody has written down.**

Nobody argued with it and a man of fifty-six said that a year is the time the flood came and not the time the book was closed, and that the flood year is the year the west end of the Needle went under nine feet and has not come up, **and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that the flood year is the ninth year after the Long Fracture and that the year turned on the first of January and that the eighteenth year after the Long Fracture began on that day, and that a year counted from the flood and a year counted from the day a book was closed are two years and are not joined.**

Nobody asked her which year it is and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked her and that she was not going to enter a number on it, **and that a man of fifty-six said out loud in the yard that the count of years after the Long Fracture is seventeen and that on the first of January it is eighteen, and that he said it in a yard and not in a room and that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage in a room is not written down.**

The first of January was a morning with nothing on it, and a man of fifty-six read the ledger of readings out in the yard at about eleven in the morning, because a ledger is read out, and it is one for April, none for May, one for the twenty-ninth of June, one for the sixth of September, one for the ninth of October and one for the third of November.

Beside the index a man of seventeen keeps there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road asked whether the boy could be told to write a seventh, and a clerk of nineteen years said no in four seconds and gave the reason in a sentence.

"**The rule under the sixth is the only part of that index that is a rule and he put it there and nobody is going to use it up on somebody else's account.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that a ledger with six dates and a rule under the sixth has told this district three times in eleven weeks that the seventh is not due, and that a rule a person put under their own line is not a rule the room can spend, **and that there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading and that a man who has nine refusals has spent something on every one of them.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the rule under the sixth is the only instrument in this district that has refused to be used, and that it was made by a boy of seventeen who is unpaid and unasked and nobody's keeper, **and that an instrument that refuses to be used is not an instrument and is a person, and that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and that the boy of seventeen is in neither that count nor the count of five.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the boy of seventeen is in neither count and that she entered it because nobody else would, and that the count of protected things is five and did not move, **and that a passage twice, a loss once, a failure of a remedy once and a refusal to be asked again once are the five, and that nobody added a sixth in January, and that a category is not a bucket and a bucket is not a column.**

A man of fifty-six read that out again in the yard at about half past eleven and a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong, **and that there are six dates on one line in that ledger and a rule under the sixth and no seventh, and that the rule under the sixth was put there by a boy of seventeen and that nobody is going to use it up on somebody else's account.**

---

The hundred and eighty-three coppers was read out at the ward market counter at about noon on the first of January for the second time in three weeks, and it is a page in the ward market's own book with a heading and no name against it, and a reader who is about fifty read it out and read the sixth column out first, as she does.

Nobody refused. A man of fifty-six said that the figure is not to be struck and that nobody has ever said who is owed it and that the answer is not to be struck either, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a page with a heading and no name against it is the only page in that book that has been in it since the middle of the year.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the second reading is not a smaller thing than the first and is not a larger one, **and that a page read out twice in three weeks is a page that has become a habit, and that a habit is not a finding, and that a person who cannot say who is owed a hundred and eighty-three coppers is not a person who has stopped owing it.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper was in the yard and said one thing about the second reading, **and a man of fifty-six said that a word written down wrongly is a word that can be argued with in about four years, and that a page read out twice is a page that can be argued with in about four years, and that the district has decided which of the two it would rather have and has not said so out loud.**

Nobody took it up and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the not-reading is not entered as a refusal and that nobody has struck the page, **and that the figure of a hundred and eighty-three coppers and the figure of twenty-one years are in two books and are not added and that there is no rate and that a year is not a price and a price is not a year.**

A stranger at the counter asked the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, at about one in the afternoon, what a security was in this district, and he gave the answer that is in the market's own book, and a clerk of nineteen years read it back out of the counter's hand and got nothing wrong.

"**A security is a thing a stranger can go and look at. If you cannot go and look at it, it is not a security.**"

The stranger wrote it down. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the man has just described a district and not his own lot, and that the sentence in the counter's hand has been there since the eighth of March, **and that a rule that is true of a district and not of a man is the most dangerous kind there is, because nobody in the room can find the man it is not true of.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said nothing and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said nothing and that she was not asked to write it down and did.

**AND ON THE FIRST OF JANUARY A STRANGER AT THE WARD MARKET COUNTER ASKED WHAT A SECURITY WAS IN THIS DISTRICT AND WAS TOLD THAT A SECURITY IS A THING A STRANGER CAN GO AND LOOK AT, AND HE WROTE IT DOWN, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID THAT THE SENTENCE HAS BEEN TRUE OF A DISTRICT SINCE THE EIGHTH OF MARCH AND THAT NOBODY IN THE ROOM CAN FIND THE MAN IT IS NOT TRUE OF.**

---

**AND THE MONTH TURNED ONCE AND IT TURNED ON THE FIRST OF JANUARY, AND THE MONTH BEFORE IT WAS DECEMBER AND THE MONTH BEFORE THAT WAS NOVEMBER, AND THE YEAR THAT BEGAN ON THAT DAY IS THE EIGHTEENTH YEAR AFTER THE LONG FRACTURE, AND THE BOARD CARRIES FOUR DATES AND NO FIFTH, AND THE COUNT OF PROTECTED THINGS IS FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE, AND THE COUNT OF CONDITIONS WITH NO END ON IT IS FOUR AND DID NOT MOVE.**
