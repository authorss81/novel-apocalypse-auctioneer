# Chapter 177: The House at the Crossing

---

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten went out to the crossing on the seventeenth of the second month on his own account, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he went and that nobody sent for him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

He said the reason himself before anybody asked him for it and gave no reason for saying it in a yard, and a man of fifty-six entered afterwards that he was not asked and did not ask.

"**I dig that ground nine days in ten. It is not a mystery and it is not a page. I went to see whether the house is shut.**"

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who has said the second half of a thing about nine times in eleven weeks has now said it about a man at a crossing, **and that a register of the western tide names the owner and gives an address of one house at a crossing, and that a register is a register and not a mystery, and that the man at the crossing is not to be said dead by anybody in this district.**

There was no clerk on the road and there was no minute, and what came back was a page in a man's own hand which a clerk of nineteen years read out in the yard at about half past two in the afternoon because a page is read out.

The house at the crossing is one house with a door and a shuttered window and a yard about thirty feet across, and the shutter was down and the door was shut, and there is a woman at a door about nine inches open and she is not the owner and she is about sixty-one.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the loam-digger knocked once and the woman opened the door about nine inches and that he asked one question and that she answered it, and that he was not asked twice and was not asked why, **and that the answer is on the page in his own hand and it is twenty-five words, and that nobody in this district is going to ask the woman a second question and that the count of not-askings about her is not a column.**

"**He has not been in this house in four years that I have been here and I am not going to say where he is.**"

Nobody argued. A man of fifty-six said that a woman who will say that a man has not been in his house in four years and will not say where he is has told this district one true thing and one refusal, and that the two are a sentence and not a document, and that the sentence was said to a man who digs loam and not to a yard.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a register of the western tide names the owner and gives an address, and that the register does not say the man is dead and nobody in this district is going to say it for him, **and that the not-saying is not entered as a claim and is not a column, and that the woman's refusal is not entered as a refusal of a rule and is not added to the thirteen.**

**AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM WENT OUT TO A CROSSING ON HIS OWN ACCOUNT AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-ONE AT A DOOR SAID THAT THE MAN WHO OWNS A FARM FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS HAS NOT BEEN IN THAT HOUSE IN FOUR YEARS, AND WOULD NOT SAY WHERE HE IS.**

---

The seed came to the Road House on the tide on the sixteenth of the second month, which is the fourth of the four dates on a bucket, and a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said it out loud in the yard on the seventeenth before anybody asked her, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said the second half before she was asked and that she is not going to put a number on it.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a sheet is put in front of a family on a cart with the seed and that the seed is the reason the sheet gets read, **and that a district which has spent a month reading sheets has not once asked what the sheet was riding on.**

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the sheets come down at the end of a month on a cart with the seed and a man puts them down at the head of a lane and goes, and that this district has had that sentence for four days, **and that a man who puts a sheet down and goes is a man who has done his part, and that the part he has done is the only part of it anybody in this basin is paid for.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a sheet that arrives with seed and a sheet that arrives alone are two arrangements and are not the same, **and that the six households at the sluice end of the loams were asked in the fourth week of January and the seed came on the sixteenth of the second month, and that the order is a fact about a road and not about a house, and that the two have not been joined and are not going to be.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether a household that took seed and refused a sheet on the same morning has done anything this district can use, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the household which refused a sheet said its reason was that the season had not started, and that the season had not started on the morning the seed came either.

**AND THE SEED CAME TO THE ROAD HOUSE ON THE TIDE ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH, AND THE SHEETS COME DOWN ON A CART WITH THE SEED, AND A DISTRICT WHICH HAS SPENT A MONTH READING SHEETS HAS NOT ONCE ASKED WHAT THE SHEET WAS RIDING ON.**

---

The gate is chained and the chain is new, and the loam-digger said so on the page, and he said that the ground inside the gate has been walked on and not by boots with nails in them, and that a field that has been walked on is a field somebody has been checking.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the rest of it himself in a sentence that is in the minute in his own words and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty.

"**Somebody walks that field about twice a month and looks at the head race end of it, and the sluice is open, and I have never once seen the man.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who checks a field about twice a month is a person and not a house, and that a person who checks a field and is not the owner and is not named in a column is the fifth thing this district does not have in another place, **and that the fifth thing is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and a man who checks a field is not that and is not a sixth.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who checks a field about twice a month has not been asked by anybody in this district and is not going to be, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a clerk of the Underwriters of the Upland stood at that sluice for about half an hour on the third of February and wrote nothing down, and that the two are not joined and neither is a column.**

Then the join everybody wanted, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said it out loud in the yard, and a clerk of nineteen years said no in four seconds to being asked to put it in a line, and gave a reason that a man of about nineteen counted and got twenty-three.

"**The head race is eleven miles from that gate. Nobody has measured it and nobody has asked. Two places are not a road.**"

Nobody argued. A man of fifty-six said that a district which has spent four months refusing to join two things has never before refused to join two things that would have let it explain where a mile and a bit of standing water came from, **and that the refusal is correct and is the most expensive sentence anybody in this yard has said this month.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a mile and a bit is not a road and is not a channel and is not a culvert and that anybody who says a thing is a mile from a gate has said it is near, **and that near is the whole of what a man can say about water and that the district has been running on near since the first of the second month.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve and neither moved on the seventeenth of the second month, **and that a clerk of nineteen years has now been asked to join two things four times in three days and has said no four times and that four is a count and not a column.**

**AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM SAID THAT SOMEBODY WALKS THAT FIELD ABOUT TWICE A MONTH AND LOOKS AT THE HEAD RACE END OF IT, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS REFUSED IN FOUR SECONDS TO WRITE THAT THE HEAD RACE IS ELEVEN MILES FROM THAT GATE.**

---

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row was in the yard at about four in the afternoon and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, whether he would go out and stand in that gate while the sluice is open.

He said no in four seconds and gave the reason in a sentence, and it is in the minute in his own words and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-nine.

"**I am the fifth of the five. A man in a gate holding a sluice is a man holding a sluice, and I have been one of those once.**"

The room let that stand. A man of fifty-six said that a man who has been a security since the ninth year and has been told so in a room and has said twenty-four words about it to a man's face has now been asked to go and stand in a gate, **and that the question was the district's own and not the house's and that the man who asked it did not know what he was offering.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that he asked and that he did not know, and that a man who is a bearer of a thing is not a pair of hands, and that a person standing in a gate holding open a piece of iron is a bearer whether anybody calls him one or not, **and that the list of securities this district has is five long and the fifth of the five has just refused to be a sixth in the plainest words anybody has used in this yard in a month.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five posted securities was asked in the six things and answered and was not asked why, **and that a refusal given by a person about what he will stand in is not a refusal of a rule and is not entered as one, and that it is the first refusal the man has given in this district's business and that the two of those are two things.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a man who will not stand in a gate has done the only thing a man in a box can do, which is say no out loud in a room with about nine people in it, **and that a man in a box has been saying no out loud in this yard for nine years and that the first one anybody heard was on the ninth of January and it was twenty-four words.**

A man of fifty-six said that the man of about thirty-one has now said no in a yard three times, and that the three are three, and that three is not a column and is not added to the count of not-askings, **and that a person who has said no three times in a district that keeps asking has still not been asked why and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.**

**AND THE FIFTH OF THE FIVE POSTED SECURITIES SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS TO STANDING IN A GATE AT THE FARM, AND SAID THAT A MAN IN A GATE HOLDING A SLUICE IS A MAN HOLDING A SLUICE AND HE HAS BEEN ONE OF THOSE ONCE.**

---

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel was in the yard at about half past three and said the other half of the page the man of about thirty-four who digs loam had brought back, and it is in the minute in her own words and it is thirty-six words.

"**A man who checks a field and is not the owner is a man somebody sent, and if nobody sent him he is a man who came, and both of those are about nine months old.**"

Nobody argued. A man of fifty-six said that a woman who takes in washing has been in about forty doorways in nine years and has been asked what a person was doing in one of them about nine times, **and that a man who walks a field about twice a month is a person she has probably seen and could not place, and that the two of those are not joined and that she is not going to be asked to place him.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a person who goes into about forty houses a week and is asked nothing is the second instrument this district has that reaches a person who is not in a household, and that the two instruments are a bucket and a basket and that neither of them is on a list.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the second instrument in this district is a woman with a basket and the third is a man with a bundle of straw, and that neither of them has ever been entered in anything, **and that a bundle of straw is not a lot and carries no toll and a man who carries one is not a bearer of it, and that a mark may not carry a job and a bundle of straw is not a mark.**

A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale at the sluice end of the loams and has stood at it nine years came up the bank at about half past one in the afternoon and said that she weighed about nine sacks of seed on the sixteenth and that the seed came in on a cart at the Road House and came on a barrow down to her.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a scale that can weigh nine sacks of seed cannot weigh a season, and that a season is the thing every document in this district's business is actually about, **and that a woman who has stood at a scale nine years has been weighing the only thing anybody has actually got and it is not the thing anybody is arguing about.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure of about nine sacks was said by her and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked, **and that a sack is not a figure and a barrow is not a security and that a woman who is not paid to stand at a scale is not a keeper of one, and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and did not move on the seventeenth of the second month.**

A man of fifty-six said that there are now three things in this district that reach a person who is not in a household, which are a bucket, a basket and a barrow, **and that the district has three instruments that work without anybody coming to a yard, and that it has never once entered any of the three and is not going to, and that three is not a column.**

The interval was done at about half past four in the afternoon and a man of fifty-six did it and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down.

"**The thirty-first of March is forty-two days off today and the first of April is forty-three, and the eleventh of the second month was six days ago at forty-eight and forty-nine, and a figure with no day on it is not an interval.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is eighty-five and is a count of days and not a column, **and that the board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for fifty-five days and that nobody wrote on it on the seventeenth of the second month, and that the count of boards is five and the count of lines is six and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether anybody is going to send for the man of about forty-three who buys standing seed-crop, and a man of fifty-six said that the not-sending is entered and the count is where it was, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that he has now said out loud in a yard that he did not know it was an entry, **and that three people not saying a thing is three people not saying a thing and is not a count and is not a column.**

Then the question that was not answered, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked it in the yard at about half past three in the afternoon and nobody answered it and it went into the minute with nobody's name against it, and it is in his own words and it is twenty-one words.

"**If the owner has not been at his house in four years and the gate is chained, who is the farm.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the question is not the same as the one this room asked on the fourteenth of January about who owns this district's public lot, and that the two questions are two questions and he is not going to have them joined, **and that a register names an owner and a man who is not seen is not a man who is dead, and that the two halves of that sentence have been in this district's books since the fourteenth of January and have not been joined and are not to be.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the question was not answered and that a question which is not answered is not a question that has been put into a column, **and that there is no column for it and none was made, and that a farm with a chain on the gate and a register in a book and a man who digs it nine days in ten is the whole of what a farm with an absent owner is in this district.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that he digs the ground and that the ground does not care who owns it and that the sentence is a finding and not a joke, **and that a man who owns a farm he has not seen in four years still owns it, and that the district's own rule about a lot with no bearer does not apply here because a lot is not a farm and a farm is not a lot and the two have never met in any book this district has read.**

Nobody took that up. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a lot has seven parts and that a farm has a house and four fields and a sluice, and that a sluice is not a part and a field is not a use and a house is not a bearer, **and that the two of those are not joined and the count of six did not move, and that she was not asked whether they should be joined and is not going to be.**

A man of fifty-six said that the farm is still unsold and that a man who offered two thousand four hundred coppers and a cart of meal for the standing crop and the sluice on the twenty-ninth of December went west on the thirtieth and has not come back, **and that a transfer needs a holder to transfer from and this farm has a register and no man, and that the two of those are not the same and that the day of the season is the thirty-first of March.**

Nobody took that up. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against cannot close a transfer that has no named holder, and that this has been entered four times in five weeks and that the four is a count of entries and not a column, **and that a sluice standing open on a farm with no bearer is a sluice and not a lot, and that the count of things this district does not have is five and did not move on the seventeenth of the second month.**

**AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A FARM FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS IS STILL UNSOLD AND A MAN WHO WALKS ITS FIELD ABOUT TWICE A MONTH HAS NEVER BEEN SEEN, AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS FORTY-TWO DAYS AWAY.**
