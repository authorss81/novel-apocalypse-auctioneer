# Chapter 163: Thirty-Four Miles East

---

He walked it. Thirty-four miles east out of the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning on the seventeenth of January, and he had a horse once for six of the miles because a haulier of about forty who passes this road twice a week offered him one, and he did not pay for it and was not asked to.

There was no clerk of nineteen years on the road and there was no minute, and a man of fifty-six had said so out loud in the yard before he got to the gate, **and that what comes back from a road is a page in a man's own hand and that a page in a man's own hand is worth less than a minute and is not the same thing.**

He thought about the thirty-first of March for eleven of the thirty-four miles and about the seventy-six days that run to it from the fourteenth of January, and about the fact that a figure he had said out loud in a yard three days before was a number and not a thing he could spend.

The haulier came up behind him again at about the sixth mile and said good morning and then said nothing else for four miles, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the second half of something at the tenth mile and the haulier did not hear it and he had not meant him to.

The road is metalled for twenty-one of the thirty-four miles and is not metalled for the rest, and the first nine miles of the unmade part are where the loams run out and the ground begins to go up, and a man who has walked to a place he does not know in the eighteenth year after the Long Fracture counts the hills and not the days.

The town is one street with a market cross in it and the counting house is at the east end, and it is a building with a door and a counter inside the door and a board on the wall outside, and from the road you can see the counter and the board and nothing else of it at all.

The board on the wall is painted, not chalked, and it gives the name of the house and the hours and nothing else, and it is not one of the five boards in this district and is thirty-four miles from any of them, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood in the road and looked at it for long enough that a woman of about thirty carrying a basket stopped and looked at him looking at it.

There are four houses with counters in this town and three of them have shutters down at half past ten in the morning, and the fourth is the one with the board painted on the wall, and the man who can be found wrote on his page that the fourth is the only one open and that he did not ask why and that the answer would have been that the fourth is the one that expects a stranger.

**AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF JANUARY THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST STOOD THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST OF THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN IN FRONT OF A BUILDING WITH A COUNTER IN IT, AND THERE WAS NO CLERK ON THE ROAD AND NO MINUTE, AND WHAT CAME BACK WAS A PAGE IN HIS OWN HAND.**

---

The man behind the counter came out when he came in, and he is about forty and he is a clerk of the house and he is not a person in this district, and he asked what he wanted and did not wait to be told who he was.

"**What do you want.**"

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said that he wanted a receipt, and that in the fourteenth year a dray and its two shafts were sold out of a yard in this district and he was the seller, and that he has never had the buyer's half.

The man behind the counter said that a seller keeps the seller's half and the buyer's half is the receipt, and that this is not a thing anybody in that house will explain twice, and that he had said it once.

Nobody took that up, because there was nobody. A clerk of nineteen years was not there and the man who can be found put it on the page afterwards in his own hand and the page says in his own hand that the man said it once and did not say it again and was not asked twice.

Then the second thing, and the man behind the counter said it before he was asked for it, and gave no reason, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against set it down and underlined it once and did not underline it twice.

"**The receipt is given to the man who brings the thing in, and the man who brought it in is in this district, and you are not him.**"

Nobody argued with it, and the man who can be found said the other half, and the man behind the counter let him say it.

"**And I am not the man who brought it in either. I am the man who wrote the line on the bond that said it was discharged on sale. That was in the ninth year and I have not thought about it since and you may write that down.**"

**AND A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY BEHIND A COUNTER IN A COUNTING HOUSE THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST SAID THAT A RECEIPT IS GIVEN TO THE MAN WHO BRINGS THE THING IN, AND THAT THE MAN WHO BROUGHT IT IN IS IN THIS DISTRICT, AND THAT THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST IS NOT HIM.**

---

There was a woman at the far end of the counter and she was about thirty-five and she was not in a room and she had a book in front of her and a woman of this district's records office is thirty-four and is not her and the two are not to be confused **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that once and once only.**

She looked up once, at about the fourth minute, and did not look up again, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not go down the counter to her and did not say her name and did not say his own, and the man behind the counter read the terms of the house out loud to both of them.

The terms are on a printed sheet and the sheet is on the counter with a nail through the top of it, and the nail is not a board and is not one of the five, and the man who can be found wrote that down too.

> **THE HOUSE TAKES THE FIGURE A PERSON OWES AND NOT THE LAND THE FIGURE IS ON.**
>
> **THE HOUSE WILL NAME A BEARER FOR A FIGURE AND WILL FILL THE COLUMN ITSELF.**
>
> **THE NAMED PERSON WILL NOT BE ASKED WHETHER THEY WILL STAND.**
>
> **A FIGURE IS DISCHARGED WHEN THE HOUSE HOLDS A RECEIPT FOR IT. THE HOUSE KEEPT THE RECEIPT AND WILL SHOW IT AT THIS COUNTER TO A STRANGER WHO ASKS.**
>
> **THIS SHEET IS THE TERMS OF THE HOUSE. THE HOUSE IS NOT A PERSON.**

The man who can be found read it out himself, twice, standing in the road afterwards, and the second reading is the one he wrote on the page, and the man of about nineteen who was not there did not count it and a man of fifty-six did not hear it and the reading is his own and is not the rule of the counter.

Nobody argued with it. He put on the page that a house thirty-four miles east has five sentences on a counter with a nail through the top of them and that the fourth of the five says that a figure is discharged when the house holds a receipt for it, and that a man in a yard four miles from a lower terrace said the same thing about a dray in the ninth week of January, and that the two are a receipt and are not joined.

**AND THE TERMS OF A HOUSE THAT GUARANTEES THINGS WERE ON A COUNTER THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST WITH A NAIL THROUGH THE TOP OF THEM, AND THE FOURTH OF THE FIVE SAYS THAT A FIGURE IS DISCHARGED WHEN THE HOUSE HOLDS A RECEIPT FOR IT, AND THE HOUSE KEEPT THE RECEIPT AND WILL SHOW IT TO A STRANGER WHO ASKS.**

---

Then the part he had come for, and he asked it in his own words and it is on the page and it is thirty-four words, and the man behind the counter answered it in twenty-six and did not soften it and did not refuse it twice.

"**A dray sold in the fourteenth year, money taken by a man who said he was buying for this house, and I would like to see whether the house holds a receipt for it.**"

"**The house holds receipts. I cannot show you one without the man who brought the thing in, and the man is four miles from where you live, and if he comes I will show it to him and not to you.**"

The man who can be found said that the man would come, and that he had asked him and the man had said yes and named the nineteenth of January himself, and the man behind the counter said that was not his business and was in the right to say so.

The room let it stand. He put on the page that the refusal was given once and was not given twice, **and that the man behind the counter did not look at the woman at the end of the counter while he was saying it, and that he wrote that down because it was a thing he had seen and not a thing he had been told.**

While he was standing there a woman of about thirty and a man of about forty-five came up to the counter with a sheet, and the man behind the counter did not stop what he was doing and then he stopped it, and the man who can be found stood four feet away and watched all of it and said nothing for about two minutes.

The man of about forty-five put the sheet down and the man behind the counter turned it round and read the face of it and then took a pen and went to a column with a man's name already in his own hand at the top of it and a ruled space under the name.

He filled the space. He did not ask the man of about forty-five anything at all, and the man of about forty-five did not offer anything, and there was no pause in which an asking could have gone in and been refused, and the whole of it took about eleven seconds and the man who can be found counted them.

Then the woman of about thirty said his name out loud, once, to the counter, and the counter wrote it down, and that is the only time anybody in that building said a name that the man who can be found heard in two days.

He marked it afterwards that the man of about forty-five was not asked and did not refuse and did not say yes, **and that the record of that sheet in that building will say that the man stood at a counter and signed, and that the two sentences are not the same sentence and that a district which cannot tell them apart has no way to be told things apart.**

The form the man of about forty-five put down is a printed sheet with four lines at the top and a column at the side, and he wrote the shape of it on the page afterwards and wrote that he wrote it from memory and that a shape written from memory is a shape and not a copy.

> **GUARANTEE OF THE FIGURE SET AGAINST THE LABOUR OF A HOUSEHOLD AFTER THE SEASON. THE COLUMN HEADED BEARER. THE BEARER NAMED IS NOT ASKED WHETHER HE WILL STAND. THE SHEET IS KEPT BY THE HOUSE. A STRANGER MAY READ IT AT THIS COUNTER AND MAY NOT TAKE IT AWAY. THE HOUSE IS NOT A PERSON.**

He wrote on the page that the sheet he had watched being filled had four lines above the column about a figure and four lines below it about a discharge, and that the column was the only part of the paper with a person in it, **and that a house which writes everything it does on the face of a sheet has still taken the one thing a person cannot check, and that the one thing it has taken is the inside of a man of forty-five who was standing four feet away from a man who could have said something and did not.**

He wrote on the page that the man of about thirty had not been asked either, and that she had said the name and the counter had written it, and that a name said out loud by one person to another is the only way a person gets into a column in that building, **and that this district's rule is that a person must not be asked, and that a rule which looks like protection and can be turned into a way of not asking anybody is a rule that is doing the same work from both sides.**

Then he asked one question of his own, at about half past eleven in the morning, and he asked the man behind the counter and not the woman at the end of it, and the man answered it in thirteen words without looking at the sheet.

"**If a man wants his name off that sheet, what happens to it.**"

"**He may take it off. He may not take the figure off. The figure is the house's and the name is his and the two are on one piece of paper because one piece of paper is cheaper.**"

He wrote that on the page and wrote that he did not ask the second half of the question, which was what the man would be paid the season's labour for, and that the answer to that half is on the face of the sheet already and is one of the four lines at the top, **and that a man may take his own name off a piece of paper in a building and cannot take the season's work off it, and that this is the whole of what a person can do about a house in a room, and that this district has no instrument for anything else and has not had one since the eighth of March.**

He asked whether a man could be told before the season that his name was in a column, and the man behind the counter said that he could, and that the house tells every named person once a season by letter, and that about nine of those letters a year are returned and that the house does not send them twice, **and that a man who has been named and has not been told is a man the house has done all it is required to do, and that the word required is on the wall outside the door and not on the counter.**

Nobody argued with that. He put on the page that the word required is on a painted board thirty-four miles from this district and that this district has five instruments it has built and not named and has never once painted a word on a wall, **and that a house that paints a word on a wall is doing the district's work badly and in a town, and that the two are not joined and that he is not going to say the second one is better.**

Nobody in that building said anything to him about any of it, and he did not ask, and the page says he did not ask, **and the page also says that he stood four feet from a counter and watched a man be made into a column and did not open his mouth, and that this is now in two books and is the second thing in this district's business that he has watched happen to another person.**

---

**AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF JANUARY A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY BEHIND A COUNTER REFUSED IN TWENTY-EIGHT WORDS TO SHOW THE RECEIPT FOR THE SALE OF A DRAY TO THE MAN WHO SOLD IT, AND GAVE AS THE REASON THAT THE RECEIPT IS GIVEN TO THE MAN WHO BRINGS THE THING IN AND THAT MAN IS IN THIS DISTRICT, AND THE REFUSAL WAS GIVEN ONCE AND NOT TWICE.**

---

He walked back. Thirty-four miles, and he did not take the horse the second time, and he thought for nineteen days' worth of distance about a tank with about two days in it and about a counter four feet away from his face.

He got into the yard of Lot Seventeen at about dark on the eighteenth of January and a man of fifty-six was still standing about nine yards from the middle of it and did not go to the man.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he came back on the eighteenth and not the seventeenth, and that he had said he would be back when he was back, and that a man who says that and comes back on a day has given a day after all and did not say so when he said it.

She was not there. He put the page on the trestle table about half an hour after he came in and did not go and find her, and a man of fifty-six said that a man who has been thirty-four miles in three days and has not gone to find anybody is a man who has brought something back that is not a document.

A man of fifty-six read the page out in the yard because a page is read out, and he read it out twice, and a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong, and the page is a man's account of two days and not a minute and the difference between the two is whose memory it is.

The page is the second instrument this district has built on a road and has not named, and the count of instruments built and not named is six and did not move on the seventeenth or the eighteenth of January, **and a page in a man's hand is a page and not an instrument and is not going to be entered as one.**

Nobody took up the fourth of the five, and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a house which shows a stranger a receipt on request has nothing to hide, and that this district has been arguing for a month about whether a house should show its terms and has never once asked whether a house can.

A man of fifty-six said that a house that shows a stranger a receipt is a house that knows exactly which receipt to show, **and that the man of about thirty-one is going east on the nineteenth to stand at a counter and be shown a piece of paper, and that this district sent him to do it, and that the yard has now sent two men to the same counter on the same road inside three days and has not entered either of them as a column.**

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said the other half of it and said it before anybody asked her, and it is in the minute in her own words and it is twenty-eight words, and the man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-eight and said it out loud.

"**You went thirty-four miles to find out whether a house would show you a piece of paper, and it would, and it showed you the ones it chose.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that the sentence in the counter's hand says that a security is a thing a stranger can go and look at, **and that a house with a counter and a nail and a painted board is the most inspectable instrument any of them has ever come across, and that the district has spent eleven weeks teaching people to insist on exactly this, and that it has worked, and that what it got was a stranger standing four feet from a column being filled in.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the woman is right and that a house that shows you the paper is a house that has decided which paper, and that the finding of the eighteenth of January is not about the house and is not about the four men of this district who have been posted, **and that it is that a person can be written in a column in a building four miles from a road a stranger is standing on, and that nobody in the building has broken a single rule and that the rules are all on the face of the paper and were read out to me twice.**

Nobody took that up and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and that a house with a counter is not one of them and is not a seventh, **and that the count of things this district does not have is five and that a way to be told what a house has decided to show you is not one of the five and is a sixth and is not entered.**

The yard let it stand **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two men at a counter in three days is not a delegation and is not a party, and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve, and that neither moved on the seventeenth or the eighteenth of January.**

**AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST WALKED THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST AND BACK IN THREE DAYS AND CAME HOME WITH A PAGE IN HIS OWN HAND, AND A HOUSE THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST HAS FIVE SENTENCES ON A COUNTER AND WILL SHOW A RECEIPT TO A STRANGER WHO ASKS AND WILL NOT SHOW IT TO THE MAN WHO SOLD THE THING.**
