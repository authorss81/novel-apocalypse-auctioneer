# Chapter 151: The Fourth Date Is the Last One

---

The room met at ten in the morning on the twenty-fourth of December because the board outside its door carried that date. The date was the fourth of the four the board had carried since the twenty-eighth of September, and nobody had written a fifth.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she had not been asked to write one and had not offered, **and entered that the two facts are in one line on purpose and that she wanted them on two lines and did not do it.**

The four are the twenty-eighth of September, the twenty-sixth of October, the twenty-fourth of November and the twenty-fourth of December. Three are in a hand that cannot be read and were written by a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel. The fourth is in a hand anybody can read and was written by a man of fifty-six who does count for a living, who was not asked to write it, and who has said he would not have written it if he had been asked.

A man of fifty-six read the four out and nobody argued about them, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the finding about hands has not stopped being a finding because the year turned, **and that a board a person can read is a board a person can argue with and a board in a hand that cannot be read is a board a person has to come and ask about, and that the two are two things.**

Then the question, and the man who mends fencing put it, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**When this date is spent the room has nothing that says when it meets, and a room with nothing is a room that has ended.**"

A clerk of nineteen years counted the words in that sentence once and got twenty-five and did not count them again, **and entered that she did not count them again because a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road told her on the first of November not to, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.**

Nobody took it up for about nine seconds. Then a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said the other half, and it is nineteen words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got nineteen and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got nineteen and said it out loud and then said it again.

"**A board is a place a line of chalk goes and a wash is a thing that has to be done.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the two sentences are the whole of the problem and that neither of them is about a date. **And a clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of entries carrying a finding in them since the eighteenth of December is two, that the first is hers, that the second is the room's, and that she entered the count before she wrote the line and not after.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten was in the room, and he had come the two miles twice in a month and a half and had not been asked to, and he said that a room that meets because a board says so is a room that meets on somebody else's ink, and that the woman who wrote the first three dates is not a clerk of this room and was never made one.

Nobody argued with him, and a man of fifty-six said that nobody was going to ask her to take it and that she said in December that she is not going to say why she writes in a hand that cannot be read, **and that a clerk of the ward market asked her the same question on the second of December and she gave the same answer, and that the two occasions are not joined.**

Of the six who hold the ledger, two came. A clerk of nineteen years entered that four of the six did not come and that nobody asked them why, **and that the six are not a column and a number on them would have been a column and a column is a thing a person is paid for.**

The two that came were the man of about thirty-four who digs loam and the man of about nineteen who walked in from the road, and they did not speak to each other before the noon hour, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered both of those things and entered that she had not asked either of them why and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in the room for an hour and a half and was not asked to speak, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that this is the second time in a fortnight a man who can be found has been in a room and let, **and that the first was on the eighteenth of December and that the two are not joined and that she is not going to join them.**

He said one thing at about half past eleven, and it is in the minute in his own words, and a clerk of nineteen years did not count it because nobody asked her to.

"**A charter tells you what it reaches. A board tells you when to come. I do not know what this room is now and neither does anybody else in it.**"

The room let it stand. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the man who can be found has said the thing the room has been carrying for six days, **and that a charter of four sheets closed on the eighteenth of December has no way to be argued with and a board that ran out this morning has no way to be argued with either, and that the district has two documents it cannot argue with and one of them is a piece of chalk on a plank.**

A man of fifty-six said that a document which cannot be argued with is not a defect and is a thing the district has and not a thing it lacks. The finding of the morning is the other one. A room whose only instrument runs out on a date is a room a clerk can end, and the clerk has said she is not going to, and a clerk saying she is not going to is a person and not a rule, and the two are two things and a person can be tired.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage in a room is not a rule and is not written down, the clerk of nineteen years did not write it down, and a rule a man says against his own advantage in a room is not written down and he would not have it written down, and the room is therefore a room that a person is holding together.

Then he said the other half, and it is in the minute in his own words, and it is twenty-two words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got twenty-two both times and did not say so.

"**A room held together by one person lasts exactly as long as that person, and I do not know how long that is.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that the man who mends fencing has now said the second half of a thing before he was asked for it and that the count of that is three and is not a column, **and that the three are a clerk of nineteen years, a man of about nineteen and a man of about thirty-four, and that the three are not joined to each other.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF DECEMBER THE BOARD CARRIED FOUR DATES AND THE FOURTH WAS THE LAST OF THEM AND NOBODY WROTE A FIFTH, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT SHE WAS NOT ASKED TO WRITE ONE AND DID NOT OFFER, AND A CLERK IS A PERSON AND NOT A RULE, AND THE ROOM HAS A CHARTER IT CANNOT ARGUE WITH AND A BOARD THAT HAS RUN OUT AND THE MAN WHO CAN BE FOUND SAID HE DID NOT KNOW WHAT THE ROOM WAS AND NOBODY ANSWERED HIM.**

---

A stranger came into the yard at about eleven in the morning and asked the woman who was nearest, which was a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel, what the room was for. She said it was a room, and he asked what a room was for, and she said that it was for the people who stood in frames, and he wrote that down.

A man of fifty-six heard it and did not correct her, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing heard it and said nothing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered afterwards that four people in the yard had been asked the same question in about nine minutes and had given four answers, **and that a room is for the people who stand in frames, a room is a room with a date on it, a room is a thing that can be argued with, and a room is four sheets that cannot be certified, and that none of the four is a finding and that a stranger wrote the first one down on the back of a board.**

The stranger wrote on the back of the fourth toll board and not on the plank, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not ask which board to write on, **and that the count of boards is five and did not move and that the count of lines is six and did not move and that the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty, and that a man writing on the back of a board is not using an instrument and is using a board.**

He wrote the word ROOM and four lines under it, and the four lines were about eleven in the morning on the twenty-fourth of December, and a man of fifty-six said that a stranger can take that cold, and that a room this district cannot describe is a room a stranger has described on its back board, and that the two are two things and that neither of them is a fault.

**AND A STRANGER ASKED AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF DECEMBER WHAT THE ROOM WAS FOR, AND FOUR PEOPLE GAVE FOUR ANSWERS IN ABOUT NINE MINUTES, AND HE WROTE THE FIRST ONE DOWN ON THE BACK OF THE FOURTH TOLL BOARD, AND THE COUNT OF BOARDS IS FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE AND THE COUNT OF LINES IS SIX AND DID NOT MOVE AND THE SEVENTH LINE AT THE NINTH PLACE IS RULED AND EMPTY.**

Nobody took it up and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the district's own instrument is a page of six columns and eleven rows on a trestle table and that it is a better description of a year than this plank is of a room, **and that the two are two things and that a stranger holding both in the same afternoon has not been given one of them twice.**

---

The board was not washed off, and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said on the twenty-fourth of December that it would be washed and a man of fifty-six said that it would not, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered both of those in one line and did not join them.**

He gave the reason and it is in the minute in his own words: the fourth date is the only date on that board anybody can argue with, and a room which washes the last date off a board has told every stranger who comes to the yard that there is no date, **and a stranger cannot tell the difference between a room that has no date and a room that has ended, and the two are the same to anybody standing in the yard.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the board was not washed and that a man of fifty-six said out loud that it would not be, and that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage in a room is not a rule and is not written down, **and that she was asked whether to write it down and he said no in four seconds, and that the asking is entered and the count is where it was.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**Leave it up. A man who cannot read it will ask me what it says and that is the point of a board.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a board a person has to come and ask about is a board a person comes to, and that this district's strongest instrument is a book that works if a person comes to the yard, and that a plank nailed to a post outside a room is a book that works the same way and costs nothing and holds one date, and that the two are two things.

A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale at the sluice end of the loams and has stood at it nine years was in the yard to weigh two sacks of meal. She said that a sack is a thing you can weigh and a date is a thing you cannot, and that a person who keeps a scale is asked about a sack and is not asked about a date, and that the reason is that a sack belongs to somebody and a date does not, and that the two have never been in one line in any book she has read.

Nobody asked her why she said it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had been entered before she spoke as not speaking for the yard, **and that this is the fourth time she has been entered that way and that the count of it is four and is not a count of not-askings and is not added to one.**

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures came to the yard at about one in the afternoon and was not asked for anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, and that she carries water to about ninety houses and that the figure of ninety was said by her and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked.

She said one thing and it went into the minute in her own words, and it is twenty-four words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-four and a clerk of nineteen years did not count them and said so and he said count them anyway and she did and got twenty-four both times.

"**You have four dates on a plank and I have four dates on a bucket and one of them is nearer.**"

The room let it stand and a man of fifty-six entered it in the minute and did not join it to the hundred and eighty-three coppers. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she had wanted to join the two and had not and that this is the twenty-ninth time, **and that the page has a heading and no name against it and a bucket has no heading and no name and that the two are not the same instrument.**

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing said the other half, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the plank has four dates on it and a bucket has four dates on it and that a bucket is carried to ninety houses and a plank is carried to none, and that the two are two things.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF DECEMBER A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT WHO CARRIES WATER AND DOES NOT READ FIGURES SAID THAT A PLANK HAS FOUR DATES ON IT AND A BUCKET HAS FOUR DATES ON IT AND ONE OF THEM IS NEARER, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT SHE HAD WANTED TO JOIN IT TO THE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THREE COPPERS AND DID NOT, AND THE COUNT OF THAT IS TWENTY-NINE.**

---

The train on the siding had stood three hundred and sixteen days on the twenty-fourth of December, and a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and the sum was put under the figure out loud at about two in the afternoon in the yard, and the sum is from the eleventh of February and is not carried forward out of any book.

"**Seventeen days left in February after the eleventh, then one hundred and eighty-four to the thirty-first of August, which is two hundred and one. Thirty for September, thirty-one for October, thirty for November, thirty-one for December.**"

Two hundred and one and thirty is two hundred and thirty-one, and thirty-one is two hundred and sixty-two, and thirty is two hundred and ninety-two, and the first of December is two hundred and ninety-three, **and the twenty-fourth is the twenty-third day after the first, so two hundred and ninety-three and twenty-three is three hundred and sixteen, and the thirty-first of December is three hundred and twenty-three, and the day after that is three hundred and twenty-four, and that day is in the month after this one and the month has a name and this chapter does not use it before it comes.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said again that a man who does a sum in front of strangers is not checking a number and is teaching it, and that he said in December that he would stop saying it in December and start again in January, and that it is still the twenty-fourth of December and that he has not started.

Nobody asked him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had said it and that nobody was asked, **and that beside the index there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date, and that the count of readings is one for April, none for May, one for the twenty-ninth of June, one for the sixth of September, one for the ninth of October and one for the third of November, and that the refusals to read are nine.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the refusals have not moved in nine weeks and that a man who has refused nine times has spent something on every one of them, and that the departure is unspent, and that there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading.

He said the last of that in the minute in his own words, and a clerk of nineteen years read Ivo Stenn's separate sheet out in the yard afterwards, because a minute is read out, and read it back after the ledger as she reads it every time, **and got twenty as printed and nineteen as called, and entered both figures and entered that the two are in two places and are not joined.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF DECEMBER THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND SIXTEEN DAYS, AND THE COUNT OF REFUSALS TO READ IS NINE, AND THE DEPARTURE IS UNSPENT, AND THERE IS STILL NO DATE IN THE LEDGER FOR THE NEXT READING, AND THE SHEET IS NINETEEN WORDS AND IS TWENTY AS PRINTED.**

---

The six askings that carry a minute-figure are fifty-one, thirty-nine, forty-four, thirty-one, twenty-six and forty-four, which is two hundred and thirty-five minutes in all. A man of fifty-six read them out in the yard on the twenty-fourth of December and a clerk of nineteen years entered them, and entered that no seventh asking was asked in December and that the two people who hold the boundary at the west end were not asked again.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a room which has a charter it cannot argue with and a board that has run out also has a boundary held by two people it has not asked in eleven weeks, **and that the three are the only instruments in this district that can survive a room, and that a boundary survives because two people are holding it and a charter survives because nineteen people closed it and a board survives while the ink lasts.**

Nobody asked him and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve, and that neither moved on the twenty-fourth of December, and that the answer to what a public office is for was not answered on the twenty-fourth of December either.

The office's name is in five places and in no register. Eleven trades could not state what it did. Four answers were given in four rooms and were not joined. Four gaps in four books were not joined to each other, four documents mention no office, one sentence is in the column for things that are not findings, and the question is in a public book dated the fifth of July.

And nobody asked the registrar why she holds two lines apart. A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked her on the twenty-fourth of December and that she was not going to enter a number on it, **and that the number the registrar keeps for herself is hers, and that a clerk of nineteen years keeping a different number in a different book is a thing two people are doing and the two are not added to each other.**

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that two people keeping two numbers in two books and not adding them is the only reason the district is not a worse place than it is, and that nobody has written that down and that he is not going to have it written down, **and that a rule a man says out loud against his own advantage in a room is not a rule and is not written down.**

Then the yard, at about half past three in the afternoon, and the last entry of the twenty-fourth of December is eight lines and it is in the clerk of nineteen years' own hand.

The eight lines name the four dates and the finding and the count of two, and they do not say what the room is, and the man who can be found said at about half past eleven that he did not know what it was and nobody answered him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody answered him and that a question which is not answered is not a question that has been put into a column, **and that there is no column for it and none was made.**

And nobody wrote a fifth date on the board in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fourth of December, **AND THE FOURTH DATE WAS SPENT IN THE MORNING AND THE BOARD WAS NOT WASHED, AND THE ROOM HAS A CHARTER OF FOUR SHEETS THAT CANNOT BE CERTIFIED AND A BOARD WITH FOUR DATES ON IT AND NO FIFTH, AND A MAN WHO CAN BE FOUND SAID HE DID NOT KNOW WHAT THE ROOM WAS, AND NOBODY ANSWERED HIM, AND THE COUNT OF ENTRIES WITH A FINDING IN THEM SINCE THE EIGHTEENTH OF DECEMBER IS TWO.**
