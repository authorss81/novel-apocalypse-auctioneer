# Chapter 190: The Next Page

---

The counts were read out at about ten in the morning on the eighth of the third month and the man who read them was a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he asked on the seventh of the third month whether he might read them out and that she said yes and was not asked for a reason.

He read them the way he reads everything, which is as one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them, and there were about nine people in that yard and nobody interrupted him, and a clerk of nineteen years read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong.

"**Five things this district does not have. Six instruments built and not named. Three documents nobody owns. Five protected things. Four conditions with no end on it. Five boards, six lines full and a seventh ruled and empty. Thirteen refusals to join two things, and twelve of the registrar's own. Nine refusals to read, and one departure unspent, and no date in the ledger for the next reading.**"

Nobody argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that a man who does the counting for a living does not get to do all of it, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that he was not a man who does the counting for a living and had been told so, **and that a man of fifty-six who objects to another man doing his counting in a yard and then does two of the counts himself has said a thing out loud and is not going to be thanked for it and has not been thanked.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the two of them have now read figures out loud in this yard more times in one month than anybody in this district has done anything, **and that the count of the second half said before being asked has not been looked at since the twenty-ninth of December and that a count of readings is a count of readings and is not one of the counts in the list and is not going to be.**

Then the man of fifty-six read the last two himself at about ten minutes past ten, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he read them and that she did not ask him to and that the two of them are the only two figures in the whole of it that change, **and that a count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November is one hundred and six today and the board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for seventy-six days and that nobody wrote on it.**

Nobody in that yard said anything about the seventy-six. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether anybody was going to wash the board before the first of April, and a clerk of nineteen years said no in about two seconds and gave no reason, **and that a board which has carried a spent date for seventy-six days is a board and is not an instrument and is not one of the five things this district does not have and is not going to be written on by anybody.**

**AND ON THE EIGHTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING READ OUT EIGHT COUNTS IN ONE RUN OF WORDS IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX READ THE LAST TWO, AND NONE OF THEM HAS MOVED SINCE THE SECOND OF JANUARY.**

---

The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace came up the bank on the tenth of the third month at about eleven in the morning and had not been sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

He said that the mend in the crack is on the ninth day, and that the tank is at about a day and a half, and that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam has said three days and has not said the other figure because he was not asked for it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure of a day and a half was said by the man of about fifty-five and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked, **and that a tank at about a day and a half is a figure and not a term and has nobody standing under it, and that a tank at about a day and a half is not a condition with no end on it and is not one of the four and the count of four did not move on the tenth of the third month.**

Nobody argued with that. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether anybody was going to be told about that in this district, and a man of fifty-six said that a man who keeps a building is not its bearer and is not going to be entered as one, **and that a tank in a building nobody owns is a tank nobody is responsible for, and that the shelter has not denied anybody entry at any time in nine years and is not going to and that is not the same sentence as the one about responsibility.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the fourth holding of the nine has now not taken a drop for a month, since the first of the second month, and that the house has not written to anybody about it and is not obliged to, **and that a month of a house not writing is a month of a figure not moving and the two are one silence and not two.**

The woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was at the top of the hill with a yoke when he came up the bank and she was not asked for anything, and she said that the loam under the plate is about a foot deep in the wet and that it was about a foot deep in the ninth year, and that her shoulder has been the same shoulder for nine years and is not the same shoulder this month.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked about the shoulder and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a woman of fifty-eight doing four yoke-loads a day on a hill four miles from this yard is a supply and not a figure, and that a supply is not a condition with no end on it and is not one of the four and is not one of the five protected things.**

Then the man of about fifty-five said the other thing, and it is the thing he came up the bank for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked for it and that she is not going to ask him why he said it.

"**I am not going to put it in a fifth time. It has been in there nine days and I would rather have a crack with a mark on it than have a mend that comes out in three pieces in a week's time.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, what a mark on a stone would be, and the man of about fifty-five said that it would be a line of charcoal and that it would be a mark because it is drawn, and then said that a line of stones is not a mark and that a line of charcoal is not a mark either, **and that a mark is cut and that is laid, and that the rule about a mark has not been retired and is not going to be bent to make a stone tank respectable.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the rule was not reached for on the tenth of the third month and was not retired, and that the standing test case is not extended and she was not asked to extend it and is not going to be, **and that a man who has chosen a mark he is not allowed to call a mark is a man and not an instrument and the count of six did not move on the tenth of the third month.**

**AND ON THE TENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH THE TANK FOUR MILES DOWN A LANE IS AT ABOUT A DAY AND A HALF, A MEND IN A CRACK HAS BEEN IN IT FOR NINE DAYS, AND THE MAN WHO PUT IT THERE SAID THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO PUT IT IN A FIFTH TIME.**

---

The page, and the man of about fifty-five did the sum in his own head on the step at the back of his building and would not do it out loud, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked for it and that she is not going to ask him again.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam worked it out afterwards from the days the man of fifty-five had said out loud in this yard and got to the end of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the working out was done by a man who was not a clerk and was not asked to be one and is not going to be, **and that a page with about forty spaces and thirty-four days in it has about six of the first left in it and that a third is not a rate and two thirds is not a rate either.**

Nobody argued with that. A man of fifty-six said that a page which is going to be full in about six days is a page with an end in it that a man brought about by doing the right thing, **and that the end of a page is the only kind of ending in this district's business that a person can bring about by doing the right thing, and that he said that on the fifteenth of the second month and has said it again and is not going to say it a third time.**

Then the other half of it, and the man of about fifty-five had brought it up the bank in his head and had not brought it out, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him about it at about half past twelve in the afternoon and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he asked and that he answered.

He said that the heading on the fourth page is three sentences long and that he wrote it himself in the fourth year and that it is in the minute of the fourth of January, and that there are about nine pages in that book, **and that the fifth page has not got his heading on it and that whoever fills the fifth page is going to have to rule a column and a heading and is not going to write a heading he has not been given.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man who wrote a heading himself in the fourth year is six days from a page with somebody else's work at the top of it, **and that a heading a man wrote himself is the last thing anybody is going to get changed and the first thing anybody is going to need changed, and that he said that on the fifteenth of the second month in a lane and has said it again and it is not a new finding.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about fifty-five was not asked whether he wanted the fifth page as well as the fourth and the sixth, and that he was not asked on the fifteenth of the second month either, **and that a man who has been filling a page nobody asked him to fill is a man and not a keeper and is not a clerk of anything and is not going to be made one.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row, in the six things, at about two in the afternoon, to read out the list of the five, and the man of about thirty-one said no, and gave the reason himself, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-nine.

"**The list is not written. I have not got one in my hand and neither has anybody, and a man who cannot write a list cannot read one out.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that this is the fourth time in a yard that the man of about thirty-one has been asked to do a thing in this district's business and said no, and that four times out of five times he has been asked he has said no, **and that the refusal is not a refusal of a rule and is not entered as one, and that four is a count of things a man was asked and is not a column and is not a rate and is not added to the count of not-askings about anybody in particular.**

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that the reason the Underwriter's power is not begun in this district is that a man cannot list what he has posted, and that the man who would be standing in the list if it were written has just given the reason for it out loud in a yard, **and that a man who is the fifth of the five and has said the district's own reason better than the district has is not a column and is not a rate and is not going to be put in a book.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the list of the five is not written for the seventh time in a month and that the reason is entered and the reason is not a delay, **and that the reason has changed its shape five times since the second of January and has not moved, and that a shape is not a figure and the count of five did not move on the tenth of the third month.**

**AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY SAID THAT THE LIST IS NOT WRITTEN AND THAT A MAN WHO CANNOT WRITE A LIST CANNOT READ ONE OUT, AND THE LIST OF THE FIVE IS NOT WRITTEN FOR THE SEVENTH TIME IN A MONTH.**

---

Then the other thing, and it was in the yard at about half past three in the afternoon and it was the last thing in the ten days that anybody said that was not about a count.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said that a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said a thing to him on a road on the fourth of the third month, and that he is not going to write it down, and that he is not going to carry it in his head for the rest of his life either, and that he wanted to know what he was supposed to do with it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said that in a yard and that she was not asked about it and that she was in the yard and had come on her own account, **and that the two of them were in the same yard at the same hour on purpose for the eighth time since the eighteenth of December and that she was not asked to write the arranging down as anything and is not going to be.**

The registrar of this district's records office said it in the ordinary voice of a person who has thought about a thing for a week, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it is in the minute in her own words.

"**Put it in the book. The book is mine, not yours, and if you do not put it in then it is a document and it is not a document.**"

Nobody in that yard argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that the registrar of this district's records office refused in four seconds on the fifteenth of the second month to put a sentence said at a counter into the ward market's own book and gave the reason before she was asked, **and that she has now told a man that the only place his sentence could go is that book, and that the two of those are the same woman and are not two rules.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a records office may print a house and may not print a person, and that the rule arrived in this district in a lane on the second of the second month, **and that a man who has been refused a book and has then been offered the same book as the only place a thing can live has not been given a second chance and has been given a shape.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man the figure of twenty-one years is against what he was going to do, and he said that he was going to do nothing with it, **and that a man who has come back from four miles with a sentence in him and has been told there is one place for it and cannot put it there is a man with a sentence and is not a clerk of a records office and is not going to be one.**

A boy of nine was in that yard on the tenth of the third month with the woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him, **and that he was not asked a second thing and was not asked a first thing, and that his name is not in the minute and the minute says a boy of nine.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the boy is in a column in a building two days' walk away and is in a yard four miles from a records office and is in neither of the two books this district keeps, **and that a man who reads eight counts out loud in a yard on the eighth of this month and a boy of nine who is in none of them are two facts about one morning and are not a column and are not to be added.**

**AND A MAN CAME BACK FROM FOUR MILES WITH A SENTENCE IN HIM AND THE REGISTRAR OF THIS DISTRICT'S RECORDS OFFICE TOLD HIM THE ONLY PLACE IT COULD GO IS A BOOK SHE HOLDS, AND HE IS NOT GOING TO PUT IT THERE.**

---

The last of the tenth of the third month was the gate, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam read out at about half past four in the afternoon what the district had that it had not had on the twentieth of the second month, and it is in the minute in his own words and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-seven.

"**A man who came on his own and was not asked. A copy in a coat. A man who has said out loud that he is one man in a room. A stub in a woman's hand.**"

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that the second of the four is the one that is going to be a problem, **and that a page in a coat has an owner and that this district's entire method is a page with no owner, and that the three documents nobody owns are the only reason it has ever been able to prove a thing against a house and against a person, and that the two are not the same proof and are not joined.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of documents nobody owns is three and did not move on the tenth of the third month, **and that a copy a man of about twenty-three keeps in the inside of his coat is a copy and not a fourth, and that a man who bought his own paper for nine weeks is a person and not a keeper and is not anybody's.**

A man of fifty-six said the interval out loud at about half past four and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down.

"**The thirty-first of March is twenty-one days off this day and the first of April is twenty-two, and the first of April is the last day a person may put their hand to a notice.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked one question in the six things at about half past four in the afternoon and nobody answered it, and it went into the minute with nobody's name against it, and it is in his own words and it is thirty-three words.

"**The tank is at about a day and a half and a boy's name is in a column, and neither of them is on a board. Which of the two goes on first.**"

The gate stayed shut for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that the room met on the tenth of the third month with no instrument on it for the sixth time, and that about nine people came to a yard to find out what there is here to find out about, **and that there is a page four miles down a lane with about six spaces left in it and a stub in a woman's hand and neither of them is a thing a yard can act on.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a district which can count twenty-one days and cannot decide which of two things goes first is a district that has run out of the other kind of counting, **and that the fifth thing this district does not have is a way to say that a toll lands on a place, and that a tank and a column are two places and are not the fifth thing and are not a sixth.**

**AND ON THE TENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A QUESTION WAS ASKED IN THE SIX THINGS AND WAS NOT ANSWERED, AND IT WAS WHETHER A TANK OR A CHILD'S NAME GOES ON A BOARD FIRST, AND THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND NINETY-TWO DAYS.**
