# Chapter 194: Two Households At A Gate

---

She found it at about seven in the morning on the seventeenth of the third month and she did not have to go into the building to find it, because the butt is against the back wall and the lid was off it and there was about a foot of water in the bottom of it and none of it was rain.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam was on the lane at about half past seven and he had not been sent for and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him and that she is not going to ask him why he came at that hour.

He said that the channel four miles out past the loams is standing about two feet deep in about nine hundred yards of its length, and that a channel that has not carried water since the first of the second month is carrying it now, **and that nine hundred yards of standing water in a channel that goes past a butt is a backwash and that the word for it in this district is a word about a river and not about a thing anybody did.**

The woman of about thirty-eight who keeps the brick building said the other half of it and she said it from the top of the steps with her hands wet, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-one.

"**I have had a butt at nine days since the twelfth and it went out in one night, and I did not lose it in the night, it was here in the morning.**"

Nobody in that yard argued with that. A man of about fifty-six said that a butt that goes out overnight after nine days is a butt whose supply was never the butt, **and that a butt is a store and not a source and a store is not a water and the two of those are the whole of what has happened in four hundred yards of this district in one night.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about thirty-four who digs loam, in the six things, whether the water in the channel was the water he had put through the sluice on the night of the thirteenth, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that it was and that he would say so again in a yard and that he was not going to be asked to be sorry for it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about thirty-four who digs loam was not asked to be sorry and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a man who put nine hours of water through a sluice on his own account and whose water emptied a butt four hundred yards away is not a bearer of the channel and is not a keeper of the butt and is not a security and the count of six did not move on the seventeenth of the third month.**

Nobody took that up. The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace came down his own lane at about eight and stood at the corner of it looking at the water in the gutter, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked to come and was not sent for.

He said that his tank is at about nine days and that it has been at about nine days for four days, and that the mend in the crack is out of the crack and has been since the fifteenth.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether the mend coming out meant the water had gone somewhere, and the man of about fifty-five said that he did not know and that he was not going to guess, **and that a man who has a crack in a stone tank that has been put four times has not got a system and has got a stone and a crack and about nine days, and that the three of those are not a fourth of anything.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that about forty people are in that building and that a tank at about nine days for a building of that size is a figure and not a term, **and that a man who keeps a building is not its bearer and is not going to be entered as one, and that a tank in a building nobody bears is a tank nobody is responsible for, and that is a different sentence from the one about the man.**

**AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH THE BUTT AT THE BACK OF A BRICK BUILDING AT THE SLUICE END WENT OUT IN ONE NIGHT AFTER NINE DAYS, AND A CHANNEL FOUR MILES OUT THAT HAS NOT CARRIED WATER SINCE THE FIRST OF THE SECOND MONTH IS STANDING ABOUT TWO FEET DEEP IN ABOUT NINE HUNDRED YARDS.**

---

The man from the tram arch was in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about eleven in the morning on the seventeenth with a sheet and a box of nails and he was not sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him how many sheets he had printed and he said seven, in two months and a half, and gave the two rates himself in a sentence that a man of about nineteen counted and got twenty-four.

"**Four coppers for a sheet about one thing and twelve for a sheet about two things, and this one is about two things.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a sheet about two things costs twelve coppers and a sheet about one thing costs four and that both of those are his and not this district's, **and that a man who prints a house's claim at his own rate and not at the house's is a man who has put his own price in a house's document, and that a price in a document is a term and this district has no term on any of it.**

He said the second half of that himself and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in his own words, and he said he was not going to read the sheet out and that a sheet is read out by a person in this district and not by a man with a box of nails.

A clerk of nineteen years read it out in the yard and it took her about a minute, and he stood and listened to all of it and did not correct one word of it.

> **THE THIRD OF THE FOUR PRINTED THINGS. THE CLAIM OF A FAMILY TO ONE PLACE IN A FLOOD SHELTER AT THE SLUICE PASSES TO THE UNDERWRITERS OF THE UPLAND ON THE DAY NAMED AT THE FOOT OF THIS SHEET.**
>
> **THE DAY AT THE FOOT OF THIS SHEET IS THE TWENTY-NINTH OF DECEMBER AND NO OTHER DAY.**
>
> **THE CLAIM HAS PASSED. THE FAMILY HAS NOT BEEN ASKED AND THE FAMILY IS NOT NAMED.**

Nobody said anything for a moment. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the day at the foot of that sheet is the twenty-ninth of December, and that it is not on the sheet the family was given, **and that a house which has put the deciding day on its own sheet and not on the family's sheet has named a day in a place the family cannot get at, and that this district said the same thing about it in the second month and has not got a better sentence.**

It was not contradicted. A man of fifty-six said that the sheet is accurate, and that every figure on it is right, and that the day named is the day named, **and that a sheet with no wrong figure on it is a sheet nobody in this district can call a lie, and that a district that can only answer a lie has already lost the argument about the truth.**

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about nine people, whether anybody had checked the sheet, and he said no and gave the reason himself and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-nine.

"**I have not checked it and I will not check it, and a man who prints what a house gives him does not get to say the house was wrong.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked once and answered once, and that a man who declines four corrections in a season and a check on the seventh sheet is one man doing the same thing seven times, **and that a habit is not a finding and is not a column and is not a rate and is not going to be entered as one.**

A man of fifty-six said that this district has a remedy and the remedy is that a person who cannot read a sheet is told what is on it by a person who has read it, and that the man of about twenty-three is that person, and that the man of about twenty-three gave his only copy to a drawer in a building with a door on it on the sixteenth of the third month and the man with the slate found him on the fourteenth.

The man from the tram arch said no and said it fast, **and that a person who cannot read a sheet does not want a person with a slate to read it to her, and that the two of those are the same sheet and are not joined.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet is still on the wall of the yard of Lot Seventeen and that nobody has taken it down, **and that a sheet nailed to a wall in this district is not an instrument and is not a board and is not a page and is not entered as any of the four, and that the count of six did not move on the seventeenth of the third month.**

**AND A MAN FROM A TRAM ARCH NAILED THE SEVENTH SHEET HE HAS PRINTED TO THE WALL OF THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH, AND EVERY FIGURE ON IT IS RIGHT, AND HE DECLINED A CHECK AND SAID HE WILL NOT CHECK IT.**

---

She denied them on the eighteenth of the third month at about eight in the morning and she did it from inside her own gate with the gate open, and about fourteen people were standing in the lane and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

The first household is five people and the man of them is about twenty-nine and the woman of them is about twenty-six, and the second household is a man of about twenty and a woman of about forty-one, and none of the four has a name in any book this district keeps and the four are not four addresses.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in the six things, in front of everybody, why, and she gave the reason and gave it before he had finished the question and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the second half came out ahead of the asking and that she is not going to put a number on it.

"**That place is mine and it is the only place I have and it is the only place on that sheet, and if I let two households into it I have three claims on one place and a place with three claims on it is a place a house can take off me.**"

Nobody in that lane said the opposite. A man of fifty-six said that a person who has kept a building for four years and has one place in it has learned in four years what the sheet of four printed things does, **and that she learned it in four years and a house learned it before that, and that a person and a house learning the same thing at different speeds is not a coincidence and is not a column and is not entered as one.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam asked whether anybody was going to tell the man of about twenty and the woman of about forty-one what the reason was, and a clerk of nineteen years said that she had said it in a lane in front of about fourteen people and that it is in the minute in her own words, **and that a reason said in a lane is a reason and is not a notice and is not a finding and is not a thing two households can act on this morning.**

The man of about fifty-five who keeps the flood shelter came down his own lane at about half past eight and looked at the fourteen people and did not go to the brick gate, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him whether he would take them in, and he said no in about two seconds and gave the reason himself and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-two.

"**It is not my building and it is not my page and I have never turned anybody away in nine years.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the shelter at the top of that lane has not denied anybody entry at any time in nine years and is not going to, **and that that is not the same sentence as the one about a brick building four hundred yards away, and that the two buildings are two buildings and a man of thirty at a counter has said there are two and has never said there was one.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in that lane at about nine in the morning and he asked the woman of about thirty-eight one question and it was whether there was anything he could do, and she said that there was not and that she had not asked him.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man who offers to do something in a lane and is told there is nothing and does not go on being offered is a man who has been told a thing, **and that a man who has been told there is nothing he can do at a gate is not a bearer of that gate and is not a keeper of it and is not a party to it and cannot be held to it and is not going to be made one.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the district was going to do about it, and a man of fifty-six said that the district is going to do nothing about it in a lane, **and that the district opened a thing it could not name and did not know where the water went and has now got a butt four hundred yards away that is out, and that a district which opens a thing and cannot name the one who shuts it has moved the bill, and that he said that in a lane in the second month and is not going to be quiet about it now.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the district did not decide to empty the butt and did not know it would be emptied and has entered none of it as a decision, **and that the decision not to do anything about a tank on the twenty-eighth of the second month and the water that went down a channel on the night of the thirteenth are two things and are not joined, and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count at twelve and neither moved on the eighteenth of the third month.**

**AND ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WHO KEEPS A BRICK BUILDING AT THE SLUICE END DENIED TWO HOUSEHOLDS ENTRY IN HER OWN LANE, AND THE BUILDING FOUR HUNDRED YARDS AWAY HAS NOT DENIED ANYBODY ENTRY AT ANY TIME IN NINE YEARS.**

---

The afternoon of the eighteenth was the sheet on the wall, and about nine people stood in front of it for about an hour and a half and nobody took it down.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the sheet names a day the family's sheet does not and hands a place to a house without naming a family, and that those are two breaches and not one, **and that a person who has been told about a breach in a lane and cannot read is a person who has been told about a breach in a lane and has nothing in her hand to put in front of anybody else.**

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing came down the lane at about ten in the morning with nothing in his hands and had not been sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him and that she was not asked why he came.

He read the sheet on the wall out loud to the man of about twenty and the woman of about forty-one and to the man of about twenty-nine and the woman of about twenty-six, and he read it the way he reads out a document, which is one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them, and nobody interrupted him and the four of them did not stop him.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him what he was going to tell them, and the man of about twenty-three said that he was not going to tell them anything and that reading it out was the whole of it, **and that a man who cannot read a sheet and is told what is on it by a person who has read it is not the same as a person who was asked, and that the two are a remedy and not a consent and are not joined.**

A man of fifty-six said that this is the only remedy this district has ever had for a sheet and that it is a man, **and that a remedy that is one man standing in a lane is not an instrument and is not a keeper and is not a security, and that the man of about twenty-three is not the fifth of the five and is not going to be a sixth of anything.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam asked him whether he was going to keep a copy of what he had read out, and the man of about twenty-three said that the copy is in a drawer in a building with a door on it and that a copy in a drawer is not a copy in a lane, **and that he has read out about nine figures from a head and will not write them down in a lane, and that a figure a man writes down in a lane is a figure a house can ask him for.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that no second question was put to him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a man who will read a sheet out in a lane and will not write down what he read is a man doing the only kind of public work this district has, and is not paid for it and is not a keeper of it and the count of six did not move on the eighteenth of the third month.**

Then he said the thing he had come for, to the four of them and not to the yard, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-nine.

"**The day at the bottom of it is the twenty-ninth of December. It is not on the sheet you were given. I have got no way to make them take it off and I am not going to stand here and tell you that I have.**"

Nobody in that lane said anything about it for a moment. A man of fifty-six said that a man who reads a sheet out in a lane and then says what he cannot do about it is a man doing more for these four people in nine minutes than this district has done in four months, **and that a man who says what he cannot do is not a refusal and is not a column and is not a rate and cannot be counted either way and the record says asked and answered.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the four of them were going to do, and the man of about twenty-nine said that he did not know and that he was going to sleep, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked a second thing and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a sheet at about four feet by three nailed to a wall a man can walk up to is the only thing about this business that a person can do something with on a wet morning, **and that a sheet nailed to a wall is not an instrument and is not a page and is not a board and is not entered as any of the four, and that a man who reads it out is not an instrument and is not on a list.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether anybody was going to say any of this on a board, and a clerk of nineteen years said no in about two seconds and gave no reason, **and that a board is not an instrument and a sheet nailed to a wall is not a board, and that the fifth thing this district does not have is still the fifth and is not going to be named this morning.**

A man of fifty-six gave the interval out loud at about half past four in the afternoon and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down, and the sum went under the figure the way it always goes.

"**The thirty-first of March is thirteen days off this day and the first of April is fourteen, and the day at the foot of the sheet on the wall is the twenty-ninth of December and was seventy-nine days ago.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody wrote on a board on the seventeenth or the eighteenth of the third month, and that the board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for eighty-three days, **and that the boards number five and the lines number six and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty, and that a sheet nailed to a wall is not a board and the count of five did not move.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against looked at the sheet for about nine seconds and then said that he was not going to read it out in a yard and that there is no version of this district in which he does, **and that a man who has read a sheet out four times in a month has not become a clerk and is not going to be one, and the refusals to read are nine and were nine this morning.**

The man of about twenty-nine in the first of the two households was still in the lane at about five in the afternoon and nobody spoke to him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody spoke to him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a household which has been denied a place and is standing in a lane at five in the afternoon is a household which has not been asked anything and has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way.**

**AND ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS THIRTEEN DAYS AWAY AND THE DAY AT THE FOOT OF A SHEET NAILLED TO A WALL IS THE TWENTY-NINTH OF DECEMBER, AND TWO HOUSEHOLDS ARE STILL IN A LANE, AND THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND NINETY-NINE DAYS.**
