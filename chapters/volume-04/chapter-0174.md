# Chapter 174: The Tenth Line and the Fourth

---

A haulier of about forty who passes that road twice a week came up behind them at about the sixth mile and said good morning and then said nothing else for four miles, and a man of fifty-six said afterwards that a man who says good morning and then nothing for four miles is a man who has decided the conversation is over, and that this district has no instrument for that either.

A woman of about fifty-two who has the seventh holding at the top of a lane off the Verge and has had it since the fourth year was at the top of the road in a cart at about half past ten in the morning and had not been asked to be, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

They went up the road on the thirteenth of the second month, eleven miles to the second reservoir, and the reason they went was a tank four miles down a lane that has been full since the first of the second month and a fourth holding that did not take a drop of it, and there was no clerk on the road and no minute, and what came back was a page in a man's own hand which a clerk of nineteen years read out in the yard on the evening of the same day because a page is read out.

The man of about thirty at the counter came down the road to meet them, which is the fifth time he has come down that road, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he came and that nobody sent for him and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk of a house is a clerk of a house and not a friend of this district and is not to be promoted into one, **and that the man of about thirty at the counter has a book under his arm and has never once been asked to put it on a table in this yard, and that a book a house's clerk brings is not a book this district has.**

He had a delivery note with him about the size of a man's hand and he put it on the trestle table and turned it round, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and a man of fifty-six read it out twice and a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong.

> **NINE HOLDINGS ARE ENTERED ABOVE AND ONE LINE IS ENTERED BELOW THEM. THE LINE BELOW IS THE HOUSE'S OWN AND IS NOT A HOLDING. NO FIGURE IS ENTERED AGAINST IT AND NO PERSON SIGNS FOR IT.**
>
> **THE HOUSE DELIVERS TO A HOLDING WHEN THE PERSON WHO HOLDS IT OPENS A GATE. THE HOUSE DOES NOT DELIVER TO A HOLDING THAT DOES NOT TAKE.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the second of the two is the first sentence anybody has put in writing in this district's business that says out loud that a house is not obliged to do the thing this district has been waiting for it to do.

A man of fifty-six said that a house which writes that on the face of a note in the ordinary hand has done the thing this district spent the fourth week of December praising it for, and that the sentence is in the same breath as a line with nothing in it, **and that a house that tells you what it does not do is a house that has built a door where the wall was, and that a printed gap is cheaper than a hidden clause and this is a gap you can walk through.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two findings of the twenty-fifth of December and of today are the same finding said about two sheets, and that the resemblance is entered and is not added to anything, **and that a house which writes its terms on the face of every sheet is a house this district has been learning from for a month, and that what it has learned is how to refuse on paper without leaving a gap at all.**

**AND ON THE THIRTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A HOUSE'S CLERK READ OUT A DELIVERY NOTE THAT SAYS THE HOUSE DOES NOT DELIVER TO A HOLDING THAT DOES NOT TAKE, AND A LINE UNDER THE NINE HOLDINGS CARRIES NO FIGURE AGAINST IT AND NO PERSON SIGNS FOR IT.**

---

The seventh was not signed for, and the woman who has held it since the fourth year said why in a sentence and a clerk of nineteen years entered it in her own words.

"**Your man was not at the gate when it came and I am not going to sign a page for a delivery a man did not bring to me.**"

Nobody took that up. A man of fifty-six said that a holding which was not signed for because a clerk of a house was not standing at the gate is a holding that is not refused and not accepted, **and that a house which does not deliver to a holding that does not take has now been told by one of its own nine that the other side of that sentence is a gate with nobody at it.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two of the nine holdings were not signed for in the first week of the second month and that the two are the farm and the seventh, and that the two are not joined, **and that a woman who has not had to open a gate in nine years is a woman whose part of a delivery went into a tank and not into a field, and that the two are not the same and the district has not asked her which it was.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a head race a mile and a bit filled in about four hours in the first week and went to eight of the nine, and that a man who stands in the mud at a fourth holding until his boots go has told this district more about a delivery than the delivery note has, **and that a page in a man's hand about a day he spent in a field is not a minute and is not going to be entered as one.**

So two of the nine holdings were not signed for, and one of the two was not signed for because a clerk of a house was standing somewhere else, **and a holding that is not signed for is not a holding that has refused, and a man who is not at a gate is not a man who said no.**

---

Then the question, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked it in the yard and not in the six things, and the man of about thirty at the counter refused it in about four seconds and gave no reason at all.

"**Where does the water go, when the fourth does not take it.**"

The man of about thirty at the counter said no and gave nothing else, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked about it and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that this is the second refusal with no reason given by a clerk of a house in this district's business, that the first was on the first of the second month and was a question about whether the fourth is a holding, and that two refusals with no reason are two and are not a column and are not added to the thirteen.**

A man of fifty-six said that a refusal with no reason is not a refusal of a rule and is not entered as one, and that this district has a word for the second of those two things and not for the first, and that a man who has now refused twice without a reason has done a thing and is not going to be asked to do it a third time.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said that the answer to the question is written on the face of the note they have all just read out, and that a house which does not deliver to a holding that does not take is a house that has answered the question and answered it in advance, **and that a man who asks a second time after a house has written the answer on a piece of paper is not asking a question and is making a noise.**

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the first of the two refusals was a question about what a line is and the second is a question about where a thing goes, and that the two questions are not the same and that the man who refused both is a clerk and not a party, **and that a clerk who is not a party can be spoken to and is not obliged to answer, and that this is the whole of the law and this district has now had it said twice without a reason.**

**AND ON THE THIRTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED WHERE THE WATER GOES WHEN THE FOURTH DOES NOT TAKE IT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY AT A COUNTER REFUSED IN FOUR SECONDS WITH NO REASON GIVEN, AND THAT IS TWO REFUSALS WITH NO REASON AND NOT A COLUMN.**

---

The man of about thirty at the counter said the other half of a thing before anybody asked him for it, and it is in the minute in his own words and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted it and got thirty-five and said it out loud.

"**The house has not delivered to the fourth since the first of the second month and has not written to anybody about it, and a house that does not write is not obliged to say.**"

Nobody took that up for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that this district spent the eighth of the second month finding out that a house which has not written to anybody can write tomorrow, and that tomorrow has come a number of times since, **and that the finding has now turned round and is about a house, and that a house which does not write is not only holding a thing back, it is holding a thing up, and that the two are one silence and not two.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace said that a district which has spent a month learning that a house's silence is a weapon has just been shown that a house's silence is also a shield, and that the finding is the same finding seen from the other side, **and that a person who cannot tell a weapon from a shield by looking at it is a person who has not looked at it and has been told about it by two different people who both wanted something.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about thirty at the counter said the second half of that before he was asked for it and that she is not going to put a number on it, **and that the count of the second half said before being asked has not been looked at since the twenty-ninth of December, and that a house's clerk and a route keeper and a man who sells on a tram arch are three people in this district who will not say a figure and that the three are not a column and are not added to each other.**

Nobody argued. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, whether the house knows the eleventh of the second month has come and gone, and a man of fifty-six said that nobody in this yard knows and that the woman at the far end of that counter said out loud on the ninth that the house has not named it, **and that a house which does not name a day and does not write about a holding that stopped taking are the same house and the two are not joined, and that the district has now been told twice by the same building and has not been told once by a person.**

**AND A CLERK OF THE HOUSE SAID THAT THE HOUSE HAS NOT DELIVERED TO THE FOURTH SINCE THE FIRST OF THE SECOND MONTH AND HAS NOT WRITTEN TO ANYBODY, AND A HOUSE THAT DOES NOT WRITE IS NOT OBLIGED TO SAY.**

---

On the fourteenth of February they went out to the farm four miles out past the loams, and the lane stops being metalled for about the last half mile, and the three households that work the farm were at the top of the lane and were not asked anything, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the only reason they are not asked anything is that nobody in this district has ever said out loud what they would be asked.

Nobody took that up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the three households work a farm and none of them owns it, and that asking a household whether it wants a sluice open is asking a household to be an owner, and that none of them has agreed to be one and has not been asked, **and that the man of about forty-three who buys standing seed-crop offered two thousand four hundred coppers and a cart of meal for the standing crop and the sluice on the twenty-ninth of December and went west on the thirtieth, and that he has not come back and that nobody has sent for him.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the not-sending is entered and the count is where it was, and that a clerk of nineteen years has been asked once this month whether anybody is going to send for the man of about forty-three and has entered that the question was not answered and that nobody is going to be sent.

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures came up the lane behind them and was not asked for anything and said that the water at the shelter is coming down a little every day and that she has stopped carrying it up, and that this is the first time in nine years she has stopped, and that a man who digs loam is not to write that down in a place a house can read.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked about the tank and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that a woman who says a thing is not to be written where a house can read it has said the same sentence as a man of fifty-six in this yard and neither of them is going to have it written down.**

Then the sluice at the low end of the second field, and it was still open, and the water going through it is not counted by anybody, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that an uncounted sluice is the only instrument in this district that nobody has ever had to be given a term to, and that there was nobody at the farm and there has not been anybody at the farm since the thirtieth of December.

A man of fifty-six said the two things out loud in the yard on the evening of the fourteenth and a clerk of nineteen years entered both of them in one line and did not join them, **and that the tank at the back of the flood shelter at the sluice end of a lane off the lower terrace was full on the fourteenth of the second month, and that the fourth holding of the nine on a sheet nailed in a shed eleven miles up a road had not taken a drop, and that the two of those facts have now been entered in one line more than once and that she is not going to say how many times and that the number is not a column.**

Nobody took that up and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a yard that has two facts it will not join has found the only thing in this district's whole business that is worth not joining, **and that a man who dug a channel in the eighth month of last year can tell a person that most of what gets joined in this basin gets joined because somebody in a hurry wanted a page to look finished.**

A man of fifty-six said the interval out loud at about half past two in the afternoon on the fourteenth of the second month and a clerk of nineteen years stood close enough to write it down.

"**The thirty-first of March is forty-five days off this day and the first of April is forty-six, and the eleventh of the second month was three days ago and was forty-eight and forty-nine, and a figure with no day on it is not an interval.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the district has now said that sentence about a figure six times in five weeks and that six is a count and not a column, **and that a clerk of nineteen years entered the count of unentered days since the twenty-fourth of November as eighty-two on the fourteenth of the second month, and that the two figures are on two pages and are not the same figure.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count is eighty-two and is a count of days and not a column, and that the days are not added to the askings and the askings are not added to the days, **and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve and that neither moved on the thirteenth or the fourteenth of the second month.**

**AND ON THE FOURTEENTH OF THE SECOND MONTH THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH WAS FORTY-FIVE DAYS AWAY AND THE FIRST OF APRIL FORTY-SIX, AND THE TANK FOUR MILES DOWN A LANE WAS FULL AND THE FOURTH HOLDING HAD NOT TAKEN A DROP, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED BOTH OF THOSE IN ONE LINE AND DID NOT JOIN THEM.**

---

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing himself before anybody asked him for it, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is twenty-eight words.

"**A line with nothing in it and a space with nothing in it are the same shape and I want them joined and I do not know why.**"

A clerk of nineteen years said no in four seconds and gave a reason and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted it and got twenty-one.

"**A house rules a space for itself. A district rules a space for a keeper. Those are not the same space.**"

Nobody took it up. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the refusal is the fourteenth she has given in this district's business and that it is entered in the book she keeps and not in this yard, **and that the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve and neither moved on the fourteenth of the second month, and that a resemblance noticed by a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing is a resemblance and not a finding.**

A man of fifty-six said that a man who notices a shape twice in three weeks and asks for it to be joined both times has found the one thing this district's method cannot do, and that the method can look at things and cannot tell two of them apart, **and that the count of lines full on the boards of this district is six and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty, and that a board and a book are not the same instrument and the two are not joined.**

The last of the day was the note going back into the man's book, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, at about half past four in the afternoon, what the line under the nine holdings is for, and the man of about thirty at the counter said no and gave nothing else.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked in the six things and answered and was not asked why, **and that a line with no figure against it and no person signing for it is a third thing and not a column and that a third thing has no name in this district, and that a thing this district cannot name is not automatically an instrument, and that the count of six did not move on the fourteenth of the second month.**

A man of fifty-six said that a book with nine holdings in it and a tenth line under them that is the house's own is a book about the house and not about the water, and that the house has kept a line for itself under the only instrument anybody in this basin can go and look at.

Nobody argued. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a line a house keeps for itself is a toll with no bearer on it, and that he has said that sentence about a tank in this yard and is not going to say it twice in one week, **and that a toll with no bearer on it is the exact shape of the fifth thing this district does not have, and that the shape is not the thing and is not to be entered.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way of saying that a toll lands on a place, and that a line a house keeps for itself under nine holdings is a resemblance and not a finding, **and that the count of things this district does not have is five and did not move, and that a resemblance is not a sixth and was not entered.**

**AND A HOUSE HAS KEPT A LINE FOR ITSELF UNDER THE ONLY INSTRUMENT ANYBODY IN THIS BASIN CAN GO AND LOOK AT, AND IT CARRIES NO FIGURE AGAINST IT AND NO PERSON SIGNS FOR IT, AND THAT IS A RESEMBLANCE AND NOT A FINDING.**
