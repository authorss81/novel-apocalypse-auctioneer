# Chapter 159: The Fifth of the Five

---

The room had no date on it and was called for a purpose, and the purpose was carried by a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide, who went along the lane off the lower terrace and the two streets behind it on the eighth of January in the afternoon and told nine people a thing that was not true, which was that a man was going to be told something in a room and had agreed to it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the woman told nine people and that nobody counted the nine out loud and that a count of what people have come to watch is a column this district does not have, **and that the woman was asked, in the five things, whether the man had agreed, and she said he had, and that what she had told the nine was that he had agreed to be told and not that he had agreed to anything else.**

He had been asked, in the five things, on the seventh of January, at a forge end off Salt Row, and he had said he would come, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked before he came and that asking a man whether he will be told something is not consent and is not a release, **and that for nine years the record on the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row says not asked, and that a record which says not asked is not a refusal and is not a yes, and that both of those words are now in the same minute about the same man.**

About a dozen people came on the ninth of January and among them were four of the six who hold the ledger, and the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row came in at about ten in the morning and stood by the door for a while and did not sit down, and nobody asked him to.

He had come early and had been standing outside the gate since about a quarter to ten, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went out to him at about half past nine and came back in and gave four things in the yard in his own words, and a clerk of nineteen years entered all four and did not join them.

The four were that he had a forge to open and had left a boy in it, that he had walked because a cart would have been two hours, that he had thought about not coming about nine times on the road, and that he had a question and had decided to ask it in the room and not on the road.

Nobody asked him which of the four was the reason and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked and that she was not going to ask and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

A man of fifty-six said that a man who leaves a boy in a forge to find out whether he has been a security for nine years is a man who has already decided something and came to hear whether the room agreed, **and that the district has spent eleven weeks building a room where a person can find out a thing and has had about nine people come to it and one of the nine is the reason the room was wanted.**

**AND ON THE NINTH OF JANUARY THE ROOM HAD NO DATE ON IT AND WAS CALLED FOR A PURPOSE, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY AT A FORGE END OFF SALT ROW STOOD OUTSIDE THE GATE FROM A QUARTER TO TEN AND WAS ASKED ON THE SEVENTH WHETHER HE WOULD COME AND SAID HE WOULD, AND HE WAS TOLD IN THE ROOM THAT HE HAD BEEN A SECURITY SINCE THE NINTH YEAR AND SAID YES BEFORE ANYBODY ASKED HIM ANYTHING, AND FOR NINE YEARS THE RECORD ON HIM SAYS NOT ASKED, AND A RECORD WHICH SAYS NOT ASKED IS NOT A REFUSAL.**

---

They told him at about half past ten in the morning and it took about nine seconds, and a man of fifty-six read it out of the box and did not read it out of the fourth of the five, and the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row said yes before anybody asked him anything, which a clerk of nineteen years entered as a thing that is not consent and is not a refusal, **and entered that a man who is told he has been a security and says yes is not a man who has agreed to have been one, and that the two are nine years apart and the room cannot close the nine years.**

Then he said the other thing, and it took him longer, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-seven words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got thirty-seven both times and did not say so.

"**I would have said yes if anybody had asked me, and I have not been angry about it for nine years, and I want that written down in words that I have not been angry about it.**"

Nobody took it up. A clerk of nineteen years entered the request and entered that she was asked to write it and did, **and entered that a man who has not been asked for nine years and has not been angry and says so is not a person who has consented to anything, and that a record of not being angry is not a consent and does not close the ninth year.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the second half of that is the hardest sentence anybody has said in this yard in eleven weeks and that it is a man doing the district's work for it and getting nothing for it, **and that a district which needs a person to be gracious about its own instrument has one person and no instrument, and that the two are not joined and the two are not added, and that a man who cannot be angry about a thing is a man who has not decided the thing was a wrong, and that the two are two things and that nobody in the room is going to decide which of them it is for him.**

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against got up and went and came back with a paper, and the paper is a bond of the ninth year and it is the fourth of the five, and a man of fifty-six asked him to read the third of the three lines out loud, and he read it and then read it again.

> **GIVEN: ONE DRAY AND ITS TWO SHAFTS. THE SAID DRAY IS DISCHARGED UPON SALE. THE PERSON GIVING IT SHALL NOT BE ASKED.**

The room did not argue for about nine seconds and then a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide said the thing that made the rest of the morning, and it is in the minute in her own words and it is thirty-five words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got thirty-five and said it out loud.

"**You put that man in a bond with a line in it that says he shall not be asked, and you are the man who wrote the line, and that is the whole of it.**"

**AND ON THE NINTH OF JANUARY THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST READ OUT A BOND OF THE NINTH YEAR WHOSE THIRD LINE SAYS THAT THE PERSON GIVING IT SHALL NOT BE ASKED, AND THE LINE IS HIS, AND HE SAID HE TOOK IT OFF THE DISTRICT'S OWN THIRD FORM OF A NOTICE, AND NINE YEARS IS HOW LONG IT TOOK A MAN WHO KEEPS A FORGE TO POINT AT IT.**

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said that before he was asked for it and gave the reason after, and the reason is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-nine words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got thirty-nine and did not count them again.

"**The third form had three lines on the face about being asked and not being asked and I had been handling that form all year and I took the shape of the sentence and not the sense of it.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that a form a district prints in order to protect a person has been turned inside out on a bond by the man who prints most of them, **and that a sentence can be carried from a sheet to a sheet with its shape and without its work, and that the district has found that about figures twice already and neither time about a sentence.**

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row said one thing about the sentence and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-six words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got thirty-six and did not count them again.

"**I have been a security for nine years and it is written down nine yards from where I stand and I did not know, and that is not a thing anybody can argue with me about.**"

A man of fifty-six said that he was right and that it was the answer to the question a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had asked in a yard on the twenty-sixth of December, which was how many the man the figure of twenty-one years is against has posted, and that the answer is five and that a man who has answered it in a room has answered it out of his own mouth and not out of a box.

---

At about half past eleven in the morning they read the other four of the five out one at a time, and the argument about the fourth of them is the one a clerk of nineteen years entered as a finding and the other three went without one

And it took about half an hour.

And the fourth of the five is a dray and its two shafts, given in the ninth year and sold in the fourteenth, and a man of fifty-six said that a security which was discharged on the sale is not a security and that the paper says so on its own face, and that the district has been calling all five of them a security and one of the five says on its face that it stopped being one in the fourteenth year.

Then he asked who received the payment on the sale, and the room did not know, and a man of fifty-six said that a security is discharged when somebody has received what it was given for, and that nobody in this yard can say who received it, and that the receipt went into the world with a dray and two shafts and a man who mends wheels for a living.

The room let it stand and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row has now been told that a paper about him stopped being a security in the fourteenth year

And a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was told it and that he said nothing and that she was not asked why, **and that a discharged security is only discharged if somebody received it, and that the man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper has been asked a thing about a receipt and said once in November that a receipt is the difference, and that the two occasions are not joined.**

Then the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row asked his question from where he was standing by the door, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is twenty-six words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got twenty-six both times and did not say so.

"**If the fourth of your five stopped being a security because a man paid for a dray, what stopped me being one, and who took it.**"

Nobody took it up and a man of fifty-six said that he was right, and that a man who posts a person has posted a person, and that nothing in this district can be got back off a figure by the person the figure is on, and that the twenty-one years standing against his own name is a figure of the same kind and is not a different kind of thing.

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the two of them are the only two people in the yard with a figure of the same kind standing against them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said the second half before she was asked and that the count of that is not a number she keeps, and that a number on it would have been a column and a column is a thing a person is paid for.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood up, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he stood up, **and that this room has not had a man stand up to say one thing in eleven weeks and that the standing up is entered and is not a finding, and that the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row turned round when he did it.**

**AND A SECURITY WHICH WAS DISCHARGED ON THE SALE IS ONLY DISCHARGED IF SOMEBODY RECEIVED IT, AND NOBODY IN THIS YARD CAN SAY WHO RECEIVED IT, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO MENDS A DRAY AT A FORGE END OFF SALT ROW ASKED WHAT STOPPED HIM BEING ONE AND WHO TOOK IT, AND NOBODY ANSWERED HIM.**

Then the room came to the part that would not go, and a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide put it in a sentence, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the room had gone round the question three times that morning and had come back to the same place, **and that three times round a question is not a finding and is not a column and is not a rule and is three,** and it is in the minute in her own words and it is forty-eight words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got forty-eight both times.

"**A security is a thing a stranger can go and look at. You cannot post him without writing him where a stranger can read him, and I have refused one reading of my son's name in a market and I will not pretend the two men are different.**"

The room let it stand and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said that the sentence in the counter's hand is twenty-five words and it has been in the market's book since the eighth of March, and that it is true of a district and not of a man and that nobody in the room can find the man it is not true of, **and that the rule which came into this yard on the second of January says a person is a thing a stranger can go and look at and that the man of about thirty-one passes it by accident and that the accident is the only reason he can be posted at all.**

**AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD HOUSE ON THE TIDE SAID THAT A MAN CANNOT BE POSTED WITHOUT BEING WRITTEN WHERE A STRANGER CAN READ HIM AND THAT SHE HAS REFUSED ONE READING OF HER SON'S NAME IN A MARKET AND WILL NOT PRETEND THE TWO MEN ARE DIFFERENT, AND NOBODY ARGUED WITH HER.**

---

The page was written at about one in the afternoon in the ward market's own book, and the registrar of this district's records office wrote it and a man of fifty-six said so and she did not say that writing a page and reading a page out are two different instruments and that a person can be perfectly good at one of them.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the registrar of this district's records office wrote the page and did not read it out, and that the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice and that the four go out twice on the morrow, and that a reader who is about fifty will read the fourth of the four first and then the first because people read the top of a page.

A man of fifty-six asked what the page was to be called, and a clerk of nineteen years said it did not have a name and that nobody had given it one, and that she was not going to give it one on her own account because a heading a person writes for a book is a heading a person can be asked about.

The page has a heading and four entries under it and about two inches of empty paper at the foot that is not a line and was not ruled, and a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper asked what the two inches was for and was told that nothing was for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the space at the foot of the page is not a line and was not ruled.

A man of fifty-six asked her whether she was going to rule it and she said no in four seconds, and gave the reason in a sentence, and the sentence is in the minute in her own words and it is twenty words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty and said it out loud.

"**A line somebody rules is a line somebody expects to be filled in, and this one is not expecting anybody.**"

Nobody argued with it and the man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a page with a heading and nothing against the heading is a shape this district has already got one of and that the one it has got is a hundred and eighty-three coppers, **and that the two figures are not added and there is no rate in this basin that turns one into the other and that a heading is a heading and a page is a page and the two are two things.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure of a hundred and eighty-three coppers is a page in that book with a heading and no name against it and was read out on the twenty-second of December and again on the first of January and is not to be struck, **and that the answer is not to be struck, and that two pages in one book with nothing against the heading are two pages and are not a column and are not a pattern.**

Then the man about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row was asked, in the five things, whether he wanted the four entries read out at a counter on the morrow.

And a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked and that he said yes and that the four are a day a week, a covered bay, a right to a pitch and a dray that was discharged, **and that none of the four is a sum and that the fifth is a man and that the fifth is not on the page and that the reason is not on the page either and that a page with no reason under it is a page that has been put there by a person who is not asking to be argued with.**

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not write the list that day and a man of fifty-six asked him afterwards, in the yard, why not, and nobody asked him in the room and that was entered, **and that a man who answers a question in a yard instead of a room has told a yard and not a room, and that he chose the yard and that the two are two things**, and he gave the reason in a sentence and the sentence is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-nine words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got thirty-nine both times and did not say so.

"**With his name in it I publish a man. Without his name in it I publish that I posted a man, and both of those are the ward market counter, and I have no third place to put it.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the man who can be found had said yes in four seconds on the second of January to publishing everything he had ever posted, and that this is what yes turned out to be, and that a yes in four seconds is a cheap thing to say and an expensive thing to have said, **and that he was told the cost on the seventh of January at a forge end off Salt Row by being asked whether he would come, and that the asking was the cheapest part.**

Then the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row said the last thing of the morning, and he said it to the man the figure of twenty-one years is against and not to the room, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it to him and that she was not asked to leave and did, **and that the four of the five are entered on a page in a book in a ward market and that this is not one of the four and is not entered in that book, and that the two are two things.**

He said it in twenty-four words and a clerk of nineteen years counted them once and got twenty-four and did not count them again, and she entered that she did not count them again and that the not-asking about this is entered and that the count of a man of about thirty-one who mends a dray at a forge end off Salt Row is not a column and carries no number on it.

"**You came to the forge in the ninth year and you asked me for the dray and you did not ask me anything else.**"

Then he went back to the forge, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said in the yard that a man who has been a security for nine years and has never been asked has now asked one question in one day, **AND THE LIST WAS NOT WRITTEN ON THE NINTH OF JANUARY, AND FOUR OF THE FIVE ARE ON A PAGE IN THE WARD MARKET'S BOOK, AND THE FIFTH IS A MAN WHO WENT BACK TO A FORGE AT ABOUT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING AND ASKED ONE QUESTION, AND A QUESTION A PERSON ASKS IS NOT A FINDING AND IS NOT A COLUMN.**
