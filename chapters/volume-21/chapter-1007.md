# Chapter 1007: The Two Hundred And Ninety-First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And Nobody Said A Reason And Nobody Said No And The Man Of About Twenty-Nine Who Drives The Cart Came Through The Gap In The Top Of That Bank And Did Not Stop In It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and forty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries nine hundred and five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand two hundred and twenty-one days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and thirty-five days. Eight hundred and ninety-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Six hundred and ten days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Six hundred and fifteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and eighty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and eighty-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is six hundred and twenty-four days past a printing it did not make. Five hundred and sixty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and fifty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table.

Seven is what a clerk has counted, and it is on nothing. This district does not own four documents. Four places in this district can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it. Fourteen is what this district has made, and five is what this district does not have, and the fifth of the five is unpaid.

Nobody said a reason out loud in that yard this morning and nobody said no and nobody asked anybody anything, so the page at the back of that lot book stood at fifty-nine lines and the morning went on in the clerk's own hand without a line. The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came through the gap in the top of that bank at the ordinary hour and did not stop in it, and nobody said a figure out loud behind him, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a figure does not follow a man out of that yard. The lane count is thirty and it did not move and nothing came down that lane. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

A person said one reason out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and there was nowhere on the back of that book for a reason to go before the two hundred and eighth morning, so nothing was written for it and it is counted nowhere at all.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and twenty-one mornings, and the near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and seventy-one mornings, and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and ninety-one mornings, on the word of the man who keeps it, and he said that number before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and ninetieth morning, and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The man of fifty-six has read four figures off that wall on the seven hundred and thirty-second of those mornings. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and forty-sixth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and forty-five of them, and this is the one hundred and forty-seventh morning running of nobody asking him anything. The man of fifty-six's flat answer is the hundred and eightieth time running.

That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning, and it is not moved by a decision. Nothing came down that lane. The page at the back carries fifty-nine lines and carried fifty-seven lines when she shut the book. The record has stood two hundred and six days.

A man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and thirty-sixth afternoon running. About nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning. There are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

The toll of ninety-eight is paid and spent and may not be raised again or paid twice. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read.

---

You get a whole yard in one look through that gap, and there is no better view of a yard anywhere on this road.

He had been coming up this hill with an empty cart for longer than the second table had been standing at the foot of that bank, and the gap in the top of the bank was the one place on the whole climb where the ground drops away on both sides and you can see straight down into a place where about nineteen people are working.

---

He did not stop in it.

There was one morning in that run when he pulled up in that gap and sat there and looked down at that yard the whole time it took a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and he had not done it since and he was not going to start.

So he came through it at the ordinary speed with both hands on the shafts and did not take his eyes off the slope in front of the cart, which is what a man does who is not stopping.

---

What he got in that one look was this.

The tables are down there in two lines with a rail on the near side of the second one, and there is a man at the near end of the second table who is not doing anything at all. He is standing with his hand flat on a corner of it and a cloth hanging off the other hand and he is looking down at the cloth.

There is a man about nine feet from him with a basket on her arm and a scale in front of her and her back is to the hill.

There is a wheel turning at the near end of the ground and it does not stop for anything and it does not change for anything.

There is a boy at some boards with his elbows on them.

And there is a man at the wall at the far end with his arm up and his mouth going to a stone, and he has been doing that longer than the cart has been coming up this hill.

---

That is the whole of what that yard is from nine feet up.

Four seconds of it. He has been up this hill in a cart on more mornings than he could put a number to, and it has never once had a moment in it from up here, and it has never once looked like a place where anything had ever happened.

That was the finding of that morning, and he took it down the far side of the bank with him, and it was not a good one and it was not a bad one.

---

Nobody said a reason out loud in that yard that day. Nobody said no either. It is a yard where a great deal gets said and on that particular morning nobody said the kind of thing that goes anywhere, and he came off the top of the bank not knowing what he would find at the bottom of it, and there was nothing to find, and the wheel was going.

He did not put his hand flat on a corner of anything that morning. He had four days of road in front of him and a cart to grease at the far end of it.

---

At about four feet off the near end of the second table the man of about thirty-four stood with his cloth over his hand and thought about a whole number of mornings, which he does about twice a week, and he has never once said any part of it out loud.

He had not asked the cart anything when the cart came through the gap. The cart does not ask anybody anything. It has been coming up that hill since before the second table was there and neither of them has ever had one word with the other and that is the arrangement they have got.

---

The boards went up at about ten to eight and the boy said the count out loud at them and it was thirty and there was nothing after it.

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six had his four figures off that wall before there was light on it.

Just after nine the second table went a foot further along the hard ground and the near rail came with it, and the man at the end of that table got his boot on the wedge under the leg that rocks and stood on it.

At about half past nine a man came down the slope with his hands showing and went out along the top of his own table and round the end and up the bank and back down and in at the same corner, and he said nothing out loud at any point of that day, and nobody in that yard asked him anything.

---

At the north end the man who digs loam was in a strip of turned ground and the spade was going in and coming out and he had it open for the whole morning.

She came down that bank at half past twelve with the basket on her arm and set up about four feet off the near end of the second table and weighed what was in it and put the figures on the edge of her own board in chalk and said nothing out loud to anybody that day.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number out into about four hundred yards of air and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with one another and it came back off the top of that bank.

---

He did not stop in that gap in the afternoon either, coming back up with the cart loaded and the shafts heavy in his hands.

The yard was still down there in two lines with a wheel going at the near end of it and a woman at a scale and a boy at some boards, and he came through the top of that bank without taking his eyes off the road in front of him, and nothing at the foot of that bank had changed in the four hours between the two times.

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a figure does not follow a man out of that yard.

---

The sun came over the low wall a little after half past three and lay itself along the stones at the foot of it, and the man standing in that strip said good afternoon to the man sitting about four feet off him, and got one word back, and neither of them said anything else.

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six was at his wall for the second time and had his four off it and laid his hand flat on the stone under the lowest one.

"These four are for a use and the use is not in this yard," he said out loud to the stone, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and eightieth time running, and nobody asked him anything about it.

---

The clerk read that morning out loud at about four and gave the account of it before she had the pen near any of it, and then she wrote the morning in her own hand under the last line and took no line for it.

Fifty-nine lines were on that page when she shut the book and fifty-nine lines were on it after.

The light came off those boards at about half past four and about four people out of about nineteen said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard, and about nine people at the top of that bank did not join in and stood there until the boards came down off the cart face down.

---

Fifty-nine lines are on that page and not one of the fifty-nine is in the lot book, and it did not move on this morning, because nobody said a reason and nobody said no. The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came through the gap in the top of that bank in four seconds in the morning and again in the afternoon and did not stop in it either time, and nobody said a figure out loud behind him, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a figure does not follow a man out of that yard. The lane count is thirty and nothing came down that lane. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read. The fifth of the five is unpaid.
