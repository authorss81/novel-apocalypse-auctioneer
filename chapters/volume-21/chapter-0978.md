# Chapter 0978: The TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-SECOND Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Of About Thirty-Four Said One Reason Out Loud Once About Himself At About Four Feet Off The Near End Of The First Table And The Page At The Back Took One Line Carrying The Morning And Not The Reason And Nobody Answered Him And Nobody Thanked Him And It Carries Fifty-Five Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and seventeen days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and seventy-six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and ninety-two days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and six days. Eight hundred and sixty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and eighty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and eighty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and fifty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and fifty-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and ninety-five days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and thirty-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and twenty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. Seven is what a clerk has counted. It is still seven. Four documents this district does not hold. Four places can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it.

Nobody at those boards put anything to the man of fifty-six. One reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back of that lot book took one line for it. The line carries the morning. It does not carry the reason. Nobody has been thanked for it. This district does not have five things, and it is not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and ninety-two mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and ninety-one. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and forty-two mornings and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and sixty-two, on the word of the man who keeps it. It was said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and sixty-first morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. That is the seven hundred and third of those mornings. The lane count is thirty. No page came down that lane. It is not moved by a decision. That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it. It has a column with one name. The heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The page at the back carries fifty-five lines. It did not carry one more today. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the seven hundred and third of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and seventeenth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and sixteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and seventy-seven days, and the page at the back took one line this morning and the morning was read out loud once before it was written. That morning was read out loud, once, and written after it. Nothing came down that lane. None of it is in the lot book. None is on the page at the back. The second space there is empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and then he stood at the near end of the first table with his hands behind his back.

---

---

By the time the light had come the whole way across the eleven feet the boards were up and about nine people at the top of that bank were on their way down it, and at the near end of the first table a man of about thirty-four stood with his hand flat on the top of it waiting for the yard to fill.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and put his hand flat on the stone and left it there while the light came across the eleven feet and reached the place his hand was, and nobody asked him anything.

---

At about ten to eight the boy said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure, and then went and stood at the near end of the first table with his hands behind his back and waited for the light to come across the boards.

---

A little after nine that second table went a foot further along the ground and the near rail came with it on the near side. The man at the end of that table came round the outside of it and put his heel down on the wedge under the leg that rocks and sat down with his weight still on it.

---

The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and took his place at about four feet off the near end of the first table, nine feet along from the corner. The man of about thirty-two put his own table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel and went along the top of it with the cloth, and the turner's wheel went on turning and the man who makes round things said nothing out loud.

---

At about eleven the man of about thirty-four said it, once, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about nineteen people's hearing. "I have been squaring up the leg of that table every morning for longer than most of the men at it have stood at it, and my knee on that side is the reason for that, and that is all of it, and I am going back to the end of it." Then he went back to the end of it.

---

Nobody answered him. Nobody thanked him. Nobody asked him to say it again and nobody asked him what the reason was for and nobody asked him whether it was already written down anywhere, and nobody at those boards asked him one word about any of it, and the turner's wheel went on turning over the whole of it. The man of thirty-eight worked on at about four feet off the near end of that table for the rest of the morning and did not look over at him, and the man at the end of the second table put his head down over his own work, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet went on with what they had been doing.

---

The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve and set up about four feet off the near end of the second table and weighed three things and chalked three figures on the edge of her own board and said nothing out loud to anybody that morning. She heard him. She had the chalk in her hand at the time and she heard a man at the other end of that yard say one thing out loud and put her chalk away and weigh the next one.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number into about four hundred yards of air and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and nobody wrote that one down either. And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man said one thing out loud at that yard this morning about himself, and that about four of them have not said what it is to hear a person say a plain thing about their own body where about nineteen people can hear it, and that about four of them have said nobody answered him, and that about four of them have not said what would have happened if somebody had answered him.

---

The page at the back of that lot book has a place about four inches from its foot where the last line does not sit level with the ones above it, because there is a worn place in the paper there and the clerk has never ruled it out. Nobody in that yard has asked her about the worn place and she has never mentioned it, and the line she wrote this morning went over the top of it.

---

About half past three a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off a man of about sixty-four at the foot of that low wall. They said good afternoon. A man of sixty-one said good afternoon. He said nothing else. Nobody went near him, and he was not asked one question.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he still does not know what the four of them are for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and fifty-first time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And then the clerk read that morning out loud at about four feet off the near end of the second table, once, in the ordinary voice, and nobody at those boards asked her to read it again, and she wrote it after.

---

"The two hundred and sixty-second morning," she said. "One line goes on the back of that book this morning and the line carries the morning and nothing else goes on it. I have given you the account out loud before I wrote it, and that is the whole of the account."

---

Then she wrote the morning under the last line on that page in her own hand and took no line for it, and the pen went back into the holder, and nobody at those boards said one word about the page while she did it.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stayed on that bank while the boards came down. The boards came down, and the second table stood where it stands, and the near rail was on its near side. And the man of about thirty-four stayed at the end of the first table after the boards had come down and put his own boot on the wedge under the leg that rocks and stayed there with his weight on it. The woman who keeps a scale tied the cloth over her scale and went up that bank with the basket on her arm.

---

That page carries fifty-five lines. None of them is in the lot book. Three of the first thirty-three were never read. Seven is what a clerk has counted. Fourteen is what this district has made. Five is what this district does not have. The fifth of the five is unpaid. A line there is not a payment. A gatepost sheet carries four hundred and eleven. By how much, nobody has done. The toll is ninety-eight. It is spent. It may not be raised or paid twice. The low wall's ground is public lot. Since the one hundred and seventeenth afternoon. It is not a payment of that. The bid ran four hundred and fifty days. It was not run. It is not a lot. It will not be. The heading over that name says whoever. The name does not satisfy the rule. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The other space is on different paper. It is empty as well. The record has stood one hundred and seventy-seven days. Nobody asked what it is days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six one thing. Nobody asked the man of thirty-eight one thing. One reason was said out loud in that yard this morning. One line went on that page carrying the morning. It did not carry the reason. Nobody thanked the man who said it. Nobody asked him for it again. A woman has weighed things there nine years. She has not been given anything. She has not asked for anything. He has no bed, no wage, no office. He has asked for none of the three. The lane count is thirty. It did not move. That page is at fifty-five lines. Nobody asked anybody anything about any of it.
