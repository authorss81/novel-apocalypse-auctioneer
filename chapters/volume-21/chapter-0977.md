# Chapter 0977: The TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FIRST Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Woman Of About Fifty-Two Came Down That Bank With A Frame And A Bag And Asked Nothing And The Second Space On That Form Was Empty In Front Of Her And She Did Not Fill It And Nobody Asked Her To And The Page At The Back Carries Fifty-Four Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and sixteen days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and seventy-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and ninety-one days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and five days. Eight hundred and sixty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and eighty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and eighty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and fifty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and fifty-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and ninety-four days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and thirty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and twenty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. Seven is what a clerk has counted. It did not change. Four documents are not held here. Four places can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it.

The man of fifty-six was left alone to work this morning. No reason was said out loud this morning. Nobody said no. So that page did not move. The morning went on in her own hand. Five is what this district does not have and it stays unpaid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and ninety-one mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and ninety. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and forty-one mornings and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and sixty-one, on the word of the man who keeps it. It was said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and sixtieth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. That is the seven hundred and second of those mornings. The lane count is thirty, unchanged. It is not moved by a decision. That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it. It has a column with one name. The heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The page at the back carries fifty-four lines. There was no fifty-fifth. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the seven hundred and second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and sixteenth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and fifteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and seventy-six days, and the page at the back took no line this morning. The morning was read out loud, and then it was written. Nothing came down that lane. None of it is in the lot book. None is on the page at the back. The second space there is empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard the whole of that and said nothing.

---

The light found the eleven feet and the boards went up, and about nine people at the top of that bank came down it, and at the top of it a woman of about fifty-two stood a while before she came off it at all.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six stood at that wall and said the four off it and got all four, and put his hand flat on the stone under the bottom one, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

---

At about ten to eight the boy said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard the whole of that and one of them asked him what he was doing and he said it again.

---

Just after nine the second table went a foot further along the ground and the near rail came with it and set down hard, and the man at the end of that table came round the outside of it, put his heel down on the wedge, and stayed standing there a while with his weight on it before he sat.

---

The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing, and worked at about four feet off the near end of the first table with his back to the boards, and nobody said one word to him. The man of about thirty-two put his own table up about four feet along from the second table, squared it with the back of his heel, and took the cloth along the top of it, and the turner's wheel went on turning.

---

At about eleven the woman of about fifty-two came down that bank with a frame in her right hand and a bag over her left shoulder. The frame was a wooden one, about the size of a door off, and there was nothing in it. She carried it down the slope in front of her with the bottom edge clear of the ground, the way a person carries a thing they do not want scratched. She put the frame down in the sun at about four feet off the near end of the first table and stood it against the leg of it, and then she took the bag off her shoulder and set it on the ground beside her, and she took a form out of the bag and looked at it and put it back and took it out again.

---

Nobody said good morning to her. She said nothing to anybody. She asked nothing at all that morning, and nobody at that yard asked her anything either, not of the tables and not of the boards and not of the man of thirty-eight working nine feet along from where she was standing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman carried a frame down that bank at about eleven with nothing in it and that about four of them have not said what a frame with nothing in it is for.

---

The form had two spaces on it and the first one had something in it and the second one was empty, and it was empty there in front of her with the light on it for the best part of an hour. She did not put anything in it. Nobody at those boards asked her to. Nobody at those boards said one word to her about it at any point of this morning, and she folded the form once and put it back in the bag and zipped the bag.

---

She stood the frame against the leg of that table, and picked it up again before long and stood it against the near rail instead, and looked through it at the second table and the people at it, and it made the middle of that yard look further away than it was. She did not say anything about what she was doing.

---

The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve and set up about four feet off the near end of the second table and weighed two things and put the figures on the edge of her own board in chalk, and said nothing out loud, and the woman with the frame did not come over to her and did not ask her anything. At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number into about four hundred yards of air and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and the turner heard it over his own wheel and said nothing. Nothing was written down anywhere in that yard for it.

The frame she carried down that bank has a rebate cut round the inside of it, and nothing has ever been in the rebate, and the corners of the rebate are worn through where the wood has been lifted out and put back a number of times. About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the corners of that frame are worn through and that about four of them have not said what it is that goes in a frame like that.

---

At about half past three that yard had about four feet of sun on the stones and two men on it, a man of about sixty-four at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one four feet off him. They said good afternoon. A man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else. He was not asked one question, and nobody went near him.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he still does not know what the four of them are for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and fiftieth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And then the clerk read that morning out loud at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in the ordinary voice and once only, and she read it before her pen went near the page.

---

"The two hundred and sixty-first morning," she said. "No line goes on the back of that book today and the morning goes on in my hand. I have said it out loud so that the yard has one account of it and not two."

---

Then she wrote the morning under the last line in her own hand and took no line for it, and the page carried fifty-four lines before her hand went down and fifty-four lines after.

---

The light came off the boards at about half past four and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard went with it, said by about four people out of about nineteen. About nine people at the top of eleven feet looked up the bank once first. And the woman of about fifty-two picked the frame up off the near rail where she had stood it, and put the bag on her shoulder, and went up that bank with the frame in her hand and nothing in it, and the second space on that form went up with her in the bag and it was still empty.

---

That page carries fifty-four lines. None of them is in the lot book. Three of the first thirty-three were never read. Seven is what a clerk has counted. Fourteen is what this district has made. Five is what this district does not have. The fifth of the five is unpaid. A line there is not a payment. A gatepost sheet carries four hundred and eleven. By how much, nobody has done. The toll is ninety-eight. It is spent. It may not be raised or paid twice. The low wall's ground is public lot. Since the one hundred and seventeenth afternoon. It is not a payment of that. The bid ran four hundred and fifty days. It was not run. It is not a lot. It will not be. The heading over that name says whoever. The name does not satisfy the rule. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The other space is on different paper. It is empty as well. The record has stood one hundred and seventy-six days. Nobody asked what it is days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six one thing. Nobody asked the man of thirty-eight one thing. No reason was said out loud this morning. No line went on that page. A woman has weighed things there nine years. She has not been given anything. She has not asked for anything. He has no bed, no wage, no office. He has asked for none of the three. The lane count is thirty. It did not move. That page is at fifty-four lines. Nobody asked anybody anything about any of it.
