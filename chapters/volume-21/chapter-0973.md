# Chapter 0973: The TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SEVENTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Woman Who Keeps A Scale Said One Figure Of Her Own Out Loud Once At Her Own Scale And Nobody Answered Her And Nobody Thanked Her And Nobody Asked Her What She Weighs Things For And The Page At The Back Carries Fifty-Four Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and twelve days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and seventy-one. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and eighty-seven days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and one days. Eight hundred and sixty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and seventy-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and eighty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and fifty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and fifty-four days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and ninety days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and thirty marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and sixteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. Seven is what a clerk has counted. It did not move. Four documents this district has not got. Four places can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it.

The man of fifty-six was not asked anything out loud this morning. No reason was said out loud this morning. Nobody said no. So that page did not move. The morning went on in her own hand. This district does not have five things. They are not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and eighty-seven mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and eighty-six. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and thirty-seven mornings and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and fifty-seven, on the word of the man who keeps it. It was said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and fifty-sixth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. That is the six hundred and ninety-eighth of those mornings. The lane count is thirty. Nothing came down that lane. It is not moved by a decision. That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it. It has a column with one name. The heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The page at the back carries fifty-four lines. No fifty-fifth went on it. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and ninety-eighth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and twelfth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and eleven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and seventy-two days, and the page at the back took no line this morning. That morning was read out loud once, and written after. Nothing came down that lane. None of it is in the lot book. None is on the page at the back. The second space there is empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard every word of it.

---

---

By the time the light had come the whole way across the eleven feet the boards were up, and about nine people at the top of that bank were on their way down it. The woman who keeps a scale was already at the foot of it with the cloth still knotted round her scale.

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall, one after the other and without a pause you could put a finger into, and got all four of them, and put his hand flat on the stone, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and nobody asked him anything.

At about ten to eight the boy said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty. He said no figure after it, and he said out loud that he was saying no figure, and he stood at the near end of the first table with his hands behind his back until the boards stopped being interesting to him.

---

The second table went a foot further along about nine and the near rail came with it and set down hard on the same ground, and the man at the end of that table came round the outside of it, put his heel down on the wedge, and sat down with his weight still on it.

---

The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and took his place at about four feet off the near end of the first table. He worked. Nobody in that yard said one word to him out loud and he said none, and he did not stop when the boards came off the cart behind him.

---

The man of about thirty-two put his table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel and went along the top of it with the cloth, and the turner's wheel went on turning, and at the far end of the second table the man who makes round things put a thing down and picked it up again and said nothing out loud at any point of this morning.

---

The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with the basket on her arm and set up about four feet off the near end of the second table, and untied the cloth and weighted it over the back of the leg with the stone from her pocket. She weighed a bar. She waited for it to come still. She wrote the figure on the edge of her own board in chalk and said nothing to anybody. She did two more the same way, and after the third she stood with the chalk still in her hand instead of putting it away. Nine years, and she could not have told anybody what was different about this morning. The scale was the same scale, and the board was the same board with the same figures on it going down the edge in her own hand. What she could tell, and did not tell anybody, was that the figure was in her mouth and that she had put it there herself and that nobody at those boards had asked her for it. She had no intention. She had simply run out of ways of not saying it.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number out into about four hundred yards of air and four people said it after him out of step with each other, and the turner heard it over his wheel and said nothing. She was standing at her own scale with her hand on the upright.

---

And then she said it, once, in the ordinary voice, at her own scale, about four feet off the near end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing. She said the figure she had weighed last, out loud, into the open air of that yard, and she said it to the middle distance and not to anybody, and then she put the chalk in her pocket.

---

Nobody answered her. Nobody asked her what she weighs things for. Nobody thanked her and nobody asked her to say it again, and nobody asked her where the figure had come from, and nobody at those boards put one line on the page at the back of that lot book. The turner's wheel went on turning. The man who digs loam went on at the north end. The man of thirty-eight worked at the near end of the first table and did not turn round. About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard a figure go out of that yard at about half past one and then went on doing what they had been doing.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the woman with the scale put a figure of her own out loud this morning for the first time in nine years, and that about four of them have not said what it is like to have nine years of figures on a board and say one of them out loud to nobody, and that about four of them have said nobody stopped her, and that about four of them have not said what anybody did with it afterwards.

---

She went on weighing after that. She weighed three more things and chalked all three on the edge of her own board, and she said none of those out loud, and her hands were not steady for the first of the three and were steady for the last of them.

The man who digs loam has been at the north end of that yard for longer than most of the tables have been in the places they are in. He has never asked that yard for anything he could have got by digging, and nobody at those boards has ever asked him what the strip is for, and both of those things have held for the whole of this stretch.

---

A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall at about half past three, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him. They said good afternoon. A man of sixty-one said good afternoon. He said nothing else. He was not asked one question.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he still does not know what the four of them are for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and forty-sixth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And then the clerk read that morning out loud at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in the ordinary voice and once, to about nineteen people, and she read it before she put her pen to anything.

---

"The two hundred and fifty-seventh morning," she said. "Nothing goes on the back of that book today, so the morning goes on in my hand, and that is all I have to say about it and I have said it out loud."

---

Then she wrote the morning under the last line in her own hand and took no line for it, and the page carried fifty-four lines before she shut the book and fifty-four lines after.

---

At about half past four the light had gone off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen. About nine people at the top of that bank did not join in, and they stayed. and the second table stood where it stands. And the woman who keeps a scale stayed where she was, at her own scale about four feet off the near end of the second table, while the rest of that yard went up the bank. She stood at her own scale about four feet off the near end of the second table until the last of the others had gone over the top, with four figures chalked on the edge of her own board and one of them out loud in about four hundred yards of air behind her.

---

That page carries fifty-four lines. None of them is in the lot book. Three of the first thirty-three were never read. Seven is what a clerk has counted. Fourteen is what this district has made. Five is what this district does not have. The fifth of the five is unpaid. A line there is not a payment. A gatepost sheet carries four hundred and eleven. By how much, nobody has done. The toll is ninety-eight. It is spent. It may not be raised or paid twice. The low wall's ground is public lot. Since the one hundred and seventeenth afternoon. It is not a payment of that. The bid ran four hundred and fifty days. It was not run. It is not a lot. It will not be. The heading over that name says whoever. The name does not satisfy the rule. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The other space is on different paper. It is empty as well. The record has stood one hundred and seventy-two days. Nobody asked what it is days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six one thing. Nobody asked the man of thirty-eight one thing. No reason was said out loud this morning. No line went on that page. A woman has weighed things there nine years. She has not been given anything. She has not asked for anything. He has no bed, no wage, no office. He has asked for none of the three. The lane count is thirty. It did not move. That page is at fifty-four lines. Nobody asked anybody anything about any of it. A figure went out in the open air that morning. It is not on that page and not in the lot book. It did not go down that lane.
