# Chapter 1008: The Two Hundred And Ninety-Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And The Turner's Wheel Went On Turning Through The Whole Of That Morning Without Slowing And The Man Of Thirty-Eight Was At The Near End Of The First Table And Was Asked Nothing Out Loud And Said Nothing Out Loud

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and forty-seven days is the age of that figure.

The board carries nine hundred and six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand two hundred and twenty-two days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and thirty-six days. Eight hundred and ninety-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Six hundred and eleven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Six hundred and sixteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and eighty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and eighty-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is six hundred and twenty-five days past a printing it did not make. Five hundred and sixty-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and fifty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table.

Seven is what a clerk has counted, and it is on nothing. This district does not own four documents. Four places in this district can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it. Fourteen is what this district has made, and five is what this district does not have, and the fifth of the five is unpaid.

The turner's wheel went on turning through the whole of this morning without slowing and the man of thirty-eight was at the near end of the first table and was asked nothing out loud and said nothing out loud, and no reason was said out loud by anybody in that yard, so the page at the back of that lot book stood at fifty-nine lines and the morning went on in the clerk's own hand without a line. The lane count is thirty and it did not move and nothing came down that lane. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

Somebody put a reason into the air of that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and the back of that lot book was not a place a reason could go until the two hundred and eighth morning, so no line went on it for that reason, and that reason is counted nowhere.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and twenty-two mornings, and the near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and seventy-two mornings, and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and ninety-two mornings, on the word of the man who keeps it, and he said that number before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and ninety-first morning, and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The man of fifty-six has read four figures off that wall on the seven hundred and thirty-third of those mornings. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and forty-seventh night of that run, having slept on six hundred and forty-six of them, and this is the one hundred and forty-eighth morning running of nobody asking him anything. The man of fifty-six's flat answer is the hundred and eighty-first time running.

That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning, and it is not moved by a decision. Nothing came down that lane. The page at the back carries fifty-nine lines and carried fifty-nine lines when she shut the book. The record has stood two hundred and seven days.

A man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and thirty-seventh afternoon running. About nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning. There are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

The toll of ninety-eight is paid and spent and may not be raised again or paid twice. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read.

---

That wheel is the plainest sound in that yard and he has been making it for longer than the near rail has been standing on the near side of that second table.

It does not have a meaning in it. It is not going anywhere and it is not carrying anything and it does not stop when something happens and it does not change when something else happens, and every figure that has ever gone into that yard has gone over the top of it.

---

He put his heel on the treadle at about half past seven and it came up to speed inside of a minute, and after that it was the ground he was standing on rather than the work.

On some mornings he slows it without meaning to. He has done it twice this week and there was nobody within twenty feet of him at either time and the wheel came back up to the speed it goes at and nobody in that yard noticed that it had been anywhere else.

This morning he did not slow it once.

---

There was a bar of wood on that wheel that had been on since before the light.

He took it off at about ten and held it out at arm's length and turned it in front of his own eyes the way he has turned a hundred of them, and it went all the way round without a break in it, and the edges of it ran sound from one end to the other.

He put it down on the stand and picked up another bar out of the rack.

Nobody in that yard will ever know that, and he did not want anybody in that yard to know that, and he was pleased about it for about as long as it takes to get the next piece of wood up to the height of the wheel.

---

Across the ground, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, the man of thirty-eight was working.

He had come down that slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and he had gone out along the top of his own table, round the end of it, up the bank, across and back down and in at the same corner, and he had been doing it every half hour or so since, and he had not said one word out loud at any point of that day.

Nobody has said one word out loud to him either.

---

That is the arrangement of that man at that end of that ground, and the wheel lets him have it.

From where he stands the turner can see the whole of the man of thirty-eight's route without turning his head, because the route is about thirty feet long and it is all in front of him. He has watched that route go round and round for longer than he would say.

Two men in one yard, about forty feet apart, saying nothing at all, and the whole of the day going on round them.

---

At about half past twelve she came down that bank with her basket on her arm and set up about four feet off the near end of the second table, and the man who digs loam was at the north end with his strip open and the spade going in and out of it, and at the far end of the second table a man had a small stove going and something in it that wanted watching.

At the boards the boy had the lot book wedged against the rail and his elbows along the top of the rail, and he had said the count out loud at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and had told that yard there was nothing after it.

---

The man of about thirty-four was at his own corner of that table with his cloth over his hand.

He squared the corner at about ten and went along the top of the table from the near end to the far end and back again, and then he stood at the corner with his hand flat on it and looked at nothing in particular for the better part of an hour.

Every figure in that yard comes to him where he stands, and so does every sentence, and what comes out of that corner of that table goes up that bank about four hundred yards.

He has never once walked over to a scale in that yard. He has never once asked anybody anything. He is not a man anybody in that yard goes to, and there is a wheel turning at the near end of the ground that has never once been stopped by him.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for it.

It came across that yard and went over the turner's head and hit the bank and came back a little thinner, and about four people said it after the man out of step with each other.

The turner had it over his own work the whole time it went by and it made no difference to him whatever. He had been turning that wheel since before that man came to that bank with a tally, and he was not going to change a thing about his morning because a figure had crossed his head on its way to a slope.

---

The light came over the low wall a little after half past three and lay down along the stones at the bottom of it.

The man standing in that strip of sun had been standing in it since about nine, and a man of sixty-one was sitting about four feet off him on the stones with his coat folded beside him where he folds it every afternoon.

"Good afternoon," the one on the stones said across to him.

"Afternoon," said the man at the foot of the wall, and then the sun went on coming down the stones and neither of them said anything else at all.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six had his four figures off that wall and got all four of them, and he put his hand flat on the stone under the lowest one and left it there.

"You can get them all four every morning and it does not tell you what they are for," he said out loud to the stone in the voice he uses for that wall.

About nineteen people in that yard heard that for the hundred and eighty-first time running, and about four people at the top of eleven feet had not asked him one question about any part of it since before the middle of the winter came on.

---

The clerk read that morning out loud at about four feet off the near end of the second table and gave the account of it out loud before there was a mark of it on the page.

"The two hundred and ninety-second morning," she said. "Nothing has been said out loud in this yard this morning that goes on the back of that book, so the morning goes in my hand by itself. That has gone out loud before any of it was written."

She wrote the morning and took no line for it, and the page at the back of that lot book carried fifty-nine lines when she shut it and carried fifty-nine lines after.

---

He came off that treadle at about half past four and let the wheel stand.

That is the only silence that yard makes in a day and he has let it stand at that hour for longer than that table has been in its present place, and this afternoon he stood at the near end of his own ground with his hands down at his sides and let the nothing stand there a while.

Across that ground the man of thirty-eight had come in at his corner and was putting his cloth away, and the boards came down off the cart face down, and about nine people at the top of that bank stayed where they were until the last of them had gone.

The bar of wood that had come off the wheel at ten was lying on the stand where he had put it, and it was still round.

---

Fifty-nine lines are on that page and not one of the fifty-nine is in the lot book, and it did not move on this morning. The turner's wheel went on turning through the whole of that morning without slowing and a bar of wood came off it at about ten and was round all the way, and nobody in that yard will ever know it. The man of thirty-eight was at about four feet off the near end of the first table from about half past nine until the light went off those boards and said nothing out loud at all and was asked nothing out loud. The lane count is thirty and nothing came down that lane. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read. The fifth of the five is unpaid.
