# Chapter 0971: The TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIFTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Woman Who Keeps A Scale Chalked A Figure On The Edge Of Her Own Board And Did Not Say It And The Boy Said The Lane Count And Said No Figure After It And Said So Out Loud And The Page At The Back Carries Fifty-Four Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and ten days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and sixty-nine. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and eighty-five days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and ninety-nine days. Eight hundred and sixty days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and seventy-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and seventy-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and forty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and fifty-two days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and eighty-eight days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and twenty-eight marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and fourteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. Seven is what a clerk has counted. It did not go back this morning. This district does not own four documents. Four places here can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it.

Nobody put anything to the man of fifty-six out loud this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything either. No reason was said out loud this morning. Nobody said no. So that page did not move. The morning went on in her own hand. Five is what this district does not have. It is not paid. The five things are still not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and eighty-five mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and eighty-four. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and thirty-five mornings and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and fifty-five, on the word of the man who keeps it. It was said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and fifty-fourth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. That is the six hundred and ninety-sixth of those mornings. The lane count is thirty. It did not move this morning. It is not moved by a decision. That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it. It has a column with one name. The heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The page at the back carries fifty-four lines. It did not carry one more. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and ninety-sixth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and tenth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundredth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and seventy days, and the page at the back took no line this morning. The morning was read out loud first. It was written after that. Nothing came down that lane. None of it is in the lot book. None is on the page at the back. The second space there is empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard the whole of that and said nothing back.

---

---

The boards went up when the light had found the eleven feet, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, one behind another and none of them hurrying the one in front. The wind came off the top of the bank flat, the way it does when there is nothing much behind it, and it lifted the corner of the lot book off the rail it was wedged against.

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six stood at that wall and said the four off it, one after the other and without a pause you could put a finger into, and he got all four. He put his hand flat on the stone and left it there while he looked at the four again, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything about any of it.

At about ten to eight the boy said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure, and then stood at the near end of the first table with his hands behind his back until the boards stopped being interesting to him.

---

Just after nine the second table went a foot further along that ground and the near rail came with it on the near side of it, and the man at the end of that table came round the outside and set his own boot on the wedge and sat down again without looking at anybody.

---

The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and worked at about four feet off the near end of the first table, where he has worked. He did not stop for anything and did not turn round when the boards came down off the cart behind him, and he said nothing out loud at all this morning, and nobody in that yard put anything to him out loud at any point from the boards going up to the light going off them.

---

The man of about thirty-two put his own table up about four feet along from the second table, squared it with the back of his heel, and went along the top of it with the cloth, working from the far end back to the near end and taking the same width of cloth every time. He did not speak. He has never spoken at that table. At the far end of the second table a man who makes round things had his apron off, his sleeves rolled and a bar in his hand. He turned one of those round things over twice while he thought about it and set it down again, and he said nothing out loud, and nobody at that table could point to a morning of any stretch on which he did.

---

The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with the basket on her arm, and set her own scale up about four feet off the near end of the second table. She untied the knot, folded the cloth over the back of the leg, weighted it with a stone she carries in her pocket for that, and stood at it a while before she put anything on it.

---

Then she took a bar out of the basket, set it on, and waited for it to come still, and wrote the figure on the edge of a board of her own with the bit of chalk she keeps in the same pocket as the stone. She looked at what she had written. She ran her thumb along the chalk, put it back in the pocket, and went on standing at the scale, and she said nothing to anybody.

---

Nobody stood close enough to have heard it if she had said it. She has nine years of figures on that board in her own hand and a bit of chalk worn down to a nub, and she has never once read one of them out loud to anybody, and she did not start this morning, and nobody in that yard knew there was a figure on the board at all.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman with a scale has stood at that bank for nine years without ever once saying where she weighs what, and that about four of them have not said what it is like to have a figure in one hand and a chalk in the other and no use for either, and that about four of them have said nobody asked her this morning either, and that about four of them have not said whether that is the same thing.

---

The number the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said went out at about half past one into about four hundred yards of air. The turner heard it over his own wheel and did not stop turning and neither of them said anything, and nobody in that yard wrote it down. About four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, each of them a half beat behind the last. He put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards, waited about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and then went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it. And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the woman with the scale said nothing this morning at the point where that number goes into the air, and that about four of them have not said whether that was because she was not asked or because nobody was going to hear, and that about four of them have said the figure on her own board is where it was, and that about four of them have not said what anything is going to be done about it.

There is a place at the near end of that first table where the grain of the wood has been rubbed out of the top by thirty-eight going at it, and it is the only part of that table anybody in that yard would call worn. About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man has worn a patch out of a table without once being asked why he was doing it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near him and he was not asked one question.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he still does not know what the four of them are for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and forty-fourth time running, and nobody asked him anything. He has been putting his hand on that patch of stone for so long that it is a paler colour than the stone around it, and he finds it without looking for it.

---

And then the clerk read that morning out loud at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in the ordinary voice and once only, to about nineteen people at the foot of that bank, and she read it before she put her pen to anything.

---

"The two hundred and fifty-fifth morning," she said. "Nothing goes on the back of that book this morning, so the morning goes on it in my hand and nothing else does. Say it back to me if you did not hear it."

---

Then she wrote the morning under the last line on that page in her own hand and took no line for it, and the pen went back into the holder, and the page carried fifty-four lines before she put her hand flat on it and fifty-four lines after.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard went with it, said by about four people out of about nineteen. About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not join in and did not go up. The boards came down the way they come down, and the second table stood where it stands, and the near rail was on the near side of it.

---

And then the woman who keeps a scale tied the cloth over her scale, knotted it, and put the basket on her arm, and she did not go up that bank with the rest of them. She stood where she was until the last of the others had gone over the top, and then took the chalk out of her pocket and looked at the figure on the edge of her own board, and did not say it, and went up that bank.

---

That page carries fifty-four lines. None of them is in the lot book. Three of the first thirty-three were never read. Seven is what a clerk has counted. Fourteen is what this district has made. Five is what this district does not have. The fifth of the five is unpaid. A line there is not a payment. A gatepost sheet carries four hundred and eleven. By how much, nobody has done. The toll is ninety-eight. It is spent. It may not be raised or paid twice. The low wall's ground is public lot. Since the one hundred and seventeenth afternoon. It is not a payment of that. The bid ran four hundred and fifty days. It was not run. It is not a lot. It will not be. The heading over that name says whoever. The name does not satisfy the rule. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The other space is on different paper. It is empty as well. The record has stood one hundred and seventy days. Nobody asked what it is days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six one thing. Nobody asked the man of thirty-eight one thing. No reason was said out loud this morning. No line went on that page. A woman has weighed things there nine years. She has not been given anything. She has not asked for anything. He has no bed, no wage, no office. He has asked for none of the three. The lane count is thirty. It did not move. That page is at fifty-four lines. Nobody asked anybody anything about any of it.
