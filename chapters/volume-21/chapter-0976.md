# Chapter 0976: The TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTIETH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Second Number Went Into That Yard On The Same Morning And It Was Not Hers And The Man Who Makes Round Things Said One And Nobody Has Said What Either Number Was About And The Page At The Back Carries Fifty-Four Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and fifteen days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and seventy-four. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and ninety days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and four days. Eight hundred and sixty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and seventy-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and eighty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and fifty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and fifty-seven days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and ninety-three days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and thirty-three marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and nineteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. Seven is what a clerk has counted. It did not go back. This district owns no four documents. Four places can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it.

Not one thing was put to the man of fifty-six out loud this morning. No reason was said out loud this morning. Nobody said no. So that page did not move. The morning went on in her own hand. Five is what this district lacks, and the five are not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and ninety mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and eighty-nine. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and forty mornings and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and sixty, on the word of the man who keeps it. It was said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and fifty-ninth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. That is the seven hundred and first of those mornings. The lane count is thirty. It did not move, and it is not moved by a decision. That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it. It has a column with one name. The heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The page at the back carries fifty-four lines. It did not carry a new one. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the seven hundred and first of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and fifteenth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and fourteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and fifth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and seventy-five days, and the page at the back took no line this morning. She read that morning out loud first, and wrote it after. Nothing came down that lane. None of it is in the lot book. None is on the page at the back. The second space there is empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and one of them asked him why and he said it again in the same voice.

---

The boards went up when the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At the far end of the second table the man who makes round things had his sleeves rolled and a thing in his hand and the fire going in the small stove at the north end of that table.

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them and laid his hand flat on the stone afterwards and stood there with it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything about any of it.

---

At about ten to eight the boy said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and then stood at the near end of the first table and looked at the boards until they stopped being interesting to him.

---

At about nine that second table went a foot further along the ground and the near rail came with it on the near side. The man at the end of that table came round the outside of it and put his heel down on the wedge under the leg that rocks and stayed there a while with his weight on it before he sat.

---

The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and worked at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and the man of about thirty-two put his own table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel and went along the top of it with the cloth, and the turner's wheel went on turning.

---

The man who makes round things worked at the far end of the second table all morning and said nothing out loud to anybody at any point before about one. He has been at that end of that table for a long stretch of mornings and the only words anybody there has ever had from him have been about where to put a thing down. He turned a thing over and looked at the place where it had been out of the fire and put it back in. He took it out again and looked at the same place. He has been doing that at the same end of that table for longer than the near rail has been on the near side of it.

---

The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve, before that, and set up about four feet off the near end of the second table and weighed three things and chalked three figures on the edge of her own board and said nothing out loud all morning. She heard both of the numbers go out that morning. She said nothing about either of them, then or afterwards, and she could not have told you afterwards which one she had heard first.

---

At about one he said a number out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, at the far end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing. He said it while he had his thumb on the rim of the thing and he said it to the man next to him and not to anybody else. Then he turned the thing over again, put it back on the stone to cool, and said nothing else for the rest of the morning, and the man next to him went on with what he was doing and did not look up.

---

Nobody answered him. Nobody asked him what the number was about. Nobody asked him where the number came from and nobody asked him to say it again, and nobody at those boards put a line on the page at the back of that lot book, and the man at the end of the second table put his head down over his own work.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number into about four hundred yards of air, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and the turner heard it over his own wheel and said nothing. Two numbers had gone out of that yard that day and about four hundred yards of air had taken both of them, and nothing at those boards had moved for either.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that two figures went out on one morning and that about four of them have not said which of the two heard both, and that about four of them have not said what either number was about, and that about four of them have said the man at the far end of the second table has never said one out loud in any stretch of mornings anybody can remember, and that about four of them have not said what changed.

At the far end of that second table the thing he said out loud at about one had been out of the fire twice before he said it and back in once, and he had not cleaned it, and there is no more in that yard this morning about what any of it was for than there was before he opened his mouth at about one.

---

A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall at about half past three and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him. They said good afternoon. A man of sixty-one said good afternoon. He said nothing else. Nobody went near him. He was not asked one question.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he still does not know what the four of them are for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and forty-ninth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And then the clerk read that morning out loud at about four feet off the near end of the second table, once, and she read it before she wrote anything, and about nineteen people at the foot of that bank heard it.

---

"The two hundred and sixtieth morning," she said. "There is nothing going on the back of that book and the morning goes on it in my hand, and I have said that out loud and I will say it again tomorrow."

---

Then she wrote the morning in her own hand under the last line on that page, and took no line for it, and the page carried fifty-four lines before she shut it and fifty-four lines after.

---

By about half past four the light had come off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen. About nine people at the top of that bank went on talking it over after it. The boards came down the way they come down. And the woman who keeps a scale tied the cloth over her scale and went up that bank with the basket on her arm, and the man who makes round things put the fire out in his stove at the far end of the second table and went up behind her, and neither of them said anything to the other on the way up.

That page carries fifty-four lines. None of them is in the lot book. Three of the first thirty-three were never read. Seven is what a clerk has counted. Fourteen is what this district has made. Five is what this district does not have. The fifth of the five is unpaid. A line there is not a payment. A gatepost sheet carries four hundred and eleven. By how much, nobody has done. The toll is ninety-eight. It is spent. It may not be raised or paid twice. The low wall's ground is public lot. Since the one hundred and seventeenth afternoon. It is not a payment of that. The bid ran four hundred and fifty days. It was not run. It is not a lot. It will not be. The heading over that name says whoever. The name does not satisfy the rule. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The other space is on different paper. It is empty as well. The record has stood one hundred and seventy-five days. Nobody asked what it is days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six one thing. Nobody asked the man of thirty-eight one thing. No reason was said out loud this morning. No line went on that page. A woman has weighed things there nine years. She has not been given anything. She has not asked for anything. He has no bed, no wage, no office. He has asked for none of the three. The lane count is thirty. It did not move. That page is at fifty-four lines. Nobody asked anybody anything about any of it. Two numbers went out into that air that morning. Neither one is on that page.
