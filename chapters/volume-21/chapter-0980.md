# Chapter 0980: The TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOURTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Of Thirty-Eight Said Nothing Out Loud At All From About Nine Until About Four And Nobody In That Yard Asked Him Anything Out Loud At Any Point Of It And The Page At The Back Carries Fifty-Five Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and nineteen days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and seventy-eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and ninety-four days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and eight days. Eight hundred and sixty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and eighty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and eighty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and fifty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and sixty-one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and ninety-seven days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and thirty-seven marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and twenty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. Seven is what a clerk has counted. It did not go back this morning. Four documents are not in this district. Four places can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it.

The man of fifty-six got no question out loud from anybody this morning. No reason was said out loud this morning. Nobody said no. So that page did not move. The morning went on in her own hand. The five things this district does not have are not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and ninety-four mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and ninety-three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and forty-four mornings and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and sixty-four, on the word of the man who keeps it. It was said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and sixty-third morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. That is the seven hundred and fifth of those mornings. The lane count is thirty. It has not moved, and it is not moved by a decision. That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it. It has a column with one name. The heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The page at the back carries fifty-five lines. No new line went on it. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the seven hundred and fifth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and nineteenth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and eighteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and seventy-nine days, and the page at the back took no line this morning. That morning was read out loud and written afterwards. Nothing came down that lane. None of it is in the lot book. None is on the page at the back. The second space there is empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and nobody in that yard said one word back to him about either part of it.

---

---

The light came across the eleven feet and the boards went up, and about nine people at the top of that bank came down it in the loose line they come down in, and at the foot of it the ground was already being worked at about four feet off the near end of the first table.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and the two men nearest him went on with what they were doing and did not look over at him.

---

At about ten to eight the boy said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and said no figure after it and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and one of them asked him why and he said it again in the same voice he says everything in.

---

Just after nine the second table was carried a foot further along and the near rail came with it, and the man at the end of that table came round the outside of it, set his own boot on the wedge, and sat down again.

---

The man of thirty-eight came down that slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and took his place at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and he worked there from about nine until about four without saying one word out loud at any point of it. He worked with his hands and not with a tool. He brought the pieces in off the ground where they had been left and set them in a line along the near edge of that table, and when the line was full he went back for more, and he came back the same way each time, so that anybody watching long enough could see the route he took and know the whole of it. He has no coat on in that weather. He has a place at that table that nobody has given him and nobody has taken off him and nobody at that bank has ever put a question to him about any part of it.

---

At about ten the man of about thirty-two put his own table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel and went along the top of it with the cloth. The turner's wheel went on turning. At the far end of the second table the man who makes round things said nothing out loud at any point of this morning.

---

At the north end the man who digs loam had the strip open and did not come out of it. Nobody at those boards said good morning to him and he said nothing out loud to anybody. The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with her scale in a piece of cloth and set it up about four feet off the near end of the second table and weighed three things and chalked three figures on the edge of her own board. She said nothing out loud. She did not look over at the near end of the first table and she was nine feet from it.

---

Nobody in that yard asked the man of thirty-eight anything out loud at any point from the boards going up to the light going off them. Not the man at the end of the second table, who was eight feet off him. Not the boy, who stood at that corner twice. Not the woman with the scale, who worked nine feet away for three quarters of an hour.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man of thirty-eight has worked at about four feet off the near end of the first table every morning of this stretch without anybody in that yard asking him one thing out loud, and that about four of them have not said what a man does with a morning that nobody takes any part in, and that about four of them have said he came down that slope at half past nine and went up it after the light went off the boards, and that about four of them have not said what he would have said if anybody had asked him.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number out into about four hundred yards of air and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and nothing at those boards took it down.

The man of thirty-eight had been at that table long enough that the nails in it had been gone over with something more than once, and he did not put any of them back in himself, and nobody in that yard ever asked him about that either. About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he has been at that table a long time and that about four of them have not said how he knows that the nails have been gone over.

---

A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall at about half past three and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near him, and he was not asked one question.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he still does not know what the four of them are for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and fifty-third time running, and nobody asked him anything. And at about four the man of thirty-eight stopped work at the near end of the first table and stood with his hands in front of him and looked at the line of pieces along the top of that table without touching any of them, and nobody said one word to him, and he went up that slope about nine feet behind the others.

---

And then the clerk read that morning out loud, in the ordinary voice, once, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and she read it before she put her pen to anything.

---

"The two hundred and sixty-fourth morning," she said. "The back of that book stands where it stood yesterday and the morning goes on it in my hand. That is the account and there is nothing else in it."

---

Then she wrote the morning under the last line in her own hand and took no line for it, and she shut the book and put her hand flat on the cover of it, and the page carried fifty-five lines before that and fifty-five lines after.

---

The light came off the boards at about half past four and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen, and about nine people at the top of that bank did not join in and did not go up. And the woman who keeps a scale tied the cloth over her scale and knotted it and put the basket on her arm and went up that bank last of all but two, with the chalk in her pocket and every figure she had weighed that morning still on the edge of her own board and not one of them said out loud, and the page at the back of that lot book carried fifty-five lines and the lane count was thirty and it had not moved on any morning of this stretch.

---

That page carries fifty-five lines. None of them is in the lot book. Three of the first thirty-three were never read. Seven is what a clerk has counted. Fourteen is what this district has made. Five is what this district does not have. The fifth of the five is unpaid. A line there is not a payment. A gatepost sheet carries four hundred and eleven. By how much, nobody has done. The toll is ninety-eight. It is spent. It may not be raised or paid twice. The low wall's ground is public lot. Since the one hundred and seventeenth afternoon. It is not a payment of that. The bid ran four hundred and fifty days. It was not run. It is not a lot. It will not be. The heading over that name says whoever. The name does not satisfy the rule. The second space on that form is empty. Nobody has asked for it to be filled. The other space is on different paper. It is empty as well. The record has stood one hundred and seventy-nine days. Nobody asked what it is days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six one thing. Nobody asked the man of thirty-eight one thing. No reason was said out loud this morning. No line went on that page. A woman has weighed things there nine years. She has not been given anything. She has not asked for anything. He has no bed, no wage, no office. He has asked for none of the three. The lane count is thirty. It did not move. That page is at fifty-five lines. Nobody asked anybody anything about any of it. He worked from about nine until about four. Nobody asked him one thing out loud.
