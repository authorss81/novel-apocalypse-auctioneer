# Chapter 1005: The Two Hundred And Eighty-Ninth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And She Said A Figure Out Loud Once At About Half Past One At Her Own Scale And Nobody Answered Her And Nobody Thanked Her And Nobody Asked Her What She Weighs Things For

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and forty-four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries nine hundred and three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand two hundred and nineteen days. Nobody has entered anything for nine hundred and thirty-three days. Eight hundred and ninety-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Six hundred and eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Six hundred and thirteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and eighty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and eighty-six days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is six hundred and twenty-two days past a printing it did not make. Five hundred and sixty-two marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and forty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table.

Seven is what a clerk has counted, and it is on nothing. This district does not own four documents. Four places in this district can read those three lines. Four conditions have no end on it. Fourteen is what this district has made, and five is what this district does not have, and the fifth of the five is unpaid.

She said a figure out loud, once, at about half past one this morning, at her own scale, and nobody answered her, nobody thanked her and nobody asked her what she weighs things for, and no reason was said out loud by anybody in that yard, so the page at the back of that lot book did not move and the morning went on in the clerk's own hand without a line. About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a figure has gone into that yard on more mornings than a line has gone on that page, and that about four of them have not said what the difference is. The lane count is thirty and it did not move and nothing came down that lane. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

A reason went out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, when the back of that lot book could not have taken it at all, and so it was not entered anywhere, and it is counted in no place in this district, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and nineteen mornings, and the near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and sixty-nine mornings, and nobody thanks the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and eighty-nine mornings, on the word of the man who keeps it, and he said that number before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and eighty-eighth morning, and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The man of fifty-six has read four figures off that wall on the seven hundred and thirtieth of those mornings. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the six hundred and forty-fourth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and forty-three of them, and this is the one hundred and forty-fifth morning running of nobody asking him anything. The man of fifty-six's flat answer is the hundred and seventy-eighth time running.

That lot book is on the boards. It has five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever. The second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning, and it is not moved by a decision. Nothing came down that lane. The page at the back carries fifty-seven lines and carried fifty-seven lines when she shut the book. The record has stood two hundred and four days.

A man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the two hundred and thirty-fourth afternoon running. About nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning. There are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

The toll of ninety-eight is paid and spent and may not be raised again or paid twice. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read.

---

There is a thing about the pan she has never got used to and has never tried to get used to, which is that it wants to go.

Any weight in that pan on a morning when the wind comes over the top of that bank wants to lean and wants to swing a little at the low point of the swing, and the only way to get the figure is to stop giving it anything to object to. So she stands there with her hands off and lets it object.

It took her about two years to learn that. After that it was the best part of the day.

---

The boards were up at about ten to eight and the count went out loud at them off that boy before anybody in that yard had moved a hand. It is thirty. He has not put anything after it for longer than she has been keeping her own figures, and this morning he said the not-putting in the same breath as the count.

The man of fifty-six was at his wall before there was light on it. She saw him from up the slope with his arm up and his mouth going, the same as yesterday, and the same as the morning before that.

---

At about half past nine a man came down the slope with his hands showing and went along the top of his own table, round the end of it, up the slope and back down, and in at the same corner, and did not break the route once.

Somebody was always saying or writing or not saying something in that yard, and she had worked out over a good many years that the times when she heard anything at all were the times she was not listening.

She came down at half past twelve with the basket on her arm and set up about four feet off the near end of the second table.

---

She put three things on the pan between half past twelve and half past one, and wrote three figures on the edge of her own board in chalk, in the gap she leaves at the end of each one, and she did not say any of them.

That is the ordinary morning. She has said four figures out loud in that yard in nine years and every one of them was a morning she had decided to say one on.

What she had learned, and had not expected to learn, was that deciding was most of it.

---

At half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number out into about four hundred yards of air in the ordinary voice, and about four people said it after him out of step with each other, and it came back off the top of that bank.

She waited. She had not decided to say anything that day and she waited for the ordinary minute to get to its end.

It did not get to its end. The number went away down the slope and the yard went back to being a yard with a wheel going round at the near end of it, and she stood there with the chalk in her hand.

---

So she put a bar on her own pan and waited for it to come still, and when it came still she said the figure out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, at her own scale, in about nineteen people's hearing.

She did not raise it. She did not say it twice. She said it the way she would say the time of day to somebody standing next to her, and then she wrote it on the edge of her own board in the gap, and then she went to the basket for the next thing.

---

Nobody answered her.

Nobody asked her where the figure came from and nobody asked her what she weighs things for and nobody said thank you to her and nobody said one word of anything. The man at the far end of that table did not look up off what he was doing. The man of thirty-two went along the top of his own table with his cloth and did not turn his head.

And about four hundred yards off at the north end, the man who digs loam had his blade in the ground at the moment it came out of her, and it stayed in the ground while she took her hand off the chalk and put the chalk in her pocket, and then he took it out and went on with it.

She was about four hundred yards from him and there was a wheel going the whole time. She could not have said whether he heard it, and nobody asked her, and she went on with the basket.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a figure has gone into that yard on more mornings than a line has gone on that page. And about four of them have said that nobody ever told her to. And about four of them have not said what the difference is.

---

She did not stop there. She had not stopped there on the mornings before this one either, and the reason she had not stopped there was that she had not known what the stopping was for.

By about half past two that day she had it, and it was this: she was not going to get anything out of that yard by saying a figure in it, and she had been arranging her mornings for a good while as though she might.

Nine years of a trade where the answer arrives at once. Nine years of a man saying what he wants and the thing being put on the pan and the figure being the same every time for the same thing.

She had been standing in that yard this morning with a piece of chalk in her hand like somebody waiting on a turnstile.

---

The turner came off the treadle at about half past four and the wheel stood still for the first time that day, and about four people out of about nineteen said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stayed up there.

At about four o'clock, before any of that, the man of fifty-six had taken his four figures off that wall and put his hand flat on the stone under the lowest one.

"It is not for me and it is not for anybody standing in this yard," he said out loud, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and seventy-eighth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

The clerk gave the account of that morning out loud before she had the pen near any part of it, which is the way she does it, and nobody in that yard had asked her to do it and nobody has ever asked her why she does it.

"The two hundred and eighty-ninth morning," she said. "Nothing has gone out loud in this yard this morning that goes on the back of that book, so this morning goes in my hand and there is no line in it. That went out loud before a word of it was written."

Then she wrote the morning under the last line in her own hand and took no line for it, and the page at the back of that book carried fifty-seven lines when she shut it and carried fifty-seven lines after.

---

She knotted the cloth over her own scale and put the basket on her arm and went up that bank with the chalk still in her hand, and at the top of it she stopped and looked back down at the yard for as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

What she had wanted, that morning, was to be asked.

What she had got was to have said one, in a yard of about nineteen people, with a wheel going over the top of it, and to have gone on with the basket.

---

Fifty-seven lines are on that page and not one of them is in the lot book, and it did not move on this morning. A woman said a figure out loud once at about half past one at her own scale and nobody answered her and nobody thanked her and nobody asked her what she weighs things for, and the man who digs loam about four hundred yards off left his blade in the ground while she took her hand off the chalk and then went on with it, and she could not have said whether he heard it. About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a figure has gone into that yard on more mornings than a line has gone on that page, and that about four of them have not said what the difference is. The lane count is thirty and nothing came down that lane. Three of the first thirty-three lines were never read. The fifth of the five is unpaid.
