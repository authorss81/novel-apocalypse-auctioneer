# Chapter 433: Thirty-One Marks

The clerk of nineteen years put her finger on the top mark of that board at about ten past eight and left it there, and nine people there had come down the bank to watch a woman count a month that had not been over when they thought it was.

It was the thirty-first of the eleventh month, which is the last day of it, and the light came up clean and there was no wind. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about a quarter to eight, before there was anybody else in that ground, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the age of that figure as a figure about the figure is a hundred and eighty-two days, and that there is no day-count for the sheet itself.

The man of fifty-six did not read the four figures off that wall at about half past nine. He had been on the other side of it since about ten past eight with his arms down, watching a woman's finger arrive at a mark about every four minutes, and a few people noticed that he had not read them and nine noticed that he had been there the whole time.

---

She started at the top, which was this morning's, and she named that day out loud. Then she went down to the mark below it and named that one, and she went all the way down with her finger on the wood where anybody standing behind her could see it arrive. The boy of about nineteen counted with his own eyes and his own finger and his own page, and said so when somebody standing beside him asked him what he was doing.

"I am counting for me. Nobody has asked me to count for anybody else."

"You have said that before."

"I have said it twice today, and I have said it every day this month, and some of you keep standing where I can see you and then asking me what I am doing."

She got to the mark for the first of this month at about twenty past ten, put her finger on it, and said the day out loud again, and stood up straight. Nine people there who had not known it was coming worked it out at the same moment, and four of them said so out loud.

The man of about nineteen counted what she said and got sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and two people near the boards heard the number come out of his mouth.

"**From the first of the eleventh month to this morning is thirty-one marks and it is thirty-one days, and those are the same figure, and a person can put a finger on either end of it and get the number I got, and the first time anybody in this district had that figure it was off a list and not off a board.**"

The man of fifty-six said one word from the other side of those boards.

"Again."

She put her finger on the top mark and went down all thirty-one again in front of about nineteen people, and he read every mark before her finger arrived at it, and he got all thirty-one of them, and some people said out loud that a man who has read four figures off those boards every morning for six months had just read thirty-one marks off them in about two hours and had not read a number once.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at that end of the table through all of it with his left hand in its cloth and his right hand flat on the boards. He said nothing until about half past eleven and then he said two things, and the second was longer than the first, and nine people heard both.

"That is the first check anybody has run in this district since the middle of the sixth month, and it is the only one of the two figures this yard has ever had that anybody could have got wrong."

Somebody in the middle of that ground said that nothing had been got wrong. The mender said yes, and that was the whole of what he was going to say about it, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch and did not come up and did not say one word.

Then the clerk of nineteen years said out loud at that table, to the yard and not to the boy, that a figure somebody has entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day, and that she had written that sentence on the eighteenth of the sixth month and was writing it again, and that the second time a clerk writes a sentence is a habit and a habit is a finding, and she was entering that she had a habit.

Nobody argued with her about it. Two people said afterwards that the sentence had been said out loud four times in this ground this month and that this was the fifth and that nobody had said anything different any of the five times, and the boy of about nineteen said that five was a figure and not a sentence and he was not going to write it down.

---

At about twelve the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank with the water coming off his coat and the bundle under his arm and stood at that end of the table for about four seconds, and nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him. He was not asked for anything and nobody asked him what the water had been that morning and nobody asked him what it was that day, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and he went up the lane.

At about twenty past twelve the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat and watched a woman count a month out for about eleven minutes without saying anything. A voice at that near end said that a man who watches a check instead of running one is a man who has worked out which of the two he is, and he said that was right, and that he had worked it out on the thirtieth of the tenth month and had not been asked to say so then either.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked a question out loud and the clerk of nineteen years answered it, and two people listened and one of them wrote nothing.

"You counted it. Does that mean anybody can check the other one now."

"No," she said. "It means one of them is a figure about a count and the other one is a figure about a list, and this morning I turned one of them into a figure about a count by putting my finger on both ends of it." She shut her page. "The ninth month was thirty-one and it was entered off a list by somebody who is not in this district, and nobody wrote down where the mark was, and that figure is thirty-one for as long as this district is going. It cannot be checked. Not by me and not by you and not by whoever comes after both of us."

Nobody said anything about that. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure this district has had since the middle of the sixth month cannot be checked by anybody in this district, and entered the reason, and entered that the reason is a figure about a board and not about a person.

---

At about two the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came off that east wall and asked her a question and she answered it in one breath. He asked her whether the word was still in her margin. She said it was.

"Thirty-two days."

"It is thirty-two days old. I have got it over a figure about a list, and there is nothing on that figure now but thirty-one, and the word has nothing on it at all, and I am not going to take it off, because the reason I put it there has not changed and nobody has checked any of the other nine columns." She did not look up. "You have asked me about it twice this month. You are not asking me because you think I have forgotten."

"No," he said. "I am asking you because I would like to know what it is like to have written a word down and then found out there was nothing under it. I have not got a word for that and I have had a name in my mouth for twenty-six days and neither of those is going on a page."

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked her a question twice about a word in her margin, and entered the answer, and entered that the answer carried no figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he had not been there at half past nine and nobody said anything about that. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and nine people watched a man lift a wheel with one arm and not one of them said a word about it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and four people within nine feet of him did not look at him, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

At about four a voice at that near end said out loud that a month had never been checked in this district by anybody, and that it had been taken off a list, and that the two of those are not the same thing and that a clerk who has said so is not a clerk who has found something, and a few people agreed and nobody wrote it down.

---

She wrote the count on her own page at about half past four with her finger still on the board behind her, and she wrote it as a number and not as a sentence, because it is a number and because she has found out this month what happens when a clerk puts a sentence on a page. Nine people watched her do it and about four of them counted the marks on that board a second time while she wrote and got the same number and said so out loud.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the month this district has just been living in is thirty-one, that the count was made off marks on a board in the open in daylight with about nineteen people standing at the wall of it, and that it is a figure about a count. She entered that a figure entered off a list on the thirtieth of the tenth month turned out to be a figure about a count, and that a figure that reproduces is a figure about a count and is not a finding, and that the second time she has entered that sentence is a habit and the habit is a finding. She entered that the ninth month is thirty-one and cannot be checked, and the reason, and that the reason is a figure about a board and not about a person.

The bid has stood open ninety-one days and was not run this day and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and twenty-one days past. The rule said out loud in that ground on the tenth of the tenth month is fifty-one days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is forty-six days out of date, was not altered, and has nothing written beside it, and a month was the shortest interval this district had evidence for and the figure on that line is now a month and sixteen days out of date. The figure entered off a list is thirty-two days old and has now been checked once, and being checked once is not the same as being a finding. The word unchecked is in the margin of her own page and is over that figure and over nothing else. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid.

At about half past five the man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall for the first time that day, and got all four of them, and some people said out loud that it was the first time that day.

"**The board carries three hundred and forty-one, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and fifty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and seventy-one, and three hundred and thirty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He said that it was the first time that day and that it was, and that he had spent a morning counting instead and would do it again tomorrow and would not be asked to.

Going down from the top, that board carries this morning, then thirty, then the first of the eleventh. The mark below that one is the first of the tenth month. The mark below that one is not there, because the first of the ninth is a mark nobody in this district has ever seen or can put a finger on, and a figure somebody wrote down about a mark that is not there is a figure about a list and not a day-count, and it is going to read thirty-one for as long as there is anybody here to read it.
