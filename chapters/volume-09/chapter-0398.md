# Chapter 398: The Thing Nobody Is Asking For

---

The far end of that yard is nine feet from the low wall and four hundred yards down the lane from the step of two stones, and it is the only part of that yard that is dry when the rest of it is not, and a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally, and has said of himself that he is fifty-one, was standing at that end of it at about ten on the twenty-sixth of the tenth month with a flat book under his left arm, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures off those boards out loud over the mender's shoulder and got all four, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane at about eleven, read the four figures as he went past, got all four, and went on up, and nobody in that yard asked him about the eleven miles and nobody in that yard said one word to him about a man standing at the far end of it.

"**The board carries three hundred and six, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and twenty-two days, the days nobody has entered anything since the twenty-fourth of November is three hundred and thirty-six, and the first day of the eighth month is eighty-six days past.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally has been in that yard this morning and has not been asked for anything, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a clerk may not enter that he is a holder of anything and has not entered it and is not going to.

---

About half past ten about four people in that yard worked out, out loud, in the ordinary way, that the six households of the man at the far end of that yard have not moved since the twenty-first of the fourth month, which is a hundred and eighty-eight days, and one of them said the figure out loud in that yard, and the man of about nineteen wrote it on his own page in his own hand, and it came out on his page at the same figure as the one off the ladder, and nobody asked the man at the far end of that yard whether it was right.

Then about four people in that yard looked across the yard at the man with the flat book under his arm, and one of them took a step towards him, and about four other people in that yard said, at the same time and not to him, that a figure came off a ladder is a figure about a ladder and that nobody has asked him for it and nobody is going to, and the man of fifty-six said out loud that a man keeps a tally for himself and that a figure nobody has been asked for is not a refusal and is not a figure anybody in this yard is holding, and the one who had taken a step stopped where he was.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure of a hundred and eighty-eight days was said out loud in that yard this morning and that it is a figure about six households and about a ladder and is not a figure about the man standing at the far end of that yard, and that he was not asked for it and has not been asked for it on any day this month, and that a clerk entered it out of a ladder and is not going to ask a man to confirm a figure she can get off a ladder.

Then about four people in that yard went to those boards to see whether the figure was on one of them, and it is not, and about four other people in that yard said out loud that a figure that comes off a ladder is a figure a board does not carry, and that everything on those boards is counted off a day and that the six households are counted off a ladder, and that a ladder is not a board and is not a date and is not a place and does not have a name on it.

About four people said that is the first time anybody has said that out loud and about four of them said that a figure off a ladder is a figure somebody worked out at some point with a pencil and did not write down where, and the man of fifty-six said out loud that he could not have got that figure off a board this morning and had not tried to and was not going to go up that lane to find out where a man worked it out, and about four of them said that is the right answer and about four of them said that it is also the answer that means nobody in this district knows any more about six households than they knew in the fourth month.

A figure about six households is not on any board in this district. A ladder is not a board and is not a date and is not a page. A figure somebody worked out with a pencil and did not write down where is a figure this district is carrying and not one it can check, and the length of the ninth month was entered off a list by somebody else a while back and can never now be checked for the same reason. Nobody has been asked about that either.

At about half past eleven the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came into that yard and stood at the far end of it beside him, about four feet off, with her arms folded, and about four people in that yard said out loud that her four empty houses have not been offered and are not going to be offered again before the first day of the season after this one, on her own statement, and about four of them said that four empty houses are four figures about four houses and are not a figure about her, and neither of them said one word to either of them.

Two people stood at the far end of that yard for about half an hour and said nothing to each other and nothing to anybody. Her four empty houses are four figures about four houses and are not a figure about her, and they were not counted today and were not offered today, and nobody in that yard said one word about them to her, and she was not asked and has not been asked.

---

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud at that far end of the yard, to nobody, and about four people heard it, and the man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-three and wrote it down, and the count is seventy-three because that is what is printed there.

"**Six households have not moved for a hundred and eighty-eight days and I have got the figure off a ladder and I have never once been asked for it and I am not going to be, and I would like it written down, in whatever page you keep, that nobody asked me, because in a year somebody is going to say I gave a figure out in a yard and I did not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said out loud in that yard that nobody had asked him for a figure and that he was not going to be, and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked and has said not asked every day this month, and that a clerk entered his sentence and entered not one of his figures and that a clerk may not enter that he is a holder of anything because nothing in this yard makes him one.

Then the two of them stayed at that far end until about half past three, and about nine people in that yard watched two people stand about four feet apart for about two hours and not one word passed between them, and at about half past three the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally shifted the flat book from under his left arm to under his right and his left hand did not go the way he sent it, which took him about four seconds longer than it would have taken a hand that worked, and about nine people in that yard saw that happen and not one of them said one word about it, and he put the book back under his left arm and went up the lane.

A man's hand did not do what he sent it to do this afternoon in full view of about nine people, and nobody said one word about it and nobody asked him about it. A hand is not a figure and is not entered and is not a holding and is not a job.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down to that far end at about half past two and stood about nine feet off the two of them and did not go any closer, and about four people in that yard watched him stand there and about four of them said out loud that he has been up that bank once this month and did not knock and that standing nine feet off two people is the same gesture in a different direction, and he said out loud that he was not asking either of them for anything and was not going to, and about four of them said that nobody believed him and about four of them said that was not the point.

A man stood about nine feet off two people at the far end of that yard and said out loud that he was not asking either of them for anything. He did not ask either of them, and he was not asked himself and cannot be counted either way. A man who goes up a bank once in a month and does not knock and does not ask twice is a man who did not knock this afternoon either.

---

The rest of that day went ordinary and nothing in it changed on purpose. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch before nine and stood on the bank at about ten, and his right hand did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in this day. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about half past four with his hook in his hand and was wet to the chest. The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was against the east wall with his cart and the tent rolled on the back of it where it has been since the thirtieth of the June, and he was given nothing.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in that yard from about ten until about four and said nothing out loud today and nobody asked him for anything and he asked for nothing, and about four people in that yard said out loud that there are a great many things in this district that nobody asks anybody about and that a day on which none of them moved is a day, and about four of them said that is the first time anybody has said that out loud and that it sounded like nothing and that nothing changing on purpose is a finding and not a habit.

Six households, four empty houses, a flat book, a man's hand, the standing water in a ditch and the figure on a page in a room four hundred yards up that bank are six things this yard did not ask anybody about this afternoon, and six is a figure about six things and is not a figure about six people. Nothing in this district was asked and nothing was refused, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move today and stands at two hundred and sixteen days.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and eighty-nine days back and was named out loud in that yard once this afternoon by a clerk and was closed in nothing. That bid stands at fifty-six days and was not run today and nothing has been said about closing it. The first day of the eighth month is eighty-six days past. The figure on the second line of that lot book is eleven days out of date and was not altered. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six and no name went into it by anybody.

By the time the light had gone the far end of that yard was wet like the rest of it and there was nobody standing at it, and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally had gone up that lane with a flat book under his left arm and had not been asked for anything by anybody at any point in the day.
