# Chapter 405: What a District Does With a Label

About four people asked the same question at the near end of that trestle table inside a quarter of an hour of each other, and the man of fifty-six gave the same answer three times, and by the third one he had to stop in the middle of it and put his hand flat on those boards and start again.

It was the third of the eleventh month. The ground had gone soft at the south end of that yard and dry under the boards. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures out loud over the mender's shoulder and got all of them, and said the word four times with them, and got the word four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and thirteen, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and twenty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and forty-three, and three hundred and four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that four hundred and eleven is on the sheet at that gatepost and has not moved, and that the figure about that figure is a hundred and fifty-four days old, and that neither of those two is a figure about a day anybody spent.

---

"Is the word staying on them?"

That was the first one, from a man who had not asked it to anybody in particular, at about half past ten. The man of fifty-six took his hand off those boards and turned round to the yard instead of to the wall, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and five and put it down.

"**It is staying, and here is the reason and I am going to be asked this four more times this week, and the reason is that the word is the only thing about those twelve figures that anybody in this district is able to check. Four figures off a wall are a thing a man has got in his head, and the word is a thing a person can stand at that page and read, and if a person reads it and it is not there then something is wrong, and there is nothing else on that page that a person can do that with.**"

"That is not a reason for a word," somebody said. "That is a reason for a column."

"It is a reason for a word and it is not a reason for a column, and the two of those are a breath apart and everybody in this yard can hear the gap between them."

At about eleven the second man asked the same question and got the same answer in the same words, and two people who had been standing at the far end of that yard for ten minutes said out loud that they could tell from about nine feet off that it was the same answer, and the man of fifty-six said that was the point of it.

At about a quarter past eleven the third man asked it, and the man of fifty-six got about eleven words into the answer and stopped, and put his hand flat on the boards, and stood there for a few seconds, and then he said the rest of it in shorter sentences than he had started with.

"It is staying. I am not going to take it off. I have got a reason and I have given it twice and I am not going to give it a third time in one morning to a different man who has not heard the first two."

"That is a refusal."

"It is an answer and the third one is shorter. Take it or leave it."

---

At about one somebody asked the clerk of nineteen years to rule a fifth column on that page, with the word over it, so that anybody could see in one line which of the nine carried it and which of the nine did not.

She said no before the question was finished, which about four people said was rude and one of them said was the correct answer.

"There are four columns of not-askings on that page and there have been four since before the first of the ninth," she said. "A column with a word over it is not a column of not-askings, and a column with a word over it is not a column of people, and a new column of that shape is not an empty column and is not the same column and is not added to the old ones. There is no fifth of them and I am not going to rule one for a word I wrote in a margin at half past seven in the morning on my own."

"You wrote it on your own."

"I did, and I would rather twelve figures in a margin than five columns with nothing in them, and I have watched a page fill up with headings this month and there is not one person on it."

Nobody ruled a fifth column and nobody filled a column of any kind, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled before the first of the ninth and was still empty at about six, and it is empty now.

Then a man at the near end of that table looked at the twelve in the margin and counted them on his fingers. He got to the seventh one and stopped, and said out loud that the seventh one was about six households, and that the figure had come off a ladder and not off a board. About four of them looked across the yard at the far end, where a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally, and has said of himself that he is fifty-one, was standing with a flat book under his left arm.

"That one is a figure somebody worked out with a pencil and did not write down where," somebody said.

"It is also one of the twelve and it has got the word on it," the clerk said, before the man at the far end had said anything at all. "I put it there this morning. It crossed the same night as the other eleven and I did not ask him and I am not going to."

Nobody walked over to him. Nobody asked him for the figure. The record about the not asking says not asked, and it has said not asked every day this month and it said it again this afternoon.

Then, at about half past two, a man near the low wall said one thing out loud in that yard that about four of them heard and that the man of about nineteen counted and got fifty-four and put down.

"**I read those four figures out loud over that man's shoulder every morning of this month and this morning I have not read one of them out loud, and I did not decide not to, and I noticed at about eleven that I had not, and I have not read one since.**"

The yard was quiet for about four seconds.

"That is a cost," somebody said.

"That is a finding," said somebody else, about a second later.

"It is a man who has been in a yard for six months reading a wall he is not responsible for, and he has stopped, and nobody told him to stop." The mender had not moved from the end of that table. "I am not going to say which of your two words is the right one and neither of you is going to get it out of me in a yard with no roof in it."

---

At about four the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down to the end of that table and stood about nine feet off it with his right hand in his coat and did not say one word out loud that anybody has written down.

He had been on that bank since about half past two and he had not come down because there was a thing he wanted to hear and was not going to be the one to start it. He had wanted to hear a man say out loud, in front of about nineteen people, that a word on a figure is the only part of that figure anybody can check, and that it would be said without being asked for it, and about half past ten that morning he had heard it, three times, from a man who had been put off by two different people and had not stopped.

Now he was standing nine feet off a table at four o'clock in the afternoon with nothing to do with himself, because the thing he had come down for had been said before he got there and he had missed the first two of the three.

That is the whole of what he was thinking about and it was not a document and it was not a figure. Six feet would have been being asked something. Nine feet was near enough to read a page and far enough that a man could say what he liked about it.

The man of about nineteen came about four feet along that table and stopped and did not say anything either, and after about a minute the man in the coat said that the word was the only part of the twelve figures anybody could check and that it was in the margin and not over a column.

"I know," the boy said. "I have been putting it in my own hand since about eleven this morning and it takes four seconds and I have written out thirty of them and I have not missed one and I could not tell you what any of them is for."

"That is a fact about a boy and not a finding about a word."

"It is a fact about a boy," the boy agreed, "and about a hand, and a hand is not a figure and is not entered and is not a holding and is not a job."

---

Ordinary is the word for what was left of that day, and nothing about the twelve figures changed in it. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch before nine, came up out of it about ten, and took the four figures off the end of that table and got every one of them, and said the word with them, and that right hand of his stayed below the level of that shoulder at every hour of that day. The road keeper was up that lane at about eleven and once more at about four, was right off that wall both times, said the word with them, and nobody in the yard asked him about the eleven miles or about whether the word was staying. The reed cutter came up that bank at about half past four and was wet to the chest, and he was not asked about the word or about anything else. The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was against the east wall with his hand-cart, the tent still rolled on the back of it, and he was given nothing and nobody asked him about it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure carrying a word is a figure nobody will read out loud again, and that one man of about nineteen in that yard has already stopped reading four figures out loud and did not decide to and was not asked to stop and has not been asked about it since. The bid has stood open sixty-three days and was not run today. The first day of the eighth month is ninety-three days past and has been entered every morning this month by a clerk and has not been asked about. The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and ninety-six days back and is open and was not named out loud today. The rule said out loud in that yard on the tenth of the tenth month is twenty-three days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is eighteen days out of date and was not altered. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid and the count is still five.

At about half past five she came down that bank, filled her two buckets at the trough, took them up the two goes, and stood at that table long enough to read what was in that book, and nobody said anything to her and she said nothing to anybody.

By the time the light had gone, twelve words were in the margin of that page where they had been at half past seven, and nothing in that yard was any different for them, and about four people said out loud on their way out that a day on which nothing got better and nothing got worse is a day, and that is not a thing to be proud of and it is not a thing to be ashamed of either.
