# Chapter 395: The Two Buckets

---

The door four hundred yards up that bank was shut when that yard filled up on the morning of the twenty-third of the tenth month, and it was shut again at about half past five. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and nobody in the yard went up that bank.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures off those boards out loud over the mender's shoulder and got all four. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam read them standing at the end of that table and got all four. The road keeper came up that lane at about eleven, read the four figures off the boards as he went by, got all four, and went on up, and nobody in the yard asked him about the eleven miles, and nobody said one word to him about the door.

"**The board carries three hundred and three, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and nineteen days, and two hundred and ninety-four days separate the second of January and this morning, and the four columns of not-askings on that page have been ruled since before the first of the ninth and nothing has ever gone in one of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that four columns of not-askings is a figure about four columns, and that a column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is not a fifth of that shape, and that a mark may not carry a job and neither may a man.

Then the man of about nineteen counted what the mender had not said, which is a thing he has never done before, and which about four people in the yard told him not to do. He did it anyway. The count came to seventy-two, and he put it at the top of his own page under the two figures he had brought in with him, and it is the first thing he has written on that page this month that is not a figure off a board.

He read the two lines in that lot book standing at the end of the table, out loud, in the ordinary voice, from the top of the first line to the end of the second, and did not touch the book, and stopped there.

"**I have counted what he said and I have counted the two lines in that book, and they are two different counts and one of them is about a man and one of them is about a lot, and I have put both of them on one page and that is my page and not yours, and I am not going to read the second one back from the top for anybody.**"

The man of about nineteen counted what the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had said about the water, and nobody had asked him to. A count somebody is told not to make and makes anyway is a count and not a finding. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing is a man and not a figure, and the eighty-five words he said at about two are a figure about that sentence and are not a figure about him, and the seventy-two at the top of that page are a figure about the boy's own and are not a figure about anybody.

About four people said that the boy had just read a book out loud in a yard and that nobody had asked him to.

"A reading is an account of a lot said out loud so that anybody standing there can check it," somebody said. "Reading a thing out loud in a yard to no one is a thing a man does."

"Nobody is asking you to do it again," somebody else said, and about four of them said that, and nobody in the yard asked him to read it back from the top, and he was not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them said one thing out loud at the east wall, to nobody in particular, and about four of them heard it and nobody counted it, and the tent was still rolled on the back of his hand-cart where it has been since the thirtieth of the June.

"**There are two stones on that step and I have put a foot on the bottom of them about four hundred times and I have never once been past the top one, and I am not going to be, and a man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them is a man who puts tables up and that is the whole of him.**"

The step of two stones has not been crossed by anybody from that yard this month, and a man who puts tables up has said out loud that he is not going to cross it. A person who has said out loud that a thing is not going to happen has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way. A table is a table and is not a counter and is not a market and is not a document.

---

At about ten about four people there stood at the near end of that trestle table looking at the boards and then at the bank, and one of them said out loud that there are two doors in this district in reach of a morning walk. One of them is on a building that is a lot in a book. The other one is four hundred yards up that bank, on a room about nine feet by eleven with a shelf in it and two books on the shelf.

"Nobody has knocked on that door since the fourteenth of this month," somebody said.

"And the man who went up it on the fourteenth did not knock either," somebody else said, and about four of them said that, and nobody at that end of the table looked up the bank to see whether the door was shut.

About four other people said that the woman who keeps the two books has kept them for nineteen years, and has never once been asked to put anything in either of them for anybody. There is one line in one of those two books in her own hand that nobody asked her to write, and it has not been read aloud by anybody, and it is not going to be read aloud this morning. A thing nobody has read is a thing that is not a reading and binds nobody.

The man of fifty-six said out loud that he agreed with all four of them, and that a person keeping a book is not a keeper of it and is not holding anything, and has not been asked to hold anything.

The second of those two books was not opened, and nothing in that room touched it this morning. The one line in it was not read aloud by anybody, and nobody is going to put down what it says, and nobody has been asked what it says. A book in a room with a shut door is not a book a stranger may walk up to and read, and is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own.

---

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the far end of that yard from about ten until about half past eleven with her arms folded. About four people said out loud that her four empty houses have not been offered, and are not going to be offered again before the first day of the season after this one, on her own statement, and about four of them said that four empty houses are four figures about four houses and are not a figure about her. She did not answer, and she was not asked, and her arms stayed folded the whole time.

At about two the mender said one thing out loud in the yard about the trough at the bottom of that bank, and about four people heard it, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got eighty-five, and about four people in the yard heard that count come out and none of them said a word about it.

"**There is a woman of fifty-eight who has carried two buckets of water up that bank every day for nineteen years and there has never been a day anybody in this yard asked her whether the water was any good, and I have watched her do it four hundred times and I have never once said a word about it, and that is not a thing I am proud of this afternoon and I am not going to stand here and be praised for it.**"

He said out loud in the yard that nobody has ever asked a woman of fifty-eight whether the water was any good, and that she was not asked this afternoon either. A person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past four the same man took a sheet of paper out of his coat and a pencil out of his pocket and got about nine feet down that lane, and stopped, and put the pencil back in his pocket, and put the sheet back in his coat, and stood where he was for about twenty seconds, and then came back to that table and said nothing whatever about it.

About nine people in the yard saw a man with a sheet in his hand walk nine feet down a lane and turn round and come back.

"We would have asked her," somebody said, to the person next to him and not to the mender.

"Nobody in this yard asks anybody anything."

Neither of those was said to the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing. He heard both of them, and he did not answer either.

---

At about half past five the woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the first of the two goes. At the top of that go she turned round and came back down it to the table.

She read the two lines in that lot book standing up, the way anybody can, with her hand on the near edge of the boards and the buckets standing on the ground beside her. She read from the top of the first line to the end of the second one without stopping and without touching the book.

Then she said nothing to anybody at all, picked the two buckets up, and went on up the bank with them.

Nobody there said one word to her.

About four people in the yard were standing about four feet from her while she did it, and about four of them looked at each other over the top of her buckets, and about four other people looked at the boards. The mender had his right hand flat on that table and did not lift it. The man of about nineteen did not count anything and did not write anything on his own page.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way past a table this evening. She was not asked to, and she did not read anything back from the top for anybody, and no name of hers is in any column and none is going to be. A clerk did not enter that she was read to and is not going to enter it. She said nothing to anybody this evening, and that is entered as nothing and not as a refusal.

Then the clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of the day, and the rest of the day was nothing. The bid stands at fifty-three days and was not run. The first day of the eighth month is eighty-three days past. The figure on the second line of that lot book is eight days out of date and was not altered, and the person who read it this evening did not alter it. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid, and the count is still five, and no sixth is proposed. The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank this evening with two buckets. Nobody in the yard paid her for carrying them. Nobody has ever paid her. She was not asked about it.

By the time the light had gone the two buckets were on the step of two stones four hundred yards up that bank and the door was shut, and the fanlight over that door was doing the thing it has done every evening since before this district began keeping figures, which is to let in the light from the room behind it and not enough of it to read anything by. About four people said good evening to each other on their way out, and about four of them said good evening to nobody in particular.
