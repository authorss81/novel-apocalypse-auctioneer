# Chapter 424: Something Is Going To Be Said

Nobody at that table knew what it was going to be. By about eleven four of them had said out loud there that something was, and the four of them were wrong about the morning and right about the week. The boy of about nineteen worked out that whatever it was going to be it had to be said and not just read.

It was the twenty-second of the eleventh month and the wind had gone round again and this time it stayed. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall at about half past nine, with his palm on the boards, and got every one of them, and said the word with them four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and thirty-two, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and forty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and sixty-two, and three hundred and twenty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and seventy-three days. A figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet, and there is no day-count for the sheet.

---

The first of the four was a woman who had come down that bank to get water, and who had never said anything else in that place in six months. She said it to the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road as he went past with his cart.

"There is going to be something said out loud here," she said, "and you may as well know it now as at eleven."

"How do you know that," the road keeper said, without stopping.

"Because two men said so on the low wall yesterday afternoon and one of them said it again at the trough this morning, and I do not listen to men at the low wall on purpose and I listened to that one."

The road keeper went on up that lane. Three people at that end of the table heard the whole of it, and the man who puts tables up said out loud that a thing said twice in a yard on purpose before it has happened is a thing that has been said twice.

"It has not been said twice yet," said the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing. "It has been said once by a man who does not know what it is and once by a woman carrying two buckets, and if you count those two as a habit then about four of you have been making habits out of nothing since the first of this month, and I am the one who has been letting you."

Nobody said the third one out loud until about eleven. At about eleven a man said it from the middle of the ground, in the ordinary voice, and it went across the whole place and nobody answered it.

"It is going to be said on a day this week," he said.

"That is the fourth one," said the man who puts tables up, and nobody said the fifth, and about nine people at that table counted four and one of them counted three, and the boy of about nineteen said that three was right and that he had been one of the three and had stopped saying it at about ten to avoid making a habit of it.

A man at the near end of that table asked how he knew it was going to be said and not just read. The man of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy-five and read it back to himself in a low voice, and three people at that table heard the number come out of his mouth. He said it had taken him about as long as the man of fifty-six takes to read four figures to work out that a thing said out loud and a thing read from a page are two different objects, and then the boy of about nineteen said it out loud.

"**Anything that goes in a book in this district has been read by somebody before it gets there. Anything that is said out loud in this yard has not, and if a thing is going to happen in this yard this week then it is going to be said, and I do not know who is going to say it and I am not going to be the one who finds out by hearing it.**"

Nobody read that back from the top. Two people at the near end of that table said that a boy of about nineteen had just said out loud that he was going to be told something and not asked for it. The boy said that was not the same thing, and that he had not been told anything and had not been asked for anything, and that the record about both of those says not told and not asked. He wanted both of those written down, and he was not going to ask her to write them.

One of the four was the man who puts tables up, and he said it from the east wall without turning round, and two people at that table said that they had not known he was one of the four until he said it.

"I have been putting that table up for six months," he said, "and something is going to be said over it, and I am not going to tell the four of you which of you is right about the day and I am not going to be the one who is."

"You are the one who is going to be the one who says it," said the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing.

Nobody answered him and nobody said the fifth thing, and the man who puts tables up went on with the thing he was doing at the cart, which was nothing. The tent is still rolled on the back of that hand-cart, where it has been since the thirtieth of the June.

At about half past eleven the clerk of nineteen years said that she was not going to write either of them, and the boy said that was correct. Three people at that table said that two people declining a thing in the same four seconds was the nearest this yard had come to a rule since the thirtieth of the June.

---

At about twelve the man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch to his thigh and heard all of it from the water. He said one thing up the bank and he did not raise his voice to say it, which was the first time in about a month that he had said anything and not raised his voice.

"You have all been telling each other a thing is going to happen in a yard for two days," he said, "and not one of you has said what, and if you had said what then it would have been said twice, and the second time would have been the one that stuck, and about four of you would be standing here on the day remembering that somebody said it in advance, and that is how the last four things in this yard died."

"You have just said that in advance," somebody said.

"I have said it after," the loam man said. "I have been in this ditch for two months and I am the only person in this yard who cannot get out of it in a hurry, and if I am going to be the last one to hear things then I am also going to be the last one to say them, and I would like somebody to write that down, and half of you are not going to."

The clerk of nineteen years did not write it down. She said out loud that a man had asked and that she was not going to, and that the reason was that a man who has said a thing out loud in a yard and has not been asked a question has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood his nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat. A voice at the near end of that table said that four of the people who had said something was going to be said were saying it at him, and he said, "Two of them are."

"Which two."

"None of them is going to tell you which two, and I am not going to say, and I would like it noticed that I have not been asked for a name this month by anybody here including the four of you."

Nobody had asked him for a name. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a person said out loud there that nobody had asked him for a name, and entered that she was not going to repeat what the name was, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about two the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was against the east wall with his hand-cart, and the tent is still rolled on the back of it, and he was given nothing, and nobody asked him about it. He had been quiet all morning, which four people at that table noticed, and one of them said out loud that a man who has been carrying something in his mouth for sixteen days has not been carrying it out loud this morning. The man who puts tables up said that was a figure about a mouth and not about a thing.

At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and did not stop at the low wall at all, which he had done three times in the last four days. Two people noticed and neither of them said anything about it until he was out of sight.

A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall from about ten with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands. A man came past him at about half past two with a hook in his hand, stopped for about four seconds, and went on, and nobody said a word about either the stopping or the hook. The record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

At about three the woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way back up. She said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her. One person at the near end of that table said that a woman who asked one question on the nineteenth of this month and has said nothing since is a woman who has got what she asked for. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud that it was a figure about a person and not about a yard, and that he would thank them not to keep it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open eighty-two days and was not run today, and that nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and twelve days past. The rule said out loud in that yard on the tenth of the tenth month is forty-two days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is thirty-seven days out of date, was not altered, and has nothing written beside it. The word unchecked is in the margin of a clerk's page and is over nothing. The line under those twelve words is twenty-three days old. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and fifteen days back and is open, and no bell was pulled and no night was named.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full. The woman of fifty-eight read the two lines standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures for the last time that day and got all four of them, and the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about four with the bundle under his arm and was not asked for anything.

At half past five on that bank four people were still standing there. One of them said the thing was going to be said tomorrow, and another one said it was not going to be said tomorrow at all, and a third said it would be said before the middle of the week. None of the three had said what, and none of the three was a person who was going to say it.
