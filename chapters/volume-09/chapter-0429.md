# Chapter 429: A Name Out Loud

At about eleven a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said a name out loud in the open, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and it took about four seconds, and nobody wrote it down, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a name was said and did not enter what it was.

It was the twenty-seventh of the eleventh month and the light was hard and clean and there was no wind. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall with his hand flat on the boards and got every one of them, and said the word with them, and got the word four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and thirty-seven, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and fifty-three days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and sixty-seven, and three hundred and twenty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and seventy-eight days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet. The two of those are the only figures anybody in that yard has about it.

---

He said it at about eleven without any warning and he did not raise his voice and he did not put his hand on anything. He said it the way a man says the name of a thing he has carried in his mouth for twenty-one days, and the whole of what he said was the name, and then he stopped, and he did not say anything after it.

Nobody read it back from the top. Nobody said anything for about eleven seconds, and the eleven seconds were longer than any silence anybody at that table could remember there, and the man of fifty-six did not look round and the clerk of nineteen years did not write.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing from the far end of that trestle table, with his left hand in its cloth. The man of about nineteen counted what he said and got sixty-six and read it back to himself in a low voice, and two people heard the number come out of his mouth.

"**A name is not a reading. Nobody has read that back from the top and it binds nobody. You have said a man's name in a yard on a morning and it is in about nineteen heads and it is on no page and it is not going to be on a page, and I want the yard to hear me say that before anybody starts.**"

"You told me on the fifteenth of this month," said the man who puts tables up, "that by the end of this month somebody's name would be in something, and that it would be because somebody said a sentence out loud twice. I have said nine words out loud four times to four different men in this yard and a fifth time three days ago, and I have never said the rest of them, and you are right. It is in something. It is in the air. That is all that has happened."

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch to his thigh at that moment and heard all of it, and he did not come up out of the bank, and two people at that table looked down at the water and then looked at each other.

---

The clerk of nineteen years took her page out at about a quarter past eleven and held it in both hands and did not put it on the table. She said what she was going to say to about nine people and not to the yard, and about nine people heard it and the rest were too far off.

"I am going to write down that a name was said out loud in this yard this morning. I am not going to write down what the name was. I heard it. I was standing nine feet from him and I heard every part of it, and I am not going to stand in a yard of nineteen people and say that I did not, because two men have asked me this month whether my hearing is going and I have worked out that neither of the two of them was asking about my hearing."

She put the page down and wrote one line and turned it round so that two people could read it, and then she did not turn it back.

The line said that a name was said out loud in the open. There was nothing under it. There was no name on it and no figure beside it and nothing on the back of it, and the clerk said out loud that there was nothing on the back of it either, and that two people had already looked.

"You have put a line on a page with nothing under it," said the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing. "You have done the thing you have refused to do for five months."

"I have put a line on a page with a date on it and a place on it and a man on it and nothing under it, and that is not a column and it is not a not-asking and it is not a figure. I have nine columns on this page and there is not one of them for this, and I am not ruling a tenth one today for a thing that was said in the air and not in the yard."

"Who was it," said the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up.

She looked at him for about as long as it takes the man of fifty-six to read four figures.

"I am not going to tell you what I heard, and I am not going to stand in this yard and tell anybody that I did not hear it, because I did. I am not going to look at the line and read it back, because the moment a clerk reads a name back in a yard it has been in the yard twice, and you of all men know what happens to a thing in the air the second time."

Nobody read it back. Three people at that table said out loud that the reading of that lot stands exactly where it stood. One of them said the other one had said the same thing on the twentieth and the day before, and the other one said that was because it had not moved and not because either of them had said it twice.

---

At about twelve the boy of about nineteen put his hand on the clerk's page, flat, over the one line, and asked her a question out loud in front of about nineteen people, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing answered him in about four seconds, and she did not, and two people at that table said afterwards that they had not known until that moment that a person could be asked a question in that yard and have it taken off them.

"May I put that on my page."

"No."

He waited. About nine people waited with him.

"Because a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person whose name it is," the mender said. "That is the reason and it took about four seconds and I have said the whole of it. A figure on a page can be argued with. A figure on a page can be got wrong and found out and written down wrong and found out again. A name on a figure cannot be got off it by the man who wears it, and it will still be there in six months when he is somewhere else and cannot come back and take it off, and that is not a rule of this district. That is what a name does to a piece of paper."

"You have not been asked for a name either," the boy said.

"I have not, and I have not been asked for a name by anybody in this yard since the nineteenth of the tenth month, and I am not going to be asked, and if I am asked then I will say no in about four seconds and the yard can enter that I said no."

Then the boy of about nineteen put his own page face down on the boards of that trestle table. He did it with the flat of his left hand and he did not take it back up for about an hour. He had done it three times before in this district, in the sixth, the ninth and the twelfth of the tenth month, and each of those three times had been about a figure of his own. This was the fourth, and it was the first of the four about a page that was not his, and two people at that table looked at the back of it and about nine looked away.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy of about nineteen put a page face down on a table, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said no to a request in about four seconds and gave a reason. She entered the reason, and she did not enter a name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat. He had said nothing out loud at any point since about eleven the day before, and about nine people at that table had looked at him twice to see whether he was going to, and he was not.

"You said a name," he said, to the man who puts tables up, across about nine feet of ground.

"I said a name."

"Not mine."

"No. If it had been yours you would have known it, and you are the only man here who could have stopped it going into a book and you are not going to be able to stop it going into anybody's head, and neither am I, and that is the whole of what happened this morning." The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had his hands in his pockets. "I have said it four times to four men and I have never once said it in front of you, and I have thought about that for eleven days and I have not worked out why, and I have stopped working on it."

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not answer that. Three people at that table said afterwards that a man who had said, on the thirteenth of this month, the sentence this month has turned on had said seven words to a man who had just put a name into the air. Neither of the two sentences had been read back from the top, and the two of them together were the eleventh month of this district in about four sentences.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank with the water running off him onto the stones and the bundle under his arm, and stood at the end of that table for about four seconds. About nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and he was not asked for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and he went up the lane.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane, got the four figures off that wall as he went past, and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about half past two the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane and went up it and did not stop, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm. About nine people watched him, and not one of them said a word.

At about three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands. Nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open eighty-seven days and was not run today, and that nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and seventeen days past. The rule said out loud in that yard on the tenth of the tenth month is forty-seven days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is forty-two days out of date, was not altered, and has nothing written beside it. The word unchecked is in the margin of a clerk's page and is over nothing and is not over any of the nine columns. The line under those twelve words is twenty-eight days old. **A name was said out loud in that yard at about eleven and was not written down by anybody, and a clerk entered that a name was said and did not enter what it was, and the record about the not asking says not asked.** Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid.

At about half past five the buckets came down that bank empty and went up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures for the last time that day and got all four of them.

About nineteen people heard a name in the open at about eleven this morning, and two of them have already said out loud that they do not remember it exactly. The man who said it went down that lane at about half past four and did not stop at the table. The two lines in that book were on the face of a book in the open all day with the stone where it has been, and there is nothing on any page in this district that says what was said.
