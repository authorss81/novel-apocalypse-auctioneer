# Chapter 434: A Day He Spent Walking

A man of fifty-six did not read the figures off that wall on the first of the twelfth month, and nine people there noticed that he had not, and four of them said so out loud, and he went down that lane at about ten and was gone for an hour and a half.

It was the first of the twelfth month and the wind came round to the north-west in the night and was coming down that bank in gusts that stopped and started. By ten there were about nineteen people in that yard of Lot Seventeen.

The clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and eighty-three days. She entered it at about seven, before there was anybody else there, and she entered that she had entered it before light and that the man of fifty-six had not been there to hear her enter it.

---

Two of them went after him about a quarter past ten and did not find him and came back. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing from that far end and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got thirty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and two people near that wall heard the number.

"**Sixty-one days on from the first of the tenth is this morning, and there is nothing at that gatepost, and nobody stood at it, and looking at it is not a finding.**"

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch and heard all of it and said from the water that he had been told on the seventeenth of the tenth month that a thing which does not arrive has no face and that a clerk is not going to write one for it, and that he agreed with that clerk, and that he did not think the man of fifty-six walking down that lane had made it any better or any worse.

Nine people at that table looked at the man of fifty-six's empty patch of wall, and four of them said out loud that he had read four figures off that boards every morning for six months without a single day missed, and that this was the first, and that the first was on a morning he had gone to look at a post instead.

The clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of fifty-six read no figure off that wall this morning, and entered that he had gone down that lane and had come back, and entered that a day a person spends walking is a day that person did not read, and entered that a day somebody spent is not a thing this district does not have.

---

The man of about fifty-six came back up that lane at about half past eleven and stood at that wall and put his palm flat on the boards and read the four figures, and got all of them, and said the word with them, and got the word four times. Two people said afterwards that he read them about two hours later than he has ever read them, and that nobody knew whether the two hours were the point or were not the point.

"**The board carries three hundred and forty-two, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and fifty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and seventy-two, and three hundred and thirty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He said one thing after that and it was to the people standing nearest the boards and not to the yard behind him.

"I have read those four numbers every morning since before the middle of the fifth month, and this morning I went down that lane to look at a post with a nail in it and a sheet on it, and there was nothing on that post that I had not known would not be there, and I have lost about two hours of a morning I have been doing this for six months." He took his hand off the boards. "I am not saying that is a price anybody should pay and I am not saying it is not. I am saying that the figure on that sheet is not going to move because I walked down a lane, and I would like whoever is keeping that in their head to know that a man who has read a figure every morning for six months can be got to lose a morning over a post."

Nobody kept it in their head. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there, and entered the thing, and entered no figure about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and took up the nine feet with his right hand in his coat. A voice at that near end said that a man who had come down that bank five days running with nothing on him had done it again on a morning when nothing had come four hundred miles off, and he said that was right, and that he had been awake since about four and had not been able to do anything with the hours between, and that was not a thing he was going to put on a page.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said one thing out loud from that east wall and it was to four people and not to the yard. He said that the man of fifty-six had walked down that lane and looked at a post and come back and read four figures two hours late, and that the whole of what had happened this morning was a man choosing between two things he could do with a day, and that some of them were standing there treating this as a day something was going to arrive on.

"It is the first of a month," said somebody. "Things arrive on the first of a month."

"What arrived," said the man who puts tables up.

Nobody answered him. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked a question there and that nobody answered it, and entered that the record about the not answering says not answered, and that a clerk does not enter a reason and does not enter a face.

Then the boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud to the man of fifty-six and it was about four seconds long and nobody else heard it.

"You said on the seventeenth of this month that nobody was going to check me on anything for a fortnight, and that is today, and you are the only person here I would have minded being checked by." He put his page down. "I am not asking you to. I am telling you that a fortnight is up and that about four other people are going to be disappointed and that you will not be one of them."

The man of fifty-six said that he was sorry about it, in the ordinary voice, and a couple of people at that table said afterwards that a man of fifty-six had said he was sorry about a fortnight that a boy of about nineteen had set for himself and nobody had set for him.

---

At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank and stood at that end of the table for about four seconds with the water coming off him, and four people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him, and he was not asked for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches with one arm, and nine people watched and nobody said a word.

At about half past three the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank and got the four figures and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day, and two people noticed and neither of them said anything. At about four a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him to go down that lane with the man of fifty-six or with anybody else, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about four a man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was asked out loud by the boy of about nineteen whether a man who has lost a morning to a post ought to have lost it, and he said no in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got forty and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and two people near the east wall heard the number.

"**A day is not a thing a man spends on purpose and it is not a thing anybody takes off him, and if it had been either then I would have said so and you would both have known it.**"

The boy of about nineteen said that he had been about to say something else and had not, and that about four people at that table had noticed him not saying it.

At about half past four a woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with two empty buckets and said one thing out loud to nobody in particular, and a few people heard it.

"Sixty-one days. I have carried water on this bank for nineteen years and I have never once counted from a day to a day, and I have got a figure that I would give anybody who asked me and I have not got the day it comes from and I would not be able to tell you where to start counting." She filled the buckets. "That is the whole of what I know about that post and I have known it for nineteen years and nobody ever asked me."

Nobody asked her. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that the thing was not a figure about a sheet, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open ninety-two days and is three months, and was not run this day, and that nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. She entered that the figure on that gatepost has not moved on any of the fifty days and that its own age is a hundred and eighty-three days, and that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and twenty-two days past. The rule said out loud in that ground on the tenth of the tenth month is fifty-two days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is forty-seven days out of date, was not altered, and has nothing written beside it. The figure entered off a list on the thirtieth of the tenth month is thirty-three days old and was checked on the last day of the month it describes, and the check is a figure about a count and is not a finding. The word unchecked is in the margin of her own page and is over that figure and over nothing else. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six on this day and on the day before and on the day before that. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her.

About four hundred yards off there is a post with a nail in it and a sheet on the other side of the nail, and the sheet has the figure that has been on it since the first of the sixth on it, and it is about four hundred yards from that table, and it did not have anything on it this morning that it did not have on every other morning of this year. A man stood in front of it for about as long as it takes that woman to fill one bucket. Then he walked back up a lane to a board with four figures on it that he read two hours later than he has ever read them.
