# Chapter 428: About Four Seconds

The figure on the second line of that book was forty-one days out of date at about a quarter to nine in the morning. The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said so out loud to nobody at about ten, and a boy of about nineteen asked a question about it at about eleven and got an answer in about four seconds that he did not like and has not yet worked out.

It was the twenty-sixth of the eleventh month and there was no wind at all in the place for the first time in about a week. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

The man of fifty-six got the four figures off that wall at about half past nine with his palm down on the boards, and said the word with them, and got the word four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and thirty-six, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and fifty-two days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and sixty-six, and three hundred and twenty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and it did not move this morning, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it did not move. She entered the age of it as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and seventy-seven days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that no day-count for that sheet exists in this district to be entered.

---

"Forty-one days," said the man who puts tables up, to the cart, to two people, and to nobody in particular. "I have been putting that table up for six months and that figure has been on the end of it for forty-one days and it is the only figure on that table that has not changed, including the figures on the boards behind you, which change every morning because somebody rubs them out."

Nobody said anything about it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a figure out loud there and entered the figure. She entered that she had been asked for it by nobody, and that she was going to write it down because a figure a man says out loud in a yard is the only kind anybody there has ever got.

That is different from the figure he gave at a trough on the nineteenth, and two people at that table noticed the difference. The man who puts tables up said out loud that he knew they had noticed, that the number from the trough was a number about a man and this one was a number about a page, and that the difference between those two things was the whole of what he had understood about the place in six months.

At about eleven the boy of about nineteen asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing a question in front of about nine people. The boy counted what the mender said and read it back to himself twice, and the mender asked him whether the number was a figure about a sentence or a figure about a man. The boy said it was a figure about a sentence, and that was the end of it.

"I want to ask you what happens to a figure that nobody can check, and you told me yesterday and I have thought about it all evening and I do not agree with you."

"You did not ask me yesterday either. I answered a question you asked on the day before that one, and you have been carrying both of them since, and you have come back with the same question in a different coat."

"I have been carrying both of them since. I want to say it again in a different form and find out whether you have a different answer." The boy put his page down on the boards and kept his hand on it. "If a thing is said out loud in that yard tomorrow, and about nineteen people hear it, and nobody writes it down, and on the day after that four of those nineteen people are asked what was said, what are they going to answer?"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing answered it in about four seconds, and about nine people heard him, and two of them said afterwards that it had taken about four seconds.

"They are going to answer with the thing they heard," he said, "and four of them are going to answer with four different things. The four of those are going to be a figure about four people and not about what was said, and that is the whole of what happens, and I have been standing in it for six months and it has happened four times."

He did not say what was going to be said and he was not asked. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a question was asked out loud in that yard and answered in about four seconds, and that the question was about a thing that had not happened yet. She entered that she was not going to enter what it was, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about half past eleven the man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch to his thigh and said one thing up the bank without raising his voice, which is the third time in about a week that he has not raised it.

"You are all getting ready for it. I have been in this ditch for two months getting ready for nothing and I can tell the difference from where I am standing." He did not stop work. "If it is a figure then you are all going to be standing about four feet from me in about a day, and I would like one of you to have worked out in advance what you are going to do with your hands."

Nobody answered him. The boy of about nineteen said that he had worked out what he was going to do with his hands, which was that he was going to put them in his coat, and the loam man said that was the first sensible thing anybody had said there in a fortnight, and went back to the water. That right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day, and nobody said one word to him about it.

At about twelve the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood his nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat. A voice at the near end of that table said that he had come down that bank at nine on one morning, at ten on the next, at half past ten on the three after that, at half past ten on the two after that, at half past ten yesterday and at twelve today, and that somebody ought to work out whether there was a figure in that.

"There is a figure in it," he said, "and I am not going to say what it is, and half of you have already worked out what it is, and I would like half of you to notice that you worked it out and that nobody told you and that I am not going to confirm it either way."

Nobody confirmed it either way. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a person said out loud there that four people had worked out a thing and that he was not going to confirm it, and entered that a person who has said a thing out loud in a yard has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about one the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank wet to the chest with the bundle under his arm and stopped at the end of that table for about four seconds. About nine people were within four feet of him, and nobody said a word to him, and he was not asked for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He was not offered an arm and he did not ask for one, and he went up the lane.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, got the four figures off that wall as he went past, and stopped at the low wall and looked down into that ditch for about a minute. Nobody asked him what he was looking at, and nobody asked him about the eleven miles.

At about two the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane and went up it and did not stop. The near wheel on that cart was dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, set it down, did it again about four seconds later, and got back on the seat. About nine people there watched a man lift a wheel with one arm, and not one of them said a word about it.

Then the boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at the near end of that trestle table, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing counted what he said and got eighty-four, and about nine people heard the number come out of his mouth, and two people said that a man with a cloth on his hand had just counted a boy of about nineteen for the first time in this district.

"**A figure that nobody can check is still a figure, and the only thing wrong with it is that there is one of it and nobody can get hold of the other, and I have had a fortnight of not being checked on anything, and I have thought about the whole of that, and somebody has to be checked on something, and I do not know who and I am not going to be the one who says so out loud in that yard.**"

Nobody read that back from the top. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy of about nineteen said out loud in that yard that somebody has to be checked on something and that he did not know who, and entered that she was not going to ask him, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and entered no name.

At about half past two a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and the man who puts tables up came past him with the cart, stopped for about four seconds, and went on. Nobody said a word about either the stopping or the cart, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open eighty-six days and was not run today, and that nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and sixteen days past. The rule said out loud in that yard on the tenth of the tenth month is forty-six days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is forty-one days out of date, was not altered, and has nothing written beside it. A clerk entered that figure this morning because a man said it out loud and asked for it, and entered the reason she gave for writing down a figure a man had said rather than a figure she had got off a board. The word unchecked is in the margin of a clerk's page and is over nothing and is not over any of the nine columns. The line under those twelve words is twenty-seven days old. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures for the last time that day and got all four of them.

About half past five there were about nine people left there, and two of them knew what was going to be said in it in about seventeen hours. One of the two was at the east wall with his hand-cart and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and the other was at the far end of that trestle table with his left hand in a cloth. Two people looked at one of those two and two people looked at the other, and neither of them said anything. The boy of about nineteen was at the near end of that table with his own page in his coat and his hands in his coat and no idea what for.
