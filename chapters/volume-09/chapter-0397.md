# Chapter 397: The Thing on the End of That Table

---

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them got the figure off the top board at about twenty past eight on the twenty-fifth of the tenth month, standing at the near end of that trestle table on the side away from the boards, where nobody usually stands, and he got it, and then he stood there and looked at the boards for a while as though the boards were a thing he had not seen before, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures out loud at about half past nine and got all four, and the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up read them again at ten and got all four, and about four people in that yard said out loud that two men have now got the same four numbers off that wall and a third of them has not looked at the wall at all this morning, and nobody in that yard said who the third one was.

"**The board carries three hundred and five and the train on that siding has stood six hundred and twenty-one days, and one hundred and forty-six days is how long that record has been in force, and the figure on the figure on that gatepost is a hundred and forty-six days old, and those are two figures and not one figure.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the rival record and the age of the figure about that figure are two figures counted off one day and one exclusion and that they land on one interval and that is not a slip and is not a finding.

---

At about half past ten the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up put his forearm on that table where it had been for six months and looked down at the end of it, and the stone was on the lot book at the corner nearest the boards, and the clerk's page was under the same stone, and the boy's page was under that one, and the two chairs were at the table and the man of fifty-six was standing at the near end of it with his hand flat on the boards and the lot book had two lines in it and the second of the two said about a foot of standing water in a ditch this morning, and the page had nine columns ruled on it and nothing written in one of them.

He took his forearm off the table and stood back about four feet and looked at all three of those things from where a stranger would be standing, and then he said one thing out loud in that yard, in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard it and the man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-six and wrote it down, and the count is eighty-six because that is what is printed there.

"**A man walks up to that table from that gate and he sees a stone and two lines in a book and a page with nine columns on it and nothing in them, and he cannot tell which of the figures in that book is current, and it is not because the book is unclear. It is because the book is clear. That is the whole trouble and it is on the end of that table in the open where anybody can walk up to it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man looked at the end of that table from about four feet back and said what a person walking up to it can and cannot tell, and that what he cannot tell is which of the figures in that book is current, and that a lot book is a lot book and is not a sheet and is not a column of not-askings and is not a column of people and is not a document this district does not own and is not a fifth of the five things this district protects and is not a seventh of the six instruments it has built and not named, and that a book a stranger may walk up to and read is a document and a book in a room with a shut door is not one, and that a thing being clear is not the same as a thing being right.

Then about four people in that yard said the ordinary thing, which is that a stranger could go and fetch somebody, and about four other people in that yard said that a stranger four hundred miles off is not going to come here, and about four of them said that a book a stranger may walk up to is a book for a stranger who is standing in front of it and not a book for a man on a road, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud that a man who can be fetched is a man in this district and a man who cannot be fetched is a man four hundred miles off with a sheet nailed to a gate, and about four of them said he was right and about four of them said that the two of those are not the same person and have never been in the same room.

A count of the people who answered a door is a count of the answerers and not a count of the people, and the difference between a number made by knocking and a number made by walking is the whole of what a removal is. No figure on that page today and none on those boards was made by knocking, and none was made by walking. A name is not a figure.

---

At about two the man about thirty-seven who puts tables up put his hand on the stone and moved it about an inch, which is the first time anybody has touched that stone since it was put there, and he put his hand back on it and pushed it square again and said one thing out loud, and about four people heard it, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy-one.

"**I have moved that stone an inch to see if it was loose and it was not loose and I have put it back where it was, and I want it said in whatever page you keep that I did that and not a clerk of nineteen years and not a boy of about nineteen, and I am not a keeper of that book and nobody has asked me to be.**"

The stone on that lot book was moved about an inch and put back, and the man who moved it is a man who puts tables up, and not a clerk of a house and not a clerk of nineteen years, and is not a keeper of anything. The two lines in that book were not altered, nothing was written beside them, and no figure was added to that book. A stone on a book is not a figure and is not a mark and carries no job.

At about half past two the man of about nineteen read the five headings on that clerk's page out loud, one after another, standing at the end of that trestle table, and then read the four words over the four columns under them, and the man of fifty-six stood about nine feet off with his hand on the boards and listened to all nine of those and did not say one word, and about four people in that yard said that is nine things a person can say a word about and not one of them has a name in it.

About four people in that yard then asked him, in the ordinary way, which of the two lines in that lot book is the current one, and he looked at the stone and then at the two lines and then at the four of them, and he said out loud that he did not know and that he would not guess in front of about nineteen people, and about four of them said that is the second time in three days a man in that yard has said he does not know, and about four of them said that the man of fifty-six said it about a month and this one said it about a book, and the man of about nineteen said out loud that a man who does not know a thing and says so in a yard is doing the only thing there is, and about four of them said that nobody had asked him what to say and he said that he was not waiting to be.

Then about four people in that yard said out loud that a stone on a book is not a market and neither is a book on a table, and one of them said that a market is a place where a person is asked a question and that the last time anybody in this district was asked one at that table was the thirteenth of this month, and the mender said that the thirteenth of this month was the day a toll was read out, and about four of them said that was a reading and not a question, and nobody in that yard said one word about the difference.

Then about four people in that yard said out loud that a page with nine columns ruled on it and nothing in them is the same as a page with nine columns ruled on it and something in them, and about four other people said that it is not, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud that the difference between them is what somebody chose to put in and the reason they chose it, and that one of those two is on a page a clerk keeps in her own coat and the other one is on a page under a stone in the open, and about four of them said he was right and about four of them said that a page under a stone in the open with nothing in it is a page a stranger can walk up to and read and get nothing out of, and that is not a column of people and is not a record of anybody and is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own.

Nine columns with nothing in them is a figure about nine columns and is not a figure about nine people and is not a finding about anybody. A page of a clerk and a page under a stone in the open are two pages and are not one page. The whole of what this district has made in six months is the one under the stone, and the clerk of nineteen years is not going to say so in her own hand on a page nobody can walk up to.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came to the end of that table at about three and read the two lines in that lot book out loud from the top in the ordinary voice, both of them, and put his right hand flat on the boards beside the stone while he read the second one and it did not close, and about four people in that yard watched his hand on the boards and none of them said a word about it, and he took it back into his coat when he had finished.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man read the two lines in that lot book out loud in that yard this afternoon and that nobody read them back from the top, and that a thing read back by nobody binds nobody, and that the second of those two lines is ten days out of date and was not altered and has not been corrected and has not been replaced and has no second figure beside it and is not going to get one from anybody in this yard, and that a line which has stopped being true is not a wrong line and is not a mistake anybody made.

---

The rest of that day went ordinary. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up that bank at about ten with his legs wet to the knee and read the four figures off the boards at the end of that table, and got all four. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane twice, once near eleven and once near four, and read the four figures off that wall both times and got all four both times, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about the eleven miles and nobody said one word to him about a stranger.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in that ditch until the light went, with his hook and his bundle, and came up the bank about ten minutes after everybody else had left the wall. The man who puts tables up was at the east wall with his cart for the rest of the afternoon and was given nothing and nobody asked him about the tent, which is still rolled on the back of that hand-cart and has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the June.

That bid has stood open fifty-five days and was not run today. The first of the eighth month went past eighty-five days ago. The reading of that lot is where it was on the twelfth of this month, which is begun and not finished, and it stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person, and there is nobody in that yard who has been asked for one. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six and no name went into it by anybody.

By the time the light had gone the stone was square on the lot book at the corner nearest the boards, and the two lines in it were the two lines that were in it this morning, and a page with nine columns on it was under the same stone with nothing written in one of them, and a stranger walking up to that table in the morning would see exactly what about nineteen people saw today and would be able to read every word of it.
