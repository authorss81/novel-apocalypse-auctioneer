# Chapter 399: Ninety-Eight Days at the Foot of a Wall

---

The low wall at the south end of the yard of Lot Seventeen is about four feet high and about nine feet long and it has a gap in it at one end that a man can get his feet through, and a man of about sixty-four has been sitting at the foot of it every night since the morning of the twenty-first of the seventh month, and on the twenty-seventh of the tenth month he was at the foot of it at about ten in the morning with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures off those boards out loud at about half past nine and got all four, and the man of about nineteen wrote all four down, and about four people in that yard said out loud that a man at the foot of that wall since the morning of the twenty-first of the seventh month is a hundred and ninety-eight days, and about four of them said that is a figure about a man and not a figure about his hands and that nobody in this yard has any business counting a man's hands.

"**The board carries three hundred and seven, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and twenty-three days, the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is two hundred and seventeen days, and that record has been in force a hundred and forty-eight days without being read out in this yard once.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that a man of about sixty-four is in his ninety-eighth day in this district and has slept on ninety-seven of the ninety-eight nights of his run, and that the one night he did not sleep on is the night of the twenty-first of the eighth when he stood in a sound and did not lie down, and that she entered that then and entered it again today and is not going to say a word about why he stood.

---

At about eleven about four people in that yard came down that lane and said good morning to the man at the foot of that wall, and about four other people in that yard said good morning to each other instead, and about four of them said it at the same time so that nobody could tell which of them had said it to whom, and the man of about sixty-four said good morning back to all of them, whichever of them it was, and about four of them said that is a man saying good morning to about nineteen people when about four of them said it, and he said that is how a yard works and that is not a complaint.

The man of fifty-six came back down that lane at about eleven from the fence end, where he has gone most mornings for six months, and read the four figures again and got all four, and about four people in that yard said out loud that a man who has read those boards every morning for six months and has not missed one is a fact about a man and is not a finding and is not a figure, and he said that he would be the first person in this yard to say that is not a medal, and about four of them said that is the closest he has come to saying anything about himself in a month, and he said nothing else and went up the lane again at about half past eleven.

At about one the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came down the lane and stopped about nine feet off the man at the foot of that wall with a piece of wire in his right pocket and about nine inches of it, and about four people in that yard watched him stop, and the mender did not ask him anything and nobody in that yard asked him anything, and after about four seconds the mender went on up the lane and put the wire in the fence at the top of it where he has been putting wire for five months, and about four people said that a man who came nine feet towards a person and then went away has said something and about four of them said he has not said a word, and nobody in that yard asked that man anything that afternoon, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and a piece of wire in a pocket is not an offer and was not offered and was not taken.

At about two the child of about eight came down that lane with a woman he belongs to and stopped at the gap in that low wall and put his hand through it and looked at the man sitting behind it, and the man of about sixty-four looked back at him, and nobody in that yard asked the child one thing, and he was not asked whether anything works or does not work, and after about a minute he took his hand out of the gap and went on up the lane with the woman he belongs to.

A child of about eight put his hand through a gap in a low wall and looked at a man, and was asked nothing and asked nothing, and the woman he belongs to stood behind him for about a minute and said nothing to anybody either, and then took him on up the lane. Nobody in that yard knows whether that wall keeps anything off the weather, nobody has written it down, and nobody is going to write it down in this month either.

---

At about half past four the man of about sixty-four got his hand out from under his own thigh, where it had been since about ten, and put it flat on the stones in front of him, and about four people in that yard said that he has no use in either hand and about four of them said that is a thing said about a man and not a thing anybody can see, and he said one thing out loud about it and about four people heard it and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy-six and wrote it down, and the count is seventy-six because that is what is printed there.

"**I have had nothing in my hands since the morning of the twenty-first of the seventh month except this wall and a coat and about the length of that yard, and I have not been asked what I am going to do with them, and I am not going to be, and if somebody in this district is going to worry about it they can worry about it on their own account and not on mine.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said out loud that nobody has asked him what he is going to do with his hands, and that a clerk is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands because nobody asked her, and that a man who has had nothing in his hands for ninety-eight days is not a holder of a lot and is not a bearer of a reading and is not the second reader and is not a post and is not a steward and is not a job nobody asked him for, and that none of those has been said to him in this yard and none of them is going to be.

Then about four people in that yard said out loud that he should be asked to sit in the second chair, and about four other people in that yard said, at the same time, that nobody in that yard is going to ask him to sit in the second chair, and he heard both of those and about four people said he heard both of them, and about four of them said that is a man being discussed in a yard about nine feet off him and that is not a thing anybody is going to do twice, and he said nothing at all about the chair and was not asked about it and was not offered anything.

At about four the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart went up that lane and did not stop, and the man at the foot of that wall lifted his head and watched him go by and put it down again, and about four people in that lane said that is a man watching a man go past and that is not a reading and is not a record and is not a figure about either of them, and nobody in that yard or that lane said one word to the man at the foot of the wall about the cart or about the lane or about the hour.

At about half past three a man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in the standing water at the low end of a ditch about three feet deep behind a building with two doors, and about four people in that yard said out loud that a man who has been in standing water since the middle of the ninth month is the only man in this district who knows what is in that ditch every morning, and about four of them said that nobody has ever asked him, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said out loud that nobody is going to ask him either and that a man who works in a thing every day and has never been asked what is in it is a man who has said something already by standing in it, and about four of them said that is not an answer and he said that it is the only one anybody is going to get.

A man who works in standing water in a ditch was in that ditch this afternoon and was not asked what was in it, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The only person who knows when the figure on the second line of that lot book stopped being true is a man who was asked for nothing on any day this month and was not asked today.

---

The rest of that day went ordinary. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch before nine, was on the bank about ten, and went back in after lunch, and his right hand did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane at about four, got the four figures off that wall without stopping, and went on up without stopping at that wall either. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about half past four, wet to the chest, and said good evening to nobody in particular.

That bid has stood open fifty-seven days and was not run today. The first of the eighth month went past eighty-seven days ago. The figure on the second line of that lot book is twelve days out of date and was not altered. The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and ninety days back and is open and was named out loud in that yard once this afternoon and closed in nothing. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six and no name went into it by anybody.

About half past seven, when the light had gone off that yard and off that low wall, the man of about sixty-four got up off those stones the way he has got up off them every night since the morning of the twenty-first of the seventh month, which is with his left forearm on the coping first and then his hip, and on the ninety-seventh night that forearm got him up in about a second and a half, and on the ninety-eighth night it took him about six seconds, and about four people in that lane who had not gone home yet stood about nine feet off and watched that happen and not one of them said one word about it, and he got up and took his coat off the stones and did not fold it because it did not need folding and went up that lane on his own.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was in his ninety-eighth day in this district and has slept on ninety-seven of the ninety-eight nights, and that a figure he folds his coat for is the night he is about to sleep and that night has not happened and is a hundred and ninety-ninth and is the only night in this district that is one more than a day, and that nobody in that lane was sent on nine days today and nobody asked him to go and he did not go, and that a man whose trade is over is not a failure and is not a tragic figure and is not redeemed, and that a clerk is not going to say that about him and is not going to say the other thing either.
