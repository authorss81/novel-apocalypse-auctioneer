# Chapter 400: Four Things in One Yard

---

The twenty-eighth of the tenth month came in with a hard clean light on that bank and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of fifty-six was at the end of that trestle table before anybody else that morning, and he read the four figures out loud over nobody's shoulder and got all four, and the man of about nineteen wrote all four down and got the same figure off every one of the four, and nobody in that yard counted how long the man of fifty-six had been standing there.

"**The board carries three hundred and eight, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and twenty-four days, and the days nobody has entered anything is three hundred and thirty-eight counting from the twenty-fourth of November, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and it carries the same day on it that it has carried since the first of the sixth.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on that sheet is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that its own age as a figure about the figure is a hundred and forty-nine days counted off the first of the sixth and the day it took effect not counted in, and that the sheet's own tenure on that post is not a figure this district has and that she is not going to invent one, and that a figure about a figure is not a day-count for a sheet.

---

At about half past ten four things this district has spent a month on were in that yard at the same time for the first time since the thirteenth of the eighth month, and about nine people in that yard stood about nine feet off the middle of it and looked at the four of them one after another without saying anything for about two minutes.

The lot book was on the end of that trestle table under the same stone at the same corner, with two lines in it. The man of fifty-six was at that table with his hand flat on those boards, reading figures off a wall that has carried the twenty-fourth of December and has not been washed. The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was about nine feet off the near end of that table with his right hand in his coat. And the second line in that book said about a foot of standing water in a ditch this morning, and the ditch was over the low wall, and nobody in that yard had said a figure about it and nobody had been asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud about the four of them, and about nineteen people heard it, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy and wrote it down, and did not say the count out loud, and about four people in that yard wanted him to and he did not.

"**Three of those are figures this district can check and one of them is a figure in a book in the open that nobody can check and has not been able to since the fifteenth of this month, and all four of them are on that table or behind it at the same time, and I am not saying that is a finding because I do not think it is.**"

Three figures on a wall and a figure in a book in the open stood in one yard at the same time this morning, and one of the four is out of date by thirteen days and was not altered and has nothing written beside it, and there is no column anywhere in this district for saying which of the four is the worst one and nobody in that yard was asked for one.

At about eleven about four people in that yard said out loud that the thing to do about it is to say which of the figures in that book is current, and about four other people in that yard said, in about four seconds and with a reason, that saying which figure is current is putting a word beside a figure and there is no column on that page for it and no fifth column of that shape is going to be ruled, and about four of them said that the boy had been told in about four seconds on the tenth of this month that he is not the second reader and has not been asked since, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that nobody was going to rule a column this morning either.

A column for saying which figure in a book is current is not a column of not-askings and is not a column of people and is not a column of anything this district has. A new column of the same shape is not an empty column and is not the same column and is not added to the old ones. Four columns of not-askings is a figure about four columns, and no fifth was ruled today.

---

At about two the clerk of nineteen years shut her page at about half past two, earlier than she has shut it on any day this month, and about four people in that yard asked her why, and she said one thing back to them in about four seconds and the man of about nineteen counted it and got ninety-six and read it back off his own page to nobody, which is what he does, and which is not a reading of anything.

"**Tomorrow is the twenty-ninth of this month and the day after that is the last day of it, and I am not going to stand here this morning and tell you how many days this month has, because nobody in this district has counted it and a month nobody has counted is a month nobody has checked, and I would be reading a number off a list and calling it a count, and the man of fifty-six has told me this week that he cannot get it off a board either, and I have believed him.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the month after this one is not a figure this district has, and that the length of this one is not a figure on her page either, and that she is not going to enter one before she has counted it off a day she can name, and that a figure somebody has entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day, and that she has written that sentence three times in four months and that the third time it is a habit and a habit is a finding and she is entering the finding about herself.

Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures off those boards for the last time that day at about half past four and got all four, and the man of about nineteen wrote them down for the last time that day and got the same figure off every one of the four, and about four people in that yard said out loud that tomorrow is the twenty-ninth of this month and the day after that is the last day of it and that a clerk is going to have to count marks off that board, and the man of fifty-six said one thing out loud about it and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got eighty-nine and wrote it down, and his page for that day had four figures on it and three of them were the man of fifty-six's and one of them was the clerk's.

"**I have read the figures off that wall every morning for six months and I have got them every morning, and I am not going to be told how many days a month has out of a list, and if anybody counts them off that board tomorrow I will stand here and read them, and a figure somebody has entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day and I have been on this side of it since the first of the sixth.**"

A man who has read the figures off that wall every morning for six months said out loud that he is not going to be told the length of a month out of a list, and that he will read it off that board if anybody counts it. A figure somebody entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day, and a man who put the first figure in a book four hundred yards from that gate has said that sentence out loud in that yard before today and was not asked to and was not asked for anything else.

Then about half past five the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came to the end of that table and read the two lines in that lot book out loud from the top in the ordinary voice, both of them, standing up, and did not put his hand on the boards, and put the book back down under the same stone at the same corner, and about nine people in that yard heard all of it and not one of them read it back from the top, and a thing read back by nobody binds nobody, so that afternoon's reading of those two lines bound nobody, and it was not a reading of the lot and did not advance it, and the reading stands where it stood on the twelfth of this month.

---

The rest of that day went ordinary. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch before nine, stood on the bank at about ten, and went in again after lunch, and his right hand did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in this day. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane at about four, got the four figures off that wall, and went on up, and nobody asked him about the eleven miles. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about half past four, wet to the chest, with his hook in his hand and his bundle under his arm. The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was against the east wall with his cart, the tent rolled on the back of it where it has stayed since the thirtieth of the June, and he was given nothing and nobody asked him about it.

That bid stands at fifty-eight days and was not run today and nothing has been said about closing it and nothing is going to be said about it in this yard tomorrow either. The first day of the eighth month is eighty-eight days past and a ladder is spent as a ladder. The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and ninety-one days back and is open. The reading of that lot is where it was on the twelfth of this month, which is begun and not finished. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six and no name went into it by anybody. The fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid and the count is still five, and two days' work have gone into things in this district since the sixth of this month and neither of them has been paid for and a day's work is not a toll.

About four people in that yard said out loud, at about half past six, that this district has been living inside a figure it has never counted since the first of the sixth month, and about four of them said that a yard is a room with no roof and a figure nobody in the room knows is a figure nobody in the room can say out loud, and the man of fifty-six said that he would be at that table in the morning and would read whatever was on those boards, and about four of them said good and about four of them said nothing at all.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, carried them up the two goes, and read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody in that yard said one word to her.

By the time the light had gone the man of fifty-six was at the end of that trestle table with his hand flat on those boards and the four figures on them were the same four figures that had been on them at about half past eight, and about four people in that yard said good evening to each other on their way out. The stone was square on the lot book at the corner nearest the boards. Over the low wall at the south end of that yard, out of sight of everybody standing at that table, a ditch about three feet deep had standing water in the bottom of it, and no figure about how much was written anywhere in this district except on one line in one book in the open, and that line was thirteen days out of date, and nobody in that yard knew what was in the water and nobody had been asked and nobody was going to be.
