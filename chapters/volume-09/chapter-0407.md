# Chapter 407: The Fifteenth, Said Out Loud

Two men at the near end of that trestle table were working out on their fingers when the figure on the second line of that book would be a month old, and one of them got to the fifteenth of this month and the other one said nothing at all and put his hand flat on the boards and looked at the two of them.

It was the fifth of the eleventh month, and the light was coming up off that bank sideways, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall in the ordinary way, over the mender's shoulder, and got all of them, and said the word with them after each one, and got it four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and fifteen, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and thirty-one days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and forty-five, and three hundred and six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

That sheet is four hundred and eleven and the day on it is the day it has always carried. A clerk of nineteen years entered the figure, and entered that it is a hundred and fifty-six days old as a figure about a figure, and entered that the two of them are figures about a figure and are not a day-count for a sheet and are not a figure about how long a sheet has been on a post.

---

The stone was on that book at the corner nearest the boards, where it has been for a month, and about four people at the near end of that table read the two lines standing up in about four seconds, because they are two lines and they have not changed since the thirteenth of the tenth month, and everybody in the yard knows them.

> One lot. A building with two doors. About six and a half thousand bucketfuls of water. No person in it.
>
> One lot. A ditch about three feet deep behind a building with two doors. About a foot of standing water in it this morning. No person in it.

"Nobody is going to be a stranger to that book," the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said from the east wall, where he was with his hand-cart. "There is not one person in this yard who cannot read it."

"Which is not the same as there being a stranger."

"There is going to be a stranger one day. That is the entire argument for that book and it is the only one anybody in this yard has got."

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down to the end of that table at about eleven and read the two lines out loud from the top, both of them, standing up, without touching the book, with his right hand in his coat, and he put his hand back in his coat afterwards and the clerk saw that and entered nothing about it.

Then he said one thing out loud with a reason, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-eight and put it down and read it back to himself in a low voice, which is a thing he does when he does not trust a figure.

"**The second of those two lines stopped being true on the fifteenth of the tenth month, and on the fifteenth of this month it will have been out of date for a month, and a month is the shortest interval this district has evidence for, and I am not able to give anybody the reason why and I am not going to be asked for it, and what I am able to say is what a month old means for the only person I know of who would be affected by it, which is a man who walks up to that table with money and wants to know what he is buying, and he will not be able to tell, and neither will the man standing next to him, and neither will the man who put it there.**"

Nobody read that back from the top. About four people said so afterwards and about four said it did not matter, and a thing read back by nobody binds nobody whatever it is about.

"Twenty days," somebody said, from the near end of that table. "It is twenty days out of date this morning and it is a month on the fifteenth and I have been working at that since about half past ten and you have just said the same thing I have been saying."

"I have said a different thing. You have been working out a day. I have been saying what a month is, and there is a reason for that and it is not on any page in this district and I am not going to say it in a yard with no roof in it."

"There is a reason," the mender said, from the end of that table, with his left hand in its cloth. "And I am not going to give it either, and I am going to be asked for it four times this week, and any one of you who asks me is going to get a no, and I want that no entered as a no about an explanation and not as a refusal of a figure, because a refusal of a figure is one of the eight and this is not that."

"You have said you would not say it before," somebody said.

"I have said I would not say it three times and I have not said it, and three times somebody has taken my not saying it as a rule I made about the district, and it is not a rule I made. It is a thing I know and I am not giving away."

---

At about one about four people said that the two dates were on the page now, and that a pair of dates in a book is a thing anybody can check against a day they can count, and about four other people said that nobody had put them there.

"They are on the page," the man in the coat said. "Not in the book. I have not put a day into that book and I am not going to, and the two dates are on the end of a table in a yard in daylight, and that is where they are going to stay until somebody with a reason puts them anywhere else."

"You have said that before," the man of fifty-six said, from the wall. "On the twentieth of this month you stood about nine feet off that table and said the whole of how that figure was got and named the man who knows the day, and you did not put a day in the book either, and the figure is a day older today than it was then."

"That is what I am doing," the man in the coat said. "I am doing it again."

The clerk of nineteen years entered that the two dates are on a page in the open and in no book. A day a clerk names is not a lot, is not a figure of a lot, is not a figure of a person, is not a toll, is not a holder, is not a remedy and is not a term. She entered the pair of them as a fact about two days and not as a correction of any figure, and no line in that book was altered and none is going to be.

---

Then the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about twenty past two with his hook in one hand and his bundle under his arm, and stopped at the end of that table, and read the two lines standing up the way anybody can, people at the near end of that table went quiet in a way that had not happened in the yard since before this district began keeping figures.

He has not been asked about anything this month. He was named in the yard as a description on the twentieth of the tenth month, and was not asked, and was not going to be. He is the only person in this district who knows when the figure on the second line stopped being true. He stood a few feet from a page with a word in the margin of it for about a minute, and about nine people were watching a man not answer a question, and he had not been asked one.

He said good evening to nobody in particular and went up the lane, and his hands were not touched and nothing was asked about them and he was given nothing.

---

At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane and did not come into that yard and stopped at the low wall, which he has done about nine times since the middle of the ninth month, and nobody asked him anything, and he read nothing at all, not the boards, not the page, and not the two lines in the book.

The near wheel on that cart was dragging and it was dragging again. He got it up about nine inches off the ground with his right forearm and set it down, and did it again about four seconds later, and got back on the seat.

About nine people in the yard watched a man lift a wheel about nine inches off the ground with one arm and not one of them said a word about it.

"He comes up that lane and reads nothing," somebody said.

"He comes up that lane and reads nothing, and the day there is somebody who wants a thing read back from the top he will still read nothing, because he has never once done it and he was never asked, and that is the only honest answer anybody is going to get about him."

"Nobody was going to make him the second reader."

"Nobody is going to tell him anything at all," the mender said, "and a second reader is nobody in particular, and I am not going to say that in a way that sounds like he is somebody in particular, because he is not, and about four of you would be writing his name in something by the end of the month if I say it wrong."

Then the man with the cart went down that lane and did not come up it again that day, and about four people said good evening to him as he went past the end of that table, and he said good evening back.

---

What was left of that day was the man in the ditch, the road keeper, the reeds, the cart and the buckets, in that order, and nothing anybody in that yard said changed the order. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch before nine, came up out of it about ten, read the four figures at the end of that table and got every one of them, and went back down the bank, and that right hand of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point. The road keeper came up that lane twice, had the four figures off that wall both times, was right both times, and stopped at the low wall for a few minutes this afternoon and looked down into that ditch without saying a figure, which is the first time he has done that since he came down that lane, and nobody asked him about the eleven miles, and about four people said he had not asked about anything. The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was against the east wall with his cart and the tent rolled on the back of it where it has been since the thirtieth of the June, and he was given nothing.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the second line of that lot book is twenty days out of date and was not altered and has nothing written beside it, and that a line which has stopped being true is not a wrong line and is not a mistake anybody made. The bid has stood open sixty-five days and was not run today. The first day of the eighth month is ninety-five days past, and nobody in the yard has said out loud what the ladder was for and nobody has been asked. The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and ninety-eight days back and is open and was not named out loud today. The rule said out loud in the yard on the tenth of the tenth month is twenty-five days standing. A man of about sixty-four is in his hundred and sixth day and has slept on a hundred and five of the hundred and six nights, and the night he is about to sleep, which has not happened, is a hundred and seventh. Nobody wrote a name in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and it was empty at about six.

About half past five the buckets came down that bank empty and went up it full, as they have every evening for nineteen years, and she read what was in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody and nobody said one word to her.
