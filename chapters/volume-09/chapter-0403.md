# Chapter 403: Twelve Figures and a Word on Them

The clerk of nineteen years got to the end of that trestle table at about half past seven, before there was anybody else in the yard, and wrote one word twelve times in the margin of her own page in her own hand, with a space left after every one of them.

It was the first of the eleventh month. The frost was off the ground and the light came up clean behind that bank, and the first day of a month is the day every figure that crossed into it has to say where it came from, and about nine people in the yard had not known there was such a day until the man of fifty-six said so at about half past eight.

He read the four figures out loud with his hand flat on them, and got all of them, and the man of about nineteen put all four down on his own page, and the clerk of nineteen years did not read hers out loud to anybody.

"**The board carries three hundred and eleven, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and twenty-seven days, and nobody has entered anything for three hundred and forty-one, and three hundred and two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved on any day of this month. A clerk of nineteen years entered that, and entered that its own age as a figure about the figure is a hundred and fifty-two days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a day-count for a sheet and that there is no day-count for that sheet because there never was one.

---

By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen and about four of them had already noticed the word in the margin of that page and had not said anything about it.

At about half past ten the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud in the yard, in the ordinary voice, in front of all of them, and the man of about nineteen counted what she said and got eighty-five and put it on his own page in his own hand and did not read it back to himself.

"**There is a word on twelve of the figures on this page and it is the only word on the page, and I am going to say it out loud once so that about nineteen of you have heard it from a person and not off a sheet, and then I am going to stop saying it, because a word a person says four hundred times is a rule, and a rule this district makes costs nothing, and nobody can be made to keep it.**"

The word is unchecked. She said it once and she wrote it twelve times.

Nobody said a word for about four seconds, which was the longest anybody in the yard had gone without saying anything since before this district began keeping figures.

"That is a word on a figure," somebody said.

"It is a word on a figure and it is not a column and it is not a finding and it is not a rule, and I have not put it over any of the nine columns on that page and I am not going to."

"Whose twelve?"

She turned the page round on the boards so that about nine people could see it. The twelve were in the margin in her own hand with a space after each. The board stood at three hundred and eleven, the train on the siding at six hundred and twenty-seven, the days nobody has entered at three hundred and forty-one, and the days from the second of January at three hundred and two. The pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given was two hundred and twenty-one. The ninth of the nine printed nights was a hundred and ninety-four days back, the six households of the man at the far end of that yard a hundred and ninety-three, and the days on which nobody has put a line on a board two hundred and fourteen. The record set against four places four hundred miles off was in force a hundred and fifty-two days, the age of the figure about that figure was a hundred and fifty-two days, the mornings that table had stood were a hundred and seventeen, and the first day of the eighth month was ninety-one days past.

"There are five others on that page," the mender said, "and they have not got it."

"They have not got it because they did not cross anything," the clerk said. "The bid does not cross anything. The stay of the man at the foot of that wall does not cross anything. The rule that was said out loud in this yard on the tenth of this month does not cross anything. The figure on the second line of that book does not cross anything. And a thing four hundred miles off that prints at an interval does not cross anything either, and I am not going to put a word on a thing that has not got a boundary to cross."

"You have told us what has not got it as well," the man of fifty-six said.

"Somebody would have asked me inside a day," she said. "I would rather it was in the yard with the rest of it."

---

Then the mender said the second thing out loud that morning, from the end of that trestle table, with his left hand in its cloth and his right hand flat on the boards, and about nineteen people heard it, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy-seven and put it down, and about four people heard that count come out of his mouth and one of them said it was the same figure twice in a morning.

"**The third month is eight months back this morning and it was seven months back yesterday, and both of those are true, and the page that carries seven is not a wrong page and nobody in this yard is going to go back and alter it, and the reason the two of them do not agree is that a month went past, and I am not going to have that written down any smaller than it is.**"

The clerk of nineteen years wrote eight on that page. She wrote it in the place where the figure went, and she did not go back over anything, and the last four pages on that pile still carried three, four, five and seven where three, four, five and seven were what they were on the day each of them was written.

"Which of the two is it," the man of about nineteen said, "the seven or the eight?"

"Both," she said. "Yesterday's page says seven and it said seven yesterday. Today's page says eight and it says eight today. Nothing in this district is wrong and one of the two figures is a month out of date, and a figure that is a month out of date is not a figure anybody has to be ashamed of in this yard."

"Then somebody is going to read the two of them side by side one day," somebody said.

"They can," she said. "I am not going to go and make them agree. There is no figure on that page for what I would have to write if I did, and I am not going to invent one at seven in the morning and enter it as a finding."

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure carried across a month nobody counted is a figure about a list and not a day-count, and that a figure of a month said backwards in a yard changes on the first day of a month and the pages that carry the old figure are not altered. The pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is two hundred and twenty-one days and last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, and from this morning the third month is eight months back. The string *a clerk of a house* is on that page inside the figure's own name and is not a person standing in the yard.

---

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down to that table with his right hand in his coat and stood about nine feet off it and looked at the twelve words in that margin for a long time without saying anything.

He was not thinking about the twelve. He was thinking about a page with two lines in it on the end of the same table, under a different stone. He was thinking about how a word in a margin was going to look on a book a stranger could walk up to. He had not asked anybody to write a word anywhere, and somebody had written twelve of them that morning without being asked. That was the second time in twenty-two days a person in this district had done a thing out loud that nobody had asked for and had been entered for it, and the first time had been a woman of fifty-eight and a pair of buckets and nineteen years.

He had a reason for standing nine feet off and it was the same reason as yesterday and it is not a document. He did not say any of it and he was not going to be asked about it.

Then somebody at the near end of that table asked the clerk whether the word was going to stay on those twelve figures, and she put her hand flat on the page and did not look up, and said that she was not going to answer that this morning.

"You are going to have somebody ask you that every day for a week," the man of fifty-six said.

"Then I will answer it on a day I have thought about it," she said, "and not on the morning I wrote it, because the answer I would give this morning is the answer a person gives about ten minutes after a thing they have just done."

About four of them said that was fair and about four of them said that a clerk of nineteen years ought to be able to answer a question in four seconds, and she said that a clerk of nineteen years could answer a question in four seconds and that the four seconds were the easy part.

---

What the rest of that day was, in the order it happened, was the man in the ditch, the road keeper, the reeds, the cart, the buckets and the light. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch before nine, came up out of it about ten, took the four figures off the end of that table and got every one of them, and went back down that bank to the ditch, and that right hand of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the rest of that day. The road keeper came up that lane twice, at about eleven and again near four, had the four figures off that wall both times, and got them both times, and nobody asked him about the eleven miles and nobody said one word to him about a word in a margin. The reed cutter came up that bank at about half past four with his hook in his hand and was wet to the chest, and nobody said one word to him about the word, and he was not asked about it. Against the east wall stood the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up, with his hand-cart behind him and the tent rolled on the back of it, and he was given nothing and nobody asked him about it. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall from about ten with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and he was not asked to sit in the second chair and nobody in the yard proposed it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open sixty-one days and was not run today, that no person in the yard said one word about that figure or about closing it, that a bid nobody has run does not have a birthday anybody here is keeping, and that a figure nobody has said out loud has not been said. The first day of the eighth month is ninety-one days past, and a ladder is a figure about a ladder and not a figure about a man. The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and ninety-four days back, was named out loud in the yard once this afternoon, and was closed in nothing. The rule said out loud in the yard on the tenth of the tenth month is twenty-one days standing, and that is a figure about a rule and not a figure about how many days it has been kept. The figure on the second line of that lot book is sixteen days out of date and was not altered. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six, and no name went into it by anybody.

At about half past five the woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, carried them up the two goes, and read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way past. She said nothing to anybody. Nobody there said one word to her.

By the time the light had gone the man of fifty-six was at the end of that trestle table with his hand flat on those boards, and above the top mark there was clean wood that had not been cut into since before this district began keeping figures, people said good evening to each other on their way out and about four of them said good evening to nobody in particular.
