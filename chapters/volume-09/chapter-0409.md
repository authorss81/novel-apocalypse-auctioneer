# Chapter 409: Eight Days Off a List

The clerk of nineteen years put her page down open on the corner of that table at about eleven with the corner turned back, and the word in the margin of that line faced up, and the figure under it was a figure off a list, and it was eight days old.

It was the seventh of the eleventh month and the light had gone flat and the low wall at the south end of that yard was the only thing in it with a shadow. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

At about half past eight the man of fifty-six put his palm flat on those boards and read the four figures over the mender's shoulder and got all of them, and said the word with them and got it four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and seventeen, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and thirty-three days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and forty-seven, and three hundred and eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that its own age as a figure about the figure is a hundred and fifty-eight days. She entered nothing else about that sheet, and she entered no day-count for it, because there is none to enter.

---

Eight days is a short time. It is not long enough for a figure off a list to be a figure anybody in that yard has stopped doubting, because nobody in that yard has doubted it either, and not doubting a figure is not the same as having checked it.

About four people at the near end of that table read that line standing up in about four seconds, and about four of them said out loud that the figure was thirty-one, and about four other people said out loud that it was a figure off a list, and nobody said anything else about it, and that went on for about ten minutes.

"It has been there eight days," somebody said.

"It has been there eight days and nobody has looked at it once, and that is not a finding about the figure and it is not a finding about the eight days. It is a thing about a yard."

"A figure somebody has entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day," the clerk said, from the end of that table, with her hand flat on the corner of her own page. "I wrote that sentence on the eighteenth of the sixth month of this year. I wrote it again on the nineteenth of the tenth month. I wrote it again at two o'clock on the twenty-eighth of the tenth month, and I entered the finding about myself on the same page that day, and I am writing it now for the fourth time in five months and I am not going to pretend the fourth is different from the third."

"It is not different," somebody said.

"It is not different and it is not going to be, and that is what I have been putting in these entries for five months and nobody has ever read one of them out loud to me including me."

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing out loud at the end of that table with his right hand in his coat, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and six and put it down and read it back to himself in a low voice, which is a thing he does when he does not trust a figure.

"**I have been in this yard every morning this month and I have watched that figure sit on a page in the open for eight days without anybody putting a finger on it, and I know exactly what that is, because I put a figure in a book in the open on the thirteenth of the tenth month and there has been a word put in a margin beside twelve other figures and not one of them is that figure, and I want somebody in this yard to understand that those are two different problems and I have not got the answer to either of them.**"

Nobody read that back from the top. A thing read back by nobody binds nobody, and about four people said that out loud at the time and about four of them did not, and a man who has said a thing out loud in a yard and has not had it read back has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and the record about it says not read back.

---

At about half past ten the man of fifty-six asked the clerk of nineteen years one question, and about four people at the near end of that table turned round to hear it.

"Where did that figure come from."

"Not off a board."

"I know that it did not come off a board. I have been reading boards for six months. I am asking you where it came from, and nobody has asked you that in eight days and I am asking it now because a figure about a list is the only one on that page and I have read the other twelve every morning and I have never once thought about that one, and I do not think that is a thing about me."

"You want me to say it out loud."

"I want to know whether you can say it out loud without it turning into a rule."

She said it out loud. It came out of a list in a room four hundred yards up that bank, and the list has been in that room for nineteen years, that nobody in this district has read the list and nobody in this district owns it, and the figure is on the page because there was a line to fill and she filled it, and because there was a day to enter and she entered it, and that is the whole of how it was got.

"Then I could not do it," the man of fifty-six said, when she had finished.

"None of us could do it," the mender said, from the end of that table. "You have just asked a clerk to stand in a yard and describe a thing a man can only do in a room, and that is not a rule anybody can keep, and the price of it is that nobody can be made to do it, and the figure on that page is eight days old and it is not going to be checked by either of those."

"It is not going to be checked by anybody," the clerk said. "I have known that since the moment I wrote it down and I wrote it down anyway, and that is the fourth sentence in five months and I am not going to enter a fifth one about it."

---

At about two the man of about nineteen wrote that figure on his own page, which is the first figure off a list he has ever put there, and he put it at the bottom under a line with a space after it, and then he read the whole page back to himself.

Then he wrote it a second time, underneath, to see whether he got the same figure.

He did not. The first one was a figure and the second one was a figure and they were the same figure and he could not tell which of the two he had copied and which of the two he had got, and about four people at the near end of that table watched a boy of about nineteen do that and about four of them said nothing at all.

"That is the first figure on that page I cannot get twice," he said.

"You have not got the other four twice either," somebody said. "You have got them four times."

"I know which four I have got four times," he said, "and I have got the other one twice and both of those were off the page and not off a board, and there is a difference and I cannot write the difference down."

At about four he came down to the end of that table, the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, and stood about nine feet off it. He looked at the twelve words in that margin, and then at the one line under them that had a figure on it and nothing behind it. He said nothing out loud. About four people said that a man standing nine feet off a page in the open is not a reading and is not a finding.

At the far end of that yard the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally, and has said of himself that he is fifty-one, was still standing there with a flat book under his left arm. The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was still a few feet off him with her arms folded. About four people said out loud that a day a person spends standing at the end of a yard is a day, and that a day somebody spent is not a thing this district has and has never been entered as one. Nobody at the far end of that yard said one word.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure which is not a day-count and is not on a board is a figure somebody wrote down once, that a figure which is written twice on a page can be checked by whoever is holding the page and by nobody else, and that a page a clerk keeps in her coat is not a page under a stone, and that the two of those are different instruments and neither of them is a second of anything.

What was left of that day was the man in the ditch, the road keeper, the tables-up man against the east wall, the man of about sixty-four at the foot of the low wall, and the light going, in that order, and nothing in it was a decision anybody made. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch before nine, came up out of it about ten, got the four figures off the end of that table and had all of them, and went back down the bank, and that right hand of his stayed below the level of that shoulder the whole of that day. The road keeper came up that lane at about eleven and again at about four and had all four off that wall both times, and nobody asked him about the eleven miles and nobody asked him about the figure off the list. Against the east wall the man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them stood with his cart and the tent rolled on the back of it, and he was given nothing, and nobody asked him about the tent, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the June. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall from about ten with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody in that yard offered him anything this afternoon, not a chair, not a page, not a figure, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open sixty-seven days and was not run today. The first day of the eighth month is ninety-seven days past, and that is the whole of what anybody in that yard can say about it. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred days back and is open and was closed in nothing. The rule said out loud in that yard on the tenth of the tenth month is twenty-seven days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is twenty-two days out of date and was not altered. There is a man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds who knows the day it stopped and was not asked on any day of this month, and the record about the not asking says not asked. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, the count is still five, and a day somebody spent is not a thing this district has and is not a toll.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and she read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, people were standing a few feet from her and at the time they looked at the boards, and she said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her.

Nothing in that yard changed on purpose between ten in the morning and the light going, and about four people said out loud on their way out that a day on which nothing changed on purpose is a finding and is not a habit, and one of them said he did not believe it and about four of them said they did not either, and both of those went out of that yard at the same time.
