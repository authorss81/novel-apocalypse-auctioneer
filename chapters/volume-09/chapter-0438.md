# Chapter 438: The Cloth Came Off

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing took the cloth off his left hand on the fifth of the twelfth month at about eleven, and about nine people were close enough to see it happen, and not one of them said one word about what it was, and the hand at the end of it did not close.

It was the fifth of the twelfth month and the light came up hard and clean off the south end of that ground. By ten there were about nineteen people in the ground of Lot Seventeen.

At that wall the man of fifty-six read the four figures with his palm down on the boards, got all of them, said the word with them, and got the word four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and forty-six, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and sixty-two days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and seventy-six, and three hundred and thirty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and eighty-seven days, and entered that there is no day-count for the sheet.

---

He did it with his right hand. He got the cloth off the left one at about eleven in about four seconds, and he folded it once and put it into the inside pocket of his coat with his right hand, and the left hand was in the air for about as long as it takes the man of fifty-six to read four figures and then it was at his side.

The hand opened about halfway and stopped. It did not close on itself. He held it in front of himself and looked at it the way a man looks at a thing he has been carrying, and then he put it down at his side and left it there.

About four people at that table said afterwards that they had expected something else, and about four of them said they had expected exactly that, and two people said that they had no idea what they had expected and that it had not mattered.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said one thing out loud from the east wall and nobody had asked him anything.

"Six months."

"Six months," said the man who mends fencing. "Since the eleventh of the June. And I am not going to say anything else about it, and if one of you stands there and waits for me to, you are going to be stood there for about an hour and I will not say it."

Nobody stood there. About nine people went on doing what they had been doing and about nine people went on not doing what they had been doing, and the two of those are the same number of people and are not added together.

---

A voice at the near end of that table asked him a question at about half past eleven and he said no in about four seconds, and then he gave a reason, and about nine people heard the reason and about nine did not.

"Are you going to be all right."

"I am not going to be asked that."

"You have been asked nothing since the nineteenth of the tenth month and you have just said that you will not be asked it."

"I said I would not be asked what I am going to do with my hand. That is what I said. You have asked me a different question and you have not noticed, and about four of you in this yard do that every day, and the two words are two questions." He did not raise his voice. "I am not all right and I am not not all right. The hand does not close. I have known that since the eleventh of the June and I have known it every morning since and I have got a fence up in about four hours a day with it."

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was asked a question there and did not answer it, and entered that a man said a thing out loud, and entered the thing, and entered that a hand has not closed since the eleventh of the June and that a hand is not an instrument this district has made, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of fifty-eight said one thing out loud at about twelve and she said it to that ground and not to him, and a few people heard it and nine did not.

"I have carried water to eleven houses for nineteen years and I have a hand that has never been what it was on the day I started, and I have never once said that out loud to anybody, and I am not saying it now to anybody in particular." She said good afternoon to a man at the near end of that table and went up the bank with two empty buckets. Nobody said one word to her and nobody asked her anything and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat and looked at the mender's left hand at his side for about a minute and said nothing at all about it.

"You are going to be asked about it about four times a day for about a month," he said, "and you are going to be asked it by people who are not asking you."

"I know."

"I am telling you so that you know that I know, and I am not going to stand here and watch a yard do it to a man." He did not move the hand that was in his coat.

"I have a hand in this coat and it has not closed properly since the tenth of the eighth month. I have not taken it out in the open in front of anybody since the fifteenth of the eleventh, when I put it flat on those boards for about four minutes and it did not close. The only person who saw that was a boy of about nineteen, and he put nothing on his own page, and he has been asked about it four times this month and he has not written it down." He looked at the ground. "I am not going to do that. I have thought about it for two days and I have worked out that I am not going to, and that is not a figure and it is not a decision anybody has made for me."

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing and entered no figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked. Two people at that table said afterwards that two men in a yard had compared a hand and had not made anything of it, and that neither of them had said the word out loud, and that the clerk had written down that a hand is not an instrument and had written nothing else about either of them.

---

At about half past one the man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of that bank and got the four figures at that end of the table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder, and he went and stood at the end of the table about a foot from the man whose cloth was off, and neither of them said anything, and about nine people watched two men stand a foot apart and say nothing.

At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank and stood at that end of the table for about four seconds, and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him, and he was not asked for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches with one arm, and nobody said a word.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him a hand and nobody offered him a cloth, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went past him at about four and said good afternoon and the man of about sixty-four said good afternoon back, and neither of them said anything else, and four people at that table watched that happen.

Then a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about ten past four and said one thing out loud, and it was not to the man whose cloth was off.

"I have been watching a cloth come off a hand for two months and I worked out in the first month that it was not coming off and then it did, and I want to say one thing about it and then I am going up the bank with eleven buckets." She did not stop. "Nobody has thanked him. I have been waiting two months for about four of you to thank him and none of you have, and I am not going to either, and I would like it noticed that the reason none of you have is that you worked out in the second month that thanking him would be a rule, and the rule would be true on the day somebody kept it and false on the day somebody did not, and you are all frightened of a sentence that could turn out to be a habit."

Nobody said anything. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk may not enter that a hand is better than it was, and did not enter it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the boy of about nineteen asked one question out loud, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing answered it, and the boy of about nineteen counted the answer and got fifty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and four people heard the number.

"Will it close."

"No."

"Have you asked somebody who knows."

"There is nobody in this district who knows that and I have not been told there is anybody, and I have not asked, and I am not going to be the man who goes round this yard asking a person to be told about his own hand." He did not raise his voice. "If somebody comes and tells me then I will hear it. That is the whole of what I have got and I have had it since the eleventh of the June."

---

She started that page a different way that afternoon, at about ten past four, with the pencil held above it and not touching it for about four seconds first. What she wrote was a list of what had been said and not answered, and she numbered the lines, and she left the first line of the page for the thing she was not going to enter and left it there empty, and about four people at that table noticed an empty first line and one of them said out loud that a clerk who leaves the first line empty has told you more than a clerk who fills it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open ninety-six days and was not run this day and that nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. She entered that a cloth was taken off a hand at about eleven in that yard and that the hand did not close, and that she was asked by somebody whether the man was going to be all right and that she entered no answer to it because she was not asked a question, and that a clerk may not enter that a hand is better than it was, and she did not enter that, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and twenty-six days past, and a ladder is spent as a ladder. The rule said out loud in that ground on the tenth of the tenth month is fifty-six days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is fifty-one days out of date, was not altered, and has nothing written beside it. The figure entered off a list on the thirtieth of the tenth month is thirty-seven days old. The word unchecked is in the margin of her own page and is over that figure and over nothing else. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and it is the one of the five that this morning somebody would most have liked to pay and did not.

The two empty buckets went down that bank at about half past five and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that book standing up on her way past, said nothing to anybody, and was not said one word to by anybody there. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures for the last time that day and got all four of them.

Into the inside pocket of that coat went the cloth at about eleven this morning, and it has been in a pocket rather than round a hand ever since, and the man whose it is went up that bank at about half past five with his left hand open at his side and the fingers of it not shut, and a few people watched him go and one of them said out loud, after he had gone, that it was not better, and nobody disagreed with her and nobody said that it was worse.
