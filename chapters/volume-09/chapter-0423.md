# Chapter 423: The Price Out Loud

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was asked at about a quarter to ten to say the price of a rule out loud a fourth time, and he said it. A clerk of nineteen years wrote down that he had said it and wrote down nothing else, and that is the whole of what happened at that end of the table for about a minute and a half.

It was the twenty-first of the eleventh month and the light came up flat off that bank and the ground was still soft from two days of it. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

The man of fifty-six said the four figures out loud from that wall with his hand flat on the boards and got all of them, and said the word with them, and got the word four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and thirty-one, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and forty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and sixty-one, and three hundred and twenty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it did not move. She entered the age of it as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and seventy-two days. A figure about a figure is not a day-count for a sheet and no figure of that kind has ever been printed in this district.

---

The rule was said out loud here on the tenth of the tenth month and it was ninety-five words long, and its price was seventy-two, and neither of them is on a sheet. It has stood forty-one days. It was not kept for four days after it was said, and a clerk entered that too, and it was said again on the twenty-second of that month at ninety-five words and seventy-two, and nothing has been done about it since.

"You have not said the price since the eighteenth," said the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, from about nine feet off, without turning round. She had been standing there for about four minutes and had not stopped at the table.

"I said no on the eighteenth," the mender said, "and I asked the clerk to enter that I did not say it, and I said out loud that not saying it was the point and not the omission."

"That was a fortnight ago and I am asking you now and I am not the yard." She turned round. "There are nine people here and four of them have never heard you say it and the other five have heard you not say it, and between those two things this yard has arrived at a place where the price of a rule is a thing that is said by nobody, which is a different thing from a thing that is said and not obeyed, and I do not know which one of the two is worse and I am not going to guess in front of nine people."

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put his right hand flat on the boards, and his left hand was in the cloth, and he looked at the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale for about as long as it takes to say a number twice.

Then he said it, in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard all of it, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got sixty-seven and put it down on his own page and read it back to himself about four minutes afterwards.

"**A rule that costs nothing is a rule nobody can be made to keep, and that is the whole of the price, and I am not going to say it a fifth time, and if any of you want to know what to do about that then I am the wrong man to ask, because I have not worked it out and I have had six months.**"

Nobody read it back from the top. The clerk of nineteen years wrote one line and shut her page, and about nine people who could see the page said that one line was all that had been written.

"That is one line," said the man who puts tables up, from the east wall.

"That is one line," she said, "and it says that a man said a thing out loud, and it does not say what the thing was. I am not going to say what the thing was, because he told me in the same breath that he was not going to say it again. And if I put it on a page then a man in five weeks can be shown a page and told that a clerk took it down while he was standing here, and that is how this yard got a man at that table in June."

Nobody said one word about the man at that table in June, and the record about the not saying says not said, and the mouth is not a page and is not a document and is not an instrument this district has built.

"You are both wrong about the same thing," said the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds, from about eleven feet off, where he had come up the bank for the first time in about eleven minutes, and three people turned round. "A price a clerk writes down is a price the yard can do what it likes with. A price said out loud in a yard is a thing about nineteen people. You have spent six months getting that the wrong way round and neither of you has noticed, because you have both been trying to keep it."

"I have not been trying to keep anything," the mender said. "I have been refusing to be the man who says it a fifth time."

"That is trying to keep something," the reed-cutter said, and went back down the bank, and nobody said anything about it, and he was not asked anything on the way down.

---

At about half past eleven the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale went up that bank. She did not stop at the table on the way, and a man said out loud that her four empty houses have not been offered and are not going to be offered before the first day of the season after this one, on her own statement. She said from about eleven feet up the bank that somebody had been keeping her figure and it was not his to keep.

At about twelve the clerk of nineteen years wrote the not-asking down in the same words she has written it in every day of this month. The man of about nineteen read what she had written over her shoulder and said that it was the same sentence, and that the only thing that had changed in it since the ninth of this month was one word. She said he was wrong, and that nothing had changed in it at all since the first of this month, and he said the first of this month was twenty days back and that a sentence written the same way twenty days running is not the same sentence.

"You want to know what the twenty-fourth one is," she said. "It is a habit. I have written it twenty-four times and it is a habit and it is a finding about me and not about him, and I have said that out loud in this yard before and I am not going to say it again this month."

He said he was not asking her what the twenty-fourth one was. He said he was asking whether she had noticed that a sentence repeated is a sentence that has stopped carrying anything, and she said yes, and said that was the second time this month somebody had noticed something about it and that she had entered both.

At about half past twelve the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and took up his nine feet off the end of that table and said nothing out loud. A voice at the near end of that table said that this was the fourth morning running he had come down to it and said nothing, and another voice said that a man who says nothing four mornings running has said the same thing four mornings running.

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch to his thigh from before nine until about four, got the four figures at the end of that table at about ten, and said the word with them four times. That right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day, and nobody said one word to him about it. The road keeper came up that lane twice and got the four figures both times, and was not asked about the eleven miles.

At about two the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came up the bank and stopped at that end of the table and asked the boy of about nineteen a question out loud, and the boy counted what he said and got forty-two and read it back to himself twice.

"**If a thing is said in this yard and nobody reads it back from the top, and a second person says it back four days later and it is not the same, which of the two is the thing that was said?**"

The boy of about nineteen did not answer for about as long as it takes the man of fifty-six to read four figures. About nine people stood where they were and let him not answer.

"They are both the thing that was said," he said, "and one of them is what happened and one of them is what the yard has got, and this district has been putting those two in the same column since the first of this month, and about four of you have worked that out and none of you has said it out loud because saying it out loud means somebody has to say which of the two goes in the page."

Nobody answered him. The clerk of nineteen years wrote nothing at all, and a voice at the end of that table said she had never written nothing so fast in her life, and she said that was not a sentence anybody needed to write down.

A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall from about ten with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands. Nobody offered him a hook, a page, a figure or a hand, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open eighty-one days and was not run today, and that no person there proposed anything about closing it. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and eleven days past. The rule said out loud in that yard on the tenth of the tenth month is forty-one days standing. A man said the price of that rule out loud at about a quarter to ten this morning in about four seconds, and a clerk entered that he said it and entered no figure, and the reason she gave out loud for entering no figure is at the end of that table and not on a page. The figure on the second line of that lot book is thirty-six days out of date, was not altered, and has nothing written beside it. The word unchecked is in the margin of a clerk's page and is over nothing and is not over any of the nine columns. The line under those twelve words is twenty-two days old. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and no sixth is proposed.

At about half past five two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her.

Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures at about half past five and got all four. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about four with the bundle under his arm, stopped at the end of that table, and was not asked for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody standing there could have said at half past five what the price of that rule had done to it, and the price had been said out loud in about four seconds, and there is a line on a clerk's page that says a man said a thing, and the thing is not on the page.
