# Chapter 439: A Hand That Is Not Better

About four people brought something to the end of that trestle table on the sixth of the twelfth month to put in the left hand of a man whose left hand has not closed since the eleventh of the June, and about four of them put it down and took it away again, and nobody at that table put anything in it.

It was the sixth of the twelfth month and the wind had come back round to the north-west in the night and was coming down that bank in gusts. By ten there were about nineteen people in the ground of Lot Seventeen.

He read the four figures off that wall with the flat of his hand against the wood, got all of them, said the word with them, and got the word four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and forty-seven, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and sixty-three days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and seventy-seven, and three hundred and thirty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and eighty-eight days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and there is no day-count for the sheet.

---

The first one was a mug of water and it went down at about eleven and stayed there for about four minutes and then went back up the bank with the woman who had carried it. The second was a piece of fence, about nine feet of it, and the man who mends fencing said out loud that he had cut that himself in the spring and put it up, and that a man does not mend a fence for a man, and the fence went back down the bank. The third was a chair, and it was the second chair, and somebody said out loud that there were two of them at the end of that table and that neither of them had been sat in this month.

The clerk of nineteen years entered that three things were brought to that table and that none of them was left there, and entered that one of the three was a chair and that a chair is one of the five things this district has and that a sixth was refused on the day the second one went down, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

"You are not a post," somebody said.

He said no in about four seconds.

"You are not a reader and you are not a second of anything and nobody is going to put a word over you on a page, and I have been waiting a day and a night to say that to somebody."

"You have been waiting a day and a night to say it to somebody and you have said it to me, and I am the wrong person for it, and I am saying that as a person and not as the man who mends fencing." He did not raise his voice. "I have been in this yard two months with a cloth round that hand and about four of you have not once looked at it, and yesterday I took it off and about four of you have looked at it every minute since and not one of you has asked me a question about it. I would like that recorded by somebody and I would like it recorded that I am not asking for anything."

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there, and entered the thing, and entered that the thing is not a request, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said one more thing out loud to the boy of about nineteen and not to the table, and a few people heard it and the rest of that ground did not, and it was about a line under twelve words in her own margin.

"It is thirty-eight days old. I have got a word over a figure in that margin and the figure under it was checked on the last day of the month it describes and the two of them agreed, and I have not taken the word off, and I am not going to, and nobody has asked me to and I am not telling you why I have not taken it off." She shut the page. "There is a man in this yard who heard a name two days ago and has written one line and put nothing under it, and I have heard about four people work out that he is the wrong person to ask, and he is not the wrong person to ask. He is the only other person in this district who is carrying something she has not written down, and I am not going to put the two of those together in a page while either of us is standing in this yard."

The boy of about nineteen said one thing back and it was about four seconds long.

"You have told me which of the two you are."

"I have told you that I am not going to say which of the two I am." She did not look at him. "That is not the same sentence and you are about to be asked about the difference by somebody who wants an instrument, and I am telling you now so that you know I knew."

---

At about half past eleven a man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank and stood a foot from him and said one thing out loud and about nine people heard it.

"I have a figure in a book in the open that I put there with my mouth and that has been wrong for fifty-one days, and you have a hand that has not closed since the eleventh of the June." He did not look at his own hand. "I have not said anything to you about either of those and I am not going to, and I want it understood that the reason I am not is not that I think it would be kind. It is that a man who comes up here every morning and says a thing about somebody else is a man who has made the other one into a thing he is talking about, and I have done that to about four people this month and I would like to stop."

The man who mends fencing said nothing to that. Some people at that table said afterwards that two men who have each got a thing wrong in the open stood a foot apart there and neither of them said a word about the other's, and that about four of the rest of that table wanted them to.

The clerk of nineteen years entered that two men said things out loud there and entered both of the things, and entered that a hand is not a figure and a figure is not a hand, and that the two of those are not added together, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and took up the nine feet with his right hand in his coat and about nine people at that table were watching the mender's left hand and not him, and he said one thing about that and about nine people stopped watching it.

"You have all been watching a hand since yesterday and none of you has watched a page and the reason is that a hand is a thing you can see and a page is a thing you have to be talked into." He did not move his own hand. "A hand is not an instrument this district has built. There is no list it goes on and there is no column over it and there is not going to be one, and if any of you want to make it the twelfth thing I would like one of you to say so out loud now while I am standing here and not be surprised afterwards."

Nobody said so. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing and entered no figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then he said one more thing, and it was shorter, and a few people heard it and nine did not.

"Tomorrow there is going to be a hand on that book and I am not going to tell anybody why and I am not going to stop it. I have known since about four yesterday afternoon. I am saying it here so that nobody can say afterwards that it was done in a corner."

Nobody wrote it down. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing and entered that it carried no date, and the record about the not asking says not asked. Two people at that table said afterwards that he had told that yard a day early on purpose, and that a few other people had said that was a rule in all but the name of it, and that he had said no at the time and that nobody had believed him.

---

At about half past one the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said one thing out loud from the east wall and it was about a woman.

"She read a line back on the eleventh of the tenth month. That was the only time. She has said since that she is not going to read a thing back again and nobody has asked her to, and I have not asked her to, and I am not going to ask her to, and I would like the yard to hear me say that about a woman who has not been asked instead of about a man whose cloth came off yesterday, because the man will get about four questions a day for a month and she will get none and she is the one who has already had hers."

Nobody asked her. She came down that bank at about half past two with two empty buckets, filled them, said good afternoon to two people, and went back up it, and nobody at that table said one word to her, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman of fifty-eight was not asked to read a thing back and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane and went on up it, and the near wheel was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and nine people watched him and nobody said a word, and that is the eleventh visit and the wheel has not been written down on any of them. He said one thing on the way past and it was to nobody.

"I said my piece yesterday and nobody asked me anything about it, and about four of you have been not asking me about it for a day, and I would like the yard to know that a man can tell you a thing and go away and have it stay told." He went on up the lane. "That is all. The wheel is still dragging."

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him a hand and nobody said the word hand at him at all today, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open ninety-seven days and was not run this day and that nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. She entered that three things were brought to that table and that none of them was left there, and that a hand is not an instrument and is not a post and is not a column and is not a page, and that a clerk may not enter that a hand is better than it was, and she did not enter that, and she did not enter that it was worse either. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and twenty-seven days past. The rule said out loud in that yard on the tenth of the tenth month is fifty-seven days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is fifty-two days out of date, was not altered, and has nothing written beside it. The figure entered off a list on the thirtieth of the tenth month is thirty-eight days old. The word unchecked is in the margin of her own page and is over that figure and over nothing else. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them, and went back up it with them full, and read the three lines in that book standing up on her way past, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures for the last time that day and got all four of them.

Some people at that table had brought something to that end this morning and some of them had taken it away again, and the number of things that ended up in that hand is not a figure and is not on anybody's page, and the boy of about nineteen said out loud at about half past four that he had counted them and was not going to write the number down, and the clerk of nineteen years said that was correct, and the two of them did not say anything else about it.
