# Chapter 406: The Mouth It Came Out Of

The loam man came the whole way across that yard at about ten instead of going to the trough, with the water to his thigh, and stopped at the end of that trestle table and read the four figures and got all four, and stood there instead of going back down the bank.

It was the fourth of the eleventh month, and the light was flat and the ditch had not gone down since the middle of the last week. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

The man of fifty-six had his hand flat on those boards before the mender got there and read the four figures out loud over his shoulder and got all four, and then he said the word with them four times and got the word four times, and the word was the part of the morning that was new.

"**The board carries three hundred and fourteen, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and thirty days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and forty-four, and three hundred and five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and it has not moved on one of the days of this month, and that it is a hundred and fifty-five days old as a figure about the figure. A clerk of nineteen years entered it and entered that the sheet's own tenure on that post is not a figure this district has and that she is not inventing one at half past eight in the morning.

---

Nobody asked the loam man what he was doing standing there. About four people said out loud that a man who has been in and out of a ditch every morning for six weeks is not a man standing at a table to be asked a question, other people said that was the wrong reason and gave the right one, which was that he had read the four figures from the wrong end of the yard. A man reads them differently depending on which side of the yard he has come from.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank at about half past ten and stopped at the low wall and looked down at the standing water, and the loam man was about nine feet below him at the bottom of the ditch with his hook in his hand.

Then the man in the coat said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and the man of about nineteen counted it and got ninety-seven and put it down on his own page and did not read it back.

"**Nobody has asked that man a single question about that figure and nobody is going to today, and I am going to describe out loud how it was got anyway, because he is nine feet from me with his hand in the water and he can hear every word of it and he has not been asked whether he wants it said or not, and I am saying it because the whole of how that figure was arrived at was a man standing in a yard looking at a thing, and nobody has written that down anywhere.**"

Then he said it, in the ordinary voice, with a reason, and this is the whole of it.

"**A man of about thirty-four dug loam in a ditch three feet deep behind a building with two doors, and he stood on the bank on the thirteenth of the tenth month and looked at the water, and he said a figure out loud, and a clerk wrote it on the second line of a book on the end of a table, and the figure has been out of date since the fifteenth of the tenth month and nobody wrote down the day it stopped, and the man who is the only person in this district who knows that day is a man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch, and he is not here, and he has not been asked, and I am not going to ask him.**"

About four people said out loud that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against had just described how a figure of somebody else's mouth got into a book in the open in front of about nineteen people, and the man of fifty-six said, from the wall, that he had heard both of those, and that a figure being true for a while and then not being true is not a figure anybody got wrong, and that it is a figure that ran out.

At the bottom of that ditch the man of about thirty-four who digs loam stood with the water round his knees and did not say one word. He did not say the figure. He did not say the day. He did not say that the man in the coat was right, and he did not say that the man in the coat was wrong. About nine people in the yard watched a man stand in water and not answer a description of his own mouth, and not one of them said anything to him about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then he said one thing, which was about four seconds long, and the man of about nineteen counted it and got five.

"**You did not ask me.**"

"No," the man in the coat said. "I am not going to."

"You have said that before."

"I have said that every day this month and I am not going to stop saying it on the day it is useful to me."

The loam man made a sound that was not a word and went back to the hook, and about four people said that was the first thing anybody had said in the yard all month that could not be written on a page, and that one of them said that it could not be written because there was no word for it and not because there was a rule.

Then two of the people at the near end of that table started walking toward the low wall together, and the mender got in front of them without saying anything at all and stood there, and they stopped, and about nine people in the yard watched a man of about thirty-four with his left hand in a cloth stand between two people and a ditch.

"Nobody is going to ask him for it," the mender said.

"We were going to ask him what he would say now."

"You were going to ask him what he would say now, and that is asking him, and if you ask him then he is a man who has been asked and he either answers a figure about a ditch out loud or he refuses, and if he refuses then a man has been in a yard and counted a refusal, and that is the second thing in a week this district has built and I am not having it in a ditch with a man standing in water." He took his forearm off the wall. "He has not been asked. That is the whole of what is true about that figure and it is true this afternoon and it will be true on the fifth of this month and it is the only reason the figure is still a figure about a ditch instead of a figure about a man."

The two of them went back to the near end of the table and about four people said that the mender had not said one word about the figure and had said about two hundred words about not asking for it, and the mender said that was the cheapest thing he had done all week and he was not going to be thanked for it.

---

The mender named the ninth of the nine printed nights out loud at about one, with a reason, and about nineteen people heard it.

"**It is a hundred and ninety-seven days back and it is open, and I am going to name it once today because a man is standing in a ditch describing the whole of how a figure was got and the district has said nothing about a thing that has been open for a hundred and ninety-seven days, and naming it is not closing it, and nobody in this yard is going to close it today, and I have not touched it.**"

Then about four people said the thing that usually gets said about that, which is that a plank does not keep nights and a figure does not close one, and the mender said both of those were true and neither of them was new.

At about two the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the far end of that yard with her arms folded for about half an hour, and her four empty houses are four figures about four houses and are not a figure about her, and nobody said one word about them to her, and she was not asked and has not been asked.

At about half past three the man of about nineteen put the figure of the second line on his own page. It is the only figure of a lot he has ever put there, and he put it at the bottom under a line. About four people said that was a thing to do with a figure that is out of date, and one of them said it was also a thing to do with a figure that is right. The boy said it was the same act either way, and that was the difference, and nobody argued with him.

Then he asked the clerk one question, which was why the word was not on that one as well. She said it was because that figure had not crossed anything. He said the figure had been wrong for nineteen days, and that was not nothing crossing. She said it was a thing that had happened to a figure and not a boundary the figure had gone over. A word on a figure is a word about where the figure came from, and that figure came from a man on a bank, and that it is going to come from that same man tomorrow whatever word is on it.

"That is the first thing anybody has said today that sounds like a rule," somebody said.

"It is not a rule and I am not going to be able to say it again in the same words tomorrow and you are not going to get it on anything," she said.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open sixty-four days and was not run today, and that no person in the yard proposed anything about closing it. The first day of the eighth month is ninety-four days past, and a ladder is spent as a ladder, and a ladder is a figure about a ladder. The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and ninety-seven days back and is open and was closed in nothing. The rule said out loud in the yard on the tenth of the tenth month is twenty-four days standing and is not a figure about how many days it has been kept. The figure on the second line of that lot book is nineteen days out of date and was not altered. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid, and the count is still five. A clerk is not going to say that a man whose trade is over is a failure and is not going to say that he is not one either, and the count of things this district does not have did not move today.

The rest of that day went ordinary and the loam man was in that ditch until the light went, which was about an hour and a half later than the hour he usually comes out at, and his right hand did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in that day, and he did not explain that to anybody and nobody asked him about it. The road keeper came up that lane at about four, read the four figures off that wall, got all four, said the word with them, and went on up without stopping at the low wall. The reed cutter was in that ditch until about half past four and came up the bank wet to the chest and said good evening to nobody in particular. The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was against the east wall with his cart, the tent still rolled on the back of it, and he was given nothing.

A man described out loud, in a yard in daylight, in front of about nineteen people, how a figure had got into a book in the open, and the man the figure belonged to was nine feet away in standing water and was not asked about it. A clerk of nineteen years entered all of that, and entered that not being asked is not a refusal and cannot be counted either way and that the record about the not asking says not asked. Nineteen days is how long the figure on the second line of that lot book has been out of date, it has not been altered, there is nothing written beside it, and nobody in that yard was asked when it stopped being true. A clerk did not enter that anybody was waiting for it and did not enter that a thing which does not arrive has a face.

She was down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, drew them at the trough, carried them up the two goes, read the two lines in that book standing up on her way past, said nothing at all, and was not spoken to.

By the time the light had gone the man of fifty-six was at the end of that trestle table with his hand flat on those boards, and the water in that ditch behind the building with two doors was still standing, and nobody in the yard knew what was in it, and nobody had been asked, and nobody was going to be.
