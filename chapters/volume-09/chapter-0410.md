# Chapter 410: The Clean Wood Above the Top Mark

The man of fifty-six read his four figures at about half past nine and got all four, and at about ten the woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with two empty buckets, and she was the only person in the yard who did not look at those boards at all.

It was the eighth of the eleventh month and the light was clean and hard off that bank and the ground under the boards was dry for the first time in nine days. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

"**The board carries three hundred and eighteen, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and thirty-four days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and forty-eight, and three hundred and nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that its own age as a figure about the figure is a hundred and fifty-nine days, and that those two are the only figures about that sheet anybody in this district has, and that she entered the second of them and not the first and not a third.

---

At about eleven the man of about nineteen read the twelve out loud.

He read them off the margin of her page, going down them, in the ordinary voice, with the word at the end of each one, and he got all twelve, and he did not stop, and about four people said that the ninth one was where his voice went and about four of them said they had all heard it, and he read the tenth and the eleventh and the twelfth anyway.

The four on the wall behind him were the same four the man of fifty-six reads every morning, and the eight that were not on the wall were on that page and nowhere else, and a word on a figure is a figure about how the figure was got.

At about half past ten the road keeper came up that lane and read the four figures off that wall as he went past, got all four, and went on up without stopping at the low wall, and nobody in the yard asked him about the word and nobody asked him about the eleven miles. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up that bank at about ten with the water to his thigh, read the four figures at the end of that table, got all four, said the word four times, and went back down to the water, and that right arm of his stayed below the level of that shoulder all day, and nobody said one word to him about it.

Then the mender, from the end of that trestle table, said the other five out loud, in the ordinary voice, with a reason, and the man of about nineteen counted it and got one hundred and forty-six and put that down on his own page under a line.

"**There are five figures in this district that do not have that word on them and I am going to name all five so that about nineteen of you can see that they are not forgotten, and they are a bid nobody has run, a stay of a man at the foot of that wall who has been given nothing, a rule said out loud in this yard that costs nothing, a figure on the second line of that book that is out of date, and a thing that prints four hundred miles off at an interval, and none of the five of them has crossed a boundary that anybody in this yard has had to count, and that is why there is no word on them, and that is the whole reason and it is not a reason anybody is going to be satisfied with.**"

Then he said the sixth thing, which is about the one figure on that page that has the word on it and a figure that nobody can check.

"That one is nine days old, it came off a list in a room four hundred yards up that bank, nobody in this district owns the list and nobody has read it, and it is the only figure in this yard that carries a word saying how it was got and cannot be checked by anybody, and about four of you have been saying for a week that the word is the only checkable thing about those twelve figures, and I have not argued with you and I am not arguing with you now, and I am putting the two of them in the same yard this morning because that is where they have been for nine days."

Nobody read either of those back from the top. About four people said that a thing said out loud and not read back binds nobody whatever it is about, and the man of about nineteen said that out loud, and about four of them said that was the fourth time anybody in the yard had used the same sentence in a fortnight.

---

At about half past eleven he came down to the end of that table, the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, and put his right hand in his coat before he got there, and stood about nine feet off it, and about four people said that a man standing nine feet off a page in the open had done it every day this month and had not been asked to say it once.

He had come down that bank at about eleven with one thing in his head, and by about a quarter to twelve he had lost it. Two men at that table had just put seventeen figures in the same yard and had not put a single one of them in the wrong place. A man who has been standing nine feet off a page for twenty-two days waiting for somebody to get something wrong does not enjoy it when they get it right.

That is not a complaint and he did not make one. Nine feet was near enough to read a page and far enough that a man could say what he liked about it, and he had not told anybody that and was not going to, and about four people in the yard were watching him not say anything and about four of them were watching him not go closer, and neither of those is the same thing and he knew it.

At about one the man of about nineteen read the four figures off that wall in the ordinary way, without the word. About four people had asked him to, and he wanted to see whether he could still do it, and he got all four. Then he read the four again with the word and got the word four times. About nine people said out loud that a boy of about nineteen had just done the thing he did not read out loud any more on the third of this month. He said he was not going to make a habit of it.

Nobody made a habit of anything. The man of fifty-six read his four at about two and got all four, and at about three and got all four, and at about four and got all four, and the word four times each time, and at about half past four he stopped saying the word with them, and about four people noticed, and he said he had said it four times in a morning, and that was the whole of a day's worth for him and he would pick it up again in the morning.

"You have had a morning off it."

"I have had a morning off four figures. They are still on that wall and I have still got them and I will read them in the morning."

At about half past four the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane and did not come into that yard and stopped at the low wall, and nobody asked him anything, and he read nothing, and the near wheel on that cart was dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and set it down and got back on the seat, and about nine people watched that happen and not one of them said a word.

At the far end of that yard, which is the only part of it that is dry, the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally, and has said of himself that he is fifty-one, stood from about ten until about two with a flat book under his left arm. The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale stood about four feet off him with her arms folded. About four people said out loud that his six households have not moved for two hundred days, that there is a word in the margin of a page in the open beside that figure, and nobody has asked him for it and nobody is going to. She did not answer, and she was not asked, and her four empty houses are four figures about four houses and are not a figure about her and are not going to be offered again before the first day of the season after this one, on her own statement.

Then nothing happened for the rest of that day, which is the honest account of it.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch before nine, came up out of it about ten, read the four figures at the end of that table, got all four, and went back down the bank to the water, and that right arm of his stayed below that shoulder line all day, as it has for a month. The road keeper came up that lane at about eleven and again at about four, got all four both times, and nobody asked him about the eleven miles. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in that ditch until about half past four and came up the bank wet to the chest and was not asked about anything. The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was against the east wall with his hand-cart, the tent rolled on the back of it where it has been since the thirtieth of the June, and he was given nothing and nobody asked him about it. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall from about ten, with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands. He was not asked to sit in the second chair and nobody proposed it. He is in his hundred and ninth day in this district and has slept on a hundred and eight of the hundred and nine nights. The figure he folds his coat for is the night he is about to sleep, which has not happened, and it is a hundred and tenth.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open sixty-eight days and was not run today, and that no person in the yard proposed anything about closing it. The first day of the eighth month is ninety-eight days past and a ladder is spent as a ladder. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and one days back and is open, and no bell was pulled and no night was named. The rule said out loud in the yard on the tenth of the tenth month is twenty-eight days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is twenty-three days out of date and was not altered. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid and the count is still five. A clerk entered no day in any book today, and no figure in any book today, and did not enter that anybody was honoured and is not going to.

At about half past five the woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with the two buckets she had carried up, filled them at the trough, carried them up the two goes, and read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her.

The last man down that lane was the man of fifty-six. He read the four figures at about half past five and got all four, and then he stood with his hand flat on those boards for a while with his other hand under his coat at the wall, the way he has stood for nine mornings, and about four people said good evening to each other on their way out.

Tomorrow is the ninth of this month and the day after that is the tenth, and about four people said both of those out loud at the low wall on their way out and nobody gave a reason for saying them, and the clerk of nineteen years did not enter either of them.

By the time the light had gone there were about four inches of clean wood at the top of that board, above the mark he had cut that morning. Nobody in that district had ever had a reason to cut into it. There will be a mark cut in it tomorrow and the day after that and the day after that. Nobody standing in the yard at half past five in the evening could have said how many of them there would be, and that is not a figure and it is not a finding.
