# Chapter 404: A Word on a Figure

The man of fifty-six got his four figures off that wall at about half past nine and said the word with them, all four times, and about nine people in the yard heard a word come out of a man's mouth that they had only ever seen in a clerk's hand.

It was the second day of the eleventh month, and it was the second day of the twelve the clerk had marked. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about four of them had come in expecting to be asked something.

"**Three hundred and twelve, unchecked. Six hundred and twenty-eight, unchecked. Three hundred and forty-two, unchecked. Three hundred and three, unchecked.**"

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved on any day of this month. A clerk of nineteen years entered the figure, entered that it is a hundred and fifty-three days old as a figure about the figure, and entered that the sheet's own tenure on that post is not a figure this district has and is not going to be one.

That is not how he reads the wall. He has read the wall the ordinary way for six months and he got the ordinary way out of it this morning as well, and then he said the word four times afterwards, and about four people said that a man who has read a figure off a board every morning for six months ought to know that there was no word on that board.

"There is not," the man of fifty-six said. "It is in a clerk's hand in the margin of a page under a stone and I read it off that page, and the page is on this table and the wall is behind me, and I have got a memory for four figures and not for a page."

"You are going to take a word off a wall that is not on the wall."

He said the rest of it out loud then, with a reason, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and thirty and put it down without reading it back to himself, which is a thing he has not done for eleven days.

"**No. I have been asked about chalk this morning twice and I am not going to do it, and here is the reason and it is the last time I am going to give it. A figure on a wall is a thing a man cuts every morning and gets the same every morning. If I put a word in chalk on that wall then that word is a figure on that wall, and I have to get it right every morning for the rest of my life, and if I get it wrong one morning then the figure under it is a figure on a page nobody has checked, and I am not doing that to four figures that have been right every morning since the twenty-fourth of December.**"

"That is a very long speech for a man who says he is not going to say things again."

The man of fifty-six said that was the first thing he had said in the yard for six months that he had not said before, and about four of them said good, and about four of them said nothing.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall again at about ten and got all four, and read the four figures again at about four and got all four, and said the word four times each time, and by the last time of the day the word took him a second longer than the figure did.

---

At about eleven the man of about nineteen could not read her hand. He had been at the end of that table for a quarter of an hour with his finger going down the margin of that page and stopping at each of the twelve, and the first four of them he could get and after that he could not, and he said so out loud to about four people, which is a thing he has not done in six months.

"It is not a bad hand," the clerk said. "It is that you are used to figures and a figure is a thing with a shape and this is not."

"I can read everything else on that page."

"You can read the nine columns because I have never once written a word that was not already a heading somebody gave me. I wrote those twelve myself this morning at about half past seven and there was nobody standing here when I wrote them."

"So it is a word you made up."

"It is a word the yard made up itself and I did not make it up. Somebody said it in this yard on the nineteenth of this month and about nine of you heard it, and nobody wrote it down until this morning, and that is the only reason it is in my hand and not in yours."

The man of about nineteen said that a figure took him a second to write down and a word took him about four, and that he had been putting figures on that page all month and had not had to think once, and this morning he had to think on every one of them. About four people said that was the first thing anybody had said out loud about a word doing to a person what a figure does not do, and the clerk said she had entered that and had entered it as a fact about a boy and not as a finding.

---

At about half past one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down to the end of that table, put his right hand in his coat before he got there, and stood at the near end of it about nine feet off the page and read the twelve words in that margin, one at a time, going down them the way a man goes down a list of figures.

He was the only man in the yard who had put a figure in a book a stranger could walk up to and had watched it stop being true, and nobody had been able to do one single thing about it. This morning a clerk of nineteen years had written down, in a word, the whole of what he had been unable to say out loud for twenty-two days. She had done it at half past seven, before there was anybody in the yard, and nobody had asked her to, and he had not been there to see her do it.

He wanted to say something about that. He worked out about four sentences and took all four of them apart again standing nine feet off that table, and what he said in the end was not one of them.

"**I have been reading that page since half past seven from about nine feet off, and I have got twelve words off it, and I want it entered that I am not the one who is going to say what they are for, because I have got a figure in a book on this table that has been out of date for seventeen days and I have not been able to say one word about it in this yard, and a man who cannot say a word about one figure does not get to say a word about twelve.**"

Nobody read that back from the top either, and about four people said that a thing said out loud and not read back binds nobody whatever it is about, and the man in the coat said that he knew and had worked it out before he said it.

---

At about two somebody said the ordinary thing, which is that if a word is going to be read out loud with a figure every morning then the word is part of the figure and the district might as well rule a column with the word over it, and about four other people said that in about the same breath.

"No," the mender said, from the end of that trestle table, with his left hand in its cloth. "A word on a figure is a label and a label is not a measurement, and a column with a word over it is not a column of people, and a new column of that shape is not an empty column and is not the same column and is not added to the old ones. There are four columns of not-askings on that page and there have been four since before the first of the ninth and no fifth of that shape is going to be ruled by anybody in this yard this afternoon."

"You have said that three times this month," somebody said.

"I have said it three times this month and I will say it a fourth, and the reason I say it more than any other thing I say in this yard is that every one of the three times somebody thanked me for it, and a man who gets thanked for a thing stops knowing whether the thing is true."

Then the same man said the other thing, which is that if the district is going to put a figure on the length of a month and read it out loud every morning, then that is a rate, and a rate is a thing that is already true of you whether anybody says it or not, and there is not one in this district and there never has been one.

"You have said that nine times since the middle of the fourth month," somebody said.

"Nine times is nine days and it is not a figure about how many days it has stood. It is a figure about how many days somebody has been in a yard saying a thing out loud, and I am not going to be the one who turns that into a column."

---

At about half past three the man of fifty-six read one of the twelve out loud in the yard, all of it, the figure and the word, from the top of the number to the end of the word, and about four people said that was a reading.

It was not a reading of that lot. It was not a reading of anything in the yard, and about four people said so at the time, and nobody read it back from the top, and a thing read back by nobody binds nobody. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands where it stood on the twelfth of this month, and nobody advanced it this afternoon by standing at a table and saying a number out loud.

"I could read it back," the man of about nineteen said, and did not.

"You could," the man of fifty-six said, "and if you did it, then about four people in this yard would be bound by a word I made up at half past nine this morning, and none of them asked for it, and I am not doing it to you."

The rest of that day went ordinary. The road keeper came up that lane at about eleven and again at about four, read the four figures off that wall both times, got all four both times, and nobody in the yard asked him about the word or about the eleven miles. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch before nine and was still in it at about half past four, and his right hand did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point. The reed cutter came up that bank at about half past four and was wet to the chest. The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was against the east wall with his cart, and the tent was rolled on the back of it, and he was given nothing. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall from about ten with nothing in his hands, and he was not asked to sit in the second chair and nobody proposed it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a word was read out loud with four figures off a wall this morning by a man of fifty-six, and that it bound nobody, because no second person read it back from the top. The figure on the second line of that lot book has been out of date for seventeen days and was not altered, and not one person in the yard said a word about the water in that ditch. The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and ninety-five days back and is open. The bid has stood open sixty-two days and was not run today. The first day of the eighth month is ninety-two days past. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, the count is still five, and no sixth has been proposed.

She came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, carried them up the two goes, and read those two lines standing up on her way past without touching the book, and she said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her.
