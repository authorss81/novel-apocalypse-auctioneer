# Chapter 432: The Day Before A Day

A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about a quarter to ten with the day's flour on her hip and stopped in the middle of that ground and said the one thing that everybody near the trough had been thinking since about eight and had not said.

It was the thirtieth of the eleventh month and the sky was the colour of a wet road and the wind had not got up. By ten there were about nineteen people in the ground of Lot Seventeen.

With his hand flat on the boards the man of fifty-six got the four figures off them before half past ten, and said the word with each of them, and got the word four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and forty, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and fifty-six days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and seventy, and three hundred and thirty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and eighty-one days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not about the sheet.

---

The woman put her hip against that low wall and said it to nobody in particular and to the nine people standing near enough to hear it.

"Tomorrow is the last day of this month, and some of us have been waiting all morning for somebody to say what happens on it, and I have worked out that nobody is going to, and I would like to stop waiting." Nobody answered her. "I do not want to be told what is going to happen. I want to know whether I am going to be standing in this yard, so that I can carry the right number of buckets, and I have asked nobody and I am asking you."

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the far end of that table with his left hand in its cloth and he said the thing that four people had come down that bank to hear, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got thirty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and four people heard the number come out of his mouth.

"**Nobody has told you and nobody is going to tell you and you have not been refused, because there is nothing on any page in this district that says what the last day of a month is for.**"

Two people said out loud that they had not expected that, and one of them said he had come down that bank at about half past nine for the opposite reason and would go back up it now. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there, and entered the thing, and entered that it was not a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing back and it was to him and not to anybody else. "That is the first true thing anybody has said to me this month, and you are the only person in this yard who has said one, and I am not going to thank you for it because a person who is thanked for a thing stops being an argument and becomes a thing somebody said." She took her hip off the wall. "I will carry eleven either way. Eleven is what I have carried on this bank for nineteen years and it has not changed on the first of a month or the last or in between, and I would like that written down somewhere and I am not going to ask you to write it down."

Nobody wrote it down. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud there and entered the thing and entered the number she gave and entered that nobody asked her for it and that the number is a figure about a woman and a bank and not about a lot, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch to his thigh from about ten and stayed in it, and the water was up over the place where he had been standing four days ago. He said one thing up the bank at about half past ten and it was not addressed to anybody.

"You are all waiting for a day. I have been in this ditch for two months and I have not once known which day it was, and I worked out in the first week that it made no difference to what I was doing, and I have been right about that every day since." He did not stop work. "The water went up twice this month and down once and I have not written any of it down and there is nobody in this district I could give it to, and I am not saying that to be owed something. I am saying it because some of you are standing at that table treating the end of a month as though it were going to be a thing that happens, and it is going to be a day, and I would like to know what all the waiting is for."

Nobody told him. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered no figure about it, and the record about the not answering says not answered.

At about eleven the boy of about nineteen was asked by four different people across about an hour what he thought would happen, and he gave all four of them the same answer, and the answer was eleven words long, and nobody wrote it down.

"I have a page and a pencil and no instructions."

At about half past eleven the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said out loud, to that east wall and not to the yard, that the thirtieth of the last month was the day a figure about a month was entered off a list, and that he had been standing there on that day and had watched a woman put one word in a margin and stop, and that he had not thought about it once in thirty days, and that it had come back to him on the twenty-ninth and had not let go since.

Nobody said anything about that. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud and entered the thing and entered that it carried no figure, and entered that a man has had a thing in his mouth for twenty-five days and that the two of those are not the same thing and are not added together.

---

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and took up the nine feet with his right hand in his coat. Four people looked at him and he looked at the man who puts tables up and then at the book under that stone and then at the ground between them, and said nothing for about as long as it takes the man of fifty-six to read four figures.

"You are waiting for me to say something," he said.

"You are waiting for me to say something," said the man who puts tables up, "and the difference between those two is that you have already said yours and I have not."

Nobody said which of those was true. Two people said afterwards that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against had come down that bank for the fourth day running with nothing on him and had stayed twenty minutes for the fifth, and that nobody had asked him for a name on any of the five, and that the record about the not asking says not asked on all five.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank wet to the chest and put the bundle down on the stones and stood at that end of the table for about four seconds. Four people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him, and he was not asked for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and he went up the lane.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about half past two the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches with one arm, and four people watched a man lift a wheel with one arm and not one of them said a word.

At about three a man of about sixty-four got up off the stones at the foot of that low wall and stood for about four seconds with nothing in his hands, and then sat down again about two feet along, and nobody offered him a hand, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

At about quarter past three the man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of that bank and got the four figures at that end of the table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day, and two people noticed it and neither of them said a word.

At about half past three a woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with two empty buckets and said one thing out loud to nobody, and a few people within nine feet of her heard it and nobody else did.

"I read two lines in that book every morning going up or coming down and I have read the same two lines for two months and I am not going to read them out loud to anybody this month, and if somebody asks me to I am going to say no, and that is not a rule and it is not for any of you." She filled the two buckets. "That is all. I said it so that about four of you would not build a week out of it."

Nobody built a week out of it. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman of fifty-eight said a thing out loud there and entered the thing and entered no figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open ninety days and was not run today, and that nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and twenty days past, and a ladder is spent as a ladder. The rule said out loud in that ground on the tenth of the tenth month is fifty days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is forty-five days out of date, was not altered, and has nothing written beside it. The figure entered off a list on the thirtieth of the tenth month is thirty-one days old and has not been checked by anybody. The word unchecked is in the margin of her own page over that figure and is over nothing else. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six on this day and was empty at about six yesterday, and those are two figures about two days and are not added together. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures for the last time that day and got all four of them.

Eleven buckets went up that bank at about six, which is what has gone up it for nineteen years, and she did not look back, and the boy of about nineteen turned his own page over at about ten to six, and two people looked at the front of it and not one of them said a word about what was on it, and some of them had spent that whole day waiting for a day that had turned out to be nothing, and one of them wrote nothing down about that either.
