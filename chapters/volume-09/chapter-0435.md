# Chapter 435: A Date And Not A Count Of The Same Thing

A man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said a day out loud there on the second of the twelfth month and nobody had asked him for it, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote it into a book on the end of a table, and about nine people watched a woman put a date under a figure that is wrong and leave the figure wrong.

It was the second of the twelfth month and the light came up hard and clean off the south end of that ground and there was no wind in it at all. By ten there were about nineteen people in the ground of Lot Seventeen.

That morning the man of fifty-six kept his hand on the boards the whole time he was reading and got all four figures, and said the word with each of them, and got the word four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and forty-three, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and fifty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and seventy-three, and three hundred and thirty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and eighty-four days, and entered that there is no day-count for the sheet.

---

He said it at about half past ten from the top of that bank with the water coming off his coat, and he did not raise his voice, and nobody at that table had asked him anything.

"It was the fifteenth of the tenth month and I was in that ditch until about half past four and I came up the bank on my hands and my knees and the water came over me as I got to the top." He did not stop, and nobody stopped him, and about nineteen people heard it. "I have not been asked for that day by anybody in this yard in eight weeks. I am saying it because there is a book on that table with a figure in it that stopped being true on a day and nobody wrote the day down, and I am the only person who knows the day, and a book that says what is in a ditch this morning and does not say when it stopped being that is a book a stranger reads wrong."

The clerk of nineteen years took her pencil out. Nobody said a word. She did not write it on her own page. She walked to the end of that trestle table, moved the stone off the corner of that book with both hands, and put it down on the boards beside the book, and about nine people at that table took a half step back.

She wrote one line under the two that were in it, and it took her about eleven seconds, and she put the stone back on the corner of the book where it has been for two months.

> One lot. The figure on the second line above stopped being true on the fifteenth of the tenth month.

She read it out loud to herself, once, and did not read it back from the top, and four people at that table said afterwards that she had read it to herself and that a thing read to the person who wrote it binds nobody.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about eleven, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said no to it in about four seconds before the clerk of nineteen years had answered it, and she did not, and a few people at that table said afterwards that they had not known until that moment that a question could be taken off a person standing there.

"May I read it back from the top."

"No. A thing read back from the top has to be read back by somebody who is not the person who wrote it, and the only person standing at this table who is not the person who wrote it has a hand in a cloth and about four of you would call that a favour done to a page, and a favour done to a page is not a reading." He did not raise his voice. "She read it to herself. That is what happened. If you want to know what it says you can walk up to it and read it, because that is the whole of what it is for."

Nobody read it back. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked a question there and that it was not answered by the person it was asked of, and entered that a thing read to the person who wrote it is not read back from the top, and the record about the not reading says not read back.

---

Nobody said anything for about as long as it takes the man of fifty-six to read four figures. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the far end of that table and he said two sentences, and about nine people heard both, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A wrong count is not struck by writing a correct one beside it, and what she has just written is not a correct one and does not strike anything, and the rule this district is working on was written against a second figure and not against a second kind of thing.**"

Then the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said one thing from the east wall, in about four seconds, and it was not a question.

"It is a date. A date is not a figure of a lot and it is not a figure of a person and it is not a toll and it is not a holder and it is not a remedy and it is not a term. She has put a day under a number and the number is still wrong and she has not touched the number." He turned round and looked at the book. "I have been in this yard for two months waiting for somebody to do something to that book and what she has done is the only thing anybody could have done to it without breaking six things."

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch to his thigh at that moment. He did not come up out of the bank, and a few people at that table looked down into the water and then looked at each other, and one of them said out loud that a man who has said a number in a yard and had it written down was not going to want to be looked at this morning.

Nobody looked at him on purpose. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who said a figure in the open on the thirteenth of the tenth was standing there this morning, and entered that the figure on the second line of that book is the one he said, and entered no name against it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the second half of it about twenty minutes later, and he said it to that table and not to the yard, and the boy of about nineteen counted it and got one hundred and four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**She is not going to enter that the rule has been improved. She is not going to enter that a thing has been solved, and she is not going to enter that a stranger walking up to that table tomorrow can tell which of the three lines is current, because he cannot, and the first line has a figure in it and the second line has a figure in it and the third line has got a day in it and there is no way of telling him off that page which of the two numbers is the one that was true this morning.**"

"I was not going to enter it," the clerk of nineteen years said.

"I know what you were not going to enter. I have been listening to what you enter for six months." He did not raise his voice. "A clerk may enter that a date is not a count of the same thing. A clerk may not enter that the rule got better. I have watched about four of you try to make those two the same sentence this month and you cannot."

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard, and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk may not enter that the rule has been improved, and entered that she had not entered it, and the record about the not asking says not asked. Two people at that table said afterwards that the man who said it and the woman who entered it had between them stopped a sentence in about four seconds and that neither of them had raised a voice.

---

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat, and looked at that book for about as long as it takes the man of fifty-six to read four figures, and said one thing.

"That book is worth more now and I am not going to say by how much, and some of you have been waiting two months for somebody to say a number about it and there is not going to be one."

"Who says it is worth more," said the man who puts tables up, from the east wall.

"Nobody. I said it and I am not going to say it again." He did not move the hand that was in his coat. "It has a third line in it. That is what has happened today. Whether a thing has three lines in it or two is not a figure and I am not going to make it into one, and if any of you write that down as one I will say so out loud and you will not enjoy it."

Nobody wrote that down. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing and entered no figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked. She entered separately that a man asked a question in that yard and that it was not answered by the person it was asked of, and that a clerk does not enter which of the two people at that table stopped it.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank with the water to his thigh and got the four figures at that end of the table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. He stood at the near end of that table for about a minute and looked at the ground between the boards and the low wall, and did not look at the book, and nobody told him to and nobody asked him why he did not.

Then a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about twenty past one and stopped, and read the three lines standing up, and said one thing out loud to the stone rather than to anybody.

"There are three of them. I have been coming up this bank for nineteen years and there have been two, and I am not going to tell the rest of you which two I know by heart, because one of the two is a number about water and it is the wrong number and I would rather not say that out loud in a yard than a man I have never met." She put her hand flat on the top of that book for about a second and did not move the stone. "That is all. A person who has carried eleven buckets up a bank for nineteen years can tell you that there are three lines today and two were there yesterday and that is not the same as a figure."

Nobody asked her which two. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that she was not asked which two lines she knew, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about half past two the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about nine people watched a man lift a wheel with one arm and not one of them said a word.

At about three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything, and nobody said that a date is a thing he could be given, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open ninety-three days and was not run this day and that nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. She entered that a line was written in a lot book on the end of a trestle table in the open and that the line is a date and not a count of the same thing, and that it was written out of a figure a man gave with his mouth on a day he was not asked, and that he has not been paid for it and cannot be. She entered that the figure on the second line of that book is forty-eight days out of date, was not altered, has nothing written beside it, and has a date written under it, and that a date written under a wrong figure does not correct the figure. She entered that a clerk may not enter that a rule has been improved, and did not enter it. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and twenty-three days past. The rule said out loud in that yard on the tenth of the tenth month is fifty-three days standing. The figure entered off a list on the thirtieth of the tenth month is thirty-four days old. The word unchecked is in the margin of her own page and is over that figure and over nothing else. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, which takes her about two seconds longer than two lines takes her, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her.

There are three lines in that book now and the stone is back on the corner of it. The second line is still wrong and nobody there has corrected it, and the third line does not say how much water there is in that ditch this morning and does not pretend to, and about four people have already worked out that a stranger walking up to that table tomorrow will read the second line first and take it away with him, and that the third line will be under it and that he will not know what the third line is for.
