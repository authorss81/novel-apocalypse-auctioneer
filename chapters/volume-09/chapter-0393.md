# Chapter 393: Sixty-One Days

---

The man of about nineteen had two figures written at the top of his own page in his own hand when he came into that yard, fifty and fifty-seven, and he had put a line under each of them, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and nobody had asked him to carry them anywhere.

The twenty-first of the tenth month came in with a low sky and no wind in it, and the man of fifty-six read the four figures off those boards out loud and got all four, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read them after him and got all four, and about four people in that yard said out loud that the two of them reading the same four numbers every morning is a thing two men do and not a finding.

"**The board carries three hundred and one and the train on that siding has stood six hundred and seventeen days, and two hundred and ninety-two days separate the second of January and this morning, and that bid has stood open fifty-one days and it was not run on any one of them, and the reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands where it stood on the twelfth of this month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the figure on the second line of that lot book is six days out of date and was not altered, and that a reading which is begun and not finished is not a thing that has been read back by anybody and binds nobody, and that the reading stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and that the fourth of those is a person, and that no person in that yard has been asked for one and she is not going to ask.

---

At about half past ten the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came to the end of that trestle table and put his right hand in his coat before he got there and left it there, and about nineteen people in that yard watched him do it and about four of them said out loud that he has been doing that every morning since the thirteenth of this month and that it is a coat and not a hiding place, and he said that it is a hand and not a decision and left it where it was.

Then he said the thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and fourteen, and he counted it twice, because it is the longest thing anybody has said in that yard since he began writing figures down.

"**A month is the shortest interval this district has evidence for, and a figure written in a book a stranger may walk up to is out of date before anybody has finished looking at it, and I put the first one in there, and there is another figure I put in a book four hundred yards from that gate that is sixty-one days, and that is the one I am afraid of, and it is a figure about how often a body four hundred miles off prints and not a figure about the body, and a figure somebody has entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything for about a minute and a half, which is the longest anybody in that yard had gone without saying anything since before this district began keeping figures.

Then about four people in that yard said out loud, all at once, that he is the one who put it there, and he did not say that that was not so, and about four other people in that yard said that a man who puts a figure in a book that goes out of date is the man who has to keep it, and about four of them said that a man who put a figure in a book four hundred yards from a gate has been afraid of something for about four months and has never once said so out loud in that yard, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud, in the ordinary voice and with a reason, that a man who has said in a yard that he is afraid of a figure has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and about four people said he was right and about four of them said that is twice in three days that a person in that yard has been told what a sentence of theirs is not.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure about a body's habit is not a figure about a body and is not a promise about a later day, and that the sixty-one days was named out loud in that yard this morning by the man who put it in, and that the interval a body four hundred miles off prints at is sixty-one days and that nothing came on the first of this month and that it is now twenty days past a printing it did not make, and that a clerk may not enter that anybody was waiting for it and did not enter it, and that a thing which does not arrive has no face and a clerk is not going to write one for it.

---

At about one the yard argued about it, in the ordinary way, and the argument lasted about two hours and about nine people were in it, and it came down to three things that can be done with a book a stranger may walk up to and read.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put them out loud in that yard, standing at the end of that table, and nobody wrote any of them down and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got eighty-three, and about four of them asked him to say them again and he said them again in the same words and nobody counted the second telling, and about four of them said that the second telling was the one that had cost the man of about nineteen the most work.

"**You can change the figure, you can say which figure is current, or you can take the book off that table, and this district has ruled the first one out on its own page and the second one is a thing no one in this yard can do without adding a column, and neither of those has happened and the third one is not going to be said out loud in this yard this afternoon by anybody, and I have not said it.**"

Nobody in that yard said the third one out loud, and one person said out loud that he had not said it either, and about four of them said that a book off that table would be a book nobody could walk up to, and one of them said that out loud before the other one could get there, and about four of them said that was the first time anybody had said it in that yard.

Then about four people in that yard said out loud that a yard in daylight is a room with no roof and that a bid which has stood open fifty-one days without being run is not a market, and about four of them said it is the same sentence said over, and one of them said that a market is a place where a person is asked a question and that this yard has not asked one for six months, and nobody in that yard argued with her about it.

And the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said one thing out loud at about three that about four people heard, and nobody counted it, and it was not about a figure at all.

"**I have been in that lane about nine years and I have watched men carry things down it and put them down and pick them up again, and the one thing I know about a thing that is in the open is that it gets read wrong, and it gets read wrong by people who did not come here on purpose, and there is nothing anybody in that yard can do about a person who is four hundred miles off and reads a figure off a page and does not know what day it was written.**"

About four people in that yard said that is a man who keeps a road talking about reading and about four of them said that a man who keeps a road for nine years has been four hundred miles off twice, and he said he had not and that both times he had been in this district, and nobody asked him which two, and neither of the two was named out loud and nobody in that yard said one word about it again.

Then the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them said one thing out loud at the east wall, to about four of them and not to anybody else, and his hand-cart was behind him with the tent still rolled on the back of it where it has been since the thirtieth of the June.

"**A table with four legs out of a door is a table until somebody asks somebody a question at it, and nobody has asked anybody a question at that table in six months, and I put the second chair at the end of it on the eighth of this month and I have not been asked about it once since, and I am not going to be asked, and that is the end of what I know about that table.**"

About four people in that yard said out loud that a table with four legs standing in the open is a table and is not a counter and is not a market, and that a chair put down at the end of it without being asked is a chair and carries no job, and is not a sixth of the five things this district protects and is not a seventh of the six instruments it has built and not named, and that the man who put it there is not a keeper of it and was not asked and has not been asked.

---

The rest of that day went ordinary. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch before nine and stood on the bank at about ten with the water to his thigh, and went back in after lunch and was still in it at about half past four, and his right hand did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in this day and nobody asked him about it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane at about eleven and again at about four, read the four figures both times, got all four both times, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about a month or about a figure going out of date.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up the bank at the end of that afternoon with his hands white to the knuckle and stood at the top of it for about a minute before he went, and about four people in that yard said good evening to him as he went up the lane and he said good evening back and nobody said one word to him about the second line of that lot book and he was not asked about it.

That bid has stood open fifty-one days and was not run today. The first of the eighth month went past eighty-one days ago. The figure on the second line of that lot book is six days out of date and is not altered and has nothing written beside it. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six and no name went into it by anybody.

At about half past six the man the figure of twenty-one years is against took his right hand out of his coat at the end of that trestle table and put it flat on the boards beside the lot book, and it lay there open and did not close on the edge of the table when he put his thumb across it, and about four people in that yard watched that happen and not one of them said a word about it, and he put it back in his coat and went up the lane.
