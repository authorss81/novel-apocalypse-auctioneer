# Chapter 437: The Man Who Said It Out Loud

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of that ditch on the fourth of the twelfth month and stood at the end of that trestle table and looked at the first line of that book and at the third line of that book, and did not look at the second one, and about nine people watched a man not look at a line for about a minute and a half and not one of them said one word.

It was the fourth of the twelfth month and the frost had gone off the boards of that table by about half past eight. By ten there were about nineteen people in the ground of Lot Seventeen.

The four figures came off that wall in the ordinary voice with the man's hand flat on the boards, and he got every one of them, and said the word after each, four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and forty-five, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and sixty-one days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and seventy-five, and three hundred and thirty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and eighty-six days.

---

He had been in that ditch since about half past eight. He came up the bank at about eleven with the water to his thigh and stood at the end of that table and did not say anything, and about nine people were within four feet of him in about half a minute.

Then he said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it took about four seconds.

"That is mine."

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that a man has said that a line in a book in the open came out of his mouth, and entered that nobody read it back from the top, and the record about the not reading says not read back.

Nobody asked him a question. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the far end of that table and he said nothing at all for about as long as it takes the man of fifty-six to read four figures, and then he said one sentence and it was not a question either, and the boy of about nineteen counted it and got forty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and two people at that table heard the number and some of the rest of that yard did not.

"**Then you have been in this yard for eight weeks with a line in a book in the open that is yours and it is wrong, and some of you have known that, and I am not going to ask you how you have been getting through the mornings.**"

"I have not been getting through them," the loam man said. "I have been standing at that end of this table about four times a week for two months and putting my hand flat on those boards, and the boards have not said anything to me once, and the figure on that page is not the boards." He did not look at the book. "I put a number in a yard on the thirteenth of the tenth month and a woman wrote it down and it went on a page in the open, and it has been wrong for fifty days, and I have not slept a whole night about it since the second week of the ninth, and I have not told anybody that until this morning and I am not telling you now either. I am telling the table that it is mine, because the man who cuts reeds put a day under it two days ago that somebody said out loud, and I would like it on the page that the man who said the number and the man who said the day are two different men, and that the number is still wrong and the day is right."

---

Nobody asked him whether he was still afraid of the figure, and a few people at that table wanted to ask him and did not, and two of them said afterwards that they wished they had asked. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was not asked a question in that yard and that he was not refused one either, and entered that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

"You have not corrected it," somebody said from the middle of that ground.

"No."

"You could put a right figure under it. You could take the book and boil it and write it again from the bottom."

"I know what I could do. I have worked out what I could do about once a week for two months and I have not done any of it, and the reason I have not done it is that the second line is a line in a book in the open, and a wrong count is not struck by writing a correct one beside it, and a correct one is not a strike." He turned round and looked at the ditch and not at the table. "There is a day under it now. I did not put that day there and I cannot take it out and I would not if I could, because a day is not a number and it is the only one of the three things on that page that anybody can stand a finger on and check, and the other two you cannot."

Then the man of about thirty-one who digs loam went back down the bank into the water, and four people at that table said after he had gone that a man who says a thing out loud and then goes back into a ditch is a man who has said the whole of it, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud and went away, and entered the thing, and entered no figure.

At about twenty past twelve the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud from that far end, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got thirty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and a few people heard the number and the rest of that ground did not.

"**Nobody has asked him about it and that is the correct thing, and it is not a rule and it is not mine, and about four of you want him asked and about four of you do not.**"

Nobody said anything about that. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that a man was not asked a question and was not refused one, and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat and looked at the man in the ditch for about a minute without saying anything.

"That is the second time this month somebody has come into this yard and owned a thing out loud and not one word of it has been read back from the top," he said. "A name on the twenty-seventh and a number this morning, and both of them in the open, and both of them on nobody's page, and both of them given to about nineteen people and not to a second one."

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said one thing up the bank without stopping work and without coming out of the water.

"That is because a second one would make it a reading and a reading that is read back by one person binds nobody anyway, and you have been in this yard long enough to know that better than I do." He did not stop. "And you have got a name of your own you are not saying and we have all worked that out in about a fortnight and nobody has asked you, and I am not asking you, and I want it understood that I am not asking you because I have not been asked myself this morning."

Nobody wrote any of that down. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and that another man said a thing out loud there, and entered both of the things, and entered no name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank and stood at the end of that table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm, and four people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him, and nobody asked him whether the day was right, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and he went up the lane. He is the only person in that district who knows what the water was that morning and nobody has asked him and nobody was asked on his behalf and he cannot be paid for it and cannot be thanked for it and about four people at that table wanted to do both and did not.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about half past two the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about nine people watched him and nobody said a word.

At about half past three the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came off the east wall and stood at that table and said one thing out loud and a few people heard it clearly and the rest of that ground did not.

"Two men have owned a thing in this yard this month and neither of them was thanked and neither of them was given anything, and the man who has the day in that book has not been asked whether he is right about it once in two days, and the reason nobody has asked him is that about four of us have worked out that if we ask him and he says no then the day is a day nobody can stand a finger on, and that is the one thing on that page we have got."

Nobody said anything about that. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that the clerk does not enter a reason for a man not asking another man a question, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open ninety-five days and was not run this day and that nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. She entered that a man said out loud in that yard that a line in a book in the open is his, and that the line is fifty days out of date, and that it was not altered, and that a correct figure was not written beside it, and that the third line of that book is a date and does not correct the second line. She entered that the man who said the number and the man who said the day are two different men and that nobody read either thing back from the top and that the record about the not reading says not read back.

The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and twenty-five days past. The rule said out loud in that ground on the tenth of the tenth month is fifty-five days standing. The figure entered off a list on the thirtieth of the tenth month is thirty-six days old. The word unchecked is in the margin of her own page and is over that figure and over nothing else. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures for the last time that day and got all four of them, and the boy of about nineteen turned his own page over at about ten to six, and the man who digs loam was still in that ditch at about half past five and was in it at about half past four the day before that, and nobody has ever written down what time he comes out.

Three lines, and only the first of them is still true: the second is a figure about water and is not, and the third is a day and says nothing at all about how much water is in that ditch this morning. The man who said the second one said out loud in that yard this morning that it is his, and about four people heard him, and nobody has written that on a page, and nobody will, and the second line is still there under a stone in the open for anybody who walks up to that table to read a figure that a man has been standing next to for two months.
