# Chapter 402: Thirty, and a Figure Off a List

The clerk of nineteen years put her finger on the mark at the top of that board at about ten past eight and left it there while she counted the days out loud between it and the bottom of the wood, and the top mark was this morning's.

It was the thirtieth of the tenth month, which is the last day of it, and about nine people in that yard had known that since before light and had not said the word for it to anybody. The frost was gone off those boards by eight. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

The man of fifty-six had his palm on those boards before the mender got to that end of the table and read the four figures out loud over his shoulder and got all of them, the way he has done every morning for six months.

"**The board carries three hundred and ten, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and twenty-six days, and nobody has entered anything for three hundred and forty, and three hundred and one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that its own age as a figure about the figure is a hundred and fifty-one days, and that there is still no figure on her page for the sheet's own tenure on that post and there is not going to be one this morning either.

---

She started about ten. Nobody had asked her to and nobody said one word about it while she was doing it.

She put her finger on the mark at the top, which was this morning's, and named that day out loud. Then she went down to the mark below it and named that one, and she went all the way down in front of about nineteen people who could see her finger arrive at every mark. The man of fifty-six stood on the other side of her and read each mark before she got to it, and got all of them. At the far end of that trestle table the man of about nineteen counted with his own eyes and his own finger and his own page, and said so when somebody standing next to him asked him what he was doing.

"I am counting for me," he said. "Nobody has asked me to count for anybody else."

"You have said that before."

"I have said it twice today, because twice today somebody has assumed it."

She got to the mark for the first of this month at about twenty past ten, put her finger on it, and said the day out loud again, and straightened up. The man of about nineteen counted what she said and got sixty-seven and wrote it on his own page and did not read it back to himself, which is a thing he has not done for ten days.

"**From the first of the tenth month to this morning is thirty marks and it is thirty, and both of those are days I can name, and anybody standing at that wall can put a finger on either end of it and get the same figure I got, and that is not the same kind of thing as a figure anybody has read out of something else.**"

"It is not the same kind of thing and both of them are figures," somebody said, from the middle of that yard.

"It is not the same kind of thing," the man of fifty-six said, from the other side of the wall. "One of them you can put a finger on. That is the whole of the difference and I have been trying to say it for ten days with my hands."

"Nobody has said what the other kind is."

"Somebody has," the man of about nineteen said. "It is what I have been putting on my own page since the nineteenth of this month and it has got no name on it, and I am not going to give it one in a yard."

The man of fifty-six read the thirty marks out loud once more as she put her finger on them, and got all thirty of them, and about four people said out loud that a man who has read a figure off that wall every morning for six months had just read thirty of them off it in a few minutes and had never once read a mark that was not there.

---

She wrote it down at about half past ten, on her own page, in her own hand, with the two days named at either end of it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the month this district has just been living in is thirty, that the count was made off marks on a board in a yard in daylight with about nineteen people standing at that wall, and that it is a figure about a count. She entered that the ninth month is thirty-one and the seventh is thirty-one and the fifth is thirty-one and the third is thirty-one, and that the second month is the only month any person in this district has ever said out loud was short.

"That is the first of those five figures anybody in this yard has had a reason to say," somebody said.

"It is the first of those five figures this district has got off a board," the man of fifty-six said. "They are not the same and I have been careful all morning."

Then the mender, from the end of that trestle table, with his left hand in its cloth and his right hand flat on the boards, said that a figure anybody can put a finger on and a figure nobody can put a finger on are the same figure in a different place, and that a district which works out which one it is going to be living in has a job in front of it.

"You have a job," the woman of thirty-six said, from the far end of the yard. "You have said the word. Do you want it written down anywhere?"

"No," the mender said. "If I say a word in this yard twice somebody will rule something for it, and I have watched that happen to two other words this month."

"Two words in a month is a column," somebody said, "and a column is not a man."

"It is not a man and it is not a rule and it is not a rate," the mender said. "A rate is a thing that is already true of you whether anybody says it or not, and this district has not got one, and putting a figure on the length of a month is not how you get one."

Then she wrote one more line, and she wrote it lower down and away from the other entries, and about four people at the near end of that table leaned over to see whether she was going to stop.

She did not stop.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the month after this one is thirty-one, that she did not count it, that she took it off a list, that she is entering it as a figure about a list and not as a day-count, and that the word unchecked is on it and is going to stay on it for as long as the figure does.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a date is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named. She named two days out loud in that yard this morning and about four people wrote them down. A day a clerk names is not a lot, is not a figure of a lot, is not a figure of a person, is not a toll, is not a holder, is not a remedy and is not a term. There is a person at that wall and there were four of them behind her, and what she wrote down says the two days and nobody's name.

Somebody said out loud, to about four of them, that a figure nobody can check has just gone on that page in front of about nineteen people, and that about four of them thought the whole of this morning was that and the rest of it was counting.

---

At about eleven her hands had gone. She put them under her arms for a minute the way a man does it at that table, and then took them out again and wrote with them, and about four people noticed and none of them said anything about it.

The man of fifty-six stood beside her for the whole of that and did not read anything out loud after about half past ten. He read the marks while she counted them and got all of them. Then he stopped, and the yard noticed the stopping, and he said one thing out loud about it, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got ninety.

"**I have read a figure off that wall every morning for six months and this morning was the last morning I read one that anybody standing at that wall could have got, and from tomorrow morning there is a figure on that page that came off a list nobody in this yard has seen, and I am going to read the other ones off those boards every morning the same as I always have, and I am not going to be told what the length of anything is by anybody.**"

"Nor by the list," somebody said.

"Especially not by the list," the man of fifty-six said. "A list is a thing somebody wrote down once and a board is a thing somebody cut every morning, and I know which of those two I have been reading off for six months."

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam, from the low wall, where he had been standing since about half past ten with the water running off him, said that the ninth month was thirty-one as well. It was entered off a list by somebody else a while back, and nobody ever wrote down where that mark was.

"So that one cannot be counted either," the mender said.

"That one cannot be counted now or ever," the loam man said. "Not by me, not by him, not by her, and not by whoever comes after the three of us. It is a figure about a list and there is no list anybody in this district owns."

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure of thirty-one for the ninth month was entered off a list and that nobody wrote down where the mark was, and that a figure entered off a list whose source nobody wrote down cannot be checked by anybody afterwards. That is a cost about a board and not about a person, and a clerk is not going to put the word against a man about his own hands.

---

What was left of that day was the same four things it is every day, and it took longer than the day before, and about four people said so at the low wall and one of them said it was the first time in a month that a day had been called ordinary out loud in that yard. The road keeper came up that lane twice, near eleven and near four, had the four figures off that wall both times, missed nothing either time, and asked nobody about the eleventh month or about anything else. The reed cutter came up that bank at about half past four and was wet to the chest and said good evening to nobody in particular. The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was against the east wall with his hand-cart and the tent rolled on the back of it where it has been since the thirtieth of the June, and he was given nothing. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall from about ten with his coat folded on the stones beside him, and nothing in his hands, and he was not asked to sit in the second chair and nobody in that yard proposed it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid has stood open sixty days and was not run today, that no person in that yard said one word about closing it, and that a bid nobody has run does not have a birthday anybody in this district is keeping. The first day of the eighth month is ninety days past, and nobody in that yard has given a reason for that figure in a month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and ninety-three days back, was named once that afternoon, and was closed in nothing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is fifteen days out of date and was not altered. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled before the first of the ninth and it was empty at about six, and a thing read back by nobody binds nobody. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the count is still five.

At about half past five the woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes, and she read the two lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her.

By the time the light had gone the man of fifty-six was at the end of that trestle table with his hand flat on those boards, and the four figures on them were the four figures that had been on them at about ten past eight, and the top mark was this morning's, and above the top mark there was clean wood nobody had ever cut into, and nobody in that yard could say how much of it there was.
