# Chapter 436: A Number That Moved, And A Reason

A clerk of nineteen years entered a number that had not moved in two months and a reason under it, and about nine people at that table found out about the second part of that before they found out about the first part of it.

It was the third of the twelfth month and the sky was low and grey and a few people had come down that bank expecting nothing to happen. By ten there were about nineteen people in the ground of Lot Seventeen.

He put his palm down on those boards, said the four figures out loud, got all four of them, and said the word with each of them, four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and forty-four, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and sixty days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and seventy-four, and three hundred and thirty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and eighty-five days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet.

---

She wrote it on her own page at about eleven and she wrote the number first, the way she has written every number for two months, and then she stopped and put her pencil down on the boards and said out loud that she was not going to write the rest of that line yet.

"Eleven," she said. "It was ten yesterday. It is eleven this morning and I am not writing why until somebody in this yard has said a reason out loud that I would be willing to write under it, because I have entered a number on a page for six months without a reason under any of them and it turns out that is a thing a person can do and not a thing this district should do again."

Nine people at that table looked at each other. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the far end of it with his left hand in its cloth and he did not say anything for about a minute and a half, and a few of them took that as an answer and a few of them took it as an insult.

Then the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said out loud from the east wall, in about four seconds, that the reason is that a book in the open is a thing a person can walk up to and read, and that it had two lines in it yesterday and has three today, and that the third one is a day, and that a day is not a count of the same thing and is not a seventh of anything.

"A date is not a seventh of anything," he said. "It is not a fifth column of not-askings. It is not a protected thing and it is not a condition with no end on it and it is not a rate and it is not a toll and it is not a holder and it is not a remedy. It is a day, and it went into a book that anybody may walk up to, and a book a person may walk up to is what this district makes instruments out of. That is the whole reason and it took about four seconds."

The clerk of nineteen years wrote the number and then the reason, and she entered that the reason was given out loud in the open and not asked for, and entered that a count that moves needs a scene and a reason and both were on the page that morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

She entered separately, in a second hand on the same page, that a date is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and that the third line of a lot book is not a lot and is not a reading of the rival record and is not a ninth of anything, and that a third line in a book is not a market.

---

At about half past eleven the boy of about nineteen asked her a question out loud in front of about nineteen people and she answered it in one breath and did not put her pencil down.

"Eleven of what."

"Things this district has made. It was ten since the middle of the sixth month and it is eleven now, and the eleventh is a book with three lines in it that a person four hundred miles off may walk up to and read without being told by anybody which of the two numbers on it is this morning's." She did not look at the page. "It is not a post and it is not a keeper and it is not a column and it is not a rule. Nobody has been given anything and nobody has been asked to carry anything and if somebody writes a name in a column beside that number then the eleventh thing is a person and the tenth is a rule and both of those are wrong and I will know which one I wrote."

"You are going to have to say what the other ten are," said the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing.

"I am not. Nine of them are on a page in a hand that is not mine and the tenth is a book with two lines in it yesterday morning, and if I list them I am a woman with a list of things other people made." She shut the page. "I have entered a number and a reason and I am not entering the ten."

Nobody said anything about that. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing and entered no figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart said one thing out loud from the lane, where he had stopped with a hand up, and it was the first time in about eleven visits that he had said anything standing there, and nine people turned round.

"I have been coming up this lane with a wheel that drags since the middle of the sixth month and I have never said anything about it, and about four of you have watched me lift it about nine inches with one arm eleven times and not one of you ever asked me what the arm is for." He let the cart down. "It is not for the cart. I cannot lift it any higher than that and it is going to be like this until somebody who is not me looks at the axle, and that is all I came up here to say and I have said it and I am going on up the lane now."

Nobody said a word. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that a figure about a man and a wheel is not a figure about a road, and that the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was not asked about it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and took up the nine feet with his right hand in his coat, and about nine people at that table were standing further back than they had been standing for a fortnight, and he noticed that and said nothing about it for about twenty minutes.

"You moved a number this morning," he said, "and two days ago a woman put a date under a wrong figure, and between those two things there is not one name in this district." He did not move the hand in his coat. "I want to say the thing I have been not saying since the first of the tenth month, and I am going to say it once and then I am going to leave it alone. Nothing that happened this week could have happened with my name on it. I have gone over it every morning for eleven days and there is no version of the last ten days where a page with my name at the top of it is the version anybody wanted. That is not a complaint and I am not asking anybody for anything and nobody has asked me for a name since the nineteenth of the tenth month and nobody is going to."

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered that the thing contained no name and no request, and entered that nobody had asked him for a name, and the record about the not asking says not asked. Two people at that table said afterwards that the man who said it had said it in a yard of about nineteen people and had not been asked a question and had not been thanked, and that neither of those things was going to be on a page.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of that bank and got the four figures and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder. He did not go to the end of that table and about four people at that table noticed that he did not go to the end of that table, and none of them said anything about it.

At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank and stood at the end of that table for about four seconds, and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him, and he was not asked for anything, and nobody asked him whether the third line was right, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches with one arm.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him a page and nobody offered him a figure and nobody offered him a chair, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

At about four the boy of about nineteen asked the clerk of nineteen years a question out loud in front of about nineteen people, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud before she answered, and the boy of about nineteen counted it and got forty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and two people heard the number come out of his mouth.

"**A figure that moves has a reason under it and a reason has a mouth on it, and a man said the reason out loud this morning and about nine of you heard him, and that is the whole difference between eleven and ten.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said that he was right and that she was not going to enter the number of people who heard it, and that a reason is a thing a person said and not a thing a book holds, and that she had entered the reason and not the hearing of it. The boy of about nineteen said that was the second time that week somebody had taken a question off him and that he was getting used to it and did not think he was.

Nobody read that back from the top. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that it was not read back, and the record about the not reading says not read back.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure this district has had since the middle of the sixth month moved this morning, that it is eleven and it was ten, and that a clerk entered the reason and the reason was given out loud in that yard and not asked for, and that a count that moves needs a scene and a reason and both are on this page. She entered that a date is not a seventh of anything and is not a lot and is not a reading and is not a rate and is not a toll and is not a holder and is not a remedy and is not a term. She entered that no column was ruled this morning and that nobody was given anything and that nobody was asked to carry anything.

The bid has stood open ninety-four days and was not run this day and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and twenty-four days past, and a ladder is spent as a ladder. The rule said out loud in that yard on the tenth of the tenth month is fifty-four days standing. The figure on the second line of that lot book is forty-nine days out of date, was not altered, and has nothing written beside it. The figure entered off a list on the thirtieth of the tenth month is thirty-five days old. The word unchecked is in the margin of her own page and is over that figure and over nothing else. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures for the last time that day and got all four of them.

Eleven sits at the top of that ledger with a reason under it in her own hand, and some people have read the reason standing at that table this afternoon and nine have not, and it does not say anywhere on that page what the other ten are, and she has said out loud that she is not going to say, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and twenty-seven days back and was not named in that yard this morning and was not closed.
