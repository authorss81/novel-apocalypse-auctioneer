# Chapter 396: Four Days in a Week

---

The trough at the bottom of that bank was drawn from twice on the morning of the twenty-fourth of the tenth month, once by a woman with two buckets at about half past five and once by a man with a kettle at about eight, and the water in it was where it has been for a week. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures off those boards out loud over the mender's shoulder and got all four, and the man of about nineteen put all four down. About four people said out loud that two men reading the same four numbers every morning is two men and not a finding, and that it is a good thing one of them can still get them after six months.

"Six months," somebody said. "He has got them every morning for six months."

"Then let him have them."

"**The board carries three hundred and four, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and twenty days, a hundred and eighty-seven days is how far back the ninth of the nine printed nights is, and the first day of the eighth month is eighty-four days past and a ladder is spent as a ladder.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. The ninth of the nine printed nights is open at a hundred and eighty-seven days, is named in the yard today, and is closed in nothing. A figure about a night is not a figure about a person and is not a figure about the ninth of anything. A thing on a page in a yard and a thing on a page in a book four hundred yards from that gate are two things and not one thing, and neither of them has been closed.

---

At about eleven the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane with his shafts down, and he did not come into that yard. He stopped at the low wall, which he has done about nine times since the middle of the ninth month, and nobody asked him anything and he read nothing.

About four people in the yard watched him stop. One of them said out loud that the man who drives a cart is out on that road four days in a week and is in this yard on the other three.

"Every week since the middle of the ninth month," somebody else said. "Four days. I have not missed one."

"That is a fact about a man with a cart. It is not a figure about this district and it is not a rule, and it is not going to be one."

The man of fifty-six said out loud that a week is not a month and a month is not a term, and that none of the three is a figure anybody can be bound on.

Then the mender said one thing out loud from the end of that trestle table, and about nineteen people heard all of it, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and put it on his own page, and then read it back to himself in a low voice, which is a thing he does when he does not trust a figure.

"**A fact about a man with a cart is a fact about a man with a cart and is not a rule, and a rule is not a fact, and neither of them is a finding, and the three of those are three different things and about nine people in this yard keep saying them as one, and I have said a rule out loud in this yard and a rule costs nothing and nobody can be made to keep it, and a man on a road four days in a week is not that and does not answer it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a fact about a man with a cart, a rule and a finding are three different things. A rule is not softened by a fact standing next to it, and a fact is not made into a rule by a person repeating it, and none of the three is a finding. Nothing said out loud this morning is on a sheet in this district, and nothing said out loud this morning has been written on the clerk's page.

Then about four people in the yard went straight on doing what about nine people in the yard do most mornings, which is to take the figure about how often a man with a cart is out on a road and turn it into a figure about how often a thing binds in this district.

"Four a week," the first of them said, "and about twenty weeks. That is about eighty."

"Four a week over a year is nearer two hundred."

"You have not counted a year."

"You have not counted twenty weeks."

"Nobody here has ever counted a week out loud in his life."

They went back and forth about it for about nine minutes, and the two of them were both right, and both of the figures were about something, and about four people said that it is a figure and it should go on a board.

The mender stopped them in about four seconds, and about nine people heard him do it, and none of them argued with him about it afterwards. What he said was that a man with a cart is not a second reader, and a second reader is nobody in particular and is found by whoever is standing there and is not found the same way twice. Four days on a road are four days on a road and are not a count of how many times anybody read anything back. The boy was told in about four seconds on the tenth of this month that he is not the second reader, and has not been asked for it since, and is not going to be asked.

A figure about a man's week went onto the page of the man of about nineteen who counts this morning. A figure about a man's week is not a figure about sittings and is not a figure about readings and is not a figure about a district, and nobody in the yard was appointed to anything and no name went into any column.

---

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came to the end of that table with his right hand in his coat and stood about nine feet off it. About four people said out loud that he is the man who read a thing back from the top on the fourth of this month into a book a stranger may walk up to, and about four other people said that he is the man who put the first line in that book.

"Both of those are so," he said.

"Nobody was going to ask you."

"I know that. I am saying it before one of you decides to be the one."

He read the two lines in that lot book out loud from the top, in the ordinary voice, both of them, standing up. Then he said one thing out loud, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got eighty-four, and about four people heard it and nobody put it down.

"**I have not read anything back from the top today and I am not going to, and a man with a cart out on a road four days in a week is not a reason why anybody in this yard is bound by anything, and a rule this district made that costs nothing is a rule nobody can be made to keep, and the two of those facts sitting next to each other in one yard is the whole of what this yard is.**"

A man stood at the end of that table this afternoon and said out loud that he is not the second reader of anything, and has not been told he is, and has not been asked. A fact, a rule and a finding are three things, and putting a figure about a man with a cart next to a figure about a rule is not arithmetic. Nothing was printed on any sheet in this district today.

Then the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them said one thing out loud at the east wall about the second chair, and about four of them heard it and nobody counted it, and the tent was still rolled on the back of his hand-cart.

"**I put that chair at the end of that table on the eighth of this month without being asked and nobody has asked me about it since, and a man of fifty-six sat in it on the eighteenth and got up out of it and I have not asked him about that either, and a chair is a chair and a forearm is a forearm and I have got neither of mine and I have not been given anything.**"

The second chair went down without being asked on the eighth of this month and nobody has asked about it since. One man has sat in it since, and a sitting is a figure about a sitting and not a figure about the chair and not a figure about the man. A chair is one of the five things this district protects, and a second chair was refused as a sixth of them on the day it went down. A man who puts tables up is not a keeper of tables and is not a holder of anything, and nobody asked him about the tent and nothing was given to him.

---

The near wheel on that cart had been dragging since before the middle of the ninth month and it was dragging again. He got it up about nine inches off the ground with his right forearm and set it down, and did it again about four seconds later, and got back on the seat.

Nobody in the yard helped him and nobody asked him to. About nine people in the yard watched a man lift a wheel about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and not one of them said one word about it.

The road keeper came up that lane at about four, read the four figures as he went past, got all four, went on up, and did not stop at the low wall, and nobody said one word to him about the eleven miles.

At about four the man with the cart went down that lane and did not come up it again that day. About four people said good evening to him as he went past the end of that table, and he said good evening back, and about four of them said good evening to each other instead. Nobody there said one word to him about the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, which was ruled and was empty at about six, and no name went into it by anybody.

The rest of that day went ordinary. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch before nine, came up out of it at about ten, and went in again after lunch, and his right hand did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in this day. The reed cutter was in that ditch until about half past four and came up the bank wet to the chest, and nobody offered him an arm and he did not ask for one.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid and the count is still five, and no sixth is proposed. Two days' work have gone into things in this district since the sixth of this month and neither of them has been paid for. A day's work is not a toll.

That bid has now been open fifty-four days and was not run today, and nobody in this district has said one word about closing it. The first of the eighth month went past eighty-four days ago. The figure on the second line of that lot book is nine days out of date and was not altered and nothing was written beside it. The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in the yard from about ten until about two and said nothing out loud today, and nobody asked him for anything, and he asked for nothing.

By the time the light had gone the four figures on those boards were the four figures that were on them at about half past eight. A man with a cart had been out on a road and had come up a lane and had not come into a yard, and a wheel had been lifted about nine inches off the ground by one man with one arm in front of about nineteen people, and none of it is a figure about this district and none of it is on a page.
