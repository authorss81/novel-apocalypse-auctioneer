# Chapter 440: A Hand On A Page

On the seventh of the twelfth month the left hand of the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came out from under the boards of that trestle table and lay flat and open on the second line of that lot book, and the figure on that second line was still wrong, and the third line was a date, and there was a stone on the corner of the book, and about nineteen people were in that yard of Lot Seventeen and not one of them said one word about what the hand was doing there.

It was the seventh of the twelfth month and the light came up clean behind that bank and there was no wind at all. By ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

The four figures were read off that wall in the ordinary voice with a man's palm flat on the boards, and all four of them were got, and the word was said with each of them and got four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and forty-eight, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and sixty-four days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and seventy-eight, and three hundred and thirty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and eighty-nine days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet. She did not write the figure itself, because she is going to say it out loud to nine people in the ordinary voice before the light goes, and she has said that out loud to one person, and a clerk who says a figure and writes it and then says it again is a clerk with a habit.

---

The man who mends fencing was at the far end of that table from about half past eight and he did not go to the end of it for three hours. At about half past eleven two people at the near end said out loud that about four of them had been waiting since yesterday afternoon and that he had said in this yard at one the day before that he was going to do something and would not say what it was, and neither of those two people said what they thought it was.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came off the east wall at about a quarter to twelve and stood about nine feet off that table and said one thing out loud, to a few people and not to the rest.

"He is not going to do it because anybody is watching and everybody in this yard knows that and I would like about four of you to be honest about whether you would have said something to him this morning if you had thought he was not going to do it until this afternoon."

Nobody answered him. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked a question there and that nobody answered it, and entered that the record about the not answering says not answered, and that she is not going to enter what anybody would have done.

At about twelve the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and took up the nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat, and he looked at the mender for about a minute and a half and said nothing at all, and a few people at that table said afterwards that they had expected him to say something and that he had not, and that the not saying was the thing.

---

At about half past twelve the mender walked to the end of that table and put his left hand on that book. He put the flat of it on the second line, the whole length of it from the first word to the last, and he did not put it on the third line, and the stone was on the corner of the book and stayed there the whole time.

The hand went down and stayed down for about four minutes. It did not close. The fingers lay open and flat and did not move, and a few people at that table could see the ends of them from where they were standing and nine could not.

Nobody standing there said one word about what the hand was doing there. The clerk of nineteen years did not write anything about it and said out loud to the boy of about nineteen that she was not going to write anything about it, and he said that he was not going to either, and a few people at that table heard the two of them say that and the rest of that ground did not.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch and he came up out of the bank at about ten past one and got the four figures at that end of the table and said the word with them four times, and he stood about two feet from that end of the table and looked at the second line of that book and at the hand on it for about a minute and a half, and then he went back down the bank. That right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day.

Then the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and two people near the east wall heard the number.

"**Nobody is going to say anything about that hand and that is the correct thing to do, and the reason it is correct is that about four of you have been waiting two days for somebody to say something about it, and a sentence said about a hand by the first person in the yard is a rule by the end of the week.**"

Nobody said anything about the hand. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that a hand is not an instrument this district has made and is not the twelfth thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody asked either of them anything. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a hand was put on a book in the open and that nobody asked about it and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and she entered no figure about it and she entered nothing about whether it was better than it had been the day before, and about four people at that table noticed the pencil not moving and one of them said out loud, afterwards, that the pencil not moving was the entry.

---

At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank and stood at that end of the table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm, and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him, and he was not asked for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and he went up the lane. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and nobody said a word about it on any of his visits this month.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and thirty-ninth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and thirty-eight of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the measure of the whole thing in the ordinary voice, in one paragraph, at about half past four, and nine people at that table heard every word of it and the rest of that ground did not.

"**The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved on any of the fifty days. The bid is open ninety-eight days and was not run on one of them. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person, and the fifth is a remedy, and neither of those has been said out loud in this yard this year. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is ruled and is empty. The third line of that lot book is a date and the second line is still wrong on the face of it. The old shelter's charter is still wrong on its face in a book a stranger may walk up to. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and thirty-one days back. The figure on the second line of that lot book is fifty-three days out of date and has not been altered. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and twenty-eight days past. And three hundred and thirty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

She read that out loud to about nine people and did not read it back from the top, and a thing read back by nobody binds nobody, and a few people at that table said afterwards that they had wanted to be asked to read it and that nobody asked them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The word unchecked is in the margin of her own page, in her own hand, over nothing, and has been there since the first day of the eleventh month, and the figure it is written over was checked on the last day of that month and reproduced, and she has not taken the word off and is not going to, and nobody in that yard asked her about it.

---

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight came down it after them and stopped at that table and read the three lines in that lot book standing up, which takes her about two seconds longer than two lines took her, and she read them and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her.

Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall for the last time in that fifty days and got all four of them, and the boy of about nineteen turned his own page over at about ten to six, and a few people at that table looked at the front of it and not one of them said a word about what was on it.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was still in that ditch at about half past five and was in it at about half past four the day before that, and nobody has ever written down what time he comes out.

A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about ten past five with the last of that day's flour on her hip and read the three lines standing up, and said one thing out loud to the stone.

"There is a hand on the wrong line and there is a day under the wrong line and both of those are true and neither of them is a figure." She put her hand on her hip. "I have been coming up this bank for nineteen years and I have never been able to tell you what is in that ditch and I am not going to start guessing at a page, and that is the whole of what I have come down here to say on the last morning of the year this district has had."

Nobody answered her. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered no figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Off the second line at about half past five went that hand, back down to the mender's side, and it stayed there with the fingers open, and the stone back on the corner of the book, where a man moved it about an inch in the middle of the tenth month and then put it back. The second line is still wrong, and the third line is a day and does not say how much water there is in that ditch this morning, and nobody standing there said one word about what the hand was doing there.
