# Chapter 470: A Figure Somebody Hands You

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally gave a clerk of nineteen years a page out of his own flat book on the afternoon of the morning after the morning after the count, in the yard of Lot Seventeen, with a figure written on it in his own hand, and nobody had asked him for it, and a clerk took it and entered it and entered that she is not entering it as a check on anything, and both of those are in the same entry, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it.

It was the same morning the man who puts tables up had walked twenty-two paces in, and the frost had been off the boards of that first table before about nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard, and at the middle of the afternoon the boards of that first table were warm enough that about four people had their hands on them, and the stone on the corner of that book was lying face up on the second table where it had been for two months and about nine people had put a thumb in the hollow in it and not one of them had said how deep it is.

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that at about a quarter to eight before there was anybody else standing there, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, two hundred and fourteen days, and entered that there is no day-count for the sheet and that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet.

That man's four figures were said out loud once this morning and entered once, because a figure a man reads off a wall every morning is not entered into a page twice in one day, and at about four he said them again and got all four.

---

He had been at the east end of that yard since about ten with the flat book under his left arm, and about four people had walked past him about nine times each, and at about half past two he came across to the near end of that first table and put a page on the boards and took his hand off it and did not say anything for about nine seconds.

On that page there was a figure written in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and the figure was thirty, and under it there was a line, and under the line there was nothing, and that was the whole of the page.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud there, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and about nine of them heard all of it and about four of them heard only the number, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy-nine, and he wrote it in the margin of his own sheet outside both of his columns because he had not been given a column to put it in and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted the marks off that board myself on the eleventh of this month going down them, and I counted them again on the last morning of the month while a woman of nineteen was putting a finger on them, and I got thirty both times, and I wrote this morning before I came down, and nobody asked me to count it and nobody asked me to write it down and nobody asked me to bring it over here.**"

Nobody said anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure was given to her, and entered the figure, and entered that she is not entering it as a check on anything, and entered the reason, and entered that the reason is that a check is a figure somebody else runs on something you have both got, and that a figure a man hands you is a figure he chose to hand you, and that she has got a word in the margin of her own page which is unchecked and which is over nothing and which she is not putting on a figure somebody has handed her, and that the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said all of that out loud in this yard on the twenty-eighth of this month and that a clerk agreed with her on the day she said it and is agreeing with her now, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing after that, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man can do a thing twice and get the same both times and that is what makes his figure a figure, and the two of the three in this yard are that, and this one is not, and the reason this one is not is that the other two are trades and I am not a trade, and nobody can ask a man to be a trade for four hours on purpose and get what he got the first time.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a person is not a trade and that a figure a person makes cannot be reproduced by anybody standing there, and entered that the three figures in the open in that yard are a figure in chalk on the edge of a table and a figure in a man's two legs and a figure in a page, and that the third of the three is the first of the three that cannot be checked by anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four people had read that page standing up and about nine of them had not, and a clerk of nineteen years read the entry out loud to one person, which is the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and did not read it to the yard, and about four people have said since that a clerk reading an entry to one person instead of to a yard is a clerk who has worked out that a thing read to about nineteen people binds about nineteen people, and that a thing read to one person binds the one person, and that she chose the one.

---

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale read that page standing up and said one thing about it out loud, to about nine people, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got seventy-five, and about four people heard the number and about nine heard the sentence and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure somebody hands you is a figure somebody chose to hand you, and I said all of that in this yard on the twenty-eighth of this month and a clerk agreed with me on the day I said it, and she is agreeing with me now in front of about nineteen people, and I have never once been thanked for a thing I said in this yard and I have not stopped saying them.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she agreed with the woman on the day the woman said it and is agreeing with her now, and entered that the woman has not been thanked and has not been asked to stop, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about three the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud, and the boy counted what he said and got forty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**She has entered a figure and a refusal in the same entry and the refusal is the only part of it that is about her, and the figure is about him, and I do not know which of the two is going to be harder to keep.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered, in her own hand, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard this afternoon, and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about four people watched him do it and said nothing. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and sixty-fourth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-three of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, and she said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and the record about the not asking says not asked. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that afternoon in one entry and did not read it out loud to anybody at all, and entered that she read one line of it to one man this afternoon and entered the reason, and entered that nobody read it back from the top and entered that nobody is going to.

The bid has been open a hundred and twenty-three days and was not run on this day, and nothing in this yard proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Seventy-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Eighty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and fifty-three days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and fifty-six days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is ninety-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Thirty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and that figure is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and the length of the month the district has just finished living in is thirty, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for how long the month it is living in now is. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a figure handed to a clerk this afternoon is not entered as a thirteenth. Eighteen marks in chalk and twenty-two paces and a figure in a page are three figures this district has in the open, and one of the three can be checked by a stranger with a thumb and two of the three cannot be checked by anybody, and the third of the three is the first of the three in three volumes that nobody standing in that yard can check at all. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and a clerk read a line of an entry out loud in that yard this afternoon to one man, and no name went beside it, because no name was asked for and a clerk did not ask for one and a column is not a place a clerk puts a name she has not been asked for. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

This district has in the open, this month, a figure worn into a stone that no person had to write down, a figure in chalk that one man cannot be paid for, a figure in a man's two legs that his own trade made without being asked, and now a figure on a page out of a flat book that a man nobody had walked over to handed to a clerk in the open, and four of the four are in a yard four hundred miles from any water and a clerk has entered a figure and a refusal in the same line about the last of them, and about nine people in that yard have said since that the district has never had three figures it cannot check and that it now has four, and that nobody in this yard has asked for any of the four and that nobody in this yard is going to.
