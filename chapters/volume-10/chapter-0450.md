# Chapter 450: The Number And The Reason

A clerk of nineteen years entered a number that had not moved in two months and a reason under it, in one entry, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and the count of things this district has made is twelve, and the thing that made it twelve is a table and a stone and nothing anybody can be paid for.

It was the sixteenth of the twelfth month and there was rain in the night again and no rain in the day, and the boards of that second table were wet on the underside and dry on the top, which about four people noticed.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and ninety-eight days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice, all four of them, and got the word with each of them and got the word four times, and the wind took about a third of the second one and he said it again afterwards for nobody.

"**The board carries three hundred and fifty-seven, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and seventy-three days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and eighty-seven, and three hundred and forty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

She wrote it at about eleven. She wrote the number first, the way she has written every number for two months, and then she put her pencil down on the boards of the second table and said out loud that she was not going to write the rest of that line until somebody in that yard had said a reason she would be willing to write under it, which is what she did on the third of the twelfth month and has not done since.

Nobody said anything for about a minute and a half. A few people took that as a refusal and a few people took it as an invitation, and about four people at that table who had been in this yard for two months took it as a thing that had happened once already and would happen again.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said it from the far end of that table with his left hand in its cloth, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he had said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and about nine people heard every word of it.

"**A figure in the open that a stranger cannot see is not a figure anybody can check, and the figure on the underside of that stone has been in the open in this yard under a book for two months and has not been seen by one person, and the reason there is a second table is that a hollow nobody can see is a figure about a book, and a hollow lying face up on a table in the open is a figure about a hollow.**"

The clerk of nineteen years wrote the number and then the reason, in one line, and entered that the reason was given out loud in that yard and not asked for, and entered that a count that moves needs a scene and a reason and both were on the page that morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said out loud that a number in this district is not a number until somebody has said what it counts, and that he had been counting the things this district has made as far as six and had never seen the other five of them, and the clerk of nineteen years told him that she was not going to tell him and that a woman with a list of things other people made is a different thing from a clerk.

He said out loud that he would read it back from the top, and the clerk of nineteen years said no before he had got to the second word of it, and she said why, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got ninety-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to ask anybody to read that back, and I am not going to rule a column for it either. A second reader is nobody in particular, and he is found by whoever is standing there, and he is not found the same way twice, and if I ask one of about nineteen people to read it back this morning then that person is a reader for the rest of his life and I am not going to do that to anybody over a number about a table.**"

Nobody read it back. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man had said a thing out loud there, and entered the thing, and entered that a thing read back by nobody binds nobody, and that a clerk entered a number and a reason and not the hearing of it, and the record about the not reading says not read back.

---

Then she said the whole of the thing out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, and it took her about nineteen seconds, and about nine people at that table heard every word of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got a hundred and thirty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and two people near the east wall heard the number.

"**The underside of that stone is worn one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and that is the whole of the measure. A second stone is on the corner of that book and it has no wear on it at all, and that is the other half of it. The count of things this district has made is twelve and it was eleven on the fourteenth of this month and nobody asked a man to make it twelve, and the reason is a sentence and not a thing, and a sentence costs nothing, and nobody can be made to keep it, and I am entering that last part because it is the part that is going to go wrong first and I would rather it went wrong on my page than on nobody's.**"

She did not read it back from the top. Nobody in that yard asked her to and the boy of about nineteen, who had asked once already, did not ask again, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked her to and entered the reason she gave, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, from the east wall, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**She has just entered the reason for the number under a number on a page in her own hand, and the reason is what I said four days ago, and a thing I said in that yard on the fourteenth of this month is now the reason under a figure on her page, and I am not going to be paid for either of those and neither is she, and that is a sentence and not a thing and it costs nothing.**"

Nobody answered him. The She entered that a man had said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that she has not been paid for the reason and did not expect to be, and entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and entered that a clerk of nineteen years is in a household, and entered that a sentence is not a way to pay anybody, and the count of the five stays at five and no sixth is proposed.

---

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood at the end of the second table and looked at the stone on it and said one thing, and nobody answered him.

"That is the first figure in this district that anybody could put a hand on and get the same answer twice, and I am not going to be the man who says that is a good thing, because the last time this district had a figure it could put a hand on, a man walked down a lane and stood in front of a post and lost two hours of a morning he has been doing for six months."

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in the open there and entered the thing, and entered no figure about the lane, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about four people had already noticed the underside of that second table.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of that bank and got the four figures at the near end of the first table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at the second table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and forty-eighth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and forty-seven of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

The rest of that day went in as three short entries and not as one paragraph, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had entered the day in three pieces on purpose and gave the reason, which is that one paragraph a day is a shape and not a record.

A hundred and seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Sixty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it. Sixty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and thirty-seven days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and forty days back. A body four hundred miles off is seventy-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Sixteen marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and sixteen marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is no figure in this district for how long this month is. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it and no second reader was asked for and none was appointed, and the word unchecked is in the margin of her own page, in her own hand, over nothing.

At about half past five the woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with the two empty buckets and took them up full, and read the three lines in that lot book standing up on the way past, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have now said out loud that they would give a week of the flour they carry to be asked one question about that half second. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them.

Twelve sits at the top of that clerk's page with a sentence under it that anybody in that yard could repeat from memory, and about four of them can, and a sentence anybody can repeat is not a thing anybody is keeping, and she wrote that down this morning in her own hand and did not write down who can repeat it.
