# Chapter 491: The Two Figures Are Named In One Sentence

A man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said out loud, in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twentieth morning after the count, in front of about nineteen people, in the ordinary voice, a sentence with both figures in it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the sentence and entered that it is the first time in five volumes that one sentence has carried a figure a man reads off a wall and a figure nobody had to put down, and entered that the two figures in that sentence are still two figures and the sentence does not make them one, and about four people in that yard have said out loud that the sentence is the truest thing anybody has said in that yard this month and that being true is the thing this district has least use for.

It was the twentieth morning after the count and there was a hard clean light on that ground from before seven and the boards of that second table were warm by nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and thirty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and eighteenth of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and ninety-one, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and seven days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and twenty-one, and three hundred and eighty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He said it at about ten past ten and he had walked down that lane and up it five times in eleven days, and about nineteen people heard the whole of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put a table up in this yard in the first week of this month and I have been walking down that lane ever since, and what I have been walking down there for is that this yard has two figures in it and everybody in it has been talking about one of them or the other one, and I am going to say both of them in one sentence and I am not going to be asked anything afterwards. The board on that wall is three hundred and ninety-one days old and it is one day out from the day it names, and a man read it off there this morning and got it right. The hollow in the underside of that stone is about a book standing still on a table, and it cannot be a day out, and it cannot be checked.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that it is the first time in five volumes that one sentence has carried both figures, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen asked him, out loud and in front of about nine people, which of the two figures he thought the district should keep if it could only keep one, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put a table up and I have not been paid for it and I am not going to be, and that is a third thing in this yard and it is not a figure and I am not going to let anybody make it one. Keep both. That is the whole of my answer and I have said it out loud in front of about nineteen people and I am not going to be asked a second question about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said keep both, and entered that she is not entering that as a rule, and entered that a rule is a sentence a second person can be held to and a man who says keep both in a yard is a man who has said a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of fifty-six said one thing, in the ordinary voice, from about nine feet off the near end of that first table, and about nineteen people heard it, and nobody at that wall had heard him say anything that was not a figure in six months.

"**I have got those four off that wall every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and I have got all four of them every morning, and I have never once been asked whether I was right, and this morning a man said a sentence with one of my four in it and a figure I have never been able to read in it, and he did not ask me anything either, and I noticed that, and I have been noticing things in this yard for six months and I have never once written one down.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has not written a thing down in six months, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He has been counting the days he has read them out loud for about a hundred days and he did not know until this morning that about nine people were counting them with him, and that is the first thing this month that has cost anybody anything and nobody has noticed it except him and me.**"

Nobody said anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally then opened the flat book under his left arm for about four seconds, at the east end of that yard, in the open, with nobody standing near him, and closed it again, and about nine people within four feet of that end of the yard saw him do it and not one of them said a word about it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man opened a book and closed it again and entered no figure about either, and entered that a man who keeps a tally in this district has opened that book in the open in front of about nine people eleven times in five volumes and has never once been asked why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that ditch at about ten and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and a boy of about nineteen standing next to him counted the four and got four and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and a clerk entered that a boy counted a man saying four figures and got four and entered that the count of a man saying figures is a count of a man and not a check on the figures, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten and heard the whole of it, and a clerk entered that he was there and entered that nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way, and there are thirty-six of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and said the four figures at the near end of that first table four times over. At the east end of that yard the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane and read the four figures off that wall on the way past and was not asked about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three, near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm without stopping for anybody in that yard. At about half past three there was a man of about sixty-four at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything, nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-second night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and forty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Ninety-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and seventy-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and seventy-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and ten days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Fifty marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are thirty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those figures moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and one sentence with two figures in it is not a thirteenth. It stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say and the fourth of them is a person, and the reading of that lot did not advance this morning, and nobody in that yard has looked at a fourth line. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it. Six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and it is not paid, and the count of the five is five and nobody in that yard proposed a sixth this morning.

The two buckets came down that bank at about half past five, both of them empty, and went back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall for about a minute afterwards without moving, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that a man who said one thing out loud in a yard this morning for the first time in six months stood at a wall for about a minute afterwards like a man waiting to be asked something and was not.

One sentence was said out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people on the twentieth morning after the count with a figure a man reads off a wall in it and a figure nobody had to put down in it, and a clerk entered that the sentence does not make them one figure, and a man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said keep both and is not going to be asked a second question about it, and a man of fifty-six said one thing out loud for the first time in six months and wrote nothing down, and the two figures are in that yard at the same time now and neither of them has been checked and one of them cannot be, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six.
