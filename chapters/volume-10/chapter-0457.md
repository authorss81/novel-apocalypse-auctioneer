# Chapter 457: One Short And One Long

The clerk of nineteen years worked out a second column on the wall in that yard on the twenty-third of the twelfth month, in the open, in about nine minutes, and a voice at the near end of that table said out loud, before she had finished, that a clerk who finds three errors in a row is a clerk who has made up the rate she is finding them at, and about nine people heard the voice and about four of them agreed with it.

It was the twenty-third of the twelfth month and the wind had gone round to the north-west in the night and was coming down that bank in gusts that stopped and started, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred and five days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote nothing down, and about four people at that table noticed that she wrote nothing down the day after she wrote a difference down.

"**The board carries three hundred and sixty-four, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and eighty days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and ninety-four, and three hundred and fifty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He has got that figure right every morning for a hundred and ninety-one mornings and the third of the four is a day out from the day it names, and he does not know that, and a clerk said it out loud yesterday in a yard he was standing in.

---

The second column she worked out was not one of the four. It was the column on the same board about the mornings a table has stood, and the day it names is the seventh of the seventh month of this year, and the day is written at the head of it in a hand that is hers, because she wrote it herself in the seventh month and about four people have said since that it is the only day on that wall anybody in this district put there.

The number she arrived at was one more than that column carries, and yesterday the number she arrived at on the third of the four was one more than that column carries as well, and about four people at that near end worked the two out loud in about nine seconds and neither of them said the word for it.

"A clerk finding three errors in a row is a clerk who has made up the rate she is finding them at," said a voice at the near end, and the clerk of nineteen years said that she had not made up a rate and had found two, and the voice said that two is three with the third one still to come, and that is how a rate gets made up, and that a clerk who finds what she expected to find has not found anything.

Then the boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, and the clerk of nineteen years entered it and did not answer it.

"**One of them is a day short and one of them is a day long, and a rate a clerk made up would come out the same way twice, and these have not come out the same way, and I have written both of them down and I have not written the word shape.**"

Nobody argued with him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to use the word shape about two columns, and entered the reason, which is that she does not know yet which way the third one is out, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

She then said out loud, in the ordinary voice, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got sixty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and about nineteen people heard all of it.

"**From the seventh of the seventh month of this year to this morning is a hundred and sixty-eight mornings and that column carries a hundred and sixty-nine, and the difference is one day, and this time the column is a day long where yesterday it was a day short, and I am entering the difference as a day and I am not entering a shape.**"

Nobody answered her. The clerk of nineteen years entered that the difference is one day and entered that this column stands and entered that nothing on that wall was altered, and entered that a clerk is not going to enter a pattern on two columns, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was at the end of the second table through all of it and he said one thing at about a quarter past ten, to about four people and not to her, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and about nine people heard it.

"**Nineteen years I have put tables up and nobody in this district has ever asked me how many mornings one of them has stood, and there is a figure about that on that wall and it has been on that wall longer than I have been putting them up, and I have never once wondered about it, and I would like it said out loud that I have never once wondered about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that a column about a table has been on that wall since the seventh of the seventh month, and entered that a man who puts tables up has never been asked about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked. Nobody thanked him and nobody said a word about what he had said.

---

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of the table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about one he came up a second time, stood at that table, and said one thing out loud to about four people and not to the yard, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a figure on that wall about how many mornings a table has stood, and there is a figure on that wall about days, and a man in this district has been putting a mark in chalk on a table every morning for three weeks, and not one of the three of them is about the other two, and that is the first thing I have ever noticed about a board.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that three figures in that yard are not about each other, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about eleven the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud, whether a column being a day long is a worse thing than a column being a day short, and the clerk said that it is the same thing, and that a person in this district is not going to be given a way of choosing which of the two he would rather had, and entered that a question was asked out loud and entered that nothing was proposed, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up out of the lane with his left hand in its cloth and stopped about four feet off the wall and looked at the marks the way he looks at a post, and said nothing at all, and nobody said anything to him, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at that second table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about four people watched him do it and said nothing. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fifty-fifth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-four of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day in one entry and read it out loud to about nine people and did not read it back from the top, and entered that nobody read it back and entered the reason.

A hundred and fourteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Sixty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Seventy-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and forty-four days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and forty-seven days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is eighty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Twenty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and twenty-three marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is still no figure in this district for how long this month is. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it and three people said a thing out loud in that yard today and no name went beside any of them. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them, and about four people at that table have said since that they heard two differences said out loud in two days and that neither of the two was said to the man who reads that figure every morning.

Two columns on that wall are a day out and they are out in opposite directions, and a boy of about nineteen has written both of them on his own sheet and has not written the word for what they are, and about four people in that yard have said out loud that the word is shape, and a clerk of nineteen years has entered that she is not going to use it until she has stood in front of a third column, and there is one more of the three left and nobody knows yet which way it goes.
