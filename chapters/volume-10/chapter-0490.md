# Chapter 490: A Man Reads The Three Lines And Does Not Read One Of Them

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood at the near end of the first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the nineteenth morning after the count and read the three lines in that lot book out loud in front of about nineteen people for the second time in a volume, and read two of them, and did not read the third one, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two lines of a three-line book have been said out loud in a yard and that the third one is a date and a date does not have to be said out loud to be a figure of a man, and about four people in that yard said out loud in the ordinary voice that a man who has read two thirds of a book in front of nineteen people has said more about it than the man who said nothing, and the man himself said nothing at all about that and put his right hand in his coat.

It was the nineteenth morning after the count and the boards of that second table had been dry since about seven and the light came up over the top of that bank about four minutes later than it had come up the day before, and nobody wrote that down, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and thirty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing near enough to hear that have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and seventeenth of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and ninety, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and six days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and twenty, and three hundred and eighty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He did it at about a quarter past eleven and he stood up to do it, which he had not done in that yard for a volume, and about nineteen people watched a man who is the figure of twenty-one years is against put his own hand on a book that is not his and read two lines out of three in about eleven seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and fifteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The first line is a lot and it is the same lot it has always been. The second line is a figure about that lot and it is wrong on the face of it and it has been wrong since the day this book came out and I have known that for ninety-five days. I am not going to read the third one out loud in a yard and I told a clerk of nineteen years on the thirteenth morning after the count that I was not going to, and I have not, and about nineteen people watched me not do it then and about nineteen people watched me not do it again this morning.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not read the third line, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale then said one thing out loud, to about nine people, standing about four feet off him, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You have just done the harder thing and about four people in this yard think you have done the easier thing, and both of those are wrong, and the reason both of them are wrong is the same reason, which is that not reading a line out loud is not a thing anybody can check either. I can stand here and see that his mouth did not move. I cannot stand here and see what he thought.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a line a man did not read aloud is not a figure about the man, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one more thing, to about nine people, in the ordinary voice, and the boy counted it and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to be asked today by about four people why I did not read it and I am telling the yard now, in front of all of you, that I am not going to answer it, and that the reason is that I have not worked out the answer, and that a man who has not worked out an answer is allowed to not say one, and that this is the first time in five volumes anybody has said that out loud in this yard.**"

Nobody said a word. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man is allowed to not say an answer, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody asked him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him and entered that she did not ask and entered that she is not going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing, in about four seconds, at the end of that second table, and the boy counted it and got forty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is the fourth thing anybody in this yard has said out loud this month that they were not going to answer, and none of the four of them is a rule, and all four of them are in the same yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering that as a pattern, and entered that a pattern is a thing two people have to agree on and about nine people in that yard did not agree on anything this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten and heard all of it, and a clerk entered that he was there and entered that nobody asked him whether the man the figure of twenty-one years is against is the man the fifth of the five things this district does not have is about, and entered that she is not going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are thirty-five of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume, and a clerk entered the count and entered that it is a count of marks and not a figure about this morning.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in that day. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing all morning.

The road keeper came up that lane a little after one, got the four figures off that wall as he went past, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about three the cart came up that lane with the near wheel dragging, the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and nobody in that yard stopped him. A man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands when the clerk entered that a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him at about half past three, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-first night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and forty days is how long that bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Ninety-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man and the man has now said out loud in a yard that he is not going to read it. It is ninety-nine days since the rule said out loud in that yard was said, and a clerk entered that figure this morning as a figure about a rule and not about how many days the rule has been kept. A hundred and seventy days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and seventy-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard has watched any of it.

Forty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are thirty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those figures moved today. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and two lines read out loud in a yard is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and it stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person, and the second of the five was named out loud in a mouth seven days ago and is not paid. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk wanted it on the page and did not put anything in it. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, it is not paid, and no sixth has been proposed, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

The buckets went down that bank at about half past five, both of them empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that a woman who has read that book twice a day for a volume read it again this afternoon while a man who has refused to read it for a volume stood nine feet off her doing nothing. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that he was at that wall the whole time and did not look over at the book at any point in the day.

Two lines of a three-line book were said out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people on the nineteenth morning after the count, and the third line was not, and a man said out loud in front of all of them that he was not going to answer the question about why, and a clerk entered that a man is allowed to not say an answer, and nothing was corrected on any of the three lines, and the reading of that lot did not advance, and it is still the fourth of the five, and the fourth is a person, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was empty at about six and will be empty tomorrow.
