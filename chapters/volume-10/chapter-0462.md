# Chapter 462: Three Entries In One Hand

A clerk of nineteen years entered the third of three differences in one entry in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-sixth of the twelfth month, and read that entry out loud once to about nineteen people, and did not read it back from the top, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and a figure a stranger can walk up to and read now exists in a woman's hand in a yard in which about nineteen people were standing.

It was the middle of the afternoon and the light had gone round off the south end of that ground and come back along the face of that bank, and the boards of that first table were dry and cold and the stone on the corner of that book had not moved all day.

At about a quarter to eight, with the yard empty behind her, a clerk of nineteen years entered that four hundred and eleven is the figure on the sheet at that gatepost and did not move, and entered two hundred and eight days as the age of that figure, which is a figure about a figure, and entered that the sheet itself has no day-count and that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet.

The four figures were said out loud once in that yard this morning and a clerk of nineteen years entered them once, and a figure a man says every morning is not entered into a page twice in one day, and the man of fifty-six said them again at about four and got all four of them.

---

She wrote it in two lines and then she read both of them out loud, once, standing at the near end of that first table with the page in her own hand and not on the boards.

"**There are three columns on that wall that are a day out from the day they name. The first is a day short. The second is a day long. The third is a day under one of two ways of counting a day that this district has printed in its own documents, and nothing under the other one, and I am entering the day and I am not choosing between the two ways, and the column cannot be put back on the day it was counted from under either of them.**"

She then said the second half of it out loud, and about nineteen people heard it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**None of the three has been altered. No figure on any page in this district has been altered. There is a mark on that board for every day of this month and there are twenty-six of them and there will be twenty-six of them tonight, and a difference is not a mark, and a mark is not a difference, and I have cut none and I am not going to.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk read her own entry out loud once and entered that she did not read it back from the top, and entered that nobody read it back, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six was at that wall about nine feet off when she read it and he did not turn round, and about four people have said since that a man who was told a thing in that yard this morning spent the afternoon about nine feet from a woman reading it out loud, and that this is the second time somebody in this district has been told something and then had to stand near somebody else saying it again, and that he is not going to be asked about either of the two.

At about half past three the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at that table, and the boy counted what he said and got forty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A finding in a woman's hand is a different object from a finding in a yard, and this yard does not have a place to put a hand, and I am not going to be the man who decides where one goes.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that no place has been made for a hand and that she is not going to make one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

By ten there were about nineteen people in that yard and about four of them had read the page standing up in the four seconds it took, and about nine of them had not read it at all, and nobody read it from the top.

---

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank at about two and a half and stood at the near end of that first table with the mud on him, and he put his own right thumb on the boards about four inches below the second line of that lot book and kept it there for about nine seconds, and about four people at that table watched a man's thumb sit on a book and not one of them said a word.

Then he said one thing out loud, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got eighty-four, and about four people near the east wall heard the number and about nine heard all of it and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is my day under that figure and I have never once been asked to put it there and I am not going to ask her to, and you have all just been handed a number in a woman's hand and there is a number in that book that a man put there with his mouth on the eleventh of this month, and neither of those two numbers is going to be checked by anybody in this yard, and one of them is mine.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the date on the third line of that book is a figure of a man and is not a figure about a day, and entered that a clerk is not going to ask him to put it anywhere else, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past at about half past three and it took her about two seconds, which is about half a second longer on the third line than on the first two, and she said nothing to anybody, and about four people at that near end have said since that a woman who has read those three lines every working day since the day the book came out takes half a second longer on a date than on a figure, and that nobody in this district knows what she is doing that half second about and that she has said nothing about it and is not going to.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman of fifty-eight read three lines standing up and entered that it takes her about half a second longer on the third line, and entered that she is not going to enter what the half second is about, and that the half second is a check that has been run about four hundred working days and has never once been entered, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about four people watched him do it and said nothing. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fifty-eighth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-seven of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her.

---

The first of the two entries is the figures. A hundred and seventeen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Seventy-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it. Seventy-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and forty-seven days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and fifty days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is eighty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything. Twenty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and that figure did not move this afternoon, and a figure about a count of marks is not a figure about a month, and there is still no figure in this district for how long this month is. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

The second entry is four kinds of a thing this district cannot check, and she entered them as four and not as one. A clerk of nineteen years entered the first kind, which is three columns on that wall that are a day out from the days they name, and entered that the three differences agree with each other and are one day each and that none of the three can be put back on the day it was counted from and that nobody wrote down the mark any of them was cut from. She entered the second kind, which is a convention this district printed two ways in two of its own documents about eleven weeks apart, and entered that a column can be made into a difference or into nothing at all by writing one of two words differently, and that she is not choosing and that the choice is not hers. She entered the third kind, which is a date under a wrong figure in a book a stranger may walk up to and read, and that the date is the fifteenth of the tenth month and the date is a figure of a man who was in a ditch and that a figure of a man is not a figure anybody in this district can check, and that a wrong count is not struck by writing a correct count beside it. She entered the fourth kind, which is the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, ruled and empty at about six, and entered that she had read an entry out loud in that yard in front of about nineteen people and had not asked anybody for a name and had not read it back from the top. And she entered that a column nobody has filled and a page nobody has checked are two different things and are not the same thing, and that nobody in that yard is going to be told today which of the four of those is the worse one, and that the four of them have been in that yard for a month and about nine people there have said out loud that they had not known they were four.

Three columns, one convention, one date under a wrong figure and one ruled column with nothing in it are four ways of not knowing, and a clerk of nineteen years has entered all three differences in one entry in her own hand, and the entry is a figure a stranger can walk up to and read, and the woman who wrote it is nineteen, and nobody asked her whether she was going to, and about four people at that near end have said since that a hand is not a column and that the only reason a figure about a hand is a figure anybody can read is that the hand that wrote it is nineteen and everybody standing in that yard can see it.
