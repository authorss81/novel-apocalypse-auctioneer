# Chapter 473: The Sentence Does Not Do That

A man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fifth morning after the count that he had tried yesterday's sentence on three figures that are not the hollow in the stone, and that it did not work on any of the three, and he gave the reason for each of them in his own words, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the three reasons and joined none of them.

It was the fifth morning after the count and there was a low thin sun on that ground that did not get above the bank until nearly ten, and the chalk on the edge of that second table had gone pale in the night where a man had been at it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in it.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as two hundred and seventeen days, and entered that an age of a figure is a figure about a figure and that the sheet has no day-count and always has not had one.

The four figures on that wall were said out loud once in the morning, by the man of fifty-six, and got by him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered them once, and he said them again at about four and got them again.

"**The board carries three hundred and seventy-six, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and ninety-two days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and six, and three hundred and sixty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He put the mark down first, the way he does, in chalk on the edge of that second table at about ten, and he did it with the back of his thumb and he did not say anything while he did it, and there were twenty-one of them in a row along that edge going back to the morning that stone went face up, and about four people in that yard know that figure and about nine do not.

Then he stood with his back to the table and said the thing he had come to say, and he said it in about four seconds, and about nineteen people heard all of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I tried it on my own marks yesterday afternoon and I tried it again on the twenty-two paces and I tried it on the page out of that flat book, and it does not do any of the three, and I would rather say that in this yard this morning than go on carrying it round for a month.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that this is the fifth morning and it is the second, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He gave the first of the three reasons at about twenty past ten and he gave it slowly, looking at the boards, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put these down. That is the whole of the difference between these and that stone and it is all of it there is. Somebody has to write a figure down before it can be a day out, and I have to put mine down, and I put one down every morning whether anybody is standing here or not, so there is a morning in it and a morning can get in, and that sentence has nothing to say to me at all.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud and entered the thing in his own words, and entered that he put a mark in chalk on the edge of that table every morning and that there are twenty-one of them and that she is entering that as a count of marks and not as a figure about a day, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He gave the second of the three reasons at about eleven and he did not look up for it, and the boy counted it and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The twenty-two paces are the other one. A man put those in himself for his own reasons with his own two legs and nobody asked him, and by that sentence those cannot be a day out, and that is right, and I have had two days with it and it has not once told me whether twenty-two paces is a figure. It cannot tell me that. Being the wrong kind of thing is not the same as being a thing.**"

Nobody answered him and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to add twenty-two paces to anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The third of the three reasons came at about half past eleven and it was the one he had come to say and he said it last, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**And the sentence itself is on a page now, and I can put my thumb on that page the same as anybody. So the one thing that came out of yesterday is a figure somebody can check and it is a sentence, and the figures I have been putting down on my own table since the morning that stone went face up are still four things I cannot check, and I am not being given anything for the twenty-one, and I am not going to be.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his thumb on her own page, and entered that she is not entering that as a check, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four people at that near end have said since that a man who keeps a count of marks in chalk every morning and cannot be paid for keeping it was told yesterday morning that a figure nobody has to put down is a good kind of figure, and that he went home and tried it on the one figure he has and it did not hold, and that he came back and said so out loud before anybody could work out that it did not hold for him.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and said nothing at all for about an hour and a half, and nobody asked him whether the sentence was right, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and about four people at that table have said since that he heard all three of the reasons and did not say one word to any of them, and that at about half past one he put his right thumb on the page the clerk had written the sentence on and left it there for about four seconds and took it off again and said nothing.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of that bank at about one and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times. At about half past one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood about nine feet off that table and said one thing, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A sentence that is true of one thing in a yard is not a sentence. You have all had that for two days now and one of the three of you has proved it on his own figures, in his own words, before nine of us, and nobody in this yard is going to write it down as a rule and I am not going to be the man who asks for it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that it is the second time a thing in that yard has been said twice and that she is not entering it as a rule, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the road keeper went up that lane, got the four figures off that wall as he passed, and was not asked about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three, and the near wheel was still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up the lane. A man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of that low wall at about half past three with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands; nothing was offered to him and nothing was asked of him, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and sixty-seventh night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-six of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

She entered the rest of that day in two entries and did not read either of them out loud to anybody at all, and entered that she had not, and entered the reason.

The first entry is figures and none of them moved. A hundred and twenty-six days is how long the bid has been open and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it. Eighty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered and nothing correct stands beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Eighty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and fifty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and fifty-nine days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is ninety-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Thirty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and the twenty-one marks in chalk on the edge of that second table are a different count made by a different hand on a different thing, and neither of the two was added to the other by anybody. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a sentence a man can put a thumb on is not a thirteenth, and a man who has proved that a sentence does not apply to three figures has not made a fourteenth. That lot is still being read and it is not finished, and it has got to the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say and the fourth of them is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and five people said a thing out loud in that yard today and not one of the five has a name written beside it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and what the fifth of them is has not changed either, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count of them has not moved and no sixth has been put in front of anybody.

The second entry is the sentence and the three reasons and the refusals, and she entered that a man had said a thing out loud in that yard and had given three reasons in his own words and that she entered all three and joined none of them, and entered that a sentence about a class of figure is not a sentence a person standing in a yard can use on a figure in front of him, and that one of the three people who said so had said so in the morning and not the afternoon.

About half past five the two buckets came down that bank empty and went back up it full, and a woman of fifty-eight went past that first table reading the three lines in that lot book standing up, exactly the way she does every working day, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. The man of fifty-six came at that wall afterwards and read the four figures off it for the last time that day and got all four of them.

A man put his own marks in chalk on the edge of his own table every morning for three weeks and nobody paid him for them and nobody asked him for them, and yesterday a man who puts tables up told the yard that a figure nobody has to put down is a figure that cannot be a day out, and this morning that same man stood with his back to his own table and told the yard it did not work on him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered three reasons in a woman's hand and joined none of them, and the count of what this district has made is still twelve.
