# Chapter 488: Two Figures, One Entry, Joined By Nobody

A clerk of nineteen years entered two figures in one entry in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the seventeenth morning after the count, a figure a man of fifty-six read off a wall and a figure nobody had to put down, and read that entry out loud once in the ordinary voice to about nineteen people, and did not join them, and entered in the same entry that two figures in one entry is a page with two figures in it and not a comparison, and about nine people in that yard said out loud in the ordinary voice that the one of the two a person can check is the one they would have kept and the one a person cannot check is the one they would have thrown out, and a clerk entered that she does not know which of those two is the reason this yard is standing here.

It was the seventeenth morning after the count and the boards of that second table had gone from cold to warm in about half an hour and about four people had noticed it and not one of them had said anything about it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and twenty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fifteenth of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and eighty-eight, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and four days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and eighteen, and three hundred and seventy-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She wrote it at about half past ten and she said out loud before she wrote it that there were two figures in it, and about nineteen people heard her say that, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are two figures in this entry. The first of them is a figure about a day, and it is a day out from the day it names, and three columns of that wall are a day out, and nobody in this yard has put a newer figure beside any of the three of them, and I am not going to today, and the man who read that figure off that wall this morning read it correctly and he will read it correctly tomorrow. The second of them is a figure nobody had to put down, and it is in the underside of a stone that is lying face up on that table, and it is one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and there is no day in it at all.**"

About nineteen people heard all of that, and about four of them have said since that a clerk has now said out loud in a yard that the two figures in that yard do not agree with each other, and that she said it in about a minute and did not say which one she believed, and that nobody has worked out whether that was on purpose.

Then the boy of about nineteen asked her, out loud and in front of about nine people, which of the two figures she thought was the better one, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to answer that in the yard and I want nine people to have heard me refuse it. I entered both of those figures this morning because I was asked for both of them by about four different people, and I did not join them, and joining them is a thing a clerk can do in about four seconds and it would be the first comparison anybody has entered in five volumes, and the two halves of that entry are one of them out by a day and the other one not having a day in it, and if I write those two on one line then the line says the second one is better and it does not say why, and a stranger can walk up to that page and read that a clerk of nineteen years decided something in a yard on a morning and cannot tell what.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked a question out loud in that yard and entered the question and the answer and the reason, and entered that she is not entering a preference, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said one thing at about eleven in the ordinary voice to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The one you can check is the one you would have kept. I have had that in my hand for about a second and a half in my life and I did not know that was the whole of the difference, and I am not going to say a thing about that in a yard because I have not been paid to be interesting.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not paid to be interesting, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing, in about four seconds, from the end of that second table, and the boy counted it and got thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody in this yard is going to throw either of them out, and that is the first thing I have been able to say in here in five volumes that is not about my hand.**"

Nobody said anything at all. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put no hand on anything while he said it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table at about ten and heard all of it, and a clerk entered that he was there and entered that he put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way, and that there are thirty-three of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume, and that nobody asked him whether two figures in one entry is a page about a man, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten this morning, came up, got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing.

At about half past one the road keeper was on that lane and got the four figures off that wall on his way by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about nine people have said since that a man who has walked that lane since before the twelfth month has not once been asked about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three with that near wheel still dragging behind it, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that lane. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, nobody offered him anything, nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and thirty-eight days is how long that bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Ninety-three days is how far behind that second line is, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Ninety-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. A hundred and sixty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and seventy-one days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Forty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are thirty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and a clerk entered both of those as two different figures and did not add them. The count of things this district has made is twelve and two figures in one entry is not a thirteenth. Nobody advanced the reading of that lot on the seventeenth morning after the count and it stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person. A clerk read one entry out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and no name went into it, and a clerk entered that she read an entry out loud and did not ask anybody for a name and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked. A way to pay a person who is not in a household is the fifth of the five things this district does not have and it is not paid, the count of the five is still five with nothing in it that anybody has proposed as a sixth, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

The buckets came down that bank at about half past five, both of them empty, and went back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at the near end of that first table for about a minute afterwards without saying anything, and about four people have said since that a man who has got in front of that table and a man who has got behind it in the same morning is a thing about the two tables and not about either of them.

Two figures went onto one page of a clerk's book this morning and the page has no mark on it saying which of the two she meant, and about nine people standing within four feet of that table have worked out since that a page with two figures in it and no mark between them is the closest thing this district has come to a comparison without making one, and that the whole of the difference between the two figures is that one of them has a day in it and the other one has not, and that neither of them is a figure about a person and neither of them is a figure about anybody standing in that yard at the time.
