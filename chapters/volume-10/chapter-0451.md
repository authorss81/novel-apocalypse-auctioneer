# Chapter 451: A Figure A Man Reads Every Morning

A clerk of nineteen years stood at that wall for about eleven minutes with her hands behind her back and went down one column with a finger she was not touching it with, and she did not write anything down, and about nine people in that yard watched a clerk not write and did not hear what she was doing.

It was the seventeenth of the twelfth month and the light was white and hard and there was no wind. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and ninety-nine days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet.

The man of fifty-six put his palm on the boards and said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and nobody in that yard has ever once heard him get one of the four wrong.

"**The board carries three hundred and fifty-eight, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and seventy-four days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and eighty-eight, and three hundred and forty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale asked him at about half past nine, out loud and in the ordinary voice and to everybody, what the third one of the four actually counted, because she has been in that yard long enough to know that two of those four are about days and two of them are about something else, and she wanted the third one settled before somebody came along and wanted it settled differently.

"It is days since the twenty-fourth of November on which nobody in this district has entered anything in a book," said the man of fifty-six. "That is a figure about words and not about a date, and it is the one of the four that nobody has ever asked me to explain, and you are the first."

"And is it right."

"It is right this morning," said the man of fifty-six, "the same as it has been right every morning since the middle of the sixth month, and I have got it every morning and I have never once counted the marks behind it, and there are no marks behind that one. It is a figure somebody wrote down once and we have all been saying it out loud ever since."

Nobody answered him. The She entered that a man had had said a thing out loud in that yard, and entered the thing, and entered that a man of fifty-six said that he has never counted a mark against that figure and did not do so this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked. She entered that separately, on the same page, and she entered no figure with it.

---

She was at the wall from about half past nine. The column she went down is the third of the four, and the count on it is a count of days, and a count of days is a figure anybody can take apart if they know the day it was counted from, and the day it was counted from is written at the top of that column in a hand that is not hers and is about four months old.

She put a finger beside the top mark and did not touch it and came back down the column about a quarter of an inch at a time, the way a person walks a fence, and at the bottom she stood still for about four seconds and then went back up it the whole way, and about nine people in that yard watched her do both of those things and not one of them said a word.

The number she arrived at was one more than that column carries.

It has been one more than that column carries for longer than anybody in that yard has been reading that wall, and the same is true of two other columns on it, and there is nobody in this district who can say which day either of the other two is counted from because nobody wrote it down, and a figure cannot be put back on the day it was counted from because there is no day to put it back on.

A man has been getting that figure right every morning for six months. Getting a figure and checking a figure are two different things and about four of the people in that yard have just watched the difference happen about eleven feet from where they were standing.

The clerk of nineteen years did not write the number down. She turned round to the boy of about nineteen, who was watching her, and she said one thing out loud in the ordinary voice and about nine people heard it.

"I have been looking at a column."

"You have not been writing," said the boy of about nineteen.

The boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got a hundred and twenty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and about four people near the east wall heard the number and not one of them heard what she was counting.

"**I have not been writing, and I am going to tell you why I have not been writing, and then you are going to do the rest of it yourself. The rest of it is that a clerk who enters a figure she got by walking a column is entering a figure she got by walking a column, and a figure is not a check on a column because the person who walked it is the same person who reads it off the wall every morning. I found something this morning. I am not going to say what it is in this yard today, and I would be obliged if nobody here asks me about it before I have decided what I am going to do with it.**"

Nobody said anything. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a column was being looked at on this day and entered no result, and entered that she has entered no figure against that column this day and has entered none since the third of the twelfth month, and entered that a clerk is not going to enter a result of a count she made on her own, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up out of the lane at about eleven with his left hand in its cloth and stopped about four feet off the wall and looked at the marks on it the way he looks at a post, and said one thing out loud, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That board has been the second thing in this district that people can walk up to for six months and I have watched a man of fifty-six say the same four off it every morning for six months and I have watched a woman of nineteen years walk a column of it twice this morning with her hands behind her back, and one of those two people is counting and the other one is reading, and nobody has ever told me which of them finds out more.**"

Nobody answered him. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to enter which of the two of them finds out more, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen wrote the day in the left half of his sheet and the page in the right half of it and then went and stood next to the clerk of nineteen years and watched her walk that column, and he counted her pace, about a quarter of an inch a time from the top to the bottom, and wrote the pace down in the right-hand half of his own sheet, and about four people in that yard have since said out loud that a boy who will write down how fast a person walks a column has found the first thing anybody has found about that wall in six months.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy of about nineteen counted how fast she walked a column and entered the pace as a figure about a boy and not about the column, and entered that she is not going to enter the column.

At about one the man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of that bank and got the four figures at the near end of the table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at that table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he read four figures and was not asked whether he had counted anything. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and forty-ninth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and forty-eight of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

She entered the rest of the day in four lines and not in one paragraph, because a clerk of nineteen years entered on the sixteenth of this month that one paragraph a day is a shape, and she is not going to take it back four days later.

A hundred and eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Sixty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and has not been altered.

Sixty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month, and the first day of the eighth month is a hundred and thirty-eight days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and forty-one days back. A body four hundred miles off is seventy-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything. Seventeen marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and seventeen marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is no figure in this district for how long this month is. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them, and he did not know anything about the eleven minutes he had spent not being asked.

The man of fifty-six got that figure right this morning for the hundred and eighty-eighth morning running and it is a day out from the day it names, and there are two other columns on that wall in the same condition, and about nine people in that yard have seen a column walked twice and not one of them has been told what came of it.
