# Chapter 495: A Clerk Reads Two Entries And Does Not Read A Third

A clerk of nineteen years read two entries out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-third morning after the count, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and did not read a third one that was on the same page, and about nine people heard both of the two and about four of them asked out loud which two on the page were the two she had read, and the clerk said out loud that she was not going to tell them, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six that evening, and it was empty at about six the evening before that, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she read two entries out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people and did not read the third one and entered no figure about the third one and entered the reason, which is that a clerk who has not worked out what a thing is cannot read it out loud to make it a figure.

It was the twenty-third morning after the count and the boards of that second table had gone cold again at about four the evening before and the light came up thin and white off the south end of that ground, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and thirty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing near enough to hear that have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and twenty-first of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and ninety-four, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and ten days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and twenty-four, and three hundred and eighty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The first of the two was read at about half past ten and the second at about a quarter past eleven, both standing at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand and not on the boards, and about nineteen people heard the first and about nine heard the second, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said each time and read the number back to himself in a low voice both times.

"**The first is this. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam asked for a thing to be entered and it has been entered, and the thing is that being let is a thing that happened to him, and being let is not a figure and is not a payment and is not a column, and I have entered all three of those on the same line as the thing he asked for, and I want nine people to have heard that so that nobody comes and tells him later that I entered it as something bigger than it was.**"

About nine people heard all of that, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she read the second one, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The second is this. Four people said out loud in that yard yesterday that they had assumed a man had nothing to say, and four other people said out loud that those four were wrong, and I have entered both, and I have not joined them, and a clerk does not join a sentence to a sentence to make one sentence, and a page with two things on it is a page with two things on it. That is the second thing I have read out loud this morning and there is a third thing on this page and I am not going to read it.**"

Then about four people at the near end of that first table asked out loud which two on the page were the two she had read, and the boy counted what was said and got one hundred and twelve and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and the clerk answered them out loud, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twelve and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No, and I am not going to tell you, and the reason is that about four of you would go and look at the third one, and the third one is a thing I put on that page yesterday because somebody in this yard said it out loud and I have not worked out what it is yet, and I am not going to read it out loud in front of about nineteen people to make it a figure, because that is the whole trick and I have watched about four people in this yard do it in the last three weeks and not one of them knew they were doing it.**"

Nobody said anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that four people asked a question out loud in that yard and entered the question and the answer and the reason, and entered that a clerk read two entries out loud and did not read a third, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got thirty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Read the third one tomorrow. If you cannot read it by tomorrow then you have told about nine people that you could not, and that is worth more to this yard than the third one is.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering tomorrow as a plan and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten and heard all of it, and a clerk entered that he was there and entered that nobody asked him whether the third thing on that page is about him, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing, and there are thirty-nine of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and nobody in that yard asked him about the third thing on that page either. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing all morning.

At about twenty past one the road keeper was on that lane, got the four figures off that wall going past, and was not asked about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three, near wheel dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up the lane without stopping. About half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and forty-four days is how long that bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Ninety-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and seventy-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and seventy-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and thirteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Fifty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are thirty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved today. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and two entries read out loud in a yard is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot has not advanced and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person, and that book has no fourth line in it. A clerk read two entries out loud in that yard today and did not read a third, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and no name went into it, and about four people have said since that a clerk of nineteen years has now read something out loud in that yard on four days in thirteen and the column has been empty on all four, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and it has not been paid, the count of the five is still five and nobody proposed a sixth today, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have has now gone ten days without being said out loud in a mouth.

The buckets came down that bank at about half past five, both of them empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that a man who said one thing out loud in a yard four mornings ago said nothing at all this morning and that the yard has worked out by now that a thing said out loud in that yard is a thing that gets said once.

Two entries on one page were read out loud in that yard in front of about nineteen people on the twenty-third morning after the count and a third entry on the same page was not, and about four people asked out loud which two those were and were not told, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk who has not worked out what a thing is cannot read it out loud to make it a figure, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing told her to read it tomorrow, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and no name went into it on the three days of this fortnight so far on which an entry has been read out loud in that yard.
