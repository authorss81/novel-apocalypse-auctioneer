# Chapter 456: One Day, Entered As A Day

The clerk of nineteen years said out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people in that yard on the twenty-second of the twelfth month, that one of the four figures a man of fifty-six reads off that wall every morning is a day out from the day it names, and the man of fifty-six was about four feet off from her with his palm flat on the boards, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about it.

It was the twenty-second of the twelfth month and the ground had gone hard again at the top after two days of rain, and about four people at that table had come down the bank expecting the same morning they had been getting all month.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred and four days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six put his palm on the boards and said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote nothing down this morning, on purpose, because they were the four that were on that wall yesterday as well.

"**The board carries three hundred and sixty-three, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and seventy-nine days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and ninety-three, and three hundred and fifty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He has got that figure right every morning for a hundred and ninety mornings and nobody in that yard has told him there is anything on that wall worth being afraid of.

---

She went back to the wall at about half past ten and stood in front of the third of the four columns with her hands behind her back, and about nine people in that yard watched a clerk stand in front of a column and did not hear what she was doing.

It was the same column she had walked twice on the seventeenth of this month. She had entered on that day that a column was being looked at and had entered no result, and she had said out loud in that yard that she had found something and was not going to say what it was in that yard, and about four people had said since that they had been waiting eleven days to be told and that they were not going to keep waiting much longer.

She put a finger beside the top mark and did not touch it and came down the column the way a person walks a fence, and at the bottom she stood still for about four seconds, and then she went back up it again the whole way, and this time she said the figure out loud as her finger arrived at each mark, because a clerk who says a figure out loud in a yard cannot afterwards say she only thought it.

At the top she named the day the column says it is counted from, which is the twenty-fourth of November of the seventeenth year, written at the head of that column in a hand that is not hers and is about four months old. At the bottom she named this morning.

The boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got fifty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and about four people near the east wall heard the number and not one of them heard what she was counting.

"**From the twenty-fourth of November of the seventeenth year to this morning is three hundred and ninety-four days and that column carries three hundred and ninety-three, and the difference is one day, and I cannot tell you which day the column is a day out from because nobody wrote down the mark it was cut from.**"

Nobody answered her. The clerk of nineteen years entered that the difference is one day and entered that the column stands and that nothing on that wall was altered, and entered that no figure on any page in this district was altered this morning, and entered that a difference is not a correction, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the boy of about nineteen said out loud, to about nine people and not to her, that the third of the four is the third of the four, and she entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four feet from where she was standing there was a man of fifty-six with his palm still on the boards. Nobody in that yard crossed the four feet. Nobody asked him a question and nobody told him a thing and he did not turn round, and about nine people who were standing near enough to see both of them have said since that they stood there and let it happen and that not one of them has been able to give a reason for it in better words than that.

---

By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and a clerk of nineteen years had said one figure out loud twice in one morning, and the first of the two was a figure about a man and the second of them was a figure about a column.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch from about a quarter to eight until about ten and came up out of the bank and got the four figures at the near end of the first table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about eleven the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of the second table with his left hand in its cloth and his right hand flat on the boards, and he was not doing anything, and about four people standing near him said afterwards that they had been watching him not do anything for about as long as it takes a woman to fill a bucket.

He said one thing out loud at about a quarter past eleven, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A difference is a figure about two figures and it is not a day and it is not a column and it is not a job, and I would like it left off that table.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk is not going to put a difference on the end of that table, and entered that a difference is not a correction and not a mark and not a page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale asked at about half past eleven, out loud and in the ordinary voice and to everybody, what a person standing in that yard is supposed to do with a figure they now know is a day out, and nobody answered her, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that a question was asked out loud in that yard and entered the question and entered that nothing was proposed, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about one the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came down the lane, looked at both tables, and went up the lane again, and nobody asked him why he had come down, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past one the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up a second time and stood at that table and said nothing at all. At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at the end of that second table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he stopped for about four seconds at the third of the four and went on up the lane. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and nobody said a word about it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fifty-fourth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-three of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day in one entry and read it out loud to about nine people and did not read it back from the top, and nobody read it back, and she entered that nobody read it back and entered the reason.

A hundred and thirteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Sixty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Seventy-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and forty-three days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and forty-six days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is eighty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Twenty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and twenty-two marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is still no figure in this district for how long this month is, and she wrote the first sentence she wrote on the eighth of this month out loud in that yard and it is the third time she has written it in five volumes, and the second time was a habit, and a habit is a finding. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it and a clerk said a figure out loud in that yard this morning and no name went beside it because no name was asked for. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, which takes her about two seconds longer than two lines takes her, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them.

The difference is a day and it is on a page of a clerk's own, and the column it belongs to stands where it stood, and about four people in that yard have worked out that there are two more columns on that wall that are out by the same amount and that nobody has said so out loud yet, and the man of fifty-six got that figure right this morning for the hundred and ninetieth morning running and it is a day out from the day it names, and he does not know that, and nobody in that yard is going to be the one who tells him today.
