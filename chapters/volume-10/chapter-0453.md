# Chapter 453: Nine Thumbs And A Line Of Chalk

Nine people put a thumb in the hollow in the underside of that stone between about ten and about half past twelve on the nineteenth of the twelfth month, one after another, in front of about nineteen people, and not one of them said a word about how deep it is.

It was the nineteenth of the twelfth month and the air had gone thin and bright and about four people at that table noticed that a thumb in a hollow makes almost no sound, and that nine of them had made that almost-no-sound in a row without anybody saying anything in between.

By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred and one days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six got the four off that wall in the ordinary voice, all four of them, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and the four of them went past the clerk of nineteen years and she did not write any of them down and about four people noticed that she did not.

"**The board carries three hundred and sixty, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and seventy-six days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and ninety, and three hundred and fifty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The first thumb was the clerk of nineteen years herself, at about ten, and she did it without saying anything and she wiped her hand on her skirt afterwards, which is a thing a person does and not a thing a clerk does. The second was the man of fifty-six. The third was the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale. The fourth was the boy of about nineteen, who put his thumb in and then said out loud, to about nine people, that it was the fifth time he had put a thumb in that hollow and that he still could not say when it started, and the man of fifty-six said that he could not either and that they were two people not being able to say the same thing about the same hollow.

The fifth was the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up, who put his thumb in and then said out loud that he had put tables up for nineteen years and had never once been asked to make anything that lasted as long as a hollow, and about four people at that table said afterwards that they had not thought of a hollow as a thing a person could make.

The sixth and the seventh were a woman of fifty-eight and a man the figure of twenty-one years is against, and the two of them did it one after the other without a word between them, and about nine people in that yard have said since that the man put his thumb in at about the same moment as the woman and that nobody could tell afterwards which of the two went first.

The eighth was the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, with his right hand, and he did not look at his own thumb while it was in and he took it out and put that hand in his pocket, and he said nothing at all, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The ninth was the man of about thirty-four who digs loam, at about half past twelve, with the mud still on him from the ditch, and he put his thumb in and wiped it on his thigh and went straight back down the bank, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day.

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud after the ninth thumb, in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard all of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure in this district that a stranger can check has to be checked by somebody standing there, and a figure that nobody has to enter is a figure nobody can check, and those are the same sentence twice and not one sentence, and nobody in this yard has put the two halves of it next to each other before this morning, and I am not going to put them next to each other now, because I do not know which half of it this district wants.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she did not put the two halves of it next to each other and gave the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked. She entered that nine people put a thumb in a hollow in the open and that not one of them said a figure out loud, and entered no figure about the hollow, and entered that a hollow is not a figure and a thumb is not a figure and a clerk is not adding a thumb to a hollow.

Then the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud, and he said it standing at the end of that second table with his bundle on the ground at his feet, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got ninety and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been putting a mark in chalk on the edge of that table every morning since the morning the stone went face up, and there are five of them, and that is a count I made with my own hand on my own table, and I am not going to be paid for it and there is nobody in this district I could be paid by, and I am not asking anybody for anything and I would like it written down that I am not asking anybody for anything.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that a man of about thirty-seven has put five marks in chalk on the edge of that table and that she is entering the number he said and that she is not entering it as a day-count and is not entering it as a line on a board, and entered that a mark in chalk is not a line on a board and is not a toll and is not a rate and is not a price and is not a wage and is not a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and entered that the count of the five things this district does not have is five and that none of them has been paid and no sixth has been proposed, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody thanked him. About four people at that table have said since that they wanted to thank him and that there was no way to do it without turning a mark in chalk into something it is not, and that this is the fourth time in a month that somebody in that yard has wanted to say thank you and could not.

---

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said one thing out loud at about one, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The district's best instrument this month is a hollow in a stone that nobody made on purpose, and there is not one mark in chalk on that table that anybody can check either, and I have watched nine thumbs go into that hollow this morning and I have watched five marks go onto that edge and I would like somebody standing here to tell me which of the two of those is worth having.**"

Nobody answered him. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer it, and entered the reason, which is that a clerk who answers a question nobody asked her is a clerk making a fifth column, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood at that table and said one thing to about four people and not to the rest.

"Nobody put a thumb in that stone yesterday and nobody has put one in it today except about nine of you, and the reason is not interest, it is that there is a hand about nine feet of it in the open for the first time in two volumes, and a hand is the only tool this district has that does not have to be written down first."

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered no figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he stopped for about four seconds and looked at the stone on the second table on his way past and did not put a thumb in it and went on up the lane. At about half past two the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about four people watched him do it and said nothing.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fifty-first night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and fifty of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day in two entries, and the first of them is figures and the second of them is not, and she said out loud which one was which before she wrote either of them.

A hundred and ten days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Sixty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it. Seventy days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and forty days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and forty-three days back. A body four hundred miles off is seventy-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything. Nineteen marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and nineteen marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is no figure in this district for how long this month is. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person.

The second entry is a line and a half long and it is this: the word unchecked is in the margin of her own page, in her own hand, over nothing, and has been there since the first day of the eleventh month, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and nothing went into it, and nine thumbs went into a stone in the open and not one of them went into a column and none of them is going to.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on the way past, which takes her about two seconds longer than two lines takes her, and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at that end of the table have now said out loud that they would rather she said the half second's worth out loud than carry it up that bank and down again tomorrow. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them.

There are five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table and nine thumbs in that hollow and one of those two figures can be checked by a stranger who walks up to that yard and the other one cannot, and about four people in that yard have worked out which is which, and about nine of them are going to find out tomorrow that the count of the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a sentence that is going to be said out loud in that yard, and the man the sentence is about is standing in it with his left hand in a cloth.
