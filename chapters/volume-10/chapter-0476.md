# Chapter 476: Entered As Not A Rule

A clerk of nineteen years entered two sentences in one entry in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the seventh morning after the count, at the middle of the afternoon, and read that entry out loud once to about nine people, and did not read it back from the top, and the two sentences were a figure and a refusal of that figure, and a person who walked up to that page could not tell which of the two was the finding.

By ten there were about nineteen people in that yard and nine of them were still standing in it at four, and it was the middle of the afternoon by then and the light had gone round off the south end of that ground and come back along the face of that bank, and the boards of that second table had been in the sun since about one and the stone lying face up on it had got warm, and the four figures on that wall had not been said out loud again since about half past eight that morning, and a clerk entered that and entered the reason, which is that a figure a man reads off a wall every morning is not entered into a page twice in one day.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud, in front of about nine people and with her hand still flat on the boards, which of the two sentences in that entry was the finding, and the clerk said that she did not know, and entered that she does not know, and entered the reason.

---

She had been at that table since about a quarter to eight and she had written the entry at about half past two and had not read it to anybody until about ten past three, and about nine people were within about nine feet of her when she read it.

"**I have entered two things on one line of this page and both of them are true and I am going to read them both. A man who puts tables up said a thing out loud in this yard four mornings ago and a man who mends fencing said the same thing out loud this morning, and the two of them gave two reasons and the two reasons are not the same reason. That is the first of the two. I am not entering that as a rule, and I am not entering it as a finding, and I am not entering it as a habit, and I have not got a word for a thing said twice by two people who got there two ways.**"

Then she read the second half of it, in the same voice and at the same table, and about nine people heard all of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got one hundred and ten and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The second of the two is this. If I have written down that a figure can be wrong, and have also written down that I am not going to call that a rule, then a person who walks up to this page and reads one line of it has got no way of telling which of the two halves of it I found and which of the two halves of it I am refusing. There is no mark on this page that says which is which and I am not going to put one, because a mark like that is a column and I am not ruling a column today.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk read her own entry out loud once and entered that she did not read it back from the top, and entered that nobody read it back, and entered the reason, and entered that a figure and a refusal of that figure are in one entry and that there is no mark on the page saying which of the two is the finding, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four people at that near end have said since that a page a stranger may walk up to and read is the only thing in this district that is not a figure about a person, and that about two hours ago a clerk put a figure and a refusal of that figure in one line of one, and that there is nothing on it saying which is which, and that the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was the one who asked, out loud, in a yard, which of the two was the finding.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and said nothing at all that afternoon, and about nine people in that yard have now watched him not say anything for two days running, and about four of them have said since that a man who put his own hand into a reason this morning has gone quiet about it, and that nobody is going to ask him why, because he gave the reason on his own and that is a different thing from being asked for one.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank at about half past three and stood about nine feet off that table and read the page standing up for about eleven seconds and gave it back and said one thing, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read a page a stranger can walk up to, and I have done the one thing everybody in this yard does when they read one, which is to decide which half of it is the interesting one, and I decided, and I decided in about eleven seconds, and I have not got a way of telling you what I decided on.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a person reading her page can get a finding out of it in about eleven seconds, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about four with two full buckets and put them down on the boards of that first table to rest her arms, and she read that page standing up in the four seconds it took, and she said nothing to anybody about it, and about four people at that near end have said since that she read it in the same four seconds she reads the three lines in that lot book, and that she is the only person in this yard who reads a page and a book at the same speed.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman read a page standing up and entered that it took her about four seconds, and entered no figure about what she got out of it, and entered that she was not asked and did not offer, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, who had asked the question in the first place, said one more thing out loud at about four, to about nine people, and nobody counted it and nobody wrote it down, and it is set down here because it was said in that yard in front of about nineteen people and about nine of them heard every word of it.

"**I have got about nine people in this yard who can tell me which of the two halves of my page is the one I found, and not one of them can tell me which of the two halves of it I was wrong about, and there is no mark on that page that says so, and a page a stranger may walk up to and read is the only thing this district has got that is not a figure about a person.**"

Nobody asked the woman of fifty-eight what she got out of that page, and nobody asked the man the figure of twenty-one years is against what he decided in eleven seconds, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank at about half past four with the water coming off him onto the boards, read that page standing up on his way past, and it took him about nine seconds, and about four people have said since that it took him longer than it took the woman who reads that book every working day, and that a clerk entered both of those and entered no figure about either, and that nobody asked him to read it a second time, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up out of that bank with the water coming off him onto the boards, got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about one and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that second table and said nothing, and nobody said a word to him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The road keeper went up that lane at about two, took the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got that wheel up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and kept going. A man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall at about half past three with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands; nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

---

Nothing in that afternoon was corrected and nothing in it was filled. The bid has now been open a hundred and twenty-eight days, it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. The figure on the second line of that lot book is eighty-three days behind, nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. The rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month, which is eighty-eight days. It is a hundred and fifty-eight days since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-one days back, and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is ninety-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody has watched any of it.

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move this afternoon or this morning, and its own age as a figure about the figure is two hundred and nineteen days, and there is no day-count for the sheet. Thirty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and twenty-three marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table, and the two counts are two counts. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a figure and a refusal of that figure in one entry is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance this afternoon and it stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person. A clerk read an entry out loud in that yard this afternoon to about nine people and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and nothing went into it, and the same column is named a second time in that entry because a clerk wanted it on the page twice and still did not put anything in it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, there are still five of them, and what the fifth of them is has not changed, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and nobody has proposed a sixth.

The two buckets went back up that bank full at about half past five, and a woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that he said the third of the four the way he says all four, and that this morning a man in that yard said out loud that a hand which cannot close is a figure with no day in it, and that the two of those were about four feet apart at about a quarter to twelve, and that neither of them moved.

Two sentences went into one line of a clerk's page in a yard in front of about nine people this afternoon, and one of them was a figure and one of them was a refusal of that figure, and a woman stood at that table and asked which of the two was the finding, and the woman who wrote it said she did not know.
