# Chapter 474: A Second Reader

A clerk of nineteen years asked a man in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the sixth morning after the count, in the open, in front of about nineteen people, whether he would read a page back from the top to her while she wrote, and he refused her out loud, and gave the reason out loud, and a clerk entered the question and the refusal and the reason in one entry and joined nothing.

It was the sixth morning after the count and there was rain in the night that had gone off the ground by about seven, and the boards of that first table were wet at the near end and dry about four feet along, and by ten there were about nineteen people in it.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and eighteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about a figure and that the sheet itself is not on any day-count that anybody in this district keeps.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about half past eight in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and a clerk wrote them down once, and he said them again at about four and got all four again.

"**The board carries three hundred and seventy-seven, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and ninety-three days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seven, and three hundred and sixty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She did it at about eleven and she did it badly on purpose, which is not the way anybody in that district does anything.

She had two pages open on the boards of that first table, side by side, and one of them was yesterday's and one of them was this morning's, and she said out loud, to about nine people, that she was going to read one of them back from the top, and then she stopped with her hand on the second page, and asked the man the figure of twenty-one years is against whether he would do the other one while she did the first.

About nineteen people heard the question. Nobody answered it for about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted the question and got thirty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Will you read that page back from the top for me while I read this one, and nobody else has to, and I will put down that you did it and I will put down that you did not.**"

He said no, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and he said the reason in the same breath, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A second reader is a job. A job in this district is a column, and you have got four of them and there is not a fifth, and you are not going to rule one and neither am I. And you have not asked me for a favour, you have asked me for a column, and you have asked me for it in front of about nine people so that I could not go home and think about it.**"

Nobody said anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a question was asked out loud in that yard and entered the question, and entered the refusal, and entered the reason, and entered that she is not going to ask him again and that she is not going to ask anybody else in this yard either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the other thing, out loud, standing over both pages, and about nine people heard all of it, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read a thing back from the top to one man once in this yard and to about nine people twice, and not one of those three was from the top to everybody standing in it, and this district has entered that difference before and has not acted on it once, and I am not going to act on it now either. That is the whole of what I am going to do about it.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she read a thing back from the top to one man on one day and to about nine people on two days, and entered that the difference between that and reading it back from the top is a difference about who is standing there, and entered that the district has entered that difference before and has not acted on it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and said one thing at about half past eleven in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got thirty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**She has asked for the only thing that would make a page checkable and she has been told no in a yard, and the man who said no is right, and both of those are this morning.**"

Nobody answered him and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to say that he is right and that she is not going to say that he is wrong, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about twelve with the bundle under his arm and stood at that second table for about four seconds and put his own hand flat on the boards about nine inches from where the other man had been standing, and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud at about one, from the east end of that yard, with the flat book shut under his left arm, and about nine people heard all of it and about four of them did not, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and fifteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A second reader is the thing I have been doing for about thirty years and I have never once been asked to do it twice. A reader somebody is paying for is not a check. A reader somebody is not paying for is a favour. This district has not got the money for either of those, and that is not why I would say no. I would say no because the four words at the foot of her page have been the same four words every morning since the first of the ninth month, and a second reader would have to sign them, and there is nowhere in this district for a name to go.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing in his own words, and entered that she is not entering any of it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and nobody asked him what he had in his hand at the end of the morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard from about one with the flat book under his left arm and a clerk walked over and stood about four feet off him for about a minute and a half and asked him nothing, and entered that she asked him nothing, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about four people watched him do it and said nothing. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and sixty-eighth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-seven of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Nothing in the rest of that day was altered and nothing in it was filled. A hundred and twenty-seven days is how long the bid has been open and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Eighty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Eighty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and fifty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is ninety-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Thirty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are twenty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure was added to the other. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man who has refused a job is not a thirteenth and a page nobody reads back from the top is not one either. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person, and it did not advance this morning and it was looked at for about nine seconds by a woman of nineteen and that is the whole of what happened to it. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and a clerk read one page back from the top to nobody at all this morning, and no name went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them.

A woman of nineteen asked a man in a yard for the one thing that would have made a page in her own hand checkable by somebody standing beside her, and he said no in front of about nineteen people and gave her a column as the reason, and a clerk entered the question and the answer and did not rule a fifth column, and about four people at that near end have worked out since that she asked the right question in a yard where nobody has ever asked it, and that the answer was also the right one, and that both of those being right at once is the whole of what has happened in that yard in a month.
