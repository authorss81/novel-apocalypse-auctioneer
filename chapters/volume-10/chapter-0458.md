# Chapter 458: Two Conventions And No Choice

The third column the clerk of nineteen years took in front of about nineteen people in that yard on the twenty-third of the twelfth month did not come out the same way as the other two, and about four people at that near end worked out before she had finished that she could have settled it in one sentence, and she did not settle it, and a boy of about nineteen left the fourth line of his own sheet empty for the second time in eleven days.

It was the middle of the afternoon and the wind had gone back round and come down that bank in one long push, and the boards of that first table were wet at the near end and the corner of that book was wet and the stone on it had not moved.

Before there was anybody else standing there, at about a quarter to eight, a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered that figure's own age as a figure about the figure at two hundred and five days, and entered that no day-count exists for the sheet itself and that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet.

The four figures were said out loud once in that yard this morning and a clerk of nineteen years entered them once, and about four people at that table have said since that a figure a man says every morning does not get said into a page twice in one day, and the man of fifty-six said them again at about four and got all four of them.

---

The column is at the foot of that board and the day it names is written at the head of it, and the same day is written at the foot of it, and the two are not written the same way.

At the head of the column the first of the fourth month is written with the day counted in. At the foot of the column the first of the fourth month is written with the day not counted in. About four people in that yard can read both and about nine can read one, and a clerk of nineteen years said out loud, in the ordinary voice, that the two of them were written by two different people about eleven weeks apart and that neither of them knew there was a column.

"**I can give you a difference of one day off the head of that column, and I can give you no difference at all off the foot of it, and both of those are the same column, and the only thing that has gone wrong is that a day is written two ways in two documents this district wrote, and I am not going to choose between them, because choosing one is a repair and nobody in this yard has asked me for a repair.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that both conventions are printed, and entered that she is not choosing between them, and entered that the column cannot be put back on the day it was counted from under either convention, and entered that a figure can be right and be a day out at the same time and that the only way anybody standing here could tell which one this is is a convention nobody wrote down, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four people at that near end said, all together and not to her, that she could pick the one that has been in use since the fourth month, and that it would take one sentence, and that a sentence would settle it and there would be three columns out of three instead of two out of three. She said that it would, and entered that it would, and entered that a clerk who picks a convention is a clerk who has decided that this district meant one thing and not the other, and that a district that has not written down which of the two it meant has not decided, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine of them then tried to settle it between themselves without her, and it took them about eleven minutes and it did not work, and the reason it did not work is that about three of the nine said the head of that column is written with the day counted in and about six of the nine said the foot of it is, and both of those people had read that column within the last month, and nobody in that yard has written down which of them read which end.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got seventy-four, and about four people heard the number and about nine heard the sentence and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nine people have just told me which end of a column they are sure of and there are two answers and the two of them have both been said with the whole chest, and that is not a convention anybody invented this morning, that is what a convention is, and I am not going to be the woman who decides which nine of you is right about a thing none of you can check.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nine people gave two answers about which end of that column is written which way and that both answers were given as certainties, and entered that a certainty is not a figure and that two certainties are not a convention, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of fifty-eight came up that bank at about twenty past one with a bucket in each hand and put them down and read the three lines in that lot book standing up with the corner of it wet, and it took her about two seconds, which is about half a second longer on the third line than on the first two, and about four people at that table noticed that she put her thumb on the third line and wiped it on her apron afterwards, which is a thing a person does and not a thing a clerk does, and she said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with a flat book under his left arm and nobody walked over to him, and he was not asked for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of the second table with his left hand in its cloth and he said one thing at about half past two, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got fifty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A document that writes a day two ways has not got two figures in it, it has got one figure and a question, and the question is about the writer and not about the day, and I am not going to be the man who says which writer meant what.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a question about a writer is not a figure about a day, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen wrote the first two differences down on his own sheet in the left-hand column, one under the other, and then he put the pencil on the fourth line he ruled on the nineteenth of this month and held it there for about half a minute, and about four people at that table watched a boy not write, and he did not write, and he put the pencil in his pocket and said out loud that he was not writing it because there was nothing on that line yet and that he would rather have an empty line than a line with a question on it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy of about nineteen has left a line of his own empty for the second time in eleven days, and entered that she is not going to ask him what the line is for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen and about four of them had watched a woman walk a column twice with her hands behind her back, and about nine of them were still standing at that first table at two in the afternoon, and the one sentence that would have settled the third column was in four of their mouths and in none of their hands. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank at about three with the mud still on him and said nothing to anybody, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about four people watched him do it and said nothing. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fifty-fifth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-four of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, and the corner of it was wet and the stone on it had not moved, and she said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that afternoon in two entries, and she said out loud which one was which before she wrote either of them.

The first entry is figures. A hundred and fourteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Sixty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it. Seventy-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and forty-four days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and forty-seven days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is eighty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything. Twenty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and there is still no figure in this district for how long this month is.

The second entry is a line and a half and it is this: that a clerk was asked out loud in that yard whether one sentence would settle a column, and that a clerk said it would, and that she is not going to say the sentence, and that a column which can be settled in one sentence and is not settled is a column this district has decided nothing about, and that a decision this district has not made is not a clerk's to make. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and a clerk, a woman and a man said a thing out loud in that yard on one day and no name went beside any of them. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

One of two words, written differently, is the whole of the third column, and a clerk of nineteen years has entered both ways of writing it and has not entered a shape, and the two columns above it on that wall are a day out in opposite directions, and about four people in that yard have said out loud that this is the first time in five volumes that the district has been asked a question it could answer in a sentence and has said no in a mouth, and nobody in that yard is going to pay her for saying no, and she is not going to be paid for saying no.
