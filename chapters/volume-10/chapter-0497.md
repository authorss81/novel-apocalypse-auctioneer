# Chapter 497: The Third Thing Is Read Out Loud And Is Not A Figure

A clerk of nineteen years read the third thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fifth morning after the count, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and about nine people heard all of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she has read it and that it is a short sentence on her own page and that about nine people asked her to read it and that she had said on the day before that she could read it and had not read it, and entered that the third thing is a sentence a man said out loud in that yard on the twentieth morning after the count and that a sentence is a figure about a sentence and not a figure about anything, and the man who said it was four feet away and was not asked whether it was about him.

It was the twenty-fifth morning after the count and there was no wind at all in that ground and the light came up clean off the south end of it and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on that sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and thirty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that there is no day-count for the sheet and never has been one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing near enough to hear that have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and twenty-third of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and ninety-six, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and twelve days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and twenty-six, and three hundred and eighty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She read it at about half past ten, standing at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand and not on the boards, and about nine people heard all of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got ninety-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk read a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

"**The third thing is this. A man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said out loud in this yard on the twentieth morning after the count that being true is the thing this district has least use for, and I entered it on this page on the day after he said it, and I have not known since then what it is, and it is a sentence about a sentence and not a figure about a book, a stone, a table, a board, a man, a hand, or a day.**"

About nine people heard all of that and about four of them have said since that a clerk of nineteen years read a page out loud in a yard and told about nine people that the thing on it was a figure about nothing in particular, and that this is the first time anybody in this district has read a thing out loud in a yard and put it in that category.

Then the boy of about nineteen asked her, out loud and in front of about nine people, whether a figure about a sentence was a figure at all, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I do not know and I have written on this page before today that I do not know, and a figure that cannot be checked is not a figure I can argue about either, and the two of those are the same shape and one of them has a man in it. What I can tell you is that it is on a page, and that means about nine people can read it tomorrow who cannot read it today, and that is the whole of what a page does in this district and it is not nothing.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked a clerk a question out loud in that yard and entered the question and the answer and the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was four feet away and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was four feet away and entered that nobody asked him whether that sentence was about him and entered that she is not going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He said one thing out loud, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said that sentence in a yard five mornings ago and I have walked down that lane three times since and about four people have not said one word about it and about four people said one word about it on the day, and I have worked out what I think about that and I am not going to say it in a yard.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard, entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing, at the end of that second table, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I told her on the twenty-third morning after the count to read it or say she could not, and she took a day, and she said she could not, and this morning she read it, and the yard has not worked out which of those three things was the right one and I have not either.**"

Nobody answered him and nobody said a word. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and entered that nobody in that yard had worked out which of the three of those things was the right one.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten and heard all of it, and a clerk entered that he was there and entered that he put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table in the ordinary way and that there are forty-one of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume, and the record about the not asking says not asked. Out of that ditch at about ten came a man of about thirty-four who digs loam, and he got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times. At that east end there was a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally, with the flat book under his left arm, and he said nothing.

About half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles by about nine people standing within hearing. The cart came up at about three with that near wheel dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop. A man of about sixty-four was sitting at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and he had been there since about half past three, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and forty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. One hundred and one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and seventy-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and seventy-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Fifty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are forty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a sentence read out loud in a yard is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot stands this morning where it has stood since the second of the twelfth month, at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person, and nobody in that yard advanced it. A clerk read one thing out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and no name went into it, and a clerk entered that she read a thing out loud in that yard and did not ask anybody for a name and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, it is not paid, the count of them is five and there is no sixth of it, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have was named out loud in a mouth on the thirteenth morning after the count and has not been paid since and has not been said out loud since either.

The two buckets went down that bank at about half past five, both of them empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall for about half a minute, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that he has stood at that wall for half a minute on three mornings out of the last six and that a clerk read a sentence about a sentence out loud in that yard this morning and the man who read four figures off a wall every morning stood there while she did it.

The third thing on that page was read out loud in that yard on the twenty-fifth morning after the count in front of about nine people and it is a figure about a sentence and not a figure about a book, a stone, a table, a board, a man, a hand or a day, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had said on the day before that she could read it and had not, and that the man who said the sentence was four feet away and was not asked whether it was about him, and a clerk may not enter that a figure which cannot be checked is better than what came before it, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and a name went into it on no day of this fortnight.
