# Chapter 494: A Man Asks The Yard To Put A Thumb Down

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of that ditch at about ten on the twenty-second morning after the count and said out loud, in the yard of Lot Seventeen, in front of about nineteen people, in the ordinary voice, that he was going to put his thumb in that hollow in the underside of that stone, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who has been in that ditch every morning for three weeks and who has not been asked anything by anybody in five volumes said one thing out loud on the twenty-second morning after the count, and about four people in that yard said out loud in the ordinary voice that they had assumed that man had nothing to say, and about four other people said out loud that the four people who had assumed that were wrong, and a clerk entered both of those and joined neither.

It was the twenty-second morning after the count and the boards of that first table had gone from wet to dry in about an hour and the stone lying face up on the second table had been cold under about four hands at about nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and thirty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing near enough to hear that have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and twentieth of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and ninety-three, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and nine days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and twenty-three, and three hundred and eighty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He came up out of that bank at about ten with the mud on him to the knee and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that second table first, the way he does, and then he said it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and thirteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in that ditch every morning for three weeks and I have come up out of it about two hundred times and nobody has ever asked me one question, and I have not been angry about it, and I want to say that out loud before I say the other thing, because about four people in this yard have been careful with me and about four people have not and both of them have been for about a month. I am going to put my thumb in that hollow this morning and I am telling about nineteen people before I do it and not one of you has to do anything.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said he has not been angry, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He put it in at about a quarter past ten, and it went in about as far as a thumb goes, and he took it out again and wiped his hand on his thigh, and about nineteen people watched a man who has not said anything out loud in that yard for three weeks put a thumb in a hollow in a stone and say nothing about how deep it is.

Then he said the second thing, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is a figure about a book standing still on a table and it is a figure about a man who has been in a ditch every morning and about four people have told me in the last two months that it cannot be about me. I am not saying it is about me. I am saying that I have been in here every morning for three weeks and about four people have been letting me not say anything about it, and that being let is a thing that happened to me, and I would like it entered that being let is a thing that happened to me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man asked for a thing to be entered and that she has entered it, and entered that being let is not a figure and is not a payment and is not a column, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, to about nine people, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Being let is the thing every one of us in this yard has been doing to somebody else for a month, and I have been one of the four, and I am saying so in front of about nineteen people and not to one person, and that is the only way I can think of to say it that is not a complaint.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman entered herself among four people and entered that the four are not named, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody here has ever asked me whether I wanted to be let either, and I have wanted to be asked about once, and I have not got round to saying so, and I am not going to now, and a man who has not got round to it in five volumes is not going to get round to it in a yard.**"

Nobody said anything at all. A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table at about one and heard all of it, and a clerk entered that he was there and entered that nobody asked him what he thought of being let, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table in the ordinary way and there are thirty-eight of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume, and a clerk entered that a man has put thirty-eight marks in chalk on the edge of a second table in thirty-eight mornings and has never been asked for one of them and has never been paid for one of them. At the east end of that yard a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles and nobody has asked him in a volume. At about three the cart came up with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and nobody in that yard said a word to him. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and about nine people within four feet of him were busy letting each other not be asked, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and forty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Ninety-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and seventy-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and seventy-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and twelve days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Fifty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are thirty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved today. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a thing that happened to a person is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance on the twenty-second morning after the count, and it stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and nothing went into it, and the record about the not asking says not asked. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, and that the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and that the count is five and no sixth is in it, and that six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

At about half past five both buckets came down that bank empty and were carried back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that a man who reads four figures off a wall every morning stood about nine feet off a man who came up out of a ditch and put a thumb in a hollow in a stone, and that the two of them did not look at each other and neither of them looked away.

A man who digs loam in a ditch every morning for three weeks came up out of it on the twenty-second morning after the count and said one thing out loud in front of about nineteen people and asked for a second thing to be entered, and what he asked to have entered was that being let is a thing that happened to him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it and entered that it is not a figure and is not a payment and is not a column, and about four people said out loud in that yard that they had assumed that man had nothing to say and about four other people said out loud that those four were wrong, and a clerk entered both and joined neither, and a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale entered herself among the four in front of about nineteen people and not to one person, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six.
