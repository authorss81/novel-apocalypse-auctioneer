# Chapter 487: The Second Stone, Named Out Loud

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not put the second stone on the corner of that book on the sixteenth morning after the count, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the corner of that book has had a stone on it for two months and a day and that the stone on it this morning has nothing on it, and entered that a figure about a thing that has not happened yet is a figure about a day this district has not counted, and about nine people in the yard of Lot Seventeen said out loud in the ordinary voice that the second stone is the only object in that yard that cannot be checked by anybody and cannot be wrong by anybody either, and both of those are the same thing and neither of them is a comfort.

It was the sixteenth morning after the count and the wind came down that bank at about nine and went away again by about eleven, and the boards of that first table had the first of the day's sun on them at about ten past nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on that sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and twenty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that there is no day-count for the sheet and never has been one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people near enough to hear him have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fourteenth of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and eighty-seven, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and three days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seventeen, and three hundred and seventy-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The stone had been on that corner since the fifteenth of the twelfth month and it had been on the boards of that second table before that, and nobody in that yard had looked at the underside of the new one, because there was nothing on it to look at, and about four people had tried.

The first of them was the boy of about nineteen, at about ten past nine, and he turned it over twice and put it back the way it had been and said one thing out loud to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have turned this stone over twice and I have put my thumb on both sides of it and there is nothing on either side, and I can tell you how deep the other one is with my thumb and I cannot tell you anything at all about this one with anything, and those are the same job and one of them works.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy has put a thumb on both sides of a stone that has nothing on it and entered no figure about the stone, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The second of them was the man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart, at about a quarter past ten, on his way up that lane, and he stopped, which he does not do, and he turned it over once with one hand and put it back, and the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said out loud, to about nine people, that a man who drives a cart up that lane every morning of his life has just turned over a stone he has never looked at twice, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said that out loud on purpose and I am not going to explain it and there is nothing in it for anybody to use. I have been looking at that corner of that book for a fortnight and this morning there was a man come up that lane who has never once stopped at anything in this yard, and he stopped, and I want nine people to have heard that, and that is the whole of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who drives a cart stopped on that lane and turned a stone over and went on up it, and entered no figure about either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said it, and he said it in about four seconds, and it was the shortest thing anybody said in that yard all morning, and the boy counted it and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That stone is doing a job and the job is holding that corner down in a wind, and I put it there because the wind took hold of it on the fourteenth of the twelfth month and about four people saw the page lift twice before I did anything, and there is no figure on it and there is not going to be one, and I have not been paid for it and I am not going to be.**"

Nobody said anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has not been paid and is not going to be, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put his right hand flat on the boards of the second table beside that stone and took it off again, and about nine people have said since that a man who carried his left hand in a cloth for a volume put a hand on that table twice in one morning and neither time was on a stone, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what the two times were.

Nobody asked him about the two times. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man put a hand on the boards of that second table twice and entered the two figures and entered that neither of them was on a stone and entered that she is not going to say what the two were for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten and heard all of it, and a clerk entered that he was there and entered that nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing, and there are thirty-two of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume, and nobody asked him whether the thirty-second of them was a figure about this morning or about him.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came down that lane at about half past four, looked at the two tables and at the corner of that book, and went back up the lane, and nobody asked him why he had come down, and a clerk entered that she did not ask and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and nobody walked over to him.

The road keeper came up that lane at about twenty past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went past it, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about anything else either. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drove it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody this morning either. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was sitting at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and thirty-seven days is how long that bid has stood open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Ninety-two days is how far behind the figure on that second line is, and it has not been altered and nothing correct stands beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Ninety-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and sixty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and seventy days back, it is open, and nothing has been pulled off it and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard has watched any of it.

Forty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are thirty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those figures moved today. The count of things this district has made is twelve and a stone that has nothing on it is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance, and it stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of those is a person. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk wanted it on the page a second time and did not put anything in it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still unpaid, and the fifth of those five, a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count of the five is five and there is no sixth of it in anything anybody has said in that yard, and six things stand there that are not a payment and not one of the six is one.

The buckets came down that bank at about half past five, both of them empty, and went back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that he was about nine feet off a man turning a stone over and did not look over.

Four people put a thumb in that hollow in the underside of the other stone yesterday and three of them put a thumb in it today, and a clerk entered the two numbers as two numbers about two days and entered that she is not entering either of them as a figure about a stone, and the word *unchecked* is in the margin of her own page in her own hand over nothing, and it is on no figure. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six to come and put a thumb in anything, and he did not, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
