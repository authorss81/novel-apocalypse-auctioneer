# Chapter 455: A Day

A man of about thirty-four said out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people in that yard on the twenty-first of the twelfth month, that he is afraid of a day, and nobody in that yard agreed with him, and nobody asked him for it a second time, and a boy of about nineteen counted what he said and put the count down on his own sheet and did not read it back to himself.

It was the twenty-first of the twelfth month and the sleet had come down properly in the night and gone, and the ground was hard on top and soft underneath, and about four people had come down that bank expecting the same morning they had been getting all month.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred and three days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote nothing down this morning, on purpose, because they were the four that were on that wall yesterday as well.

"**The board carries three hundred and sixty-two, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and seventy-eight days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and ninety-two, and three hundred and fifty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He has got that figure right every morning for a hundred and eighty-nine mornings and he does not know that there is anything in this yard worth being afraid of and nobody in that yard has told him.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of the second table at about half past ten with his left hand in its cloth and his right hand flat on the boards, and he was not doing anything, and about four people standing near him said afterwards that they had been watching him not do anything for about as long as it takes a woman to fill a bucket.

Then he said it.

He did not raise his voice and he did not stop in the middle of it and he did not look at the clerk of nineteen years while he was saying it, and about nineteen people in that yard heard every word of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he had said and got a hundred and thirty-seven and had the number written on his own sheet in the margin outside his two columns before the man had finished.

"**I am afraid of a day. Not the figure on the sheet at that gatepost, which has not moved and which I am not afraid of, and not how old that figure is as a figure about the figure, which is a figure about a figure and not about anything. A day. Every figure in this district is a multiple of a day and I have spent five volumes making sure that nobody else in this basin has to know that, and I am the one who has to know it, and I put the first figure of mine into a book that anybody may walk up to and read, and a figure in a book that a stranger may read is a figure I have to keep, and there is no way to stop keeping it.**"

Nobody said anything. A She entered that a man had said a thing out loud in the open there and entered the thing, and entered that he was not asked for it again and was not asked for it once, and entered that nobody in that yard agreed with him and that she is not entering that anybody disagreed with him either, and entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he is afraid of a figure has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He did not read that number back to himself in a low voice, which about nine people at that table have told him to do every time he counts anything, and about four people near the east wall heard the number and about nine heard the whole of the sentence, and the clerk of nineteen years noticed that he had not read it back and did not say one word about that to him.

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy of about nineteen counted what a man said and entered the figure and entered that the count is a figure about the boy and not about what the man said, and entered that the count is in the margin of his own sheet and not in either of the two columns he ruled on the twelfth of this month, and entered that nobody has been asked what to do with it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody asked him for it again. That is a fact about the afternoon and it is the whole of the afternoon, and about nine people in that yard have said since that they had about four sentences ready and that none of them of the four was a fair question, and that the man who said the thing did not appear to want a sentence from anybody.

---

By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and the four figures on that wall had been said once already that morning, and a clerk of nineteen years had not written them down.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch from about a quarter to eight until about ten and came up out of the bank and got the four figures at the near end of the first table and said the word with them four times, and he stood at about two feet from the man who had spoken for about a minute and a half and said nothing, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about eleven the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one more thing out loud, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice, because about nine people had told him to go on doing that and he had said he would.

"**A day is the only figure in this district that cannot be written on a page and cannot be taken off one. I am not asking for anything and nobody is going to give me anything, and I have said it once and I am not going to say it again.**"

Nobody answered him. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man had said a thing out loud in that yard, and entered the thing, and entered that he said he was not going to say it again, and entered that nobody asked him to say it again, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank at about twelve and stood about nine feet off that table and said one thing, and nobody answered him.

"That is the first time in six months a man in this yard has said out loud what a thing in it is going to cost him, and he has not asked anybody to pay any of it, and I am not going to be the man who starts."

A clerk of nineteen years entered what that man said, in her own hand, and entered that he had not asked anybody for anything and that nobody had offered him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard from about ten until about one with a flat book under his left arm and nobody walked over to him, and he was not asked for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and it took her about two seconds longer than two lines takes her, and she said nothing, and nobody there said one word to her. At about one the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at the second table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and nobody said a word about it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fifty-third night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-two of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day in one entry and read it out loud to about nine people and did not read it back from the top, and nobody read it back, and she entered that nobody read it back and entered the reason.

A hundred and twelve days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Sixty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Seventy-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and forty-two days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and forty-five days back. A body four hundred miles off is eighty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Twenty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and twenty-one marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is still no figure in this district for how long this month is, and she wrote the first sentence she wrote on the eighth of this month out loud in that yard and it is the third time she has written it in five volumes, and the second time was a habit, and a habit is a finding. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. Five marks in chalk on the edge of the second table, entered as a man said them and not as a day-count. Nine thumbs in a hollow, entered as nine and not as a figure about the hollow. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it and a man said a thing out loud in that yard this morning and no name went beside it because no name was asked for. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the man it is about was standing in that yard all morning with his left hand in a cloth and has not been asked about it and did not say anything about it.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, which takes her about two seconds longer than two lines takes her, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and that half second is the only thing in that yard today that nobody has asked about and that somebody has already said out loud. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them.

He went back to the fence he had not finished at the north end of that ground and mended a post, and the boy of about nineteen did not read the number back to himself in a low voice, which is the first time in fourteen days that he has not done that with a number he counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had not been asked what he was going to do with it.
