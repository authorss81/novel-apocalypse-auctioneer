# Chapter 463: The Last Day Of This Month

A clerk of nineteen years told about four people in that yard on the twenty-seventh of the twelfth month, in the ordinary voice and out loud, that the last day of this month is the thirtieth, and that nobody in that yard had ever been told, and then entered that she had said a date and not a figure, and entered that she was not going to say how many days the month has, and then entered that she is going to count something in three days at that wall in the open.

It was the twenty-seventh of the twelfth month and the wind had come back round to the north-west again in the night and was coming down that bank in gusts that stopped and started, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred and nine days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about the third of them, and nobody is going to, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she is not going to.

"**The board carries three hundred and sixty-eight, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and eighty-four days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and ninety-eight, and three hundred and fifty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He has got that figure right every morning for a hundred and ninety-five mornings and one of the four he says out loud every morning is a day out from the day it names, and he knows it now, and he got it again this morning, and he has not said one word about it and nobody is going to ask him for a second time.

---

She waited until about eleven to say it, and she said it to about four people and not to the yard, and about nine of them heard it anyway, and she said it standing at the near end of that first table with her hand not on the boards.

"**The last day of this month is the thirtieth. Nobody in this yard has ever been told that, and I have known it since the first of this month and I have not said it, and I am saying it now and I am not saying how many days this month has, because I do not know, and because a clerk who says the date of the last day of a month and then says how many days it has has entered a figure in a mouth, and I have been telling this yard since the eighth of this month that a figure somebody entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she said a date out loud in that yard and entered that she said a date and not a figure, and entered that the length of this month is not a figure on any page in this district, and entered that saying a date is not entering a figure and that about four people heard the date and that no figure was given to any of them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said out loud, to about four people, that he has stood in that yard every day this month and has known for three weeks that this month was going to end and has had no idea when, and that a man who puts tables up for a living is asked about a date before he is asked about anything, and that nobody in this yard has asked him one.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has not been given a figure and has not asked for one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen wrote the date on his own sheet in the left-hand column, which is the column that is a day, and he did not write anything in the right-hand column, which is the column that is a page, and about four people at that table have said since that a boy who ruled those two columns himself on the twelfth of this month has now put a day in one of them on his own and left the other one alone, and that he did it without being asked and read nothing back to himself.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy of about nineteen wrote a date on his own sheet and entered that she did not ask him what he wrote it for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the last thing of that morning, out loud, and about nineteen people heard it.

"**I am going to stand at that wall on the thirtieth of this month, in the open, in daylight, with about nineteen people standing behind me, and count the marks off that board from the mark for the first of this month to that morning's mark, and name both days, and enter the figure the count gives. I am entering that I am going to, and that is the first plan I have entered in five volumes, and a plan is not a promise, and I am not going to say out loud which day I am going to count, and I have just said the date of the last day of the month and that is a different thing and I am not going to be asked to make it the same.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she is going to count marks off that board in the open on the last day of this month and entered that she has entered no figure and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about half past twelve the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got thirty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Three days is a short time to be right in, and it is the only time anybody in this district will ever get, and I am not going to stand here and wish it were longer.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came down the bank and stood at the east end of that yard with his flat book under his left arm and nobody walked over to him and nobody asked him for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood about nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat and said one thing.

"A date is the easiest thing in this district to say out loud and it is the only one of them nobody has to be given a second reader for, and that is not because it is true, it is because nobody has ever been hurt by a wrong date in a yard."

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and that he is not going to be asked whether a wrong date has hurt anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at that second table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fifty-ninth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-eight of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

The rest of that day went in as one entry at about ten past five, and she read it out loud to about nine people and stopped there and did not read it back from the top, and entered that nobody read it back and entered the reason.

A hundred and eighteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Seventy-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Seventy-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and forty-eight days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and fifty-one days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is eighty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Twenty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and twenty-seven marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is still no figure in this district for how long this month is, and a clerk said the date of the last day of the month out loud this morning and entered a date and not a figure. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it and a clerk entered a plan in that yard today and no name went beside it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and on her way past that first table the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. The man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time in that day and got all four.

A yard in which nobody had ever been told when a month ends was told this morning by a woman of nineteen who entered a date and no figure, and she has entered that she is going to stand at that wall in three days and count something in front of about nineteen people, and about nine of them have said since that they did not know they were going to be asked to stand there until this morning, and that the only notice anybody in that yard got was a date, and that a date is not a figure and cannot be checked and did not need to be.
