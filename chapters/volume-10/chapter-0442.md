# Chapter 442: One Inch In The Middle

A man of about thirty-four put a cloth back on his left hand at about ten past nine in front of about nineteen people, and the hand went into it and did not come out, and nobody asked him why it would not close, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

It was the ninth of the twelfth month and there was a low grey sky and the light came off the south end of that ground without ever getting warm. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and ninety-one days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet.

He read the four off that wall with his palm flat on the boards and got all four of them, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and about four people at that wall heard the four and went straight on with what they were doing.

"**The board carries three hundred and fifty, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and sixty-six days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and eighty, and three hundred and forty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He has been doing that since the middle of the sixth month, and nobody has ever asked him what he does at the other end of a day, and a clerk may not enter what he does at the other end of a day.

---

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about ten with her hip against the boards to rest it, and she put her hand flat on the top of that lot book for about a second, and then she said it out loud to the yard and not to anybody in it.

"Two months. I have been waiting two months for about four of you to thank the man who mends fencing and nobody has and I am not going to either, and I am not saying that to get a thank out of him, because I would be saying it to get a thank out of him and you can all hear that I would be."

Nobody disagreed with her. That is the thing about it. About nine people at that table heard the whole of what she said and not one of them said the opposite and not one of them thanked him, and a few people afterwards said out loud that they had been waiting to see whether anybody would, and that watching nobody do it was the thing that had happened.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the far end of that table. He did not say thank you and he did not say that she was wrong about the two months. What he said was one sentence, and he said it to the boards.

"**She is not going to do it so that I do not have to, and I would rather it was said out loud than not said at all, and it is a thing about about four of you and not a thing about her.**"

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody thanked anybody, and entered that she is not entering what anybody in that yard was thinking, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

Then he got the cloth out of his inside pocket with his right hand and shook it open, and put his left hand into it, and worked the cloth down over the hand with two fingers of the right, and the fingers went in flat and stayed flat and the cloth came off at the wrist, and that is what the hand does now and has done since the eleventh of the June.

The man of fifty-six watched that happen from about six feet off. Nobody asked him anything about his own hand either, which is fair, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a hand went into a cloth in the open at about ten past nine and that a clerk may not enter that a hand is better than it was and did not enter it, and entered that going into a cloth is not a recovery and is not a cure and is not a return, and entered that a stone is not a cure and a table is not a cure and a figure is not a cure, and that she is not going to list what is.

The boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got fifty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and the clerk of nineteen years looked at him and said nothing, and he said nothing, and about four people near that end of the table heard the number.

---

At about half past ten the man of fifty-six came to the far end of that table on his own, and nobody had asked him to, and he put his own thumb into the hollow on the underside of that stone, which was still face up in the right hand of the man who mends fencing, and he had to be shown it, and the man who mends fencing turned the wrist about two inches and did not hand it over.

The man of fifty-six pressed in and took it out and pressed in again and then stood there with his thumb wet and grey on it and his face doing the thing a face does when a person is trying to work backwards out of a thing.

"I cannot say when it started," he said. "I have been reading figures off a wall in that yard since the middle of the sixth month and I have never once in my life counted how deep anything is."

Nobody asked him to. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six put his thumb in a figure and that he said out loud that he could not say when the figure started, and entered that nobody asked him to, and entered that a figure anybody can feel is a figure nobody has to enter and that a clerk is not going to enter that either of those is the better of the two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

She entered the measure of it again in the ordinary voice, once, because a clerk may enter a figure as many times as a clerk can stand to say it out loud.

"**The underside of that stone is worn one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and that is a figure about a book standing still on a table and it is not a figure about a day and it is not a figure about a hand and it is not a figure about a man.**"

---

The stone went down on the ground at about eleven, face up, by the side of the boards and out of the way of the boots, in the one place on that ground where about four people had to step over it every morning to get to the end of the table. It was put down by the right hand of a man of about thirty-four and nobody gave him anything for putting it down and nobody was thanked and nobody said he had done a kindness.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that an object was put on the ground in that yard in the open and that a clerk is not entering what it is and is not entering what it is not, and entered that a stone is not a lot and is not a document and is not a place and is not a column and is not a figure anybody can be shown to have entered, and entered that a figure in the open in a yard that a stranger can walk up to and touch is a figure about a thing and not about a person, and that the district does not have one of those and has had this one since the book went out.

At about half past twelve the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank and got the four figures at the near end of the boards, and stepped over that stone going in and stepped over it coming out, and did not look down at it either time, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day.

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood about nine feet off that table and looked at the thing on the ground for about as long as it takes the man of fifty-six to read four figures and said nothing at all, and a few people at that table said afterwards that they had expected him to say something and that he had not.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about half past two the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and nobody said a word about it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and forty-first night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and forty of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day in one paragraph at about half past four, and nine people at that table heard every word of it and the rest of that ground did not.

The bid is open a hundred days and was not run on this day and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Fifty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and it has nothing written beside it, and there is a date under it. Sixty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has been standing since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and thirty days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and thirty-three days back. A body four hundred miles off is sixty-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything, and a clerk may not enter that anybody was waiting for it. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. Nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and nine marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is still no figure in this district for how long this month is. The word unchecked is in the margin of her own page, in her own hand, over nothing. Nothing went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and it was empty at about six. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household.

Nobody read that back from the top. A thing read back by nobody binds nobody, and a few people at that table said afterwards that they had wanted to be asked to read it and that nobody asked them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her.

The book on the other end of that table is lying uncovered with nothing on the corner of it, and the stone is on the ground by the boards where the boots go, and about four people have stepped over it this morning and not one of them has put a thumb in it.
