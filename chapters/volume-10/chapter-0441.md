# Chapter 441: A Figure Nobody Had To Put Down

A stone was on the corner of that lot book at about half past eight with the rough side of it up, and about nine people at that end of the table had gone past it that morning without picking it up, and nobody in that yard had ever turned it over, and at about half past eight of that morning nothing had happened yet.

It was the eighth of the twelfth month and the light came up clean behind that bank and there was no wind at all in that ground.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. She entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and ninety days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six put his palm flat on the boards and read the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and forty-nine, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and sixty-five days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and seventy-nine, and three hundred and forty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He has done that every morning since the middle of the sixth month without a day missed, and nobody in that yard has ever asked him what he does with the other half of a morning.

---

The boy of about nineteen came off the boards with his own page in his hand and asked his question in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people, and that yard did not go quiet. It went ordinary, the way a yard does when a person asks a thing that everybody has been carrying.

"May I ask a thing that is not about the four figures."

"You may say a thing out loud," said the clerk of nineteen years, without looking up from her page. "You may not rule a column for it."

"How many days has this month got."

The man of fifty-six turned round from the boards. He is a slow man with a grey coat and he has the look of somebody who has been interrupted in a thing he does forty times a day and is not in a hurry to go back to it.

"I have read that board every morning since the middle of the sixth month," he said. "I have got four figures off it every morning and I have never been wrong about one of them in your hearing or out of it. I do not know how many days this month has. Neither does anybody standing in this yard. That is not a figure about a board. It is a figure about a month, and nobody has counted the month, and I am not going to stand here and invent one because a boy of nineteen has asked me a fair question."

Nobody said anything for about as long as it takes to fill a bucket. The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said one thing out loud from the east wall, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he had said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A month is the shortest interval this district has any evidence for, and the length of this one is the last figure in the calendar nobody has counted, and a man of fifty-six is about to have to read two of his four off a length that has never been counted by anybody. He is not wrong. There is nothing in this yard to be right with.**"

She entered that a man had said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered no figure about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she entered, in her own hand, on the same page, a second thing, and she said the second thing out loud before she wrote it, which she has done twice in five volumes.

"**A figure somebody entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day, and that is the third time I have written that sentence in five volumes. The second time was a habit. A habit is a finding.**"

Nobody answered her. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked a question in that yard on this morning and that she had not been asked it and that nobody had answered it for her, and entered that she entered her own sentence and not the answer to his, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The boy of about nineteen did not go over to the man of fifty-six and the man of fifty-six did not go over to the boy, and a clerk may not enter which of them was being careful.

---

At about a quarter to nine the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up out of the space under that trestle table on his knees, and about four people near the far end stepped back to let him get his shoulders through, and he was carrying a stone about the size of two fists together in his right hand and his left hand was in the cloth it has been in since the eleventh of the June.

He had been under those boards for about as long as it takes a woman to fill a bucket, and nobody had asked him why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He put the stone down on the boards edge up, and got the cloth out of his left hand with his teeth, which took him about four seconds, and folded it once and put it in his inside pocket, and stood there with the hand open and flat and not closing.

Then he put his right hand on the stone on the corner of that lot book and turned it over.

Nobody in that yard had ever seen the underside of a stone. That is a thing you can say about about four people standing there and it is not a figure about anybody, because the man who turned it was not in the yard when the book went out and neither were the other three, and the stone has been on that corner through two volumes of people putting things on tables and taking them off again.

The underside is worn. It is worn one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and the wear is smooth and grey and slightly dished, the way a thing goes when a heavy book has stood in one place on it and moved about an inch each time the book was moved and not one inch each time it was set down.

"That is a figure about a book standing still," said the clerk of nineteen years. She had not been asked. "It is not a figure about a day and it is not a figure about a hand and I am entering it as a figure about a book standing still, and if anybody in this yard has a better word for it they can say the word out loud and I will enter the word instead."

Nobody said a better word. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a stone that has been on the corner of a book has an underside and that the underside is worn one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and entered that a wear is not a mark and a mark may not carry a job, and entered that a stone is not a document and is not a book and is not a lot and is not a place and is not a column, and entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost did not move and that there is no day-count for it.

She did not enter that the hand was better than it had been the day before, and a clerk may not enter that.

---

By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and the four figures on that wall had been read off it once already that morning, and the stone was face up in the right hand of a man at the far end of that table.

At about ten the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank with the water to his thigh and got the four figures at the near end of the boards and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. He stood about two feet from the far end of that table for about a minute and a half looking at the underside of a stone, and then he went back down the bank without saying one word about it.

At about half past ten the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank with the last of that day's flour on her hip, read the three lines in that lot book standing up, and put her thumb in the hollow of the stone without saying anything about it either, and went back up the bank.

At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank and stood at that table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm, and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him, and he was not asked for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and he went up the lane. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and nobody said a word about it on any of his visits this month.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fortieth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and thirty-nine of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of the day in one paragraph at about half past four, and nine people at that table heard every word of it and the rest of that ground did not.

The bid is open ninety-nine days and was not run on this day and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person and the fifth is a remedy. The figure on the second line of that lot book is fifty-four days out of date and has not been altered and has nothing written beside it. The rule said out loud in that yard on the tenth of the tenth month is fifty-nine days standing. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and twenty-nine days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and thirty-two days back and was not named in that yard this morning. A body four hundred miles off is sixty-eight days past a printing it did not make and nobody watched anything. There are eight marks cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and eight marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and she is not entering a length. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said one thing out loud at about five, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A stone on a corner of a book is a thing four of you have walked past for two months, and one of you has just turned it over with a hand that will not close, and neither of those is going on a page, and I am saying that out loud so that about nine of you can hear that I know it.**"

Nobody answered him. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that nobody answered him and that she is not going to enter what anybody would have said, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up, which takes her about two seconds longer than two lines takes her, and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her.

Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them, and the boy of about nineteen turned his own page over at about ten to six, and a few people at that table looked at the front of it and not one of them said a word about what was on it.

The lot book is lying on that table with nothing on the corner of it at all, and the stone is face up in the right hand of a man of about thirty-four who is standing at the far end of the boards, and nobody in that yard has said one word about the stone or about the hand or about the inch.
