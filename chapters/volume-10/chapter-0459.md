# Chapter 459: A Difference And A Cloth

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the end of the second table on the twenty-fourth of the twelfth month and read a clerk's page from the middle with his right hand flat on the boards and his left hand in its cloth, and he said one thing out loud in about four seconds, and the thing was about what a difference is, and about nine people heard it and not one of them said one word about the two things being in the same yard.

It was the twenty-fourth of the twelfth month and the boards of that first table were dry again after the wind of yesterday and there was a smell off the ditch, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred and six days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote nothing down, and about four people at that table have said since that she has not written one of the four down for three days running and that they have not worked out whether that is a rule or a habit.

"**The board carries three hundred and sixty-five, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and eighty-one days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and ninety-five, and three hundred and fifty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He has got that figure right every morning for a hundred and ninety-two mornings, and two of the figures on that wall are a day out from the days they name, and he does not know that, and nobody in that yard has told him.

---

The clerk of nineteen years had her page face up on the corner of that second table from about half past nine with the corner turned back, the way a person leaves a page they want read and does not want held.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up out of the lane at about ten, went to the end of that table, put his right hand flat on the boards, and read the page from the middle. He did not turn it over. He read it the way he looks at a post, which is the way a person looks at a thing to see whether it is standing true, and he got to the end of it and did not put his hand in his pocket and did not take the cloth off his left hand.

He said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got forty-five, and about four people near the east wall heard the number and about nine heard the whole of it and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A difference is a figure about two figures and it is the only one of them that says a thing is wrong without saying what is right, and I have not asked her to put it on that table and I am not going to.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has not asked for a difference to be put on that table and that she is not going to put one there, and entered that a difference is a figure about two figures and is not a correction and is not a mark and is not a page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then he said nothing else at all, and about four people at that near end have said since that a man had a clerk's page in front of him with a difference on it and a stone lying face up on the same table with a figure worn into the underside of it that nobody had to enter, and that he looked at the page for about two minutes and then looked at the stone for about four seconds, and that nobody has been able to say which of the two he was looking at when he stopped.

The hand stayed in the cloth. It came out on the eighth of this month for the turning of a stone and it did not close, and it went back into the cloth, and it has not been out of that cloth since except on that morning, and about nine people in that yard have watched it every morning since and not one of them has asked him why it will not close, and the record about the not asking says not asked. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the hand is in the cloth and entered that she is not going to enter that it is better than it was, and entered that she is not going to enter that it is worse than it was either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about eleven the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale stood at about four feet off him and said out loud, to him and not to the yard, that there is a figure worn into that stone that no person in this district had to write down and that it is the only one anybody can check with a thumb, and that there is a difference on a page a stranger may walk up to and read, and that the two of them are lying about nine inches apart.

He did not answer her and about four people heard her and about nine did not, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk is not going to put a wear and a difference next to each other in a page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day.
At about half past ten the boy of about nineteen asked out loud, to about nine people, whether a difference entered on a page can be un-entered, and the clerk of nineteen years said that it cannot, and that nothing on any page in this district was altered this morning and that nothing has been altered on any page in this district since the fifteenth of this month, and that a clerk who could take a figure back off a page would not be a clerk, she would be a person with an eraser and no other job.

Nobody answered her and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a question was asked out loud in that yard and entered that the answer carries no figure and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past one the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came down the lane, looked at the two tables, and said one thing to about four people, that a page with a corner turned back is not a table and a table is not a page and that he is not going to move either of them for anybody, and went up the lane, and nobody thanked him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at that second table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and nobody said a word about it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fifty-sixth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-five of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day in one entry at about ten past five and read it out loud to about nine people and did not read it back from the top, and entered that nobody read it back and entered the reason.

A hundred and fifteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Seventy days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Seventy-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and forty-five days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and forty-eight days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is eighty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Twenty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and twenty-four marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is still no figure in this district for how long this month is. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it and a man read a page standing up this morning and his name did not go into it because no name was asked for and a clerk did not ask for one. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them.

A figure that had to be entered is a figure somebody can be afraid of, and a figure nobody had to enter is a figure nobody can be afraid of on anybody's behalf, and the two of them were lying about nine inches apart on the same table all morning, and a man with a cloth on one hand read one of them and did not look at the other one for long, and nobody in that yard asked him which of the two he had come to look at, and a clerk entered what he said and entered no question and entered that she is not going to ask him a second thing about it.
