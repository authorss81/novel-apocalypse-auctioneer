# Chapter 482: What A Figure He Cannot Check Is Worth

A man of fifty-six said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twelfth morning after the count, and it was the first thing he has said in that yard that was not a figure, and it settled nothing and could have settled two things, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it and did not read it back from the top, and nobody asked him a second question.

It was the twelfth morning after the count and the light came up white and hard off the south end of that ground and there was no wind at all, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and twenty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count.

The four figures on that wall were said out loud once in the morning and got by him, and entered once by a clerk, and he said them again at about four and got them again, and about nine people standing near enough to hear him have said since that he said them the way a man says four things he has known since breakfast.

"**The board carries three hundred and eighty-three, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and ninety-nine days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and thirteen, and three hundred and seventy-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He said it at about ten past eleven and he said it standing at that wall with his back to the yard, and about nineteen people heard all of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure I cannot check is worth exactly what a figure I can check is worth, right up to the morning somebody does check it, and on that morning it is worth nothing, and there is no third thing it is worth in between that I have ever found. I have read four figures off this wall every morning since before the middle of the sixth month. Two of the four I could walk over and look at. Two of the four I could not. I have known which is which for six months and nobody in this district has ever once asked me.**"

Then he turned round and looked at the ground about four feet in front of him and said the last of it, which was shorter, and the boy counted it and got thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You have all spent a month not asking me anything and that was the right thing to do and I am not saying it was not. I am not going to say anything else today.**"

Nobody said anything at all. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he said it with his back to the yard, and entered that nobody asked him a question and that she is not going to ask him one and that nobody in that yard is going to ask him one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

She did not read the entry back from the top to anybody, and entered that she did not, and entered the reason, and about four people at that near end have said since that a clerk read an entry out loud to nine people three days ago and did not read this one to anybody at all, and that the difference between those two is a man standing at a wall with his back turned, and that a page in a clerk's hand is a page a stranger can walk up to and read, and that this one has got a thing in it that somebody in this yard said once.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and he did not look over at that wall for about nine minutes afterwards, and about four people at that table have said since that a man looked at the ground for nine minutes and then went back to work, and that nobody in that yard is going to ask him what he was doing during them.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about twelve with the last of that day's flour on her hip, read the three lines in that lot book standing up, and said one thing out loud to about nine people, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He has just told nine of us that he knows which two and which two, and he is the only person in this yard who does, and none of us is going to ask, and that is correct, and I want it entered that it is correct and that we are all going to spend the rest of our lives in this district not asking, and that is a different thing from not knowing.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank at about half past twelve and stood about nine feet off that first table and said one thing, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a man in this yard who can end two arguments before lunch and he has just told about nineteen people he can, and the price of that is a yard that agreed sixteen days ago not to ask him anything, and there is no way to buy him out of that and I am not going to try and I would not take it if I could.**"

Nobody thanked him, nobody answered him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard, and entered the thing, and entered that he is not going to be thanked this morning and is not going to wait to be thanked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past four with two full buckets, put them down on the boards of that first table to rest her arms, read the three lines in that lot book standing up in about two seconds, said nothing to anybody, and picked the buckets up again and went on up the bank with them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman read three lines standing up and entered no figure about it and entered that she was not asked and did not offer, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that near end have said since that a woman who reads that book every working day walked past a man who had told about nineteen people that morning that he could end two arguments before lunch, and did not stop, and that nobody in that yard has said which of the two of those is the more use.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and put a thumb in the hollow on the second table on his way past and said nothing. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that second table and said nothing to anybody, and nobody asked him what he made of a man of fifty-six giving a sentence away for nothing, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and was not spoken to.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles, and this time he stopped for about four seconds and looked at the man of fifty-six standing at the wall and then went on up the lane. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and seventy-fourth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-three of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and thirty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it. Eighty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Ninety-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and sixty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-six days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Forty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are twenty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a sentence a man of fifty-six said once is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and four people said a thing out loud in that yard today and no name went beside any of the four. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

A second reader was not asked for and was not given and the entry of that stands where it was entered, and a clerk entered that she did not ask for one today and that nobody offered her one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that he said them the way he has said them for two hundred and ten mornings and that this morning was the first of those in which he had said anything else in that yard at all.

The man in this yard who reads four figures off a wall every morning is the only person in it who could tell anybody which two of the four he could go and look at, and this morning he told about nineteen people that he could and told them nothing about which, and about nine people have worked out since that the sentence was worth more than either of the two things it could have settled, and about four of them have said out loud that it was, and nobody in that yard is going to ask him which two.
