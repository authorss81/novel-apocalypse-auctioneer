# Chapter 445: The Day Under The Figure

The clerk of nineteen years asked a man of about thirty-four a question in the open at about half past ten in front of about nineteen people, and she had gone at it sideways for about eleven days and she went at it straight this morning, and the man gave her a day out loud and nobody had asked him for it.

It was the eleventh of the twelfth month and the light came up hard off that bank after two days of rain, and the ditch at the back had gone down about two inches since yesterday and had not gone down to a foot.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and ninety-three days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet.

The man of fifty-six read the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and fifty-two, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and sixty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and eighty-two, and three hundred and forty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and the clerk of nineteen years had been one of them since before there was anybody else standing in it, and she had gone at that question sideways for about eleven days.

She asked him at the top of that bank, and she asked it standing about four feet off him and not close enough to touch him, and she wrote it down afterwards and not before.

"Were you in that ditch until about half past four on the fifteenth of the tenth month."

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk of nineteen years asked a question in that yard and entered the question, and entered that she had wanted to ask it for eleven days, and that a day count is a thing a clerk wants very badly and wanting it is not a reason to ask it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He stood in the mud of the bank with the spade over his shoulder and looked at her for about as long as it takes to fill a bucket, and about nine people at that table stopped what they were doing, and about nine other people did not stop anything at all.

"I was in that ditch until about half past four on the fifteenth of the tenth month," he said, "and I came up the bank on my hands and my knees and the water came over me at the top, and I have been in that ditch on and off since the middle of the ninth and I have never once been asked which day it was by anybody in this yard."

Nobody said anything. The clerk of nineteen years entered the day, and entered that she entered it because a man said it out loud in the open, and entered that nobody counted a mark and that there is no mark on any board anywhere that belongs to that day, and entered that the entry is hers and that a clerk's entry about a day a man said is a figure of the man and not of the ditch, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the reason out loud before she wrote the second line of it, which is the fourth time in seven days.

"**A clerk may not enter that a man's day is a figure, and a clerk may not price it, and a day's work is not a toll and there is no holder in this district for a toll to be paid to, and I am not going to put a price on a day that a man spent in a ditch on the fifteenth of the tenth month, and nobody in this yard is going to pay me for asking him the question.**"

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the last part of what the yard had not said, and he said it to the ground and not to her, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he had said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I was in there for a day and the day is a figure of me and not of the water, and the water is in that ditch and I am not, and a clerk is not going to put a price on it and neither is anybody else, and that is the whole of what I have got to say about the fifteenth of the tenth month.**"

Nobody answered him. She entered that a man had said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that he has not been paid and cannot be and that a clerk is not entering that as anybody's fault, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The book on that table has three lines in it and the third of them is a date, and the date on it is the fifteenth of the tenth month, and the man who has just said that date out loud is the man who was standing in the water on it.

> One lot. The figure on the second line above stopped being true on the fifteenth of the tenth month.

She did not go to the end of that table. She stood where she was and looked at the book from about four feet and did not go to it, and about four people at that end of the table noticed her not going to it and about four of them did not.

"You are not going to write anything on that book," said the boy of about nineteen, out loud, and he was not asking.

"No," said the clerk of nineteen years. "There are three lines in that book and there is no fourth line, and I am not taking the third one out and I am not putting a figure under it and I am not putting a name beside it, and if a date under a figure turns out to be the day a man spent in a ditch then that is what a date under a figure is, and a date under a figure was put there to be read by a stranger and not by the man who was in the water."

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy of about nineteen had said a thing out loud in that yard, and entered the thing, and entered that she did not write on the book, and entered that the third line was not altered and not struck and not superseded, and entered that the second line is still wrong on the face of it, and that nothing correct has been written beside it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about eleven from the far end of that table, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got sixty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and about four people at the east wall heard the number and nobody heard the whole of it.

"**A public record in this basin has been a record of a thing for nine volumes, and it has got a date under a figure in it, and the date is a man, and there is no way to take the man out of it and leave the date, and I am not going to be the one who says that is a fair price for a stranger's morning.**"

Nobody answered him. The She entered that a man had said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to enter that the rule has been improved, and did not enter it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about half past twelve the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood about nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat and said one thing to about four people and not to the rest.

"Two men in this district know what that date is and one of them said it out loud this morning and one of them gave it to a woman in the same yard eight weeks ago, and neither of those two men has been asked anything, and I am not going to be the one who asks either."

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered no name, and entered that the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about one the man of about thirty-four who digs loam went back down that bank with the water to his thigh and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at that table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and he was not asked whether the fifteenth of the tenth was the day he had said, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and forty-third night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and forty-two of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day a few minutes short of five, and read the second half of it out loud to about four people and not to the rest, and the reason she gave was that the first half of it was about a man and a man does not need a yard to hear about himself.

The bid is open a hundred and two days and was not run on this day and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Fifty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and a clerk entered this morning a day a man said out loud, and entered that entry is on her own page and not on the book, and that the third line of that book was not altered. Sixty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and thirty-two days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and thirty-five days back. A body four hundred miles off is seventy-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person, and this district has now got one on a page in the only way it has ever got one, which is by asking a man in a ditch in front of about nineteen people and being given the answer out loud. Eleven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and eleven marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is no figure in this district for how long this month is. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and a clerk who entered a day a man said out loud this morning did not enter his name beside it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household.

---

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up, which takes her about two seconds longer than two lines takes her, and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank at about half past five and stood at the end of that table for about a minute and looked at a date under a figure and did not touch it, and the stone on the ground by the boards is the only figure in that yard that nobody standing there is responsible for, and nobody said one word about that either.
