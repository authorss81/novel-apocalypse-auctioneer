# Chapter 478: Is A Table An Instrument

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against asked the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up, in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the ninth morning after the count, in the open, in front of about nineteen people, whether a table is an instrument, and got an answer that took longer than anybody in that yard expected, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it in his own words, and entered no figure in her own.

It was the ninth morning after the count and the wind had been in the south all the morning and had gone round to the west by about eleven, and the boards of that second table had a leaf on them that had not been there the day before, and by ten there were about nineteen people in it.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and twenty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and not a figure about the sheet.

Once that morning the four figures on that wall were said out loud and got by the man of fifty-six, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again.

"**The board carries three hundred and eighty, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and ninety-six days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and ten, and three hundred and seventy-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The question was asked at about ten and it was four words long, and about nineteen people heard it, and about nine of them have said since that four words went further in that yard than anything anybody has said there in a month.

"**Is a table an instrument.**"

Nobody answered it for about nine seconds, and then the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said one thing out loud, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got forty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been waiting eight days for somebody in this yard to ask me that, and I have got an answer ready and I am not going to say it the way I have got it ready, so you are going to have to stay here for it.**"

About nine people stayed. The clerk of nineteen years said out loud that she was going to write what he said in his words and not in hers, and about nineteen people heard her say it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He put his own hand flat on the boards of the second table and he left it there for the whole of it, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and ten and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The count of what this district has made is twelve. You all know that and none of you has asked me where twelve came from and I am going to tell you, and then you are going to ask me whether I am a man who made eleven things or a man who made one thing twice, and the answer to that is one thing twice, and the difference between a thing a man does twice and a thing a man does once is not a difference about the thing at all. It is a difference about whether anybody standing here is allowed to ask me to do it again.**"

Then he went on, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fourteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You are allowed to ask me for a table. Every one of you could come down that lane and say I want a table and I would put one up. Nobody in this district can ask me for twenty-two paces. Nobody can ask me for a figure of me. If you had all been standing here on the twenty-fifth of this month instead of on the eighth, there would be a hundred and twenty-three paces in this yard instead of twenty-two, and you would not have got the number out of me once, and neither of those is a thing anybody can order off me and both of them are a thing I do.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the whole of it in his own words, and entered that she is not entering a table as an instrument and that the count of things this district has made is twelve, and entered that a thing a man is asked for is not the same kind of thing as a figure he makes, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then he said the last part of it, at about eleven, and it was the part about four people at that near end have said since they would not have given it up for anything, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and seventeen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You are all going to want to say something to me about the twelve and you are not going to, and I am telling you now, in front of about nineteen people, that I said on the twenty-fifth of this month that I am not going to be thanked and not going to wait to be thanked, and I am not going to change that because a woman of nineteen has written a sentence down with my reason in it. That is not a thing I am owed and it is not a thing I have been given, and the two of those are the same thing today and that is the whole of what I have.**"

Nobody answered him and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he was not thanked and that she is not going to thank him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and said one thing in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He has just described his own trade in a yard and has not been given a thing for it and has told nine people in advance not to give him one, and that is the first time in five volumes that a person in this district has refused a thing before it was offered.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that she would not answer it and would not say why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about half past eleven with the bundle under his arm and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that second table, and did not put a thumb in the hollow, and nobody said a word to him about the mark and about four people at that near end have said since that yesterday that same man put a thumb in the hollow on his way past and today he did not, and that nobody in this yard has worked out which of the two of those is about the hollow and which of the two is about the chalk.

About four people at that near end have said since that a man was asked one question in that yard that morning and answered it for about six minutes, and that a clerk has entered the whole of it and not one figure of her own about any part of it, and that nobody has worked out yet which of those two is the more remarkable.

At about one the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank with the water to his thigh and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm, and nobody said a word to him all morning.

Nothing asked the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds why he did not put a thumb in that hollow this morning when he did yesterday, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, took the four figures off that wall as he went past, and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the cart came up and went on up the lane with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop. A man of about sixty-four was sitting at the foot of that low wall from about half past three, his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and seventy-first night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and seventy of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Nothing in that day was altered and nothing in it was filled. The bid has now been open a hundred and thirty days, it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. The figure on the second line of that lot book is eighty-five days behind, nothing correct stands beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. The rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month, which is ninety days. It is a hundred and sixty days since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-three days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named this morning. A body four hundred miles off is ninety-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in this district has watched any of it.

Thirty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are twenty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure is a figure about a day. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man has said out loud this morning where the twelve came from, and a clerk entered the whole of it and entered no figure of her own in it, and the count did not move and no thirteenth was proposed by anybody. The lot is being read and it is not finished with, and it has not got past the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and that fourth one is a person. Five people said a thing out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is one of five and none of the five has been paid, and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and nobody proposed a sixth.

At about half past five the two buckets that go down that bank every day went down it empty and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing at all to anybody, and there was nobody in that yard who said one word to her. The man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them.

A man stood in a yard this morning and said out loud where the count of twelve came from, and told about nine people in advance that he was not going to be thanked for it, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote the whole of it down in his own words and entered no figure of her own anywhere in it, and nobody in that yard gave him one thing, and the count of what this district has made is still twelve and it did not move.
