# Chapter 498: Two Figures In The Open And No Rule About Either

About nine people in the yard of Lot Seventeen argued on the twenty-fifth morning after the count, at the middle of the afternoon, about whether a figure nobody has to put down and a figure three columns of a wall are carrying a day out are the same kind of thing, and about four of them were certain, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the four certainties and entered no figure at all, and entered that a certainty is a thing a person says and not a thing a page has, and a boy of about nineteen read his own page out loud to about nine people and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally asked the clerk in the open whether she was going to copy any of it, and she said out loud that she was not, and the four figures on that wall were not said out loud a second time that day and a clerk entered that and entered the reason.

By ten there were about nineteen people in that yard and nine of them were still standing in it at half past four, and the light had gone round off the south end of that ground and come back along the face of that bank by then, and the boards of that second table had gone cold again at about four, and the four figures on that wall had not been said out loud a second time that day and a clerk entered that and entered the reason, which is that a figure a man reads off a wall every morning is not entered into a page twice in one day.

At about a quarter to eight, with that yard empty behind her, a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered that the age of that figure is two hundred and thirty-seven days, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

---

About four people at the near end of that first table asked, out loud and within four feet of each other, why the man of fifty-six had not said the four figures off that wall a second time that afternoon, and a clerk of nineteen years answered them out loud, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure a man reads off a wall every morning is not entered into a page twice in one day. I have entered that he did not say them a second time this afternoon and I have entered the reason, and about four of you have been asking me about it for about ten minutes and I am giving you the same answer four times and none of the four of you is going to get a different one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that about four people asked a question out loud in that yard and entered the question and the answer and the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The first of the four was the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale and she came at about half past two with the last of that day's flour on her hip, and she said one thing out loud, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That they are the same kind of thing, and I am certain of it, and I have been certain of it since a clerk of nineteen years said a sentence out loud in this yard on the seventeenth morning after the count, and I said then that she was right and I have been arguing with about four people about it ever since.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she said she was certain, and entered that a certainty is not a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The second of the four came at about ten past three and it was the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally, and he had the flat book open under his left arm where nobody had asked him to open it, and he said one thing, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That they are not the same kind of thing, and I am as certain about that as she is about the other one. The one in that stone has been in that yard longer than I have, and the one on that wall is the same figure it was yesterday and the day before, and a thing that does not change is a different kind of thing from a thing that is one day out from where it says it is, and both of those are in front of me at four o'clock this afternoon and I am standing between them.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he said he was certain, and entered that she is not entering his certainty as a figure either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The third of the four came at about twenty past three and it was the man of about thirty-four who digs loam, and he had not been out of that ditch since about ten, and he said one thing, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in a ditch in this yard every morning for three weeks and about four of you have decided in the last two days that one of those two figures is the better one, and I want to say the thing I have been not saying. I do not know which one is the better one. I have got one thumb in a hollow in a stone and I know how deep it is and that is all I know about it, and I have read a figure off a wall about two hundred times and I do not know whether it is right, and neither of those two is a better one than the other, and I am the man the third line of that book is about and I have never once said so out loud and I am saying it now.**"

Nobody said anything at all, and about nine people heard it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has said out loud in that yard that he is the man the third line of that book is about, and entered that she is not entering the third line, and entered that she is not entering a date, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The fourth of the four was the boy of about nineteen, at about half past three, and he said one thing, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That it does not matter, and that is a certainty too, and I am the only one of the four of you who has been counting a number with his own eyes since the eighth day of this month and I have not written it down anywhere, and about four people in this yard are certain about two figures and none of the four of you is certain about a number, and I am not certain about anything, I am just counting.**"

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally then read his own page out loud, in the open, at about nine feet off that first table, which nobody had asked him to do, and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud, whether she was going to copy any of the four certainties, and the clerk said that she was not, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to copy one word of it. The day I copy a certainty out of a man's mouth into my own page it is a figure on a page in this district, and the only instrument this yard has ever had for telling a certainty from a wrong answer is a day, and there is no day in this afternoon, and I am not going to put a thing on a page that has nowhere to go to be found out wrong.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked her a question out loud in that yard and entered the question and the answer and the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said nothing at all that afternoon, and about four people at that near end have said since that the only person in that yard who was not certain about anything was a man who has said four things out loud in that yard in eleven days, and that nobody is going to ask him whether being quiet is a position.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about a quarter past four and read the page standing up without being asked and said nothing about it. The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came down the lane at about half past four, looked at the two tables, and went back up it, and nobody asked him why he had come down, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about twenty past one the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall without stopping and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the cart came up that lane, near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine driving it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody standing there. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and about nine people within four feet of him were busy being certain about something else, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-six of them, and that she is not going to put down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and forty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this afternoon, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. One hundred and one days is how far behind the figure on that second line is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and a man has now said out loud in that yard that he is the man it is about. A hundred and six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. It is a hundred and seventy-six days since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and seventy-nine days back, it is open, nothing was pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard has watched any of it.

Fifty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are forty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and four certainties are not either of those two figures and were not added to them. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and four certainties are not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and it stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person, and a man has said out loud this afternoon that he is the person and the reading did not advance a line. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard this afternoon and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk wanted it on the page a second time and did not put anything in it. Six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count of the five is five with no sixth in it.

At about half past five the buckets went down that bank, both of them empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that he was about nine feet off four people being certain about two figures and did not look over, and that a man in that ditch this afternoon said out loud that he was the man a third line of that book is about and that the man of fifty-six said nothing at all about it.

Four certainties went onto one page of a clerk's book on the twenty-fifth afternoon after the count and a clerk of nineteen years entered all four and entered no figure, and a man said out loud in that yard that he is the man the third line of that book is about and a clerk entered that and entered neither the third line nor a date, and the two figures in that yard are still two figures and neither one of them is the better one and the yard has four certainties about it and no instrument, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and will be empty tomorrow morning.
