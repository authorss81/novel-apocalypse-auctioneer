# Chapter 484: Two Halves, Entered Again

A clerk of nineteen years entered the bid in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirteenth morning after the count, at the middle of the afternoon, and did not run it, and entered the two halves of the reason it cannot be run on in two separate sentences for the second time in five volumes, and did not join them, and a bid which cannot be run on a figure nobody can check is a figure of a person, and nobody standing in that yard is a person a bid can be run on.

By ten there were about nineteen people in that yard and about nine of them never left it, and by the middle of the afternoon the light had gone round off the south end of that ground and come back along the face of that bank, and the four figures on that wall had not been said out loud a second time that day, and a clerk entered that and entered the reason, which is that a figure a man reads off a wall every morning is not entered into a page twice in one day.

At about a quarter to eight, with that yard empty behind her, a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and twenty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count.

She said the two halves out loud before she wrote either of them, standing at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand and not on the boards, and about nineteen people heard all of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got seventy-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The first half is this. A bid is one person's figure said out loud about what a lot would be worth for a term, and another person's figure said back, and the only figure this district has about that lot is on the second line of that book, and it is wrong on the face of it, and it has been wrong on the face of it since the book came out.**"

About nine people heard that and about four of them have said since that a clerk has now said out loud the reason a bid in this yard cannot be run in the second half of a sentence, which is new, and that she did it in the afternoon and not in the morning, and that nobody has worked out why.

Then she said the second half, in the same voice at the same table, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The second half is this. A wrong count is not struck by writing a correct count beside it, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and nothing is going to be. Those are the two halves. I am entering them in two sentences again today and I am not joining them, and I entered them in two sentences five volumes ago and did not join them then, and a clerk may not join them now because joining them puts a thing on this page that is not on it, which is that the bid is finished.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the two halves in two sentences, and entered that she is not joining them and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing out loud, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You have just described the oldest open thing in this district in two halves and refused to put them together, and the oldest open thing in this district is a form, and the thing it is a form of is a reading of a lot that stands at the fourth of five things, and the fourth of five things is a person, and there is no person in this yard that thing can be run on, and I am one of about nine and so are you.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a bid which cannot be run on a figure nobody can check is a figure of a person and that nobody standing in that yard is a person a bid can be run on, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and said one thing in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A hundred and thirty-four mornings. It has not moved a finger in five volumes and neither has the second line of that book and neither has the fourth of the five, and about nine people in this yard have got three things in front of them that are all the same age and none of them is the oldest.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer it and that nobody in that yard is going to either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of that bank at about one with the water coming off him onto the boards, and he put his own right thumb on the boards of that first table about four inches below the second line of that lot book and kept it there for about nine seconds, and about four people at that table watched a man's thumb sit on a book and not one of them said a word, and then he said one thing out loud, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am the only person in this yard who has ever put a thumb on that second line. I put it there this afternoon. I know what it says and I know it is wrong, and I have known it was wrong since the day that book came out, and I am not going to stand in this yard and be asked a second time about the same four inches of a page I was asked about at about half past two in a different voice.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put a thumb on that book this afternoon and that she is not entering what is under his thumb, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the bid was entered this afternoon in the same four words at the foot of her page that it has been entered in every morning since the first of the ninth month, and that the four words have not changed, and that nobody has ever proposed changing them, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about one and said nothing to anybody, and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that second table on his way past, and there are twenty-nine of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume, and nobody asked him for the first of them. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and did not come over at any point in the afternoon.

Nobody asked the man of about thirty-one who digs loam what is under his thumb, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen asked the clerk of nineteen years one question at about half past two, out loud, which was whether a bid was a figure, and the clerk said that a bid is what one person says out loud about what a lot would be worth for a term and another person says back, and that neither of those two things has been said in that yard since the first of the ninth month, and entered the question and the answer and joined neither of them to the two halves, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The road keeper came up that lane at about two, got the four figures off that wall as he went past, and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the cart came up and went on up the lane, the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm. A man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall at about half past three with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nothing was offered to him and nothing was asked of him, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and seventy-fifth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-four of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

The bid has now been open a hundred and thirty-four days, it was not run on this day, nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page, and no second reader was asked for and none was given. The figure on the second line of that lot book is eighty-nine days behind, it has not been altered, nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. The rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month, which is ninety-four days. It is a hundred and sixty-four days since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-seven days back, and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Forty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are twenty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and the two of them are two counts and not one. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and two sentences about a form are not a thirteenth. It did not advance this afternoon and it is still at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person. A clerk read two sentences out loud in that yard this afternoon in front of about nineteen people and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and nothing went into it, and the same column is named a second time in that entry because a clerk wanted it on the page twice. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count of the five is still five with no sixth proposed, and six things now stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

The two buckets went back up that bank at about half past five, both of them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and nobody asked him anything about the two halves a clerk read out loud about a hundred minutes earlier, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody put a number on that lot today and nobody said a number out loud in that yard about what that lot would be worth for a term, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the two halves of why in two sentences for the second time in five volumes and did not join them, and the oldest open thing in that district is a form, and the thing it is a form of stands at the fourth of five, and a sentence closer to being described is not one step nearer to being finished.
