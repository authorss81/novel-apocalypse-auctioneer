# Chapter 475: The Second Time It Was Said

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the same distinction out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the seventh morning after the count that a man who puts tables up had said out loud in that yard four mornings ago, and it took him about four seconds, and his reason for it was not the other man's reason, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was the second time the sentence had been said in that yard and entered that the two men gave two reasons and joined nothing.

It was the seventh morning after the count and the frost had been off the boards of that first table before about eight, and there was a hard clean light on the boards of that second table with the stone lying face up on it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in it.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and nineteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that nothing about it is a figure about the sheet.

The four figures on that wall were said once in the morning by the man of fifty-six and got by him, and entered once by a clerk, and said again at about four and got again, and nobody in that yard crossed the four feet between that wall and the near end of that first table at any point in the day.

"**The board carries three hundred and seventy-eight, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and ninety-four days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and eight, and three hundred and sixty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He had been at the end of that second table since about ten past ten with his left hand in its cloth, and he waited until about a quarter to twelve, and about nine people were within four feet of him, and he said the thing once and it took about four seconds.

"**A figure nobody has to write down is not a day out, and it is not a day, and that is one sentence and I have now heard it once in this yard and I would like to know what it is for.**"

Then he gave the reason, and it was not the batten and it was not the twenty-two paces, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and eighteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Four mornings ago a man told you that a figure you have to write down can be a day out and one you do not cannot, and I have had four days with that and I have found the reason for it is not his and it is mine. I can put this hand flat on a page and I cannot make it do anything. If I were on that page it would be a figure about a day, because I would have had to put it there and the day would be in it. I cannot put anything anywhere. There is no day in this hand to be out from and that is the whole of it.**"

Nobody said anything at all. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the reason was his own words and not hers, and entered that she is not going to enter anything about the hand being better than it was than it was on the day he came out of the space under that trestle table, and entered that nothing about that hand is on a page in her book except the reason he gave for a sentence, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four people at that near end worked out while he was standing there that he had not been asked any of that, and about nine of them looked at the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, who was standing about nine feet off with his right hand in his coat, and he had not moved.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing out loud, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is twice. I have been in this yard when twice was two men doing two things on two mornings, and this is twice and it is two men saying one sentence, and a sentence is not a hand and it is not a boot and it is not a pace, and the two of you have both arrived at it and neither of you has the other one's way of getting there, and I am not going to be here when a clerk writes down that two men in this yard agreed.**"

Nobody thanked him and nobody answered him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard, and entered the thing, and entered that he is not going to be thanked and is not going to wait to be thanked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then a clerk of nineteen years said out loud what she was going to enter, before she entered any of it, and about nineteen people heard it, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering that this is the second time that sentence has been said out loud in this yard, and I am entering that the two men who said it gave two reasons and that the two reasons are not the same reason, and I am not entering it as a rule, and I am not entering a third time, and if a third person in this yard says it in the next four weeks I am going to enter the third one and I am still not going to join them.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of things this district has made is twelve, and that a sentence is not a thirteenth thing this district has made, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was not in that yard on this morning and nobody sent for him, and about four people at that near end have said since that a man was not told that his own sentence was going to be said again by somebody else four days later, and that he would not have minded, and that nobody knows that he would not have minded because nobody asked him.

About four people worked out while that entry was being written that a clerk of nineteen years had just entered two reasons in two men's own words and had refused to join them, and that the whole of what a sentence turned out to be worth in this district was about eleven minutes of one man's afternoon with a batten, and about nine people at that table have said since that a thing can be a figure in five volumes and be worth one afternoon, and that both of those are true.

Nobody asked the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up what he made the other half of his reason out of, and a clerk entered that she did not ask, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about one with the last of that day's flour on her hip, read the three lines in that lot book standing up, and went over to the second table and put her thumb in the hollow of that stone for about four seconds and took it out and said nothing at all, and a clerk entered that a woman put a thumb in that hollow and entered no figure about the hollow, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank at about half past one and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and was not spoken to.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and sixty-ninth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-eight of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Two men in that yard said one sentence out loud on two mornings four days apart and a clerk of nineteen years entered both of them in two entries with the reasons in their own words and entered that the reasons are not the same reason, and did not join them, and entered that the count of things this district has made does not move and that a sentence is not a thing this district has made.

Nothing else in that day moved either. A hundred and twenty-eight days is how long the bid has been open and it was not run, and nothing was proposed about closing it. Eighty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, unaltered, with nothing correct beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Eighty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. A hundred and fifty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-one days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is ninety-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything. Thirty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and that is a count of marks.

The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and four people said a thing out loud in that yard today and no name went beside any of the four. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that he was about nine feet from a man who said out loud that he cannot make his hand do anything, and that he did not look over and that nobody thinks he did not hear it.

A pattern got named in a yard on this morning for the first time in five volumes and a clerk entered the naming of it and entered a refusal of it in the same entry, and the man who said the sentence the second time put his own hand into the reason, and the man who said it the first time was four miles away doing something else, and the count of instruments in that district is twelve and a sentence said twice by two different men with two different reasons behind it is not a thirteenth and is not a rule and is not going to become either of those.
