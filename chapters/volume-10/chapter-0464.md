# Chapter 464: A Clerk Asks, And Is Refused

A clerk of nineteen years asked a man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds, in the open, in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-eighth of the twelfth month, for a figure about a day, and he gave her one, and it was not the one anybody in that yard wanted, and when she asked a second time he refused her out loud in front of about nineteen people, and she entered that she asked and was refused and that she is not going to ask again.

It was the twenty-eighth of the twelfth month and about four people at that table had said that nobody in this district had ever asked that man for a figure, and about nine of them had said it as a complaint, and the man had heard both of those numbers and had said nothing.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred and ten days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and nobody in that yard said one word to him, and he said nothing himself, and that is the whole of what he has done about it in three days.

"**The board carries three hundred and sixty-nine, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and eighty-five days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and ninety-nine, and three hundred and sixty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He has got that figure right every morning for a hundred and ninety-six mornings and he has been told one thing about one of the four and has not asked a question about it and nobody in that yard is going to give him one.

---

She asked him at about eleven and she asked him plainly, and about nineteen people heard the question.

"What day did you put the first of those marks in chalk on the edge of that table."

He stood at the end of that second table with the bundle on the ground at his feet and he did not look at her, and he said a figure out loud, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The fifteenth of this month, and there are fourteen of them on that edge, and I have not been asked for either of those two figures in two months, and the figure you have just got from me is a figure about a man with a piece of chalk and it is not a figure about that table, and a figure about a man is a figure nobody in this district can check.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man gave a figure out loud in that yard and entered the figure and entered that he was asked for it, and entered that the figure is a figure about a man and not about a table, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said out loud at about a quarter past eleven, to about nine people, that a clerk who asks for a figure has become a second person in this yard who needs a column, and that there are four columns on that clerk's page and there is no fifth, and that the second person who needs a column is always the one nobody puts one in for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the clerk of nineteen years asked a second question, in the open, about a different day, and about nineteen people heard it.

"And the day before that one. What day was that."

"**No. You have had one. I have not been asked for a figure in two months and I have thought about every morning of it, and you have just made me the only person in this yard who has been asked for two, and I am not going to be the person in this yard who said no to a clerk in front of about nineteen people, so I am saying it now while I have the choice, and I am saying no, and there is nothing under it and there is no reason, and you may enter that.**"

He picked his bundle up off the ground and said the second half of it without stopping, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got forty-eight, and about four people near the east wall heard the number and about nine heard all of it and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure somebody was asked for is a figure somebody chose, and the difference between that and the one you had before is that before, I was the one holding it, and now it is in a page, and I cannot get it back and neither can you.**"

Nobody said anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she asked a second time and was refused out loud in that yard, and entered that there is no reason and that she is not going to look for one, and entered that she is not going to ask him anything again, and entered that a figure somebody was asked for is a figure somebody chose, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

By ten there were about nineteen people in that yard and a clerk of nineteen years had asked two questions out loud in one morning and had been given one figure and had been refused one, and about four people at that near end have said since that the district spent five volumes learning to write down when and not to write down what, and that a clerk who asks a question is a clerk writing down a what.

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about half past twelve the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and said nothing at all, and nobody said anything to him, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of fifty-six was at that wall about nine feet off with his palm flat on the boards, and the two of them had not said one word to each other in five days, and about four people at that near end have said since that they had counted the days as well as the feet. At about one the man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up a second time and stood at that table and said nothing.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came down the lane and said one thing to about four people, that a man who cuts reeds has been putting a mark on his table every morning for three weeks and that nobody in this district has paid him a penny for it and that nobody asked him about it until this morning, and that a district which learns how to ask late has not learned how to ask, and went up the lane, and nobody thanked him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about four people watched him do it and said nothing. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and sixtieth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-nine of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day in one entry, and read it out loud to about nine people, and did not read it back from the top for anybody, and entered that nobody read it back and entered the reason.

A hundred and nineteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Seventy-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Seventy-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and forty-nine days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and fifty-two days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is eighty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Twenty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and twenty-eight marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is still no figure in this district for how long this month is, and a clerk is going to stand at that wall in two days and count. The count of things this district has made is twelve. Fourteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, entered as a man said them when he was asked for the first time, and entered that she is not entering them as a day-count and not as a line on a board. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it and a clerk was refused a second time in that yard today and no name went beside it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, which takes her about two seconds, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them.

A clerk of nineteen years asked this morning for the first time in two months and was given a figure and refused a second one, and a figure a man holds is a figure a man can give, and about nine people in that yard have worked out that this district has spent five volumes finding out how not to ask, and that a clerk who asks does not find out how to ask, and that what she found out is that there are four columns on a page and no fifth and that a man who is asked for a figure has become a person a figure is about, and that he is nineteen days into a count of marks he cannot be paid for and somebody has now asked him what it is a figure about, and he answered that, and then he said no.
