# Chapter 460: A Silence Held On Purpose

About four people in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fifth of the twelfth month said the third of the four figures out loud in the same voice as the man who was saying it, a second after he had said it, and he went on saying the four every morning after that without once saying why four other people had started doing it, and nobody standing there told him, and a boy of about nineteen asked out loud that morning to be the one who told him, and a clerk of nineteen years refused him in the open, in the ordinary voice, and gave a reason.

It was the twenty-fifth of the twelfth month and the air had gone thin and bright and the light off the south end of that ground was hard enough to make a man narrow his eyes at the boards, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote nothing down until about eleven.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred and seven days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and while he was saying the third of them a voice about two feet to his left said the same figure after him in the same voice, and a second voice said it, and a third, and he did not stop and did not turn round and got all four.

"**The board carries three hundred and sixty-six, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and eighty-two days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and ninety-six, and three hundred and fifty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The boy of about nineteen asked at about eleven, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, that it be him that told the man of fifty-six, and he said he had thought about it for two days and that he had it written down on his own sheet and that he would say it in the ordinary voice and would not put it in a column, and about four people at that near end said afterwards that a boy of nineteen asking to be the one to say a thing like that out loud is the bravest thing anybody had done on that ground in a month, and the clerk of nineteen years said no.

"Not because you would say it badly," she said. "Because you are nineteen and he is fifty-six and he has read four figures out loud in this yard every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and nobody has ever asked him one question, not one, in all that time, and a boy of nineteen is not going to be the first person in this district to ask him one. I am not going to stand here and tell that yard that this is a question, and I am not going to send a boy in my place, and there is nobody else in this yard I am willing to be instead of him."

Nobody said anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked to be the one who would tell a man something and entered that she refused and entered the reason, and entered that nobody asked her who was going to do it instead, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen did not argue and about four people at that table have said since that he did not argue, and that they had been waiting all morning for him to argue, and that a boy who took a refusal without arguing had been refused by somebody who had thought about it.

At about half past eleven the clerk of nineteen years said one more thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got one hundred and four and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and about nineteen people heard it, and it is the decision this morning produced and it was the whole of what this morning produced.

"**I am going to say it in this yard, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of everybody standing here, and I am going to say it in this yard because a thing that is only true in a yard where two people are standing is not true, and I am not going to ask him first whether he wants to be told, because a man who is asked whether he wants to be told has been asked a question about a figure he has been getting right every morning for six months, and there is nobody in this yard he could answer.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a column is being looked at and that no result of it has been given to anybody, and entered that she is not going to be asked to give it, and entered that a clerk who enters a result and gives it to a man is a clerk who has decided which man gets a figure and which man does not, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

By ten there were about nineteen people in that yard, and about four of them have said since that they worked out what the morning was for while it was happening, and that a yard that has agreed not to say a thing is a thing that can be held for a while, and that the day it stops being holdable nobody is going to be able to say which of them let it go.

---

At about ten past ten the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank, wiped his right hand on his thigh, and said one thing out loud to about four people, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man who says four figures out loud every morning for six months and has never once been wrong is not a man who is right, he is a man who has been reading, and the difference between those two things is the whole of what is happening at that wall this month, and nobody standing here is going to be the one who tells him which one he is.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of the table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about half past eleven the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of the second table with his left hand in its cloth and said nothing at all, and nobody said anything to him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood about nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat and said one thing to about four people and not to the rest.

"A yard can hold a thing. It is not a strong thing and it does not get stronger by being held, and every day it is held is a day the people holding it get better at holding it and not one day better at saying it."

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has not asked for a figure and has not been given one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came down the lane, looked at the two tables, and went up the lane, and nobody asked him why he had come down, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at that second table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fifty-seventh night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-six of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day in one entry, read it out loud to about nine people, and did not read it back from the top, and entered that nobody read it back and entered the reason.

A hundred and sixteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Seventy-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Seventy-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and forty-six days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and forty-nine days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is eighty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Twenty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and twenty-five marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is still no figure in this district for how long this month is. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it and a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard this morning that she is going to do a thing about, and no name went beside it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and two of the four were said a second time by about four other mouths while he was saying them.

A man of fifty-six has got that figure right every morning for a hundred and ninety-three mornings and this morning four people standing at that table said the third of the four along with him, and nobody told him why, and nobody is going to tell him why, and about four people there have said since that the silence is the loudest thing in that yard and that it is being held on purpose by people who know what it is for, and that the man it is held over has not asked to be let off and is not going to be asked.
