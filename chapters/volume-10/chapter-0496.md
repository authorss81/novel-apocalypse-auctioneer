# Chapter 496: The Yard Decides Not To Name The Third Thing

A clerk of nineteen years did not read the third thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fourth morning after the count, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had told her the day before to read it or say she could not, and about nine people in that yard had waited until about eleven to find out which of the two it was going to be, and she said out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, that she still did not know, and entered that she did not know, and entered that a man told her to read it or say she could not and that she has said the second and not the first, and entered no figure about the third thing, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six that evening and had been empty on every day of this fortnight.

It was the twenty-fourth morning after the count and the light came up hard off the south end of that ground before seven and the boards of that second table were warm by about nine and about four people had noticed it and not one of them had said anything, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and thirty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing near enough to hear that have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and twenty-second of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and ninety-five, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and eleven days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and twenty-five, and three hundred and eighty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

About nine people were standing near the near end of that first table at about a quarter past ten doing nothing at all, and about four of them had been doing nothing there since half past eight, and a clerk of nineteen years came and stood next to them and said one thing out loud in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I still do not know, and I have said the second of the two things a man told me to do yesterday, and I want about nine people to have heard me say that instead of the first, because about four of you came here at about nine this morning to hear me read a thing out loud and you are going to go away without it and I would rather you went away having heard it said than having waited for it.**"

About nine people heard all of that and nobody said anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the third thing as a figure and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen then asked her, out loud and in front of about nine people, what the third thing on that page is, and she said no out loud, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. And I am not going to tell you that I cannot, either, because I can read it out loud perfectly well, it is a short sentence and it is on my page and I could say it now, and I am not going to, and the reason is that about four people in this yard have read a thing out loud in the last three weeks that they did not understand and it turned into a figure, and I have watched it happen, and that is the last thing this district has to learn how to do.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked her a question out loud in that yard and entered the question and the answer and the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is the first time a clerk in this district has refused to make a figure out loud, and every other thing I have watched in here this month has been somebody making one, and I have not got anything to add to it and I have not been going to for five volumes.**"

Nobody said a word. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and entered that nobody answered him.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came down that lane at about two and said one thing to about nine people without stopping, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I walked down this lane eleven days to say a sentence with two figures in it and I said it, and about four people told me it was the truest thing anybody had said in this yard, and none of those four people has said one word about it since, and I have come down here today to find out whether saying a true thing out loud in this yard does anything at all, and I have found out, and I am going back up the lane.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man came down that lane to find out whether a true thing does anything and entered that he has not entered the answer because he did not say it, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He put his own hand flat on the boards of that second table for about a second on his way past and went on up the lane.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table at about one and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of it in the ordinary way and said nothing, and there are forty of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume, and a clerk entered that count and entered that it is a count of marks and not a figure about this morning. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and a clerk entered that a man put his own thumb in that hollow on his way past and entered that she did not ask him how deep it is and entered the reason. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all that morning.

The road keeper came up that lane a little after one, took the four off that wall, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about three a cart came up that lane and the near wheel on it was still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and about nine people within four feet of him were busy waiting to hear whether a clerk would read a thing out loud, and nobody offered him anything, and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and forty-five days is how long that bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. One hundred days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and seventy-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and seventy-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fourteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Fifty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are forty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved today. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a refusal to read a thing out loud is not a thirteenth. It is still at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, the fourth of them is a person, and nobody in that yard advanced the reading of that lot today. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk entered that she did not read anything out loud today and entered that the column is empty and that she entered that, and the record about the not asking says not asked. Nobody in that yard paid the fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count of the five is five with no sixth in it, and six things stand there that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

The two buckets came down that bank at about half past five, both of them empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that about nine people were standing at the near end of that first table at about a quarter past ten this morning doing nothing at all, and that a clerk came and stood among them and told them she was not going to give them what they were waiting for, and that a man of fifty-six read his four figures at about four and walked past about nine people who were still standing there.

A clerk of nineteen years said out loud in that yard on the twenty-fourth morning after the count that she still did not know what the third thing on her own page was, and said the second of the two things a man told her to do the day before and not the first, and a man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came down that lane and said out loud that a true thing said in that yard does nothing at all and went back up it, and nothing was read out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and has been empty on every day of this fortnight, and the third thing is still on the page and is not a figure.
