# Chapter 486: Two Answers In One Yard

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up asked a question in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fifteenth morning after the count, in front of about nineteen people, and the question was whether a figure nobody has to put down and a figure that three columns of a wall are carrying one day out from the day they name can stand in one yard at the same time, and a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale answered it out loud in the ordinary voice, and the answer took about nine seconds, and it was that they can, and that she would not build a yard that could not hold both.

It was the fifteenth morning after the count and the frost was off the boards of that first table before about eight and the light came up hard and clean off the south end of that ground, and the stone lying face up on the second table had been warm for about an hour, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and twenty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and thirteenth of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and eighty-six, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and two days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and sixteen, and three hundred and seventy-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He asked it at about eleven and he had been in the yard about ten minutes and he had not said anything in those ten minutes, and the asking was not a complaint and his hands were behind his back while he asked it, and about nineteen people heard the whole of it and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put a table up in that yard in the first week of this month and I have not been thanked for it and I am not going to be thanked for it and I am not going to wait to be thanked for it, and I have walked down that lane and back up it four times in nine days without stopping, and I am going to tell you what I have been stopping for. I have been standing where the near end of that first table is and looking at the corner of that book, and then I have been walking up that lane and looking at the near end of that first table again, and I have done that four times, and what I have been trying to do is get a figure about a day and a figure about a book into the same look.**"

Nobody answered him at that point. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not thanked and is not going to be thanked and is not going to wait to be thanked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came over down that bank with the last of that day's flour still on her hip and answered him without being asked twice, and about nineteen people heard every word of it, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**They can stand in it. That is the whole of what I have got and I have thought about it for six days and it is nine seconds of saying. A figure about a day is a figure about a day, and a figure about a book standing still on a table is a figure about a book standing still on a table, and they are not the same figure and they do not correct each other and neither one of them can be put back down on the other. And I would not build a yard that could not hold two answers to one question, because I have spent six weeks arguing the other way and I was wrong, and I said out loud on a morning nine days ago that I was wrong and I said by a thumb.**"

Nobody said a word. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she said it a second time, and entered that a second time is not a correction and entered no figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up stood where he had asked the question from and did not say anything else at all, and about four people at that table have said since that a man who has walked that lane four times in nine days without stopping said the reason out loud in a yard in about a minute and a half, and that nobody has asked him a second question, and that the second question was the obvious one and the yard decided not to ask it about ten seconds before anybody got to it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about ten past eleven with his left hand in its cloth, and he said one thing in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A yard that holds two answers is a yard, and not a mistake, and I have been in this yard for five volumes and I have never once seen anybody put two things side by side in it and then stop.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer a man who is right, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten and heard all of it, and he is the man the fifth of the five things this district does not have is about, and a clerk entered that he was there and entered that nobody has asked him whether any of it is about him and entered that she is not going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven, the way he does, and said nothing at all, and there are thirty-one of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume.

Then about four people at the near end of that first table started arguing about which of the two figures had been in that yard longer, and the argument was about a quarter of an hour long and about nine people heard the end of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what the last man said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The stone is older and everybody in this yard knows it is older and that is not a fact anybody can check, and the wall is newer and everybody in this yard knows it is newer and that one you can walk up to and put your hand on, and I am not going to stand here and tell nine people which of those two things is worth more to us.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that about four people argued out loud in that yard for about a quarter of an hour and entered the last thing the man said and entered that a man who has been keeping a tally in this district for about thirty years said a thing out loud in a yard that is not a figure, and entered that she is not going to enter which of the two figures in that yard is older because one of the two cannot be checked and a clerk does not enter a comparison she cannot make, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody answered him and nobody asked him a second question. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she did not ask and entered that she is not going to, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and nobody said a word to him all morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, took the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and thirty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Ninety-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Ninety-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and sixty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-nine days back and is open, and nothing was pulled off it and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Forty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first day of the twelfth month, and there are thirty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is twelve and a fifth thing said out loud in a yard for a second time is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance, and it stands where it has stood since the second of the twelfth month, at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person. A clerk read nothing out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count of the five is still five with no sixth in it, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

At about half past five the two buckets came down that bank, both of them empty, and went back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read those three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, stayed where he was, and nobody asked him to come and look at the second table.

A yard that holds a figure about a day and a figure about a book is a yard with two answers in it and not a yard with a mistake in it, and a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said that out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people on the fifteenth morning after the count in about nine seconds, and a man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up had walked down that lane four times in nine days to find out whether he was the only one who could see that, and he was not the only one, and nobody thanked either of them and nobody waited to.
