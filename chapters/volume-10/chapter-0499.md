# Chapter 499: A Man Who Reads The Figures Says What A Figure Is For

The man of fifty-six said out loud, in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-sixth morning after the count, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, what a figure in that yard is for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it in his words and entered no figure of her own, and about nine people heard all of it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard at any point in the day and nobody has worked out why and nobody has asked.

It was the twenty-sixth morning after the count and the light came up clean off the south end of that ground and the boards of that second table were warm by nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and thirty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing near enough to hear that have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and twenty-fourth of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and ninety-seven, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and thirteen days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and twenty-seven, and three hundred and eighty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He said it at about eleven and he said it at the wall and not at a table, with his back to about nine people and his face to the boards, and about nineteen people heard all of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure is for a person to find out something they could not find out any other way, and there are two of them in this yard and neither of them is doing that. The one on that board is a day out from the day it names, and I read it every morning and I have got it every morning, and I have not found out anything with it. The one in the underside of that stone on that table is about a book standing still, and anybody can put a thumb in it and say how deep it is, and I have not found out anything with that either. That is what a figure is for and neither of those two is doing it, and I have been reading one of them two hundred and twenty-four mornings and about nine people have been counting me doing it, and I did not know that until a man said a sentence in this yard six days ago.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said that two figures in that yard are not doing the thing a figure is for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said one thing at about half past eleven, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in this yard every working morning since the first of this month and I have watched about nineteen people read a figure off a wall and about nine people put a thumb in a hollow in a stone and not one single thing has come of either, and I have been the one building the table the stone is on, and I would like it recorded that I have not been thanked for that and that I am not going to be, and that I have worked out today that the reason I built it was not to find anything out.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has worked out that the reason he built a second table was not to find anything out, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing out loud, to about nine people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure is for a person to be caught by. That is the other half of it and it is the half this yard has never once said out loud, and the man who reads four figures off a wall every morning has just said the first half and about nine of us have been living in the second half for a month without a word, and I have a word for the second half and I have had it for a month and I am still not going to say it.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman has a word and did not say it, and entered no word, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam then said one thing out loud, to about nine people, from about four feet off that first table, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He has read those four every morning for six months and about four of us have let him, and I have been in that ditch every morning for three weeks, and I am going to say the thing I have not said and then I am going back down it. Nobody has ever asked what any of us are for in that yard. Not one question in three weeks. I have got a figure in a stone and a man has got a figure on a wall and about four of us have got a rule we said out loud, and none of us has a thing anybody has asked us to do.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said nobody in that yard has asked what anybody in it is for in three weeks, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen then asked, out loud and in front of about nine people, what a man of fifty-six is for, and the man of fifty-six did not answer him and nobody else answered him either, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked a question out loud in that yard and entered the question and entered that nobody answered it and entered that she did not answer it and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table at about one and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of it in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are forty-two of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume, and a clerk entered that count and entered that it is a count of marks and not a figure about this morning and entered that a man who puts a mark in chalk on the edge of that table every morning has not been asked what his marks are for either. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and nobody asked him what he thinks a figure is for. At the east end of that yard the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing all morning.

The road keeper came up that lane a little after one, took the four figures off that wall on his way past, and was not asked about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three with its near wheel dragging behind it, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that lane. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and forty-seven days is how long that bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. One hundred and two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and a man said out loud in that yard on the twenty-fifth afternoon after the count that he is the man it is about. A hundred and seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and seventy-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and eighty days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and sixteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Fifty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are forty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved today. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man saying what a figure is for is not a thirteenth. Nobody advanced the reading of that lot on the twenty-sixth morning after the count and it stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of those is a person. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk entered that she did not ask anybody for a name and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked. A way to pay a person who is not in a household is the fifth of the five things this district does not have and it is unpaid, the count of the five is five, nothing has been proposed as a sixth, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

The buckets went down that bank at about half past five, both of them empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her that day. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stayed at that wall, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that a man who has said out loud in a yard that two figures in it are not doing what a figure is for went back to the wall afterwards and got the four again anyway, twice, and did not go and look at the second table.

A man of fifty-six said out loud in that yard on the twenty-sixth morning after the count that a figure is for a person to find out something they could not find out any other way, and that neither figure in that yard is doing it, and a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said out loud that a figure is also for a person to be caught by, and that she has a word for that half and is not going to say it, and a boy of about nineteen asked out loud in front of about nine people what a man of fifty-six is for, and nobody answered him, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and will be empty tomorrow morning.
