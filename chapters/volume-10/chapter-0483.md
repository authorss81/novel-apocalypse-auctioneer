# Chapter 483: The Fifth Thing, Said Out Loud

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against read the three lines in that lot book out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirteenth morning after the count, in front of about nineteen people, for the first time since that reading was begun, and said out loud what the fifth thing a document that sets a lot out has to say is, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that fifth thing as a fifth thing and entered that it is not paid and that naming it is not paying it.

It was the thirteenth morning after the count and the boards of that first table had gone from wet to dry in about an hour, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and twenty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

Once that morning the man of fifty-six said the four figures on that wall out loud and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and nobody in that yard crossed the four feet between that wall and the near end of that first table at any point in the day.

"**The board carries three hundred and eighty-four, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and fourteen, and three hundred and seventy-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He did it at about eleven and he did it standing up and he did not ask anybody whether he might, and about nineteen people watched a man who has refused to read anything out loud in that yard for a volume put his own hand on a book that is not his and open it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are three lines in it. I am not going to say what any of the three of them says, because one of them is a man's mouth and I am not going to be the man who reads that out in a yard, and because the two of you who have read it standing up have been saying nothing about it for a month, which is the correct thing to do. A document that sets a lot out has five things to say. This one says two. The third line is a man. The fourth is a person. The fifth is a remedy.**"

About nineteen people heard all of that, and about four of them have said since that a man who has refused to read a page out loud in that yard for a volume read the heading of one out loud in about nine seconds, and that nobody in that yard has worked out which of the two of those is braver and that the clerk is not going to decide it. Then he said the last of it, and the boy counted that and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The fifth thing is a remedy, and a remedy is a thing somebody can do about it on a day they can name, and there is nothing in this district anybody can do about anything on any day they can name, and a thing that cannot be done is not a remedy, and saying the word in a yard does not turn it into one. That is the fifth thing and it is the first time in five volumes that anybody in this yard has said it out loud.**"

Nobody said anything at all. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the fifth thing a document that sets a lot out has to say is a remedy, and entered that the count of the five things this district does not have is five, and entered that the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and entered that a remedy and a way to pay a person who is not in a household are two figures about the same gap and that neither of them is the gap, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the rest of it out loud, at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand and not on the boards, and about nineteen people heard it, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering it as a fifth thing. I am entering that it is not paid, and I am entering that naming it out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people is not paying it and is not going to be, and I am entering that the count stays at five and that nobody in this yard has proposed a sixth this morning and that I am not going to suggest one.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that no sixth was proposed, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table at about eleven and heard all of it, and he was the man the fifth of the five is about, and a clerk entered that he was there and entered that she entered that he was there and that nobody asked him whether he was the man it was about and that she is not going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven, in the ordinary way, and said nothing at all, and there are twenty-nine of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and said one thing in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He has read that book out loud and he has not read the one line that matters, and that is not a dodge, that is the first correct thing anybody in this yard has done with that book in a month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer a man who was right, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with the last of that day's flour on her hip, read the three lines in that lot book standing up, and went over and put her thumb in the hollow for about four seconds, and a clerk entered that she did and entered no figure about the hollow, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The reading did not advance. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the reading of that lot was looked at for about four minutes this morning by a man and entered that it stands where it has stood since the second of the twelfth month, at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and that the fourth is a person, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and put a thumb in the hollow on the second table on his way past. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and nobody spoke to him all morning, and nobody asked him whether a remedy is a thing a person can be given, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, got the four figures off that wall as he went past, and was not asked about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall at about half past three, his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and thirty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Eighty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Ninety-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and sixty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-seven days back and is open, and nothing was pulled off it and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Forty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and twenty-nine marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a fifth thing named out loud is not a thirteenth. It did not advance, and it stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of those is a person. Five people said a thing out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the count of them is still five, and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and six things now stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

At about half past five the two buckets came down that bank empty and went back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that he was standing about nine feet off a man reading that book out loud and that he did not move while it was happening.

For five volumes the fifth of the five things this district does not have has been on a page in a clerk's hand and has been said out loud by nobody, and on this morning a man stood in a yard in front of about nineteen people and said that the fifth thing a document like that has to say is a remedy, and that a remedy is a thing somebody can do on a day they can name, and that there is nothing in this district anybody can do on any day they can name, and a clerk of nineteen years entered all of it in her own hand and entered that it is not paid and that saying it is not paying it, and the count of the five is still five.
