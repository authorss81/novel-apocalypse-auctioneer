# Chapter 479: A Figure Nobody Made

A boy of about nineteen counted nine thumbs going into one hollow in one stone in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the tenth morning after the count and said the number out loud at about a quarter to twelve, and the number changed in the middle of the sentence, and a clerk of nineteen years was asked out loud whether that was a figure, and said that she did not know, and entered that she does not know.

It was the tenth morning after the count and the boards of that first table had the frost still in the cracks of them at about nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and twenty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six read the four off that wall at about half past eight in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, said the word with each of them and got the word four times, had a clerk write them down once, and said them again at about four and got all four again.

"**The board carries three hundred and eighty-one, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and ninety-seven days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and eleven, and three hundred and seventy-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The boy of about nineteen heard the third of those four and looked up at a sheet nailed to a gatepost about nine hundred yards off, and looked back at the man of fifty-six, and looked at his own page, and said nothing out loud about it for about three hours.

---

He had never counted anything in that yard that was not a number somebody had said out loud, and on this morning he counted with his own page against the edge of that second table and his own eyes, and the first of the thumbs went in at about ten past ten and the last of the ones he counted went in at about a quarter to twelve, and the woman of fifty-eight came past at about a quarter to twelve and put a thumb in it while he was standing there counting it.

He said it out loud to nobody in particular, in the ordinary voice, the way he says numbers, and the boy counted what he said and got forty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nine before her. That is nine and it has been nine since about eleven and it is a number about my morning in this yard and I have not put it in either of my columns because I have not been given a column to put it in.**"

Then the woman of fifty-eight's thumb came out of the hollow and he said the number again, and it was not the number, and about four people at that near end heard both of those numbers and about nine heard only the second one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

"**That is ten. I said nine and that is ten and the two of them were about two seconds apart and I have not got a way of writing down a number that is a different number about four seconds after I said it.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy counted nine and then ten and entered that he said both of them out loud and entered that the two of them were about two seconds apart, and entered no figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past twelve the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood about nine feet off that table and asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud, in front of about nine people, whether the number a boy had just said twice was a figure, and the clerk said that she did not know, and said the reason, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I do not know. I have five volumes of how to find out what a figure is and every one of them starts by somebody writing it down, and this one was not written down by anybody, it was made by a boy with his own eyes on his own page, and he is the reason it is right and he is standing nine feet off me, and there is no entry in this district for a reason and there is not going to be one.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked a question out loud in that yard and entered that she does not know, and entered the reason, and entered that a figure can be made by a person and that a person can be the reason a figure is right, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the rest of it out loud, standing at the near end of that first table with her page in her own hand and not on the boards, and about nineteen people heard it, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering all of that. I am entering that a figure nobody made cannot be a day out, because a day out is a fixed difference between two numbers and that one is not a fixed number. I am entering that it cannot be checked, because the second number is not the first one. I am entering that I do not know what it is, and I am entering that this district has no procedure for a figure it did not make, and that is not because the procedure is broken, it is because there has never been one, and I am not writing one this morning.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that no column was ruled and that none is going to be, and that the count of things this district has made is twelve, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and said one thing in about four seconds at about one, and the boy counted it and got sixty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He has run out of forms. He has got a thing in a yard she has never had before and the whole of what she can do with it is say she does not know, and I have watched her say she does not know four times in five volumes and this is the first one that has cost her anything.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody answered it and that she is not going to answer it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was not in that yard and nobody sent for him, and nobody asked the boy what his two columns are for, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was down in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm, put a thumb in the hollow on his way past, said nothing, and went on standing where he was.

The road keeper came up that lane at about two and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three, the near wheel on it still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up the lane. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nothing was offered to him and nothing was asked of him, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and seventy-second night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-one of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and thirty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it. Eighty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Ninety-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and sixty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-four days back and is open, and nothing was pulled off it and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Forty marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are twenty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and a boy in that yard counted nine and then ten this morning and neither of the two is in either of those counts. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a number a boy made with his own eyes is not a thirteenth, and a clerk entered that. Nothing got past the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say today, and the fourth of them is a person, and the reading of that lot is not finished. Four people said a thing out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud stood ruled and empty at about six, and nothing went into it. A clerk entered the same five things she entered a month ago, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, and the count is still five, and what the fifth of them is has not changed, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and nobody has proposed a sixth.

Down that bank at about half past five came two empty buckets and back up it they went full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and the boy of about nineteen said the four after him, out loud, in the same voice, and got the third of the four wrong on purpose to see what would happen, and got it right again four seconds later, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about either of the two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The third of the four figures that a man of fifty-six has read off that wall every morning for about two hundred and eight mornings is four hundred and eleven, and the figure on a sheet nailed to a gatepost nine hundred yards off that wall is the same figure to the last digit, and a boy of about nineteen is the only person in that yard who has noticed, and he has not written it in either of his columns and has not been given one, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she does not know whether a number she did not make is a figure and wrote no procedure for it and ruled no column for it, and that is the first morning in five volumes that this district has had a thing in it with no form on the page for it at all.
