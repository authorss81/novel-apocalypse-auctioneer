# Chapter 461: He Said Nothing

The clerk of nineteen years told a man of fifty-six, on the twenty-sixth of the twelfth month, in the open, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, that the third of the four figures he reads off that wall every morning is a day out from the day it names, and he read it out loud twice more that day and got it, and he said nothing.

It was the twenty-sixth of the twelfth month and about four people had stopped saying the third of the four along with him, and about four other people had noticed that they had stopped, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred and eight days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about half past eight in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and nobody said one word to him about any of it, and about four people at the near end of that first table watched a clerk of nineteen years walk to the wall and stand about four feet off him and wait.

"**The board carries three hundred and sixty-seven, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and eighty-three days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and ninety-seven, and three hundred and fifty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He has got that figure right every morning for a hundred and ninety-four mornings and nobody in that yard has ever run a check on him, and a check is not a question you ask a man in a yard.

---

She said it at about eleven, standing where anybody standing at that table could see her, and about nineteen people heard every word of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got one hundred and three and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and about four people near the east wall heard the number and not one of them heard what she was counting.

"**The third of the four figures you say out loud every morning is a day out from the day it names. The column says the twenty-fourth of November of the seventeenth year. From that day to this morning is three hundred and ninety-eight days and the column carries three hundred and ninety-seven, and the difference is one day, and the column stands, and I have altered nothing on any page, and I am not going to, and I am saying this in this yard and not to you, because I am not asking you a question and you are not being asked one.**"

He was standing at that wall with his palm flat on the boards about four feet off her. He took his hand off the boards, and he put it back, and he said nothing at all.

Nobody said anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six was told a thing in the open in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he said nothing, and entered that he was not asked a question and that she is not going to ask him one, and entered that nobody is going to ask him one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said out loud, to about nine people and not to him, that a man who has been getting a figure right every morning for six months and finds out this morning that the figure is a day out has not been doing anything wrong and has not been doing anything right, and that there is no third thing for him to have been doing, and that a person standing there who wants to go and tell him he was not wrong is going to have to stand in front of him and be quiet afterwards.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about twenty past eleven the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud and in the ordinary voice and in front of about nine people, whether the man of fifty-six had been asked whether he wanted to be told, and the clerk said that he had not and that he was not going to be, and the woman said that a man ought to be asked, and the clerk said that a man who reads a figure out loud every morning for six months has been asked nothing by anybody standing there ever and that she was not going to hand him a question at about eleven in the morning on the day he found out.

About four people heard all of that and about nine did not, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a question was asked out loud in that yard and entered the question and entered the answer and entered that she is not going to change it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank at about half past eleven and stood about nine feet off that table and said one thing.

"He has been the check in this yard for six months without knowing there was anything to check, and that is the whole of what has happened this morning, and I am not going to say another word about it today."

Nobody answered him and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and that he has not asked for anything and has not been given anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about twelve the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of the second table with his left hand in its cloth and said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got forty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He will say them again tomorrow morning and he will get them again tomorrow morning, and both of those are true, and I am not going to stand here and be sorry for him in front of about nineteen people.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk is not going to enter that a man is glad or sorry, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about one the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came down the lane, looked at the two tables, and went up the lane again, and nobody asked him why he had come down, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past one the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at that second table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fifty-eighth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-seven of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

She entered the rest of that day in one entry and read it out loud once, to about nine people and not to the yard, and did not read it back from the top, and entered that nobody read it back and entered the reason.

A hundred and seventeen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Seventy-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Seventy-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and forty-seven days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and fifty days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is eighty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Twenty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and twenty-six marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is still no figure in this district for how long this month is. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it and a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard this morning in front of about nineteen people, and no name went beside it, because a name was not asked for and a clerk did not ask for one. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about nine people standing near enough to see his face have said since that he looked at the third of the four when he said it, and that he looked at it the way he looks at all four of them, which is the way a man looks at a figure he has read every morning since before the middle of the sixth month.

Nobody in that yard asked him about it again, and nobody in that yard is going to, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she is not going to, and entered that the reason is that a man who has been asked a question about a figure he gets right every morning has been made into a man with a question about him, and that there is nobody in this district who can carry that and that she is not going to hand it to him this morning and let somebody else carry it after her.
