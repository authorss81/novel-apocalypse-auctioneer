# Chapter 485: Three Figures In One Entry

A clerk of nineteen years entered three figures in one entry in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fourteenth morning after the count, and read that entry out loud once to about nine people, and did not read it back from the top, and the three figures were a thing this district can now say out loud, a count of marks, and a column that has been ruled and empty for a month, and a person who walks up to that page cannot tell which of the three is the finding.

It was the fourteenth morning after the count and the light came up clean off the south end of that ground and there was no wind at all in it, and the boards of that second table had the first of the day's sun on them at about ten past nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and twenty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

Once that morning the man of fifty-six said the four figures on that wall out loud and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing near enough to hear him have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and twelfth of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and eighty-five, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and one days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and fifteen, and three hundred and seventy-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She wrote it at about half past ten and she said out loud before she wrote it that there were three figures in it and that she was going to read all three, and about nineteen people heard her say that, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got one hundred and eleven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are three figures in this entry and they are in one entry and I am not joining them. The first of the three is a figure about a thing this district can now say out loud, which is that a figure nobody has to put down cannot be a day out from a day it does not name, and that two men in this yard have said that sentence for two different reasons and a third man has proved it does not do what it looks like it does. That is a figure about a sentence and it has been on a page since the seventh morning after the count.**"

Then she read the second of the three, and about nine people heard all of it, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The second of the three is a count. Forty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are thirty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and the length of the twelfth month is thirty and is a different thirty, and those three figures are not the same figure and I am not joining them and I am not adding them. The count of things this district has made is twelve. That is a figure about a count of things and not about a day.**"

Then the third, which she took the longest over, and about nineteen people heard all of it, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The third of the three is a column that has been ruled and empty since the ninth day of this month. A clerk read an entry out loud in this yard three times in twelve days, once to about nine people and twice to about nineteen, and nobody has been asked for a name and a clerk has not asked anybody for one, and that column is still empty at about six this evening and it is going to be empty tomorrow morning, and I am entering all three of these figures on one page because I am tired, and a page with three figures in it is a ledger, and a ledger is the shape this yard has been in for ten volumes, and I am telling you that is what this is.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk read her own entry out loud once and entered that she did not read it back from the top, and entered that nobody read it back, and entered the reason, and entered that three figures in one entry is a ledger and that she has said so, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and said one thing in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**She has just named the shape this yard is in. Nobody has ever done that out loud and a clerk of nineteen has done it in a yard on a morning and it took her a whole entry and it did not change one thing.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer it, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about twelve, in the ordinary voice, to nobody in particular, and then said it again louder so that about nine people had it, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to keep counting the thumbs that go into that hollow every morning until somebody in this yard tells me to stop, and nobody is going to tell me to stop, and I am not writing it in either of my columns because I have not been given one.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a plan, and entered the reason, and that a plan is the one thing in this district that has no figure under it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and put a thumb in the hollow on the second table on his way past, and said nothing about how deep it is. The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came down the lane at about half past four, looked at the two tables, and went up the lane again, and nobody asked him why he had come down, and a clerk entered that she did not ask and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that near end have said since that a man who has told a yard twice this month where the count of twelve came from has walked down that lane and back up it four times this week without stopping, and that nobody has asked him what a table is for, and that he offered that in the open nine days ago and was not asked then either. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about one and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of a second table and said nothing to anybody, and nobody asked him whether the fourth of a second table was a fourth of the first one, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm, and nobody said a word to him.

Nobody asked the road keeper about the eleven miles when he came up that lane at about half past one and got the four figures off that wall as he went past. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was still sitting where he had been all afternoon at the foot of that low wall, his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and seventy-sixth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-five of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and thirty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Ninety days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Ninety-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and sixty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-eight days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Nothing in that day was corrected and nothing in it was filled. The reading of that lot did not advance and it stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person. A clerk read an entry out loud in that yard three times in twelve days and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and no name went into it on any of the three, and a clerk wanted it on the page a second time today and did not put anything in it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid and is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count of the five is still five with no sixth in it, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

The buckets went down that bank at about half past five, both of them empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, stayed where he was, and nobody asked him to come to the second table.

A district that can say out loud what it cannot check has not got a way to check it, and the naming is a figure and the checking is a thing, and a figure about a thing is not the thing, and this district has said the first half of that out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people on two mornings and a clerk of nineteen years entered three figures in one entry this morning and told about nine people that a page with three figures in it is a ledger and that a ledger is the shape this yard has been in for ten volumes, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and it will be there tomorrow morning, and nobody in that yard is going to put a name in it.
