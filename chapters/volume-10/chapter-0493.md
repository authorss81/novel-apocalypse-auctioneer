# Chapter 493: The Boy Asks Whether Two Hands Make A Figure

A boy of about nineteen asked a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, out loud, in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-first morning after the count, in front of about nineteen people, whether two hands in one hollow in a stone at the same time make one figure or two, and she answered him out loud in the ordinary voice, and the answer was that she did not know and that a clerk had already written on a page in this yard that she did not know about a different thing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked a question out loud in that yard and entered the question and the answer and the reason, and entered that the yard now has two questions in it with the same answer and neither of them is a procedure.

It was the twenty-first morning after the count and there was no wind at all in that ground and the light came up white off the south end of it and the boards of both tables were dry and cold by about eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and thirty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing near enough to hear that have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and nineteenth of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and ninety-two, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and eight days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and twenty-two, and three hundred and eighty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He asked it at about ten past ten with his own thumb about to go into that hollow and about nine people standing close enough to see it, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**If I put my thumb in there and a man puts his thumb in there at the same time, and I am talking the whole time so he knows I am in there, and about nineteen people can see both of us doing it, is that one figure or is that two? I have been putting thumbs in that hollow for a month and I have never once had anybody in there with me and I have not been able to work out whether that is because nobody wanted to or because nobody has.**"

About nineteen people heard all of it, and the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale answered him without being asked twice, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I do not know, and I have written that I do not know on a page in this yard already this month, and about nine people know I have, and now there are two questions in this yard with the same answer in them and the answer does not get any stronger by being said twice. Two hands at once is either a figure about two people or it is a figure about a stone, and I cannot get from here to which of the two it is, and neither can you, and that is the honest size of what any of us know about that hollow.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that two questions in that yard have the same answer and that the answer is not a procedure and that no column is ruled for it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing then put his own right thumb in that hollow and left it there for about four seconds and said one thing out loud while it was in there, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got fifty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have put a thumb in there once this month and I counted it, and I did not know there was anybody counting, and I have found out this week that about nine people have been keeping figures about the four of us who do it. I am not going to say that out loud twice.**"

The boy of about nineteen then said one thing back, out loud, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been counting since the nineteenth of the twelfth month and I told a clerk of nineteen years nine days ago that I was going to keep counting until somebody in this yard told me to stop, and nobody has told me to stop, and I am not stopping, and I am not writing it in either of my columns because I have not been given one, and I have not been given a column because there are four columns in this district and no fifth one is going to be ruled.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk of nineteen years said on the fourteenth morning after the count that a plan is the one thing in this district with no figure under it, and entered that this is not a plan, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table at about eleven and heard all of it, and a clerk entered that he was there and entered that nobody asked him whether a man who puts a mark in chalk on the edge of that table every morning is one of the four people a thumb goes into that hollow, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He put his own mark on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing, and there are thirty-seven of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that ditch at about ten, got the four figures at the near end of that first table, and said the word with them four times. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at that east end with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing, and about nine people have said since that nobody has asked him in a volume.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came down that lane at about two, put his own hand flat on the boards of that second table for about a second, took it off again, and went back up the lane without saying anything to anybody, and about nine people saw him do it and about four of them have said since that a man who has walked that lane five times in eleven days to say one sentence has come down it again today to put a hand on a table he built himself and has gone back up without saying a word, and that nobody asked him why, and the record about the not asking says not asked. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man put a hand on the boards of that second table for about a second and entered that she did not ask him why and entered that she is not going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A little after one the road keeper came up that lane, took the four off that wall without breaking his walk, and was not asked about the eleven miles by anybody standing there. The cart came up that lane at about three with the near wheel dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody. At half past three a man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything, nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-third night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and forty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Ninety-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and seventy-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and seventy-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and eleven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Fifty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are thirty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved today. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a second question with the same answer is not a thirteenth. Nobody advanced the reading of that lot today and it stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of those is a person. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk entered that she did not put anything in it and entered that she is tired of looking at it, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The count of the five is five with no sixth proposed and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, which is not paid, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

At about half past five the two buckets came down that bank empty and went back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and went back up that lane about nine feet past the second table without stopping, and nobody asked him to and nobody asked him why, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and about four people standing near enough to see it have said since that a man who has stood at that wall every morning for six months walked past a table for about a year and a half of a volume and did not look down at it.

Two hands in one hollow at the same time in front of about nineteen people is either a figure about two people or a figure about a stone, and a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said out loud in that yard that she cannot get from here to which of the two it is, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two questions in that yard have the same answer and that the answer is not a procedure and that no column is ruled for it, and nothing was ruled and nothing was filled and the reading of that lot did not advance and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was empty at about six.
