# Chapter 468: The Morning After A Count

The morning after the count, a clerk of nineteen years entered in the yard of Lot Seventeen that the length of the month the district has just finished living in is thirty and that she is not entering it as a promise about a later day, and a man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall and got all four, and about nine people in that yard have said since that nobody in this district knew what a day was this morning until about eleven, and that the day was the first day of a month nobody has counted, and that a figure somebody gets right every morning does not care what kind of day it is standing on.

It was the morning after a month was counted, and the light came up white and hard and clean off the south end of that ground, and the boards of that first table were dry and cold under about four hands and nobody had put a stone back on the corner of that book.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred and thirteen days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote them down, and about four people at that near end have said since that she wrote all four down this morning and that she has not written one of them down for four days, and that nobody in that yard has worked out yet whether that is a rule or a habit.

"**The board carries three hundred and seventy-two, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and eighty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and two, and three hundred and sixty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He has got that figure right every morning for a hundred and ninety-nine mornings and the third of the four is a day out from the day it names and he knows that and has not asked about it and nobody is going to ask him, and the difference is still one day, and the column still stands.

---

About four people came down that bank that morning expecting a yard where something had been settled, and the clerk of nineteen years told them at about eleven, out loud, in the ordinary voice, what the morning was for, and about nineteen people heard it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Yesterday I counted a month and I entered the figure, and I am entering the count again this morning because a clerk who counts a thing once has not written it down, and this time I am entering what I am not entering with it. I am not entering thirty as a figure about today, and I am not entering it as a figure about the month this district is living in now, and I am not entering it as a figure anybody can use to work out a day they have not lived through. I am entering it as a figure about a count of marks off a board, and the first of that month is counted in and the thirtieth is counted in, and that is all thirty is.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a count made yesterday is not a promise about a day and entered that the difference between those two things is the whole of what she has been entering for a month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about four seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got forty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A month is the shortest thing this district can count, and it is the only one of them that stops when somebody stops counting it, and everybody in this yard is going to find out what that is like by about the middle of next month.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard, entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the sentence, and about nineteen people heard it, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure somebody entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day. I have written that sentence five times in five volumes. The second time was a habit, and a habit is a finding, and I have been calling the third and the fourth of them habits for a month because I did not have anything else to call them, and I have not got anything else to call this one either, and I am not going to stand here and pretend that not having a word for the fifth of something is the same as having a finding about it.**"

Nobody said anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that sentence the fifth time, and entered that the second time was a habit, and entered that a habit is a finding, and entered that she has no word for the fifth and is not going to invent one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

By ten there were about nineteen people in that yard, and about four of them have said since that the whole of the difference between this morning and every morning in the last month is that a figure on that wall went up by one yesterday, and that going up by one is the only thing a figure on that wall has ever done, and that it went up by one yesterday and the month it was counting finished, and nobody standing at that wall was told which of those two things had happened.

---

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch from about a quarter to eight until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day. At about half past eleven the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and said nothing at all, and nobody said anything to him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came down that bank and stood at the east end of that yard with his flat book under his left arm for about three hours and a half, and nobody walked over to him and nobody asked him for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she stood a minute and a half beside him yesterday and about a minute beside him this morning and entered that she asked him nothing both times and entered the reason.

At about half past one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood about nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat and said one thing.

"You counted a month and the month was thirty and the figure you had off the month before was thirty, and both of those are the same figure, and the whole of what you have found out is that this district has been carrying numbers correctly for six months by accident, and that is not a smaller thing than finding out you have been carrying them wrongly, and nobody is going to congratulate you and I am not going to be the man who does."

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and that he was not thanked and that she is not going to thank him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came down the lane, looked at the two tables, and went up the lane again, and nobody asked him why he had come down, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and sixty-third night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-two of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

The rest of that day went in as one entry, and a clerk of nineteen years read it out loud to about nine people and did not read it back from the top, and entered that nobody read it back and entered the reason.

A hundred and twenty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Seventy-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Eighty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and fifty-two days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and fifty-five days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is ninety-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Thirty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and that figure went up by one this morning, and it is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month. The length of the month the district has just finished living in is thirty, counted off that board yesterday in the open, and the first day of that month is counted in. There is no figure on any page in this district for how long the month this district is living in now is, and a clerk who entered one this morning off the month before it would be entering a figure about a list, and she said that out loud in that yard yesterday and said she would not do it, and she has not done it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. Three columns on that wall are a day out from the days they name and none of the three was corrected, and one convention on that wall cannot be settled and was not settled, and the clerk entered that she is not choosing between the two ways of counting a day that this district has printed in its own documents. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it and a clerk read a sentence out loud in that yard this morning and no name went beside it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them, and about four people at that near end have said since that a month got counted yesterday and nothing on that wall changed, and that the two of those are the same morning and that one of them was a month of this district's calendar and the other of them was a morning on a ladder, and that the ladder does not know what to do with a day that has been counted and goes on counting itself in any case.

A month that had never been counted was counted yesterday in a yard, and this morning there is a figure in this district that there was not there on the eighth of this month, and the debt is twenty-two days old and paid, and about four people in that yard have worked out that the three columns on that wall are still a day out and that the month is thirty and that neither of those two facts has done anything to the other one.
