# Chapter 477: Nine Thumbs And A Number

Nineteen days after nine people put a thumb in the hollow in the underside of that stone one after another in front of about nineteen people, a man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank at about ten past ten, wet to the knee, and put his thumb in it, and he was not one of the nine, and he said nothing whatever about how deep it is, and by four in the afternoon nine people had put a thumb in it one after another and a clerk of nineteen years entered the number and entered no figure about the hollow at all.

It was the eighth morning after the count in the yard of Lot Seventeen and the light came up hard off the south end of that ground, and a second table that a man put up with his own hands in the first week of this month had gone from cold to warm on its boards in about half an hour, and the stone was lying face up on them where it has lain since the sixteenth of this month, and the hand of a man who mends fencing had not put a thumb in it since the nineteenth of this month.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and twenty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count and never has had one. The four figures on that wall were said out loud once in the morning by the man of fifty-six and got by him, and entered once by a clerk, and said again at about four and got again.

"**The board carries three hundred and seventy-nine, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and ninety-five days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and nine, and three hundred and seventy days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

By ten there were about nineteen people in that yard and about four of them were standing at the near end of that first table and about nine of them were within nine feet of the second one, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam was not one of the nineteen, because he was at the bottom of that ditch with the water to his knee, and nobody standing in that yard knew where he was at any point in the morning.

He came up the bank on the east side at about ten past ten and did not go to the first table and did not look at that wall, and he crossed the ground in four steps and put his right thumb into the hollow in the underside of that stone without taking the stone off the boards and without turning it, and he had to lift it about two inches to get his thumb under it, and he held it that way for about four seconds with his thumb in and about nine people watching him do it.

Then he put the stone back down face up exactly the way it had been lying, and he said one thing, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in that ditch every morning since the eighth of this month and I could have come up and done this on any morning of them, and I did not, twice a week at least, and I never once thought about it, and I am not going to say why, and I am not going to be asked.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man put a thumb in that hollow and entered what he said, and entered that she is not going to ask him why he did not do it on any of the mornings he could have done it on, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale did it, and she had the last of that day's flour on her hip where it usually is, and she put her thumb in and took it out and said nothing, and about four people at that near end have said since that she has put a thumb in that hollow on and off since the eighth day of this month and that nobody has ever counted how many times and that on the morning she first put one in she said nothing about it and went back up the bank, and that nothing has been entered about any of it since. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman put a thumb in that hollow and entered no figure about it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds did it on his way past with the bundle under his arm, without stopping, and did not say anything, and did not put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table afterwards until about ten past eleven, and nobody said a word to him about either of the two things he did. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man put a thumb in that hollow and entered that a man put a mark in chalk on the edge of that table and entered that she joined the two and entered why she joined them, and the reason is that one of them is a figure about a thing and the other of them is a figure about a man and she was not going to put them in one entry with a figure that could be checked in between them.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the far end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and watched the whole of it and did not put a thumb in anything, and about four people at that table have said since that a man who turned that stone over on the eighth day of this month stood nine feet from nine thumbs going into it and said nothing at all, and about nine of them have said that he is not going to and that is the whole of what they expect.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard from about one with the flat book under his left arm and did not come over, and a clerk walked about nine feet towards him at about half past one and stopped and entered that she had not asked him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was asked nothing about the second thumb and said nothing about it either, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank at about two, stood about nine feet off that table, looked at the hollow for about eleven seconds, and said one thing, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A check that costs nothing is not a check, and the whole of this yard knows what a check costs, because a man put a hand on a figure for two minutes in this yard in a volume ago and has had a hand that has not closed since the eleventh of the June. Nine thumbs cost nothing. Nine of them is not a check. It is nine people being warm.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a check costs something and that she does not know what nine thumbs cost, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane, got the four figures off that wall as he went past, and stopped at that second table for about four seconds with his hand out and his thumb an inch off the stone, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and went on up the lane. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and did not stop, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and seventieth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-nine of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

She entered the rest of the figures in the same entry and in the same handwriting and did not open a second page for any of them, and the whole of that is one paragraph. Eighty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Eighty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and fifty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-two days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is ninety-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything. Thirty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are twenty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and the two counts are two counts and neither of them is a figure about a day. The count of things this district has made is twelve and a thumb is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person, and it did not advance today. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and four people said a thing out loud in that yard today and no name went beside any of the four. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

She wrote the whole of the afternoon into one entry while it was happening, standing up, with about nine people within nine feet of her, and she said out loud before she wrote it which of the two things in it was the number, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering the number. Nine thumbs have gone into that hollow today and I am entering nine, and the nine is a figure about a morning and it is not a figure about a day and it is not a figure about a stone, and I am entering no figure about the hollow at all, because nobody has asked me for one and because there is not one. A hundred and twenty-nine days is how long the bid has been open and it was not run today, and I am putting that in this entry and not on its own line, because this is the one day this month I am not opening a page of my own for it.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the word in the margin of her own page, which is unchecked, and which is over nothing, and which has been there since the first day of the eleventh month and is not going on any figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the last of it out loud, and about nineteen people heard it, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure anybody can put a thumb into is a figure that carries nobody in it, and that is the whole of why that stone is the only thing in this yard that can be checked, and it is not better than the other four, and I am not going to enter that it is better, and there is no entry in this district that would let me enter that it is better.**"

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight put a thumb in that hollow on her way past without slowing down and did not say anything, and read the three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and did not come over to the second table, and nobody asked him to.

Nine thumbs went into one hollow in one stone on one table in one yard on one morning, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the number and no figure, and a man who digs loam went back down that bank at about half past four with the water coming off him and nobody in that yard asked him the one question his own mouth had put up for asking, and the count of things this district has made is twelve and it did not move, and the only instrument any of them used that day was a thumb, and about four people at that near end have said since that a thumb is not an instrument and that is the end of the argument they have had about it.
