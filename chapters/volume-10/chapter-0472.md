# Chapter 472: Three Things About One Figure

The three of them said three different things about the same figure in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fourth morning after the count, in front of about nineteen people, and a clerk of nineteen years entered all three, and joined none of them, and the figure they were all talking about is a hollow in the underside of a stone that anybody standing there can put a thumb into.

It was the fourth morning after the count and the air had gone thin the way it goes thin here, and the boards of that second table had a shadow on them from about eleven that was the right length for the handrail of a barrow, and by ten there were about nineteen people in it.

At about a quarter to eight, with that yard empty behind her, a clerk of nineteen years entered that four hundred and eleven is the figure on the sheet at that gatepost and that it did not move, and entered two hundred and sixteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age is a figure about the figure and that there is no day-count for the sheet itself.

The man of fifty-six read the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and a clerk wrote them down once, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that the man of fifty-six is the only person in this district who says a number out loud before anybody has written it and gets it right both times, and that nobody has ever thought to count how many mornings that is.

"**The board carries three hundred and seventy-five, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and ninety-one days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and five, and three hundred and sixty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

They came at it in three goes and the first of the three was at about half past ten and the last of them was at about two, and between the first and the last of them about nine people in that yard changed their minds about something and did not say what.

The first was the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, and she had the last of that day's flour on her hip where it usually is, and she put her hand flat on the boards of the second table beside the stone and said one thing out loud to about nine people, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure anybody can check with a thumb is a figure that carries nobody in it, and I have been the only person in this yard arguing for six weeks that a figure has to have a person in it to be worth entering, and I was wrong, and the hollow in that stone has no person in it and it is the only figure any of us can check, and I would like it recorded that I was wrong by a thumb.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she had said she was wrong, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The second was the man of about thirty-four who digs loam, at about half past eleven, coming up out of that bank with the water coming off him onto the boards, and he stood there dripping and said one thing and nobody answered him, and the boy counted it and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in that ditch every morning since the eighth of this month and I could have come up and put my thumb in that hollow on any morning of them, and I did not, twice a week at least, and I never once thought about it, and I have not worked out whether that is because I did not want to know how deep it is or because it was nobody's business.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer it, and that his right arm did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, who had been at the far end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth through all of the first of those two, said one thing at about twelve in about four seconds and it was the shortest thing anybody said in that yard all day, and the boy counted it and got ten and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The wear is a figure about a book standing still.**"

Then he put his right hand flat on the boards on the other side of the stone and said nothing else at all, and about four people at that near end have said since that ten words are the whole of what has been said in that yard about a thing everybody agrees on.

The boy of about nineteen asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud and standing where about nine people could hear, whether a hollow in a stone was a figure, and the clerk said that she did not know and entered that she does not know, and the boy asked her a second thing, which was whether a hollow was a figure because a book had been put on top of it, and the clerk said that a hollow is a figure about a book standing still, and that a book standing still is a thing about five people in this district do every working day, and entered both questions and both answers and joined none of them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The third was the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, and he came at about two and stood about nine feet off that table and did not touch the stone, and he said one thing, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Three of you have now said out loud that that stone is the only figure in this yard anybody can check, and the three of you are all right, and I want it entered that the three of you agreeing is not a check. A check is somebody doing the thing twice. The three of you have done nothing twice, and the three of you have got the same answer out of a thumb.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that three people had said that one figure in that yard can be checked and that she entered all three of them and joined none of them, and entered that a person agreeing with a person is not a check, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that near end said one thing over the course of the morning and none of them said it to anybody in particular, which is that on the morning the ninth thumb went into that hollow three people had stood about nine feet apart and said the same sentence about the same stone and had each been certain, and that on this morning four of them have put a thumb in it and said nothing, and that nobody in that yard has worked out whether the three of them said the same sentence.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was not in that yard again on this day and nobody sent for him and nobody asked where he was. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that second table at about ten, as he does every morning, and did not say anything about it, and about four people watched him do it and about nine did not, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody spoke to the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally all afternoon. He stood at the east end of that yard from about one with the flat book under his left arm, and nobody asked him what he made of the three sentences, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and took the four figures off that wall as he went past, then stood for about four seconds at the second table with his hand out and his thumb nowhere near the stone, was not asked about the eleven miles, and went on up the lane. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about nine people stood about four feet away and did not say a word. From about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing at all in his hands, four people within nine feet of him did not look at him, nothing was offered to him and nothing was asked of him, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day in one entry and read it out loud once, to about nine people and not to the yard, and did not read it back from the top, and entered that nobody read it back and entered the reason.

Nothing in that entry was corrected and nothing in it was filled. A hundred and twenty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it anywhere. Eighty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and nothing has been written beside it and the third line is still a date and still a figure of a man. Eighty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. A hundred and fifty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and fifty-eight days back and is open, and nothing was pulled. A body four hundred miles off is ninety-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Thirty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, which is a count of marks and not a day. The count of things this district has made is still twelve, and three people agreeing with each other is not a thirteenth, and a clerk entered that on this morning's page. The reading of that lot is not finished and is not going to be finished this morning, and it stands where it stood yesterday, at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and that fourth one is a person. Nothing at all went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, which was ruled and empty at about six, and four people said a thing out loud in that yard today and not one of the four has a name beside what he said. The fifth of the five things this district does not have has not been paid, and the count of what this district does not have is five, and what the fifth of them is has not changed, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and nobody put a sixth in front of anybody.

The two buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall one last time in that yard and got all four of them, and about nine people standing near enough to see his face have said since that he looked at the third of the four the whole time he said it and showed nothing at all, and that a man showing nothing is not the same thing as a man who has nothing to show.

Three people in that yard said out loud on one morning that one figure in it can be checked, and a clerk entered the three of those in three sentences and did not join them, and the three of them had all got the same answer out of the same thumb, and about four people at that near end have worked out since that the one figure in that yard anybody can check is worth exactly as much as the three that nobody can, and that the reason is that agreeing with each other is not doing anything twice.
