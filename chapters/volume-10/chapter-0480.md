# Chapter 480: Three Certainties And No Figure

About nine people in the yard of Lot Seventeen argued on the tenth morning after the count, at the middle of the afternoon, about a number a boy had made with his own eyes, and about four of them were certain, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the three certainties and entered no figure at all and entered that a certainty is not a figure.

By ten there were about nineteen people in that yard and about four of them were still in it at half past four, and the light had come back along the face of that bank by then and the boards of that second table had gone cold again at about four, and the four figures on that wall had not been said out loud a second time that day and a clerk entered that and entered the reason, which is that a figure a man reads off a wall every morning is not entered into a page twice in one day.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about a quarter to eight, with that yard empty behind her, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered that the age of that figure is two hundred and twenty-two days, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count.

The first of the three was the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds and he came at about half past two and he had the bundle under his arm and he put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table before he said anything at all, and then he said it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is a figure. I do not care that nobody wrote it down. I stood nine feet off him and I watched his eyes go down that stone nine times and then I watched them go down it ten times, and that is a man making a figure with his own two eyes, and I have made one with my own two legs and nobody wrote that one down either, and I am certain about it in front of about nineteen people.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he said he was certain, and entered that a clerk is not entering a certainty as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The second of the three came at about ten past three and it was the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally and he had the flat book open under his left arm where nobody had asked him to open it, and he said one thing, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is not a figure, and I am as certain about that as he is about the other one. I have been putting numbers on pages for about thirty years and I have never in my life had one come out of a man's eyes that I could not go and find somewhere it had been put. If it is not on a page it did not happen, and I do not say that to be unkind, I say it because it is the only rule I have got and I am standing in the open using it.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he said he was certain, and entered that she is not entering his certainty as a figure either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The third of the three came at about twenty past three and it was the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale and she put her hand flat on the boards of that second table beside the stone and said one thing, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is a figure about his morning and it is not a figure about that stone, and both of those are the same true thing, and the two of you have been arguing all afternoon about which one of the two of them it is, and that is not a question anybody can settle by being sure. I have got a word for a figure that is true for a while and then is not and I am not going to say it, and it is not this one, and that is as far as I am going.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman had a word and did not say it, and entered no word, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen then read his own page out loud, which he has not done in that yard in a month, and about nine people heard the numbers on it, and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud, whether she was going to copy them, and the clerk said that she was not, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to copy a number out of a boy's own page into mine, because the day I do that it is on a page in this district and not in his head, and the only thing anybody in this yard has ever been able to do about a figure is write it down somewhere, and I have watched that happen to four things this month and I have not seen one of them come back different.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked her a question out loud in that yard and entered the question and the answer and the reason, and entered that she is not copying a number out of anybody's own page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing out loud, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You are all certain. That is three of you certain about the same ten minutes and you have got three different answers out of it, and I have watched this yard do this exact thing for three weeks over a column and it has never once settled anything, and it is not going to settle this either, and the only instrument this district has for telling a certainty from a wrong answer is a day, and there is no day in that boy's morning, and a day is not an instrument, it is a thing we all agreed to count with.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody asked the boy which of the two of them he agreed with, and a clerk entered that she did not ask and that she is not going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said nothing at all that afternoon, and about four people at that near end have said since that a man who was in that yard when nine thumbs went into a hollow yesterday and who has said nothing about any of it since is the only person in it who has not been certain about anything, and that nobody is going to ask him whether being quiet is a position.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of that bank at about one and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and nobody asked him which of the three certainties in that yard was the right one, and the record about the not asking says not asked. A clerk of nineteen years entered that three people had said out loud in that yard on that afternoon that they were certain and that she entered the three of them and no figure, and entered that a certainty is not a figure and that a certainty is a thing a person says, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The road keeper came up that lane at about two, took the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up at about three and went on up it with the near wheel still dragging, got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and did not stop for anybody. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall at about half past three with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and about nine people within four feet of him were busy being certain about something else, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-second night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-one of them, and that she is not going to put down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Nothing in that afternoon was altered and nothing in it was filled. The bid has stood open a hundred and thirty-one days, it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. The figure on the second line of that lot book is eighty-six days behind, nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. The rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month, which is ninety-one days. It is a hundred and sixty-one days since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-four days back, and is open, and nothing has been pulled and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard has watched any of it.

Forty marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are twenty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this afternoon. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and three certainties are not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot has not finished and it stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person. Four people said a thing out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk wanted it on the page a second time and did not put anything in it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count of the five is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

Both buckets were full and went back up that bank at about half past five, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them, and about four people standing near enough to see his face have said since that he was about nine feet off four people being certain about a number and did not look over.

Three certainties went onto one page of a clerk's book that afternoon and a clerk of nineteen years wrote them down and wrote nothing else, and about four people at that near end have worked out since that the one thing this district has always been able to do with a new object in it is be certain in front of each other about it, and that on this afternoon it did that and got nothing, and the number is still in a boy's own page and still in no column of anybody's.
