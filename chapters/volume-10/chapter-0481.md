# Chapter 481: Is It A Figure About A Person

A boy of about nineteen asked a clerk of nineteen years, out loud and in front of about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the eleventh morning after the count, whether the wear in the underside of that stone was a figure about a person, and was told no, and the reason was given out loud, and a clerk entered the question and the answer and the reason and entered that she noticed while she was saying it that the answer is also the one a district gives when it wants a thing to stop mattering.

It was the eleventh morning after the count and there was a clean hard light on the boards of that second table by about half past nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and twenty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The four figures on that wall were said out loud once in the morning by the man of fifty-six and got by him, and entered once by a clerk, and said again at about four and got again.

"**The board carries three hundred and eighty-two, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and ninety-eight days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and twelve, and three hundred and seventy-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He asked it at about eleven and he asked it badly, which is the only way anybody in that yard asks anything, and he had his own page in his hand and he did not put it down on the boards.

"**Is that a figure about a person.**"

Nobody answered it for about nine seconds and about nine people at that near end worked out while nobody was saying anything that the question was about the man who turned that stone over, and about four of them worked out that it was about the five people who have carried that book to that table, and neither of those was the question and neither of them was the answer.

The clerk of nineteen years said it out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. It is a figure about a book standing still on a table. That is all it is and there is nobody in it, and the reason is that a wear does not have one person in it, it has got whoever carried the book up to that table and put it down and squared it and went away, and in this district that is five people over two months and not one of them was asked and not one of them was thanked and none of the five of them did it more than about two hundred mornings.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a question was asked out loud in that yard and entered the question, and entered the answer, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the other thing, and about nineteen people heard all of it, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I want it entered that I noticed, while I was saying that, that no is also the answer a district gives when it wants a thing to stop mattering, and wanting a thing to stop mattering is not the same as the thing not mattering, and I am not going to give you a second reason to hold on to and I am not going to pretend to you that those two things are the same. It is a figure about a book standing still. That is what I have got and I am not going to get any further than that in a yard.**"

Nobody answered her and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer that a second time, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against then asked, out loud and in front of about nine people, who the fifth of those five people was, and about four people at that near end looked at the clerk of nineteen years and about nine of them looked at the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and neither of those two said anything at all for about eleven seconds and nobody has said since which of the two of them that was about.

"**I have been in that yard every day of it and I have watched a woman read three lines in that book every working day for two months, and I can tell you that four of the five of them are not clerks, and I cannot tell you who the fifth one is, and the fifth one is the only one of the five that could settle the question you have just asked her in that yard.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a question was asked out loud in that yard and entered the question, and entered that she does not know who the fifth person is, and entered that she is not going to say she does not know it because she is nineteen, and entered that a man who was one of the five was about nine feet off that table and was not asked anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and the boy asked him nothing at all about any of it, and a clerk entered that she asked him nothing and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and at about half past eleven he put his own thumb in that hollow on his way past and said nothing about how deep it is, and nobody asked him, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him and entered no figure about the hollow, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came down the lane at about twelve, looked at the two tables, and went up the lane again, and nobody asked him why he had come down, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about half past twelve and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that second table, and there are twenty-seven of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume, and nobody asked him for the first of them.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard from about one with the flat book under his left arm, and nobody asked him whether he was one of the five, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about one with the last of that day's flour on her hip, read the three lines in that lot book standing up, and went over and put her thumb in the hollow for about four seconds, and a clerk entered that she did and entered no figure about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about four people watched him do it and said nothing. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and seventy-third night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-two of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and thirty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Eighty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Ninety-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and sixty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and sixty-five days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Forty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and twenty-seven marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table, and the two counts are two counts. The count of things this district has made is twelve and a wear is not a thirteenth and a wear is not a figure about a person. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person, and it did not advance today. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and three people said a thing out loud in that yard today and no name went beside any of the three. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

Nobody put anything on that stone to measure it and nobody scraped it and nobody tried to make the hollow any deeper or any shallower, and a clerk entered that nobody did and entered that she is not going to ask anybody to.

At about half past five the two buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and a woman of fifty-eight read three lines standing up on her way past without stopping and without saying one word to anybody, and about four people at that near end watched a woman read a book she reads every working day and said nothing either. The man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day and got all four of them.

The one figure in that yard that anybody can check with a thumb is a figure about a book standing still on a table, and the book has been standing on that table for two months, and about five people in that district have carried it up there and put it down and squared it and gone away, and the wear is the only figure in that yard that is about all five of them and about none of them, and a clerk of nineteen years said so out loud in front of about nineteen people and then said out loud that she could not tell whether she had said the true thing or the comfortable one, and entered both of those and joined neither to the other.
