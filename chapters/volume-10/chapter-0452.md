# Chapter 452: A Figure Nobody Comes For

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard from about half past nine until about one with a flat book held under his left arm against his ribs, and nobody walked over to him, and he was not asked for anything, and that is four hours of a morning in a yard with about nineteen people in it.

It was the eighteenth of the twelfth month and the light was the colour of a wet road and it did not get any lighter.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred days exactly, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times.

"**The board carries three hundred and fifty-nine, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and seventy-five days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and eighty-nine, and three hundred and fifty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He has stood at that end of that yard about twice a week for two volumes. The book under his arm is flat and about the size of two hands together and it is not a lot book and it is not a page of the clerk's and there is nothing on the outside of it that anybody in that yard could read at four feet.

The clerk of nineteen years knows that he is there. She has known it for about two months and she has never written his name and she has never entered a figure off that book and she has never asked him for one, and the reason she gave out loud, twice, is that a figure somebody has not been asked for is a figure of that man's and not of anything in the yard.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about forty-eight stood at the east end of that yard for about four hours with a flat book under his left arm, and entered that she did not ask him for anything, and entered that nobody asked him for anything, and entered that his book is not a page and is not a column and is not a lot book and is not one of the things this district has made and not named, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen went over to that end of the yard at about half past ten and stood about two feet off the man and said out loud, in the ordinary voice, that he wanted to know what the book was, and the man of about forty-eight looked at him and said one thing.

"It is a tally of six households and I keep it and it is not yours and it is not hers." He did not open it. "It came out of a ladder and not off a board, and the day it was started is a day I can show you on a piece of paper, and nobody has ever asked me for the piece of paper, and if I show it to about nine people this morning then a figure of mine moves for a reason I made up standing here, and it has not moved in two volumes and I am not going to move it on the eighteenth of the twelfth month because a boy of nineteen stood near me."

Nobody answered him. The She entered that a man had said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that she did not ask for the piece of paper, and entered that the piece of paper is a thing about a man and is not a thing she can enter, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally had been at the east end of it since about half past nine with the flat book under his arm.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up out of the lane at about eleven with his left hand in its cloth and went and stood at the second table and put his right thumb in the hollow of the stone lying face up on it, and took it out, and did not say one word about it, and about four people at that table have now done that and not one of them has said how deep the hollow is.

Then he said one thing out loud, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a figure in a book under that man's arm that nobody has ever asked him for, and there is a date in a book on that table that is a man, and there is a hollow in a stone on this table that nobody has to enter, and those are three of a kind in one yard, and a woman of nineteen years stood at that wall for eleven minutes on the day before yesterday and did not write anything down at all, and I have not asked her why and I am not going to ask her today.**"

Nobody answered him. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering how many of them there are and did not enter a number, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen wrote three short lines on his own sheet at about one and a half past eleven, one for each of the three things, and he wrote them in the right-hand half of that sheet, which is the half that is a page and not a day, and then he ruled a fourth line under the other three and left it empty, and about four people noticed that he had ruled it and not one of them asked him why he had ruled it.

A clerk of nineteen years went and stood about two feet off the man of about forty-eight at about eleven for about a minute and a half and did not ask him for anything, and about nine people at that table watched a clerk stand next to a man with a flat book and say nothing, and after a minute and a half she went back to the first table and about four of those nine have said since that a minute and a half is a long time to stand next to a person without asking them anything.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale asked one question out loud in that yard at about half past eleven, and she did not ask it to him. She asked it to the clerk of nineteen years and she asked it in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard it.

"What would you do with it if somebody handed it to you."

"Nothing," said the clerk of nineteen years, "and I have given that answer before and I am giving it again in front of about nine people, and the reason is that a figure somebody hands you is a figure somebody chose to hand you, and this yard has been ruined once already by a figure somebody had to enter, and I would rather be the clerk who was never handed anything than the clerk who was handed a thing and wrote it down."

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally did not look up when she said that, and about four people noticed that he did not look up, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not look up and entered no reason for it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about one the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and looked at the flat book under that man's arm for about four seconds and said nothing, and about four people at that table said afterwards that the not saying was the thing and that they had been waiting two months for him to say something about it and that he had not.

At about half past one the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank and got the four figures at the near end of the table and said the word with them four times, and about four people at that table heard the third of the four go past and went on talking, and he said the third of the four again afterwards for the four who had stopped, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day, and he went past the east end of that yard on his way to the ditch and did not stop there. At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at that table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and fiftieth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and forty-nine of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day in the shortest entries she has used in six months, four words and five words and six words, and about four people at that table have said out loud that she was being short on purpose and that she did not give the reason twice.

A hundred and nine days. The bid is open and was not run and nothing was proposed. Sixty-four days. Second line, wrong, unaltered, nothing beside it. Sixty-nine days. The rule said out loud on the tenth of the tenth. A hundred and thirty-nine days. First day of the eighth month. Two hundred and forty-two days back. The ninth of the nine printed nights. Seventy-eight days past. A body four hundred miles off, and nobody watched anything. Eighteen marks cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and eighteen marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is no figure in this district for how long this month is. Twelve. Things this district has made. Begun, not finished, fourth of the five, fourth is a person. Ruled and empty at about six.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk entered that separately and on its own and did not read it out loud.

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them.

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally went home down that lane at about six with a flat book under his left arm, and a figure nobody in that yard has asked him for is still a figure nobody in that yard can check, and the stone has been lying face up on that second table for four days and not one thumb went into it today.
