# Chapter 471: A Figure You Do Not Have To Put Down

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the third morning after the count, that a figure somebody has to write down can be a day out from the day it names and a figure nobody has to write down cannot be a day out at all, and he gave the reason, and the reason was about a batten and about the two tables and not about a column, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it.

It was the third morning after the count and the light came up flat off the south end of that ground with no wind in it at all, and the boards of that first table had gone cold again by about nine and about four people had put a hand on them to find out, and by ten there were about nineteen people in it.

At about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and fifteen days as the age of that figure, which is a figure about the figure and not a figure about the sheet, and entered that the sheet has no day-count of its own that anybody has ever kept.

The four figures were said out loud once that morning and a clerk entered them once, and a figure a man reads off a wall every morning is not entered into a page twice in one day, and the man of fifty-six said them again at about four and got all four of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and seventy-four, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and ninety days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and four, and three hundred and sixty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He has got that figure right every morning for two hundred and one mornings, and the third of the four is a day out from the day it names, and he knows that, and the difference is still one day, and nobody in that yard is going to ask him about either of those.

---

He came down the lane at about half past ten with a length of batten on his shoulder and a short length of cord in his coat pocket, and he put the batten down across the near end of the second table, and about nine people turned round to look at the batten, and he turned round and looked at the yard instead of at the batten and said the thing.

It took him about eleven seconds and there was nothing in his voice that anybody could have called a speech. He said it in the ordinary voice, standing at the near end of that second table with his hand flat on the boards, and about nineteen people heard every word of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure somebody had to write down has got the day it was written in it, and that is the whole of why three columns on that wall can be a day out from the day they name, and a figure nobody had to write down has not got a day in it to be out from. That is not a rule and I am not making one. It is a thing I know from putting tables up. I laid this batten across these two at about ten and I measured the gap at the near end with my heel and I measured the gap at the far end with my heel and I got the same both times, and there is no day in either of those two numbers for them to be a day out from.**"

Nobody said anything at all. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the reason was his own words and not hers, the way she entered the reason a man who paces a board gave her in this yard four mornings ago, and entered that no rule was entered with it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the other half of it out loud, standing where anybody standing at that table could see her, and about nine people heard all of it, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A why is a figure about why, and this district has five volumes of pages on it and not one of them has a why on any of them, and a man who gives one of those out loud in a yard is a man who can be asked for a second one, and I am not going to ask him for a second one and nobody standing in this yard is going to either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she had said that and entered that a why is not a figure about a day, and entered that the reason a man gave is on a page in his own words and not in hers, and entered that no second reason is going to be asked for and that she is not going to ask him whether he wanted to say the first one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about eleven the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table with his left hand in its cloth and his right hand flat on the boards, and he said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got forty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He has said the first half of that out loud and you have all written the second half of it down, and those are not the same thing, and the one that is on your page is the one nobody can check.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not going to answer it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four people at that near end worked out while he was standing there that the sentence the man who puts tables up had said is now on a page in a clerk's hand, and that is a figure somebody has to write down, and that it cannot be a day out from the day it names if it was written down this morning, and that the sentence about the sentence is also on a page now, and neither of those two things had been in that yard at ten o'clock.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank at about ten past eleven with the bundle under his arm and stood at that second table for about four seconds and put his own hand flat on the boards about nine inches from where the other man had been standing, and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was asked nothing this morning and said a thing anyway, at about ten, to about nine people, and a clerk entered that she was not asked and that she said a thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch from about a quarter to eight until about ten and came up out of the bank with the water to his knee and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day.

At about half past eleven the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood about nine feet off that second table with his right hand in his coat and said one thing, and nobody answered him.

"**You have all just found out that a sentence you can write down is a sentence you can put right, and a man who mends fences can put that in about four seconds, and neither of those is a different kind of finding from the one you have been living in for a month, and I am not going to be the man who says it is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard, and entered the thing, and entered that he was not thanked for it and that nobody standing in that yard is going to thank him and she is not going to either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

From about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm for about two hours and a half, and nobody walked over to him and nobody asked him for anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past at about two and said nothing to anybody about any of it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The road keeper came up that lane at about two, took the four figures off that wall without stopping, and was not asked about the eleven miles. The cart came up at about three, the man of about twenty-nine who drives it went on up the lane, the near wheel still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm while about four people watched and said nothing. A man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of that low wall from about half past three with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and he was offered nothing and asked nothing, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and sixty-fifth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-four of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

She entered the rest of that day in two entries and said out loud which one was which before she wrote either of them, and the first one is figures.

A hundred and twenty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Seventy-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Eighty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and fifty-four days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and fifty-seven days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is ninety-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Thirty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and that is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and the length of the twelfth month is thirty and is a figure about a count, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for how long the month it is living in now is, and a clerk who entered one off the month before it would be entering a figure about a list. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a sentence is not a thirteenth, and a clerk entered that on the same page as the sentence.

The second entry is the sentence and the refusal, and she entered them in that order and did not join them. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard on this morning with a reason, and entered the reason in his own words, and entered that she is not entering the thing as a rule and did not put it in a column, and entered that the reason is a figure about a why and that this district has no place to put a why, and entered that a second reason is not going to be asked for out of him in this yard.

That lot is being read and is not finished with, and it has got as far as the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of those five is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and three people said a thing out loud in that yard this morning and no name went beside any of the three, because a name was not asked for and a clerk did not ask for one. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth of those five is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five and no sixth has been proposed.

Down that bank at about half past five came the two buckets, empty, and they went back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them.

Nobody in that yard has ever had to write down the depth of a hollow in a stone, and about nine people standing in it this morning heard a man say out loud that a figure nobody has to write down cannot be a day out from a day it does not name, and a clerk wrote the sentence down, and the sentence is now a figure somebody has to write down, and about four people at that near end have said since that this district has spent five volumes learning to write down when and has this morning been told out loud, in the ordinary voice, by a man laying a batten across two tables, that there is one figure in the yard it has never had to write down at all.
