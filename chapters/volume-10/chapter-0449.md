# Chapter 449: A Stone With No Wear On It

Two stones were on that yard at about a quarter to nine in the morning, which is one more than there was the day before, and about four people noticed the difference and not one of them could say what the difference was for.

It was the fifteenth of the twelfth month and the sky was high and thin and the light came over the top of that bank about four minutes later than it had the day before, which nobody wrote down. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and ninety-seven days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, and said the word with each of them and got the word four times, and about four people at that wall have started saying the four with him, which they have not done before.

"**The board carries three hundred and fifty-six, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and seventy-two days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and eighty-six, and three hundred and forty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He read them and then he looked at the second table for about three seconds, which he has never done, and went back to his four without saying one word about it.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up off that lane at about a quarter to nine with a stone in the pocket of his coat and his left hand in its cloth, and he took the stone out with his right hand and set it on the boards of the second table with the worn face up, and then he went back to the first table and took the other stone off the corner of that book and put the new one in its place, square, on the corner, where a page could not get under it.

Two stones. One of them is worn one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end. The other one has nothing on it at all.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and about four people at that table heard every word of it.

"**A stone with no wear on it is not an old stone with its figure taken off it, and the figure on the one on that table this morning is not a better figure than the figure on the one that was on the corner of that book, and I am not going to stand here and let about four of you decide that it is because it is easier to read.**"

The clerk of nineteen years answered him, out loud, to about nine people, and nobody had asked her, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Two stones is two figures and I am not adding them together and neither is anybody else in this yard. The one that is worn is a figure about a book standing still. The one that is not worn is a figure about nothing yet. That is all the two of them are and they are not a sum.**"

Nobody answered him. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud, and entered the thing, and entered that a figure was moved in the open and was not corrected and was not struck and was not superseded, and entered that she is not entering that the rule has been improved, and did not enter it, and entered that she is not entering that this district chose to do it, and did not enter that either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch from about a quarter to eight and came up out of the bank at about ten and got the four figures at the near end of the first table and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day, and he stopped at the second table on his way past and looked down at the underside of a stone that nobody had told him about, and put his own thumb in it, and took his thumb out, and said nothing at all.

---

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about ten past ten with the last of that day's flour on her hip and read the three lines in that lot book standing up, which takes her about two seconds longer than two lines takes her, and then she went over to the second table and stood over the stone on it and looked at the underside of it for a count of about eleven, and said one thing out loud to nobody in particular.

"It has got a hollow in it about the size of my thumb and mine fits in it and I am not going to say that means anything, because about four of you have been putting thumbs in it since the eighth of this month and not one of you has come back and said what it was for."

Nobody answered her. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that she was not asked what she thinks a hollow is for and the record about the not asking says not asked, and entered that a woman who keeps a scale is not entering a figure of water and did not, and entered that a thumb is not a figure and a hollow is not a figure and that the two of them have not been added to each other by anybody.

At about half past eleven the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came off the east wall, looked at the stone on the second table for about four seconds, and said nothing at all about it, and did not touch it, and about four people at that table noticed him not touching it.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood between the two tables with his right hand in his coat and said one thing.

"There is a figure lying face up on a table in a yard anybody can walk into, and there is a stone on a book that has no figure on it, and about four of you have worked out by now that we have swapped which of the two is the one with anything in it, and I am not going to be the one who says that is progress."

She entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered no figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about eleven the woman of fifty-eight came down that bank on her way past with two full buckets, and she put them down on the boards of the first table to rest her arms, and she looked at the stone on the corner of that book the way she looks at a thing she is not going to touch, and then she looked at the stone on the second table for a count of about nine, and said nothing at all about either of them, and picked the buckets up and went on up the bank.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman looked at two stones in that yard on this day and entered no figure about either of them and entered that she was not asked about either of them and did not offer, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about one the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank and stood at the second table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him, and he looked at the underside of that stone, and nobody asked him what he thought of it, and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and about four people watched him do it and said nothing.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and forty-seventh night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and forty-six of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of that day in two paragraphs at about half past four, and nine people at that table heard both of them and the rest of that ground did not.

A hundred and six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Sixty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line of that book is a date and has not been altered. Sixty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and thirty-six days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and thirty-nine days back. A body four hundred miles off is seventy-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. Fifteen marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and fifteen marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is no figure in this district for how long this month is. The count of things this district has made stands where it stood yesterday morning, and a clerk has written no number this morning and has been asked by about four people whether she is going to, and she has told about four of them that she does not know yet. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and there is a stone and a table and a hollow in that yard and none of the three of them is a sixth.

Nobody read either paragraph back from the top. The clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody read them back and entered the reason, and the record about the not reading says not read back.

The two empty buckets came down that bank at about half past five and went back up them full, and the woman of fifty-eight stopped on her way up, and read the three lines in that lot book standing up, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. Then the man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall for the last time in that yard and got all four of them.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put a stone on the corner of a book at a quarter to nine this morning and a worn stone face up on a table at the same minute, and he said nothing about either of them afterwards, and about nine people in that yard are the only people in this district who know that a figure was moved this morning and that nothing was corrected.
