# Chapter 467: Thirty Marks, Counted In The Open

The clerk of nineteen years counted the marks off that board in that yard on the thirtieth of the twelfth month, which is the last day of the month, from the mark for the first of the month down to that morning's mark, in the open, in daylight, with a finger on the wood and about nineteen people standing at the wall of it, and she got thirty, and the man of fifty-six read every mark a second time out loud in front of her and got all thirty of them, and a clerk entered the figure and entered that it is a figure about a count and not a promise about a later day.

It was the thirtieth of the twelfth month and it was the last day of it, and the light came up hard and clean off the south end of that ground and there was no wind at all in it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is two hundred and twelve days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six did not read the four off that wall at about half past nine. He had been on the other side of it since about ten past eight with his arms down, watching a woman's finger arrive at a mark about every four minutes, and about four people at that table noticed that he had not read them and about nine noticed that he had been standing there the whole time.

"**The board carries three hundred and seventy-one, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and eighty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and one, and three hundred and sixty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She started at the top, which was this morning's, and she named that day out loud. Then she went down to the mark below it and named that one, and she went all the way down with her finger on the wood where anybody standing behind her could see it arrive.

The boy of about nineteen counted with his own eyes and his own page and said so, out loud, when somebody standing beside him asked him what he was doing.

"I am counting for me. Nobody has asked me to count for anybody else."

"You have said that before."

"I have said it every day this month and some of you keep standing where I can see you and then asking me what I am doing."

She got to the mark for the first of this month at about twenty past ten, put her finger on it, and said the day out loud again, and stood up straight. Nine people there who had not known it was coming worked it out at the same moment, and four of them said so out loud, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got seventy-seven, and about four people near the boards heard the number and not one of them heard what she was counting and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**From the first of the twelfth month to this morning is thirty marks and it is thirty days, and those are the same figure, and a person can put a finger on either end of it and get the number I got, and this is the last morning of this month and the month does not end until about nine tonight, and I am entering a figure about a count and I am not entering a promise.**"

Nobody answered her. The clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the twelfth month is thirty, and entered that the first day of it is counted in and that the count was made off marks on a board in the open in daylight with about nineteen people standing at the wall of it, and entered that it is a figure about a count, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said one word from the other side of those boards.

"Again."

She put her finger on the top mark and went down all thirty again in front of about nineteen people, and he read every mark out loud before her finger arrived at it, and he got all thirty of them, and about four people at that near end said out loud that a man who had read four figures off those boards every morning for six months had just read thirty marks off them in about two hours and had not read a number once.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six read thirty marks off that board in the open and entered that he got all thirty of them, and entered that she is not entering that as a finding, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

Then she said the other thing, and it is the part about nine people in that yard have said they will remember, and she said it standing at that wall with her hand flat on the boards.

"**I have got two figures. One of them is the number I have just counted with my finger on both ends of it, and the other one is the figure this district has been carrying for the length of this month, and it came off the month before it, and nobody in this district has ever counted it. I am not going to say the second one out loud in this yard, because it is a figure about a list and not about a day, and because a figure about a list that gets said out loud in a yard gets carried by the yard instead of the list and then there is no way of telling anybody where it came from. I am telling you which of the two I have, and they are the same figure, and that is the whole of what I am going to say about the second one.**"

Nobody said anything at all. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk had two figures and entered that they are the same figure, and entered the figure the count gave, and entered that she is not entering the other figure, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four people at that near end worked out while she was standing there what the other figure was, and about four of them have said since that the two figures being the same is the best thing that has happened in that yard in a month, and that a clerk of nineteen years did not say so and would not let anybody else say it twice.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure that reproduces is a figure about a count and is not a finding, and entered that this is the second time she has entered that sentence, and entered that the second time is a habit, and that a habit is a finding, and entered that she entered that a habit is a finding twice this morning without meaning to and did not enter that it means anything.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of the second table through all of it with his left hand in its cloth and his right hand flat on the boards, and he said nothing until about half past eleven and then he said two things, and the second of them was longer than the first, and about nine people heard both, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got forty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is the first check anybody has run in this district since the last day of the eleventh month, and it is the second one, and a check that has been run twice is not a finding and is not a habit either, it is a check.**"

About four people at that table said afterwards that nothing had been got wrong. He said that it was, and that was the whole of what he was going to say about it, and the man who digs loam was in that ditch and did not say one word about any of it.

---

At about twelve the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up that bank with the water coming off his coat and the bundle under his arm and stood at that end of the table for about four seconds, and nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him. He was not asked for anything and nobody asked him what the water had been that morning and nobody asked him what it was that day, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and he went up the lane.

At about twenty past twelve the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat and watched a woman count a month out for about eleven minutes without saying anything. A voice at that near end said that a man who watches a check instead of running one is a man who has worked out which of the two he is, and he said that was right, and that he had worked it out on the thirtieth of the tenth month and had not been asked to say so then either.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and that he has not asked for a figure and has not been given one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud, in front of about nine people, whether a clerk who counts a month she could have got off the month before it has found anything, and the clerk said that she had found that the two of them are the same figure, and that the finding is about the way this district carries a number and not about the month, and that a figure about a method is a figure this district has never entered before, and entered that a woman said a thing out loud and entered the thing and entered the answer and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about two the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up came off that east wall and asked her one question, which is whether the word in her margin is still there, and she said that it was.

"Sixty-eight days."

"**It is sixty-eight days old. The word is unchecked and it is over nothing, and I am not going to take it off, because the reason I put it there has not changed and nobody has checked any of the other columns, and there is a column on that wall that three of us have now stood in front of and one of them is a day out and I have entered the difference and the difference is a day.**" She did not look up. "You have asked me about it in three weeks. You are not asking me because you think I have forgotten."

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked her a question about a word in her margin and entered the answer and entered that the answer carried no figure and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he had not been there at half past nine and nobody said anything about that.

At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and nine people watched a man lift a wheel with one arm and not one of them said a word about it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and four people within nine feet of him did not look at him, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given.

At about four a voice at that near end said out loud that a clerk spent a morning of this district's walking to arrive at a figure this district could have got off the month before it, and that the two of those are not the same thing, and a few people agreed and nobody wrote it down.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and sixty-second night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-one of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her. She entered that the length of the twelfth month is thirty and that a clerk did not say the other figure out loud, and entered that the count is a figure about a count, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

She entered the rest of that day in two entries, and she said out loud which one was which before she wrote either of them.

The first is figures. A hundred and twenty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Seventy-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Eighty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and fifty-one days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and fifty-four days back and is open, and nothing was pulled and no night was named. A body four hundred miles off is ninety days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything.

Thirty marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and this morning a clerk put a finger on both ends of them and got thirty, and the twelfth month is thirty days, and the first of this month is counted in. Every figure on every page of this district re-derives off that figure from today onwards and did not re-derive off it yesterday, and about four people at that near end have said since that a month nobody has counted is a month nobody has checked and that this month is now a month somebody has counted, and that the counting does not make it right and does not make it wrong and is the only thing that has happened to it.

The second entry is this: that three columns on that wall are a day out from the days they name, that the difference is one day on each of them, and that the count this morning was of marks and a difference is not a mark. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it, and a clerk read a count out loud in that yard this morning in front of about nineteen people and no name went beside it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count is still five.

At about half past five the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the first time that day, and got all four of them, and some people said out loud that it was the first time that day.

"**The board carries three hundred and seventy-one, that siding has had the same train standing on it for six hundred and eighty-seven days, nothing has been entered in a book for four hundred and one, and three hundred and sixty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He said that it was the first time that day and that it was, and that he had spent a morning counting instead and would do it again tomorrow and would not be asked to. The third of those four is a day out from the day it names and he read it out loud twice this morning and got it both times, and nobody is going to tell him again.

Going down from the top, that board carries this morning, and then the twenty-ninth, and then the twenty-eighth, and the mark below all of them is the first of the twelfth month, and the mark below that one is the last day of the eleventh month, and the mark below that one is the thirtieth of the tenth month, and the figure under the first of the ninth is a figure about a list and not a mark, and it is going to read what it reads for as long as there is anybody here to read it, and a clerk of nineteen years stood at that wall for about two hours this morning with a finger on the wood and did not say one word about that.
