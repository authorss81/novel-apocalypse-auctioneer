# Chapter 447: Two Seconds Longer

A woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up at about ten past nine and it took her about two seconds longer than two lines took her, and she said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and that is the whole of a morning in that yard.

It was the thirteenth of the twelfth month and the wind had gone round to the north in the night and come back south by morning, and the light on that ground was the flat white kind that comes after two days of weather. By ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about a quarter to eight and before there was anybody else standing there, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered the age of that figure as a figure about the figure, which is a hundred and ninety-five days, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got every one of them, and said the word with each and got the word four times, and the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale wrote the four down on the back of her hand while he was saying them and wiped it off again before anybody looked.

"**The board carries three hundred and fifty-four, the train on that siding has stood six hundred and seventy days, nobody has entered anything for three hundred and eighty-four, and three hundred and forty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

She was at the end of that table before the man of fifty-six had got to the end of his four, which is where she has stood every working day since the day that book came out, and she read it from the top, and she did not put a finger on it.

---

Two lines took her about a second and a half. Three lines took her about two seconds, and the extra half second went on the third one, and about four people at that table have said since that they watched her get to the third line and stop for the half second and then go on, and not one of them can tell anybody what she thought at the half second, because she has not told anybody and she is not going to.

The clerk of nineteen years had her pencil out. She has a rule about that woman and the rule is two months old and it is the shortest rule anybody in that yard has, and it is that a clerk of nineteen years does not ask the woman of fifty-eight what she has just read.

"Would you like me to enter that you read it," said the clerk of nineteen years, out loud, to about nine people, because that is the way she has asked it about twice in two months and the woman has never once said yes.

"No," said the woman of fifty-eight, and she picked up her two buckets, and she went up that bank.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman read the three lines in that lot book standing up and entered that she read them from the top, and entered that the clerk asked whether she would like the reading entered and that she said no, and entered that the clerk is not entering what she thought, and entered that a clerk may not ask, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she entered the other half of it, and she said the other half out loud before she wrote it, which is the fifth time in eight days.

"**A figure in this district that a stranger can check has to be checked by somebody standing there, and the only people who check anything in that yard are people who are carrying something, and about four of the people who carry something have read that book every working day for two months and not one of them has been asked what they make of it, and that is not a rule anybody made. It is what happens when nobody is asked.**"

Nobody answered her. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in the open there and entered the thing, and entered that no rule in this district says a person who is carrying something may not be asked what they make of a page, and entered that there is no rule that says they may, and that the second of those two is the finding, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the far end of that table with his left hand in its cloth and he said one thing out loud at about half past ten, to about four people and not to the rest, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he had said and got fifty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**She has been the only person in this yard who has ever checked a figure by standing in front of the thing it is about, and she has done it about four hundred times, and not one of those four hundred was written down, and a check nobody writes down is not a check anybody can do again.**"

Nobody answered him. She entered that a man had said a thing out loud in that yard, and entered the thing, and entered that she has not written down one of them and is not going to, and entered that a clerk entering a check she has not made is a clerk inventing an instrument, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the woman of fifty-eight came back down that bank with the two full buckets and stopped at the end of that table again on her way up with them, and read the three lines again from the top, and took the half second on the third one, and went up the bank.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman read the three lines in that lot book twice on this day and entered no figure and entered that she was not asked the second time either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about half past twelve the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came down that bank and stood about nine feet off that table with his right hand in his coat and looked at the third line of that book for about as long as it takes the man of fifty-six to read four figures and said nothing, and about four people at that table said afterwards that a man who says nothing about a line in a book is a man who has read it twice.

At about one the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that bank and got the four figures at the near end of the boards and said the word with them four times, and that right arm of his did not go above the level of that shoulder at any point in the day, and he did not look at the third line of that book and nobody told him to and nobody asked him why he did not. At about two the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up and stood at that table for about four seconds with the bundle under his arm and about nine people were within four feet of him and nobody said a word to him and the record about the not asking says not asked. At about half past two the road keeper came up that lane and got the four figures off that wall as he went past and was not asked about the eleven miles. At about three the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up and went on up it, and the near wheel on that cart was still dragging, and he got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman of fifty-eight has carried eleven buckets up that bank for nineteen years and that she was asked this morning whether she would like a reading of three lines entered and that she said no, and entered that the number of buckets is a figure about a woman and not about water and not about a figure anybody can check.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that he is on his hundred and forty-fifth night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and forty-four of them, and that she is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that day at about half past four in a single entry, and a few people at that end of the table noticed that she did not read it out loud, and about four of them asked afterwards why she had not, and she said that it had not been asked for.

The bid is open a hundred and four days and was not run on this day and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Fifty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and has not been altered and has not been explained beyond what one man said out loud on the eleventh of this month. Sixty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. The first day of the eighth month is a hundred and thirty-four days past. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and thirty-seven days back. A body four hundred miles off is seventy-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched anything. The reading of that lot is begun and not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth is a person. Thirteen marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of this month, and thirteen marks is a figure about a count of marks and is not a figure about a month, and there is no figure in this district for how long this month is. A woman of fifty-eight read three lines in a book in the open twice on this day and a clerk asked her once whether she wanted the reading entered and she said no. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid, and the fifth is a way to pay a person who is not in a household.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said one thing out loud at about five, in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got fifty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and about four people at the east wall heard the number.

"**That book is on the end of that table because I put a table under it, and nobody has asked me a single thing about that table since the day I put it up, and I have been waiting about four weeks to say so out loud and this is the morning I am saying it on.**"

Nobody answered him. The clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud there and entered the thing, and entered that nobody asked him anything, and entered that she is not going to ask him about it this evening either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about half past five the two empty buckets came down that bank and went back up them full for the fourth time that day, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up, which takes her about two seconds longer than two lines takes her, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her.

The pencil on the clerk's own page had not moved for about eleven seconds, and about four people at that end of the table noticed the pencil not moving, and one of them said out loud, afterwards, that the pencil not moving was the entry, and the clerk of nineteen years did not disagree with him out loud and did not agree with him in writing either.
