# Chapter 500: A Thumb In A Hollow

A clerk of nineteen years entered the count of the board in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-seventh morning after the count, on her own page, and did not look up, and a man of fifty-six stood at the end of the second table with his thumb in the hollow in the underside of the stone lying face up on it, and the figure in that hollow is one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and the lot book was on the end of the first table under a second stone that has no wear on it at all, and about nineteen people were in that yard, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard at any point in that day.

It was the twenty-seventh morning after the count and the light came up hard and clean off the south end of that ground before seven and there was no wind in it at all, and the boards of that second table were warm by about nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and thirty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, and a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing near enough to hear that have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and twenty-fifth of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and ninety-eight, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fourteen days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and twenty-eight, and three hundred and eighty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the count of the board on her own page at about eleven, standing at the near end of that first table, and did not look up, and about nine people came and stood at that end of the table over about four minutes and looked at what she was writing and she did not look up, and about four of them have said since that a clerk who has not looked up in that yard in five volumes looked up this morning, for about a second, at a man standing at the end of the second table, and then went back to her page.

Nobody said anything to the man of fifty-six. He had crossed the four feet between that wall and the end of the second table at about ten past eleven, which is about nine feet off a book a stranger can walk up to and read, and he had put his thumb in the hollow in the underside of that stone, and the figure in that hollow is one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and he left his thumb there for about two minutes and a half, and about nineteen people in that yard watched a man of fifty-six read a figure with a thumb that no one had to put down.

The boy of about nineteen counted the two minutes and a half and got two and read the number back to himself in a low voice, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy counted about two minutes and a half and entered the count as a count and entered that it is a count of a man standing still and not a check on the figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the boy of about nineteen asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud and in front of about nine people, what she was going to enter about it, and she said one thing out loud without looking up, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering the count of the board. I am not entering that, and I am not entering the two and a half either, and the reason is that a man with a thumb in that hollow is not a figure and a man standing still is not a figure, and I have entered about four things in this yard this month that are not figures and I have entered no figure about any of the four of them.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came down that lane at about one and looked at the two tables and did not put a hand on either of them and went back up it, and nobody asked him why he had come down, and a clerk entered that she did not ask and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table at about two and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of it in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are forty-three of them in a row going back to the ninth day of this volume, and a clerk entered that count and entered that it is a count of marks and not a figure about this morning. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and he did not go and look at the man at the end of the second table and nobody asked him whether he had. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing all day.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard at any point in that day. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not in that yard and entered that she did not ask and entered the reason, which is that nobody has asked her anything and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man who has said four things out loud in that yard in sixteen days was not in the yard on the twenty-seventh morning after the count, and that about four other people have said that they do not know when he stopped coming and have not asked, and that the yard has not worked out whether the man of fifty-six crossed those four feet because that man was not going to be standing there, and nobody has said that out loud and nobody is going to.

About half past twelve the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank with the last of that day's flour on her hip, read the three lines in that lot book standing up, went over to the second table, and stood at the end of it about four feet off the man of fifty-six without saying anything for about a minute, and then she said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got seventy-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got a word and I have had it for a month and I am not going to say it, and I want about nine people to know that I came over here on purpose to say that I am not going to say it, and I have been about four feet off him for a minute and he has not asked me what for and I would have said it if he had.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman has had a word for a month and did not say it, and entered no word, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six did not turn round and did not take his thumb out of that hollow and said nothing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six did not answer a woman who said one thing out loud about four feet off him, and entered that she is not going to ask him again and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people have said since that a man who has not turned round in a yard in five volumes did not turn round, and that a clerk has entered that he did not, and that a stranger reading that page tomorrow cannot tell whether he did not hear her or heard her and did not answer, and that both of those are in the same entry and neither of them is a figure about him.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles by anybody in that yard on that day. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and about nine people within four feet of him were busy watching a man at the end of a table, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and ninetieth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and forty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. One hundred and three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and seventy-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and eighty-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and seventeen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Fifty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are forty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved today. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a thumb in a hollow is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person, and that book has no fourth line in it and there was a second reader asked for in a yard in this volume and none was appointed and no column was ruled for one. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the count of the board on her own page and did not read it out loud and did not ask anybody for a name, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count of the five is still five with no sixth in it, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a clerk entered that the fifth of the five has now gone fourteen days from the morning a man said it out loud and has not been paid on any of them and has not been said out loud in a mouth on any of them.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her. After that the man of fifty-six took his thumb out of that hollow, which nobody had asked him to do, and said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and did not go and stand at the end of the second table again.

The man of fifty-six was at the end of the second table with his thumb in the hollow in the underside of a stone that was lying face up, and the figure in that hollow is one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and the lot book was on the end of the first table under a second stone that has no wear on it at all, and a clerk of nineteen years was entering the count of the board on her own page and had not looked up, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard.
