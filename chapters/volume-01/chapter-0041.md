# Chapter 41: The Day the Ward Market Votes

Ivo Stenn had been the freight clerk of the ward market for six years and its cooperative secretary for two, and in that time he had said no to about eleven hundred people, and he had never once, in the whole of his tenure, put a rate to anything in a room where the rate was being argued.

On the morning of the thirteenth he had two figures on the board in the counting room, and both of them were new, and only one of them was his.

The counting room is a stone-flagged room behind the market with a long table, a stove, a window that does not shut, and a door that is open from nine until the counting is done. Everything the ward market has decided in eleven years has been decided in that room, and a large part of what it has refused has also been decided in that room, and the difference has never mattered to anybody until this month.

---

The first figure was the rate.

**THE PAPER LEVY, PROPOSED, AS FOLLOWS: EVERY HOUSEHOLD OF TWO ADULTS AND ABOVE, EVERY QUARTER, AT FORTY-ONE COPPERS INSTEAD OF FOUR.**

Stenn put it up on the board himself at about nine, because it was his number, and because a man who has found a thing in his own back office after eleven years does not hand it to a clerk.

He read out what the four coppers had been for, because somebody always asked, and because the answer was the reason the whole morning was happening.

"Four coppers a quarter," he said. "Entered in this market's books for eleven years as *public notice and printed matter*. Four thousand households, more or less, and the quarter books are in the back office in a hand nobody has looked at since the sixth year, and the money has gone somewhere, and the going somewhere is the clerk's own line, and the clerk who wrote that line has been dead or gone for nine years.

"**And there is no printed matter.** That is not an accusation. It is the answer to the question a copper merchant asked me on the eleventh, which was *where is the printed matter*, and I went and looked, and there is none in this building, and there is none in the store, and Mrs Halder has been through the store twice."

Mrs Halder, who was fifty-eight and had found the ledger, put her hand flat on the table in the way that people in that room did when they meant to be entered.

"It is in the store," she said. "In a crate. Under eleven years of rate circulars that were printed by a printer in Salt Verge who went under in the eighth year, and the last one anybody in this market has seen is the fourth. So the market has been buying the printer's paper and not the printer. I do not know what to do with that and I have not known for three weeks and I have not told anybody because I did not have anybody to tell who could write it down."

"You could have told me," Stenn said.

"You could have asked me," said Mrs Halder, "and you did not, and that is the second time. **I found the book. You found the line in it. Neither of us told the other for three weeks, and on the eleventh I read you the entry and you read me the entry and we were both astonished, in a counting room, at half past four in the afternoon, like two men who had opened the same box from two sides.**"

---

Then he read out what the forty-one would buy, and it took four minutes, and every item in it had been argued for at least one day in the last fortnight, and the arguing had been done mostly by people who were not traders.

"**Nineteen coppers — the warden's inspection.** The days are published on a board kept in the trading places and read out by a clerk appointed by the review table and not by the Warden's office. The yard is hired. The timber is hired. The clerk is appointed. That is Selik Marne's instrument and this market is paying for it and I am not going to pretend we forced it out of him.

"**Eleven coppers — a public reader at this counter.** Four days a week, with a lamp, reading out loud in words the bringer did not bring whatever anybody puts on the counter.

"**Six coppers — the boards and the notice stock and the appointed clerk's board**, which is a separate thing from the reader and is the reason the days get read at all.

"**Two coppers — oil.** Which is the whole of the money the reader's lamp has ever cost this market and I want that said out loud because somebody is going to write it down as a scandal and it is two coppers.

"**And three coppers, unassigned, and the reason is entered, which is that nobody in this room can say what the three is for, and a rate that pays for a thing nobody can name is the rate this market has been collecting for eleven years.**"

He put the chalk down and looked at the room, which had about thirty people in it, and nine of whom were traders and the rest of whom were there because word had got round on the eleventh that a vote was coming and this market has never had a vote anybody could attend.

"Forty-one," he said. "And that is the whole of it, and I have not put a single copper in it for a road."

---

**"Then put one in for a road," said Ferrin Usk.**

He was at the front, because Ferrin Usk has had a stall in this market for eleven years and stands where a stall-holder stands, and he had a sheet of Lot 17's public book in his hand, which he had been reading since the eighth, and everybody in the room had been waiting for him to use it since he came in.

"Somebody has to say it," Usk said, "and I am going to say it and then I am going to vote against it, and I would like that in the minutes if this market keeps minutes, which it does not, so I will shout it twice.

"**I have read Adrian Vale's entry. The held asset. All four things and the three sets of figures and the sentence at the end.** The lease, the salvage, a repair-crew term of two seasons, sixteen hundred and some, and then the sentence: *not returnable to Mr. Vale by anybody*. And the yard will tell you that is the most honest thing anybody has written in a book in this city in a year, and I believe it, and I want to ask one question about it and I do not think the man is in the room."

"He is not in the room," Stenn said.

"Then it is a question for the book and it is a question I can put in this room anyway, because the rate is being argued here and the answer changes what the rate is for. **What happens to a held asset if the road opens and the charter is never breached?**"

The room did what that room does, which is to go quiet and wait for a clerk.

"There is nobody here who can answer it," Stenn said. "I have had three people ask me this week. It is not in the book. The book says what happens on a breach and it does not say what happens without one, and I have read the entry twice and I think that is deliberate."

"Entered by a clerk, on a crate, in front of four hundred people," said the trader from the terraces, who had been coming to this market for a month and had not yet learned anybody's name.

"And I am not saying it was not deliberate," Usk said. "I am saying that in this district nobody has ever put anything back. Not a barrow, not a handcart, not a bolt of cloth that went out on credit in the sixth year. **I have been in this market thirty-one years and I have watched eleven men take money for a thing and not deliver it and I have watched about four men be the kind of man who would put it back, and not one of them ever had the occasion.** So when a man writes *not returnable by anybody* on the front of a document, he is writing a sentence this city has no practice for, and I want the market to understand that we are being asked to trust a habit nobody has, and not to pretend that a clean sentence is a returned cart."

There was no answer to that in the room, and Stenn wrote *no answer offered* on the slate in his own hand, and the slate went on the board afterwards, which is the ward market's practice and which nobody outside the building knows about.

---

**Mrs Oveta Kraus came in at about half past ten**, which was late, and she was late because she had been at the fever ward on the other bank, which takes three hours down a stair and three hours back, and she had gone anyway on the morning of a vote, and she said so at the door before she said anything else, because it was the only part of her that the room was entitled to check.

"Right," she said, standing at the back with her hood down. "I am going to say one motion and it is the one I dissented on and I want it minuted before the vote and not after it, because I have been on the wrong side of it once and I am not going to be on the wrong side of it twice in the same month.

"**I move that this market's money toward the crossing at St. Orra be committed by the day, and renewed by the vote of this room on each published day, and that this market pay toward no office that has no day on it.** Not because I oppose the office. I do not oppose the office and I have never opposed it and I said so in a yard on the twenty-fourth and I am saying it again. My brother-in-law's woman is in the fever ward and the Wednesday list has been cancelled twice and I would like the queue to be a queue and not a mystery.

"But he asked for no expiry, and the expiry was refused, and then nobody put anything in the place of the expiry, and that is a hole in this market's position and I have been the only person saying so for a fortnight.

"**And now it is our motion.** The ward market renews the inspection by the day. If the district ever wants an office that is an office, it can have one, and this market will fund it, and this market will fund it out loud, and the market's own money is the money and the market's own vote is the vote, and then nobody can ever again stand in a yard and say that a rate was spent on an office without anybody deciding to spend it on the office."

Nobody argued with it, which was how Stenn knew it was the right motion, and he entered it before the vote and read it back, and Mrs Kraus checked it against what she had said and it was right.

---

The third thing came at about eleven, and Stenn had been expecting it since the ninth, and it was brought by nine traders and not by one, which is the ward market's way, and which is why this market has survived eleven years of collecting a levy for nothing.

**The third thing was nine coppers a quarter more** — on top of the forty-one, taking the rate to **fifty** — for a **standing crew kept at the St. Orra staging yard**: four men, a stove, a lamp, a bench, and a board, on the days when nothing is crossing, so that a man who wanted to leave a message at St. Orra had something to leave it at.

The argument for it was not clever. It was the best argument anybody had made in three weeks, and Stenn wrote that down too, because he was clerk enough to know which arguments to write down.

"A yard with a man in it is a yard people can find," said a chandler of nine standing claims. "There are eleven people on that apron every week who are trying to get a message to the other side and the other side has no way to answer, and a board with nobody near it is a board people steal the frame off."

"A yard with a man in it is a yard people come to," said a cooper. "And a yard people come to gets a queue, and a queue in this district is where the Selik Marne queue came from, and we have spent a month watching that man's whole argument turn out to be *there were eleven people on my bridge and no place for them to wait.*"

---

**Stenn refused it in public, with figures, and it took him four minutes, and it is the first thing the ward market has ever said yes about, and neither of those two facts is visible from the outside.**

"Forty-one," he said. "Not fifty. And I am the clerk who proposed both numbers, and I am the man who is asking you to take the smaller one, and I would like the room to hold on to that for about ninety seconds because I am about to say something and it is going to sound like pride and it is not.

"**It is not a road. It has no day on it. It cannot have a day on it, because the crossing it is a rate for has never been given one by anybody, and a rate paid for a thing that has not been given a day is this market's own paper levy with a different word on it, and I have found that one in a back office and I am not going to build another one on purpose.**

"Here is the figure and it is the whole of my argument. **This market's money goes out in a quarter whether the road opens or not.** Forty-one coppers, from four thousand households, every quarter, for as long as this market exists. If the road opens in March, the four coppers this market has been taking for eleven years is still being taken and the forty-one is still being taken, and the man at the St. Orra yard is still not there, and the rate has quietly become a road rate with a yard in it, and in a year somebody will read the books and find that a district with no crossing has been paying a crossing rate since the fourth quarter of this year.

"And I will tell you the other half, because nobody in this room is going to.

"**We may be wrong.** If a road opens on a day we did not plan for, then on that day this market will have a hundred and fifty-odd coppers a quarter less than a yard with a man in it would need, and four people will stand at St. Orra with a lamp and tell somebody they cannot help them, and that will be our doing and not the Warden's, because we had nine coppers a quarter in our hands this morning and we voted it down on a principle.

"So it goes in the book both ways. **The nine were refused on the day, with the reason, and the reason is that the thing has no day, and the refusal is not a statement that the yard is not wanted.** When the road has a date, the nine comes back to this room on its own and I will move it myself."

---

**The vote was taken at about twenty past eleven and it took four minutes and the ward market has not taken a vote in front of a room like this in eleven years.**

It is not a show of hands. A cooperative that collects four coppers a quarter from four thousand households cannot decide anything by a show of hands, because four thousand households are not in the room and about nine of the thirty people in it are the households.

It is done by **trades, and each trade carries its own count**, and the count is read out, and the person reading it says *and this is the number I am answering for* before they answer, and if a trader cannot say the number then the trade does not vote, and that has happened four times in eleven years.

**The inspection: nineteen coppers, the warden's days, the yard, the timber, the appointed clerk. CARRIED, on eleven trades, and the count read out was four thousand and one households, and no trade dissented, and Mrs Kraus's motion was carried with it and attached to it.**

**The reader at the counter: eleven coppers, four days a week, with a lamp. CARRIED, on eleven trades, and one trade — a chandler with a stall and no reader and nine standing claims — abstained, and said so, and the abstention is in the book, because the number of people the reader serves is not a trade and there is no trade for them.**

**The boards, the stock, the appointed clerk's board, six coppers. CARRIED, without a count, on a nod, and the clerk wrote *carried on a nod and the count was not taken and that is entered* and Stenn made her write it, twice, because that sentence is the eleventh-year paper levy all over again and he was not going to let it in quietly.**

**The two coppers of oil. CARRIED.**

**The three unassigned coppers. NOT CARRIED, and left in the rate, because a rate cannot carry a line that pays for nothing, and Stenn's own instruction was that the three stays and the three is named as unassigned with the reason entered, and the market carried the instruction because it is his market and he is a careful man.**

**The nine coppers for a standing crew at St. Orra. REFUSED, on eleven trades, and the count read out was four thousand and one households, and the reason was entered, and the refusal was entered, and the sentence *the refusal is not a statement that the yard is not wanted* was entered in his own hand at the request of four different people.**

And then the room did the thing that the ward market of Alder Reach has never done in the whole of its eleven years and which took about eleven seconds and which nobody in the room was ready for.

**Somebody said thank you.**

Not to Stenn. To the room. A man near the door, a plasterer, who had come to argue about the nine, said out loud that it was the first rate meeting he had ever been inside of and that he had come to make a noise about a yard and that he was not going to make it, and that he was sorry, and that he had better go to work.

And about nine people said a version of it, and one woman who had come to ask whether the reader would be at the counter on the day she comes in said, "Then that is the first thing this market has ever done that I could tell my sister about," and went out.

---

Stenn wrote the minute himself, because it was his meeting, and because he had learned in a month that a minute written by a clerk is a thing a room believes and a minute written by the man who proposed the rate is a thing a room can check.

He read it back at about half past twelve to a room of about eleven people, and it is short, and here it is whole.

> **THE THIRTEENTH. THE PAPER LEVY, ENTERED BY THE CLERK WHO PROPOSED IT, AT FORTY-ONE COPPERS A QUARTER, FROM EVERY HOUSEHOLD OF TWO ADULTS AND ABOVE, OUT OF A LEVY OF FOUR THAT HAS BEEN COLLECTED FOR ELEVEN YEARS FOR PRINTED MATTER THAT DOES NOT EXIST.**
>
> **NINETEEN TO THE INSPECTION. ELEVEN TO THE READER. SIX TO THE BOARDS AND THE APPOINTED CLERK'S BOARD. TWO TO OIL. THREE UNASSIGNED, WITH THE REASON ENTERED.**
>
> **NINE REFUSED, FOR A STANDING CREW AT ST. ORRA, ON ELEVEN TRADES AND FOUR THOUSAND AND ONE HOUSEHOLDS, ON THE GROUND THAT THE THING HAS NO DAY ON IT, AND THE REFUSAL IS ENTERED AS NOT A STATEMENT THAT THE YARD IS NOT WANTED, AND THE NINE RETURNS TO THIS ROOM ON ITS OWN WHEN THE ROAD HAS A DATE, AND THE CLERK WILL MOVE IT.**
>
> **MRS KRAUS'S MOTION CARRIED AND ATTACHED: THE INSPECTION IS RENEWED BY THE DAY AND THIS MARKET PAYS TOWARD NO OFFICE THAT HAS NO DAY ON IT.**
>
> **ONE ABSTENTION, ENTERED. ONE VOTE CARRIED ON A NOD WITH NO COUNT TAKEN, ENTERED AS CARRIED ON A NOD WITH NO COUNT TAKEN.**
>
> **AND ENTERED BY THE CLERK BECAUSE IT IS THE ONLY THING IN THIS MINUTE THAT IS ABOUT HIM AND NOBODY ELSE IS GOING TO PUT IT DOWN: I HAVE SPENT SIX YEARS SAYING NO AND I HAVE NEVER PUT A FIGURE IN A ROOM BEFORE, AND THE FIGURE I PUT IN THE ROOM TONIGHT IS THE FIRST THING THIS MARKET HAS VOTED YES TO, AND IT IS NOT A ROAD, AND IT IS A ROOM, AND I DO NOT KNOW WHAT TO DO WITH THAT.**

---

**Selik Marne's review of his own order of the ninth sat at two in the afternoon, in the same building, in the same room, and about two hundred people came to it, and it was the strangest afternoon of Stenn's working life.**

He had asked for it. On his own motion, with nobody's name on it, in a yard on the twenty-fourth, on the grounds that a review of a closure ought to sit in the same room as a rate, and the room was this room, and the rate was the rate, and the reason he had wanted that was now visible to everybody and it was not a pose.

A review table had sat for it. **Ravell** was there with his cold and his reading board, because his duty under the order of the ninth is to read the days on the board and not what is behind them, and he read them again at the start, in full, and nobody stopped him.

And then the Warden administrator of St. Orra stood up in a counting room and let the market that was paying for his inspection examine his own order, which is the single bravest thing anybody in Alder Reach has done in a year, and nobody in that room has forgotten it and it is not going to be in anybody's newspaper.

**The finding of the review, which the table entered in the form the table had been given, and which the ward market's traders wrote, not the Warden's office, and not Registrar Quill:**

**THE ORDER OF THE NINTH IS CORRECT. IT STATES THAT ONE OFFICE IS RESPONSIBLE FOR THE CROSSING AT ST. ORRA, IT IS CLOSED-ENDED, AND IT IS THE INSTRUMENT UNDER WHICH THE CERTIFICATE WAS SUSPENDED AND UNDER WHICH THE INSPECTION RUNS ON ITS PUBLISHED DAYS. THE ORDER IS NOT DISSOLVED. THE REVIEW FINDS NO FAULT IN THE INSTRUMENT, IN THE MANIFEST, IN THE FOUR TERMS, OR IN THE CLOSURE.**

**AND THE REVIEW RECORDS THAT THE DISTRICT DOES NOT WANT IT.**

Stenn read that finding out twice, because he did not believe it the first time, and then he read it a third time to a room of about ninety people, because a public board publishes a result and this one had been given to him as a result.

Then Selik Marne got up and said the half of it that was his, and he said it in about nine minutes, and nobody in that room has described the nine minutes since without using the word *tired*.

"I have been the Warden administrator of St. Orra for nine years," he said, "and the order of the ninth is the best piece of my own work and I wrote it in four days and I have not had a night of it. There is one office. There is one manifest. There is a certificate on a wall that anybody can see, and it came down on the twenty-first under a clause I put in my own regulation in the twelfth year because of the March evacuation, and I want to say where the clause came from: *a certified housing shall be suspended where its readings cannot be accounted for by the structure.* That is the whole of it. There is no interest in it.

"And a line came off a trace with no instrument on it on the morning of the nineteenth at twenty-three minutes to eight, on a shut and empty span, and nobody can tell me why, and Dermot Vasey and a woman whose name is not in this district suspended my certificate under that clause, and they were right, and I have not argued it and I am not going to.

"So the order is right and the closure is right and the review says so in a document a market wrote, and I am obliged to say the rest of it and I have practised it.

"**This district has decided that it would rather have no crossing than a crossing it cannot remove.** That is not a stupid decision. It is the decision of people who have watched a man write an exception into a regulation in four days, and who have read the word *closed-ended* and understood it, and who are correct to have understood it. I asked for the office. Mr. Stenn's traders refused me, and they refused me correctly, and the reason they gave is a party who benefits from a hearing should not be the party that calls it, and I have been the party that calls it for nine years.

"I lost that argument. I have kept the closure. I want both of those in the book in that order, and I want the second one entered by this market and not by my office, because my office is not going to enter the second one.

"And I am going to say the thing that nobody has said, and then I am going to go and open the sluice, which is late.

"**There is a token in a locked case in this district and a sale is being prepared, and I know it, and I have not interfered with it, and I have not put one word in anybody's way, and the reason is that I have no standing to stop it and I want that on the record before it happens and not afterwards. I have said in a yard on the twenty-fourth that there are three men in this city who could open a road across St. Orra this winter and that two of them are dangerous and one of them is me, and I have not repeated it, and I am not going to repeat it, because a road is going to be opened in a yard in this district and it is going to be opened by a public lot, and I have been trying for nine days to find a way to say that I hope it is not me who is wrong about this, and I cannot, and the sentence does not exist in my vocabulary.**

"So I will say the only sentence I have. **If that road opens on a term and the term ends, then the ending of the term is the only thing in this district that anybody will be able to check, and I have spent nine years building a crossing that could be ended by a hearing, and I would give a great deal this afternoon to have a second one.** That is not an argument. It is what a man says when he has lost and is still standing."

Nobody answered it. That is in the record too.

---

The room emptied by about four, and Stenn sat down at the table for the first time since seven in the morning, and put his head on his hands for about a minute, and then did the thing that is the actual content of the day and that nobody in the market will remember.

Ferrin Usk was still at the table, folding his sheet.

"Mr. Stenn," he said. "You have said that you found a levy in a back office that paid for nothing, and that you have proposed a rate that pays for a room, and that you have refused a rate for a yard because the road has no day. And I have been sitting here for two hours trying to work out how to make my objection to the nine and I cannot, and I have been making my objections to things for thirty-one years."

"Yes," Stenn said.

"So which was it? Did you find the money and spend it, or did you find the money and stop?"

"Both," Stenn said. "I found four coppers that had been collecting for eleven years for a thing that did not exist, and I have turned them into a room and a lamp and eleven boards, and that is a stop, and it is the only stop I have ever made in six years, and I did not make it by saying no. I made it by putting a number up on a board that people could check, and about thirty people checked it, and nine of them were the people who were going to be affected by it, and one of them abstained because there is no trade for the people the reader is for, and that abstention is now in the book, and it is the most useful sentence in the book."

Usk looked at him for a moment.

"You have just described a public lot," he said. "With a rate instead of a toll. I have been arguing with a public lot for a month and I did not know it was a thing a market could also be."

"Yes," said Ivo Stenn, to a copper merchant in a stone-flagged room, at about ten past four on the thirteenth. "And I do not know what to do with that either, and I have not got anybody to ask, and I would have thought a market full of traders was the last place in this city for a man to be stuck."

---

**And then, at about a quarter to five, the ward market's second clerk came out of the back office with a slate.**

She is the clerk who has stood in Lot 17's yard three times in a month, on the twentieth, the twenty-first and the twenty-sixth, asking each time which of two things she was doing, and she is not named in this book, and her reason for not being named is that she has not chosen a name and Stenn has never once offered to enter one, which is a piece of administrative restraint that this market has been practising for eleven years without knowing it was doing it.

There were nine people at the counter. There are always three or four people at the counter in the last hour and there were nine, because the reader had finished for the day and the lamp was still on and nobody had wanted to be the first to leave.

She had a paragraph on the slate in Nessa Pell's fair hand, copied out, and she stood at the end of the counter with it, and she said:

"I do not know who to give this to, and I have been holding it for an hour, and I am going to read it out because there is nobody else here and because I have read it four times and every time I read it I get to the end and want to read it again."

And she read it, to nine people at a market counter, with a lamp on, on the thirteenth:

> **HELD BY THE SITTING STEWARD UNDER A TERM OF SEVEN DAYS, RENEWABLE IN PUBLIC, FORFEIT TO THE REMEDY ON A BREACH OF ANY CHARTER THIS BOARD HAS RECORDED, AND NOT RETURNABLE TO MR. VALE BY ANYBODY.**

She read it twice, because she said she had read it four times, and then she put the slate down on the counter and said:

"That is the only thing anybody has brought me in a month that was news. Everything else in this book is what people are afraid of, and that is a man telling a city that he has given something away and that nobody is going to hand it back, and there is not one person on the terraces who has believed a word of any of it, and I would like whoever wrote it to know that somebody read it out loud today in a room where nobody asked them to."

And the ward market's rate meeting, which had begun at nine in the morning and ended at twenty past eleven and had cost the district four thousand and one households about thirty-five coppers more than it had cost them the quarter before, finished for the year with a clerk standing at a counter, reading a sentence out twice to nine people, because a sentence about not getting something back is the first piece of news this city has had in three weeks.
