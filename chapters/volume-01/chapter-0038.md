# Chapter 38: The First Split Auction

The trouble with a closing that opens nothing, Adrian Vale discovered on the sixth of the month at about eleven in the morning, is that every person in the yard can see at a glance that there is nothing to close.

The token was in its case in the office behind him. The case was locked. Its custody term was renewed in the yard on the twenty-ninth, on Nessa Pell's own request and on the record, and had been renewed before that on the twenty-first, and the sitting steward renews it every seven days in public in about eleven seconds, and the object inside the case was cracked through the plate and might never open again and nobody had said otherwise in public since the nineteenth.

There was no vehicle on the apron. The bus was a wreck out past the underpass inside the ring on Tarin's road map and had been since the flood year and was not coming, and the only bus in Alder Reach that had been under the Fault was standing in a service yard four streets west of this depot with its doors open and nobody in the driver's seat, and it came out of that yard by itself after eleven hours and forty minutes, and nobody in this city has ever said what that is.

And Adrian stood at a rail in daylight with about five hundred people in his yard, and he had a sheet of terms, and there was nothing in the world the terms could be for.

He had a bad hour and nobody saw it, because he had spent seventeen years learning to have a bad hour at a desk, and what he did with it was go and get a ledger and price a lot of damaged salvage, which he did not need to do, for forty minutes, until his hands were steady.

Then he came back and did the thing that was available to him, which was the only thing.

"I am going to say the awkward part first," he said, "because if I say it at the end it will sound like a defence and it is not a defence, it is the situation.

"There is no token in this yard. There is no bus. There is no road. The crossing at St. Orra is shut to everything including a box and including a cylinder, and the certificate is suspended by two people who are not me, and the only footway this district had went into the service cut on the twelfth with four children on the deck. **I cannot open anything today. I have said that in this yard in four different weeks and I am saying it in the first minute because there are five hundred people here and about a third of them have come to watch a bus.**

"What I have got is seven sheets of paper and I am going to close them. That is what I have got and I am telling you what it is before anybody in this yard has to work it out, because I have learned what happens to a room when it finds out at the end."

Somebody at the back said, "Then close nothing, then."

"Yes," Adrian said. "And that is not a joke and I am not going to pretend it was. Sit down, because I am about to do the thing I have never done, which is put a thing in a book that nobody can use, and I want five hundred people to watch me do it badly."

---

The problem was not Adrian's. The problem was that the rules of this book have no shape for what he was trying to do, and he had known that since the second of the month and had said it out loud to nobody.

A closing is when at least two independent witnesses freely accept a disclosed charter and the record takes it. A charter grants a use. Every charter in Lot 17's book since the ninth has granted a use, and every one of them has been about a token, and the token is in a locked case eleven feet behind him.

And a Stage 1 Reader can close a simple charter with one bearer, a short term, and a visible toll. He cannot split a use among bearers, and he cannot close the schedule of the twenty-ninth, and he said so in that yard on the twenty-ninth in front of two hundred people, and there is no version of the twenty-ninth in which he is now able to.

So he had brought it to the board at nine in the morning and asked them the only question that had an answer, which was:

"Can a board record a set of terms that grants no use?"

And the board had spent the morning on it, with all four seats named aloud, and had come back with an answer that was a lawyer's answer and not a clerk's, and Adrian thought it was the cleverest piece of institutional work he had ever seen anybody do in this city, and he also thought it was a dodge, and he has never been sure which, and he is going to tell the reader both.

---

"The board cannot grant a use," Nessa Pell said, reading it out, because it was her sentence and she would read it herself and she read every toll and every return in this lot. "It cannot waive a toll. It does not audit a security. It cannot strike a line off a notice board. It can refuse to record a closing, publish a result, call a review, uphold a challenge in part, and set or renew the short public custody term.

"Mr. Vale has asked whether the board may record a set of terms which grants no use and authorizes nobody to do anything.

"The answer is that the board has no such power, and that the board does not need one, and that this is the second time this month that the honest answer and the workable answer have come apart in this yard and the first time somebody has noticed.

"Mr. Vale cannot close it. He said so on the twenty-ninth and he has not stopped saying so. I cannot close it. I am a clerk and I hold a steward's key for seven days at a time. **The board cannot close it. And the board does not have to.**

"What the board can do is publish a result. So the board publishes the result. Not a closing — a publication. And a publication of terms that says on its face, in the first line, in words that cannot be read two ways: *THIS RECORD CONFERS NO USE, AUTHORIZES NOBODY TO MOVE ANYTHING, AND SEATS NO TOKEN.*

"And then the board calls a review of it, which is a power it has, which it will do in nine days, and which nobody can stop, because a review has to be called by somebody and there is no rule in this book that says a board may not review its own publication.

"And then the two fresh witnesses come forward and accept the published terms, as witnesses, in public, in the yard, having heard all of it — and that is a closing, because two independent people accepting disclosed terms after hearing them is what a closing *is*, and the only thing the record has left out is the use, and the use is the part this book says the board cannot grant.

"So the record is a closing of terms, and it does not open a road, and the reason it does not open a road is that the board is not able to open a road, and every person in this yard is to hold both halves of that sentence at once, because the half on its own is either a fraud or a miracle and it is neither."

"And you have just invented a new instrument in a morning," Adrian said.

"I noticed what four powers do when you put them next to each other," Nessa said, "which is the whole of this lot's history and the whole of my clerk's life. Mr. Vale noticed it a day later and came to me with it. **The man who found the fault in the schedule of the twenty-ninth is not the man who built the instrument of the sixth, and that is going in the record, because in four years somebody is going to write down that Adrian Vale invented a public instrument, and it is not true, and the true version has to be on a page before it stops being true.**"

---

The seven sheets took the rest of the morning and most of the afternoon, and the four hours of it were five hundred people arguing about the order of clauses, and the argument was not interesting and the result is.

Item one: a route request, with use, destination and count. The use is **one route across St. Orra for twelve minutes.** The destination is written in the two banks: the near apron of Lot 17 on this bank, the far receiving ramp on the older central bank. The count is the beneficiary count, split the way the people actually are, and it is the count of the twenty-sixth, corrected by the four of the second, and it is entered with both numbers and the reason for the difference.

Item two: the toll, in the words the travellers will hear, read aloud by Nessa Pell from a step, twice, at eleven in the morning and again at four in the afternoon, and it is the memory of the purpose of the journey, charged to each traveller on each crossing, and a returning traveller pays again, and the ninth line is in the record in the author's words because the ninth line is the only sentence anybody in this district has written that says what a second payment is not.

Item three: the bearers. Four entries, as they stood on the twenty-ninth, with Tarin Kest's refusal in the same line as his entry, and Renn Fisk, and Dessa Rill entered as a remembering passenger and *not a bearer*, and a mark.

Item four: the return crew, a rotation of three on foot from the far receiving ramp to the near apron, drawn by lot, who do not travel on the lot they witness.

Item five: a term with a stated end. One activation, ending at the return point or at twelve minutes, with no automatic renewal, and a new proposal required for any further use.

Item six: the split-use remedy of the fourth, entered whole, with the words *known to fail* at the front of it.

Item seven: two fresh witnesses, not travelling on it, who have heard the whole of it.

And a way for affected people to refuse, in public, without giving a reason, closing only when the case is physically opened.

And a new eighth line, which the yard argued about for an hour, and which Adrian did not write and would not have thought of, and which came out of a trader asking what happens to a person's barrow if the road never opens.

"I am not going to have that in it," Adrian said. "There is no barrow in a charter. There is no cargo. If somebody wants to move a thing on this they have to be in the count and the count is people, and if a man wants his barrow across he is a carrier and he is in item three and he is asked whether he is a bearer. I am not writing a lorry clause into a road."

"You do not have to write a lorry clause," Marda Osk said, from the table, with a cold. "You have to write the sentence that says there isn't one, because there isn't one, and in six weeks somebody will set a page on a wall at a tea table with a barrow on it and a line about a barrow on it, and it will be true, and it will be a lie, because there is no barrow in this charter. Write *no goods, no freight, and no carriage of any kind except the person who is carried as a person* — and then read it out loud, and then somebody who cannot read will know it."

"Seconded," said Sabin Tull.

"Entered," said Nessa Pell, "and it is the eighth line, and it is the first clause in this book that anybody in a yard has asked for because a counterfeit's second page made them think of it, and that is going in the record too, because it is the most useful thing the counterfeit has ever done for this district and the district is going to have to look at it."

---

They took the witnesses at about four in the afternoon.

They were not Adrian's witnesses. He was the seller and a seller does not witness his own sale, which is the oldest rule in this trade and the only one he has never argued with, and he stood at the rail with his hands in his pockets while two people who had never before been asked to do anything in this yard were brought forward by the board.

The first was a signal hand, the tenth of the Needle Tramway crew, who had a copied page in his hand, and who said out loud, before he accepted anything, that he was the tenth and not one of the nine and had been since the nineteenth and that nobody was to put him in a count because he was reading the counts.

"I accept the role," he said. "I have read all of it twice and the ninth line of the second sheet is the only sentence in this book I could have said out loud from memory before I came up, and I accept. I am the tenth hand and not one of the nine. My nine have been home since the fourteenth. I keep a page with every number anybody in this district has said out loud in a yard, and I have not been able to check one of them, and the count of beneficiaries on the third sheet is not in my page, and somebody in this yard is to say whether that is my failure or the document's.

"I am accepting the role anyway. And if that charter is performed and the count is short of what the people on the ground say it is, I will stand at this rail and say so, and I will not be argued with."

The second was Bett Ruhl, who had not been in Lot 17's yard in eleven years and had come because Orren Pike had told her on Thursday that there was a closing and that she would be asked, and who had said, in a kitchen, that she would be asked and would probably say no, and had then said yes on the Friday for reasons she gave to nobody.

"I cannot read," Bett Ruhl said, at the step, to five hundred people, in the voice she used for flour. "I want that said first so that nobody in this yard thinks a thing that happens next is about reading. I have had four documents on my wall for a month and I have never read one of them and I have copied the shapes of them and had four people correct me, and that is the whole of what I do in this city.

"What I do is keep a floor. And I have been a witness twice in my life, which is at this table and on a rope in a cut, and both times I was asked what I could do and I said it. So: **I accept the role. And I accept it on the condition that the ninth line is read out loud in this yard, in the words of the man who wrote it, at four o'clock and again tomorrow morning, and that I do not have to pretend I have understood it, and that if anybody in this yard believes I have understood it, that is their error and not mine.**"

"That is a strange thing to accept a role on," said the trader from the terraces.

"It is the only thing I have to accept it on," Bett Ruhl said, "and I have been thinking about it for two days, and it is known in this yard that a witness who cannot read is the correct witness for this and not a compromise, and that if you want a witness who can read then there are forty of those in this yard and none of them has ever kept a floor."

Nessa Pell entered the two acceptances and then she read the ninth line out loud, twice, and then Bett Ruhl asked her to read it a third time, and the third time is in the book because Bett Ruhl asked for it and because Nessa Pell reads every toll in this lot.

---

The record was made at about twenty past five, and it is this, exactly, in the order the board put it:

**PUBLISHED, LOT 17, THE SIXTH OF THIS MONTH, AS A RESULT AND NOT AS A CLOSING, BY THE BOARD, ALL FOUR SEATS NAMED ALOUD: MARA QUILL, REGISTRAR, NOT A VOTE AND NOT INDEPENDENT; NESSA PELL, SITTING STEWARD; SABIN TULL, UNCLAIMED SEAT; MARDA OSK, UNCLAIMED SEAT.**

**THE FIRST LINE OF THE RECORD, AND IT MAY NOT BE READ TWO WAYS: THIS RECORD CONFERS NO USE, AUTHORIZES NOBODY TO MOVE ANYTHING, AND SEATS NO TOKEN.**

**SEVEN ITEMS, IN THE ORDER OF THE PROPOSAL SCHEDULE, AND AN EIGHTH LINE: NO GOODS, NO FREIGHT, AND NO CARRIAGE OF ANY KIND EXCEPT THE PERSON WHO IS CARRIED AS A PERSON.**

**TWO FRESH WITNESSES ACCEPTED AFTER FULL DISCLOSURE, ONE OF WHOM CANNOT READ AND HAS SAID SO.**

**A REVIEW CALLED BY THIS BOARD ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THIS MONTH.**

**NO TOKEN WAS SEATED. NO VEHICLE CAME TO THE APRON. NOTHING OPENED.**

And then a yard of about five hundred people stood in a tram depot in Alder Reach, in the last hour of daylight, in the rain, and made the noise a yard makes.

Adrian Vale got the thing he had wanted at twenty-one years old. He got it at twenty-one because a clean hand is the only thing that makes a crowd obey, and he had written the evacuation order for the north stair in a clean hand, and eleven people came off the platform and four did not, and he has spent seventeen years pricing the difference.

He got a document that made a room orderly and safe and complete. Every objection was heard. Every term was read twice. Two fresh witnesses who were not travelling on it stood in a yard and accepted it in public. A remedy that was known to fail was entered with its failure on the front of it. Nobody could be sold, nothing was moved, and the whole of it was in a book that nine copies of were carried to the trading places of a district on the backs of barrows before dark.

And it was about nothing at all, and the yard knew it before he did.

He was standing at the crate with the slate in his hand and about five hundred people looking at him, waiting for him to say the thing a man says at the end of a sale, and he could not find it, and about ten seconds later somebody further back in the yard said it for him.

---

"Everybody in this yard has just signed a piece of paper about a road that does not exist."

It was Dessa Rill, and she was standing about eight feet from the rail with her hands in her sleeves, and she had not spoken for two hours, and five hundred people turned round.

"The bus is out past the underpass with no engine in it. The token is in a locked case and it is cracked and nobody knows and the man who reads things for a living has said so in this yard four times. The bridge is shut. The plank is in the water. And we have spent a day closing terms about a thing that may never open at all, and every one of you was in the room, and every one of you knew that, and not one of you went home.

"So I want to say the part that I think nobody else can say, and then I am going to go home.

"**The paper is the only thing that is real.** That is what has happened. The road is a maybe and the paper is a thing, and in six months there is going to be a piece of wall somewhere with a set of terms on it, and anybody who wants to know what anybody agreed to can go and read it, and that is the only part of the last eleven days that anybody will still have. Not the bus. Not the token. The paper.

"And I want to say the second part and then I will stop. I have paid five tolls on that river. I can tell you the price of every one of them to the penny. I cannot tell you what a single one of them was for. I have looked for a reason for three weeks and there is not one there and I did not put it there and it went. So I am one of about thirty people in this city whose memory has already been taken for this, and I am the one who drives, and I want to say the thing that a person with no reason left says, and it is this.

"**A paper about a road you cannot walk is not a small thing. It is the whole thing.** A man with a shaking hand on the upper terraces is selling people the road, and he is not lying, and he will be telling them the road is coming every day until somebody is standing in a market who can open a book and read out the terms and say: this is what is agreed, and this is what is not agreed, and this is the day it stops, and nobody has written that day, and here is the paper.

"And I would rather be a line in that than a driver on a road that does not open. And I know that is a small thing to say in front of five hundred people, and I have said it."

Nobody in that yard argued with her.

---

The closing was published at nine copies before dark and nailed to eleven boards, and at about half past six in the evening a reader at the ward market's counter on the terraces stopped reading it, because a reading at a counter is not a formality — Ivo Stenn had built a public reader out of a paper levy in the last three weeks and about forty people a day now bring a docket to the counting room and are told out loud what it says.

The reader was a woman of about fifty named Rutt, who had been a signal clerk before her shoulder went and who was the third person Stenn had hired and the only one of them who could read a nine-line claim fast, and she stopped about two thirds of the way down the fourth sheet and said:

"No. I am sorry. I cannot go on with this as a reading, because I have got to the end of the third item and there are four bearers and I can only find three, and one of the three says over his own signature that he was entered over a refusal given in the same room.

"And the fourth is a mark."

There were nine people at the counter at half past six in the evening on the sixth of this month, and one of them was Ivo Stenn, and he came out of the back office and read the fourth item twice, and then he did a thing that is now the standing practice of the ward market's counter and which took him about four seconds to think of and the rest of the year to be sorry about.

He sent a boy up to Lot 17 with the sheet and a slip, and the slip said: **THE FOURTH BEARER. WHERE IS HE. AND WHO ASKED HIM.**

The board was seated again at about nine, all four seats named aloud, because it always was, and Nessa Pell had a lamp and a slate and the published record on the crate in front of her, and she had the charter's own seventh item in front of her, which was the item that requires **two fresh witnesses who have heard the whole of it**, and the whole of it included a fourth bearer entered as a mark.

"Here is the finding," she said, "and I am going to say it in the shortest words in this book.

"**The fourth entry cannot be performed as written. A mark is not a person and the book has no form of agreement for a mark. The person it stands for is on a goods-shed floor on the far side of a river, and he was asked one question three days ago by the steward who carried the mark, and he said he did not know what the job was, and the entry says the entry stands until he is told what it is, in words, and decides — and he has not been told and he has not decided and nobody checked.**

"And the second finding, which is worse, and which Mr. Ambrose found with a bit of soap in a pencil and a three-hour stair, and which is in the beneficiary count of this very document: the count is wrong by a household, because a protected household in the service-cut yards west of this depot was counted by its steward instead of by itself, and the count was published before this record was made, and the record was made on top of it, and nobody in this yard knew there was a household in it.

"**The board cannot grant a use. It cannot waive a toll. It does not audit a security. It cannot strike a line off a notice board. It can refuse to record a closing, publish a result, call a review, uphold a challenge in part, and set or renew the short public custody term.**

"**THE BOARD SUSPENDS THE CLOSING OF THE SIXTH, IN THE FIRST HOUR OF ITS MAKING, ON THE GROUND THAT IT CANNOT BE PERFORMED AS WRITTEN AND THAT IT WAS NOT CHECKED.**"

"Seconded," said Sabin Tull.

"Seconded," said Marda Osk.

"Agreed," said Nessa Pell, "as the sitting steward, and I entered it, and I am not going to be a clerk about this and I am going to say one thing."

She put her hand flat on the crate.

"**I have held this book for eleven years. I have never once had a document in front of me that was complete, checked, and about nothing, and I have spent eleven years believing that those three things go together. They do not. What I have got in front of me is a document that is complete and checked and about nothing, and it took a reader at a counter on the terraces eleven hours to find the one line in it that could not be performed, and that line is the fourth bearer, and the fourth bearer is a man, and he was standing in a yard on the sixth of this month for about twenty minutes and nobody asked him a single question, and that is not because anybody wished him harm.**

"That is because a schedule says the terms are complete. And a schedule that says the terms are complete is the ninth thing that is not in the charter, and it is the tenth, and it will be in every one of them, and it is going to keep being in them until somebody with a bad shoulder and a good eye walks out to the top of a service cut and asks the people who live there what they are holding."

The board entered the suspension, and published it on the eleven boards in the morning, and nobody struck anything, and the paper stayed up.
