# Chapter 50: The Book, Left Open

The read-back happened on the twenty-sixth of this month, in a kitchen under the Ward Nine tram line, at about a quarter to seven in the morning, before the table opened, with two people in the room who were not clerks, and it was the last of them, and Mara Quill had made no promise about it because she had run out of ways to promise anything.

She read the whole of the run from the thirteenth to the twenty-sixth aloud, and she read the eighteenth twice over because the eighteenth is the day this district has to be able to go and check, and it took her two hours and six minutes, and she stopped nine times.

She read the vote of the thirteenth, the ward market's minute, four thousand and one households, the three unassigned coppers with the reason entered, and the trader who said that in this district nobody has ever put anything back.

She read the eighth line as it had been read out loud on the fifteenth, by a man who did not write it, with the clock and the six boxes and the two hands in it and no brass in the room at all. Then she read the reading of the object itself, an hour, entered as *the third entered in this month*, on the eighteenth, before the vehicle went, and the one entered at a quarter past five in the afternoon as *the fourth*, and the gap after it, and the sentence in her own hand about the next opening being on somebody else's word.

She read the auction, the tram arch, and the sentence *a crossing that is performed does not create a right anybody may sell*, and the sale of forty-one places at eleven coppers, and the whole of page one of a man being honest on an arch above nine hundred people.

She read the toll twice, because a toll is read twice, and then she read it a third time because Bett Ruhl asked, and Bett Ruhl said again, in front of eleven people, that she was not going to pretend she had understood the ninth line and that this was the fourth time she had asked for that.

She read the board's finding, that the order of the ninth is correct and that the district does not want it.

She read the four minutes on the apron in the rain, and the six words, and the sentence about the limit being the only thing anybody can check, and the mother's name, and the chandler's three columns, and the two hundred and twelve coppers and the *uncomputed and not nil* under it, and the man who said he would have made a very good thing out of four minutes and was not calling anybody a robber.

She read the release condition twice, once as Nessa Pell had read it at the opening of the case and once as Bett Ruhl had read it at the closing, and then she read the fact that the token had come back without the bus and that nobody knew how, and the two hundred and four grains, and the chip out of the reverse.

And at the end of it she read the line the yard had been in for the whole of it, which was about seven minutes in, and which is a man telling a man that a limit checked by one man is a man and not a limit, and which ends with *if you cannot let them then everything anybody in this district has read for five weeks is a note from a man* — and Adrian Vale, sitting on a flour crate by a wall, said, out loud, "That is the sentence I am going to have read at me in a year," and nobody in the room argued with him, and Bett Ruhl said, "It is going to be read at you by somebody with a worse accent than his," which is the closest that kitchen has ever come to a joke about the future.

Lina Vale was at the wall, with thirty-one keys, and had said nothing for two hours and six minutes, which for Lina Vale is a speech.

Then she said: "Registrar. You have read it out four times in a month and I have sat in this room three times and the last two times I brought somebody. Do it again in the autumn and I will bring somebody else, and that is not a threat, it is a booking."

"It is a booking," Mara said, "and I will enter it as a booking and not as a promise, because a promise in this book has been the expensive word all month."

---

**The board sat in the yard at ten in the morning on the twenty-sixth of this month, all four seats named aloud, and the one item of business was a key.**

The custody term of the object had run from the nineteenth for seven days and expired at midnight, and the hour of the renewal was published on the eleven boards on the twenty-first, at about eleven o'clock in the morning, in a fair hand, so that anybody who wanted to come and watch a clerk fail to renew a term on schedule would have known where to stand.

**And Nessa Pell had asked the board, on the nineteenth, for a rotation, and had said out loud that the board had no power to create one, and the minute says so, and the request stands.**

So the board did what it could do, which is: it set a term, and it published a result, and it took two witnesses, and it named a holder — and the naming is a thing it has always been able to do, because a steward's key has to be held by somebody and somebody has to be in the book.

**It offered the key to Adrian Vale first, in public, with all four seats named aloud, and he refused it in about eleven seconds, and the reason he gave is in the book and it is the shortest thing he has said in five weeks.**

"No," Adrian said. "On the twelfth of this month I put a thousand five hundred and thirty-six coppers of my own inventory into a steward's hands in a yard, in daylight, and it was the first time in seventeen years I had handed anything to anybody, and I would like the yard to understand that if I take that key back this afternoon then I took it back, and there is no version of taking it back that is not that.

"And I have thought about the other reason and the other reason is that a man who has been the wrong one in every room for four weeks and then the right one for eleven minutes should not be the man holding the key to the thing afterwards. **A man who ends the use is a man. A key that a man holds is a queue with a timetable.** That is not mine. Mr. Kest said it in this yard on the twenty-ninth of last month and I have quoted it in three documents since."

---

**And then the board had to do something it had never done, and it did it badly, and it did it in public, and the whole of Alder Reach found out about it at about a quarter to eleven in the morning on the twenty-sixth of this month.**

There were about four hundred people at the rail, because the hour had been published, and the board read out the qualifications a steward of a public lot is required to have, and the list is short, and it is in the book, and here it is whole:

**A person who can hold a count. A person who will not be replaced on the day. A person whose term is stated and whose handing-on is in the book. A person who is not a bearer, not a witness, and not a beneficiary of the lot they hold.**

**AND ANYBODY MAY SAY NO, AND SAYING NO IS NOT A REFUSAL OF SERVICE, AND THE COUNT IS PUBLISHED WITH THE DECLINE IN IT.**

There were about nine people in that yard who could hold a count. Four of them were on the board and two of those were the registrar and not a vote, and one of the four had said in public on the fourth of this month that she was a clerk who could not check the thing she was holding and that the board might replace her and the remedy would not care.

**Hester Vane** was at the rail, about thirty feet back, with a canvas bag at her feet that had nine pounds of somebody else's seed in it and that she cannot account for, and she was not on a roll, and she was not a clerk, and about four hundred people had heard her say in a yard on the eighteenth of this month, in the rain, that she knew the road and could not say why she had got on the bus.

A trader at the front said, "She is the worst possible holder of this key in this district and I say it as a man who is happy to be shouted at."

"Then say the rest of it," said Sabin Tull, from his seat.

"The rest of it is that a person who cannot remember why they did the last important thing they did is a person we cannot ask to remember why she did the next one, and there is a term on that key and it expires and it has to be renewed, and I have read this book and I know what is in it and the book says the day of the renewal is published and the hour is published and the holder is named before it is held. **I would be naming a woman who has told us in this yard that she does not know why she is here.**"

Hester Vane came about thirty feet forward, which took her a while, and she picked her bag up off the ground and set it down again in front of her, which she did not need to do, and then she said the thing, and it took about thirty seconds, and it is in the book because it is in the book.

"**I can hold a count.** I kept a barrow-load account for six years at a chandler's on the lower terrace and I was two counts out more times than I was in, and I know what a count is when it does not add, and that is all anybody wants a key-holder for.

"**I will not be replaced on the day.** I have not been replaced on a day in my life. That is not a virtue, it is a thing that happened to me, and it happened because nobody wanted the job.

"And I am not a bearer and not a witness and not a beneficiary of a road, because I did not travel on one to get anything. I travelled on one to fetch a bag and I have told about four hundred people I cannot say what was in the reason for it.

"And the reason I would take the key is the only reason anybody in this yard has given for anything in five weeks.

"**Because the last time there was a key in this lot it was in the hands of one person for five weeks, and on the nineteenth of this month that person's term expired at midnight and nobody noticed for twelve hours, and the lock was hers and the authority was nobody's. And that is how four coppers a quarter collecting for eleven years produced no paper at all, and how a footbridge has a plank in it, and how nobody has ever known who is coming.**

"And there is a girl of about nine on the far side of that river who is in no book in this city and a man in the lower terrace whose ankle will turn on wet ground and a child who died in the small hours of the twentieth of last month at an hour nobody kept, and every one of those three is the kind of thing that happens to a place where one person is doing a job and nobody has written down when it ends.

"So I would like the key, and I would like the term to be short, and I would like somebody else to have it after me, and **I would like somebody to explain to me, once, what I am supposed to do if the term expires and nobody comes.**"

"That is the standing failure of the office," said Selik Marne, from the rail, where he had been for about forty minutes and had not been asked to and had not left.

"What happens then," Nessa Pell said, "is that the person holding it says so out loud, in a yard, on the record, and a board of four seats is convened, and the yard is told within the hour, and the first person who turns up with a key is named in the book before it is held. **It is not a rule that stops it happening. It is a rule that stops it happening quietly.**"

"And I have written *that is not enough* on my own hand for eight years," said Selik Marne, "and I am not going to stop writing it, and I would like the minute to say that the Warden administrator of St. Orra does not accept it, and that he is going to keep saying so in a public yard every published day for as long as he holds the post, and that he considers that to be the correct behaviour of an office that has no expiry."

**Hester Vane took the key at about eleven o'clock in the morning on the twenty-sixth of this month, for seven days, and it was the fifth of the four seats' whole powers and it was the first time in eleven years that the key of the public case of Lot 17 had gone to a person who was not a clerk, and the term expires on the third of the new month, and the hour of the renewal is published, and her name is in the book before the key is held.**

---

**And then a child asked a question about the object, and Adrian Vale did not answer it with a number, and both the question and the answer are in the book, and it is the last thing that was entered in it this month.**

She was six. She had come with a woman who is the aunt of the boy of five who was asleep on a coat at the pegged end of the staging line on the seventeenth, and she had got about four feet in front of the crate and had been looking at the case in the office window for about a quarter of an hour with nobody stopping her, because about four hundred people in that yard had noticed her and had decided, individually and without discussion, that nobody was going to stop her.

"Mr. Vale," she said. "What is it worth?"

And Adrian Vale, who has been a reader for seventeen years and who has priced about nine thousand lots in a yard, and who put a figure on the damage at three columns on the thirteenth of this month and a figure on a shipment at three columns on the eighteenth, and who has held a number for every hard thing he has done in five weeks, did not give a number.

He said, "**I am not going to answer that with a figure and I want you to know why, and I want the whole yard to hear why, because they are all standing there.**

"Three questions, and I am going to answer all three in front of four hundred people, and you can ask me the first one again on Thursday if you like.

"**Who is listening.** Somebody always is. If I say two hundred and four grains, you will remember two hundred and four grains, and so will the man on the terraces, and so will about four hundred other people, and none of them will remember what the grains are attached to, and in a month there will be a page on a wall with two hundred and four on it and the word *token* beside it, and it will be true, and it will be a lie, because a number that has lost its sentence is not a measurement, it is a price.

"**Who bears it.** The six clinic hands that carried a box and could not use a hand. The fourteen off a roll. Tarin Kest, who has paid eight tolls on that river and cannot say why he drives the thing he drives. **Hester Vane, who is standing in this yard with a bag at her feet and who can tell you the road and not the reason.** A child in the small hours of the twentieth of last month whose name is not in any book in this city. And a man in a room in the lower terrace whose ankle turns on wet ground for the rest of his life. That is what it is worth. **It is worth what it is worth to them, and the number that comes out of that is not a number, it is a list, and the list is in the book and you can go and read it, and it is longer than the case.**

"And **who may refuse.** You. Not on that one — you are six and you were not on the bus and nobody is going to ask you. But anybody. In a yard, in daylight, by saying one word, with no reason owed and none given, and the only answer anybody owes them is *accepted*, and the window shuts when the case is physically opened and not one minute before. A boy of about fifteen stood in this yard on the fifteenth and said that word and a girl of eleven said it eleven minutes after him and the count went down by two and the sale went on, and the two of them are standing at the rail this morning on a bus nobody is going to put them on today.

"So it is not worth a number. **It is worth what it is worth to about thirty people, and the thirty are the ones who can be named, and the ones who cannot be named are the reason we do not put a number on it, and the only honest answer I have to a six-year-old in a yard is that I do not know, and I have said *I do not know* in this yard once already this month and somebody wrote it on a notice board, and it is the best notice anybody has ever nailed up in Alder Reach.**"

And the yard did not make a sound for about four seconds, and then about four hundred people began, all at once, to go home, which is a thing a yard does, and which the tenth hand of the signal crew noted in the margin of his page, in his own hand, as *they went, all together, after the child, not after him*, and which is in the book.

---

**And Rutt read the entry of the crossing aloud at about half past eleven in the morning, in the yard, to about three hundred and forty people who had not gone yet, and she had read it at the ward market counter that morning at six and again at nine, to about nineteen people, which is the standing practice and which nobody proposed and which is now simply what a counter is.**

She read the whole of the eighteenth. She read the count and the tolls separately. She read the seven grains. She read the four minutes on the apron and the mother's name. She read the line the yard had been in for the whole of it, about a limit checked by one man.

And when she came to the end she said the thing the reader's line says, and it is the rule of the counter and it was learned in the last month and it is now the reason the counter exists:

"**And if anybody in this city has told you a different number about any of it, you bring it to that counter and I will read it out again, in the words you did not bring.**"

---

**And here is what is in the book at the end of the twenty-sixth of this month, and it is entered by the registrar in her own hand, and there is nothing above it, and it is not a finding and it is not a rule and it is not a summary, and it is the only page in five weeks that nobody in a yard argued about.**

> **ENTERED BY THE REGISTRAR, ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH, AT THE FOOT OF THE PAGE, WITH NOTHING ABOVE IT.**
>
> **THE CROSSING OF THE EIGHTEENTH WAS PERFORMED ONCE, ON A TERM OF TWELVE MINUTES, WITH THIRTY-ONE TOLLS, SEVENTEEN PEOPLE ON THE FAR BANK, TWO DECLINES ENTERED AND NEITHER STRUCK, A TOKEN CRACKED THROUGH AND PARTED AT THE RIM, A SHIPMENT UNCOMPUTED AND NOT NIL, A HEARING THAT WAS CALLED AND WAS NOT DISCHARGED, AND A CHILD WHO ASKED A QUESTION IN A YARD THIS MORNING AND WAS ANSWERED WITH THE ANSWER THAT THE ONLY HONEST ANSWER IS THAT THE MAN DOES NOT KNOW.**
>
> **AND THE SEALED PAGE OF THE EIGHTEENTH WAS OPENED ON THAT DAY BY THE REMEDY BEING CALLED, AND WHAT WAS BEHIND IT WAS A PERSON AND NOT A CAUSE, AND THE NAME IS NOT IN THIS BOOK AND IS NOT GOING TO BE. THE PERSON WAS ASKED ONE QUESTION ON THE SECOND OF THIS MONTH, FOUR DAYS BEFORE THE ROAD WENT, BY THE STEWARD WHO CARRIED THE MARK, AND HE SAID HE DID NOT KNOW WHAT THE JOB WAS, AND HE WAS TOLD WHAT THE JOB IS, IN WORDS, AT LENGTH, ON THE FOURTEENTH, AND HE SAID YES, AND THE MARK CAME OFF THE ENTRY. AND HE WAS ASKED TO BE TOLD AGAIN, IN FRONT OF THE YARD, ON THE DAY, AND HE COULD NOT BE, BECAUSE HE IS ON THE FAR SIDE OF THE RIVER AND HE WAS NOT TOLD BEFORE TEN O'CLOCK THAT MORNING AND NOBODY WENT, AND THAT DELAY IS THE BOARD'S AND NOT HIS, AND THE BOARD HAS ENTERED IT AND HAS NOT APOLOGISED FOR IT IN SO MANY WORDS.**
>
> **AND HESTER VANE HOLDS THE KEY FROM THE TWENTY-SIXTH FOR SEVEN DAYS AND HER NAME IS IN THIS BOOK BEFORE THE KEY IS HELD, AND THE HOUR OF THE RENEWAL IS PUBLISHED, AND ON THE THIRD OF THE NEW MONTH EITHER SHE RENEWES IT IN PUBLIC OR SHE DOES NOT HOLD IT, AND EITHER WAY IT IS IN HERE.**
>
> **AND THE READING LEDGER IS CLOSED. Five entered in the month of the crossing, on the seventeenth, the nineteenth, the twenty-first, the twenty-seventh and the twenty-ninth. Four entered in this month, on the twelfth, the sixteenth, and twice on the eighteenth — one hour before the vehicle went and one hour at the closing, and the reader is not to read again before the twenty-fifth, and the twenty-fifth has passed, and he has not read again, and the next opening is on somebody else's word and not on his.**
>
> **AND THE COUNTERFEIT IS STILL WINNING ON SPEED. The news of the crossing reached the upper terraces about two hours before the closing was entered in this book, from a man who heard it at a rail, and about forty-one places were sold on a claim printed on page one, and the table at the tram arch read nine claims in a night on the twentieth and five of them were honest and four were not, and the honest ones sold twice as fast. That is the last entry on that subject in this book and it is not an ending. Nobody in Alder Reach has beaten anybody in this district in five weeks and the reason is on the terrace and the reason is that the paper is faster.**
>
> **AND THE SPAN AT ST. ORRA IS SHUT. The certificate has not been replaced. Two fitters who are not Warden Marne suspended it under the water office's own clause and they were right and he says so. The plank is in the service cut. The order of the ninth stands, and the review of the thirteenth of this month, which was called on his own motion with nobody's name on it, found the order correct and the district did not want it, and a standing order with no expiry is being drafted in a room on the second floor of the water office with a door on it and has not been read by anybody in Alder Reach and has not been named by anybody in Alder Reach, and the Warden administrator says the district has refused the safest answer available to it and means it.**
>
> **AND A READER, ON THE EIGHTEENTH, WROTE ON A WALL IN CHALK, UNDER THREE LINES THAT HAVE BEEN THERE SINCE THE FOURTEENTH OF LAST MONTH AND UNDER FOUR LINES IN CHARCOAL THAT A REGISTRAR ASKED TO BE WATCHED OVER: A FIFTH LINE. AND THE FIFTH LINE IS: *THE ANSWER IS NO. AND IT IS SAID OUT LOUD. AND NO REASON IS OWED.* AND BETT RUHL HAS NOT RUBBED IT OUT, AND WAS ASKED BY A CLERK WHY SHE HAD NOT, AND SAID THAT IT WAS THE FIRST THING IN A MONTH THAT HAD COME IN THAT WAS NOT NEEDED BY SOMEBODY.**
>
> **AND THIS BOOK IS OPEN.**

---

**And they walked out of the yard about ten past one in the afternoon, and it was not raining, and the man who had asked to be held to a thousand five hundred and thirty-six coppers went out through the gate behind a woman who held a key in her coat and behind a boy of about fifteen and a girl of eleven who were not on the bus, and behind the tenth hand of the signal crew, who is not one of the nine and has been since the nineteenth and keeps a page.**

Mara Quill walked out the other way, down to the tram arch, where a reader works four days a week at eleven coppers a quarter, and there is a lie in her own line and she knows which one it is, and there were about eleven people at the counter and four of them were holding dockets and were going to be told, in words they did not bring, exactly what their paper said.

Lina Vale went out with Bett Ruhl and a basket and was not thanked and said beforehand that she would not be, and Bett Ruhl said, "I know, and I'd have been insulted, and I've chalked a line on my wall and you can read it whenever you like and I don't want to be told about it."

Orren Pike went out last, with a sheet of paper folded in his coat that had eleven lines on it and had had for nine days, and nobody saw it, and it is not in the book, and the sixth line on it is his mother's name, and he has still not told anybody he moved it, and it is not going in the book.

Tarin Kest went up the marked spur to the second rail joint and stood at the chalk mark for about four minutes in the sun with his hands behind his back, and then went and opened the sluice at the low end at eleven, which is late, which he does every day of his life, and which nobody thanks him for, and which is on his schedule and not on anybody else's.

And Adrian Vale stayed in the yard with a book on a crate, and about nine hundred people had been in that yard five weeks before asking him to open a thing, and the yard was empty at about two o'clock in the afternoon, and there was a case in the office window with a cracked piece of brass in it and a key in the pocket of a cooper's wife, and a slate on the crate with a fair hand on it, and a hole in the office window where a lamp used to be.

He did not go and find anything to do. That is the whole of it, and it took him seventeen years to be able to do it, and there is no instrument in the world for it and there is not one in this book.

**A man who has spent four weeks turning every uncertainty into a figure sat in an empty yard in the afternoon and did not make one up, and the book on the crate stayed open on the page it was open on, and the book belongs to nobody, and it will be on that table tomorrow, and somebody else's hand will be on it by the end of the week, and the sentence at the foot of the twenty-sixth is the only thing in it that nobody in Alder Reach has argued about, and it is four words long, and it is not finished, and it was never going to be.**
