# Chapter 26: The Man Who Makes It Temporary

The yard was back in Lot 17 by nine the next morning, and it was a different yard, and the difference was visible from the gate: nobody was standing in the rain to be sold anything. They were standing in the rain to read.

Mara Quill had the book on the crate at nine and the two lamps over it, and the book was open at the nineteenth, and she had ruled a line under the heading so that the whole of it was on one page, which it was not, and she read the whole of it anyway, in order, taking forty minutes over the seven items and giving each of them the time it had actually taken to be wrong.

She read his own undertaking off page eleven first, twice, and then the sheet as it had been at seven twenty-five on the morning of the nineteenth, field by field, in his handwriting, and when she came to the fourth one she stopped and looked up.

"This is what the charter said about the return, on the morning that twenty-eight people got on a bus," she said. "I am going to read it twice. And then I am going to read the line that a barrow-hauler in this yard read out yesterday morning, and I am going to read the two of them together, and then nobody in this yard gets to have a private opinion about which of the two mattered more."

**RETURN. The return follows the outward term as an administrative extension of the same use. No separate toll is negotiated. Any traveler who returns pays the memory toll again.**

**The return is unwritten.**

The yard did the small noise.

Then she read the refusals, because he had asked her to and she had said she would, and because a document read in public is a document and a document with the objections left out is a pamphlet.

"This is Tarin Kest at four o'clock on the afternoon of the eighteenth, in the back office, at the table, in front of me and Mr. Vale. *I agreed to a return point. I agreed to a sentence about a road. I did not agree to a return with no terms in it, and I did not agree to this, and I am not going to pretend that this is the same thing, because the next time I open my mouth in a room I want the room to know that what I agree to is what I have agreed to.*"

"This is Warden Marne, at twenty past four on the same afternoon, and he put his hat on the table to say it, which I have not seen him do before or since. *A rule that cannot be read aloud by the people it governs has already failed the people it governs, whatever the paper says about it. Your return is not a term. It is a confidence. And I will not hold a flag on a spur for a confidence.*"

"That is the record," Mara said, "and it is in the book, and it was made by two people who were in the room at the time and who were not paid and who had nothing to gain on the nineteenth and everything to lose after it. Registrar's note: neither of those refusals has been withdrawn by either of the men, and both of them stand on this page this morning, and I am not going to round them off for the convenience of a room that would rather they were older than they are. The first was made at four o'clock and the second at twenty past four, on the afternoon of the eighteenth. Twenty-eight people got on a bus at twenty-five minutes past seven the next morning. That is thirteen hours and twenty-five minutes after the first refusal and fifteen hours and five minutes after the second, and the registrar has entered both figures and has entered neither of them as the interval, because the interval is a number a room picks and the refusals are a record two men made."

Adrian stood on the rail while she read it and did not move, and she had told him at half past eight that he would be there, and he was.

---

"Mr. Vale," Mara said, at twenty to ten. "You have nine lines."

"I have nine lines."

"Read them."

He took the sheet out of his inside pocket, and the yard made a noise at the paper itself, because everybody understood what had happened in the last four days, and Adrian understood it too, and it was the thing he had wanted most in the world for twenty-four hours and it was now costing him the yard.

He read them slowly. He read the whole of them, all nine lines, including the sentence about the second toll not being a smaller thing and not the same thing and not stated to be either, and the sentence about the joint being the return and the arm not being the return, and the sentence that a returning person is asked separately before the outward case opens, in a yard, in the words of the sentence before it, and may refuse without a reason.

Nessa Pell read them back to the yard, twice, and the yard heard a return condition for the first time in three weeks, and there was no sound at all while she did it.

"That is a term," Mara said.

"Yes."

"That is a proper term and it is the best thing anybody has put in this book, and I am going to say so in front of six hundred people, and I want it written down, because in about four days somebody is going to say that the reader cannot write one." She held the sheet up. "It is entered. Registrar Quill enters it, at ten past ten on the twenty-fourth, in the public lot book, as a return term offered for a future use and not attached to any use, and the board is notified and the board cannot act on it because there is no use, and the term stands in the book as a thing the yard can read and check and refuse next time."

"Seconded," said Marda Osk, from her crate. "By a person who has read the toll out loud to the gate for three weeks and has never once had a term to read after it."

"Seconded," said Sabin Tull, "and I would like it entered that I read it twice and I could not find a fold in it."

Nessa Pell entered it, and the entry took ninety seconds, and Adrian stood on the rail while a term he had written at five o'clock in the morning was checked by a barrow-hauler and a tea seller, and neither of them found anything, and that was the first completely honest thing that had happened to him in eleven days.

"Thank you," he said.

"Do not thank us yet," Sabin Tull said. "You have written it and we have read it and it is in the book, and it is a term for a road that does not exist, for a token that is cracked through the middle, on a river where nineteen people are sitting this afternoon. Read the rest of it."

---

"The rest of it," Adrian said.

He put the sheet down on the crate, on top of the book, and turned it round to the rail, and the two lamps were on it, and from the second row anybody who wanted to stand up and come and look could see his own handwriting.

"You are all waiting for the sentence," he said. "You have been waiting for it since half past eight yesterday and I have had a whole night to write it and I have not written it, and I am going to tell you why in the plainest words I own, and then you will do what you like with it.

"There is no procedural answer. I have looked for one for two days and there is not one. Every answer that I could give you in this yard today has the same six words in it — *the emergency is now and the paper comes after* — and I said those words on Tuesday morning in this yard and Selik Marne read them back to me in the best possible way he could manage, which was to refuse them, and he was right, and I have spent two days since then looking for a version of the sentence that is not the sentence."

He stopped, and looked at the yard, and the yard looked back, and none of it was friendly and none of it was hostile, which is worse.

"So here is what I have, and it is not a procedure, it is a list.

"I cannot read the route. I have read the brass four times in three weeks and it has given me the same four lines every time and one of them is a line about a return that is not in the record, and I cannot predict where it will come up, and I cannot tell anybody on this near bank where a bus will arrive, because when it came up last Tuesday it came up four streets west in a locked yard with nobody at the wheel and nine people already sitting in it, and they had been on it for eleven hours and forty minutes, and I did not know that and neither did they.

"I cannot post security. I have said that out loud in this yard and it is in the book, and it is the reason there was a number in a man's coat instead of a number in a book, and I am not going to stand here this morning and pretend the last three weeks were caused by bad luck in the paperwork.

"I cannot prove that the nine lines I have just read out would hold. That is the sentence I have been not-saying for two days. I have written a return condition and it is the best work I have done in a year, and I cannot tell you it will hold, because the road does not consult me, and because two days after the last crossing somebody put a hand in the socket of a bell post on this depot's apron and I have not got the faintest idea who, and the far side of that service cut is four streets west of this rail in a place I have never stood, and a man who cannot account for a bell post is not a man who can account for a joint."

"Then what are you here for," somebody said.

"I am here because there is a thing I can do and I would like to do it in the open," Adrian said, "and I am going to do it badly, and I would rather do it badly in the open than well in a room."

---

He put his hand inside his coat and took out a sheet of paper that was not the return term, and put it on the crate, and the yard recognised the paper before he said anything, because it was the same stock as the page that had come up on the notice board at four in the morning.

"That is my inventory," he said. "Lot 17. The press at three hundred and eighty by ward market. The repair-crew term, one season, three named hands. And the rest of it, which has never been in any book, including the lease on this building, which is two years and four months, and the salvage in the workshop, which is a hundred and forty lots that were last honestly priced in the flood year, and the lease on the yard, and the name on the sign over the door, which is mine, and which is the only thing in this city that nobody else can put a number on because it is not worth anything until somebody stands in this yard and says the road is real."

"Price it," said the cooperative clerk, from the front, with the rate sheet in the oilskin, and she said it the way she said everything, which was like a woman putting a coin on a table. "I have the sheet. Price it. Give me a figure and I will enter it and the yard will be able to argue with it."

"No," Adrian said.

The yard stopped.

"I am not going to give you a figure this morning," he said, "and I want the reason in the book in my own words, because the reason is the whole of what I have learned in four days. The claim this would pay is the claim for a charter that failed, and nobody in this yard knows the size of it, including me, and there is a page in this book that says a child died and it does not have her name in it, and I could put a number on my workshop this morning and every person on this rail would hear that number and believe that some of it had been settled. And I would be doing the thing I did on Tuesday. I would be putting a number on a page so that the page would look finished."

The cooperative clerk looked at him for a long moment and then put the rate sheet away inside her coat.

"That is the first time in four days anybody in this market has said no to a number they were owed," she said.

"It is not generosity," Adrian said. "It is that I do not have a number that is mine to set. That is the actual position and it has taken me four days and a child to be able to say it out loud, and I would like it in the book, and I would like the yard to understand that a man who cannot price his own loss has not become humble. He has run out of things he is allowed to be the authority on, which is a different thing, and it is worse, and it happened on a Tuesday at four in the morning with a stove going."

"And is it enough?" the repair-crew foreman said, from the front, with his bad shoulder. "The workshop. If you had put a number on it. Is it enough for my brother."

"He walked over," the man said, before Adrian could answer. "On the fourth, with a marshal and a lamp, like the hand that went on Tuesday, because his shoulder was bad and the far clinic could set it and this clinic could not. He is not in your book, Mr. Vale, because your book is a bus and he was not on one, and I would like the yard to hear me say that I have been angry about it for two days and that I have just worked out that it is not anybody's fault and that is worse."

"It is not anybody's fault," Adrian said. "It should be in the book. It should be in the book as a man who walked over on the fourth under a marshal and a lamp and is not in any count, and I will put it in myself, tonight, in my own hand, and it will be the one entry in that book that no charter supports."

"No," Adrian said. "It is not enough, and I am saying so in a yard rather than in a room, and I would like the yard to hear the reason as well as the answer, because the reason is that I have been on the wrong side of that question once and it cost nine people and a child, and the wrong side of it is *enough* — a number large enough to stop people asking. Every number I have ever given anybody has been large enough to stop people asking. It is what I am for. I am the man who makes the room stop asking, and it is a real talent, and it is why there are six hundred people here instead of a queue at the door, and it is also the reason that nobody knew what a return condition was until Tuesday morning, and the reason the market is now being sold a cheaper lie by three men on a printing plate."

Nobody in the four hundred agreed with him. He had said a bad thing about himself in public and got a silence, and the silence was not applause, and it went into the book as what it was, which was a man talking.

---

"What happens now," the tenth hand said. "To my nine. To the four children. Do I tell them something."

"Yes," Adrian said. "I cannot tell you what. Mara can."

The room turned round, and it did it fast, and Adrian found that he had lost the yard inside a sentence and had not noticed the moment it happened, and that this was not a punishment. It was simply what had happened. Six hundred people in a wet yard had read a return condition and an inventory and a list of four things a man could not do, and they had concluded, collectively, without anybody saying so, that the man on the rail had stopped being the person who knew where the road was, and that the person who knew where the road was a woman with a book.

Adrian stood on the rail for the rest of the session and he was in the room and he was not in it, and the difference was not hostility, which he would have known what to do with. It was that nobody asked him anything.

---

Mara Quill wrote the notice on a clean sheet and read it out before she wrote it, which is a thing she had never done for a notice, and it is the thing Rell Bode has been asking for since the fourteenth.

"I am going to write what we know, what we do not know, and when anybody might come," she said, "and it is going down the river stairs at noon with a marshal and a lamp and it is going to be read out on the far receiving ramp by the foreman of the signal crew, who is over there with the eight others of his nine and whose name is not in this book because his name is on a sheet at the top of his crew's roll, and it is going to be read out loud to the nineteen people in a school house and a signal hut and a ramp, and I am going to write it in the third person, because the third person is the only way to say it that will still be true on the far bank in a week."

She wrote it on the crate in the open, in a hand that went straight down the page, and Adrian read it upside down from the rail, which is a thing he had done four hundred times.

**WHAT THE NEAR BANK KNOWS.** That you were sent out on the morning of the nineteenth on a charter that named a route out and did not name a route back, and that nobody in this city has been able to fetch you since, and that the reason is not a difficulty and not a fault in any person on either bank. It is a piece of paper. There is no field in it for the way back, and the road that was run cannot be run again without a new paper, and the new paper is not written yet.

**WHAT THE NEAR BANK DOES NOT KNOW.** Whether any route will open. When. From where. Whether the object that opens it will open again, and it is cracked through the middle after four uses and it may never open again, and nobody in this city can say so either way, and a man who can read it says it cannot say so either way.

**WHEN ANYBODY MIGHT COME.** There is no announced date. There is no announced way. The official bridge at the far abutment is shut pending an inspection, and it was shut before this notice and it is not shut because of this notice. The old footbridge over the service cut is open and is being walked in daylight with a marshal and a lamp by people who are not part of any manifest, and it is slow, and it is dangerous, and it is being used. Nineteen people is nine days of that. If anybody comes sooner it will not be by the road, because the road is not written.

**WHO ANSWERS.** The registrar of the public lot at Lot 17 on this bank, and the steward of the school shelter for the children, and the foreman of the signal crew for his own hands, and the matron of the fever ward for the medicine. Not the man who wrote the charter, because he is not the answer to anything this week and he has been told so in a yard and did not disagree.

"Read it back," Nessa Pell said, from the crate, to Mara Quill, because that was the practice.

"I will not read it back to myself," Mara said. "I will read it back on the far ramp, in the open, to nineteen people, and one of them will read it back to me, and I will write down which one did."

The sheet went down the river stairs at noon with a marshal and a lamp, and it was on the far receiving ramp at two in the afternoon, and the foreman of the signal crew read it out loud in a signal hut and in a school yard and on a ramp, and a woman who could read it took it and read the second half of it herself, and the registrar wrote the name of the woman and not the name of the household, and the whole of that took a day and a half and is the single most useful thing any office in Alder Reach did that week.

---

And then a runner came up the tram apron at half past three with a slip.

The ward market's second clerk, with the handcart, again, because there is only one of her and she has a far bank to get to.

"Four boxes and a draught for the north end of the ward," she said, "and the ward is out, and the matron says on the twelfth, and I have to know which of the two things I am doing, Mr. Vale, because I have been doing it for two days and it is a different answer every day and I would like one answer that is a week long."

And the yard did the thing Adrian had told them it would do, which was to try, in front of him, in public, to agree on a word.

They could not agree on the word. It was not the medicine. It was the word. *Urgent* was Halloway's word and it was what people bought. *Scheduled* was the Warden's word and it meant a roll. *Priority* was a word from the book and it meant that a registrar had ranked them, and the one person in Alder Reach who had ever ranked anything in public had done it on a rail at four in the morning and had written a page about it afterwards.

Nineteen people were on the far bank, and four boxes and a draught were on this one, and the public road was suspended, and the official bridge was shut, and a bus had been found in a locked yard four streets west of a public lot, and there was a child in a book with no name in it, and a man had been six hundred coppers short of a claim nobody had stated, and there was a strip of paper on a wall in a locked yard in the wrong quarter of the city, and there was a line on a pilot trace in a glass case that nobody could account for.

Six hundred people stood in the wet in front of a book and could not agree on the word, and Adrian Vale stood on the rail in front of them and found that he had nothing whatsoever to offer, and that this was the first time in seventeen years that he had been in a room in that condition, and that the first time it had happened, in a different room, he had picked up a pen.
