# Chapter 43: The Sale

The yard at Lot 17 on the morning of the fifteenth held about six hundred people, which was more than had come on the sixth and about the same as had come on the twelfth, and the reason both numbers were about the same was that the district had stopped growing in its attendance and had started bringing chairs.

Nessa Pell read the index entry out at nine in the morning from a step, in the voice she used for tolls, and read it twice, and a third time at the request of Bett Ruhl, who was at the rail with her basket and her bad hip and who had walked three miles to hear a clerk retire a mark.

Then at about twenty past nine the board sat, with all four seats named aloud — Mara Quill as registrar, not a vote and not independent; Nessa Pell as the sitting steward; Sabin Tull and Marda Osk in the two unclaimed places — and did the thing it had called for itself on the sixth, on the day it had put on a board in a yard in the rain.

---

**THE REVIEW OF THE TERMS-CLOSING OF THE SIXTH, CALLED BY THIS BOARD ON THE SIXTH, SIT ON THE FIFTEENTH, IN THE YARD, BEFORE THE BOARD, ALL FOUR SEATS NAMED ALOUD.**

Nessa Pell read it out whole. It took her four minutes and she did not hurry any part of it, and the first line was the first line, exactly as it stands in the book, because it was written to be a line that could not be read two ways and the board has never once been tempted to improve it:

**THIS RECORD CONFERS NO USE, AUTHORIZES NOBODY TO MOVE ANYTHING, AND SEATS NO TOKEN.**

---

**THE FINDINGS OF THE BOARD, ENTERED WHOLE:**

**ONE. The seven items and the eighth line are SUSTAINED. A use, toll, term, release condition, and appeal path in one place in that order on one sheet. The release condition read at the opening and at the closing by a different person each time. Public refusal by saying the word with no reason owed, the window closing only when the case is physically opened. No successful crossing is a right anybody may sell. The book records what failed and no entry is struck. And no goods, no freight, and no carriage of any kind except the person who is carried as a person.**

**TWO. The fourth entry of the proposal of the twenty-ninth IS NOT SUSTAINED. A mark is not a person, and on the fourteenth the person it stood for was told what the job is, in words, at length, on a goods-shed floor on the far bank, and said yes, and the mark is retired from the entry and not struck, and the cross on the paper nailed to a gate in the service-cut yards is not this cross and no document in this book says that it is.**

**THREE. Item three is NOT SUSTAINED IN ITS LAST SENTENCE. The board published a term about a place, and a place is not a person, and this is the second finding this board has made about the same item, and the reason the first finding did not stop it is that a schedule says the terms are complete. The marked line at the top of the service cut is a Warden's rule and it remains a Warden's rule and is on the spur and has been for nine years, and this board did not write it, cannot write it, cannot move it, and is not going to pretend to have published it.**

**FOUR. This board has no power to make a Warden's rule and none of its four seats will attempt it. And the board records, in its own words, that a rule it cannot write is a rule it will still publish, and that the district is now going to find out what that is worth, and that the finding will be better or worse than a charter and the board cannot say which and is not going to guess.**

**FIVE. This record is a terms-closing. It is not a closing of a use and it never was. The use is proposed separately and the proposal is entered separately and the board has no power to grant it and will not be asked to.**

Then Sabin Tull moved the adoption of the findings and Marda Osk seconded and Nessa Pell said she would hold them as the sitting steward whether they carried or not, and Mara Quill did not vote, and it took about nine seconds, and the paper went up on the eleven boards before noon.

---

**And then, at about half past eleven, Adrian Vale put four things on the crate and told six hundred people that he was not going to choose between them.**

The yard had been waiting five weeks for a sale. What it got was a man in a waxed coat holding up four pieces of paper and saying the awkward part first, which by now was a thing this yard had a way of expecting and a way of resenting.

"I am going to describe a lot in about forty minutes," Adrian said, "and then I am going to open the refusal window, and then about two hundred of you are going to tell me things. Before any of that I have to get one thing out of the way and I have been putting it off for a week because I have not been able to write it down.

"**There are six cold boxes in this yard. They are on a cart by the office wall and they have been on that cart since the nineteenth and nobody has moved them, and they are the reason this whole month happened.**

"And the eighth line of this lot's terms — the line I did not write, that Marda Osk wrote out of a counterfeit's second page and Sabin Tull seconded, because somebody asked what happens to a person's barrow if the road never opens — says, in words: **no goods, no freight, and no carriage of any kind except the person who is carried as a person.**"

He let that sit, because the yard had heard it before and there is nothing new to say about it and everybody knew it.

"That line says no to the boxes. I wrote a proposal on the twenty-ninth with four bearers in it and a clause at two in the morning in it that put a man on a rail, and I have spent a fortnight being the man who says the awkward part, and I am going to say it now: **I am not going to be the man who quietly reinterprets his own clause to save a delivery.** Nobody in this yard should let me. If I do it this afternoon, somebody in this yard ought to stand up and stop me, and I would like it minuted that I asked for that.

"So there are four things on this crate and the yard is going to choose one, and I am not going to choose."

**"ONE. The medicine goes as people who are carrying it and are carried as persons. Six clinic hands carry six cold boxes and are carried as travellers. And the toll is charged to them, on top of the toll they are already paying for being on the bus, which is a man carrying a box paying twice because he is holding something."**

**"TWO. The eighth line is read narrowly and the reading is entered as a reading, in a book, with what it costs written at the front of it, the way a remedy is entered when everybody knows it will fail."**

**"THREE. The clinic's own line is what gives. The six-hour clock is the clinic's rule and not mine and not the road's, and the far fever ward is a third short of cots and has a draught due, and the clinic can decide that it will run its receiving station without the coolant and take that, and I have got no standing whatsoever to tell it not to."**

**"FOUR. The boxes do not go. The people go. The road is a road and not a delivery, and the medicine is carried the way it has been carried for a month, which is by three hours of river stair in the hands of whoever can carry it, and we sell a road today instead of solving a fever ward."**

---

Renn Fisk had been standing at the front of the crowd with his arms folded for about twenty minutes and he did not have to be asked, because this yard has never once in five weeks had to ask a man with a six-hour clock on him what he thinks.

"The clinic has six boxes," Renn Fisk said, "and a clock, and the clock is mine and it is not the matron's and it is not the road's, and I set it every time a box comes off a cart and I have set it about two hundred and forty times since the flood year and it is the only thing in this district I have never once been wrong about.

"**A box that is not cold within six hours is a box of soup.** Not bad soup. Soup. There is no way to argue a person out of soup who is in a fever ward, because a fever ward is not an argument, it is a body, and the body is what it is whatever is in the crate.

"So I am not going to pretend to be reasonable about this. I have lost four tolls and a barrow's worth of cold and I have never once said what a toll was for and I am not going to stand in this yard and be gracious about a clause a woman wrote out of a forged page. **I want the boxes on the bus.**"

**"I want the boxes on the bus," Marda Osk said, "and so does half this yard, and Mr. Fisk is right about the soup, and I have voted for the eighth line and I would vote for it again, and I am going to be the one who says the thing nobody wants said, which is that the four things on that crate are not four ways of doing this. They are three ways of failing and one way of doing it, and everybody in this yard can work out which one, and the reason the man who wrote two of them cannot say it out loud is that he wrote them."

Nobody in the yard argued with Marda Osk, which is a thing that happens about twice a month.

Then Nessa Pell stood up, and she had the slate, and she does not stand up.

"I have read every toll in this lot for eleven years," she said, "and I am telling the yard the plainest thing I know, and then I am going to sit down again.

"**The toll is charged to a person and not to a thing. It has been charged to a person since the ninth. It was charged to Tarin Kest and it was charged to Wenna Keel and it was charged to sixteen people on a return, and there has never once been a question in this book about whether a thing can be charged, because nothing that has ever sat in this lot has been a thing that could be charged.**

"And the eighth line does not say that a person cannot carry. It says there is no carriage. A barrow is a carriage. A cart is a carriage. A bus with a load in it is a carriage and that is exactly the thing it was written about. **A woman with a bag on her shoulder is not a carriage and has never been a carriage in the whole of the history of the world.**

"So the reading is there. It has been there the whole time. What has not been there is the price of it, and the price is what I am going to say out loud, and the yard is to write it down, and Mr. Vale is not going to say it because he would sound like a man rescuing a delivery.

"**Six of the eight clinic hands will carry a box each.** A person carrying a box is a traveller and pays the toll, once, like everybody else, and pays it as a person and not as a carrier. And a person carrying a box **cannot be the remembering passenger and cannot be the far-side responsible party and cannot stop to write anything down and cannot be handed a box by anybody else, and if they put it down on the far side then nobody is responsible for it and it is theirs.**

"That is the price. Six people who were going to travel with two hands are going to travel with none, and one of them is thirty-one years old and has a bad shoulder on the left from the third year, and the matron knows that, and the matron is on the other bank, and the matron has not been asked and I am not asking her in a yard."

"Then it is not the reading," said the tenth hand of the signal crew, from the back, with his page in his hand. "It is the reading with the cost written at the front of it, and the cost is that six of eight is a number somebody chose, and nobody in this yard chose it, and there is a man in this yard with a bad left shoulder and I do not know his name because I count and not names."

"Mr. Fisk," said Nessa Pell. "Is your left shoulder the one that took it?"

"It is."

"Then say it into the book and I will write it down and the box is on the other hand and the yard will know that the six were chosen by a man with a bad shoulder and not by anybody's opinion of what a shoulder is."

---

So the yard chose, and it chose in about six minutes, and Adrian Vale did not vote and did not have a vote and said so twice.

**THE READING ENTERED, THE FIFTEENTH OF THIS MONTH, IN THE WORDS OF THE CLERK WHO READS EVERY TOLL IN THIS LOT, AND COSTED BEFORE IT WAS AGREED TO, WHICH IS THE STANDING FORM IN THIS BOOK AND WAS SET ON THE FOURTH:**

**A PERSON MAY CARRY WHAT THEY CARRY. THE TOLL IS CHARGED TO THE PERSON AND NOT TO THE THING, ONCE, AS A PERSON. THE THING IS NOT A BEARER, IS NOT COUNTED, IS NOT CHARGED, AND IS NOT CARRIED BY ANYBODY EXCEPT THE PERSON WHO IS COUNTED.**

**AND THE COST, WHICH IS THE PART THAT MATTERS: A PERSON CARRYING A THING CANNOT BE THE REMEMBERING PASSENGER, CANNOT BE THE FAR-SIDE RESPONSIBLE PARTY, CANNOT STOP TO WRITE, CANNOT BE HANDED THE THING BY ANYBODY ELSE, AND IF THEY PUT IT DOWN IT IS THEIRS. SIX OF EIGHT CLINIC HANDS WILL CARRY ONE BOX EACH, AND THE SIX ARE CHOSEN BY THE PERSON WHO IS RESPONSIBLE FOR THE CLOCK, AND THE CLOCK STARTS THE MOMENT A BOX COMES OFF A CART, AND THE CART IS ON THIS BANK, AND THE BOXES ARE COMING OFF THE CART IN FRONT OF YOU NOW, AND THE CLOCK IS ALREADY RUNNING.**

Six boxes came off the cart in front of about four hundred people, and each one took about a minute, and each one had a number chalked on the lid, and the sixth lid was chalked last.

---

**And then Adrian Vale described the lot.**

He did it in about thirty minutes, standing at a rail, with a sheet on the crate and about six hundred people in his own yard, and he described it the way he describes everything, which is that you say the worst thing about it in the first minute.

"**Lot: the Last Fare. A brass transit token, found under this building, cracked through the plate, four activations on the record, one of them bare and failed and three of them closed in this yard with two witnesses standing on this bank each time. It may open one more time. It may never open again. It may strand the route permanently. Nobody in this city knows and the object has never said.**

"**Use: one route across St. Orra for twelve minutes. The vehicle goes out from the near apron of this lot and comes down to the return point, which is the second rail east of the bell post, under the lamp, where the ballast drops and the plate is stamped, in the bus of Tarin Kest, coming down from the far bank. And it is one activation and it is out and it is down and it is home, and it is not a road and it is not a service and it does not open again on its own.**

"**The toll, in the words you are going to hear, and I am going to read it twice, and the second time a clerk is going to read it off a slate in the yard, and anybody who cannot read can come and stand at the front.**

"**You will come back knowing the way and not knowing why you went.** Not what you were afraid of. Not the name of the person you were going to. Why you went. And it does not come back. And nobody in this yard can put it back, and I have stood in this yard and told this district what a toll is eleven times this month, and I am telling you now that I have known three people who have paid it twice, and **I cannot tell you what the second one takes, because the object has never said, and I am not going to guess at it in a yard of six hundred people.**"

He read it again himself, in the same words, and Nessa Pell read it off the slate, and there were people at the rail who had come to hear a number and had got a sentence instead and looked as though they had been short-changed.

"**Term: one activation, ending at the return point or at twelve minutes, whichever comes first. No automatic renewal. No extension. A new proposal and a new closing for any further use, and the whole of this yard back on a Tuesday arguing about a sheet.**

"**Release: the token goes into the public case at the closing whether the vehicle comes back or not. It has done it twice. A returned object is not a returned bus and nobody in this yard is going to be told otherwise today.**

"**Appeal: a hearing, in this yard, before this board, on a stated day, and anybody may call it, and the split-use remedy of the fourth is attached to it with the words *known to fail* at the front of it, because it is known to fail and you are all in the room.**

"**And there is no bidding.**"

Somebody at the back said, "Why not?"

"Because there is nothing to bid for," Adrian said. "A bid is a price against somebody else's claim to the same thing, and there is one use and it goes to the people it names, and the toll cannot be waived by this board or by me or by anybody, and if a man bids for a place on that bus he is buying a thing that does not exist and I have written that sentence into this lot's terms twice now and I am not going to unwrite it in a yard.

"And the difference between this sale and the one on the nineteenth is one sentence and I am going to say it and then I am going to stop.

"**On the nineteenth there was a document in a private undertaking with my stamp on it, and the public sheet said the entry would follow, and the entry did not follow, and a child died. There is no document. There is this book, and this book is what is being sold, and any person in Alder Reach can take it cold this afternoon with no help from anybody and check every word I have said against it.**"

---

**The refusal window opened at about half past two in the afternoon and closed when the case was physically opened, which was not until the following day at about nine in the morning, and the yard sat in it for about twenty hours, which no room in this city had ever done for anything.**

Nobody in the first four hours said anything much, which surprised about nine hundred people who had come to watch a bus.

At about five o'clock, at the back, near the gate, a boy stood up.

He was the one who refused to board in the first week of this month, and who wrote a complaint on a notice board in a school hand, and who stood up in a school gym on the twenty-sixth and asked a question that no charter in this book has ever asked and that is still in the book with no answer attached: *who tells four thousand households of this district, and by whom, and for how long, if the crossing never opens.*

**He said the word.**

Nessa Pell did not repeat it. She said, in the voice she used for tolls: "**Accepted. And the reason given, which is none.**"

And she wrote it in the book in front of six hundred people, and the book says:

> **THE COUNT OFFERED THIS MORNING WAS TWENTY-SIX. ONE DECLINE, ENTERED AT THE GATE OF THIS YARD AT ABOUT FIVE O'CLOCK IN THE AFTERNOON BY THE WORD, WITH NO REASON OWED AND NONE GIVEN, AND THE PERSON IS THE AUTHOR OF THE OPEN QUESTION OF THE TWENTY-SIXTH WHICH REMAINS PUBLISHED WITH NO ANSWER ATTACHED, AND NEITHER ENTRY IS STRUCK AND NEITHER IS CONNECTED. THE COUNT IS TWENTY-FIVE.**

And then about eleven minutes later a girl of eleven stood up about nine feet from the rail and said the same word, and Nessa Pell read it back, and the yard did not make a sound.

> **THE COUNT IS TWENTY-FIVE. ONE DECLINE, ENTERED AT ABOUT TEN PAST FIVE, BY THE WORD, WITH NO REASON OWED AND NONE GIVEN. THIS PERSON WAS OFFERED A PLACE ON THE NINETEENTH OF LAST MONTH AND REFUSED IT AND STAYED DRY ON THE NEAR BANK, AND THE ENTRY OF THE NINETEENTH SAYS *NOBODY HAD EXPLAINED THE TOLL TO HER*. SHE HAS NOW BEEN ASKED TWICE. THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN THE TWO REFUSALS IS THE WHOLE OF THE LAST MONTH AND IT IS ENTERED AS A FACT ABOUT THE ASKING AND NOT AS A FACT ABOUT HER. THE COUNT IS TWENTY-FOUR.**

And the child of eleven sat down, and Teo sat down, and about four hundred people in that yard were looking at two people who had just done the first thing anybody in Alder Reach had done in five weeks, which was to refuse in public, with no reason, and find out that the room did not stop.

**Which it did not.**

"**Twenty-four," Adrian Vale said, to about six hundred people, at about ten past five in the afternoon, with the count on the crate and the window still open and about eight hours to run in it. "Twenty-six offered. Twenty-four travelling. Two declines in the book and neither of them is struck and neither of them is a hole, and the sale has not stopped, and that has not happened in this yard before in five weeks, and I want to say why it has not happened and it is not to do with me.

"It is because the terms were all read out before the counting. A month ago the counting came first and the terms came afterwards out of a private undertaking, and that is the order that killed a child. **The order is the whole of the instrument.** The order is the ninth thing that was not in the charter of the nineteenth and it is the first thing in this one and it is in the yard now in capitals, and anybody who wants to check it can come and read the crate."

Then somebody asked him, from about thirty feet back, in a voice with no malice in it at all, which is the voice that always asks:

"Mr. Vale. Is there going to be a bus tomorrow?"

And Adrian Vale said: "**There is a case to be opened tomorrow at about nine in the morning, and it opens when it opens and not before, and the window closes when the case is physically opened and not one minute before, and I am not going to answer that question in a way that gets a cheer out of this yard, and I would like whoever wrote it down to write down that I said that.**"

And the yard waited, all afternoon and all night, in a tram depot, in a district with no crossing in it, for a case to be opened.
