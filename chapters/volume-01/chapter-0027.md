# Chapter 27: Publishing the Failure

It took Nessa Pell two days to copy the whole of it, and the reason it took two days and not four hours was that Adrian would not let her do it faster.

"The whole of it," he said, on the morning of the twenty-fifth, with the wax tablet flat on the crate. "Not the terms. The document. The paper as it stood at seven twenty-five on the morning of the nineteenth, with his hand on it, including the field with the sentence in it. Including the line under security. Including the note at the bottom that said the entry in the public book was deferred to the return term. And then the pages behind it, in order: the six objections, in the order they were given, with the two he answered and the four he did not. Page eleven. The refusals. The private undertaking, the whole of it, in full, with the stamp, because it exists and it is stamped and it is a real document and it is not in this book, and if I am going to put the number six hundred and twenty-nine in this book I am going to put the piece of paper that produced it in here too, with the fold in it where I put it."

"And the list of what is not in it," Nessa Pell said.

Adrian stopped.

"That is in the book already," Nessa said. "Registrar Quill's line from the seventeenth. *Write the list of what is not in it and read that too.* It is on the sheet in the yard, in her hand, and the man who gave it to you was seventeen and has never once been thanked for it in a yard."

"Then it goes at the front," Adrian said.

---

The list of what was not in it ran to two pages and Nessa Pell wrote it out under his direction and then read it, and it went into the book on the twenty-sixth, in public, in this order:

What the charter of the nineteenth did not contain: a return condition; a statement of what a second payment of the memory toll takes; a person who would pay a return toll separately from the person who agreed to the sheet; a security entered in the public book; the price of a returning child's walk from the receiving ramp in the dark, which was not stated; the daylight remaining after the return, which was not stated; a name for the responsible party of the school shelter group, which was a protected category and is a protected category still; a witness who had heard the whole of it, because the whole of it was never written; and any statement of what happens if the marking at the return is not where the charter says it is.

"They are not mistakes," Adrian said, when she had read it, standing in the middle of the yard with a hundred and forty people in it. "Six of those nine are things I could have written. Three of them are things that could not be written because there is a person in the way, and that is not the same as being impossible, and nobody in this yard is going to get the satisfaction of deciding which of the nine they think I did on purpose."

Then the yard came to look at it, and did what yards do, which is to read a document in about nine minutes and have an opinion on it for the rest of their lives.

---

What happened to Lot 17 in the four days after that was not a disaster and it was not a run.

The ward market suspended cooperation on the twenty-sixth, in a session that took forty minutes and in which Ivo Stenn read out four clauses and then said, in front of sixty people, that he was doing it because he could not carry a market's fuel and lamp rights into a yard where the price of the last crossing was still being argued about, and that he would be back when there was a number. He was honest about the fact that it was a suspension and not a refusal, and Stenn had been honest in that particular way since the fourteenth, and Adrian had never once been able to use it against him.

Two long-standing lots stopped coming. A chandlery that had brought salvage to Lot 17 every market day for three years went to a yard on the terraces, and a coal merchant who had never once liked Lot 17 but had liked being able to look at a book went with him, and neither of them said anything about the nineteenth in public, and both of them stopped coming, and Adrian noted the day and the reason in the book because he had learned in eleven days that the day a thing stops and the reason a thing stops are the only two facts anybody needs and the two facts nobody writes down.

On the twenty-seventh he closed the inspection yard to the public for two days.

That was the one he could not explain, and the yard asked him about it at the rail four times, and the answer was simply that he was going to price a hundred and forty lots of salvage honestly and that he was not going to do it in front of two hundred people who wanted to know what it was worth, because a man doing that in public produces the number he wants to be asked for.

He did it with Tarin Kest's two men and a tally sheet and two days and no lunch, and the hundred and forty lots came out four hundred and ninety coppers *below* the paper they had been carrying since the flood, and the reason was that thirty-one of them were things he had bought as jobbing lots and had re-priced as if he were not going to sell them in a hurry, and a man who is not in a hurry is not a real seller, and the whole of his price had been made out of a hurry he had invented in the flood year and never told the market about.

He entered that too. **Thirty-one lots were priced as though the seller were not under pressure to sell, and the seller was under pressure, and the difference is four hundred and ninety coppers against this lot's own figures and against the market's.**

---

The reading came at about eleven on the twenty-seventh, in the yard, in daylight, in front of about sixty people, and Adrian put two fingers on a damaged feed pump a trader had brought, and the writing came, and it was four lines and then a gap.

```text
PUMP, LINE SIX FEED, EASTERN RETURN.
USE: NONE THAT THIS HOUSEHOLD HAS THE STANDING FOR.
TOLL: THE TOLLS IN THIS HOUSEHOLD'S MEMORY OF WATER.
```

"That is all of it," Adrian said.

The trader, a man of fifty with a burned cuff, looked at the pump and then at him. "You used to be quicker than that."

"Yes."

"You used to be able to hold a thing and tell me what it wanted in a minute. That is four lines and a gap and a warning about my own family." The trader laid his palm on the pump. "Mr. Vale, I have brought four things to this lot in three weeks and I brought the first two because you could read. I have not brought the last two because I did not want to be standing here while you did it in front of sixty people, and I have just done it anyway, and I would like to know what happened to the reading."

Then the echo came and took him: old coins, the flat edge to the sound, the right hand going slack in its own grip, and an hour gone, and the trader standing at the rail with a pump and no reader, and Nessa Pell writing down the time to the minute.

"Entered in the book," Nessa Pell said. "A reading, the fourth entered this month, and the first since the twenty-first. One hour. The reader is not to read again until the twenty-ninth."

"I have got a hundred and forty lots to price."

"Then price them with a tally sheet and a boy," Nessa Pell said, "which is what a man with a good eye and no energy is for, and it is not a demotion, it is the job, and I have watched three clerks do it better than you."

---

Halloway Reed came on the twenty-eighth with a wagon and no oiled paper, which was the first time Adrian had ever seen the man arrive at his own lot without a sheet.

"I am not here to sell," Halloway said. "I am here because a man on the terraces has taken two lots off you and told his neighbours why, and it is worth knowing that he told them the reason was *he made a mess and wrote it down*, which is a better sentence about you than I could have paid for."

"Stenn is the ward market and he has suspended."

"Stenn is honest and he has suspended, and the ward market is out, and the coal man is out, and the chandlery is out, and I have had the clerks from all three in my yard this week." Halloway put his hands behind his back. "Mr. Vale, on the twenty-eighth of this month, Alder Reach has one road-shaped offer in it, and it is mine. It has a fifteen-day labour term on the first page and it falls on me. It is not cheap for a carrier, because I made it expensive on purpose, and it is available to anybody who can read the first page, and about a third of the people in your yard cannot read, which is another of the reasons it works and which I have never once pretended otherwise in public."

"Then say the rest of it," Adrian said. "You did not come here to be decent to me."

"I came here to be decent to you *and* to take the market, and the order of those two things is not the order I would like it to be in anybody's book and it is the order it is in mine." Halloway was not smiling. "You have done the one thing in this trade I have never been able to afford. You have published a road that failed, in full, with your own hand on it, and the price of it, and a list of the nine things it did not contain, and a child's death in a line with no name in it. I could not do that. Not because I would not. Because if I did it there would be nothing in my book to sell with. That is the whole of my business model and you have just printed it out loud in your own yard with nobody to stop you."

"I had a registrar who made me."

"You had a registrar who made you and an undertaking you broke in your own handwriting," Halloway said, "and I will not insult you by putting it on the other side. What I am going to say is the other thing, and then I am going to leave, and I would like it in your book if you write anything down. If the route goes to one man, it will not be you and it will not be me. It will be whoever is holding the first page, and in this city that is going to be me, because I am the only man anybody can buy a term off at four in the morning, and because a market with one road in it does not need a public process. It needs a shopkeeper. That is the argument I have been making since the seventeenth and I have never made it better than this, and I have watched you make yours worse every day for a week, and I would take it as a kindness if you wrote down that I said so."

He got on the wagon.

"Two lots on the terraces," he said, "and a ward market that suspends, and no public road, and nineteen people on a bank. I will hold a price on the crossing until the thirty-first and after that it goes up, and it goes up because I have six carriers who have to eat, and not because you lost. Good day, Mr. Vale."

---

The payment was on the twenty-ninth, in the yard, in daylight, with the board seated, and Adrian set the figure himself, and this is the part he will have to say out loud for the rest of his life, so he said it out loud before anybody else could.

He had three figures available to him and he had gone into the workshop at six in the morning and worked out what each of them was.

The flood-year figure was the one on the hundred and forty lots, and it was the highest, because it was a price made by a man in a hurry pretending he was not, and it was four hundred and ninety coppers better than the truth, which is the number below, which is the number that took two days.

The good figure was the one he would have used in a room with a buyer in it: press, season, salvage at current market, lease two years and four months, yard, sign, and the good name of a lot that has a book in it that anybody can read. That was the figure that would have stopped people asking.

And the third figure was the one he carried out to the crate.

"Six hundred and twenty-nine, and the ward market's rate sheet, and the nine coppers and the two hundred and fifty-eight entered beside mine, because that is what the yard priced me at on the twenty-first and I will not unprice it this morning in order to look generous.

"Plus the salvage, at four hundred and ninety coppers *below* the figures I have been carrying since the flood, because thirty-one lots were priced by a man pretending he was not selling, and the yard should have that money.

"Plus the lease on this building and this yard, two years and four months, and the sign over the door, which I am not putting a number on, because it is not mine to price. It is this lot's, and if anybody wants it they can buy the lot.

"**One thousand one hundred and nineteen coppers.** That is the inventory of Lot 17, at the prices I set this morning, and I set them, and the board has not checked them because the board cannot check them, and I want that in the book in those words, because *the board cannot check them* is the sentence that got the twenty-ninth into this position and it is the only sentence in this yard that has not changed since Tuesday."

"Seconded," said Marda Osk.

"Seconded," said Sabin Tull, "and I am going to say one thing as a board member and the clerk can record it or not. That is a real number and it is money and I know roughly what it is worth, because I have moved four hundred coppers' worth of salvage in a week for a man with a bad hip. And it is a fortnight's work. Not a year. A fortnight. I have wanted to say that in this yard for eight days and nobody was going to let me, because there was always a number coming."

"Entered," said Nessa Pell.

"And now the part that is the actual payment," Adrian said, "and I have got it in my own words and I am going to read it out myself instead of dictating it, and if I get a word wrong somebody is to stop me."

He read it off the sheet.

"**This is a payment. It is not security. A reader cannot post security and I am a reader, and the difference is not a technicality: a payment is spent, and a security is lost on a breach. A security is a thing held back in case. This is a thing gone.** I am offering it as a payment on a claim which has not been stated to me, by a body which does not exist, for a loss which is in a book under a name that nobody has asked for. It is offered to the steward of this lot to be held for the relief of the people the charter of the nineteenth touched, and it is offered to nobody else, and it is not conditional on anybody signing anything, and there is no instrument here that anybody can hold over me in six weeks.

"The charter of the nineteenth was not breached. The outward crossing was performed. The return was not, and the toll on the return was not paid, and so the record does not say a man broke a promise; it says a road was run with no way back on it and a toll nobody was asked to agree to, and that is a worse sentence than a breach, because a breach can be put in a book with the two names on it and *this* cannot."

Nessa Pell entered it, and it took two minutes, and the yard listened to all of it.

"And the shortfall," Adrian said, "is in the same entry, and it is the whole of what I have got left, and it is not going to be left off to look tidy.

"**The claim is not stated. Therefore the difference between the claim and the payment cannot be computed, and it is entered as uncomputed and not as nil, and Mr. Vale has not entered it as nil because a nil would be a number he chose.**

"**Registrar: the remedy is called. It is not discharged.** The reason it cannot be discharged is the protected category, and the child who died on the twentieth of this month is in that category, and no remedy that works by pointing at a person can ever be called for her, and it will be exactly like this in six months and in six years, and it is the price of the thing that keeps her name out of this book, and I would like the yard to hold both of those in the same hand, because the second one is not an excuse for the first one."

"Then what is it," said the barrow-hauler with the bad hip, from the back. "The first one."

"It is the reason this yard can be trusted with anybody who has no file," Adrian said. "It is the only thing in this book I am actually proud of, and it cost a child, and I will not stand in the rain and tell you it did not."

---

The yard read the whole of the nineteenth out of the book over the following two days, and the yard came to the end of it, and there is no way to know what a yard decides except from what it does.

What the yard did was this. On the evening of the twenty-ninth a barrow-hauler's association of eleven people, who had never in their lives held a meeting, met at the tram arch, and eight of them came to Lot 17 the next morning and stood in the yard while Marda Osk read the whole of the nineteenth out loud to them, from the beginning, including the nine things it did not contain, and then one of them said a thing that went into the book because Nessa Pell was there and wrote it down.

"We are not signing with Reed," the barrow-hauler said. "Not because he is a crook, and he has not been to us, and I have heard that he is even proud of his own sheet. Because you have published what a road with no return condition in it does, and it cost a child, and now there are nine lines in that book that anybody can check the next one against, and if the next one comes out of anywhere else I do not know how to check it, and if it comes out of there I can check every word of it, and I would like somebody to write down that eleven people made that decision on the thirtieth of this month and that we made it after reading the failure and not before."

"Entered," said Nessa Pell, and looked up. "That is the first recorded refusal in this book that was not a refusal of a bad thing. It is a refusal of a good thing, on the grounds that people can check it. I would like the yard to understand that I did not know that was available as a reason until this man said it in front of me."

---

Halloway Reed kept his price until the thirty-first and then did not raise it for another nine days, which Adrian did not learn about for some weeks and which by then was a fact about other people's arrangements rather than about his own.

The public book of Lot 17 for the month of the nineteenth is complete enough that a stranger could take it cold — no notes, no memory of the room, no help from anybody who was standing in it — and reconstruct the whole of the failure: what the charter said, in whose hand, in what order; what it did not say, in a list of nine; what the objections were and which four he had not answered; what he had promised on page eleven and where the promise had gone; what the security was, and the four hundred and ninety coppers by which the lot had been over-priced on paper since the flood year; what the bell post's arm had done; and what had been entered on the twenty-ninth at a value the reader set himself against an unstated claim, in an entry that did not pretend to finish anything.

Adrian read the whole of it back, alone, in the workshop, on the first of the month, with a lamp, and it took him until about two in the morning.

And at about two in the morning he noticed what was different about it, and it took him a while to say it to himself, because he had been writing documents in one hand for twenty-two years and the hand was the same and the thing was not.

Every other set of terms he had ever put in a book had been written so that a person could not get past it. That was not an intention. It was a *style*, the way a man ties his laces, and it went in the paper and the paper was meant to look like the end of an argument, and the whole of his trade had been in the fact that the person in front of him could not follow him out of the room.

This one could.

He sat in an empty workshop with a lamp and a hundred and forty lots re-priced and a lease he had just offered to give away and a book he had handed to a stranger, and he thought: for the first time since I was twenty-one, I have written something down that does not try to look finished, and I did not do it for money, and I did not do it to be liked, and the reason I did it is that somebody in this city has to be able to check me, and I am the person it turned out to have to be.

He wrote one more line in the private ledger, which is not the public book and which nobody will ever read.

*Twenty-second. Emptied the workshop. The route is not a private gate. Cost: everything I had.*

Then he put the lamp out and went to bed, which was the first time in eleven days.
