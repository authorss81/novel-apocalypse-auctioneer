# Chapter 46: The Auction

There were about nine hundred people in the yard of Lot 17 at ten o'clock in the morning of the eighteenth of this month, and the yard had been built for two hundred, and about four hundred of them were standing on the tram apron outside the rail because the rail was full, and about eleven people were on the roof of the loading bay, which the foreman of the repair crew had forbidden in a voice that carried and had then not come down himself.

The board was seated at nine with all four seats named aloud — Mara Quill as registrar, not a vote and not independent; Nessa Pell as the sitting steward; Sabin Tull and Marda Osk in the two unclaimed places — and Ravell was at the reading board with his cold, because his duty under the order of the ninth is to read the days and not what is behind them, and the days were read, and nobody took any notice.

The case was brought out at eleven minutes past nine and put on the crate, and Nessa Pell opened it, and the wax was broken, and the token came out onto a cloth, and it was cracked through the plate.

---

**And the first thing done, the moment the case was opened and before a single other thing in it, before the lot was described and before the witness pair was taken and before anybody had said a word, was the release condition, read aloud at the opening of the case by Nessa Pell, in the voice she uses for tolls, to about nine hundred people.**

> **THE RELEASE CONDITION OF THIS LOT, READ AT THE OPENING OF THE CASE, ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF THIS MONTH, BY THE CLERK AND SITTING STEWARD, AND IT WILL BE READ AGAIN AT THE CLOSING BY A DIFFERENT PERSON, AND THE TWO READINGS ARE ENTERED BOTH:**
>
> **THE OBJECT LEAVES THE CUSTODY OF THE PUBLIC CASE OF LOT 17 AND IS SEATED IN THE ROUTE-CONTROL SLOT ABOVE THE OLD FARE BOX, INSIDE THE VEHICLE. IT GOES INTO THE FAULT WITH THE VEHICLE. AND AT THE CLOSING IT RETURNS TO THE PUBLIC CASE OF LOT 17, WHETHER OR NOT THE VEHICLE RETURNS.**
>
> **AND THIS IS THE PART THAT MATTERS AND IT WILL BE THE PART PEOPLE REPEAT AFTERWARDS: A RETURNED OBJECT IS NOT A RETURNED VEHICLE. IT IS NOT A RESCUE. IT IS NOT EVIDENCE THAT ANYBODY IS ALIVE AND IT IS NOT EVIDENCE THAT ANYBODY IS DEAD. IT IS A BRASS TOKEN ARRIVING AT A PLACE IT WAS GOING TO ARRIVE AT ANYWAY, AND IT HAS DONE SO TWICE, AND BOTH TIMES NOBODY WAS IN IT.**
>
> **NOBODY IN THIS YARD IS TO BE TOLD OTHERWISE BY ANYBODY WHO HAS SEEN IT COME BACK.**

There was a long noise from outside the rail, and Marda Osk called for the tram to be quiet, and it was quiet.

---

**And then, at about ten past ten, before the case was opened and before the closing, a man on the upper tram arch was selling something, honestly, in daylight, in front of nine hundred people, and he asked for it to be noted.**

He had come at nine with about sixty of his own crew and had set up a table on the arch itself — not in the yard, on the arch, above the crowd, where everybody could see him — and he had a wax stick in his hand and not a die, and a stack of printed sheets, and the first page of every one of them was the whole offer.

He did not come into the yard. That is worth saying, because a man who had come into the yard would have been a different man.

"Mine is a claim," he said, to about forty people who had come up the arch, "and it is not a claim on this crossing, and I want the first thing anybody writes down to be that sentence, because I have watched this district spend five weeks believing that anybody selling anything at St. Orra is selling a road, and I am not selling a road and if anybody thinks I am they should stop buying and I will say so again in an hour.

"**It is a claim on the next closing.** A place at the front of the next count, for a stated price, with the same toll as everybody else, with the same terms, disclosed on the first page, in the plainest words I could find, and my name and my crew's names on the first page and not on the second.

"**Eleven coppers.** And I know what eleven coppers looks like next to a memory. I have been in this city five weeks and I have read your book and I have come to the conclusion that I am not selling you a road and that nobody in this city is, and that the road may be real for about eleven minutes this morning and after that what people are going to buy is not a road."

"And what is it?" said a woman on the arch.

"**Being early, and knowing the terms before you stand in the rain.**" He held a sheet up. "That is all. That is the entire product. And here is the whole of it on page one, and page two is the toll, in the words the lot's own clerk reads, and page three is my fifteen-day labour term, which means my crew and I work for fifteen days and then this arrangement is finished and anybody who bought may sell it on if they can stand the paper.

"And I will say the thing that nobody in this city will say and that I have said in three yards this week and been quoted in none of them, because it is true and I am the only man here with a road-shaped offer and I am not going to be coy about it.

"**Your public road is going to fail. Probably not tomorrow. Probably in a fortnight, or a month, or when the token breaks. And on the morning it fails there are about four thousand households who have been told a date by a book, and a book does not open a door, and on that morning the only thing anybody in this city will be able to buy is speed.**

"And I sell speed. With the toll printed on page one. Fifteen days. And the counterfeit on the terraces does not print the toll and that is the whole of my argument and I have been making it since the day your bus came back without anybody in it, and I have never once lied to a person in this city and I am not starting this morning."

Nessa Pell came up the arch with a slate at about half past ten, because the ward market's reader reads whatever is put on the counter and a stack of printed sheets on a tram arch is a thing put in front of nine hundred people, and she read page one out loud in full, in a yard, on a rail, to about four hundred people who could hear it, and then read page two, which was the toll, in the words of the lot.

The tenth hand of the signal crew read page three and checked the figures on it against his page, and every figure on page three was a number anybody in this district had said out loud, and he said so, at the rail, to anybody who wanted to hear it.

And Adrian Vale came up the arch at about eleven and stood four feet from Halloway Reed and said the sentence, and it is the sentence the fifth of the five things is, and he said it to a man and not to a yard:

"**Mr. Reed. What you have on that table is not a right. You know it is not a right; it is printed that it is not a right on page two, in my words, and I have read the same words out loud in this yard four times this month.**

"**A crossing that is performed does not create a right anybody may sell, assign, inherit, or give away.** And I am going to say the rest of it out loud on this arch, in front of nine hundred people, because you have been honest and the honest deserve the whole sentence and not half of it.

"What you are selling is a queue. You are selling eleven coppers' worth of *earlier*, and earlier is real, and I cannot make it not real, and in about four months a man in this city is going to work out how much earlier is worth and he is not going to be you, and he will not print page two.

"And here is the thing I have got no answer to, and I am saying it in front of your crew and not behind your back, because you said it first and you said it in a yard.

"**You are right that the road will fail.** I have known it since the nineteenth of last month and I have had a reader's reading of a cracked plate to tell me so. And you are right that on the day it fails the only purchasable thing is speed. And I have not got an instrument for that, and this yard has not got one, and about four thousand households are going to find out on a morning that a book is not a door.

"So I am not going to tell this yard not to buy from you. I am going to tell them to read page two, and to remember that the man who sold them the queue is the same man who is right about everything, and that those two facts are not going to be separated for either of us as long as we are both in this city."

Halloway Reed looked at him for a moment, and then said, "That is the first thing anybody has said to me in five weeks that was not a threat and I am going to have it in my own book in my own words, and Mr. Vale, you are going to lose today and I am sorry about it in a way that is going to be extremely useful to me later."

**Eleven coppers a place. By the time the case was opened, forty-one places had been sold, and the sheet says the number, and the number is in this book, and nobody in this yard pretended they had not bought one.**

---

**And then the case was opened, and Adrian Vale closed a charter, which is a thing he is allowed to do, and it is worth saying what he is allowed to do on this particular morning, because a yard of nine hundred people believed he could do considerably more.**

"I am a Stage 1 reader," he said, at the crate, at about ten past eleven. "I can close one charter with one bearer, a short term and a visible toll. I cannot split a use among bearers. I cannot transfer one. I cannot renew one. I cannot post security, and if you see the word *security* used this morning as though it were a thing this charter had, it is a mistake.

"**This is one charter. One bearer. One activation. One term with an end written on it. And I am the only person in this city who is going to stand here and close it, and nobody else can, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I want that in the record as the reason and not as modesty.**"

Then he read it out, all of it, in the order of the schedule, and it took about twenty-two minutes, and about nine hundred people in a tram depot heard the whole of the instrument that governs the next eleven minutes of their district's life.

**LOT.** The Last Fare. Cracked through the plate. Four activations on the record. It may open once more and it may never open again and repeated use may strand the route permanently. Nobody in this district knows and the object has not said.

**USE.** One route across St. Orra for twelve minutes. The vehicle goes out from the near apron of this lot and comes down to the return point: the second rail east of the bell post, under the lamp, where the ballast drops and the plate is stamped, in the bus of Tarin Kest, coming down from the far bank. Out and down and home. Not a road and not a service and no automatic renewal.

**BEARER.** One. Tarin Kest, sole bearer of the outward leg and the return leg, and the person half of the named pair, and not rotatable, and not replaced on the day, on his refusal of the twenty-ninth of last month entered above and his agreement to teach four men entered below.

**CONDITIONS ON THE USE, WHICH ARE NOT BEARERS.** Renn Fisk, at the near abutment, holding the roll and the refusal window, and the last person on this bank before the case opens, and a responsible party for the roll. A far-side holder, at the far receiving ramp, on the far side, for the whole of the time the vehicle is on the far side, who decides that a person arriving is somebody's and not a stranger's, and who does not send anybody anywhere, and who holds a number and not a person. A far-side responsible party, named on a sealed page held by the registrar, which opens only if the remedy is called. And a remembering passenger, **Dessa Rill, of the hands of the Line Six crew, of whom this one is one, who is not a bearer and is not proposed to be a bearer, and whose function is to hold the place and not the reason.**

**BENEFICIARIES.** The clinic, eight hands, six of them carrying a cold box each, and two of them carrying nothing. The school shelter, fourteen off the roll. Twenty-four travellers. Two declines entered and neither struck.

**TOLL.** The memory of the purpose of the journey, charged to each traveller on each crossing. A returning traveller pays again. The ninth line of the return term is in the record in its author's words: a second payment is not a smaller thing and is not the same thing and is not stated by this charter to be either.

**TERM.** One activation, ending at the return point or at twelve minutes, whichever comes first. No extension. No automatic renewal. A new proposal and a new closing for anything further.

**RELEASE.** As read at the opening of the case, and to be read again at the closing by a different person.

**REMEDY.** The split-use remedy of the fourth, entered whole, with the words *known to fail* at the front of it, and a public appeal path that anybody may call, on a stated day, in this yard, before this board.

**AND THE EIGHTH LINE.** No goods, no freight, and no carriage of any kind except the person who is carried as a person — with the reading of the fifteenth entered under it, and the cost of the reading at the front of the reading.

---

**And then the remedy was called, and calling it opened a sealed page, and what was behind it was a man, and not a cause.**

Registrar Quill said so from the front, in about four minutes, in a yard of nine hundred, with the board seated and all four seats named aloud, and she read out the rule first, because she is a person who reads the rule out before she reads the thing.

"A sealed page is a sheet I hold. It names a person a charter has to identify and may not publish. **It opens only if the remedy is called.** It is not a private list. The person it names keeps the right to refuse care, to challenge the closing, and to say so on the record, and the bearer's duty does not change because of it, and it does not become a second registry that anybody can inspect.

"And there is one on this charter, and it has been on it since the twenty-ninth of last month, and I am opening it now because the remedy of the fourth of this month is being called, which somebody is entitled to do, and which anybody in this yard is entitled to do, and which I have been expecting since about a week ago.

"**What is behind it is a person, and a name, and I am not going to read the name out, and it is not going in this book, and it goes in the sheet and nowhere else.**"

The yard waited.

"He is on the far side of the river. He is at the far receiving ramp, at the foot of a goods shed, on a floor with eleven people on it. He was entered as a mark on the twenty-ninth of last month and nobody put him there. He was asked one question on the second of this month and he said he did not know what the job was. He was told what the job is, in words, at length, on the fourteenth, and he said yes, and the mark came off the entry.

"**And the condition on this charter is that the person named on that sheet is told and given a chance to refuse, and that condition has not been met, and it is not going to be met before ten o'clock this morning, because he is three hours down a stair and three hours back and I have not gone.**

"So the board is going to have to decide something in about four minutes, and I am going to tell the board what I think before the board decides it, because I am the registrar and it is my instrument, and it is on the record that I said it first.

"**I think we go ahead.** I have thought about it for two days. A person has said yes to a job he was told the meaning of, in words, at length, by the steward who carried the mark, and the answer is on a strip and the strip is in the book and the strip is not a name. And the condition exists so that a person can refuse, and the person has not refused and has not been asked in these last four days and does not know that there is a charter, and **I would rather open a road in front of nine hundred people with one person on the far side who has not been told in the last four days than not open one at all, and I know exactly what that sentence is, and it is the sentence a man wrote on the nineteenth of last month in a different order.**

"I have written *I do not know whether that is right* in my own hand at the top of the entry, and I have written that I am the registrar and I am not a vote, and the yard is to have both."

---

**And the board decided it in about ninety seconds, and this is what it entered, and the yard of nine hundred people heard the four seats named and then heard the finding, and the finding is the reason the day went the way it did.**

**THE BOARD PERFORMS THIS CLOSING WITH THE CONDITION ON THE SEALED PAGE UNMET. THE BOARD'S REASONS, IN ITS OWN WORDS, AND THE BOARD HAS NO POWER TO GRANT A USE AND IS NOT GRANTING ONE:**

**ONE. The board is not the body that decides whether a person on the far side of a river has been told. There is no such body in this district. There is a book and four seats and a road this morning, and the road is a road whether the four seats like it or not.**

**TWO. The condition cannot be met before ten o'clock and the board refuses to record a closing on the ground of a condition it has no power to enforce, in a district where the alternative to enforcing a condition is not having a road at all, and the board has watched this district refuse the safest answer available to it twice now and does not propose to make it three.**

**THREE. AND THIS IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS, AND IT IS ENTERED FIRST ON THE SIXTEENTH OF LAST MONTH BY A MAN WHO WAS NOT ASKED: THE CONDITION IS A CONDITION AND NOT A CONSENT. A SEALED PAGE IS NOT A YES. THE PERSON NAMED ON IT KEEPS THE RIGHT TO REFUSE, AND TO SAY SO ON THE RECORD, AND TO CHALLENGE THIS CLOSING, AND NONE OF THOSE RIGHTS HAS BEEN TOUCHED BY THE BOARD AND NONE OF THEM IS EXHAUSTED BY THE ROAD OPENING.**

**FOUR. AND THE BOARD RECORDS, IN THE REGISTRAR'S OWN HAND AND AT HER INSISTENCE, THAT SHE THOUGHT SHE WAS WRONG.**

"Seconded," said Sabin Tull.

"Seconded," said Marda Osk.

"I hold it," said Nessa Pell, "as the sitting steward, and I entered the sealed page's opening, and I am going to add one thing as a clerk and then I am going to do my job, and the thing is that Mr. Kest said this out loud on a line yesterday morning to three hundred people, and the board has read his words, and the board's fourth reason is his."

Mara Quill did not vote. She is the registrar and not a vote and not independent.

---

**And the two fresh witnesses were taken at about half past eleven, and they were not Adrian's, and Adrian stood at the rail with his hands in his pockets while two people who had never been asked to do anything in this yard were brought forward by the board.**

**The tenth hand of the signal crew**, who said, before he accepted anything, that he was the tenth and not one of the nine and that nobody was to put him in a count because he was reading the counts, and that he had found four wrong numbers in five weeks and that three of them were in wardens' documents, and that this morning's number of twenty-six was the first one in five weeks he could not put on his page and that he had said so out loud on a staging line at six in the morning and would say it again on this rail.

"I accept the role. I have read all of it and the toll was read to me in the words of the ninth line and I have heard the release condition and I have heard it twice. **I am the tenth hand and not one of the nine. My nine have been home over the footbridge since the eleventh and the twelfth. I am not travelling. And if that count of twenty-four is short of what the people on the ground say it is, I will stand at this rail and say so, and I will not be argued with.**"

**Ferrin Usk**, copper merchant, a stall in the ward market and eleven years of standing in it, and a man who voted against a rate on the thirteenth and read a held-asset entry aloud in a counting room and asked a question about a sentence that this city has no practice for, and who came to this yard that morning having bought no place from Halloway Reed, and said so.

"I have not bought a place from that man and I am going to be asked why and I am going to say why in front of nine hundred people, because if I say it at a table afterwards it is worth nothing.

"**I have been in this market thirty-one years and I have watched a queue eleven times and I have been in four of them.** I know what a queue is for. A queue is for a thing that is going to open twice and is worth being early for, and I do not believe for one minute that a book is going to be that thing, and that man on the arch knows it, and the reason he knows it is that he has been building a queue since the flood year and I have watched him do it.

"**I accept the role as a witness and not as a believer.** I have read every term and the toll was read out to me in the words of the ninth line and I heard the release condition. I am not travelling. I have not bought a place and I am not going to buy one off a man whose first page I have not read, and I have read his first page, because a reader at this market's counter read it out in full at half past ten, and that is the difference between the two of us and it is the only one there is at ten o'clock in the morning."

---

**Then Adrian Vale read the token, and it cost him an hour, and it was the third reading entered in this month, and Nessa Pell entered it and the cost and the limit without being asked.**

He touched it on the crate with two fingers. The sound went flat at the edge and his right hand stayed open and the cold came up out of his stomach the way it does.

```text
THE LAST FARE
ONE ROUTE. TWELVE MINUTES. NOT A ROAD.
TOLL PER PERSON PER LEG. A RETURNING PERSON PAYS AGAIN.
WHAT A SECOND ONE TAKES IS NOT STATED AND WAS NOT STATED YESTERDAY.
THIS OBJECT IS CRACKED THROUGH THE PLATE.
IT HAS OPENED FOUR TIMES. IT IS BEING ASKED FOR A FIFTH.
IF IT DOES NOT OPEN, THE TERMS OF THIS CHARTER ARE NOT PERFORMED AND NOTHING IS OWED TO ANYBODY BY THE OBJECT.

```

He sat down for about four minutes and then got up, and Nessa Pell read the reading out in full from the slate, and the tenth hand copied it onto his page, which is the first time in five weeks that anything anybody said out loud has gone onto that page without a column for it, and she entered the reading whole:

> **A READING, THE THIRD ENTERED IN THIS MONTH, ONE HOUR, THE OBJECT CONTACTED AND NOT THE RECORD. AND THE LIMIT ENTERED WITH IT: THE READER IS TO READ ONCE MORE TODAY, AT THE CLOSING, AND NOT AFTER IT, AND THE READING AT THE CLOSING IS ENTERED AS THE FOURTH AND IS ENTERED AS A PAIR WITH THIS ONE, BECAUSE THEY ARE THE SAME ACTIVATION AND A CLERK WHO COUNTS READINGS OUGHT TO BE ABLE TO COUNT THEM AS WHAT THEY ARE.**

**And then the token went into the vehicle.**

---

**The bus was in Tarin Kest's own yard, four streets west, and it had been there since the fifth of this month, and it is worth being plain about what it is, because it is the one fact in this morning that the district could not look at for a month.**

It came out of a service yard four streets west of the depot on its own, at a time entered in the book, with its doors open and nobody in the driver's seat, after eleven hours and forty minutes, and no person in Alder Reach has ever said what that is. It has a route-control slot above the old fare box. It starts. It is the only bus in this city that has been under the Fault that anybody knows of.

Tarin drove it onto the Lot 17 apron at about four o'clock in the afternoon and got it into line and would not get out of it, and he had had it in his own yard since the fifth and started it every morning and shut it down every night and had not told anybody except Nessa Pell, who had written *he starts it* in the margin of a page in April.

"This is the man who is going to be the wrong man for this district for the next month," he said, to about nine people who were standing close enough to hear the engine, "and I have been sitting in it for a fortnight and I have started it forty times and it has started forty times, and I am going to say one thing into a recorder so that it is in the book in my words and not in yours.

"**The bus came out of a yard on its own. I do not know why. I have not looked for a reason because looking for one is the job of a man with a lamp and a page and not of a man with a shoulder. I am not joining it to anything. I want it in the book in two lines, and the registrar has agreed to keep it in two lines, and the tenth hand asked for that and I am glad he asked.**"

And it was entered, in two lines, by Nessa Pell, in her fair hand, and the tenth hand read both of them out and then said the sentence that goes with them into the book:

> **THE VEHICLE IS THE BUS THAT STOOD IN A SERVICE YARD WEST OF THIS DEPOT AND CAME OUT OF THAT YARD ON ITS OWN WITH NO DRIVER, AT A TIME ENTERED, AND IT IS NAMED AS THE VEHICLE BECAUSE IT IS THE ONLY VEHICLE IN THIS DISTRICT WITH A ROUTE-CONTROL SLOT AND IT STARTS.**
>
> **AND THE REGISTRAR HAS BEEN ASKED BY THE TENTH HAND OF THE SIGNAL CREW TO KEEP THESE TWO FACTS IN TWO LINES AND HAS AGREED, AND THEY ARE IN TWO LINES, AND THE BOARD HAS NOT JOINED THEM, AND THIS LOT'S BOOK CONTAINS NO DOCUMENT IN WHICH THEY ARE JOINED, AND THE REASON IS THAT A READING THAT CANNOT BE ACCOUNTED FOR IS NOT TO BE CARRIED ON, WHETHER IT IS A FAULT ON A STRUCTURE OR A FAULT ON THE PAPER ABOUT THE STRUCTURE.**

---

**And at about twenty past four in the afternoon, in daylight, in front of about nine hundred people, Adrian Vale closed it.**

He read the toll one more time. He read the count. He read the term, and the end of the term, and the word *no extension*, twice.

"The witnesses have been told the whole of it and have accepted in this yard, in public, after hearing all of it, and they are not travelling on it, because the toll takes the memory the appeal asks about.

"**One charter. One bearer. One activation. One term with an end on it. The release condition has been read at the opening of the case and will be read at the closing by a different person, and both readings are in this book and neither of them is mine.**

"Twenty-four travelling. Twenty-six offered. Two declines in the book and neither of them struck.

"And the thing I said on the nineteenth of last month in a different order is the thing I am saying now in this order, and I am not going to be allowed to be pleased about it, and the yard is to notice that I am not pleased, and a clerk is to write down that I said so."

Then he put his hand on the open page of the book on the crate, and the writing came and it was short.

Nessa Pell entered it. And then, at about ten to five, Tarin Kest put a brass token that is cracked through the plate into the route-control slot above the old fare box, inside the vehicle, and closed the cover over it, and the yard made a noise, and about eleven people on the roof of the loading bay came down without being told to.

---

**And at about ten past five the route opened, and the far side was the far side, and it was the far side the charter said it would be.**

The bus went out from the near apron at five minutes past five with twenty-four people in it, and the yard watched it go out of the gate and up the marked spur and past the second rail, where it did not stop, because nobody past the second rail, ever, and a chalk mark on the second rail joint had been put there by a Warden administrator nine years before and had been argued with in public in front of six hundred people on the twelfth of this month and had not moved an inch.

The token went with it, into the Fault, inside the vehicle, seated in the route-control slot above the old fare box, and the cover was shut over it, and the yard had been told in the first four minutes of the day, in capitals, by a brass thing's own terms and then again by a brass thing's own answer, that **a returned object is not a returned vehicle and is not a rescue and is not evidence that anybody is alive.**

About nine hundred people stood in the rain in a tram depot and watched a bus go out of the gate, and then there was nothing to watch, and that was the rest of the day.

On the far bank, at the far receiving ramp, the eight clinic hands came off with six cold boxes, and the fourteen off the school roll came off, and the far-side holder — a man at the end of a goods-shed floor with a chain in his hand who had been asked a question on the fourteenth of this month and had said yes — stood where the charter said he would stand and did the job, which is that every person arriving is somebody's and not a stranger's.

He asked one thing and one thing only. And the number he wrote on the wall of a shed on the far side of a river, in chalk, with a stub of a pencil worn down to nothing, was **twenty-four**, and it was right, and he had never been asked for a number before in his life and he wrote it large.
