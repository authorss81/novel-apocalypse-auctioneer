# Chapter 40: The Book Opens Again

The read-back happened on the eleventh of the month, in a kitchen under the Ward Nine tram line, at about half past eight in the morning, before the table opened, with two people in the room who were not clerks.

That was a promise Mara Quill had made on the fifteenth in front of nine people, and she had managed it once in four weeks, and she was going to be asked about that, and she deserved to be.

She read the whole of the seventh out loud. The spur, the twine, the eleven seconds, the clause about the line, the twenty-three places, the reading that took an hour and ten minutes, the gate paper, the six chimneys, the foot of the crown, the lamp that was still burning, the ankle, the eleven minutes with a rope and a bicycle chain, the sluice that was half an hour late at the low end because a Warden of twenty-five had gone up a service cut he was not required to go up.

She read it for about fifty minutes, and at the end of the fourth place she stopped, and put her hand flat on the table, and did exactly what Lina Vale had told her to do in a kitchen on the eighth of this month with a strip in her hand.

"Stop," she said. "Registrar Quill has stopped. Lina, what are the two books?"

Lina Vale answered it in her own words, standing at the wall, with Bett Ruhl on the flour crates and a trader from the terraces who had never been in this kitchen before and who was there because the exchange was where the notice went up first, and Orren Pike on the step, and two more.

"Two books about six people," Lina said. "The first is Lot 17's, and it says four, and it says *household, four, giving and receiving, one member absent from the municipal record since the ninth year and by choice*, and that is a sentence that tells anybody who knows this district everything about a household, and it was written by me, and I wrote it in a hurry, and I did not ask.

"And the second is my own book under my third barrel, and it is a strip, and it says four, and it was written by a man I have never met with a chisel in his hand, and he was not writing a strip. **He was writing a job.** He wrote *held* on it and put a cross on it and nailed a copy to a gate, and that is not a strip, and it went into a book that a woman in a kitchen reads out loud, and neither of us knew what it was for about eleven days.

"Both books are wrong by two. Not differently wrong. **The same two.** Six people, and a man counted a household because asking six people how many of them there are takes an evening he did not have, and I counted in a hurry and did not ask, and neither of us did a crime, and one of us put a man in a hospital with a mark that was a man's refusal of a name.

"And here is the thing I said on the eighth and I am saying it again with Bett Ruhl in the room, because I have been waiting four days to see whether I would still want to say it: **both numbers go in the same book.** Not the correct one. Both. With the reason underneath. Because a book with one number in it and a kitchen with the other number in it is two authorities, and two authorities in this city means a fight in a kitchen in six months over which of us is lying, and neither of us will be lying, and that is the worst kind of fight there is."

Mara Quill wrote it down, and then she said the sentence that the whole of the day had been building towards, which she had said she would say on the twelfth and had brought forward by a day because she had read a book out loud in a kitchen and had found that it needed a day less than she had thought.

"Enter it as two numbers in one entry," she said, "and the entry is dated the seventh, and both of you are named as the writers, and I am the registrar and I am putting my own instrument between them, which is the only power I have and it is not a large one."

---

The hearing was called at nine on the twelfth, in the yard, before the board, all four seats named aloud.

It was the remedy of the fourth of this month. It was a hearing, and a pooled count, and a payment rule, and the registrar had entered on the eighth, in her own hand, in advance, that the expectation of the registrar was *called, and not discharged*, and the reason, and the cost, and the cost was entered as nine coppers with the lie in it in the reader's line.

The pool was published before the hearing and published again after it, and the two numbers were set beside each other, and this is what they were.

**Published before, at nine in the morning, from the counts as they stood:** shelter — **seventeen**, corrected, and the correction is entered with the old figure beside it and neither is struck, because the board cannot strike a line. Clinic, hands — seven. Signal, this bank — one. Exchange, household — four. Protected, by mark — six. **TOTAL: THIRTY-FIVE.**

**Published after, at about eleven, from the counts as two of them stand when anybody is asked to state them and the third book is opened in the room:** thirty-five. **And the difference entered as a finding about the counting: NONE. And the reason there is no difference is that in the four hours between the two publications, a strip-chain was matched out loud in a room with two people in it who are not clerks, and the matching found the two numbers that differ, and the two numbers were put in the same entry, and both of them are now the count.**

**THE TOTAL IS THIRTY-FIVE, AND THE ENTRY SAYS THIRTY-FIVE, AND THE THIRTY-FIVE IS MADE OF TWO DIFFERENT COUNTS OF THE SAME PEOPLE, AND EVERY PERSON IN THIS ENTRY CAN BE FOUND, AND THE ONLY REASON THEY WERE NOT FOUND BEFORE IS THAT TWO BOOKS EXISTED.**

Then the pool was not drawn on, and the reason it was not drawn on was entered: **no person came forward who could say what they lost, because no crossing has been performed under this remedy and there has been nothing to lose, and the remedy of the fourth of this month was called on the twelfth on a charter that has never opened, and the registrar states that she does not know whether that is a mercy or an absurdity and has entered both words.**

The remedy was then not discharged, and the reason was entered, and the reason was Rell Bode's argument made general, and it took the form of four lines that the yard had heard before and that had never once been in a public entry:

**A remedy that works by pointing at a person is not a remedy for a person who refused a name. This remedy works by counting, and a count is a person the record cannot point at, and the person who would absorb the loss of a split use is whoever is standing nearest the fault boundary, and the fault boundary is the top of the service cut, and the people who live four streets west of this depot are in no count in this book and cannot be got into one without the harm the count exists to prevent.**

**THE PROTECTED CATEGORY IS ENTERED AS THE REASON. NO HOUSEHOLD IS NAMED. THE REASON IS TRUE AND IT IS INCOMPLETE.**

---

Rell Bode asked for the floor and was given it, and she had come with a basket and no chair, and she said the thing she had come to say, and she was entitled to say it, and it is the only argument in this yard that Mara Quill is going to try to answer, and she is going to get half of it.

"You have read it out," Rell Bode said. "All of it, twice, in a yard, in the rain, and it is the best record anybody in this city has ever kept of anything going wrong, and I want to say the other thing, and I am the only person in this yard who is entitled to, because I asked for it on the fourteenth of last month and nobody has ever done anything about it.

"**A book that records its failures loudly is a book that will be used against the people in it.**"

She said it the way she said everything, to the room.

"That is not a complaint about your honesty. It is a complaint about what honesty does to a street. You have got a book in this city that says, in your own hands: a mark was used for a job; a household was counted by its steward; a man was put on a ballast by a clause a man wrote at two in the morning; a reader cannot post security.

"And every one of those is true, and every one of those is a sentence that somebody can pick up and use. **In six weeks there will be a page on a wall at a tea table with those six things on it, in the right order, with no names on it, and it will be true, and it will be for sale, and it will sell because it is the best paper in Alder Reach.** I have watched that happen to other men. You cannot publish a failure and then complain that the failure is popular, and if you build a book whose whole product is the truth, you have built a shop, and shops do not only sell what you would like sold.

"And I am not going to be the woman who stops it, because it is the only instrument this district has got. But I am going to say it, in this yard, in front of four hundred people, and I am going to say that from today this is a book that a man can be hurt with, and that you all knew it when you decided to build it, and that the price of it is not to be argued down in a kitchen afterwards."

Mara Quill stood up and answered her, and the yard heard a registrar concede a thing in public, which had happened twice before in this district and both times had been about a dead child.

"You are right," Mara said, "and I am not going to argue with you and I am not going to soften it, and I am going to add the half of it that is yours.

"**That is the price of having a book.** Before the nineteenth this city had no instrument at all, and the absence of an instrument is what a man on a terrace with a wax stick relies on, and everybody in this yard knows that and has known it for a month. So we have a book, and the book is going to be used against people, and it is going to be used against the people in it, which are the people who wrote it down honestly, which is a different set of people than it would have been if we had kept quiet, and that is a real cost and it is yours and I am entering it.

"**And I am also entering this, and it is the answer and it is not a good one.** A book that is used against the people in it is only a danger if the book is *selective*. If the six sentences that get put on a wall are six of the four hundred we have read out in a yard, then the wall is lying and anybody can prove it by coming here, and the cost of the book is a lie that has to be checked, which is a cost this city knows how to pay. If instead the book is thin, then the wall is telling the truth and there is nowhere to go and check it.

"So the book has to be fat. Not tidier, not better arranged, not kinder. **Fat.** Every refused draft, every number anybody set and anybody else checked, every hour Adrian Vale has spent reading and what it cost, every objection nobody answered. It is going to be unreadable, and it is going to be used against us, and it is going to be the only honest paper in this city, and those are the same three things and you cannot have two of them."

The trader from the terraces said, from the rail, "Then I want to read it."

"You are on the list," Mara said. "You have been on the list since the twenty-sixth. Bring the ninth thing, the second, the third, and the one about the yard on the twenty-first, and I will read them to you."

---

The rewrite took the rest of the day, and it was five sentences long, and every one of them had been fought over for a month by somebody.

Adrian Vale put it on the crate at about two in the afternoon and the yard argued it for two hours, and at about half past four, with the board seated and the fourth sentence still going at the rail, Adrian Vale asked for the floor.

**ONE. THE USE, TOLL, TERM, RELEASE CONDITION, AND APPEAL PATH ARE WRITTEN IN ONE PLACE, IN THIS ORDER, ON ONE SHEET, AND ANYBODY MAY HAVE THE SHEET READ ALOUD TO THEM, AND NO PART OF IT IS IN ANOTHER DOCUMENT.**

The seventh item of the old schedule had said the toll had to be in the words the travellers would hear. It had never said the *rest* had to be in one place. The reason it now did is that a clause Adrian had written at two in the morning in a back office had got into a public book and killed nobody, and the next one would have.

**TWO. THE RELEASE CONDITION IS READ OUT LOUD AT THE OPENING OF THE CASE AND NOT ONLY AT THE SIGNING, AND AT THE CLOSING, AND BY A DIFFERENT PERSON EACH TIME, AND BY A PERSON WHO CANNOT READ IF THE TRAVELLER CANNOT READ.**

**THREE. ANY AFFECTED PERSON MAY REFUSE IN PUBLIC, WITHOUT GIVING A REASON, IN THIS YARD, BY SAYING THE WORD, AND THE ONLY ANSWER ANYBODY OWES THEM IS THE WORD ACCEPTED AND THE REASON THEY GAVE, WHICH IS NONE, AND THE WINDOW CLOSES ONLY WHEN THE CASE IS PHYSICALLY OPENED AND NOT BEFORE.**

**FOUR. A CROSSING THAT IS PERFORMED DOES NOT CREATE A RIGHT THAT ANYBODY MAY SELL, ASSIGN, INHERIT, OR GIVE AWAY, AND NO CLAIM UPON A SUCCESSFUL CROSSING IS A THING A PERSON CAN HOLD, AND ANYBODY WHO OFFERS ONE TO ANYBODY ELSE IS OFFERING A THING THAT DOES NOT EXIST AND IS LIABLE TO BE TOLD SO IN THIS YARD.**

"That last one is Mr. Quill's," Adrian said, "and she wrote it in a kitchen on the eighth of this month at about eleven o'clock at night, and I asked to have it read in my own words as well, because I have been the man who thought a good crossing was a thing you could hold."

"And I want it read a fifth time," Sabin Tull said, "because I have been in this yard for eleven days, and the fourth one is the first clause anybody in this district has ever written that says the whole point of a public road is that it is not property, and it took a woman with a sick child and a basket and a public failure to say it, and the failure is what taught her the words, and that is a horrible thing and I am not going to pretend it is not."

**FIVE. THE BOOK RECORDS WHAT FAILED AS WELL AS WHAT WORKED, AND A FAILURE IS ENTERED AS A FAILURE OF THE INSTRUMENT AND NOT AS A FACT ABOUT A PERSON, AND NO ENTRY IS STRUCK, BECAUSE THE BOARD CANNOT STRIKE A LINE AND CAN ONLY RECORD WHAT HAPPENED.**

---

And at about half past four in the afternoon, in the middle of that, with the board seated and the fourth sentence still being argued over at the rail, Adrian Vale asked for the floor, and the yard gave it to him, and this is what he said and it is entered whole.

"I have got something to post and I am going to do it badly and in daylight, and the board is to stop me if I am doing it wrong, because you have four seats and two of you can tell me things I do not know.

"**I am a Stage 1 Reader. I cannot post security. I have said that in this yard five times since the twenty-ninth and I have said it in the same entry as a payment on the twenty-ninth so that a later clerk could not read a payment as a posted asset, and that rule is the reason I am standing here, because a thing I cannot do is now the thing I most need to have done.**

"So I am not posting security. What I am doing is putting four things into the custody of this lot, in this book, on a consequence I will write down and abide by, and I am going to say what makes it different from a payment, and I am going to say it before anybody asks, because last time I did it the other way round.

"**A payment is spent. A security is forfeited on a breach. This is neither, and I do not have a word for it, and the word is the honest part.** A payment on the twenty-ninth was a thing gone, offered to a claim nobody stated, and the difference between it and the claim is entered as uncomputed and not as nil, and that is the only reason the entry means anything.

"This is a thing **held**. Not gone. Held in the custody of the sitting steward, under a stated term, in the public book, with four figures entered beside mine, and with the consequence written out: **if a charter this board has recorded is breached, these are forfeited to the remedy and are not returned to me and cannot be given back to me by anybody, including me.**"

He put a sheet on the crate.

"**The lease on this building and this yard, two years and four months remaining.** On the twenty-ninth I entered that lease in a payment, and everybody in this yard thought I had given it away, and I did not, and the reason I did not is the whole of the last three weeks in one sentence: there is no body to sell it to. A payment is spent and the claim is not stated, so the lease is still in my name, and it is the only thing of any value that is still in my name, and I have been sitting on it for two weeks like a man sitting on a stone.

"**The right to the salvage in it.** A hundred and forty lots, re-priced on the twenty-seventh at four hundred and ninety coppers below the flood-year figures, and the standing stock now, and I have priced it and I have been told two other prices and I am going to read all three.

"**A named repair-crew term of two seasons.** The foreman, and one hand I will name here, and the third name is a mark, and the foreman has told me this morning that he will not put his brother's name in a public book, and he is right, and the mark stands, and the entry says why.

"**And the sign over the door, which I put in a payment on the twenty-ninth and refused to price, and which is this lot's and not mine to sell, and which I am not entering here either, because I have now said that twice in public and I meant it twice.**"

He read the three sets of figures, and Nessa Pell read them onto the slate in her fair hand, which is the hand every number in this book has been written in for eleven years.

"And the reason I am doing it, in daylight, in a yard, with four hundred people, and it is not heroism, and the yard is to hear that from me rather than work it out.

"**On the nineteenth I entered the security of a charter in a private undertaking with my stamp on it, and put one line in the public book saying the entry would follow the return term. The entry did not follow the return term. A child died.** And on the twenty-ninth I entered a payment on a claim nobody had stated to me, at a figure I set, with the difference between it and the claim entered as uncomputed and not as nil, because a nil would be a number I chose. Those are two different documents and I have spent a month being unable to tell them apart in my own head, and the only reason I can now is that one of them is in this book and the other one is not." And on the sixth of this month I wrote a clause at two in the morning that put a man on a ballast, and the ballast went a foot, and a man is in a room in the lower terrace with an ankle that will turn on wet ground for the rest of his life, and the whole of the reason that happened is that I am the only man in this city who has ever been asked to hold something for the public and has never once had anything anybody could take off him.

"And I want it in the entry in this form, because it is the form that is true: **Mr. Vale was told the rule, and has obeyed it, and has done the next thing instead, and the next thing is not a power and has no name in this book.** I am not posting it because I have become something else. **I am doing it because I have run out of ways to hurt people by accident and this is the last one that is mine to give away.** And the yard is to notice that the man who is hurt is not in this book by name, and that I cannot help him, and that the four things on that sheet will not help him either, and I am not going to stand here and pretend that what I am handing over is the price of his ankle. It is not. It is the price of a habit, and habits are cheaper than ankles, and anybody who tells you the two are the same has never had to look at a person."

Before the board was asked, it renewed the steward's term. The custody of the token ran from the fifth of this month for seven days and expired on the twelfth, and on the twelfth it was renewed, in public, on the sitting steward's own request, on the record, on the ground that a term that expires in a crisis is a term that can be ended by anybody who is uncomfortable. It is the fourth renewal since the twenty-first of last month, and Nessa Pell said out loud in the yard that she was going to keep asking for it, and that anybody who thought four renewals in three weeks was a sign of anything had not read the entry from the twenty-first.

Nessa Pell entered it, and then the board did the thing it does, and it did it in nine seconds, all four seats named aloud: Sabin Tull moved it, Marda Osk seconded it, Nessa Pell said she would hold it as the sitting steward whether it carried or not, and Mara Quill did not vote because the registrar is not a vote.

**THE ASSET ENTERED AS HELD, NOT AS A PAYMENT AND NOT AS A SECURITY, BECAUSE MR. VALE IS A READER AND CANNOT POST SECURITY AND SAYS SO IN THIS ENTRY: THE LEASE ON THE BUILDING AND THE YARD, TWO YEARS AND FOUR MONTHS, AT 640 ON MR. VALE'S OWN FIGURE, 610 ON THE WARD MARKET RATE SHEET, AND 700 IN THE YARD; THE STANDING SALVAGE, AT 380, 355, AND 410; A REPAIR-CREW TERM OF TWO SEASONS, TWO NAMES AND A MARK, AT 516, 516, AND 470; THE SIGN OVER THE DOOR EXCLUDED BY ITS OWN LOT'S HOLDERS TWICE, IN PUBLIC, IN WRITING.**

**TOTAL ON MR. VALE'S FIGURES: ONE THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIX. TOTAL ON THE WARD MARKET RATE SHEET: ONE THOUSAND FOUR HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-ONE. TOTAL IN THE YARD: ONE THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY.**

**HELD BY THE SITTING STEWARD UNDER A TERM OF SEVEN DAYS, RENEWABLE IN PUBLIC, FORFEIT TO THE REMEDY ON A BREACH OF ANY CHARTER THIS BOARD HAS RECORDED, AND NOT RETURNABLE TO MR. VALE BY ANYBODY.**

**AND ENTERED, BECAUSE IT IS TRUE AND BECAUSE NOBODY ELSE WILL ENTER IT: MR. VALE HAS NOT GAINED A STAGE, HAS NOT GAINED A POWER, CANNOT CLOSE A SPLIT, CANNOT POST SECURITY, AND CANNOT OPERATE THE LOT. WHAT HE HAS DONE IS DECIDED TO GIVE SOMETHING AWAY, WHICH IS A THING A READER CAN DO, AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME IN SEVENTEEN YEARS THAT HE HAS DONE IT IN DAYLIGHT.**

Mara Quill read the book out in one sitting, from the eighteenth of last month to the twelfth of this month, in the yard, at about half past five, with the board seated and all four seats named, and it took three hours and ten minutes and about six hundred people stayed.

She read the whole of the nineteenth. The sheet with the RETURN field in it, and the deferral line, and the six objections, and the four that were not answered, and the two refusals with their times — Tarin Kest at four o'clock on the afternoon of the eighteenth, Selik Marne at twenty past four the same afternoon, and the registrar declining to enter an interval, because the interval is a number a room picks and the refusals are a record two men made.

She read the bell post, and the clean arc of broken rust, and *I do not know* in Nell Sarr's hand.

She read the finding at the maintenance spur and the eleven hours and forty minutes and the four streets and the clause that saved eight and the child who did not wake, and the hour that nobody was keeping and that the registrar refused to invent, and the sealed sheet.

She read the four documents that changed, including her own, and she read the line about the registrar who cannot certify her own instrument in her own voice, which is the sentence that has made her the most trusted and the most doubted person in Alder Reach.

She read the twenty-ninth, with the six hundred and twenty-nine coppers and the one thousand one hundred and nineteen, and the difference entered as uncomputed and not as nil.

She read the twenty-two letters — eleven from the queue, eleven from outside it, four names in common — because those are the ninth of last month and they belong in the reading.

She read the twenty-ninth of this month, the proposal of the split, with Tarin Kest's refusal in the same line as his entry, and the mark.

She read the published terms of the sixth, all eight items, and the suspension in the first hour of the making.

And then she read the seventh, and the eighth, and the ninth, and the tenth, and the eleventh, and the twelfth, ending at about a quarter to nine at night, with about ninety people left in a tram depot in the rain.

And Adrian Vale put two fingers on the open page of the book on the crate, and the writing came, and it was short, and his hand did the thing it does, and the sound went flat at the edge, and Mara Quill made him sit down and did not take her hand off the page.

Nessa Pell entered it in the ledger without being asked, because she enters every reading and its cost: **a reading, the first entered in this month, twenty-two minutes; the reader is not to read again before the fifteenth; the object was not contacted and the record was, and the record is the open page in front of forty people.**

"That is correct," Mara said, and did not let go of the book, and did not say anything else about it for a long time, and then she said one thing, into a yard of ninety people, in the rain, in a voice that was not steady and which nobody in that yard has ever described since.

"**The object was not asked.** Four activations, and a witness pair on each of three of them, and a public book with every term in it, and a remedy with its failure written on the front, and an asset held by a steward for seven days, and not one single time, since the token came out of the inspection block, has anybody put the brass in front of anybody and said *do you agree to these terms*.

"And I have been the registrar of this lot for eight years and I have never once thought to write that clause, and it is the first clause in this book that I did not get from anybody, and it is the only one that everybody in this district can now state from memory."

---

The traders of Alder Reach read the entries of this month, all of them, in the weeks after, and about a third of them went to the terraces, and that is the number in the entry.

The book is in nine copies and on eleven boards and it is read out in kitchens, and a reader with a lamp comes to the ward market counter four days a week and reads out loud whatever anybody brings him, out of a rate of forty-one coppers a quarter, and the day-rate of the reader is entered at nine coppers with the lie in it in the reader's own line.

And it is the first thing in Alder Reach that a stranger could take cold, with no help from anybody, and argue two entirely separate things from.

The first: that a public route across St. Orra is possible, and that eight terms written by eight different frightened people in five weeks add up to a road, and that a man with a lamp can say out loud in a market what every person in this city has agreed to and has not agreed to, and that no man on any terrace in this city can take that away by writing nine flat lines.

The second: that the man who wrote the schedule put a person on a rail, and that the person is not in the book by name, and that a mark was a man's refusal of a name and was used three times for two jobs in six days by three people who each thought they were helping, and that the same man on the same day put a thousand five hundred and thirty-six coppers into the custody of a market and called it a habit and not a payment.

Both of those are true, and they are in the same book, and they are in the same hand about half the time, and there is no separate drawer for either of them.

Adrian Vale signed the entry as a person who had been told the rule and had obeyed it anyway, and gave the reason out loud in a yard of six hundred people, and it was not heroism, and the yard wrote it down as not heroism because Nessa Pell writes down what people are.

And Mara Quill and Adrian Vale did not say one word to each other about the fact that they were now further apart in law than they had ever been — she held a book he could not post into, and he had signed a thing the book had no name for — and closer in practice than they had ever been, in a yard at a quarter to nine at night in the rain, passing a slate backwards and forwards across a crate while ninety people listened to a book be read out loud from beginning to end.

And neither of them said anything about it, because there was a book open between them and ninety people in front of it, and the whole of the last four weeks had taught both of them the same sentence, and neither of them had to be told it.

*A thing that is in a book in front of strangers is not a thing two people have to be brave about.*
