# Chapter 49: The Damaged Token

Mara Quill had spent eight years building instruments and she had never once, in the whole of that time, tried to keep a physical object in a public room, and the reason she had not was that she had spent eight years building the other kind of instrument and had considered the first kind to be a shop.

On the twenty-first of this month she came into the yard of Lot 17 at about half past nine in the morning with a sheet of paper, and about four hundred people were in it, and about two hundred of them had come specifically because a notice had gone up on the eleven boards on the nineteenth saying that the damaged token would be open to look at on the twenty-first at a stated hour and that anybody might turn the case over and ask questions about it and that the questions would be answered by a clerk.

**And the notice had not said the custody had been changed. The notice had only said it would be open. The custody had lapsed on the nineteenth at midnight, had been renewed on the nineteenth at about half past three for seven days, and would expire again on the twenty-sixth, and nobody in this district has ever been told a term of that kind before and there is no form in any book in this city for a term that people can read off a board.**

---

**The first thing that happened, at about ten o'clock, was that about two hundred people looked at a cracked piece of brass for about an hour, and the second thing that happened, at about eleven, was that a man stood up in the middle of the yard and made the best objection anybody has made to anything in this book, and he was right, and he was right about the counterfeit's plate, and he was right about the drawer.**

Ossie Prent had trimmed wicks at the arcades for nineteen years and had trimmed them for the lamp in a fever ward and for the lamp in a warehouse on the terraces since the flood year, and he had been one of three men in this city carrying a printing plate, and he had never folded and had never said he would, and he stood up at eleven o'clock with a wick knife in his hand because he had come to trim the case lamp and had not put it away.

"I am the last man in this yard anybody wants to hear from and I am going to say it in about four minutes and then I will go back to my work, and Mr. Vale can throw me out of his own yard and I would think he was entitled to.

"**You have got a cracked piece of brass in a glass case in a public room and you are about to make it a public instrument, and I have spent a month selling people a piece of paper that says a road exists, and I want to say out loud in front of four hundred people that the reason my paper sells is not that anybody believes it. It is that it is printed on a sheet and a sheet can be put in a hand and a hand can go three hundred yards in an hour.**

"A case cannot. **A case is where a thing is.** That is the whole of a case. It does not travel, it does not get read out at a counter at four in the morning by a woman with a lamp, and it does not turn up in a market on a Saturday with a man standing next to it who wants you to have the better of the deal. And you have all worked that out and I have watched you work it out this month, and some of you have stopped buying, and about a third of you have not, and the third that have not are not fools.

"**And here is the part I have not said out loud until now, and I have been carrying it since the sixth.** I do not have a plate for a road. I have a plate for a claim. I print nine lines that look like a settlement and there is no fold in them and there is no way to check them against a book because there is no book, and that is the whole of my trade and it is not going to stop.

"And if you put that thing in a case and you open the case to the public and you say *look at the damage, look at the crack, look at what it cost* — **then in about nine days there will be a plate in this city that prints a crack.** I will not do it. I have got a bad thing to sell and I am not going to pretend I have not, and I would rather say it here than be asked about it in a yard in a month.

"Because the crack is the product. The crack is the part of your road that nobody can argue with, and it is a shape, and a shape is the easiest thing in this city to print."

---

**And Mara Quill agreed with all of it, in about four minutes, in front of about four hundred people, and gave the answer, and the answer was not clever and it was the only one available and it did not make anybody's objection go away.**

"You are right," she said. "About all of it. I have no instrument against a sheet in a hand and this district has not built one in five weeks and may never build one.

"**And you are also right about the crack.** A crack is a shape. And I have thought about that for two days and I have come to the conclusion that a crack which is *described* is not a shape, it is a description, and a description has to be true this morning.

"Which brings me to the thing I came out here to say, and I have written it down and I have read it to myself about nine times and it is four lines and I am the registrar and I am not a vote and I am not independent.

"**A damaged object in a public case is not evidence. It is a souvenir with a lamp on it.** And the reason I have come here this morning is that I was about to enter it as evidence, and evidence is a word that does a great deal of work in this book, and there is a rule about it that I have never had cause to write down and am writing now.

"**Evidence is a thing that is connected to a claim.** And I cannot connect it. I have a crack and two hundred and four grains and a chip out of the reverse and a reading from the sixteenth that says the object does not know whether it will open again, and **I have no claim to connect any of it to, because the claims of the nineteenth and the twenty-ninth were never stated to anybody, and the one child who died has no name in any public record and the one man with the turned ankle is in a protected household and the one person at the far receiving ramp has no name in any book including mine.**

"So it cannot be evidence and I will not enter it as evidence, because a book that puts a thing in a case and calls it evidence is a book that has found a use for a thing it does not understand, and I have done that once in eight years and it is in this book on the nineteenth of last month and it cost a child.

"**It is a lot.** It is Lot 17's holding, with a condition, and the condition is the whole of the entry, and anybody may come and read the condition and go and look at the thing and decide for themselves whether the condition is the true one."

---

**And Rell Bode asked for the floor and was given it, and she was at the back with her basket, and nobody fetched her a chair, and her objection on the eighteenth of this month was not about the remedy and had never been about the remedy, and she said so before she said anything else.**

"I have got the standing objection and I have had it since the fourteenth of last month and I am not going to pretend it is a new one, and I am not going to do the thing I did on the third of this month where I made a speech for a quarter of an hour.

"**A book that keeps a piece of evidence in a drawer is a book with a drawer, and the drawer is the thing this yard has been about for a month.**

"Not the evidence. The drawer. I have watched you build a book for a month and I have watched every single thing that has gone wrong in this district go into a drawer, and I have watched the last four weeks be about taking things out of drawers, and you have now put a thing into a drawer and made it a public drawer with a lamp on it, and I would like somebody to say to me why a public drawer is not a drawer.

"And I know the answer. The answer is that anybody can look in it, and that is a real answer and it is the best one anybody has given me in a month, and I am not going to pretend it is nothing, because a drawer in a private room in this city is a room with a man in it who says no, and I have been the no."

"What would you have," said Marda Osk.

"**A room.** Not a case. A room, and an entry in the book, and somebody who goes and looks and writes down what they saw and puts their name on it, and not on a Tuesday but at an hour when the person it belongs to could be looked at by whoever wanted to."

**And the board, which has four seats and no owner and can neither grant a use nor waive a toll nor audit a security nor strike a line, said that it could not make a room out of a tram depot, and could not employ anybody, and could not fund an inspection, and could do exactly two things, which were to set a term and to renew it, and to publish a result.**

And it published a result, on the twenty-first of this month, in the yard, all four seats named aloud, and this is what the result is, and it is the last instrument this district built this month, and the next one anybody builds will be the one somebody else builds:

> **THE HOLDER OF THE LOT. The Last Fare is entered as a lot of Lot 17 with a condition and not as a relic and not as evidence. Its custody is the custody of the public case and it is renewed in public on a short term by this board and this board has no other power over it.**
>
> **THERE IS NO EXPIRY IN THE ENTRY ITSELF. There is no sentence in it that says the custody is permanent, and no sentence in it that says the custody ends, and the reason for both of those absences is that a permanent custody is a permanent exception and this district has spent five weeks refusing permanent exceptions, and a custody with no end is not an entry, it is a hiding place with a form on it.**
>
> **THE CUSTODY RUNS ON A TERM OF SEVEN DAYS AND IS RENEWED IN PUBLIC, ON THE SITTING STEWARD'S OWN REQUEST AND ON THE RECORD, AND THE HOUR OF THE RENEWAL IS PUBLISHED AT THE SAME TIME AS THE TERM.**
>
> **INSPECTABLE, ON STATED DAYS, AT A STATED HOUR. The case is unlocked and anybody may turn the object over, hold it, and ask what they like, and the answers are given by a clerk of this lot, in the yard, and no question about the object is refused on the ground that it is anybody's business.**
>
> **A ROTATION. The steward's key does not stay. It is held for a stated term by a person named in this book, it is handed on in this book, and the handing-on is a thing in the book and not a thing that happens in a corridor. The board cannot create the rotation and has been asked for it by the sitting steward and cannot do it, and the minute says so, and the request stands.**
>
> **AND A READER. On the days the case is open, a reader of the ward market counter describes the object, in the yard, to anybody who turns up, in words the asker did not bring, and describes it again to anybody else who asks, and the ward market's reader is the person who does it, and the ward market's reader is paid out of the paper levy at forty-one coppers a quarter, and the ward market voted that rate on the thirteenth of this month and the rate is in force and the reading is in the reader's line and the lie in it is written in the reader's line too.**

---

**And then Mara Quill signed the custody page, and she said what she was signing it with before anybody asked her, and about four hundred people heard her say it, and not one of them looked at her hand.**

"I am going to sign this page," she said, "with my own hand, and I am going to say before I do what my right hand is in the middle of, because it is on the record and because Mrs. Bode has been right about this book for a month.

"On the night of the nineteenth of last month, four documents in this district changed without anybody's hand, and one of them was my own instrument. The Lot 17 notice board. A clinic receiving slip. Two pages of this book that now disagree with each other about a security. And my sealed-page register, in which exactly one cell has changed: the children's row, which was page forty-three, and now reads forty-four, and a forty-third has appeared dated the nineteenth in the lot's own category with no page behind it, so that my register asserts a sheet that has never existed.

"And what I said in public about it, on the twentieth, in a yard, is that **I cannot certify my own instrument** and that the change may have come from my page, and I refused to say which of the two readings I believed, and I stand by every word of that.

"So I am signing a page with a hand that is in the middle of something I cannot account for, and I have said so out loud, and I would like it in the entry, and I would like whoever reads this in four years to know that I knew when I did it and that I did not do it quietly."

Nessa Pell entered it. And about four hundred people stood in a yard and watched a woman sign a page, and not one of them looked at her hand, and the reason is not kindness and the reason is not tact, and the reason is that in a yard of four hundred people there are about eleven who have watched this woman stop a road and about eleven who have watched her argue with a cook, and those two elevens do not have anything to say to each other about a signature, and the rest of the yard had never thought about it at all.

**And that is the fifth time in five weeks that Mara Quill's instrument has been spoken about in public, and the first time it has not cost anybody anything, and nobody in that yard has worked out why, and it is in the book as a fact without a reason, and a fact without a reason is the only kind this book has ever been any good at.**

---

**And about half past two in the afternoon the case was put on the crate in the middle of the yard, and the object came out of it, and the condition was read out in the book's own words, and it is the whole of the entry and it took about nine minutes and it is in the book exactly as it was read.**

> **LOT: THE LAST FARE. A brass transit token, about the width of a thumb, from a stranded bus in the St. Orra Underpass, found under the inspection block of this lot, and this entry is its condition and the condition is the whole of the lot.**
>
> **CONDITION: CRACKED THROUGH THE PLATE. The crack runs from the lettered face, through the plate, and out at the rim on the side that faces away from the holder, and a chip of the plate about the width of a thumbnail is missing out of the reverse, and the two edges of the break will take a fingernail. It is not cracked in one place. It is cracked through, which is a different thing, and the book has used the word cracked for it since the ninth of last month and has been wrong about the word the whole time.**
>
> **WEIGHT: two hundred and eleven grains on the eighteenth of last month, before the fifth opening. Two hundred and four grains on the eighteenth of this month, after it. The difference of seven grains is entered as measured by the sitting steward with the ward market's own scales and the scales were checked twice, and nobody in this district can say what seven grains of brass is, because a crack does not weigh anything, and the number is entered because it is a number and not because it means anything.**
>
> **OPENINGS: FIVE, on the record. One bare activation in the flood year which failed and which nobody has ever described as a charter. Four closed openings — the ninth of last month, the sixteenth of last month, the nineteenth of last month, and the eighteenth of this month. Each of the four had two witnesses standing on this bank and each of the four was witnessed by people who did not travel on it.**
>
> **WHETHER IT WILL OPEN AGAIN IS NOT KNOWN. Repeated use may strand the route permanently. The object was asked a question on the sixteenth of this month, once, in a yard, before two fresh witnesses, with its own terms written in front of the question, and one of the lines of its answer was that it does not know whether the route will close at twelve minutes or before it. The object is not a party and was not one before that afternoon and has not been asked to agree to anything.**
>
> **AND THE CONDITION IS A FACT AND NOT A WARNING. A thing whose condition is stated in this book in this yard can be argued with by anybody who turns up, and can be measured, and can be compared with what somebody is telling them it is, and this is the whole of the reason it is entered as a lot and not as a relic: a relic is a thing you look at and a lot is a thing you check.**
>
> **AND IT IS IN THE CASE FOR ANYBODY TO LOOK AT IT, AND IT IS IN THE CASE BECAUSE A DISTRICT THAT HAS SOLD A USE OF IT OUGHT TO BE ABLE TO GO AND LOOK AT WHAT IT SOLD, ON ANY DAY, WITHOUT AN APPOINTMENT, AND PUT ITS OWN HAND ON IT.**

---

**And Rutt, who is the reader at the ward market counter four days a week and who was a signal clerk before her shoulder went, read the condition out loud in the yard to about four hundred people, and then did the thing nobody had asked her to do and that is now the standing practice of the ward market's counter.**

She did not read the entry. She **described the object**, in her own words, to the yard, and then she stopped and said, "I have just read that to you and I have been reading things to people for thirty-one years and that is the first time in thirty-one years I have read a description of a thing I was holding."

And she picked it up, which nobody in that yard had expected, and weighed it in her hand, and turned it over, and put her thumb in the break at the rim, which is the only way anybody has ever found to tell that the crack is a break and not a scratch.

"It is warm," she said. "It has been in a case in a locked office since Wednesday and it is warm, and I am not going to tell this yard what that means because I do not know, and I have been careful for thirty-one years and I am not going to stop being careful at four o'clock in the afternoon in front of four hundred people."

"And on any day anybody turns up at that counter," Rutt said, "and asks what the object is, I will describe it, again, in the words they did not bring. **And if two people are ever told two different things about it by two different people, they come to that counter, and I will weigh it in front of them, and that is the whole of the service and it is not much and it is not nothing and it is paid for at nine coppers a day with the lie in it in my own line.**"

**And the case was closed, and locked, and put back on the shelf behind the office window where about four hundred people could see it through the glass, which is where it has been since the ninth of last month, and the clerk went home at about five, and the yard emptied, and the board entered the custody in the book, published it in nine copies, and nailed a copy to the eleven boards, and the entry in the book ends with a sentence which is the last line of the twenty-first and is not the last line of anything:**

> **THE OBJECT IS NOT KEPT IN THE CASE. IT IS KEPT IN THE CASE BY THE STEWARD WHOSE TERM EXPIRES. IT IS OPENED ON STATED DAYS TO ANYBODY WHO COMES. AND ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF THIS MONTH THE TERM EXPIRES AGAIN, AND THE BOOK WILL CARRY THAT DATE, AND THE HOUR OF THE RENEWAL IS PUBLISHED, AND THE PERSON WHO HOLDS THE KEY ON THAT DAY WILL BE NAMED IN THIS BOOK BEFORE IT IS HELD AND NOT AFTER, OR IT IS NOT HELD.**
