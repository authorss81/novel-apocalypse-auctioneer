# Chapter 48: What Is Left of the Hearth Line

The St. Orra crossing was held at nine in the morning on the nineteenth of this month in the yard of Lot 17, before the board, all four seats named aloud, and it was a holding and not a hearing, and Tarin Kest asked for it and explained in about nine seconds why it was not a hearing.

"A hearing is when you want to be proved right about something," he said. "Yesterday the road opened and twenty-four people went out of it and seventeen of them are on the far bank and none of them can say why they went, and I have had a lot of the night to work out what I want to be proved right about and it is not anything. **I want somebody to tell me what the road is now.** That is not a hearing. That is a man asking a room."

About six hundred people came. They came in the rain, which had not stopped, and about forty of them were people who had not been in the yard yesterday at all and had heard about it from somebody who had, and about a hundred and forty were people who had been standing in it yesterday when the limit was called and a chandler's coal did not go.

---

**Three people in that yard, inside an hour, tried to treat a crossing that has been performed as a right somebody could hold, and the fifth of the five things that came out of this book in five weeks stopped all three inside four minutes each, and the fact that it worked on all three in four minutes is the strongest argument anybody has made for it.**

**The first was a trader** who had bought a place from Halloway Reed at eleven coppers on the previous afternoon and who stood up at about ten past ten with a slip in his hand and said that he had paid eleven coppers for a place on the next count and that he had been offered ninety coppers for it that morning by a man on the terraces, and that a place in a count was a thing a man could sell.

"Page two," said Marda Osk, from the rail, where she was reading a docket somebody had brought to her because she was the only person at the rail who had said yesterday that she was not going to be a counter and meant it. "**Mr. Reed printed on page two that it is a claim on the next closing and not a right, and that a claim on a closing that does not exist is not a thing anybody can hold.** You have a slip. It is a piece of paper with a price on it and a term on it and a sentence on it that says you have got nothing except an earlier place. **You are not wrong that the eleven coppers were spent. You are wrong that what you bought can be sold on, and the reason you are wrong is written on the sheet in the man who took your money's own hand, and that is the whole of the case for that man and there is not going to be another one in this city.**"

"Can I give it back?" the trader said.

"You can do whatever you like with it," Marda said. "It is not a thing. It is a piece of paper about being earlier."

**The second was a broker's clerk** from an office on the terraces, with a wax stick and not a die, who stood up at about half past ten and said that Lot 17 had now performed a crossing, and that a crossing was a service, and that a service was a season, and that his house would broker the St. Orra season on a commission and that the commission would go to the ward market's inspection fund, which was a good offer, and he wanted it entered that he had made it in public before anybody else did.

"The case was locked at about nine o'clock last night," Nessa Pell said, from the slate, "and the custody term of that object runs from the twelfth of this month for seven days, and it expires on the nineteenth, and a case with a term on it is not a season. **If it were a season there would be a word for it in this book and there is not one, and the reason there is not one is that a season has no day on it, and a thing with no day on it is the paper levy, and we have all just found out what that costs.**"

"And the road?"

"The road was one activation," Nessa said, "and it ended at the return point or at twelve minutes, and it ended, and the term is in the book, and anybody who wants another one has to come and argue for it in a yard like this one, and there is no man on any terrace in this city who can sell you a day that has not been voted on in public by about nine hundred people who can turn up."

**The third was a man who had been on the bus.**

He was about thirty-one and he had gone out yesterday as one of the eight clinic hands and had come back on the return party, and he stood up at about eleven in the morning, at the back, with his hands wet, and he had not brought anything out of the road and had not been asked to, and this is what he said:

"I went across and I came back. I have lost the reason I went, and I know what I lost and I know what it cost, and I sat on a platform for an hour last night trying to find it.

"**And I got to thinking last night that there is one thing in this city I have that I have never had before, and it is not a place and it is not a reason and it is not anything anybody would want. It is that I have been somewhere and come back, and everybody on that river knows it, and I sat there and I thought: put that in a book. Put in a book that I did it. Not my name. Just that it happened to me and I did it and it cost me what it cost.**

"And I know how that sounds at nine in the morning in a yard. I want to say the other half as well, because I have been in this book for a month and I know what it does: **the first half of that is a man asking for something to be written down about him, and the second half of that is a man who does not want to be a fool, and the second half is the reason and the first half is the sentence.**"

Nessa Pell read the fifth thing out. She reads every toll in this lot.

"**A crossing that is performed does not create a right that anybody may sell, assign, inherit, or give away. And no claim upon a successful crossing is a thing a person can hold, and anybody who offers one to anybody else is offering a thing that does not exist and is liable to be told so in this yard.**

"You did it. It is in the book. It is on the eighteenth of this month in nine copies and on eleven boards, and anybody can come and read that you went and that you came back and that it cost you the reason you went, and that entry is going to be there in four years and in forty and it does not belong to you and you cannot give it away and you cannot sell it and you cannot pass it to your daughter, and that is not a thing I am taking off you. **It was never yours. It is a record, and it is the only kind of thing in this city that is worth having, and the whole of the argument this yard has had for five weeks is that the difference between a road and a queue is exactly this sentence.**"

The man said, "That is not what I meant."

"No," said Nessa Pell. "And it is what you said, and I have been a clerk for eleven years and I have watched about four hundred people say a different sentence from the one they meant in this yard, and it is not the sentence that is the problem. It is that the other one has no form here."

---

**And then the thing that the chapter is about, which had been standing on a goods-shed floor on the far bank since about six o'clock the previous evening with a chalk number on a wall and nothing to do with it.**

**Seventeen people are on the far bank. Six clinic hands and eleven off the school roll. They have a stamped book — the roll, chalked, in a hand nobody on this side has seen — and they have a roof, and eleven people on that floor who are not strangers to any of them, and they have no way home.**

There is no crossing. The span is shut to all carriage and its certificate is suspended by two people who are not Selik Marne. The plank is at the bottom of the service cut. The token is in a case in a locked office four streets from this rail, it is cracked through the plate, the plate is parted at the rim, and nobody in this district knows whether it will open again and the object said yesterday that it does not know which.

And a rotation of three on foot had been in the proposal of the twenty-ninth since the twenty-ninth, and had been in nobody's mind since, and Fen Ambrose had come up a three-hour stair on the fourteenth with the arithmetic written on a tally sheet, and it was in the yard at about half past eleven in the morning on the nineteenth, and it is this:

> **A RETURNING PARTY, OR A PARTY CUT OFF, IS HELD AT THE FAR RECEIVING RAMP BY A NAMED HOLDER AND IS COUNTED, AND IS NOT SENT ANYWHERE. IF THE VEHICLE DOES NOT RETURN, THE HOLDER DOES NOT LEAVE THE FLOOR AND DOES NOT SEND ANYBODY, AND HOLDS THE COUNT WHERE IT IS, AND THE COUNT IS PUBLISHED IN THE MORNING WHATEVER THE COUNT IS.**

---

**And Tarin Kest took the floor on the return crew at about noon and the first thing he did was stop about four hundred people from being pleased with themselves.**

"There were numbers going round this district for a month that belonged to a footbridge," he said, "and the footbridge has had a plank in the service cut since the twelfth, and I want every number that anybody uses about this crossed off the slate before we use one.

"**Five on the deck is what a marshal can see with a lamp and a rope. A crossing takes about twenty-two minutes. There are four usable hours in a day.** That is the old arithmetic. It is good arithmetic. It is arithmetic about a hundred and forty feet of plank with a rail on one side and a marshal at the south end, and **we have not got that, we have not got the deck, and anybody who uses those three numbers on a stair is a person who has not noticed that this district has no footbridge.**

"So here is the arithmetic of what we have got. **The river stairs. One person at a time. About three hours down and three hours up, in daylight, because it is not a stair in the afternoon and it is not a stair when it is wet.** Seventeen people is not a day. Seventeen people is about two days and a half, and the last person on that stair at dusk on the second day is not accounted for until the morning of the third, and if the weather turns on the second afternoon then the last person is not accounted for until somebody has walked up it, and walking up it is three hours.

"So the crew of three is not a rescue. **A rescue cannot be planned for that arithmetic and anybody who tells you it can is selling you a timetable.** The crew of three is a *count*. One person goes down and stays down, and one person is the light at the top, and one person is the number that gets published in the morning, and the crew does not cross back and forth and does not run and does not go to look."

**And then he named the light, and it is the finding of the whole morning and it took about nine minutes to arrive at and about four hundred people argued about it for two hours afterwards.**

"There is no marshal," he said. "And I want to explain why not, because a marshal is the correct answer and I have spent nine years watching the correct answer and I am not having it quietly.

"**Mr. Marne's office keeps one marshal and one lamp at the south end of the footbridge in the passable hours, and enters no name in any book about it, on the stated ground that a crossing without a manifest is not a crossing. And that is right, and it is the single best piece of administration in this district, and I have wanted to copy it for nine years.**

"And we cannot have it here, and the reason is not money. **If a Warden's man stands at the top of a stair with a lamp, that is a manifest. That is a man with a coat and an office behind him standing at the top of a stair deciding how many people he can see, and this district has spent five weeks learning that a manifest is the thing that makes a queue, and a queue is where Selik Marne's whole argument came from, and Mr. Marne is standing in this yard and he knows it and he is not going to argue with me about it because he is right.**"

"He is right," said Selik Marne, from the back, and nobody had seen him come in, which is nine years of practice.

"So the light is held by a person who answers for a number and not for a place, and it is **Nessa Pell**, and she is a clerk, and she has said in public that she is a clerk who cannot check the thing she is holding and that the board may replace her and the remedy will not care, and she is the right person for this lamp for exactly that reason and not for any other reason at all."

And Nessa Pell said, from the slate, in the voice she uses for tolls: "I can see about nine feet up a stair and I cannot see the turn and I have said so. **The count is published in the morning whether it closes or not, and the reason it is published in the morning is that the only way anybody in this district can check a number at the top of a three-hour stair is to walk up the stair themselves, and that goes in the book as the standing failure of the arrangement and not as a piece of good practice.**"

---

**And then Joss Amery was paid, and it took four minutes, and it is the last unpaid debt in Lot 17's book and it was the second instrument of its kind ever made.**

He is a carter. He lost **three whole days** and a barrow-load of clinic stores in the early days of this month, and a coal ration board in the wrong quarter was altered, and he cannot be examined — not refused, not unwilling, *cannot*, because what happened to him is the thing that happened to him and no person alive can describe it and he does not go to a hearing about it, and the ward market's rate books and the clinic's own list both have a hole in them the size of three days.

He can say what he lost. That is the test in the payment rule, and it is the only test, and he passes it and it took him four minutes to say it and Nessa Pell wrote all of it down.

Then three figures went up on the crate, in columns, because that is the form and the form is the whole argument:

**On Mr. Amery's own figure: two hundred and sixty-eight coppers. On the ward market's rate sheet: two hundred and forty-one. In the yard: three hundred and ten.**

And under the three figures, the fourth line, in Nessa Pell's hand, and it is the line that has appeared four times in this book and has never once been a lie:

> **THE DAYS: THREE, AND A CARTER'S DAY IS ON THE RATE SHEET, AND THE THREE DAYS ARE COMMITTED AND NOT COMPUTED FROM ANYBODY'S ARITHMETIC BUT HIS OWN, WHICH IS THE ONLY ARITHMETIC THAT HAS ANYTHING TO DO WITH HIM.**
>
> **THE BARROW-LOAD: UNCOMPUTED, AND NOT NIL. The load was clinic stores. The claim was never stated to him by anybody and therefore the difference between the load and the claim cannot be computed, and a nil would be a number he had chosen, and he has had three numbers chosen for him this month and one of them was a coal board.**
>
> **THE ALTERED BOARD: ENTERED AS A FACT. NOBODY HAS ACCOUNTED FOR IT. IT IS NOT JOINED TO ANYTHING IN THIS BOOK AND THIS BOARD HAS ASKED FOR IT NOT TO BE JOINED TO ANYTHING, AND IT WAS ASKED BY A MAN WHO KEEPS A PAGE AND A MAN WHO KEEPS A BOOK AND THEY DID NOT ASK EACH OTHER.**
>
> **AND THE REASON THE PAYMENT CAN BE MADE AT ALL, WHICH IS THE ONLY REASON, AND IT IS THE WORST ONE: HE CAN BE FOUND. Every other person in this district whose loss cannot be pointed at is not a person the book can reach, and the whole of five weeks has been about that, and Mr. Amery is a man with a barrow, a room, and a name that is on this market's books, and he is findable, and findability is the only qualification he has, and it is entered that that is what it is.**

And Joss Amery took it, and counted it on the crate, and put it in his coat, and said the only thing he said in that yard, which was: "**Then somebody can find me, and I have been three weeks finding out whether anybody can.**"

---

**And Tarin Kest said the thing he said on a bench in a yard off the terraces in the last week of the month before last, to a man who had brought four sheets out of a door on purpose, and found that the timetable had changed shape, and said so out loud in front of six hundred people and did not know whether it was better.**

"A road that does not stop when the man stops is a queue with a timetable," he said, "and I have said that in this yard and I have meant it every time, and I am going to say it once more and then I am going to tell you what has happened to it since yesterday.

"**It is not me any more.** Yesterday I was the pair and I was the address and this morning I am the man who drove a bus down onto a stamped plate and got out and said a number out loud, and the number is in a book that four people signed and about nine hundred people heard read, and the man who ends it now is **a room with four seats in it**, and Nessa Pell is one of the four and she is a clerk and she cannot see past a turn in a stair.

"So the queue I warned this yard about is not gone. **It has got a committee.** And I have been thinking about that for about an hour and a half and here is where I have got to, in front of you, and somebody had better argue with it, because I do not think it is finished.

"**A timetable I keep is a man. A timetable four people keep in a room is a rule.** And a rule can be argued with by anybody who turns up, and a man can only be argued with by about four people at a time, and one of them is me and I am on the bus.

"**And I do not know whether that is better.** I have spent nine years in this district on the side of the man, and I have never once in nine years had to consider that the thing I wanted might be worse. And it is to be noted that it was built by a man who cannot close a split and posted an asset he could not post as a security, and neither of those facts is a criticism, and both of them are the reason."

---

**And at about ten past three in the afternoon somebody asked, in the yard, without any malice in it at all, which is the voice that always asks and the only voice that ever gets an answer:**

"Can it be sold?"

Nobody in that yard of six hundred knew what the man meant for about four seconds, and then about nine of them understood at once and went in nine different directions, and the question got built in about nine minutes and the nine minutes are in the book whole because Nessa Pell wrote all of it down and because somebody read it out at the counter on the twenty-first of this month in front of forty people, which is what happened to it.

**THE ANSWER, AS IT WAS CONSTRUCTED IN A YARD IN NINE MINUTES ON THE NINETEENTH OF THIS MONTH, AND IT IS THE LAST INSTRUMENT THIS DISTRICT BUILT IN THIS VOLUME, AND IT HAS NO NAME AND WAS BUILT BY SIX HUNDRED PEOPLE:**

**NO. The object cannot be sold. It is in public custody under a term and it has no owner and it has never had an owner and a term that expires is the whole of it, and it is not Mr. Vale's to sell, in the same way and on the same ground and by the same rule as the name over the door of this building, which he has twice refused to price because it is the lot's and not his.**

**YES. The use can be sold. In a yard, in a book, with a floor and a refusal window that closes when the case is physically opened and not before, and a toll read aloud twice in the words the travellers will hear, and a term with an end written on it, and two fresh witnesses who are not travelling on it, and a remedy that is known to fail, and the failure written on the front of it. That was done yesterday afternoon in this yard in front of nine hundred people, and it is the only sale of a road anybody in this city has ever had, and about forty-one people bought a queue from a man who printed the toll on the first page.**

**AND THE THREE THINGS, WHICH ARE THE WHOLE OF WHAT FIVE WEEKS COST: THE THING CAN BE USED AND CANNOT BE OWNED. THE USE CAN BE SOLD AND CANNOT BE KEPT. THE END CAN BE CHECKED AND CANNOT BE ARGUED WITH. Everything else that anybody offers you in this city about a road is one of those three with a lie stuck to it, and the lie is always about the length of it, and the length is the only part that can be checked.**

And the man who asked said, "That is nine minutes of my life I will not get back," and about four hundred people laughed, and it is the only time anybody has laughed in that yard in five weeks that anybody has written down.

---

**And then Nessa Pell put the slate down and said, "Stop. I have to tell you something and it is not interesting and it is the last item of the day."**

"The custody of that object runs from the twelfth of this month for seven days. It expires at midnight tonight. **It was renewed four times in public** — on the twenty-first, on the twenty-ninth, on the fifth and on the twelfth — and each time on my own request and on the record, and I have said in this yard twice that a term which expires in a crisis is a term that can be ended by anybody who is uncomfortable, and I have meant it both times, and it is also true that **every single one of those four renewals happened because I happened to think of it in a yard with about four hundred people in it.**

"The twenty-first's own term ran out on the twenty-ninth of last month and stood unrenewed for a month, and I said so in a counting room to a copper merchant and I said so in a yard, and the reason it stood was that nobody had thought about it.

"And this morning at about half past seven, when I unlocked that case to let a woman of the ward market look at it, because about four hundred people asked me to, **I had a key in my hand and no term under it.** The term expired at midnight. I have been the only person in this district holding a key on an expired term for about twelve hours, and so far as I know so has nobody else, because there is nobody else who holds one, and for about twelve hours the most dangerous object in the Morrow Basin has been in a locked case in a building with a yard full of people in it, and **the lock was mine and the authority was nobody's.**

"And I am not going to stand here and be dramatic about it," said Nessa Pell, "because a book that records its failures loudly is a book that can be used against the people in it and Mrs. Bode has been saying so for a month and she is right, and I would rather be the person who says it than the person who has it said for her in a yard by somebody else. **It lapsed. Same as the twenty-eighth of last month. And the board is to notice that the reason it lapsed is that I am a clerk, and a clerk's memory is not an instrument, and this district has spent five weeks building a paper with nine copies in it and it has not once built a reminder.**"

And the board, all four seats named aloud, in about four seconds, **renewed the custody term in public, on the sitting steward's own request, on the record, for seven days**, and Nessa Pell entered it, and then she said the sentence that is the last line of the nineteenth and the first line of everything after it:

"And I am going to ask the board for one thing, and it is not in my power to ask and I am asking anyway.

"**I want a person to be able to hold this key who is not me, and to hold it for a stated term, and to have the handing-on be a thing in this book rather than a thing that happens in a corridor.** Because I have been the only key in this lot for five weeks and the reason a paper levy collecting four coppers for eleven years produced no paper is a thing exactly the same as this, and it is a thing that happens in every room in this city, and it is not anybody's villainy, and I have looked."

**The board has no power to do that. It can set and renew the short public custody term and it can do nothing else, and it said so in about nine seconds, and the minute says so, and that is where the nineteenth of this month ends, at about half past three in the afternoon, in the rain, with about four hundred people in a yard and a token in a case and a clerk who has just asked a room for a thing four seats cannot give her.**
