# Chapter 35: Splitting the Road

Adrian Vale had discovered, in the back office of Lot 17 on the twenty-eighth, with the door shut and a lamp on and a slate on his knee, that he did not know how to write down four things at once.

He had written a charter in June for a cooper with a bent axle, and a schedule in August for a chandler's yard, and a valuation in the flood year for a man who wanted to be told his building was worth more than it was. He wrote the way other people breathe: he did not have to decide to do it and he could not remember deciding to stop.

What he could not do, on the twenty-eighth, was write a use with more than one person carrying it.

He tried it four times.

The first attempt was a page with four columns and a heading for each and it looked, when he stood back from it, like a rate sheet at the ward market, which is the exact shape of the thing he was frightened of and could not stop making.

The second attempt he wrote as a sentence per bearer, one after the other, and by the third bearer the sentence had stopped being about the third bearer and had become a copy of the second with a name changed, and he threw it in the stove, and the reason he threw it in the stove was not that it was bad. It was that it was good. It was four good sentences in a row and it did not mean anything, the way four copies of a good receipt do not mean anything.

The third attempt he wrote as a rotation with days in it, because a rotation is a schedule and a schedule is the one instrument he has never been afraid of, and he got two thirds of the way through the days before he understood that he was about to say that Tarin Kest was not going to be in the bus on the third day of a rotation, and that a man who is not going to be in the bus on the third day is a man who can be replaced, and that a return point which can be replaced is not a return point.

The fourth attempt he did not throw away and it is the one that went in the book, and it is worth setting out in his own words because the whole of the next two days came out of it.

---

A return point is a **named pair**: a place, and an approach, and the person able to hold them.

The place is the second rail joint east of the bell post, under the lamp, where the ballast drops and the plate is stamped. The approach is coming down from the far bank. And the person is **the bus of Tarin Kest**.

Adrian had written that sentence in June, on a bench, in a yard, with six hundred people not yet knowing anything, and it was the best sentence he had ever written, and on the twenty-eighth he discovered that it was a sentence about one man's feet.

He wrote it out in the four attempts. In each of them, somewhere, the phrase *in the bus of Tarin Kest* had to appear, because it was the person half of the pair, and he could not take it out and could not put anything else in it, because there is no other person in Alder Reach who has been up out of that service cut with a bus in his hands.

And a charter cannot be a sentence about four men's feet at once.

He tried to solve it at about four in the afternoon on the twenty-eighth and he solved it in the wrong way, deliberately, in front of three people, because he wanted the room to see the shape of the problem before he put a wrong answer on a public page, and because he had learned in three weeks that a man who shows a room his own bad reasoning gets fewer corrections than a man who shows it a good one.

Mara Quill was there, and Nessa Pell, and Orren Pike, who was not invited and came.

"Here is the problem in one sentence," Adrian said. "A named pair has a person in it. I want four bearers. Four bearers means four people, and only one of them can be the person in the pair, and if I name the other three as bearers then two-thirds of the schedule is a person doing a job that the pair says only one man can do.

"So here is the wrong answer, and I am going to say it out loud so that somebody stops me. **The person half of the pair becomes a place to be filled.** The pair reads: the second rail joint east of the bell post, under the lamp, where the ballast drops and the plate is stamped, coming down from the far bank, *in a bus of the Hearth Line, named on the day, the name entered before the case opens.*"

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"That is half a pair," Mara said.

"Yes."

"You have named a place and an approach and a **slot**." She was not angry. That was the thing that made it expensive. "Adrian, I have read the word *named* in that sentence four times and it means something different in the last one. A person who can be named on the day is a person who can be *not* named on the day. It is the only sentence in this book that has a blank in it where the whole weight is."

"I know."

"Then why did you write it."

"Because the alternative is a road with one man's name in it until he cannot drive, and I have watched a district spend three weeks refusing to pay for the inspection because it had no end, and I am not going to hand this room a charter that ends when Tarin Kest's shoulder goes." He put the pencil down. "I would rather write a bad answer that everybody can see the badness of than a good answer that hides it, and I would rather be corrected than be right, and I am aware that the second half of that sentence is not something I have ever said before and I have said it this week for the first time."

"I know you have," Mara said.

And Orren Pike, from the door, where he had been for a quarter of an hour without anybody inviting him in:

"Mr. Vale, both of you are standing in a room doing the thing, and you are both very good at it, and I would like to point out one fact and then go away. The term in the book is *a named pair*, and the reason the term exists at all is a bell post that was in a socket four yards past the joint. Somebody moved an arm, and the arm was the person half for about twenty minutes, and nine people went into a service cut. A pair with a slot in it is the arm again. It is not a smaller version of the failure. It is the failure with a piece of paper round it."

---

That evening was at the Road House bench, which is a bench in a yard off the terraces with a lamp on a post and no roof, and which is not a place of business in any sense that anybody can write down, and where Tarin Kest had been drinking small beer for six weeks and repairing the same lock twice a day.

Adrian came out with four sheets, which is a mistake he made on purpose, because a man with one sheet is a man selling something.

"Four bearers," he said. "Outward, near abutment, far ramp, and a return crew on foot. Rotation between them on a schedule I have not written yet. I want to know whether you will be one."

"No," Tarin said, before Adrian had finished the sentence, and then he did not stop, which was the thing Adrian had been counting on, because Tarin Kest has never in his life refused a thing and walked off from it.

"Do you want to know why first?"

"I want to tell you why, and then I want to tell you that I am going to be in the book about this whether you like it or not, and I have told Nessa and she has a slate."

"Say it."

"The pair says *in the bus of Tarin Kest*." Tarin turned the bottle round on the bench. "That is not a courtesy. That is the load-bearing wall. You put a man's name in a pair because you are saying: there is one man in this city who can hold this place and this approach, and if the far side is not the road you left on, there is one man who will know what to do about it. And what he will do about it is stop a bus and walk four streets, and I have done it, and it cost eleven hours and a child.

"Now you want to make it one of four. And I understand why, and I have understood it since about the second minute, and I am not going to pretend to you that I do not, because you have come out here with four sheets and you are not selling. The reason is that you have found a man with a bad shoulder and a district with a memory, and you are trying to build a road that does not stop when I stop.

"And a road that does not stop when the man stops is not a road. It is a **queue** with a timetable." He said it flatly. "That is what the eleven in Selik's queue were. That is what a manifest is. You take the one man who cannot be replaced and you turn him into a place in a rota, and then the day comes when the rota has a man in it who has never been up out of that cut in his life, and he will not know what to do, and he will not know that he does not know, because there is a lamp and a board and a name entered before the case opens."

"Somebody has to be second," Adrian said.

"Somebody has to be second. And second is not the same as equal, and I have not got a word for what second is, and I have been thinking about it for three weeks, and here is what I have got." He turned and looked at him. "You can have four names in a room and only one pair. You can have four men who can *drive*, and that is a thing I can teach in a fortnight and I would like to teach it. And you can have a pair that is a man, and that man does not rotate out, and if the day comes that he cannot do it then the road does not open that day, and nobody has to be told a slot was filled.

"That is not a schedule. I am aware it is not a schedule. It is the only version of this I have heard of that does not put a bell post in a socket."

Adrian said, "There is a boy in this district who thinks this is a thing that happens to him, and there is a page in the book with his refusal on it and the reason, and I want to say one thing about that and then I am going to drop it: you are not the only person in that book. I have been trying to get that through a public schedule for three weeks and I have not done it once, and this evening is the first time in a month that I have not said it, and I noticed."

"Noticed what."

"Noticed that I did not need to." Adrian's hand was doing the thing it did at the edge, and there was nothing behind the feeling and no reading to account for it. "That is the whole of what happened at the school shelter a month ago and I have spent three weeks turning it into a document and the document is not the thing."

Tarin Kest did not say anything to that, which was how Adrian knew it had landed.

"And do not put my refusal in as a reason for the road not existing," Tarin said, to Nessa Pell, who was writing on the bench end with a slate on her knees in a yard in the dark. "Put in what I said about the queue. And put in that I will teach four men to drive in a fortnight and that I will be one of the four and that I will be the pair, and those three things are both true and I would like them in the same entry, because if a clerk reads only the first he has got me refusing and if a clerk reads only the third he has got me being a name on a rota."

---

Adrian read his own draft on the twenty-ninth, in daylight, in the yard, with about two hundred people at the rail, and the reading cost him an hour, and Nessa Pell entered it: a reading, the fifth entered in this month, and the last she expected to enter this month.

He did not touch the brass. The token was in its case in the office behind him under a seven-day custody term that had lapsed on the twenty-eighth and had stood unrenewed for a month because nobody had thought to renew it, and which Nessa Pell renewed that morning in eleven seconds, on her own request, on the record, with the reason that a term that expires in a crisis is a term that can be ended by anybody who is uncomfortable.

He put two fingers on his own draft on the crate, and the writing came, and it was short.

```text
FOUR BEARERS, ONE LOT.
USE: STATED. TOLL: STATED.
STANDING: STATED FOR ONE OF THEM.
THE OTHER THREE ARE NOT A READING. THEY ARE A LIST.
```

"That is all of it," he said. "And I have been a reader for seventeen years and I have never once been told by the thing I read that I had asked it the wrong question, and I have been told this afternoon, by a piece of paper I wrote, and I would like the yard to understand that it is not a failure of the reading. It is the reading working. I asked it about four people. It can hold one. That is the whole of what a Stage 1 is."

Nobody in the yard knew what a Stage 1 was, and about forty of them had heard him use the phrase, and he had never once explained it to a yard, and the not-explaining had been convenient for a month.

---

He entered it anyway, and the entering is the whole of the twenty-ninth.

He entered it as a **proposal** and not as a charter, in the form the book had used since the seventeenth, with all seven of the schedule's items, and with the one rule he got right stated in the place where it belonged, which is item six, and which is this: *where the toll takes the memory of a purpose, a witness does not travel on the lot the witness witnesses, because the remedy rests on appeal and the toll would take the memory the appeal asks about; the required pair therefore stays on the side that keeps its memory, and the names of the people who will answer on the far bank go in this record and not in the bus.*

Then four entries under the head **BEARER**, in this order.

**ONE. THE OUTWARD BEARER. TARIN KEST, of the Needle Road House, sole bearer of the outward leg, and the person half of the named pair, and not rotatable, and not replaced on the day, on his own refusal entered above and his agreement to teach entered below.**

That was the wrong thing Adrian did in public, and he did it with his eyes open, and it is the sentence that has to be said about him: **a man said no in a room and the man with the slate wrote his name in the box anyway, because the box needed four names and there were three.**

He did not pretend otherwise. He said so, standing at the crate, in daylight, in front of two hundred people, and Nessa Pell entered it in the same line: **entered over a refusal given in the same room; the refusal is part of this entry; the reason for the entry is that the schedule requires four bearers and there are three.**

**TWO. THE NEAR ABUTMENT RESPONSIBLE PARTY, holding the roll for the whole crossing and the refusal window, and the last person on this bank before the case opens. RENN FISK, medicine crew, four tolls paid, of whom one was on a bare activation, one was a choice he made himself, and two were somebody else's decision that he was inside of.**

**THREE. THE FAR RECEIVING RAMP HOLDER, on the far side, at the ramp, for the whole of the time the vehicle is on the far side, and the person who decides that a person arriving is somebody's and not a stranger's. Entered by mark and not by name, the household having refused names and the steward named in the public book as the holder of the count. A MARK, a strip reference, and a count of one.**

**FOUR. THE REMEMBERING PASSENGER. DESSA RILL, medicine crew, Line Six, five tolls, the last of them on the nineteenth, who is not a bearer and is not proposed to be a bearer and whose function is to hold the destination.**

And then Adrian did the second wrong thing, and it was the worse one, and he did it because he was in the middle of a sentence that made sense and a man in a yard does not stop in the middle of a sentence that makes sense.

He wrote, under item four, a fifth line.

**AND THE BEARERS OF THE RETURNING PARTY, being a crew of three on foot from the far receiving ramp to the near apron, drawn by lot from the trades and the shelter and the clinic, who do not travel on the lot they witness and are not to be counted among them, rotated in a schedule to be entered before the case opens.**

Which is a fifth bearer and not a fourth, and which is a rotation, and which was in the room in about eleven seconds.

"Mr. Vale," Mara Quill said, from the back, in a voice that carried because the gym was full and the yard was not and she had learned to project into a yard, "that is five."

"It is five."

"You have written a schedule that names five bearers and it is called a proposal for four, and item four says a person who is not a bearer, which means you have three, and the three are a man who will not be replaced, a man who has been the responsible party for boxes and not for people, and a mark."

"Yes."

"And the fifth is a schedule."

"Yes."

"Then a stranger could take this document cold, in a market, with no help from anybody, and count the bearers, and get a different number than you did, and there is no way on this earth to tell which of us is right, and that is the ninth thing that was not in the charter of the nineteenth." She was not being cruel. She had never once in eight years been cruel in a room, and that had always been her worst quality as an auditor, and it is why she was standing up in a yard on the twenty-ninth arguing with a man about a clause instead of sitting in a chair with a red pencil. "The ninth thing that was not in it was *a statement of what happens if the marking at the return is not where the charter says it is*, and this document has a slot where a man goes."

"It has a slot where a man goes," Adrian said, "and I wrote it in front of you, and you have told me it is half a pair, and I am leaving it in, and I am going to say why in the yard, because if I take it out now then the document is one man and I will have done this week for nothing.

"**I am leaving a slot in the pair because I do not have the power to fill it and because I have written a document I am not allowed to close.** I am a Stage 1 Reader. I can draft what a split would look like. I cannot make it, I cannot bind it, I cannot close it, and I cannot post security, and I have said all four of those things in this yard over the last month and I am saying the fifth one now, which is that there is a clause in this book that says a charter may be amended only by a new closing, and there is another clause that says a person who materially refuses cannot be counted as a willing bearer, and I have written a proposal that four people can be entered into, and the only way anybody closes it is if somebody with the power to close it stands in a room and reads those two clauses out loud to the people in it.

"There are two people in this city who can close a split. One of them is Warden Marne, and he has spent nine days making sure that nobody gives him a road, and he is right, and I would not take it from him. The other one is in this yard, and her name is on the board, and she is not a vote."

Nessa Pell said, in the voice she used for tolls: "The book will take a proposal from anybody. It will not take a closing from a reader. Both of those are in the book and one of them has been in the book since the fourteenth and nobody has ever made me say them together."

**PROPOSAL ENTERED**, Nessa Pell said. **THE THIRD ENTRY IS A MARK AND NOT A NAME AND THE FIFTH ENTRY IS A SCHEDULE AND NOT A BEARER, AND NEITHER HAS BEEN ASKED FOR BY THE PERSON IT WILL FALL ON. PUBLISHED IN THE GATE YARD AND ON THE BOARD AT THE WARD MARKET COUNTER AND IN NINE COPIES CARRIED TO THE TRADING PLACES.**

Which was Adrian's decision, and it was the first decision he had made in a month that was not about him, and it took nine copies and two barrows and about four hours of Marda Osk's afternoon.

And it is the reason the fourth entry of that schedule is still in the book in the form it is in, with a mark in it and nobody's name on it. **A mark is not a person. I knew that when I wrote it and I wrote it anyway, because the box wanted four bearers and there were three, and a schedule that says the terms are complete is a thing I have been arguing against in other people's documents for a month.** There is a fourth entry in that book with a hole in it, and the hole is going to be standing in a yard in eight days' time for twenty minutes saying nothing at all, because that is what a hole does. I am not going to be told about it by anybody.
