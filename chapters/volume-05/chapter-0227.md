# Chapter 227: The Second No

---

The twenty-eighth of April was the last full day before the last day of the month, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning.

The three figures went out loud before the yard was full, and the man of fifty-six read them back off the board and got them right, and the last day of this month was two days off and there were nineteen people in that yard and about nine of them had come to hear a man say no.

"**A hundred and twenty-five days on the board and four hundred and forty-one days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and fifty-five, and the last day of this month is two days off, and a yard is going to be asked this morning about a chair, and it is going to be asked by a man who was not going to be asked in a yard on the nineteenth of this month.**"

It is on the page that a clerk does not put a number on the order and that she was not asked to, and that the count of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is eight and did not move at about ten in the morning on the twenty-eighth of the fourth month.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the question himself, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against answered it in about four seconds and gave the reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and sixty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**No. I said in this yard on the nineteenth of this month that I would not say no in a yard about a thing nobody had asked me properly, and a body that wants a man out of this district has to come and put it to me in a room. It came to a room on the twenty-fourth and I said no in it, and I am saying no again here, and the two are not the same no. The one in the room was that nobody could ask a single holder a question. This one is that a yard is not a body and nineteen people standing in the cold are not a council, and if I took that chair off that table in front of all of you it would be because you were here and not because a man said no to a piece of paper, and I will not be a founder because a yard is full.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who refused in a yard on the nineteenth on the ground that he had not been asked properly and refused in a yard today on the ground that a yard is not a body has spent nine days learning the difference between the two, and that a room is what made the yard possible, **and that a yard which never sends anybody to a room only ever gets the answers it deserves, and that a yard that does send somebody to a room gets a different man back in the afternoon.**

It was entered that the two refusals are two sentences about two different men, and that the first was about a holder that nobody could ask a question and the second was about a room full of people who are not a council, **and that a man who had given the same reason twice would have given it once, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and that the eight did not move.**

The chair by the trestle table was moved about four feet by somebody at about half past ten and was not moved back, and nobody sat in it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a chair was moved four feet and that a chair moved four feet is still a chair and is still not a founder's chair.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half of it, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got ninety-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A Registry's page with a man's name in a column on it and a body's sheet with a chair on it are two records about two things four hundred miles apart, and one of them is a man who carried a ledger and the other one is a man who would sit in a chair, and two records that are not about the same thing cannot agree and cannot disagree, and I am refusing that join a fourth time in this district and it does not make a fourteenth of anything.**"

It was entered that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve, and that neither of them moved at about half past eleven in the morning on the twenty-eighth of the fourth month, **and that a second refusal of the same join is the same refusal, and that a fourth is the same refusal with a bigger number of people watching it happen.**

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against wrote it himself at about one in the afternoon at a table in the back room of a building at the end of the lane, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing sat opposite him and did not help, and a man of fifty-six sat at the end with the board figures on a slate and did not read them out again.

There is a pen and there is ink and the pen had to be warmed in his hand for about a quarter of an hour before it wrote anything, and he wrote four sentences and then stopped and then wrote a line at the bottom, and he signed it.

The ink went thin twice and he had to wait for it and the second time he wrote the last word of the third sentence on a second line, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, who was sitting opposite him and could see the page, said afterwards that a man writing a letter to a body that is not a place had to lean twice to finish a sentence and that he was not going to write that down anywhere.

The chair that has stood at the end of that trestle table since the twenty-second of this month is not in that room and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said so before anybody asked, and said that the chair in the room where the books are is a different chair and has a loose arm on it, and that he knew that because he had been on the step outside it until nine o'clock on the night of the twenty-fifth and had looked through the fanlight at about nine feet of lane and a bucket that was not his.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who says he will not be thanked in a yard and then signs a letter with his own name at the bottom of it is a man doing the same thing twice and not the same thing, and that a name at the bottom of a letter is not a name in a column and is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not on the standing orders of the nine and is not on a Registry's page and did not go on one this week.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in the yard the whole time and was not in the room and was not sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was in the yard and that nobody asked him to go and that he was not asked a first thing about the letter.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said what was going on it, and he said it to the yard and not to anybody, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-nine.

"**A body that writes to a village asks for a man by the last day of this month. This district is not sending one and is not saying it cannot, it is saying that it has nobody to send who has been asked, and a letter that asks for a man is not answered by a page and is not refused by a page. That is the whole of it and I will not put a chair in it.**"

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man who has written four sentences for a letter and will not put a chair in them has decided what the letter is, and that the letter is a refusal with a reason, **and that a refusal with a reason is the only kind this district has ever produced, and that the count of the eight is not going to move on it and should not.**

A man of fifty-six said that there is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and that there never has been one, **and that a charter with a term that has no end on it is not a rate and is not the fifth of the five things this district does not have and is not going to become one, and that a letter with a day on it at the bottom of it is the opposite of a condition with no end on it and the four are four.**

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man the figure of twenty-one years is against one more thing in front of about nineteen people, and he said no in about four seconds and gave no reason, and a man of about nineteen counted the reason and it was none, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk does not put a number on the absence of a reason and that no ninth refusal with no reason a clerk of a house has given was entered today, because a man the figure of twenty-one years is against is not a clerk of a house and has never been one, and that the pool is eight and did not move and the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and did not move.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing then said, out loud and in front of about nineteen people, that he was not going to ask a man who had just been asked a question he had not been asked before about a second thing on the same afternoon, and that a yard that spends a man on a second question in one afternoon gets the second answer in four seconds and not the first one at all.

The woman of fifty-eight was in that yard at about two in the afternoon and she had carried her eleven up the bank and came down at about half past one and would not go up again that day. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in his own words and not in the six things, whether she wanted to hear what the letter says, and she said no in about four seconds and gave the reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-nine, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**It is a letter about a chair and I have carried water up a hill for nine years and I am not a housekeeper of a water. Write it and send it and do not read it to me.**"

That went in: she refused with a reason, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the eight and that the eight did not move, and that she was not asked a first thing about the letter and that nobody was going to ask her, **and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one this afternoon and is not going to.**

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was not in that yard on this afternoon and her four houses still stood and had not been taken up and were not added to eleven and were not going to be, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that they were four and that the fifth of the five is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and did not move at about four in the afternoon on the twenty-eighth of the fourth month.

The day came down and the sheet went to the man the figure of twenty-one years is against and not to a clerk, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it went to a man and not to a clerk, and that a letter a man writes himself is a document somebody owns, and that the three documents nobody owns in this district are three and did not move.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH A MAN REFUSED A CHAIR IN THE SIX THINGS IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE AND SAID THAT THE TWO REFUSALS WERE NOT THE SAME NO, AND A LETTER WENT TO A MAN TO WRITE HIMSELF AND HE SIGNED IT, AND A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT REFUSED IN FOUR SECONDS TO HEAR IT READ OUT.**
