# Chapter 239: Nine Feet Of Lane

---

The eleventh of the fifth month was bright and the channel was down about a foot from where it had stood on the eighth, and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the three figures out at the gate before the yard was full and the man of fifty-six read them back off the board behind him and got the other two right, and about nine people came up the lane in ones and twos behind them.

"**A hundred and thirty-eight days on the board and four hundred and fifty-four days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and sixty-eight, and the twelfth of this month is one day off, and the first day of the next month is twenty-one days off, and about nine of us are going to go and ask somebody something today.**"

Nobody in that yard had said where. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got ninety-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale stood in that yard yesterday and said the first thing anybody has done in four months that was any use, which is that nobody has asked anybody in four empty houses a single question. I am not going to start with four houses. I am going to start at the ninth of the eleven and I am going to ask one man one question in his own doorway and I am going to write down what he says and I am not going to argue with it.**"

The woman of fifty-eight was in that yard and he asked her, before he went, in his own words and not in the six things, whether he could ask a man at the ninth of her eleven houses a question about a toll. She said yes in about four seconds and gave a reason, and the count came to seventy-two.

"**Go and ask him. I have been carrying water to that house for nine years and I have never once asked him whether he would rather somebody else carried it, and you are going to ask him something smaller than that and you are going to ask it in his own doorway, and if you ask him it in a yard I will take the bucket off you in front of everybody.**"

The clerk entered that a person who has been asked and has said yes is not a person who has been taken, and that a woman who gives a reason for saying yes has not consented to anything except the question in the doorway, and that the record about the eleven houses says nothing was changed and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and did not move.

The lane to the ninth of the eleven houses runs up behind the bank and is about two hundred yards, and there is a step at the ninth door of two stones high that has not been repaired since before the woman of fifty-eight started carrying water there, and the door is a plank door with a hasp on it and not a handle, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing lifted the hasp and did not knock.

The man at the ninth house is about forty-four and he was sitting by his own door with a mended sleeve in his hand and no coat on, and he is not a clerk of a house and does not keep a tally and has never been asked a first question in this district in nine years, and a clerk of nineteen years entered all three of those things before anybody opened their mouth in that doorway.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him his question in his own words, in the doorway, and it was counted, and it came to forty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Would your house pay a toll on a thing that holds back the water, if a body four hundred miles away sent you a sheet that says one of four places owes it. I am not asking you to pay anything. I am asking you whether you would.**"

"**No. And I am not going to tell you what I think about the four places, either, and I do not know which of the four we are, and I have not got a copy of it and I am not going to get one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a man who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move at about one in the afternoon on the eleventh of the fifth month, and that the two sentences said in that doorway were the first two questions anybody had put to that man in nine years and that a man who is asked a first question in nine years has not refused anything before it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a tally for six households was not in that yard and was not sent for and was not asked a first thing, and the clerk entered that, and entered that the three households not asked again are three and that the six stand at one signed, two refused and three not asked again and have stood there since the twenty-first of last month, which is twenty days, and that a man of not asking is not a man who has been refused and is not a count of anything.

He came back at about half past one and said what the man at the ninth house said after that, in the yard, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted, and it came to eighty-seven.

"**He said that nobody has ever asked him a question about anything, and that he has heard about the sheet from his own wife, and that a toll is not a thing he has and it is not a thing his house has and he is not going to be told by a body four hundred miles away that he is the fourth of anything, and then he said that if anybody came from this yard and asked him a second thing he would answer that too.**"

The clerk entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a man who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move at about half past one in the afternoon on the eleventh of the fifth month, and that the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and did not move and that the two are not the same figure and are not added together, and that the man of about thirty at a counter was not in this district on this day and was not sent for and has not been given a day by anybody here at any time.

The woman of fifty-eight was in that yard when he came back and had heard none of it, and the clerk entered that she was there and that nobody told her and that nobody was asked to tell her, and that a man who is asked a question in a doorway and then says it again in a yard has said it twice and that a name said twice is a name a counter can ask for, and that a thing said twice is a thing and not a name, and that she said nothing about either.

A man of fifty-six said that a man who has been asked one question in nine years and has answered it and has said he would answer a second is the only instrument this basin has that the four districts have not built yet, and that a yard which starts asking one question in one doorway is not a yard that is going to ask nine questions in nine doorways this week, and that a man who said that in a yard is not a man of thirty-four who mends fencing and has not been asked to be.

---

The lane between the yard of Lot Seventeen and the records office is about four hundred yards and it has a bank on the east side of it, and the sun comes over that bank at about half past four in the afternoon and not before.

She came out of the office door at about twenty to five with her coat on and no book under her arm, and he was standing about nine feet from that door with his hands in his pockets, and the man of fifty-six was not there and nobody sent him and the record says not asked.

He asked her one question, and he had asked her one question like it before, in a room, on the twenty-fourth of last month, and she had said no in four seconds and neither of them had referred to it since.

"**On the twenty-fourth of last month, in that room, I asked you whether you would come and stand in a channel and look at one of them, and you said no, and I said nothing at all afterwards, and I have said nothing at all for about a fortnight, and I want to say the nothing out loud so that it is not sitting in me any more.**"

She did not answer that for about nine seconds, and the fanlight over the door did not light anything, as it has never once lit anything.

"**No. You did say nothing, and you did it on purpose, and it was the correct thing to do and I would not have forgiven you for not doing it.**"

"**Then I am going to ask the other half of it, which I have not asked.**"

"**Which half.**"

"**Whether you will come on the twelfth and look at it without anybody asking you a question about it. Not because the lock needs a witness. Because you have been four hundred yards from a piece of iron for nine years, and you told about nineteen people three days ago that you wrote the rule that made this district findable, and I would like to see what you do when you are standing next to it instead of four hundred yards away from it.**"

She said it in four seconds and it was counted by nobody because there was nobody there, and what she said was that she would come at about half past ten on the twelfth if the gate would open, and that a yard is a place a person walks into and not a place a person is put, and that if he tells anybody at that yard that she said she would come, she will not come at all.

The clerk entered nothing about it on the eleventh, because nobody was in a room and nobody was asked, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was told about it on the twelfth by one of the two of them and entered that he was told by one and not by the other, **and that a room with a shut door in it is a place where a sentence goes to die and not a book, and that about nine feet of lane is not a room with a shut door in it and is not a yard either, and that a sentence said in about nine feet of lane in the last half hour of a day is not in any of the three documents nobody owns and is not a fourth of them, and that the three did not move at about half past four in the afternoon on the eleventh of the fifth month.**

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was in that yard until about four and did not go out past the loams and was not asked to go out past the loams, and the clerk entered that he was asked about nothing else this morning either, and that a man who has asked one question in one doorway in a month and is not asked a second question himself is a man who has found out the order of the two things.

The day came down about half past five and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam was not in the yard and had not been in it since about eleven and the record about the afternoon says not asked.

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH A MAN WALKED TWO HUNDRED YARDS AND ASKED ONE MAN ONE QUESTION IN HIS OWN DOORWAY ABOUT A TOLL HE HAD NEVER BEEN ASKED, AND AT ABOUT TWENTY TO FIVE A MAN AND A REGISTRAR STOOD ABOUT NINE FEET APART IN FOUR HUNDRED YARDS OF LANE AND HE ASKED HER ONE QUESTION AND SAID THE WHOLE OF WHAT HE HAD NOT BEEN SAYING FOR A FORTNIGHT OUT LOUD.**
