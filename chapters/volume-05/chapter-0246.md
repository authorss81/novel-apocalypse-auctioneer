# Chapter 246: The Third Column

---

The eighteenth of the fifth month was warm and low and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came in through the gate at about a quarter to ten with his coat over his arm and a knapsack he did not have on the fourteenth.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and forty-five days on the board and four hundred and sixty-one days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and seventy-five, and a hundred and thirty-six days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the next month is fourteen days off, and there is a sheet on this table that is not any of the nine and is bigger than any of the nine.**"

A man of about nineteen counted that and got nothing, and the clerk entered that the man of about nineteen had been asked to count the whole of the figures line and had counted only the part of it that was about the nine local charters, and that the rest of it was about a day and a fortnight and was not a figure about anything.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said what he had done, before anybody asked him, in the plain voice, and the count came to a hundred and twenty-five.

"**I have been to the second of the two. I went on the sixteenth and I was two days on the road both ways and I did not take the sheet. The man at that place is about sixty and he keeps a house with about five people in it and the sheet came to his door on the third of this month and he read it out to his own household, which nobody in four places has done, and he said he would not answer it and that he would put his own name under a line of his own if there was one, and then he said he would not do that either until he had been asked what the line was for.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has not been asked what a line is for has not refused a line, that the record about him says not asked, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a man who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the eighteenth of the fifth month.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked for the sheet on the table to be read, and a clerk of nineteen years read it out in one run of words and read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **THIS IS A COVENANT AND NOT A CONSTITUTION, AND IT SAYS SO AT THE TOP OF ITS OWN FACE.**
>
> **NINE STRUCTURES STAND IN THE WATER ABOVE FOUR DISTRICTS. THEY ARE NINE AND NOT TEN. THEY ARE FINITE. NO PERSON AND NO BODY IS THE HOLDER OF ANY OF THEM, AND NO PERSON SHALL HOLD THIS ALONE.**
>
> **THE STEWARDS ARE FOUR. THEY ARE CHANGED EVERY SEASON BY A SHOW OF HANDS IN THE PLACES THEY COME FROM. A STEWARD CARRIES THE FIGURES FOR ONE SEASON, IS NOT A HOLDER, IS NOT A BEARER OF THE WHOLE, AND MAY BE ASKED A QUESTION BY ANYBODY AT ANY TIME INSIDE THAT SEASON AND BY NOBODY AT ALL OUTSIDE IT.**
>
> **THE TOLLS ARE PUBLIC. EVERY FIGURE IS WRITTEN WHERE A STRANGER MAY WALK UP AND READ IT. NO TOLL IS FASTENED TO A PARTY OF A PLACE AND ONLY TO A NAMED PERSON, AND A TOLL THAT CANNOT BE FASTENED TO A NAMED PERSON IS NOT COLLECTED AND IS NOT REFUSED, AND THE RECORD SAYS NOT ASKED.**
>
> **THE TERM IS THREE SEASONS AND BEGINS ON THE FIRST DAY OF THE NEXT MONTH. ANY ONE OF THE NINE LOCAL CHARTERS MAY GIVE ITSELF UP ON THE FIRST DAY OF ANY SEASON AFTER THE FIRST, AND A GIVING UP DOES NOT GIVE BACK A SEASON AND IS NOT A REFUSAL.**
>
> **IF A PLACE DISAGREES, THE DISAGREEMENT GOES BY ITS OWN ROAD TO ANOTHER REGION AND IS PUT IN A BOOK THERE THAT A STRANGER MAY WALK UP TO. THE GOING IS CARRIED BY A PERSON AND NOT BY THE TOLL, AND THE COST OF THE GOING IS NOT PRICED HERE.**
>
> **THE TABLE HAS THREE COLUMNS. THE FIRST IS HEADED BY WHAT IS HELD. THE SECOND IS HEADED BY WHO HOLDS IT. THE THIRD IS HEADED BY A WORD AND NOT BY A NAME, AND THE THIRD IS EMPTY ON THE FACE OF THIS SHEET, AND IT IS EMPTY BECAUSE THE PEOPLE WHO WOULD GO IN IT HAVE NOT BEEN ASKED, AND A PERSON WHO HAS NOT BEEN ASKED HAS NOT REFUSED AND CANNOT BE COUNTED EITHER WAY. NOBODY IS TO FILL IT WHO HAS NOT BEEN ASKED. NOBODY IS TO GO TO A HOUSE THAT LET A PAGE BE COPIED AND ASK IT ABOUT A PAGE IT WAS ALLOWED TO COPY, IN ORDER TO FILL IT.**

A clerk of nineteen years wrote the three columns out on the top of the trestle table in chalk on a slate she had brought from her own office and had not been asked to bring, and the third of them was about the width of a finger and a half and about the height of two fingers and it was ruled and it was empty, and she entered that a place is a figure about a page and is not a figure about a person and is not a finding and that she was not going to make anything of it, and that the three documents this district does not own are three and did not move.

The registrar of this district's records office then said one thing about where the sheet was going to live, and she said it to the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and not to the yard, and then she said it to the yard, and a man of about nineteen counted what she said twice and got a hundred and forty-nine, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**That sheet is going into the book in my room and nowhere else, and a stranger may walk up to that door and ask to read it and will be allowed to read it, and that is the whole of what I have agreed to and I have not agreed to anything else. My rule says a person may look at a document in that office and may not carry it out. That rule was written on the twenty-first of last month and it is the only reason a notice four hundred miles away could be written, and this morning it is also the reason that a covenant can be in a book a stranger can walk up to. None of the nine local charters goes in there. Those are in nine rooms and one of the nine is in this one and I am not carrying it anywhere.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who has put a covenant in a book and has not carried it out of her room has not become a bearer of it and is not a holder of the water and is not a party of anything, and that the record about the book says not asked, and that a notice which takes effect on the first of the next month has not changed a word of her rule and is not going to.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the thing about the four stewards, and the count came to a hundred and thirty-four.

"**Four stewards for nine things and for four districts, and every one of them changed by a show of hands in the place they come from, and a steward can be asked a question by anybody for one season and by nobody at all for the rest of their life. I have been in this yard every morning for a month saying that a single holder cannot be asked a question by anybody, and I have been wrong about how to write that down, because you do not write it down by not having a holder. You write it down by having four of them and a door. I am not going to be a steward and nobody has asked me and I am saying so before somebody decides I am the obvious one.**"

A clerk entered that a man who says out loud that he is not going to be a thing has not refused the thing and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about it says not asked, and that a steward is a person and not a party of a place and is not a figure about anybody.

Then a man of fifty-six said the half of it that was about the rule on the third line, and nobody had asked him, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and forty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Nine people wrote that sentence four hundred years ago and they wrote it to stop a man exactly like the one who has been standing at the back of a book in this district for two months, and one of the nine sat down afterwards and wrote under it that a man thanked for it has been made something else and underlined it twice. I am not going to say that this sheet is that page, because a sheet in a yard is not a page in a cave and the two have never been in a room together. I am going to say that the sentence on the third line is on the sheet, and that nobody in this yard wrote it, and that a man who says out loud that a sentence is not his is doing the thing this yard was built to let him do.**"

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing asked, in front of about nineteen people, whether he might take it to the four counters in this district and read it out at each of them, and was told that he could, and he said he would want eleven coppers a week for it, and the man of fifty-six refused him and gave the reason, which was that the district is still not going to pay him and has now said so six times, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and that the eight did not move.

The man of about fifty-five who keeps a building four miles down a lane was in that yard for about eleven minutes at about half past two and said no before anybody asked him, and gave a reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and twenty-nine.

"**I have a tank over its lip with two lines of chalk on the inside of the brick and a crack that has gone the length of my hand since the twenty-fifth of last month, and the second room has nine years of beds in it and the water has not come over the step since I moved them, and that is because I am in that building every hour of every day and I have not asked anybody for a mend in four months. A steward has to be a person other people can go and ask a question, and for one season, every day, and I cannot leave a tank for a season. That is the whole of my reason and I would like it read back.**"

A clerk of nineteen years read it back to him in her own words and he said she had got it, and she entered that a man who asks for what he said to be read back is not a man who has consented to anything, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that the eight did not move at about half past two in the afternoon on the eighteenth of the fifth month, and that the protected things in this district are five and no sixth was added to them and a tank with a crack in it is not a sixth of anything.

He went at about one with the copy under his arm and came back at about half past four and said that he had read it out at three of the four counters and that at the fourth one the man behind the counter had asked him to come back in a week because a man from that counter had gone up the road that climbs and had not come back, and a clerk entered that the fourth counter is a man of about nineteen and that he was not well enough to be asked to hold a sheet and was not asked to hold a sheet, and that the record about the fourth counter says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the notice out one more time at about half past four for about nine people who had come in after eleven, and got nothing wrong, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the notice has now been read out in that yard six times and that the first of those six was thirteen days ago, and that six readings is not six consents and is not a column, and that a notice read out six times in a yard a stranger can walk into is a notice this district has made its own without anybody deciding to.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about five with two buckets and put them down where she puts them, and nobody counted them, and a clerk entered that nobody counted them, and that a woman who carries water to eleven houses and holds no figure on any of them is a person and is not a party of the first column or the second or the third.

The day came down about half past six and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there twenty-five days, and the slate with the three columns on it was leaning against the leg of the table where the notice had been, and the notice was under the leg.

**AND ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH THE FIRST REGIONAL PUBLIC COVENANT IN THIS BASIN WAS READ OUT IN A YARD AND READ BACK AND COPIED BY HAND AND CARRIED TO THREE OF THE FOUR COUNTERS IN THIS DISTRICT, AND IT WAS ENTERED IN A BOOK A STRANGER MAY WALK UP TO WITH THE THIRD OF ITS THREE COLUMNS HEADED BY A WORD AND NOT BY A NAME AND RULED AND EMPTY ON ITS FACE, AND NOBODY ASKED A HOUSE ABOUT A PAGE IT HAD BEEN ALLOWED TO COPY IN ORDER TO FILL IT.**
