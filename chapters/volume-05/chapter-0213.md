# Chapter 213: The Standing Orders

---

The fourteenth of April was a day with nothing on anybody's page until about half past three in the afternoon, and about fourteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the figures out loud and a man of fifty-six read the board out afterwards and got both of them right.

"**A hundred and eleven days on the board and four hundred and twenty-seven days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and forty-one, and the board has not been washed since the twenty-fourth of December and nobody has said out loud that it ought to be.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fourteenth of April would have been the thirteenth day of a man with a portfolio, counting the day he came in, and that he is not in this district and that nobody in this yard knows where he is, **and that a day a man is not here is not a day he has been here and that the district's own rule about a man who is in a place and not in a book has not been broken by his not being in the place and has not been kept by his being in it on the eleventh.**

Then the dray came down.

It came down the only road in this district that climbs at about half past three in the afternoon with one man on the box, and the yoke was lashed to the tailboard with about nine feet of line, and about nine people were in the lane at the bottom of it and had not been sent for and were not asked anything.

The man with the portfolio was not on it and the case was not on it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the dray came down with one man on the box and that the second man who went up on it on the eleventh of this month was not on it, and that a man who goes up a road and does not come down it in a cart has not refused to come down and cannot be counted either way, **and that the record says not asked, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and none of them has been ruled since the second of January and she did not rule one this afternoon and is not going to.**

The man of about thirty-one got down off the box and stood at the bottom of the lane, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about nine people, what had happened at the top of the road, and he gave the honest answer, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I was given a sheet. I did not ask for it and I have not read it and I am not going to say who gave it to me, and the man who went up with me is not on this cart and I did not ask him to come down either.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who says *I am not going to say who gave it to me* has said in a yard that he knows who gave it to him, **and that a man who says *I have not read it* and then has it read out in a yard an hour later is going to be asked whether he had read it, and that this district has four columns of not-askings and has never once asked a man whether he had read a thing, and that is the whole of what it is good for.**

---

Under the yoke there was a folded sheet of paper and it had not been on the cart when the cart went up on the eleventh, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked for it in the six things in front of about nine people and a man of fifty-six said a yard reads a document when somebody asks for it and asked for it.

Before it was read, the man of about thirty-one put the strip of paper on the trestle table face up, which is the way he put it down on the third of this month, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked to put it there and that nobody asked him about it and that she is not going to.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said one thing about it and said it in the ordinary voice and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That is a strip of paper with his own name on it saying the name came off a sheet, and it has been in a coat that went up a road and has come back down that road in a pocket, and a document somebody owns that nobody will produce is still a document somebody owns, and the three this district does not own are three.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man has brought a thing back into a room he was not asked to bring it into and that the room did not ask him one question about it, **and that a document a person keeps in a coat is a document somebody owns and a document on a table is a document somebody owns, and that neither of them is one of the three documents nobody owns, and that the count of three is the same three it has been since the seventeenth of last month and a sheet under a yoke is not a fourth of them.**

A clerk of nineteen years read it out in the yard of Lot Seventeen in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them, and afterwards read it back and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter, and a man of fifty-six read it a second time in the ordinary voice and a clerk entered that he did it and that she did not ask him to.

> **THE STANDING ORDERS OF THE NINE LOCKS. NINE OF THEM.**
>
> **A NUMBER IN A COLUMN ON THE LEFT AND A NIGHT IN A COLUMN ON THE RIGHT AND A COLUMN HEADED KEEPER BETWEEN THEM.**
>
> **THE NINE NIGHTS ARE THE FIRST OF THIS MONTH. THE SECOND. THE THIRD. THE FOURTH. THE SIXTH. THE EIGHTH. THE TENTH. THE ELEVENTH. AND THE TWENTIETH.**
>
> **THE COLUMN HEADED KEEPER CARRIES A HOUSE ON EVERY ONE OF THE NINE ROWS AND A PERSON ON NONE OF THEM.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds and then the man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the whole of what it was, and nobody had asked him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him and that she is not going to put a number on it.

"**A stranger's schedule has eight days in it and a word in the ninth row and this has nine nights in it, and the eight of them are the same eight nights in the same order, and so two lists of nine that agree on eight are not one list, and the one they disagree about is the ninth, and the ninth is the twentieth of this month, and that is six days off and nobody in this district can get to it.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man has just put two pieces of paper next to each other in a yard and that is not a join and is not a rhyme and is a thing anybody can check, and that a man who has stood at the back of a public book for four months has never once put two pieces of paper next to each other and read out what the two of them said. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and that it is not a refusal to join two things and is not added to the thirteen, **and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and neither of them moved at about four in the afternoon on the fourteenth of the fourth month.**

Then a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things in front of about nine people, whether the house in the column headed KEEPER is the same house nine times or nine houses, and the man of about thirty-one said he was not going to guess, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I was handed a sheet by a person who did not give a name and I was not asked a question about any of it and I have not read it and I am not going to work out what a column of houses means from the back of a cart in a yard.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who is handed a document and is not asked a question about it has been given a document and not a job, **and that the six instruments this district has built and not named are a rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security, and that a sheet of paper with a column on it is not a seventh of them, and that a man who has been handed a document and not asked anything about it is not a bearer of the document and is not a party to it and is not going to be.**

Then a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the other half of the paper out loud, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got ninety-six, and it went in the minute in his own words, and nobody picked it up.

"**There is a column on that sheet headed with a word and not a name, and it carries a house nine times and a person no times, and a man of about forty-four told this yard on the seventh of this month that there are four generations of keepers and that not one of them has ever been on a page of his. So no keeper has ever been on a page of anybody's, and it is not because pages forget people. It is because the page has a column and the column carries a house.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a Registry prints a house and does not print a person, and that this district's own records office prints a house and does not print a person and has said so four times in five months, **and that the keepers' page has been doing it for four generations, and that those three are the same sentence told three times by three bodies and are one finding and not three, and that the count of documents nobody owns in this district is three and did not move at about a quarter to five in the afternoon on the fourteenth of the fourth month and a sheet that came down on a cart is a document this district has and did not write and is not a fourth.**

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a page that names a house and not a person is a true page, and that the woman of fifty-eight walked up a bank this morning and came down it and filled two buckets and is not on that page and is not on any page, **and that a house can be a bearer of a toll and a woman carrying two buckets up a hill is a job, and that the two have never been in the same sentence in this district until this afternoon and that I am not going to let a yard join them tonight.**

---

The board was asked about at about half past four and it is the one thing in this district a person can read without a clerk standing next to it, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether the twentieth ought to go on it.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said no in four seconds and gave the reason, and a man of about nineteen counted the reason and got fifty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A date is not a finding. The seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and has nothing on it and it has been ruled since the second of January and the reason it is empty is that nobody has ever had a finding to put there, and a printed day is not a finding.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of boards is five and the count of full lines is six and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty, **and that nobody has put a line on a board on any of the fourteen days from the first of this month to this one, and that nine printed nights have come into this district in the last two days and eight of them went into a book and not onto a board, and that a sheet under a yoke on a cart is a document somebody gave us and not a line anybody found.**

Then a man of fifty-six said the thing nobody had said, and said it in the ordinary voice.

"**There is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers. There never was one, and the ninth of these things is about to be a year somebody has to pay for, and if anybody in this yard has been holding a figure back on that account I would like to hear it now and not on the last day of the month.**"

Nobody answered him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked to make a column and that she did not make one.

**AND ON THE FOURTEENTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH A DRAY CAME DOWN A ROAD WITH ONE MAN ON THE BOX AND A YOKE ON THE TAILBOARD AND A PRINTED SHEET UNDER THE YOKE THAT HAD NOT BEEN ON THE CART WHEN IT WENT UP, AND A CLERK READ OUT NINE NIGHTS WHERE A STRANGER'S SHEET HAS EIGHT, AND THE NINTH OF THEM IS THE TWENTIETH OF THIS MONTH.**
