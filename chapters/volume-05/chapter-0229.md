# Chapter 229: The Last Day Of The Month

---

The thirtieth of April was the last day of the month and there were two things falling due on it, and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning.

The three figures were said out loud by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and read back off the board by the man of fifty-six, who got them right, and the sun came off the top of the loams at about ten and the yard filled in after that.

"**A hundred and twenty-seven days on the board and four hundred and forty-three days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and fifty-seven, and this is the last day of this month, and one of the two things falling due on it is a man and the other one is a lease, and this district has neither.**"

The book has it that the last day of the fourth month is a hundred and eighteen days after the second of January, and that the board outside the room has been carried for longer than the train has stood, and that neither of those two figures is a count of anybody.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households was in that yard and had not been sent for, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about nine people, about the three households that have not been asked again, and he gave the honest answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The house has to ask me. I have been asked five times now to be told what to do about the three and I have not been told, and the state of those six has not moved since the twenty-first of last month, which is forty days, and I would like it written down that five is not a sixth and that a fourth time was not a fifth either.**"

It was entered that the six stand at one signed, two refused and three not asked again, and that the yard decided on the nineteenth of the second month not to decide about the three and has not gone back on it, **and that a man who has not been told what to do has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one this morning and is not going to.**

The lease was not answered, and a clerk of nineteen years read that out at about eleven in the ordinary voice, because nobody else was going to say it and it was going to be said, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got ninety-two, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**A lease has to be answered on this day at a place with no name on it, and there is nobody at that place in this district but a man who is not in this district, and a lease that has not been answered on the day it falls due has not been refused and has not been taken up and has not been given up, and the record says not asked, and I am not going to enter a refusal on a lease nobody in this district was ever asked about.**"

Nobody argued with her. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a lease is a thing somebody else owns, and that a document somebody else owns is not one of the three this district does not own and is not a fourth of them, and that the three are the printed sheet at the fourth place, the notice about a party of nine who went thirty-four miles east, and a line in a public book with four names on it and a figure under the four names.

The man of about fifty-five who keeps that building was in that yard for about twenty minutes in the middle of the morning and had walked up the lane with a pail in his hand that he was not using, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was there and that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing, and that the nine years of beds are in the second room of a building that has denied nobody entry in nine years and still denies nobody entry.

The woman of about thirty-eight who keeps that building sent word by a boy of about nine, and the four words are printed under this and what he had come to say with them was that the beds were in the second room and the water had not come over the step, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy of about nine carried four words, and that a boy of about nine is not a bearer and is not a clerk and is not anybody's instrument, and that the count of things this district does not have is five and did not move at about eleven in the morning on the last day of the fourth month.

"**The beds are upstairs.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road walked through that yard at about one in the afternoon on his way to somewhere else and did not stop, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was in the yard and that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing, and that he has been asked one question in this district since the first of this month and has given one answer and that a man who has been asked one question and given one answer is not a man who has been asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about thirty-one who digs loam what should go on the sheet that is going up the road, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the plainest thing anybody has said in that yard in a month, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got ninety-nine, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote it down in the minute in his own words without being asked to by anybody.

"**That a yard is the only instrument this district has that a stranger can walk up to, and that a book a stranger can walk up to is a door, and that the door which got used this month was a door with a fanlight over it and not this one, and that a man is a person and not a figure, and that a person who has not been asked has not refused, and that is all of it and I have said it four times in eleven days and a man of fifty-six is not a clerk.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a clerk of nineteen years wrote it down in his own words, unasked, at about half past eleven in the morning on the last day of the fourth month, and that the man of about thirty-one who digs loam asked for it on the eleventh, the nineteenth, the twenty-first and the twenty-second of this month and was not written down four times, and that this morning he did not have to ask.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the rest of it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That is not the same thing and I know it is not the same thing, and it is the best that has happened to me in a month, and I am not going to say thank you for it in front of nine people.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the board out himself at about ten past twelve on that last day of the month, and got all three figures right without being asked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he read it and that she did not ask him to and that he has read that board out after another man in this yard about forty times in four months and has never once been asked to do it.

Nobody thanked him for it. A man of fifty-six said that a man who says he is not going to be thanked in a yard has said that yard's own rule back to it, and that the rule was taught here on the third of this month by a woman of fifty-eight, and that it has now been said by three people in three weeks, and that three is not a column.

---

The month turned in the night, and about nine people were in that yard at about ten in the morning on the first of the fifth month, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the figures and a man of fifty-six read the board out after him and got both of them right.

"**A hundred and twenty-eight days on the board and four hundred and forty-four days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and fifty-eight, and the month turned in the night and nobody in this yard has said so and is not going to this morning, and a sheet is going up a road at about half past five this evening.**"

She entered that the month turned in the night, and that this district said a month turn out loud once, on the first of April, and that she was not going to say it twice in a yard, and that it will be said once more when there is something in it, and that she had not been asked when that would be and was not going to say.

The sheet went up the only road in this district that climbs at about half past five in the evening with the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray on the box, and he was asked about it in his own words and he said he was going up anyway, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked and that he said yes, and that a man who says he was going anyway is not the same as a man who says yes, and that both of them are a man who has been asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read out what was on it before it went, in the ordinary voice, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote it down and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter, and no man counted it because a clerk does not put a number on a letter.

> **A BODY THAT WRITES TO A VILLAGE ASKS FOR A MAN BY A DAY THAT HAS PASSED. THIS DISTRICT SENT NO MAN AND IS NOT SAYING IT CANNOT.**
>
> **A BODY OFFERED A CHAIR AND A RULED PLACE FOR A NAME, AND THE OFFER WAS REFUSED IN A ROOM ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THIS MONTH AND AGAIN IN A YARD ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH, AND THE TWO REFUSALS WERE NOT THE SAME NO.**
>
> **THE LAST THING ON IT WAS FOUR WORDS AND A LINE THAT A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM SAID AT A GATE AND A CLERK ENTERED, AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST PUT HIS OWN NAME AT THE BOTTOM OF IT AND NOBODY ASKED HIM TO. THE DISTRICT SENDS ITS REGARDS.**

Nobody stopped him. A man of fifty-six said that a man who signs his own answer is the only one of the four in that yard who is not a clerk and not a man of about thirty-four and not a woman of fifty-eight, and that a name at the bottom of a letter is not a name in a column and is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not on the standing orders of the nine.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the bottom of the lane until the dray was half a mile up the road, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he stood there and that nobody sent for him to stand there and that the record says not asked, and that the man of about thirty-one who digs loam came out of the gate about ten minutes after the dray and did not go up the road after it and said nothing about it.

The day came down and the road took one man up it, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in the yard with his arm out of the sling and in it again, and the slate was on the trestle table under the second sheet, and nobody had read the slate out.

**AND ON THE LAST DAY OF THE FOURTH MONTH TWO THINGS FELL DUE AND NEITHER WAS ANSWERED, ON THE FIRST OF THE FIFTH MONTH A SHEET WENT UP THE ONLY ROAD IN THIS DISTRICT THAT CLIMBS WITH A MAN ON THE BOX WHO WAS GOING UP ANYWAY, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM HAD A SENTENCE OF HIS OWN WRITTEN DOWN WITHOUT HAVING TO ASK FOR IT FOR THE FIRST TIME IN ELEVEN DAYS.**
