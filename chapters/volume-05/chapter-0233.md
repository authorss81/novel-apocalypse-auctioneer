# Chapter 233: The Fourth Of Four

---

The fifth of the fifth month was bright and freezing and the channel had a skin on it in the shade of the bank, and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out after him and got the other two right, and the trough at the foot of the bank had been broken by somebody with a boot about an hour before the yard filled.

"**A hundred and thirty-two days on the board and four hundred and forty-eight days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and sixty-two, and the twelfth of this month is seven days off, and a man came down that road this morning at about half past seven and nobody in this yard sent for him.**"

He came down the only road in this district that climbs. He is a man of about forty-one who keeps a road house at the top of it, past the turn, and the climb is about nine miles and the last half mile of it is a goat track along a shoulder with about two feet of drop on the left. He came down it in about four hours with a stick and his coat buttoned to the throat, and he came into that yard at about half past eleven and stood at the end of the trestle table for about twenty minutes before anybody said anything to him.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was in the yard and that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one this morning and is not going to.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the sheet out to him in the ordinary voice and did not ask him a question while he was doing it, and when he had finished the man from the road house put one hand flat on the third line of it and said his piece, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That is the fourth of the four and it came to the top of our road on the third of this month, the same morning it came to you, and I have had two days to think about it and walk down about it. Our house did not sign for it. Our house has not signed for anything since my father was alive, and he signed for nothing, and I am not a man who signs for other people and I have not been asked to.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him one more thing before anybody else could, in his own words, and the answer was counted, and it came to twenty-three.

"**Then tell me what a party of the fourth of four is at your house, and tell me whether it is a person.**"

"**It is a house with my name over the door, and a name over a door is a name on a house and not a name on a figure, and I have never in forty years been asked to hold anything, and I would know, because everybody in this basin who holds something is talking about it by now.**"

Nobody argued with him about that. The clerk entered that a house with a name over a door is a house and is not a bearer and is not a holder, and that a man who says he has never been asked to hold anything has not refused to hold anything and cannot be counted either way, and that the record says not asked.

The yard went quiet in the way it goes quiet when a thing has been said that everybody in it had already decided the other way, and the clerk entered that two places now hold the fourth of the four, that one of them holds it because a sheet says a place is the fourth of four by itself until somebody says it is not, and that the other one holds it because a man of about forty-one said so in a yard, and that neither of them can be shown to be right and that the record about both of them says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing about it out loud before anybody could put the two sheets next to each other, and it was counted, and it came to sixty-nine.

"**A sheet that numbers four places and a man at the top of a road are two things about the number four, and the number four is not a place and four is not a finding. I am not going to be the man who puts this yard in a column and a house nine miles up in the same column because two of them are the same number.**"

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam refused the join in the plain voice and it was the seventh refusal of that join in this district, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A toll on a gate and a man walking down a road in the cold are two things about two different arrangements, and a man who has walked four hours to tell us his house has not signed has not refused a toll and has not accepted a toll and has not been asked about a toll, and a seventh is a seventh with seven people watching it and the number of people watching it is not a reason.**"

It went in that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve, and that neither of them moved at about one in the afternoon on the fifth of the fifth month.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man from the road house, in the six things, in front of about nine people, whether his house would pay the toll, and the answer was counted and it came to eighty-four, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**The party of a house is a house. There is nobody in our house who has been asked to pay anything and I am not going to be the first one they ask, because the day they ask me is the day the house has signed without anybody going into the room. My answer is the same as the one your woman of fifty-eight gave about her two buckets, and I have not heard her say it and I have not read it out.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the count of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is eight and did not move at about one in the afternoon on the fifth of the fifth month, and that a man who gives the same answer as somebody two hundred yards away without having heard them say it is not a coincidence and is not a finding and is not a column.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the yard ought to rule a column of the places a toll has landed in that have not signed for it, in front of about nine people, and a clerk of nineteen years refused the fifth in about four seconds and was not asked to refuse it.

"**A column of places is a column of houses, and this district has spent four months finding out that a page with a house on it is a true page and is not a finding about a person, and I am not ruling a fifth and there is not going to be a fifth.**"

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was at the back of that yard from about one and did not come nearer the table than the edge of it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the sheet out again for about nine people who had come in after one and got nothing wrong the second time either, and the clerk entered that the sheet has now been read out in that yard twice and that a sheet read out in a yard is not a sheet anybody in that yard has agreed to.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam disagreed with something that arrived in the yard at about two in the afternoon and that nobody had asked for, and the clerk entered that nobody in that yard asked for it and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it.

> **A TOLL ATTACHED TO A GATE ATTACHES TO THE PARTY NAMED AGAINST IT. WHERE THERE IS NO PARTY NAMED, THE TOLL ATTACHES TO NOTHING.**
>
> **A TOLL THAT ATTACHES TO NOTHING IS NOT A TOLL. IT IS AN INSTRUCTION TO A HOUSE, AND A HOUSE THAT PAYS AN INSTRUCTION HAS PAID IT, AND THE HOUSE THAT DOES NOT PAY IT HAS STILL BEEN INSTRUCTED.**
>
> **A LOCK STANDS OPEN OR SHUT. IT DOES NOT STAND IN A DOOR OF A RECORD.**

He said what he thought of the second of those, out loud, in front of about nine people, and it was counted and it came to a hundred and four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That second one is wrong and it is wrong in the direction that costs somebody. You cannot get a house out of a toll by taking the name off it. The name was never what makes it heavy. A thing with no name on it that arrives at a house is a thing the house is afraid of, and a house that is afraid of a thing is a house that will find a person in it to be angry at, and there is no name on the toll for the anger to land on, so it lands on whoever is in the room.**"

Nobody picked it up. A man of fifty-six said that a man who disagrees with a rule out loud is doing the thing this yard was built to let him do, and that it is the third time in this volume that one person has done it, and that a panel may not create a consent and may not make a person a bearer and did not make a house into a party this afternoon.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about one with a bucket in each hand and put them down where she puts them, and the man from the road house looked at her for about four seconds and then looked away, and the clerk entered that he looked and that nobody asked him what he was looking at and that the record about it says not asked.

The man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale stood at the back of that yard at about three in the afternoon for about ten minutes and then went out of the gate without saying anything, and the clerk entered that he was there and that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing, and that his four houses still stand and have not been taken up and were not added to eleven and are not going to be.

The man from the road house went up the road at about two in the afternoon, which is a strange hour to start nine miles, and it takes a man about five hours going up that road in the cold, and he was still on the goat track when the light went. Nobody in that yard offered him anything to eat and nobody thought to, and the clerk entered that as a not-asking, **and entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and did not move at about two in the afternoon on the fifth of the fifth month, and that a man walked four hours down a road and five hours back up it and eleven houses of water went up a bank on the same day and nobody added the two numbers together because the two numbers are not a sum.**

**AND ON THE FIFTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-ONE CAME DOWN THE ONLY ROAD IN THIS DISTRICT THAT CLIMBS AND SAID HIS HOUSE WAS THE FOURTH OF THE FOUR AND THAT HIS HOUSE HAD NOT SIGNED FOR IT, AND TWO PLACES NOW HOLD THE FOURTH OF FOUR AND NEITHER OF THEM CAN BE SHOWN TO BE RIGHT.**
