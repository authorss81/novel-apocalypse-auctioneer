# Chapter 247: Show Of Hands In A Barn

---

The nineteenth of the fifth month was warm and the road that climbs was walked by about eleven people from the yard of Lot Seventeen at about half past six in the morning, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went up it with his left wrist inside his cuff and did not take the cuff off all day.

The figures went out loud at the gate in the dark and a clerk of nineteen years wrote them on the back of a sheet that had already been read out in a yard, and entered that she had done that on purpose and that a figure written on the back of a document somebody else printed is not a figure about the document.

"**A hundred and forty-six days on the board and four hundred and sixty-two days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and seventy-six, and a hundred and thirty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the next month is thirteen days off, and we are going up about nine miles to a building with a door in it and a floor of beaten earth.**"

They went up the four miles out past the loams and then the nine, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put his left hand on the wire above the drop on the goat track as he went past it, the same way he had on the fifteenth, and it did not take the skin off a second time because the skin on that part of his wrist had already gone and what was left was a brown scab about the length of a thumb, and nobody in that party asked him about it and one of them looked at it and then looked at the ground.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road went up it in front with the lamp he had carried down on the fifteenth and had not been asked to carry either time, and he stopped at the top field and waited for the rest of them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who waits for a party at a field gate has not been asked to wait and the record about the waiting says not asked.

The barn belongs to a household that has not been asked anything this month and did not have to be, because a barn is a room with a door and the people in it came because it is their barn and not because anybody fetched them, and a clerk entered that the record about the fetching says not asked.

About nineteen people were in that barn. Eleven of them had come up the road with the party and the other eight live in the country above the water and had come because it is their barn, and about thirteen of the nineteen were over fifty and one of the rest was a man of about nineteen who counts, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the number of people in a room is not a figure about a room and that she wrote down neither figure on purpose and is not going to be asked for them.

The barn has one window about nine inches square high up on the south wall and a floor of beaten earth that had been swept that morning and was already going brown at the middle, and there was one lantern on a shelf that was not lit, and the roof let enough light in that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not need it.

A man of about sixty-three in that room said before the sheet was put that he was not going to put a hand up and gave a reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and thirteen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been in this room since I could walk and I have voted in it about eleven times and I have never once been asked what a thing I was voting on was going to cost me. So I am not putting a hand up today, and I am not putting one down either, and if a clerk wants to write down that I said no she had better come and sit in my house for a week first, because I have not said no to anything, I have said that I do not know what is on the paper, and those are two different sentences and one of them is mine.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says he does not know what is on a paper has not refused the paper, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a man who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the nineteenth of the fifth month.

The covenant was read out in that barn at about eleven by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, standing on a bin against the wall so that the people at the back could hear him, and it was read back afterwards by a woman of about fifty-eight from the third house up the lane, who had been asked and had said yes in four seconds, and the clerk entered that a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one and that a barn does the same when a man stands on a bin.

Then it was put to them and about seven hands went up and two went down, and a clerk of nineteen years entered those two numbers and did not count the rest of the room and entered that she did not count it on purpose, and entered that a hand up is a hand up and is not a name and is not a column and is not a consent to anything that is not on the sheet, **and that the people in that room who put no hand up at all have not refused anything and cannot be counted either way and the record about them says not asked.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam disagreed with a panel that arrived in that barn at about half past twelve and that nobody had asked for, and the clerk entered that nobody in that room asked for it and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it, **and entered that a panel is a rule and a rule is not a document and is not a fourth and is not one of the six instruments this district built and not named.**

> **A COVENANT IS A FIGURE. A FIGURE IS A THING A PERSON CAN BE ASKED ABOUT.**
>
> **FOUR STEWARDS IS FOUR HOLDERS, AND FOUR HOLDERS IS A NUMBER, AND A NUMBER IS THE BEGINNING OF ONE HOLDER. THE WAY BACK FROM FOUR DOWN TO ONE IS FOUR SEASONS.**
>
> **A RIGHT TO GIVE UP IS A RIGHT TO LEAVE. A PERSON WHO LEAVES A THING HAS REFUSED IT, AND A REFUSAL IS A FIGURE, AND A FIGURE IS A THING A PERSON CAN BE ASKED ABOUT.**

He said what he thought of the third of those three, out loud, in front of about nineteen people in a barn with one window, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and seventeen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That third one is wrong in the way that costs a person, and I know that because I have watched it cost a person twice this month. A man who gives up an arrangement has not refused it. He has done the thing the arrangement was written to let him do, and if you call that a refusal then the right to give up is not a right, it is a trap with a door on it, and the five names under a line on a sheet read out in a yard on the sixteenth of this month are five people who were told they could walk out and are now going to be told they ran.**"

Nobody picked it up. A man of fifty-six said that a man who disagrees with a printed rule out loud in a room full of people who did not ask him to is doing the one thing this yard was built to let him do, and that the barn is not a yard, and that he was not going to make anything of the difference, **and that three lines printed by somebody else have now been read out in front of a room in this basin and did not turn a barn into a party and did not turn a person into a refusal.**

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the part about the four places below the water, and the count came to a hundred and twenty-six.

"**A thing was agreed in this room by the people in this room, and the four places by the roads under the water are not in this room and are not in this country, and they are going to sign their own parts on their own doors and nobody in this barn is going to hold their part up and read it out and call it done. One of those four is this yard and one of those four is a house nine miles up a road and both of them say they are the fourth of the four, and that is not resolved and it is not going to be resolved in a barn, and if anybody tries I will stand up and say so.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the four places below the water sign separately and are not the same thing as the room they were going to be told about, and that two of the four hold the fourth of the four and that neither can be shown to be right and that the record about both says not asked.

Then a man who had come to the barn from below the water and had not been asked by anybody in this district to be there read out a piece of paper and it was about nine lines long, and he read it out in the ordinary voice and a clerk entered that he was in the room and that nobody sent for him and that the record about him says not asked.

What he read out was that a body which administers the water above had written and that a body which administers the water above does not bind anybody in this room, and that its answer to a question put to it about the water above is not the answer of this room, and that where the two disagree the disagreement is not a fault and is not a vote and does not go in a column.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a body which has lost the immediate vote on a thing has not been asked about losing it, and that a body is not a person and a vote is not a consent, and that the record about the immediate vote says not asked, and that a council acquires no face in a barn and is a thing a piece of paper comes from.

The man of about fifty-six said that a man who reads out a paper in a barn in the ordinary voice has done the hardest version of the thing this basin does, and that nobody had thanked him and he had not asked, and a clerk entered that nobody thanked him and that the record about the thanking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a tally for six households said one thing in that room about what a show of hands is, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and twenty.

"**I keep the count of six households and it has not moved in twenty-eight days and it is not going to move because somebody stood up in a barn today. A show of hands is not a count of households and it is not a count of people and it is the first thing anybody in this basin has done in four months that everybody in the room heard at the same time. I have been writing down six numbers since the second month and nobody has ever read them out. Do not put me down as one of the seven, because I did not put my hand up for the sheet, I put it up for the reading out.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who is glad of a thing that is not on the paper has not joined the paper, and that the six did not move on any of the twenty-eight days since the twenty-first of last month, and that a hand put up for one reason is not a hand put up for another, and that the record about his two reasons says not asked.

The afternoon went. About four of the people in that barn had to be back down the road before the light went and three of them went and one of the three was the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and he went at about half past three because the goat track is about two feet of drop on the left and a man with a wrist he cannot close his hand on does not want to be on it in the dark.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam went with him as far as the top field and did not go up the shoulder, and at the field gate the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing could not get the top of the fence post he was going to lean on closed over his left hand, and the man who digs loam took the wire in his own right hand and twisted it twice and pushed it through and did it in about four seconds and did not say one word about it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a knot tied by one man's hand for another man is not the second man's knot and is not a job and is not a figure about either of them and did not become a seventh instrument of the six.

**AND ON THE NINETEENTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH THE COVENANT WAS READ OUT IN A BARN ABOUT NINE MILES UP THE ONLY ROAD IN THIS DISTRICT THAT CLIMBS AND PUT TO ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE IN THAT ROOM, AND SEVEN HANDS WENT UP AND TWO WENT DOWN AND TWO PEOPLE DID NOT PUT A HAND UP EITHER WAY, AND A BODY THAT ADMINISTERS THE WATER ABOVE READ OUT IN THE ORDINARY VOICE THAT IT DOES NOT BIND ANYBODY IN THAT ROOM AND HAS LOST THE IMMEDIATE VOTE WITHOUT HAVING BEEN ASKED ABOUT IT.**
