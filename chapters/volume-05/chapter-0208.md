# Chapter 208: The Grade

---

The eighth of April was the sixth day of a man with a portfolio and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the strip of paper was on the trestle table again, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said in the yard that the man of about thirty-one had put it in his coat on the fourth of this month and said in this yard that he was going to keep it there and was not going to have it read out in a yard again, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said that and that on the eighth of this month the strip was on the table and that nobody asked him to and that she is not going to ask him about it, **and that a document a person keeps in a coat is a document somebody owns and is not one of the three this district does not own, and that a man who changes his mind about a piece of paper in a yard is a man and is not a bearer and is not a security and is not one of the five things this district does not have and was refused entry into all three lists in about four seconds by a clerk who was not asked to refuse him.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the figures out loud and a man of fifty-six read the board out afterwards and got both of them right.

"**A hundred and five days on the board and four hundred and twenty-one days on the train, and the days nobody has entered stand at a hundred and thirty-five, and the fifth thing this district does not have is a way of paying a person who is not in a household and it was named out loud in this yard on the twenty-ninth of the third month and it has not been paid since.**"

Nobody argued with it. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a thing named out loud in a yard does not become a smaller thing by being said again, **and that the count of things this district does not have is five and is five, and that a strip of paper on a table is a document somebody owns and is not a sixth of anything, and that this district has three documents nobody owns and did not make a fourth this morning.**

---

The road came up at about half past ten and it came up because a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man with the portfolio, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, how he meant to walk to a place he would not name, and the answer was that he meant to walk the only road in this district that goes up.

He said the second half of it before the asking, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**There is one. It goes out past the loams and up past the top field of a farm and it is the only one in this basin with a climb on it, and I have known that since I came down the eleven mile road and I have been waiting six days to see whether anybody in this yard would work it out without me.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a stranger who has been waiting six days for a yard to do his arithmetic is a stranger who has spent six days measuring this district, and that the arithmetic is short. **The top field four miles out past the loams is the fourth holding on the delivery sheet at the second reservoir, and the fourth holding has not taken a drop since the first of the second month, and the toll on the lease is paid at the ninth place, and the ninth holding is the one nobody can account for, and a man who digs loam has refused to join two of those three in this yard on two separate days and the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and the registrar's own count at twelve and neither of them moved at half past ten in the morning on the eighth of the fourth month.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the refusal out loud and on purpose, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and that she was not asked to write the order down and entered the order.

"**A road that goes up ends where the water is and a holding nobody can account for is where the water is not accounted for and those are two sentences and this district has joined them before and been wrong, and I am not doing it a third time for a man with a case the size of a hymn book.**"

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the other half, and he said it flat, and nobody picked it up.

"**A lease pays its toll at the ninth place and the ninth place is not a road, and the ninth row of a stranger's own schedule says so in two words, and this yard has been using a word it borrowed off a body forty miles from here for seven days. I am not joining anything. I am saying that if we are walking up the only road that climbs then we are walking past a holding that stopped taking water in the second month and the water that is not coming is going somewhere and nobody in this district can say where, and that is not a rhyme. That is a road with two hundred yards of soft ground on the top of it and a holding that has not taken a drop since the first of the second month, and those are two things a person can put a hand on.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not put a day of the week to a figure, and that no weekday is attached to any date in this district, and that she is not going to correct him because he is not wrong about the water, **and that the fourth of the nine holdings has not taken a drop since the first of the second month and the man who digs loam opened a sluice for it on the night of the thirteenth of the third month and neither of those is joined to a road anybody opened in the night nine miles away, and that a resemblance noticed a second time is not a finding and a resemblance noticed a third time is a habit.**

---

The grade came up at about eleven, and it came up because a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what would go on the dray yesterday and got *nothing yet* for an answer, and a man of about thirty-one came back at about eleven with the answer and it was not nothing.

He said it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Two hundred yards of it at the top, and it has been soft since the water came down a channel on the twenty-ninth of the third month, and you cannot pull a dray up two hundred yards of that on four hands and a chain, and I know because I have tried it once.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who has tried it once and failed is a man who went and looked and that nobody in this yard knew the answer yesterday because nobody had been up there, and that the second of the five things this district does not have is a column that is a place, **and that a road with two hundred yards of soft ground on it is a place and a dray is a place and a yoke is a place and there are three places in this problem and no figure attached to any of them.**

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the other thing, and he said it to the yard and not to the man who had offered the dray, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked to and entered it.

"**There is one yoke in this district. It is on the stones at the top of a hill and it has been there since the twenty-fourth of the third month and a man of fifty-six said in a yard on that day that it was not going to be picked up again, and he said it in this yard and he was not wrong about water and he is not wrong about this either.**"

A man of fifty-six entered that he had said it and that he was not asked about it now and that nobody was going to ask him, and that the yoke has been on those stones for fifteen days and has not been carried up or down or lent or given away, **and that a yoke-load is a job and a job is not a figure and cannot be put on a page and is not going to be, and that the owner of that yoke is a woman of fifty-eight and nobody has sent for her this morning and the record says not asked.**

Then the man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said the plain thing, because his instrument does not measure this either, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That is a carrying yoke. It has two hooks on the ends of it and it has carried buckets for nine years and it has never pulled anything in its life and it is not going to pull a dray, and a dray wants a draught yoke and this district has not got one.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the answer to the question what goes on the dray is therefore nothing, and that a dray cannot go up two hundred yards of soft ground with a chain and four hands, and that a man who offered a cart on the seventh of this month has not been asked how since, **and that the two things said about the yoke this morning in this yard were one true thing and one wrong thing and that they were given about nine seconds apart by two men and only one of the two of them knew it.**

Nobody sent for her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the woman of fifty-eight was at the top of that hill at about eight in the morning and had been there since before the yard was asked for anything, and that a man of fifty-six saw her go up and did not say so, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and none of them has been ruled since the second of January and she did not rule one this morning and is not going to.

---

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against asked the registrar of this district's records office for a thing, and she was in the yard and had come on her own account and a clerk of nineteen years entered that and that nobody sent for her.

He asked her to put a name on a line. Not a name in a book that a stranger can walk up to — a day, a road, two hundred yards, a yoke, and the man of about thirty-one, so that the thing about to be done had a person at the end of it instead of a hole.

She said no in four seconds and gave the reason before she was asked, and a man of about nineteen counted the reason and got eighty-two, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**A name on a line is a name off a sheet. I told a man from a Registry on the second of this month that a holder is a name a body has taken off a sheet, and you have spent four months saying a person who has not been asked has not refused, and you are asking me to make a man into a bearer by writing him down, and I will not do it, and you knew I would not.**"

Nothing was said for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that a registrar who says no in four seconds and gives the reason before she is asked has said no in four seconds three times in seven weeks and three days, and that the first of them was on the fifteenth of the second month and the second on the second of this month, and that the three of them were about a sentence, a toll at the ninth place and a name on a line, **and that all three of them were the same sentence told three times and a man who is told the same thing three times by the same person has been told it once.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the man who asked has now asked this office for two things in six days and been given one of them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was given the page in his own hand on the twentieth of the second month and that it is a page and not a copy and that a page in his own hand is not a public thing, **and that a man who is given a page in his own hand on the twentieth of the second month and refused a line on the eighth of the fourth month has not been refused anything, because a page in a drawer and a line in a yard are two different objects and neither of them is a bearer.**

Nobody said the opposite. The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the reason and not the answer, which is what he did on the twenty-eighth of the third month, and she said the answer to a question he had not asked, which is what she had done then too, and no clerk was within nine feet of either of them and nobody counted it.

"**I know it would not work. I asked anyway because I have watched this yard do one useful thing in eleven days and I wanted to see whether it would do it by writing, and it will not, and that is the answer and I am not going to pretend I did not know the answer before I asked.**"

"**Then you asked a question you knew the answer to in front of nineteen people, and that is a thing clerks do, and I have been one, and I did not enjoy it either.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the part that had not been said, and he said it before anybody asked him for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that she is not going to put a number on it.

"**You have all day to work out that you are about to take a piece of wood off a woman's stones and put it on a cart, and a piece of wood on a cart that came out of a yard is a job, and the job is hers because it came off her stones, and she is not in the cart and she is not in the yard and nobody has said her name since the twenty-fourth of the third month. That is the fifth of the five and it is not a wage. It is the fifth of the five standing in the middle of this yard and nobody has priced it because there is no figure in this basin that prices a person who is not in a household.**"

Nobody contradicted him. A man of fifty-six said that a job taken is still a job and that the person it was taken from is still the person who does it, and that this is the second time in five days that a man has come at the same thing from two directions and got to the same place, **and that the two of them are the man the figure of twenty-one years is against and the man who digs loam and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and are not added to because a man and a man agreeing is not two things being joined.**

Then the man of about thirty-one said it, and he said it standing up and not to anybody, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I will go up and get it myself. Nobody has to come with me and nobody has to be asked and I will put it on the cart and I will not burn it and I will not break it and that is all I am going to say about it.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who says *nobody has to be asked* has just said the sentence this district has been saying for four months with a different mouth on it, and that a man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said out loud this morning that the yoke is not the thing that would pull the dray, and that a man who has been told the answer is not the answer and goes up a hill for the answer anyway is a man and not a security, **and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him what the yoke was for and entered that he was not asked to fetch it and entered that nobody stopped him, and that the not-asking is entered and is not one of the four columns and is not a fifth and she did not rule one and is not going to.**

The woman of fifty-eight was at the top of the hill at about four in the afternoon and did not come down and nobody sent for her and nobody asked her anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked about the dray and that she was not asked about the yoke and that she was not asked about the two hundred yards, **and that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said in this yard at about four in the afternoon that he would ask her himself in the morning and in his own words and not in six things, and that a clerk entered that and entered that nobody in that yard asked him why he had said it and that she is not going to.**

**AND ON THE EIGHTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH THE REGISTRAR OF THIS DISTRICT'S RECORDS OFFICE REFUSED IN FOUR SECONDS TO PUT A NAME ON A LINE FOR A TRIP THAT WAS ABOUT TO BE DONE, A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM SAID THAT A PIECE OF WOOD OFF A WOMAN'S STONES IS A JOB AND THE JOB IS HERS, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE SAID IN FIFTY-ONE WORDS THAT HE WOULD GO UP AND GET IT HIMSELF AND THAT NOBODY HAD TO BE ASKED.**
