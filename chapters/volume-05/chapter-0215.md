# Chapter 215: A Body That Writes

---

The sixteenth of April was a day with nothing on the printed sheet and nothing in the road house's book after the ninth, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in it.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said at about ten that the man who mends fencing had gone up the bank at about half past eight with nothing in his hands, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not sent for and that nobody went with him and that the record says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the figures out loud at about half past ten when he came back down, and a man of fifty-six read the board out afterwards and got both of them right.

"**A hundred and thirteen days on the board and four hundred and twenty-nine days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and forty-three, and a man has been up a bank and down one and asked a question and I am not going to tell you what it was until he does.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who gives a figure out loud and then says he is not going to say the other thing has done this district's only instrument twice in one morning, and that the two are not the same instrument. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and none of them has been ruled since the second of January and she did not rule one this morning and is not going to.

---

He said what he had asked at about eleven and he said it in the ordinary voice and a clerk of nineteen years counted it and got sixty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I asked her how many of the ninety houses she can still name. I did not ask her in the six things and I did not ask her what she wants and I have not asked her that three times in four months and I was not going to ask her a fourth time about a thing I cannot price.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six entered that a man said in a yard on the eighth of this month that he would ask her himself in the morning and in his own words, and that on the ninth of this month he did not, and that nobody asked him why for three days, **and that he asked her on the sixteenth in his own words and named the day when he said he would do it, and that the two of those are the whole difference between a man and a yard and that the yard is not going to be told about it twice.**

She said it at about half past eleven and she was not asked a first thing and a clerk of nineteen years counted it and got forty-one, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I carried water to about ninety houses for nine years and eleven of them still have a door on them and I could go back to those eleven tomorrow and I am not going to be asked for the other seventy-nine.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that *about ninety* is not a figure and that a number taken out of a thing with a word in front of it is not a subtraction, **and that eleven is a figure and that nobody in this yard has done anything with it yet and that a figure that has been entered in a book and not acted on is a day and not a wage, and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and it did not move at about half past eleven in the morning on the sixteenth of the fourth month.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in his own words and not in the six things, whether he was to write the eleven down, and she said no in four seconds and gave the reason, and a man of about nineteen counted the reason and got seventy-three, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**A name said once in a yard is a name four hundred people have and a name said twice is a name a counter can ask for, and you taught that to this yard on the third of this month, and I am not saying eleven names in front of nineteen people so that a thing I have kept in my head since the second month is in a book in a room.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who taught a yard a rule is not a man who owns it, and that the eleventh of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, **and that a woman who has kept eleven doors in her head since the second month has done the only thing in this district that has no instrument for at all, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that refusal and entered that it is a refusal with a reason and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and is not a ninth.**

---

The letter had been in the district for two days.

It came down on the dray on the fourteenth under the same fold as the standing orders and it has a seal on it and nobody broke it, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said at about one in the afternoon that a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one and that nobody asked it.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against read it out in the yard at about a quarter to two in the afternoon in front of about nineteen people, and a man of fifty-six said before it was read that a document is read out and not discussed, and that this district settled that on the twenty-ninth of December of last year and has not gone back on it, **and that a letter with somebody's seal on it is a document and is not a schedule and is not a figure and that he is going to read it and not say a word about it and that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself eleven times in four months.**

> **TO THE PEOPLE OF THE PLACE THIS DISTRICT IS. THE COUNCIL OF THE NINE LOCKS SENDS GREETING AND APOLOGISES FOR THE FIRST OF THESE COMING FOUR MONTHS LATE.**
>
> **THE COUNCIL IS A BODY AND NOT A PLACE AND THIS LETTER DOES NOT SAY WHERE THE COUNCIL SITS OR WHEN IT SITS AGAIN.**
>
> **NINE THINGS HOLD BACK A WATER ABOVE FOUR DISTRICTS AND NO PERSON HOLDS ANY OF THEM, AND THE COUNCIL ASKS WHETHER THIS DISTRICT WILL SEND A MAN TO A COUNCIL.**
>
> **THE COUNCIL ASKS FOR AN ANSWER BY THE LAST DAY OF THIS MONTH. THE COUNCIL SENDS ITS REGARDS AGAIN.**

A clerk of nineteen years read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam read the third of the four lines out again in the ordinary voice and a clerk entered that he did it and that she did not ask him to.

Nothing was said for about nine seconds and then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing that was actually in the yard, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A body that writes to a village is a body that knows the village has something, and it has known for four months because it apologises for the first of these being late, and nobody in this basin is going to tell me that a body apologises for a thing it has not counted.**"

A man of fifty-six said that this is the first letter this district has ever had from anything like that, and that a body that has never heard of this district sent a stranger into it on the second of this month, and that the two are the same body and are not joined, **and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and neither of them moved at about two in the afternoon on the sixteenth of the fourth month, and that a second refusal of the same join is the same refusal.**

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half, and nobody had asked him for it.

"**That letter asks this district for a man. This yard has been unable to produce a person on demand for the sixteen days since the thirty-first of the month before last, when a figure of twenty-one years came off this district's own page in the same line as the date, and the reason was said in eighty-two words by a woman in this yard on the eighth of this month, and a name on a line is a name off a sheet, and there is not one name in this district that anybody in this district has the right to put on a line for that letter.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the registrar of this district's records office has refused in four seconds three times in eight weeks and four days, on the fifteenth of the second month, the second of this month and the eighth, **and that the eighth of April to this afternoon is eight days and that the refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading, and that a letter asking for a man is not a refusal to read and is not the tenth and is not anything in this district's book.**

The woman of fifty-eight was in the yard at about half past three and she had come down the bank at about two and had not gone up again, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, whether a letter from a council changes anything about eleven houses, and she said the honest answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-four, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**It is a letter and I have not got a letter, and eleven houses is eleven houses this morning and eleven houses this afternoon, and a body that has never written to me is not going to tell me what a house is worth.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a woman who has refused a round, a figure, a yoke and a cart in a month has now refused a council, and that the four things she has refused are the whole of her position and that she has never once been asked to explain any of them. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked a first thing about the letter, **and that a person who is not asked has not refused and the record says not asked, and that a body which writes to a village has not thereby written to a woman on a bank, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one this afternoon and is not going to.**

And a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one more thing at about half past three and a clerk of nineteen years entered it.

"**The Council wants its answer by the last day of this month. A man from a Registry said in this yard on the fifth of this month that he has to be at a place with no name on it on that same day and there is nobody at the place but him, and I am not joining those two things and I am saying both of them out loud so that somebody else has to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fourteenth of this month is a Tuesday by the road house's reckoning and that **no weekday is attached to any date in this district and she is not going to correct him because he is not wrong about the day the dray came down.**

---

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said at about four in the afternoon that a letter with somebody's seal on it is a document this district has got and did not write, and a man of fifty-six said that this is the first thing to have arrived here out of five things this district is mixed up in, and that four of the other things were written by people in this district and one of the four is a receipt in a building thirty-four miles east.

A clerk of nineteen years read that out afterwards in the ordinary voice and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-nine, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**The charter of four sheets and the third form of the notice and the food agreement of six sheets and the receipt thirty-four miles east were all written by people in this district or by a house this district dealt with, and this is the first thing that has arrived here in five months that somebody else wrote and wanted, and it is a document this district has got and did not write and cannot produce, and a document this district cannot produce is not a document nobody owns.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a document somebody owns and will not produce is the fifth thing this district cannot do with a piece of paper and that it is not the fifth of the five and is not one of them, **and that the count of documents nobody owns is three, and that a letter a body sent to a village is the first of a kind and a kind is not a count, and that the three are the printed sheet at the fourth place, the notice about a party of nine who went thirty-four miles east, and a line in a public book with four names on it and a figure under the four names, and the three did not move at about four in the afternoon on the sixteenth of the fourth month.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the half of it that nobody had said, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A letter is the one thing a body can send that does not need a bearer and does not need a man to be found and cannot be refused by a man who is not in the room, and it has been sitting in this district for four days, and a body that has worked that out about this district is not a body that has never heard of this district.**"

Nobody contradicted him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and that she was not asked to, and that a letter is not an instrument this district built and not named, and that the count of six did not move.

**AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED THE WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT HOW MANY OF THE NINETY HOUSES SHE CAN STILL NAME, AND SHE SAID ELEVEN, AND THE FIRST LETTER THIS DISTRICT HAS HAD FROM ANYTHING LIKE A COUNCIL WAS READ OUT IN A YARD AND WAS NOT DISCUSSED, AND IT ASKS FOR A MAN BY THE LAST DAY OF THIS MONTH.**
