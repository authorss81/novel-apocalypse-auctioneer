# Chapter 219: The Ninth

---

The dray went up the only road in this district that climbs at about half past five on the morning of the twentieth of April with one man on the box and nothing else on the cart, and the yoke was not on it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk of nineteen years was at the bottom of the lane at about half past five and was not sent for and was there on her own account, **and that a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat at the third house up the lane from the scale was at her own door and was not sent for and was not asked anything, and that a yoke which was on a tailboard at the bottom of this lane on the tenth of this month was in a forge end off Salt Row being looked at by the man who owns it, and that nobody put it on the cart and nobody asked the man who owns it whether it should go.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said at about ten in the morning that a man who does not take a yoke up a road has done one thing this district did not expect and that nobody is going to be told what it was because it was not for anybody.

A man of fifty-six said that a man who says a thing is not for anybody is doing the same thing a clerk of nineteen years does when she says she is not going to put a number on the order, **and that a man of about thirty-one took a yoke off a woman's stones on the ninth of this month without asking her, put it on a cart on the tenth because he had said he would not put it on a cart until she had seen it on a cart, and has left it in a forge this morning because nobody asked him to take it, and that is two decisions in ten days that nobody in this district made and neither of them was a toll.**

About nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about half past ten and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the figures out loud and a man of fifty-six read the board out afterwards and got both of them right.

"**A hundred and seventeen days on the board and four hundred and thirty-three days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and forty-seven, and the ninth of the nine nights is this morning and there is not one person in this district who can see whether it happened.**"

A man of fifty-six said that he did not believe a dray would be back by Sunday and a clerk of nineteen years entered that no weekday is attached to any date in this district and that she is not going to correct him because he is not wrong about a dray, **and that the day this yard is on is the ninth of the nine nights on a sheet a body printed and that a body which prints a night six days in front of the night, the sheet having come down on the fourteenth, is a body that has told this district the date in advance, and that this district has no way of saying whether a night that has been printed in advance is a night or a figure.**

---

The lane four miles down from the lower terrace had about four people on it in the middle of the morning and nobody had sent anybody, and the butt at the back of the brick building was at nine days and the tank four hundred yards from it was within a hand's breadth of its lip for the second time since the first of the second month, and the crack in it was running.

A clerk of nineteen years entered both figures and entered that the butt was at nine days on the seventeenth of the month before last and at four days on the first of this month, **and that a butt going from four days to nine days in nineteen days is a figure, and that a figure which describes a butt does not describe a night nine miles off, and that she entered the figure and did not enter a finding because nobody found one.**

The man of about fifty-five who keeps that building was on the step and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about four people, whether he wanted a fifth mend in it now, and he said no in about two seconds and gave a reason, and a man of about nineteen counted the reason and got fifty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A tank with a mark on it is a tank I can see and a tank with a mend in it is a tank that comes out in three pieces, and I have said that in January and on the first of this month and I am saying it now.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked and that he answered and that she is not going to count how many times a man has said no to a fifth mend in a tank, **and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that a man who keeps a building is not a clerk of a house, and that the count of eight did not move this morning, and that the protected count is five and no sixth was added and a building that has denied nobody entry in nine years is a building and is not a sixth.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the plainest thing about the tank at about eleven, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and four, and it went in the minute in his own words, and he said it flat and in the ordinary voice.

"**A tank filling is a river doing what a river does and it is not a thing standing open on a hill nine miles off, and I have put those two next to each other twice today and I am telling this yard that I have put them next to each other twice, and a resemblance noticed a second time is not a finding and a third time is a habit, and the twentieth of this month is the ninth of the nine nights on a sheet a body printed and I am not going to let a pail of water be the witness.**"

Nothing was said for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that a man who announces his own resemblance before anybody can find it is doing the only thing this district has got that is worth more than a yard, **and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six, and that saying a thing out loud twice is not a seventh of them and did not become one at about eleven in the morning on the twentieth of the fourth month.**

The man of about fifty-five who keeps that building said the other half of it before anybody asked him and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it first and that she is not going to put a number on it.

"**Nine years of beds in this building and I have not lost one and I am not going to lose one on the twentieth of April because a crack is wet, and the water is where the water comes from and I have never once been able to say where that is and I am fifty-five and I have stopped expecting to.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who has stopped expecting to know where water comes from has not lost a question, he has only stopped saying it out loud, **and that the man said it out loud on the fifth of this month and said the same thing today and the two are the same sentence said twice and not two sentences, and that a man who gives the same reason twice is not refusing less and is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the count of eight did not move this morning.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about four people, what he would do if the water came another foot, and the man gave the honest answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I would take the beds up to the second room and I would do it at about two in the morning and I would not tell anybody until the morning, and that is not a plan and it is nine years of doing it.**"

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man who has decided in advance not to tell anybody until the morning is a man who has decided that a figure gets to be a figure for about eight hours, **and that this district has eleven houses of a round that nobody can price and a tank within a hand's breadth of its lip and a road with a man on it, and that all three of those are decided about by people who will not say so until the morning, and that is not a rhyme and I am not going to make it one at four people and a lane in the middle of the day.**

---

The woman of fifty-eight carried two buckets up the bank eleven times between about seven in the morning and about one in the afternoon and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not carry them for her, and a man of fifty-six said that a man who carried two empty buckets up a hill for a woman in the month before last and did not carry them this morning is a man who has not been asked a question and is not in a column, **and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule a fifth this morning and is not going to, and that a man who has stopped doing a thing he was never asked to do has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way and the record says not asked.**

She was in the yard at about two in the afternoon and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in the six things, in front of about nine people, whether she was going to say anything to the Council when it wrote, and she said the honest answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-eight, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I have not got anything to say to a body I have never had a letter from and I am not going to be asked to have one.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a woman who refuses in advance to be asked has not refused and that the record says not asked, **and that a letter that asks this district for a man by the last day of this month has been in this district for four days and that a yard is not a party and a room is not a party and a woman is not a man, and that there are five of the five things this district does not have and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and did not move at about two in the afternoon on the twentieth of the fourth month.**

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went up the road at about five in the afternoon and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not sent for and that he said he was going to look and was not going to say when he would be back, and that a clerk of nineteen years was not asked to go with him and did not offer.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said one thing about that, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Four people went up that road in ten days and not one of them went up it to ask anybody anything, and a road that is walked and not asked on is a road and not a corridor, and this district has been treating it as a corridor since the eighth of this month and it is not going to be one.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who is going to look at a channel and not at a lock has understood more about this district in two days than the four bodies that have written to it in a month, **and that the sixth of the six instruments this district has built and not named is a rehearsal and that a rehearsal is not a survey and that a survey is not an instrument and that a man walking up a road with his own eyes is the closest thing this district has come to one in four months and it is a man and not a thing.**

The day went down and the man of about thirty-one was not on the road and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said at about five that he was going to go up and look and was not going to say when he would be back, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and that she is not going to put a number on the order.

**AND ON THE TWENTIETH OF THE FOURTH MONTH, WHICH IS THE NINTH OF THE NINE NIGHTS ON A SHEET A BODY PRINTED, A DRAY WENT UP THE ONLY ROAD IN THIS DISTRICT THAT CLIMBS AT ABOUT HALF PAST FIVE WITH A YOKE LEFT IN A FORGE, AND A TANK FOUR MILES DOWN A LANE CAME UP WITHIN A HAND'S BREADTH OF ITS LIP FOR THE SECOND TIME SINCE THE FIRST OF THE SECOND MONTH, AND NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT SAID THE TWO THINGS WERE THE SAME THING.**
