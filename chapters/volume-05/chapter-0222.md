# Chapter 222: What A Chair Is For

---

The twenty-third of April was cold enough that the water in the trough at the foot of the bank had a skin on it at about nine in the morning, and the smell off the channel came up under the gate and stayed in the yard for about an hour and then went when the wind turned, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen because there was a chair in it and not because anybody had sent for anybody.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in it, standing with his right arm across his body the way he had stood the day before, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got them right.

"**A hundred and twenty days on the board and four hundred and thirty-six days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and fifty, and the last day of this month is seven days off, and there is still a chair printed on a sheet on a table and nobody has said yes to it.**"

The clerk of nineteen years entered that a chair is not an instrument this district has built, **and that the six are a rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security, and that a chair is a place and not a seventh of them, and that the six did not move at about ten in the morning on the twenty-third of the fourth month, and that the conditions with no end on it are four and the seat of a chair is not a fifth.**

The chair itself came out of a building at the end of the lane at about half past ten, carried on its back by a man of about twenty-three who does not keep anything and sells nothing, and he put it down at the end of the trestle table, and nobody moved it, and nobody sat in it, and it stayed in that yard for the rest of the day and the next.

It was entered that the chair was not sent for and that the man who carried it was not asked to carry it and that he said he was going past with it, and that a chair a man carries past a yard with and leaves in it is not the chair on the sheet, **and that a piece of furniture in a yard is a piece of furniture and is not one of the six and did not become a seventh, and that a chair nobody has sat in is not a founder's chair and is not going to become one by being sat in.**

Then the man of about thirty at the counter came down the eleven mile road at about a quarter to eleven, and it was the sixteenth time since the second of January, and the clerk entered that a hundred and eleven days separate the second of January and this morning, which is fifteen weeks and six days, and that the counting runs eleventh on the second of this month, twelfth on the fourth, thirteenth on the sixth, fourteenth on the ninth, fifteenth on the fifteenth and sixteenth today, and that this district has never once given that man a day to be here on.

He brought four sheets, which is what he brings, and he stood at the end of the trestle table for about a quarter of an hour before anybody said anything to him.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not ask him about the ninth holding on the delivery sheet, and he said out loud in front of about nineteen people that he was not going to, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got ninety-five, and it went in the minute in his own words, and the clerk entered that he was not asked and that she was not going to make a number of it, and that the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight, and that the two are not the same figure and are not added together.

"**I am not going to ask him that again today, and I am saying it in front of nineteen people so that if anybody in this yard says I did not ask, I have got it in the minute, and the reason is that a man who has been refused seven times in four months has never once been asked why, and if I put him an eighth time in a yard he is going to say no again and the eight is going to turn into the ninth for asking and not for refusing.**"

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man who has worked out how to stop a counter moving is a man who has found out that the count in this district runs on people and not on yards, and that he had not thought of that until this morning, and that he was not going to make anything of it.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about thirty at the counter what his house thirty-four miles east would do with a chair over that water, and he gave the honest answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**My house would want it before the water did. I have been asked what a house is doing in a column headed with a word instead of a name for four months and I could not tell you then and I cannot tell you now, and a chair is not a house and a house is not a chair, and the day they are the same thing this district is going to hear about it from a body and not from me.**"

The man of fifty-six said that a man who says he would want a thing before the water did is not a man being bought, he is a man describing a house, and that a house wanting a thing is a fact about a house and not a consent and not a person who has been asked.

The man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was in that yard and had not been sent for and was not asked anything, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, what a chair is worth, and he gave the same answer he has now given three times in eighteen days and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have weighed nine sacks a day for nine years and I have never weighed a day, and I have never weighed a chair, and a man who wants one of those three priced for him is asking the wrong man, and I have been the wrong man four times now and I am going to stop being asked and start being told.**"

The clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale has now given the same answer four times in eighteen days, and that a man who gives the same answer four times has not refused four times, **and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and none of them has been ruled since the second of January and she did not rule one this morning and is not going to.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the thing about the chair in the ordinary voice without being asked, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A chair is a thing you cannot see from where I am standing and a founder is a man somebody has already decided about, and a sheet can say both of those things in one line and mean every word of it, and a page that says it is a true page, and the whole of what this district has is a yard where anybody can stand up and say no, and a chair takes the standing up away.**"

It came at about ten past twelve and nobody had asked for it, and it arrived in the same place the others arrive in, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard asked for it and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it.

> **A CHAIR IS A PLACE A PERSON IS PUT. A PLACE A PERSON IS PUT IS NOT A FIGURE. A FIGURE A PERSON IS PUT IN IS A JOB, AND A JOB TAKEN IS A JOB, AND THE PERSON IT IS TAKEN FROM IS STILL THE PERSON WHO DOES IT.**
>
> **A TERM WITH NO END ON IT IS NOT A LONG TERM. A LONG TERM ENDS. A TERM WITH NO END ON IT IS A HOLDER.**
>
> **A NAME IN A RULED PLACE IS A NAME IN A RULED PLACE AND NOT A PERSON WHO HAS BEEN ASKED.**

Nobody disagreed with it and nobody picked it up, and the clerk entered that nobody disagreed and that she was not going to make anything of it, **and that this is the third of these since the first of April that nobody in a yard has disagreed with, and that the first came on the tenth of this month and the second on the nineteenth, and that the man who disagreed with the one on the nineteenth said he was going to keep the rest to himself and she is not going to ask him whether he has.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam put his hand flat on the post of the gate and tried to lift it with his right arm, and could not, and did not try a second time in front of anybody. A man of fifty-six said that a man who tries a thing once in a yard and does not try it twice has learned more in one morning than that yard has taught anybody in a month, and that an arm is not a lesson and is not an instrument and is not a seventh.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the second sheet out again at about two in the afternoon for about eleven people who had not been in the yard in the morning, and got nothing wrong, and it was entered that the second sheet has now been read out in that yard twice, **and that a sheet read out twice is still a sheet somebody else wrote, and that the three documents nobody owns in this district are three and a sheet read out twice is not a fourth, and that the standing orders of the nine, the council's first letter and this one are three documents this district has got and did not write and cannot produce.**

A man of about nineteen who counts was in that yard all day and had not been asked to do anything, and a man of fifty-six asked him at about half past three what he counted and why, and he said that he counted because a yard was a place where a number could be checked and that he did not know why the number mattered and that he had been counting since the second of April, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had been counting for twenty days and that twenty is a figure about a man and is not a finding.

The man of about thirty at the counter went out of that yard at about half past three with his four sheets and nobody walked out with him. It was entered that the sixteenth time cost him a road and not a refusal, and that this district has still not asked that man one question about a chair, **and that a man who came eleven miles and was not asked about the thing he has refused seven times has been in a yard for about four hours and has got nothing out of it, and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and it did not move at about half past three in the afternoon on the twenty-third of the fourth month.**

The day came down and the gate wanted a foot against it, and the chair was still standing at the end of the trestle table with nothing on it and nobody in it, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam went out of the gate with his right arm in a sling that the woman of fifty-eight had lent him at about two in the afternoon, and it was entered that she lent it and that nobody asked her to and that the record says not asked.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-THIRD OF THE FOURTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY AT A COUNTER CAME DOWN AN ELEVEN MILE ROAD FOR THE SIXTEENTH TIME AND WAS NOT ASKED ABOUT THE NINTH HOLDING BECAUSE A MAN SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX WHO KEEPS A SCALE GAVE THE SAME ANSWER FOR THE FOURTH TIME IN EIGHTEEN DAYS AND SAID HE WAS GOING TO STOP BEING ASKED, AND THE COUNTS ARE EIGHT AND SEVEN AND THEY ARE NOT THE SAME FIGURE.**
