# Chapter 234: A Rival Record

---

The wind was out of the north on the sixth of the fifth month and it went through the yard of Lot Seventeen the way it goes through a place with nothing high on three sides, and about nineteen people were standing in it at about ten in the morning.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out after him.

"**A hundred and thirty-three days on the board and four hundred and forty-nine days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and sixty-three, and the twelfth of this month is six days off, and there is a fourth thing on that table and this one is under the leg of it.**"

It was not nailed to anything. It was folded once and pushed under the leg of the trestle table where the ground had gone soft, and it had been under that leg long enough to have taken the shape of it, and nobody in that yard had put it there. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the whole of that before anybody asked him for it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A table in this yard has now had four things put on it and put under it by bodies in another county, and the fourth one somebody stood at that leg and pushed it under, and a man who pushes a paper under a table leg is a man who has stood in this yard and looked at this table, and I am not going to stand here and work out which one of them and I am not going to be asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years read it at about half past ten because a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and she read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **A NOTICE IS PUT UP BY A BODY THAT KEEPS RECORDS OF HOLDINGS. IT NAMES NO PLACE.**
>
> **IT SETS DOWN A RIVAL RECORD. A RIVAL RECORD IS A RECORD IN A BASIN WHERE A BOOK IS KEPT IN ONE ROOM BY ONE PERSON, AND THAT PERSON IS THE ONLY ONE WHO MAY LOOK IN IT, AND THAT ROOM STANDS FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM A YARD A STRANGER CAN WALK UP TO AND WALK IN.**
>
> **A RIVAL RECORD IS ASKED AGAINST. THE BODY WILL ASK IT AGAINST FOUR PLACES AND TWO OF THE FOUR HAVE NOT ANSWERED.**
>
> **THE NOTICE TAKES EFFECT ON THE FIRST DAY OF THE NEXT MONTH.**

The clerk wrote down that a notice printed by a body and pushed under the leg of a table in this district is a document somebody owns, and named the three this district does not own, and entered that the three did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the sixth of the fifth month and that a second notice from a body is not a fourth of them.

The registrar of this district's records office was in that yard and had come on her own account and was not asked a first thing, and she read it a second time standing at the end of the table with her hands behind her back, and did not say anything about it for about a minute, and the wind took the corner of it twice while she was standing there.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing then asked her, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, what she thought a rival record was, and she said it, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and ten, and it is in the minute in her own words.

"**A rival record is a record a body can ask against another record, and it is not a finding and it is not a truth and it is not a charge, and the only thing about it that is a fact is that somebody four hundred miles away has written down the shape of my room. My name is not on that notice. Nobody's name is on that notice. I have read it three times to be certain and there is not one name in it, and I am going to say the rest of this once and then I am not going to say it again in a yard.**"

"**The only line in that notice that has anything to do with me is the second one, and the second one is my own rule, and I wrote it on the twenty-first of the fourth month, and I wrote it before I knew there was a page in my office with a man's name on it or a chair on a table in this yard or a word printed four times on a piece of paper. A book is kept in one room by one person and that person is the only one who may look in it. That is my rule. It is the only rule anybody in this district has ever written down and enforced, and I enforce it, and I have turned people away from that door with it. It is also, this morning, the only reason that notice could be written.**"

"**And nobody has put my name on anything, so I cannot stand in this yard this morning and say that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, which is this yard's rule and a good one and which I would like very much to have today. What is on that notice is not my name. It is my room, and my books, and the fact that I am the only one who may look in them, and all three of those are true, and I wrote two of them myself. I cannot get a true thing off a notice by writing to a body four hundred miles away, because I would be writing to tell them a thing they have already got right, and the day I understand that is the day I stop being angry about it, and I am not at that day.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, and the clerk entered that a registrar is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not a party of the fourth of four, and that a person who has not been asked about a notice has not refused it and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the notice says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the half of it nobody had said, in the ordinary voice, with his arm in the sling, and it was counted, and it came to seventy-nine.

"**A body four hundred miles away has worked out how to find her in about a fortnight, and she has not been asked one question about it by anybody in this yard including me, and every one of us in this yard has spent four months learning how to let a stranger walk up to a book, and a body can walk up to a book, and that is not the same door but it is the same skill.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who has been in this yard for a month saying the same kind of true thing about a page is a man who has noticed the same shape twice, and that a resemblance noticed a second time is not a finding and a third time is a habit, and that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months.

Then the registrar refused the fifth condition with no end on it, before anybody asked her to, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-four, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**A rival record is asked against four places and two of the four have not answered. That is a record that stands until somebody in another county says something, and a thing that stands until a body has finished with it is a condition with no end on it, and there are four of those in this district and I am not ruling a fifth, because a body can be held to a condition and this district has nobody in it to be held.**"

The clerk entered that the four did not move at about eleven in the morning on the sixth of the fifth month, and that a condition whose end is in another county is not a shorter condition, and that a term with no end on it printed on a sheet and a record that stands until a distant body answers it are the same shape of thing.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing then said the thing that was in the room with everybody else and got it out before anybody could, and the count came to a hundred and twenty-five.

"**That notice has got the number four in it and the sheet on this table has got the number four in it and I am not going to let anybody put the two fours together before I have said it. Two documents with a four in them are two documents about two fours. The toll sheet wants four places to pay one toll. The notice wants four places to be asked about one record, and it says two of them have not answered, and one of those two is standing in this yard saying so, and I am not going to be the man who works out which four and I am not going to let anybody work it out of the two sheets either.**"

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam refused the join in the plain voice and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-seven, and it went in the minute in his own words, and neither of the two counts moved.

"**A sheet that wants a toll paid and a notice that wants a record asked against are two instruments with two purposes, and one of them takes money off a house and the other one takes a room, and a room is not money and money is not a room, and an eighth is an eighth and the number of people watching it is not a reason.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the notice out again at about three in the afternoon for about eleven people who had come in after eleven, and got nothing wrong, and the clerk entered that the notice has now been read out in that yard twice and that a notice read out twice is still a notice somebody else printed, and that nobody who has heard it read has been asked a question and the record about all of them says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat was in that yard for about ten minutes on her way to the third house up the lane from the scale, and the wind took the corner of the notice off the leg of the trestle table twice while she was standing there, and the clerk entered that she was there and that nobody sent for her and that she was not asked a first thing, and that a woman who keeps a goat is not a rival record and is not a party of the fourth of four.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was in that yard for about half an hour and stood at the far side of the trestle table with the wind on his back and did not come nearer than he had to, and the clerk entered that he was there and that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing turned the corner of the notice up off the ground with the heel of his hand before he left and put it back under the leg of the table, and the clerk entered that he did that and that nobody asked him to and that the record about the notice says not asked, and that a man putting a paper back where he found it has not agreed with it.

The day came down about half past four and the notice was still under the leg of the trestle table, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was not in that yard on this afternoon and nobody sent for him and the record says not asked.

**AND ON THE SIXTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH A NOTICE WENT UNDER THE LEG OF THE TRESTLE TABLE IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN, IT NAMED NO PLACE AND NO PERSON, AND IT SETS DOWN A RIVAL RECORD ON THE GROUND THAT A BOOK IS KEPT IN ONE ROOM BY ONE PERSON FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM A YARD A STRANGER CAN WALK INTO, AND THAT RULE IS HERS AND SHE WROTE IT ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OF THE FOURTH MONTH.**
