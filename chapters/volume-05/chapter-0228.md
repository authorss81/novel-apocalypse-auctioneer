# Chapter 228: Up The Climb

---

The twenty-ninth of April was the last day before the last day of the month, and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and nobody had sent anybody anywhere, and four men went up the only road in this district that climbs at about five that morning and came down it at about five in the afternoon.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the figures before he went and a man of fifty-six read the board out after him and got both of them right.

"**A hundred and twenty-six days on the board and four hundred and forty-two days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and fifty-six, and the last day of this month is one day off, and four men went up a road at about five this morning, and a clerk is not going to say what they went up it for until they are back down it.**"

It was entered that a man of about thirty at a counter had said four words to the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing at the gate on the twenty-third of this month as he was leaving, and that nobody heard them, and that the man who heard them entered them the next morning, **and that four words at a gate are not a document and are not one of the three documents nobody owns and did not become a fourth.**

The four words are printed under this and the rest of the gate was entered the next morning: that the house they are about is at a road nine miles up, and that the man of about thirty at a counter had said, when he was asked where the house was, that he had asked about it twice in thirty-four miles and had never been given an answer and had stopped asking, and that he was giving them a word and not a name and had never been given a name either.

"**Copied but not carried.**"

It was entered that a man who has asked twice in thirty-four miles and been given nothing and has stopped asking has not refused and has not consented and the record says not asked, **and that a word is not a figure and is not a document and is not a column, and that two houses have now given this district two words for a man and no name for him and the district still has not written it down.**

They went up at about five with a lamp and a slate and a pencil and about nine feet of line, and the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray went because he is the only one of the four who can find the turn in that road, and he was asked about it plainly, in his own words and not in the six things, and he said yes in about four seconds and gave the reason, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked and that he said yes, and that a man who is asked and says yes is not a person who has been taken.

The climb is about four miles and the last half mile of it is not a road. It is a goat track along a shoulder with about two feet of drop on the left for a quarter of a mile at a time, and the light goes early up there even on a clear morning because there is rock on the east side of it, and they were carrying the lamp from about half a mile below the track.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the plainest thing about the climb, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Four miles of it is a road and half a mile of it is a track that a goat uses, and there is a wall on the east side of the track for about a quarter of a mile and the light goes an hour early behind that wall, and we were carrying a lamp from half a mile below it and none of the four of us had been up that road before the twentieth of this month.**"

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam carried it in the right hand the whole way up. At about the top of the climb the lamp began to be a weight and he changed it to his left and then changed it back after about nine minutes, and nobody said anything about it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody said anything about it and that she was told about it at about nine in the evening by the man who had carried it.

The house at the top of the road is a house and not a page, and it is one of the houses that is in the column headed with a word and not a name, and it has kept a thing in the water for four generations and has never been asked to say who kept it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, standing in that doorway, who kept them, and the answer came out of the house in eleven words and a man of about nineteen was not there and did not count it and it went into no minute and no book.

"**Nobody in this house has ever been asked who kept it.**"

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam asked the same question in his own words and not in the six things, and the house gave him a second answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-six, and it went in the minute in the words of the man who asked it and not of the house.

"**We have not had a name for him in four generations and it is not that we have lost it. It is that we have never had it. There was never a page with his name on it. There is a page with our house on it and it goes back further than the four generations.**"

Nobody in that house gave a name. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards, on the track, that a house which has kept a thing for four generations and cannot be asked who kept it is not a house that has forgotten, and that the only name anybody in this basin has ever had for the man who turns one is a word two houses gave this district in five days in the last month, **and that a word is not a name and is not a page and is not a column, and that the ninth place on a page in a cave being ruled and empty is the same shape of nothing as the ninth place on a board in this district and he was saying that before anybody else did.**

The caves are a shoulder of rock with records in them and the records in them are four hundred years old in one case, and the light in there is a lamp and a doorway, and a house that has never let the caves be emptied will let four men in and hold a slate and will not let a page out.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked about that and was given seven words and then a longer sentence that nobody counted, and the longer sentence was that the caves have never been emptied and are not going to be emptied by four men with a slate.

"**A page is copied and not carried.**"

He said the seven words out loud twice on the track going down so that a clerk would have them twice, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said them twice and that the two are one thing said twice and not two things.

The man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing said what the doorway was like before he copied anything, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**It is a doorway and not a room and the lamp is on the step and the wind comes round the corner of it every nine or ten minutes and puts the flame over, and I copied with my left hand on the slate and my right on the pencil, and a man cannot do that for fifty minutes and I did it for fifty minutes.**"

The man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing copied one page in about fifty minutes in a doorway with a lamp in it, in his own hand, and the page has nine places on it and eight of them carry a name, and he would not read the eight names out in the doorway and would not read them out in a yard either.

The clerk entered that he was asked and that he refused with a reason, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and that the eight did not move.

The reason he gave, sitting on the step with the slate on his knee, was that a name said once in a yard is a name four hundred people have and a name said twice is a name a counter can ask for, and that he had learned that rule in this district in the last month off a woman of fifty-eight, and that he was not going to be the man who used it on eight people who were not in the room.

They came down at about five. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam put the lamp down at the bottom of the climb and could not get his fingers to close on it for about a minute, and the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray took it off him without being asked and carried it the rest of the way, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he took it without being asked and that a man who is not asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way and the record says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who mends a dray said the thing he had said at the top of that road, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**There is a house at the top of that road with a wall round a shoulder of rock behind it and a man was copying a page in a doorway with a lamp on the step, and I have been up that road three times since the eleventh of this month and I did not know any of that until this morning, and I am not going to be asked about it here.**"

And it went in that he was not asked about it here and that he had been asked about it on the twenty-second in a room and had answered nothing.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said at the gate that the copy is a copy and not a fourth document nobody owns, and that this district now holds a copy of a stranger's schedule, a copy of the standing orders of the nine and a copy of a page out of a cave, **and that all three of them are copies a man made in this district for nothing, and that the three documents nobody owns are three and did not move, and that a man of about twenty-three who has made three copies in a month in his own hand and been paid for none of them is the reason this district can put a printed day in a book and a page four hundred years old on a slate, and that the district is still not going to pay him and has now said so four times.**

Nobody said the opposite. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that the hand of a man of about twenty-three is the hand that has been doing this district's work since the sixth of this month, and that a district with one hand doing all of its copying and no money for it is a district with one finger on three documents, **and that the six instruments this district has built and not named are a rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security, and that a hand is not a seventh of them and did not become one at about half past five in the afternoon on the twenty-ninth of the fourth month.**

The day came down and the slate was on the trestle table under the second sheet, and the man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing went home with his hand open, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that his hand was open and that she was told about it by the man of about thirty-one who digs loam and not by the man it belonged to, **and that a person who has not been asked about his hand has not refused to say anything about it, and the record says not asked.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH FOUR MEN WENT UP THE ONLY ROAD IN THIS DISTRICT THAT CLIMBS AND CAME DOWN IT WITH A SLATE AND A COPY OF ONE PAGE THAT A HOUSE AT A ROAD NINE MILES UP WOULD NOT LET LEAVE, AND THE PAGE HAS NINE PLACES ON IT AND EIGHT NAMES, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE WHO COPIES FOR NOTHING DECLINED TO READ THE EIGHT NAMES OUT.**
