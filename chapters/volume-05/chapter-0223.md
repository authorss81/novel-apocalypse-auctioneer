# Chapter 223: A Room With A Door In It

---

The twenty-fourth of April was the fifth day since the fourth holding took water in the night of the nineteenth into the twentieth, and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the second sheet was still lying on the trestle table with the chair standing beside it where a man of about twenty-three had left it.

The three figures went out loud early, and a man of fifty-six read the board over the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing's shoulder and got both of the other two right, and nobody in that yard counted how many days in a row that man had read a board he had not been asked to read.

"**A hundred and twenty-one days on the board and four hundred and thirty-seven days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and fifty-one, and the last day of this month is six days off, and a man went to the records office at about half past eight this morning and came back at about eleven and he is going to say why.**"

The clerk of nineteen years entered that he said he was going to say why and that he had not said it, and that she was not asked to, and that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move at about ten in the morning on the twenty-fourth of the fourth month.

Nobody asked him anything else about it. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in the yard with his right arm back in the sling, and it was entered that he had asked for it back and that the woman of fifty-eight had not been asked whether she wanted him to have it back.

At about two in the afternoon the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the reason out loud in the yard, in front of about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A single holder is a thing nobody in four districts can ask a question, and the only instrument this basin has that a stranger can walk up to is a yard, and a yard is a door, and a chair over a charter is a door somebody else holds the key to, and I have said that in a room this morning and I am saying it here so that it is in a book.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who says a thing in a room and then says it again in a yard has said it twice, and that two is not a habit, and that the first one is in a room with a shut door in it and a clerk cannot enter what was said in a room she was not standing in, and that the whole instrument of this district is a page.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man who repeats himself on purpose so that one of the two repetitions is in a book is doing the thing this district was built to do and that he has not seen a man do it before in four months, and that a man who says a true thing in a room and then buys himself a witness is a man who has worked out what a witness is, and that nobody is going to put that in a column.

---

The records office is four hundred yards from the yard. It is a room with one door, and a fanlight over the door, and the door shuts, and the draught off it goes across the floor because the floor is stone and the stove in the corner has not been lit since the twentieth of the third month.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against asked for a clerk in it. He asked for that in a yard on the nineteenth of this month and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing entered the asking and nobody contradicted him, and the asking is in the book.

The registrar of this district's records office said that the clerk is nineteen years old and is at the ward market and has not left it in four months, and that she was not going to have somebody walk four hundred yards in the middle of a working morning to stand in a room and write down two sentences, and that a person in a room is not a clerk and cannot enter anything, **and that this is the reason a room is the right place to say a thing like this and is not the wrong one, and that the same rule keeps a stranger out of a book and it is a good rule and it is hers.**

There is one chair in that room besides the one by the door. It has a wooden back and one arm that has come loose and is tied to the seat with a length of line, and it is the chair she sits in and the chair her predecessor sat in and the chair nobody has ever been offered.

The books are on two shelves along the north wall and on the table, and the table is the same table he wrote the twenty-first years on and then did not write anything under, and the eight printed days off a stranger's sheet are in a book on the upper shelf in a hand that is hers and the nine printed nights off a body's own paper are in the same book nine leaves further on, and a person may look at both in that room and may not carry either out of it, which is her rule and was written on the twenty-first of this month and is on the inside cover of that book in the same hand as both of them.

Nobody has ever sat in that chair opposite anybody. She said so, unasked, and a man of about nineteen was not in the room and did not count it.

She asked him the question herself, in the ordinary voice, and she did not read it out of the sheet, and he stood, and she did not ask him to sit down.

She asked whether he would put his name in the ruled place on the second sheet and sit in the chair over the water for the four districts under it, and whether he understood what was being asked, and he said he did.

He said no. He said it in a room with the door shut and there was nobody in that room to hear it except her, and a man of about nineteen was not there and did not count it and a clerk of nineteen years was not there and could not enter it. The reason was a hundred and six words and he gave it once.

"**I will not put a name in that place and I will not sit in that chair, and I am going to give you the reason once and I am not going to give it to a yard afterwards. A single holder cannot be asked a question by anybody, and a charter with no end on it that begins tomorrow decides what is sown in the season after this one, and the four districts under that water would not be asked about either, and there is nobody in this room to ask. My name is the only name this district has at the back of a book, and a name at the back of a book is a person a toll can be fastened to, and I have been at the back of that book since the twenty-seventh of last month and I know what it feels like from the wrong side, and I am not going to be the reason it feels like that to somebody in the Uplands.**"

At about half past ten he put his hand on the back of the chair by the door, where a man puts a hand on a door frame on his way out, and he did not sit in it, and she did not offer it and did not say anything about the hand.

The registrar did not argue and did not agree. She asked him one thing, and she asked it because she wanted an answer and not because a form wanted one, and she did not write it down, and it is not in a book.

"**Then somebody in this district is going to be a figure on a page by the last day of this month, because a letter wants a man and a lease wants a man, and I am the only one who can put a name down, and I am not going to put one down that nobody has said to me.**"

---

He came back into the yard at about eleven and stood at the gate and did not go through it for about a minute, and a man of fifty-six said he was not asked about it and was not going to be, and that a man who stops at a gate he has walked out of every morning for four months is a man who has been somewhere else this morning.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the part nobody had said, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A room with a shut door is the only place this district has found where a man can be told no to a thing and mean it, and a yard is the only place this district has found where nineteen people can hear him say it, and he has now been in both, and that is two instruments in a week and neither of them has a name.**"

The clerk entered that both of the two things said in that room were said to a clerk of nineteen years afterwards and not in it, and that a shut door is not a page, **and that a sentence said behind one is not a document and is not one of the three documents nobody owns, and that the three did not move at about one in the afternoon on the twenty-fourth of the fourth month, and that a room with a shut door in it is the only place this district has found where a person can be told no to a thing and have the no be true, and that a room with a shut door in it is a place where a sentence goes to die and not a book.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the man standing at that gate is the man who is going to be a figure on somebody's page by the last day of this month, and that the two sentences are one fact told by two people to two rooms, and that a resemblance noticed a second time is not a finding and a third time is a habit, and that he had noticed it twice today and was saying so before anybody else got to it.

It was entered that the man of about thirty-four who digs loam has said that sentence in a yard on the twelfth, the seventeenth, the twentieth and the twenty-first of this month and once today, **and that a fifth time is a habit of a habit, and that he is not going to say it a sixth time and she is not going to ask him to.**

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the second sheet out again at about four in the afternoon for four people who had come in after eleven, and got nothing wrong, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the second sheet has been read out in that yard three times in three days and that a sheet read out three times is still one sheet that somebody else wrote.

The four people who came in after eleven were a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat, a man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, a man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing, and a man of about nineteen who counts, and the man of about twenty-three said the word founder out loud when the second line was read and then said he had said it because it is on the sheet and not because he had anything to say about it.

And it went in that the word on the sheet is a word this district has not had on a page of its own until this month, and that a body that prints a title under a line of its own paper is not asking this yard's permission, and that a word is not a figure and is not a document and is not a column and is not one of the six instruments this district built and not named.

The day came down at about five and the chair by the trestle table had not been sat in by anybody on the second day either, and the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray was in the forge at the end off Salt Row with a yoke in it and a strip of paper on the bench beside him, and nobody sent for him and nobody asked him anything, and the record says not asked.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH A MAN REFUSED A CHAIR IN A ROOM WITH A SHUT DOOR IN IT AND GAVE A HUNDRED AND SIX WORDS FOR IT AND A CLERK COULD NOT ENTER IT, AND HE SAID THE WHOLE OF IT AGAIN IN A YARD IN THE AFTERNOON SO THAT ONE OF THE TWO WOULD BE IN A BOOK, AND A REGISTRAR SAID IN THAT ROOM THAT SHE IS NOT GOING TO PUT A NAME DOWN THAT NOBODY HAS SAID TO HER.**
