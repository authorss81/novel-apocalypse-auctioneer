# Chapter 238: What He Said About It

---

The tenth of the fifth month came in grey from the west and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in it with his right arm in a sling that was not his.

The sling came from the woman of fifty-eight, who is in that yard most mornings and was in it this morning. The clerk entered that she lent it and that nobody asked her to and that the record says not asked, and that a thing a person lends is a thing a person has not given away.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out after him and got the other two right, and the yard was quiet in a way it has not been quiet on a morning in a month, and about nine of the nineteen people in it were standing on the side of the yard away from the gate as though they had agreed to and had not.

"**A hundred and thirty-seven days on the board and four hundred and fifty-three days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and sixty-seven, and the twelfth of this month is two days off, and a man was not in this yard yesterday and he is in it today and I am not going to ask him about it until he has had a cup of something in his hand.**"

The woman of fifty-eight said one thing about the sling before anybody asked anybody anything, and nobody counted it because nobody was asked to, and the clerk entered that she said it and that it went into the minute unnumbered.

She said that a sling is the same piece of cloth on the fourth day as it is on the first, and that she has lent three of them in a month and has had two of them back, and that the third one is on a man of thirty-four and not on her, and that a yard in this basin has four things a person is asked about and none of them is a piece of cloth.

He had had a cup of something in his hand by about half past ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him then, in his own words and not in the six things, what had happened to his arm. The answer was counted and it came to a hundred and fifty-two.

"**I carried a barrow up a bank on the fourth of this month at the near shaft and I told this yard at the time that I was fine at the bottom of it and not fine now, and then I told the truth about it, which is that I was not fine at the bottom of it either and I could feel it going on the last four or five steps and I said fine because it was nine people and a barrow. On the eighth I put my hand into the channel to feel the bottom of a sill because a man of about forty-one asked me to, and on the ninth I was not in this yard at all, and I want it written down that nobody sent anybody to find out where I was on the ninth and that is correct and I would have said so myself.**"

The clerk entered that a man who goes a day without coming to a yard has not refused the yard and cannot be counted either way, that the record about the ninth says not asked, and that a shoulder is not an instrument and is not a figure about a man and is not a column, **and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the tenth of the fifth month, and that a woman lent a sling and has not been thanked and did not ask to be.**

He sat down on the step of the building at the end of the lane for about two minutes before he came back into the yard, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the gate and did not follow him and was not asked to, and the clerk entered that he was let and that nobody asked him to be let and that the record about the two minutes says not asked.

Then he said the thing that was the whole of the ten days, and he said it in the ordinary voice, standing in the yard with a sling on, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A lock is the only instrument in this basin that has never needed a person. Not one. There is a pin in it that takes nothing and a frame that holds a road shut on the weight of the water and a screw that is a lever and not a wall, and the whole of it works in the middle of an ordinary night with nobody anywhere near it, and every one of the nine of them will do it again tomorrow without asking any of us for anything.**"

"**And I am not going to stand in this yard and use our own rule about people on a piece of iron. Our rule is that a person who has not been asked has not refused, and that rule is true and it is about people, and iron is not people. The honest way to say what has happened in this basin for forty days is that nine structures in the water above four districts have been standing open on eight of eleven nights, one a night, since the first of April, and nobody in this district has been to look at one of them. Not once. We have stood in this yard and given a number out loud every morning and we have never walked to a lock.**"

"**And the ninth of the nine printed nights was the twentieth of last month, and this district has said for three weeks that it cannot say whether that night happened, and it cannot say whether that night happened because nobody has been, and the reason nobody has been is that a lock cannot be asked, so there is nobody to ask, so it is nobody's.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him one question about it in his own words and not in the six things, which is how a man asks a question when he wants a fact and not a speech, and the answer was counted, and it came to ninety-nine.

"**Eight of eleven nights, one a night, since the first of April, and nobody opened them, and I have been in a yard every morning of that saying a number out loud and reading it back off a board that has not been washed since the twenty-fourth of December, and I could have walked to one of them in a morning. Any of us could have. That is the thing I am saying and I have not said it before, and I have had a day off and a day of a channel and I have said it now.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has been in a yard every morning for a month has said out loud that he could have walked four miles in a morning, and that a thing a man could have done and did not do is not a refusal and is not a consent and is not a count, and that the record about the eight of eleven nights says nothing was found because nobody went.

A man of fifty-six said that a man who has spent nine days saying the same shape of true sentence in a yard has made a habit of it and that a habit of a true sentence is still a true sentence, and that a lock is not a party to anything and a thing that cannot be asked is not a thing that has refused, and that the record about the nine of them is not a refusal and is not a consent and says nothing was found because nobody went.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing then said the part about the nine, and the count came to ninety-nine.

"**Nine of them. Not eight and not five. A district that has spent four months proving that one holder cannot be asked a question by anybody has nine of the things sitting up there that do not need to be asked a question by anybody at all, and a body four hundred miles away has offered one man the lot of them in a sheet with a chair printed on it, and the whole of what I know about the difference between that sheet and those nine is that those nine work and the sheet has not been tried.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the notice out for about nine people who had come in after eleven, and got nothing wrong for the fourth time, and the clerk entered that the notice has now been read out in that yard four times in five days and that nobody who has heard it read has been asked a question about it, and that four readings is not four consents and is not a column.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was in the yard and had made a fourth copy that week, of the notice, in about twenty minutes, in his own hand, for nothing, and he put it on the table and did not say anything about it, and the clerk entered that a copy is a copy and is not a document and is not a bearer and is not a figure about a person, and that the three documents this district does not own are three and did not move at about eleven in the morning on the tenth of the fifth month.

The man about thirty-four who keeps a road walked through that yard at about half past twelve on his way to somewhere else and did not stop, and the clerk entered that he was in the yard and that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing, and that he has been asked one question in this district since the first of this month and has given one answer and that a man who has been asked one question and given one answer is not a man who has been asked.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in that yard and had been in it since about half past ten and had not been asked a first thing, and he said one thing before anybody had thought to ask him, and it was counted, and it came to eighty-nine.

"**I do not know what to do about four places and a notice and a pin, and I am not going to stand in this yard and say that I do, because the last man who said that he did knew four days after that the man who turned out to be right was a man of about forty-one with his thumb in a hole in a sill, and a yard that is told a plan by anybody is a yard that has stopped being able to tell one.**"

Nobody said the opposite. A man of fifty-six said that a man of twenty-one years who has said out loud in a yard twice this month that he does not know something is not a man without a plan, he is a man who has found out that the finding out is the work, and that the boards in this district are five and the full lines are six and the seventh line is ruled and empty, **and that nobody has put a line on a board on any of the forty days since the first of the fourth month and a clerk entered that at about half past four in the afternoon on the tenth of the fifth month.**

The man of about fifty-five who keeps a building four miles down a lane sent nothing and nobody sent anything to him, and the two chalk lines on the inside of that brick are still there, and the crack is about the length of his hand, and he has still not asked anybody for a fifth mend in four months, and a clerk of nineteen years entered all five of those things at about four in the afternoon and entered that the man who drew two lines on the inside of a tank in the dark is not a clerk of a house and is not on a page and is not a bearer of anything, and that the record about the tank says not asked.

The day came down and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam went up the bank with his arm in a sling and stopped halfway and stood there for about a minute looking at the channel, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the gate and let him, and it was entered that he was let and that nobody asked him to let him and that the record says not asked.

**AND ON THE TENTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM CAME BACK INTO THAT YARD AFTER A DAY OUT OF IT AND SAID THAT A LOCK IS THE ONLY INSTRUMENT IN THIS BASIN THAT HAS NEVER NEEDED A PERSON, AND THAT NINE OF THEM WILL DO IT AGAIN TOMORROW WITHOUT ASKING ANY OF US FOR ANYTHING, AND THAT NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAS BEEN TO LOOK AT ONE OF THEM.**
