# Chapter 218: The Night Before

---

The nineteenth of April was the day before the ninth of the nine, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the figures out loud and a man of fifty-six read the board out afterwards and got both of them right.

"**A hundred and sixteen days on the board and four hundred and thirty-two days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and forty-six, and the last day of this month is eleven days off and the ninth of the nine nights is tomorrow and this district has no instrument for tomorrow and did not build one on the second of January and is not building one today.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a day a stranger has printed is a day a person can be asked about, and that a day in her book is a day a person can be asked about, and that the ninth of the nine nights went into that book on the fourteenth of this month in a printed column headed with a word and not a name on it, **and that this basin has never had a book in which a printed day could be found by anybody who wanted to find it, and that this district put one there on purpose in the third week of the month, and that a district which puts a day in a book and cannot get anybody to the day is the ordinary case and has been for eighteen months.**

---

It came at about eleven, unasked, into the same place the others come into, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard asked for it and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it.

> **A DRAY ON A ROAD IS A CART. A CART IS NOT A BEARER. A PERSON ON A CART IS A PERSON AND NOT A FIGURE.**
>
> **A ROAD WITH NO TERM ON IT IS A PLACE AND NOT AN INSTRUMENT, AND A PLACE MAY BE WALKED AND MAY NOT BE ASKED.**
>
> **A BODY THAT ASKS FOR A MAN CANNOT BE ANSWERED WITH A NAME, AND A NAME ON A LINE IS A NAME OFF A SHEET.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds and nobody disagreed with it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody disagreed and that she is not going to make anything of it, **and that this is the second of these in this volume that nobody in this yard has disagreed with, and that the first was on the tenth of this month, which is nine days ago, and that two rules going by without an argument in nine days is not a rule this district obeys and cannot un-obey, because the people in this yard say what they have and there is a man in it who has said what he has twice.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half of it, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked to.

"**I have disagreed with one of these in fourteen days and I have not disagreed with one, and the one I have not disagreed with is the one about a person, and I am not going to say any more about that today than I have just said.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam has been the plainest person in every yard in this district for four months and has now said the least in one, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and that she did not ask him to, **and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and a rule is not a seventh of them and did not become one at about a quarter past eleven in the morning on the nineteenth of the fourth month.**

---

The man of about thirty-one said the thing at about half past eleven and said it before anybody asked him and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked and that she is not going to put a number on it.

"**I am going up at about half past five tomorrow morning on my own account and I am not taking anybody, and the man who went up with me on the eleventh is not on this cart either and I did not ask him to come down and I am not going to be asked about it this morning.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who says *I am going to be asked about it* before anybody has asked him is a man who has learned something in a month, **and that a dray on a road on somebody's account is not a toll and is not one of the four conditions with no end on it and is not a fifth, and that those four are four, and that a letter with a seal on it that asks for an answer by the last day of this month has a day on it and is the opposite of a condition with no end on it, and she did not rule one and is not going to.**

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the thing nobody had asked him for, and it is the thing the whole of the last ten days has been standing behind.

"**Nobody in this district can reach the people who keep them, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing has a road, and a man of about thirty at a counter has a house thirty-four miles east, and a woman who keeps a goat has a door she stood in the dark on the eleventh, and a yard has about nineteen people in it, and the whole of what this yard has got is a yard and a book four hundred yards away and a man who is going up a road in the morning, and I would like somebody to write that down in my own words and I have stopped expecting it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he asked for it to be written down in his own words and that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself eleven times in four months, **and that the asking is entered and that the not-writing is entered for the second time in nine days, and that the refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading, and that a man asking to be written down for the second time in nine days is not a man who is going to get it and has not stopped asking and that the two are not a column.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in that yard at about one in the afternoon and had not been sent for, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, whether this district will send a man to a council.

He said the honest answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I am not going to say no in a yard about a thing nobody has asked me properly, and a body that wants a man out of this district has to come to this district and put the question to me in a room with a clerk in it, and I am not going to be a man who is sent to a council because about nineteen people stood in a yard and somebody read out a letter.**"

Nothing was said for about nine seconds. A man of fifty-six entered that this is the fourth time a man in this yard has refused something and given a reason in the same breath, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, **and that the count of eight did not move at about one in the afternoon on the nineteenth of the fourth month, and that a man who says he will not be sent to a council by a yard has not said he will not go to a council and cannot be counted either way and the record does not say not asked because he was asked and he answered.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the half of it that nobody had said, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The letter says it wants a man and not a bearer and not a name and not a holder, and it is the only paper anybody in this basin has sent us that asks for a person without a column to print one in, and this district has spent a month finding out that it cannot produce a person on demand, and the reason it cannot is a woman who said eighty-two words in this yard on the eighth of this month and was right, and I am not going to be the man who undoes that in a letter.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and that she was not asked to, and that a body which asks for a man and not a name cannot be answered by a page and cannot be refused by a page, **and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and neither of them moved at about ten past two in the afternoon on the nineteenth of the fourth month, and that a second refusal of the same join is the same refusal.**

The afternoon was the scale and the four houses and the woman of fifty-eight and her eleven, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, how she was going to carry eleven houses' water on one yoke, and she said the honest answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-four, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**One at a time, eleven times, and I have been doing one at a time eleven times since the ninth of this month anyway.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that a woman has now said out loud in a yard that she is going to do the same unpaid thing she has been doing for a month, **and that nobody in this yard has a figure for it and that there is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers, and that there never has been one, and that a district which cannot pay for a day has now been told twice in a month that a woman will do it anyway and that is not a wage and is not a promise and is not a fifth of anything.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a woman saying *I will do it anyway* is not a consent and is not a refusal and cannot be counted either way, **and that the record says not asked, and that this district has four columns of not-askings and has had them since the second of January and did not rule a fifth today and is not going to, and that a woman in a yard saying she will work for nothing is the single most dangerous sentence anybody has said in this yard in four months and the clerk is not going to rule a column for it and I am asking her to.**

Nobody ruled one.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was in that yard at about four in the afternoon and was not sent for and was not asked anything, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud that he was owed eleven coppers a week and had been refused four times for the same reason and had said he would not say it a fifth.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fourth refusal for the same reason is not a fourth refusal, and that the man is owed nothing and is owed a figure for what he does and that there is no figure, **and that a man of about twenty-three who copied nine printed nights onto the back of a sheet of his own buying in about fifty minutes on the sixth of this month is the reason this district can put a printed day in a book and know what it is, and that he is not going to be paid for it and has said so three times, and that a man who is not going to be paid for a thing and says so three times is not a man anybody can buy and is not a security and is not one of the six instruments this district built and not named.**

Nobody said the opposite. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man who does a thing for nothing and says three times that he expects nothing is the cheapest thing this district has ever got, **and that the district is not going to pay him, and that both of those are entered, and that neither of them is a finding and neither of them is a rate and there is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers.**

**AND ON THE NINETEENTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH A PANEL CAME INTO A YARD THAT NOBODY HAD ASKED FOR AND NOBODY DISAGREED WITH, A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE SAID HE WAS GOING UP THE ROAD AT ABOUT HALF PAST FIVE IN THE MORNING ON HIS OWN ACCOUNT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM ASKED FOR THE LAST TRUE THING HE HAS SAID TO BE WRITTEN DOWN IN HIS OWN WORDS FOR THE SECOND TIME IN EIGHT DAYS AND IT WAS NOT WRITTEN DOWN.**
