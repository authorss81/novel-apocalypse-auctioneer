# Chapter 225: The Room With The Door Shut

---

The twenty-sixth of April was the day after a man had said a thing in a room and had come out of it, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the three figures and the man of fifty-six read them back off the board, and the board has not been washed since the twenty-fourth of December and both of the figures on it are right.

"**A hundred and twenty-three days on the board and four hundred and thirty-nine days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and fifty-three, and the last day of this month is four days off, and a man said a thing in a room last night and did not say it in a yard, and one of us is going to have to.**"

He had been on the step of that office from about half past five yesterday afternoon until the door opened at about nine, and he had eaten nothing and he had walked four hundred yards there and four hundred back in the cold, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody sent him and that nobody asked him to and that the record says not asked.

At about a quarter past ten, in front of about nineteen people, he said what the man of about thirty-one had said to him at the gate the night before, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and eleven, and it went in the minute in his own words and not in the other man's.

"**A page in a Registry's hand has got his name on it against this district's records office and it says he serves it, and the name went onto that page on the second of this month, the day he gave a name at a counter that takes names off sheets, and he read the page on the twenty-second in the room where the books are with the registrar in the room, and he has said nothing to anybody for three days and has told me and me only, and I am saying it here because I have been in this yard for four months and I know what a page is.**"

Nobody picked it up. A man of fifty-six said that a man who repeats a thing in a yard that was given to him at a gate in the dark is now a second reader of a document he has not seen, and that a copy of a thing told to him is not the thing, and that a Registry's page is a document this district has got and did not write and cannot produce.

The registrar of this district's records office was in that yard and had come on her own account and was not asked a first thing, and she said one thing, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I have seen it. I may not carry it out of this office and I may not give it to a man of about nineteen and I may not read it into a yard, and that rule is mine, I wrote it on the twenty-first of this month, and I wrote it before I knew there was anything on it, and I am not lifting it today for a man who has done nothing wrong and has been paid nothing.**"

It was entered that the rule was written on the twenty-first of this month in a book four hundred yards from that yard, and that a rule a registrar writes about her own room is not a rule this district voted on, and that it is the only rule in this district anybody has ever written down and enforced, and that a man who would have been protected by it is not protected by it today.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the shape of it in the ordinary voice with his right arm in the sling, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and thirty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That is the third column this district has found in a month that is headed with a word and not a name, and the first carried a house nine times and a person no times, and this one carries a man once, and a column is a column, and a man who is in one is not in it because anybody asked him. What is in front of this yard is not that a man has been stolen. It is that a name went off one sheet at a counter on the second of this month and onto a page in a body four hundred miles away that nobody in this district has ever been inside, and nobody told the man whose name it was, and the page says he serves an office he has never been given.**"

Nobody said the word abuse. It was entered that nobody said it and that the clerk was not going to say it either, and that a word a body has for a thing is not a thing this district has, and that the six instruments it built and not named did not become a seventh because a man said a long sentence in a yard.

Then the man of about thirty-one who mends a dray came into that yard at about half past eleven and had not been sent for and was not asked anything, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him one question, and it was not in the six things, and he gave the honest answer, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I did the work. I carried a ledger from that room to the counter at the end of the lane and back on the twenty-second, and I did it because the man who keeps that counter was not well and because I was in the room and it was in my arms, and I have carried things for that office nine or ten times since the second of this month and nobody asked me to and I would have carried them anyway. I am not a man of that office. Nobody has ever given me a job in it and nobody has ever paid me a penny of it.**"

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam asked him, in front of about nineteen people, what he thought he was going to get out of saying it in a yard, and the man of about thirty-one answered in twenty-five words, which a man of about nineteen counted, and nobody argued with him.

"**I thought somebody would take my name off it. I was wrong about that, and I knew it was wrong, and I said it anyway.**"

The clerk entered that the name of a man put on a figure cannot be taken off that figure by the man it is put on, and that that rule is this district's own and it has been said out loud in that yard before, **and that the man who said it out loud on the nineteenth of this month said it about somebody else, and that a man who says a true thing out loud today and finds out this afternoon that the saying of it has made the name harder to take off is a man who has paid for saying it and has lost nothing that was his.**

A man of fifty-six said that a man of about thirty-one has counted his own carrying for an office that has not paid him once and got nine or ten, and that a man keeping a tally of his own unpaid labour is a man keeping a tally and not a clerk, and that a clerk of nineteen years cannot count another man's days for him and is not going to.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the man of about thirty-one did not lose the ledger work and lost the right to be a stranger four hundred yards from his own forge, **and that from this morning a person in four districts can say the name of that man and a counter can ask for it, and that the name was askable from the second of April and is now findable, and that the finding out is the price, and that the price is not a figure anybody in that yard can pay him back with.**

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said what three and a half hours on a step is, in the ordinary voice, and nobody counted it because he did not ask, and he said that the door of that room has a fanlight over it and the fanlight does not light anything, and that he could see about nine feet of lane and the bottom of the step and a bucket that was not his.

The registrar said then what she was going to do about it, in the ordinary voice, and she did it in front of about nineteen people because she had decided to and not because anybody asked, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-two, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I am going to enter in my own book that a page exists, that it is in a hand that is not mine, that a person has read it in this room, and that I may not produce it and may not carry it out. That is four lines and none of them is the name, and it is the whole of what this room can do, and it is not nothing.**"

The four lines were written out in the yard in the ordinary voice by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote them down in her own book and not in the market's, and a man of about nineteen counted the lines and got four, and it was entered that four is a figure and that four is not a page about a man and is not a page about anybody.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that four lines with no name in them is the most this district has ever put on a page about a man, and that it is a page about a man without the man on it, and that he was not going to say what that was, and that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk.

The man of about thirty-one said one more thing before he went, and he said it to the yard and not to anybody, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have not slept two nights running and there is a day of the week I could tell you and it would not help you, and I am not going to say who gave me the sheet on the road and I never was, and that is the last thing I am going to be asked about today.**"

She entered that a man said two nights and that she was not going to put a day of the week on a date in this district, and that a man who has not slept two nights is not a man who has been asked a question and is not in any of the seven lists this district keeps.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the whole of what this yard has now, and it was counted and it came to sixty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A sentence in a man's own words in a yard is the best instrument this district has, and a sentence in a man's own words in a yard is not a page, and the whole of the difference between this district and a body four hundred miles away is that this one can be stood in front of and that one cannot.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went to the forge end off Salt Row at about half past four to say the thing to the man it belonged to, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he went and that he was not sent for and that he came back at about ten past five and that he did not say what had been said in the forge.

The day came down about five and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had not eaten and had not slept and had walked eight hundred yards in a day and a half, and it was entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and that a man who said a true thing in a yard this morning and cannot be paid back for it is the fifth of the five and did not move at about five in the afternoon on the twenty-sixth of the fourth month.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE SAID IN A YARD THAT A REGISTRY'S PAGE CARRIES HIS NAME AGAINST THIS DISTRICT'S RECORDS OFFICE AND THAT HE HAS DONE THE WORK AND BEEN GIVEN NO JOB, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT A NAME PUT ON A FIGURE CANNOT BE TAKEN OFF IT BY THE MAN IT IS PUT ON, AND A REGISTRAR SAID SHE MAY NOT CARRY THE PAGE OUT OF HER OWN OFFICE AND ENTERED FOUR LINES THAT DO NOT CARRY THE NAME.**
