# Chapter 237: Four Hundred Yards

---

The ninth of the fifth month was still and pale and the wind had gone round to the east, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning and one of the people who is usually in it at that hour was not in it.

The clerk of nineteen years wrote that down first, before anything else, in the ordinary voice, and it went into the minute.

"**That the man of about thirty-four who digs loam was not in that yard this morning and was not in it at about two in the afternoon either, and that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing, and that a person who is not asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the record says not asked.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and thirty-six days on the board and four hundred and fifty-two days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and sixty-six, and the twelfth of this month is three days off, and there is a man who has been in this yard every morning for a fortnight and is not in it, and I am not going to say the reason and I do not know it.**"

A man of about nineteen said one thing about that, and it was counted because nobody had said not to, and it came to sixty-eight.

"**A yard is a room with no roof and I have been counting in it every morning since the second of April and this morning I counted nothing before about eleven, and it is the first morning since the second of April that I have counted nothing, and I am saying that and not making anything of it, and I do not know what I would count instead.**"

Then the man of about thirty-four who keeps a tally for six households said the thing that was in the room with everybody else, and he said it to nobody in particular, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and seven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A body four hundred miles away is going to ask four places a question, and I keep the count of six households, and three of my six have not been asked anything since the second month, and I have told this yard that twice. I am not going to work out which of my six is in which of the four and I am not going to let anybody do it out loud, and if a body asks one of my six a question that is a body asking and not this yard, and my six will know the difference, because I have told them the difference.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who has told six households the difference between a body asking and a yard asking has done more in four months than a yard has, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the ninth of the fifth month, and that a household that has been told something is a household and not a party of the fourth of four.

The man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was in that yard for the first time in a fortnight and stood at the back of it with his hands behind him, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not ask him about the chair, and the man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said the thing himself, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and nine.

"**I have four houses standing empty and I have had them standing empty since the last day of last month, and the reason they are empty is not the water and it is not a chair. It is that nobody has asked anybody in those four houses a single question. I have been asked four times in eighteen days what a chair is worth and I have been asked nothing at all about four houses, and if a body four hundred miles away is going to start asking places questions in three weeks, then the first thing anybody in this basin has to do is go and ask somebody.**"

It went in that a man keeping four empty houses and a man keeping a count of six households are two men keeping two counts and that neither of them is an instrument this district built and not named and that the six did not become a seventh of them, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and that the eight did not move at about eleven in the morning on the ninth of the fifth month, and that the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and did not move and the two are not the same figure.

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat was in that yard for about twenty minutes and said nothing that anybody has written down, and the clerk entered that she was there and that nobody sent for her and that she was not asked a first thing about the sheet or the notice or the four places.

---

The records office is four hundred yards from that yard. The door has a fanlight over it and the fanlight does not light anything, and the stove in the corner has not been lit since the twentieth of the third month, and the draught goes across the stone floor from the door in a straight line to the corner whichever way the wind is.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against went there at about half past eight that morning, before the yard, with a copy of the notice that a man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing had made in his own hand in about twenty minutes and for nothing, and a copy is a copy and is not one of the three documents this district does not own.

He asked for the notice to be read into her book. She refused and gave the reason and a man of about nineteen was not in that room and did not count it, and the reason was that a rule she wrote on the twenty-first of the fourth month says a person may look at a document in that office and may not carry it out, and that she will not put a copy of a notice into a book a stranger can walk up to, and that a copy in a book would be a thing a stranger could ask against her office, and a rival record is asked against.

He did not argue with her about it. He put his hand on the back of the chair by the door the way he had on the twenty-fourth of last month and he did not sit in it, and he said the two things he had been wrong about, both of them, in that room, and he said them in about four minutes, and he gave no reason for either of them because he had given both reasons in front of other people twice.

She asked him one thing, and she asked it because she wanted an answer and not because anything wanted one, and she did not write it down, and it is not in a book.

"**Why are you telling me and not the yard.**"

"**Because the yard will write it down and you will say what you are going to do about it, and I do not know yet what either of us is going to do, and there is one page in this district I am allowed to say a thing in that is not a book and I have been putting things in it for four months and there is nothing in it yet.**"

She said nothing to that. The clerk entered afterwards, from the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, that two people had been in that room at about half past eight in the morning and that she was told about it by one of them and not by the other, and that a room with a shut door in it is a place where a sentence goes to die and not a book, **and that she entered that he came out of that room with his face shut the way he comes out of it, and that the two things in it were a question and an answer and neither of them is in a book, and that a sentence said behind a shut door is not a document and did not become a fourth of the three.**

---

The yard had about nineteen people in it again at about four in the afternoon and something arrived that nobody had asked for, and the clerk entered that nobody in that yard asked for it and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it.

> **A NOTICE NAMES NO PERSON. A NOTICE IS EFFECTIVE ON A DAY. A PERSON IS AFFECTED BY A NOTICE ON THE DAY IT IS EFFECTIVE AND NOT BEFORE.**
>
> **AN OFFICE THAT KEEPS ITS BOOKS IN ONE ROOM WITH ONE PERSON IS THE MOST FINDABLE OFFICE IN A BASIN, AND THE FINDING IS AGAINST THE ROOM AND NOT AGAINST THE PERSON.**
>
> **A BODY THAT PUTS UP A NOTICE DOES NOT PUT UP A NAME, AND A BODY THAT PUTS UP A NOTICE HAS DECIDED IN ADVANCE THAT IT WILL NOT NEED ONE.**

The registrar of this district's records office disagreed with the first of those three out loud and in front of about nineteen people, and the count came to ninety.

"**That is wrong on the first of them and it is wrong in the way that costs a person. I am not affected by that notice on the first of the next month. I am affected by it now, this afternoon, because a man stood at the leg of that table and pushed a paper under it, and everybody within four hundred yards has read it out loud twice since, and the first of the next month is when it stops being true that nobody has asked me about it.**"

"**And the second one is a thing a body can print because it is true. The room is four hundred yards from a yard with a gate in it that will not open without a foot against the post, and any person in this basin who has heard about a yard anybody can walk up to has heard about mine, and I did that, and I did it so that a page in here could not be taken out of here by somebody who wanted it.**"

"**The third of them is the one I will not let go. A body that has decided in advance it will not need a name has decided that in advance, and the decision is printed, and the decision is the finding, and I would like it observed that nobody in this yard has made a decision of that kind about anything.**"

Nobody picked it up. A man of fifty-six said that a registrar who disagrees with a rule out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people is doing the one thing this yard exists to make possible, and that it is the fourth time since the first of April that one person has done it, and that she has been in this yard five months and is not a clerk of nineteen and is not a clerk of anything, **and that a notice read out twice in a yard is a thing two people have said and not a thing anybody has answered, and did not make a rival record true this afternoon.**

The day came down about half past five and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam was still not in the yard, and the chair was still at the end of the trestle table where it has been for sixteen days, and nobody had put a line on a board, and there is still no date in any ledger in this district for the next reading and the refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent.

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH A MAN WHO HAS STOOD IN THAT YARD EVERY MORNING FOR A FORTNIGHT WAS NOT IN IT, AND A REGISTRAR DISAGREED WITH A PANEL OUT LOUD IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE AND SAID THAT A NOTICE AFFECTS A PERSON BEFORE THE DAY IT IS EFFECTIVE ON, AND A PANEL SAID A NOTICE NAMES NO PERSON AND A BODY THAT PUTS UP A NOTICE HAS DECIDED IN ADVANCE THAT IT WILL NOT NEED ONE.**
