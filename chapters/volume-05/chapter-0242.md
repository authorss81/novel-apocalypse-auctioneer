# Chapter 242: One Of The Two

---

The fourteenth of the fifth month was bright and the wind had gone again, and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in it with his right arm out of the sling for the first time since the seventh of this month and did not put it back.

The trough at the foot of the bank had about four inches of rain in it and the gate did not want a foot against the post, because the ground behind it had gone soft over the winter and had not gone hard again, and the trestle table had the second sheet with the chair printed on it on it, and the copy of the page out of the cave underneath that, and the toll sheet on the top of that, and the notice was still under the leg where it had been pressed into the ground for eight days and had taken the shape of the leg.

The chair at the end of the table had been standing there twenty-one days, it had been moved four feet once on the twenty-fourth of last month by somebody at about half past ten, and nobody had sat in it on any of the days it had stood there, and a clerk of nineteen years entered all three of those things and entered that a chair is a place a person is put and is not a bearer and is not a founder and did not become a founder by standing still.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and forty-one days on the board and four hundred and fifty-seven days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and seventy-one, and a hundred and thirty-two days separate the second of January and this morning, and the sheet came to this yard on the third of this month, which is eleven days back, and it came to a door four miles down a lane on the same morning.**"

Then he said what the day was for, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have asked one man one question in one doorway and I have been asked a second thing and got it, and this morning I am going to walk up behind this bank and ask two more people one question each, in their own doors, and I am not taking the sheet and I am not taking anybody with me, and if either of them says no then what goes in the book is no, and if neither of them says anything then what goes in the book is not asked, and not asked is not no and it is not yes either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she had offered to walk up the lane with him and had been refused, that a refusal of an offer with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that the eight did not move, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one this morning and is not going to.

---

He went at about eleven. The lane behind the bank is about two hundred yards and it is not a road, and the first hundred yards of it is between two banks with a wire on the left that has not been tight since before anybody in this district was born. The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat was at the second gate with a pail when he went past and he stopped and asked her, in his own words, whether the goat had been at the ninth house, and she said it had not and had not been there since the woman of fifty-eight stopped letting it out on the far side, and he wrote nothing down about that and she did not ask him to.

The first door had a step of two stones on it and a plank door with a hasp on it and not a handle, and the second door had a handle and a curtain. He lifted the hasp on the first and did not knock, and he asked one question in that doorway, and it was not the six things, and then he walked about nine yards up the lane and knocked on the second, because there was a handle on it, and nobody has ever been told the difference between the two doors in this district and it is in the minute.

He was back at the gate of that yard at about half past two with nothing in his hands, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who goes up a lane with nothing in his hands and comes back with nothing in his hands has not been given anything by anybody and has not refused anything, and that the record about his two hours says not asked.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against came into that yard at about two in the afternoon with his coat open and one boot caked to the ankle, and about four people were still in it, and a clerk entered that he was in the yard and that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing and that the record about the two days says not asked.

He said one thing before anybody had thought to ask him, in the plain voice, and it was counted, and it came to ninety-eight.

"**I have been to one of the two. I am not going to say a word about the other one, and that is not a refusal and it is not a trick, and the reason is that I have not been to him. He is two days of road the other way and I have got back in the time I had, and a man of fifty-six told this yard on the eleventh that going is not the same as arriving, and he was right, and I would rather say nothing than say something I have not seen.**"

Then he said what the man at the first place had said, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and forty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**He is about fifty-two and he keeps about nine people in a house with a slate roof and the sheet came to his door on the third of this month, the same morning it came to ours, and it went under his door and nobody in that house read it out. He has still got it. He said he will not answer it, he said the reason is that he is not going to be a party of the fourth of four, and then he said the second thing, which is that nobody has come up that lane since his wife died and he would like somebody to come, and he said that to a stranger in his own doorway, and I have written down both of those and I have not written down which of them he said first.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has been asked in his own doorway and has said no with a reason has not refused the eight and is not one of them, and that a man who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that a second thing said after a first thing is not a correction of the first thing and is not a column and did not become a fifth column, **and that the eight did not move at about half past two in the afternoon on the fourteenth of the fifth month and the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and did not move and the two are not the same figure.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam refused the join that had been sitting in that yard all afternoon, and the count came to ninety-seven.

"**A man who opens his own door and says a thing in it has said it to a man standing in his doorway, and a column is a column, and a yard is a yard, and there is no third place where those two become the same thing. If a sentence said at a door goes into a line on a page then the page is not a record of the doorway, it is a record of the man who wrote it down, and this district has spent four months learning how to keep those two apart.**"

It went in that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve, and that neither of them moved at about half past two in the afternoon on the fourteenth of the fifth month, and that a refusal with eleven people behind it is the same refusal as the one with four.

Then the registrar of this district's records office said the part about the notice, from the end of the trestle table, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and thirty, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**That notice takes effect on the first of the next month and it says a record is asked against four places, and two of them have not answered, and as of this afternoon one of the four has been asked a question in a doorway by a man who went on his own two feet and wrote down what he was told, and that is an answer, and it is not mine, and a body four hundred miles away does not have to be told it twice, and I am not going to be the one who tells them, because a registrar who carries another district's answer down a road is carrying a page out of her own office and there is a rule about that and I wrote it.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a registrar who says in a yard that an answer given in a doorway is not hers has now done the same shape of thing three times this month, and that three times is a habit and he was not going to make a figure of it, and that the record about the notice says not asked, and that the first day of the next month is eighteen days off and nobody in this yard can make it a different day.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came back up behind the bank at about four in the afternoon and said what the two answers were in the plain voice, and the count came to ninety-nine.

"**One man said no with a reason. One woman said she would answer in four days and gave a reason about a child, and I am not going to argue with either of them, and I am not going to count them, and I am going to write both of them down in their own words this evening and to-morrow morning. I have asked three doors out of nine. That is three and I am not going to say it out loud twice, because a number that a yard says twice is a number a clerk can ask for.**"

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households was in that yard for about eleven minutes in the middle of the afternoon and had not been sent for, and he was not asked a first thing, and he said one thing himself before anybody had decided not to ask him, and it was counted and came to a hundred and forty-nine.

"**I keep the count of six households and three of my six have not been asked anything since the second month and the six have not moved since the twenty-first of last month, which is twenty-three days, and I have said that out loud in this yard three times and I am not going to say it a fourth. What I am going to say is that a man walked two days to ask one man one question and came back and said he was not going to say anything about the other one, and that is the first time in five weeks that anybody in this basin has said out loud that a thing was not done instead of saying it was done, and I would like somebody to write that down, because the six I keep will want to know whether we are that kind of place.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a count of six households is not a clerk of a house and is not one of the eight, that a refusal of a fifth thing with a reason does not move the eight, that the eight did not move, and that the six have not moved on any of the twenty-three days since the twenty-first of last month and are one signed, two refused and three not asked again, and that a household is a household and is not a party of the fourth of four.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale stood at the back of that yard for about six minutes at about four and looked at the two chairs and the table and the notice under the leg and went out of the gate without saying anything, and the clerk entered that she was there and that nobody sent for her and that she was not asked a first thing, and that her four houses still stand and have not been taken up and were not added to eleven and are not going to be.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about half past four with the two buckets and put them down where she puts them, and a clerk entered that nobody counted them and that a count of buckets is not a count of houses and is not a figure about a woman, and that a woman who was not asked anything this afternoon has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way and the record says not asked.

The day came down about half past six and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam went out of the gate with his arm swinging and stopped at the trough and put his right hand flat in the water that was still in it and left it there for about four seconds, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said nothing about that and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said nothing about that and that the record about four seconds says not asked.

**AND ON THE FOURTEENTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH A MAN OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS CAME BACK INTO THAT YARD AFTER TWO DAYS AND SAID HE HAD ASKED ONE OF THE TWO PLACES THAT HAVE NOT ANSWERED AND WOULD SAY NOTHING ABOUT THE OTHER ONE BECAUSE HE HAD NOT BEEN TO IT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-TWO KEPT THE SHEET UNDER HIS OWN DOOR AND SAID HE WOULD NOT ANSWER IT AND ALSO THAT NOBODY HAD COME UP HIS LANE SINCE HIS WIFE DIED.**
