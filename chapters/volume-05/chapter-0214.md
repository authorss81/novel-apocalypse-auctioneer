# Chapter 214: The Fifteenth Time

---

The fifteenth of April was a day with nothing on the printed sheet and one night in a road house's book that came down a road yesterday, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the man of about thirty at the counter came in at about a quarter to eleven and was not sent for.

The figures went out loud in the ordinary way, and a man of fifty-six read the board out after the man who mends fencing and got both of them right.

"**A hundred and twelve days on the board and four hundred and twenty-eight days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and forty-two, and a man has come down an eleven mile road for the fifteenth time since the second of January and nobody in this yard told him he was expected.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifteenth time is the fifteenth, and that the counting runs eleventh on the second of this month, twelfth on the fourth, thirteenth on the sixth and fourteenth on the ninth, and that the tenth was on the twenty-seventh of last month, **and that a hundred and three days separate the second of January and this morning, which is fourteen weeks and five days, and that the district has been carrying this man's visits in a state file for four months and has never once counted them off a day.**

A man of fifty-six said that a count that is carried is a count that is remembered, and that a count that is remembered is a guess with a date on it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that **the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given stand at seven this morning, and that this yard has said seven four times in the eleven days since the fourth of this month and has never once said what the seventh was, and that a clerk of nineteen years can read the whole of it out of a book in a room four hundred yards from here and that this yard has not asked her to in twelve days.**

---

He brought four sheets, which is what he brings, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, about the ninth holding on the delivery sheet at the second reservoir, and he refused and gave no reason.

A clerk of nineteen years entered it, and she read it out afterwards in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted nothing because a clerk does not put a number on a refusal.

"**That is the seventh refusal about the ninth holding and it is the eighth refusal with no reason a clerk of a house has given in this district's business, and the count of the eight moved today for the first time since the twenty-fourth of last month, and the two figures are not the same figure and are not added together, and this morning it is the first time in twenty-two days that a figure in this district moved because somebody said no.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the count of refusals with no reason did not move for twenty-two days and then moved today because a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the question in front of nineteen people, and that the moving of it cost this district nothing and bought a man of about thirty a day he had to sit in a yard, and that a refusal with a reason has cost two people a morning and moved nothing.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the reason out loud, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words, and it was the reason and it was not about a figure.

"**He has refused me seven times in four months and he has never once been asked why, and I am not asking him why this morning, and I want it in the book that the reason he gives no reason is that nobody in this district has ever asked him for one, and that is a reason and it is mine and not his.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a reason a man in a yard gives about another man is not a reason the other man gave, **and that the eighth is an eighth and not a ninth, and that a man of about thirty-one who mends a dray refused a bearing in sixty-seven words on the fourth of this month and in fifty-eight on the sixth and gave a reason both times and that neither of those is in the count of eight, and that the seven refusals to join two things this week were the same seven and are not a fifteenth refusal of anything.**

---

The two lists were put side by side at about one in the afternoon because a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked for it and a clerk of nineteen years wrote them in two columns on one sheet and read them back and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **IN BOTH. THE FIRST. THE SECOND. THE THIRD.**
>
> **IN THE ROAD HOUSE BOOK AND NOT ON THE PRINTED SHEET. THE FIFTH. THE SEVENTH. THE NINTH. THE TWELFTH. THE THIRTEENTH.**
>
> **ON THE PRINTED SHEET AND NOT IN THE ROAD HOUSE BOOK. THE FOURTH. THE SIXTH. THE EIGHTH. THE TENTH. THE ELEVENTH. THE TWENTIETH.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that three of the eight are in both lists and five are in the road house's book and not on the printed sheet and six are on the printed sheet and not in the book, **and that of the five that are only in the book, three of them are the fifth, the seventh and the ninth of this month, and those three are the three days of eleven that a stranger's column headed OPEN leaves empty, and that the other two of the five are the twelfth and the thirteenth, which are after the last day any printed column in this district carries.**

Nothing was said for about nine seconds and then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said it, and he said it flat, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words, and nobody picked it up.

"**Three is not eight. A man who walks a road eight times and a paper that prints eight days are two different kinds of record, and one of them is a man and the other one is a body, and a district that has both of them in one yard on the same afternoon and cannot say which of them it is looking at has got a yard and not an instrument, and a yard is the fourth thing this district does not have and is the oldest.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a yard is not a room either and that a room is not a party, and that the count of things this district does not have is five and did not move at about half past two in the afternoon on the fifteenth of the fourth month, **and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and two columns written on one sheet in a yard by a clerk is not a seventh of them, and the six are a rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security, and she refused the seventh in about four seconds and was not asked to refuse it.**

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a tally for six households was in that yard at about half past three and had not been sent for, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, what he would do about the three households that have not been asked again.

He said the honest answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The house has to ask me. I have been asked four times now to be told what to do about the three and I have not been told, and I would like it written down that I have not been told four times and that a fourth time is not a fifth.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and neither of them moved at about half past three in the afternoon on the fifteenth of the fourth month, **and that a man who has been asked four times for an instruction and has not had one has not been refused and cannot be counted either way and the record says not asked, and that this yard decided on the nineteenth of the second month not to decide about three households and has not gone back on it, and that not deciding is a decision and was entered as one at the time and is not to be entered again as if it were new.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a yard deciding not to decide about three households is a yard doing to three people nine miles away exactly what it did to a woman on a hill this month, **and that the two are not joined, and that a rhyme noticed a second time is not a finding and a third time is a habit, and that I have noticed it twice this month and I am saying so before somebody else counts it a third.**

---

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about thirty at the counter, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, what a house is doing in a column headed KEEPER, and he said the honest answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A house is what keeps a lock when a man cannot. My house has never said a keeper's name on anything and I have asked about it twice in thirty-four miles and nobody would tell me a name.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of protected things is five and no sixth was added this afternoon, **and that a man who cannot be named is not a protected thing and is not a house and is not a person and is not in any of the four columns, and that the fourth column of not-askings in this district has not been ruled since the second of January and she did not rule one this afternoon and is not going to.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that three bodies now keep a page in this basin and not one of them has ever written a keeper on any of them, **and that a body which cannot name the man who does the work cannot ask him whether he did it, and that the four generations a stranger told us about on the seventh of this month are four generations of men nobody in this district is able to ask, and that is not a rhyme with anything and I am not going to look for one.**

The day went at about five and the man of about thirty at the counter went out of the yard with his four sheets, and nobody sent for him and nobody walked out with him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was in that yard for about five hours and that four of the five were spent in it by a man who was not sent for, **and that a man who comes down an eleven mile road and sits in a yard for five hours because nobody asked him a question he could answer quickly is a man this district has cost something and has not got a figure for, and that the fifth of the five things it does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and did not move at about five in the afternoon on the fifteenth of the fourth month.**

**AND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY AT A COUNTER CAME DOWN AN ELEVEN MILE ROAD FOR THE FIFTEENTH TIME SINCE THE SECOND OF JANUARY AND REFUSED THE NINTH HOLDING FOR THE SEVENTH TIME WITH NO REASON, AND THE COUNT OF REFUSALS WITH NO REASON A CLERK OF A HOUSE HAS GIVEN MOVED FROM SEVEN TO EIGHT FOR THE FIRST TIME SINCE THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF LAST MONTH.**
