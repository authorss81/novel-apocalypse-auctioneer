# Chapter 202: A Man With A Portfolio

---

The second of April was the day the counter two days of road away is open, and the man of about thirty-one was somewhere on the road with a stub in his coat, and about fourteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning because a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat at the third house up the lane from the scale was in it.

She came at about nine and she was not sent for and nobody sent for her, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked nothing and that nobody put a first thing to her, and a man of fifty-six entered that she said in a yard on the thirtieth of the third month that she would be somewhere on the second of the next month as well, **and that a person who has said she will be somewhere on a day has not signed anything and has not been asked to and is not to be asked to what she came for.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud in front of everybody that she was one of the two that refused, and she said yes, and a clerk entered that one household has signed and two have refused and three have not been asked again and that this is the fourth morning those three have been not asked again on and that the yard decided on the nineteenth of the second month not to decide about the three and has not gone back on it, **and that a sheet that came down on a cart on the last day of the third month went to four doors and one door did not open and that a man who lives in a cart had asked for a roof to be taken off his name and was refused and that the two are not joined.**

She was there for about an hour and she was asked nothing at all and at about eleven she said one thing to nobody in particular and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-one, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I said I would be here and I am here, and being here is not the same as agreeing to it, and nobody has asked me which one I am doing.**"

A man of fifty-six said that it is exactly the same and it is exactly not the same and that both of those are true at once, **and that a person who comes to a yard is not a person who has agreed to a thing and a person who does not come is not a person who has refused and that the two have been in this district's book since the twelfth of December and that the record says not asked.**

---

The man with the portfolio came up the lower terrace road at about half past eleven and he did not stop at the yard.

He was about forty-four and he was thin and he was carrying a flat case under his arm the size of a hymn book, and he came up the road at the pace of a man who has walked a long way and does not want anybody to see how far. He went past the yard, past the counter at the ward market, and stopped at a door with a fanlight over it, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing followed him at about eleven yards' distance without being asked to and without being told not to.

Nobody stopped him. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that afterwards and not at the time, and said that a district which has spent four months being proud of a book that works if a person comes to the yard spent the morning learning what that means, **and that the man walked past a yard with nine people in it and went to a door with a fanlight over it and neither of those is a small thing and both of them are the same thing.**

The name on the case was not a name of a person. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read it out twice in a lane afterward and could not say what it was the name of, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it is a name of a Registry and that a Registry is a body and a body is not a house and not a person and that a house in this district writes on the face of a sheet and does not come to a yard and that whether a Registry does either is the whole of the second of April.**

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the sentence, and he had said a version of it in four yards in a fortnight, and a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace had said a version of it on the fifteenth of the third month and the man who prints on a tram arch had said a version of it four times and never wrote it down.

"**A book that a stranger can walk up to is a door, and we built it, and we told nineteen people it was the best instrument in this district, and it is the best instrument in this district and it is a door.**"

A man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was standing at the bottom of the bank and he said that a door is a thing you shut and a thing you open and that a door which is also the best instrument in the district is two things and not one, **and that a gate holds a road and a door holds a room and that the two are not the same and that the word on the case does not say which.**

---

The man came back into the yard at about twenty past twelve because the door with the fanlight over it had nobody in it, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that he was not fetched and was not followed and came back on his own account, and a clerk entered that and that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing.

He put the case on the trestle table and he did not open it, and a man of fifty-six said that a man who puts a case on a table and does not open it is a man making a point and that the district has four of those a month and gets nothing out of any of them.

Then he opened it, and there were eleven sheets in it, and he took out one and turned it round so that the writing was the right way up for the people in the yard, and that is the only thing he did that anybody in that yard has written down.

A man of fifty-six said that a man who turns a paper round so that four people can read it has done one thing for free and that it is on the page, **and that the sixth refusal with no reason a clerk of a house has given is six and is not a column and is not added to the thirteen, and that a man who is not a clerk of a house is not in any of the four.**

He said his business, and he said it standing up and not to anybody in particular, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I hold a renewal. The lease it renews wants a bearer named and a Registry does not keep a book a stranger can walk up to, and I have come to the one place in this basin that does.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man from a Registry said in a yard that he had come to this district because this district keeps a book a stranger can walk up to, **and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud in a lane an hour ago that the district built that door on purpose and told nineteen people it was the best instrument here, and that both of those entered the same morning and that the two are not a refusal to join two things and the counts did not move and are thirteen and twelve.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half, and he said it before anybody asked him for it, and the clerk entered that he said the second half before the asking and that she is not going to put a number on that order.

"**Eighteen months. Four hundred yards. Four hundred yards and a stranger. Nobody has come through it who was not a neighbour or a creditor and that is the good half and it is the half that ran out this morning.**"

Nobody picked it up. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who is proud of a door is a man who has not counted what comes through it, and a man of fifty-six said that a man who counts what comes through a door has stopped being a man with a door and has started being a man with a toll on a door, **and that a toll on a door is the fifth of the five things this district does not have and that this district has no way of saying where a toll lands on a place and did not make one and will not make one this morning.**

---

The registrar of this district's records office came into that yard at about half past twelve on her own account and was not sent for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she came and that nobody sent for her and that she was not asked a first thing, and the two of them had been in the same yard at the same hour on purpose nine times since the eighteenth of December and this was not one of the nine because neither of them had arranged it.

There is a fanlight over that door as well and there are two doors in this district with a fanlight over them and they are about four hundred yards apart, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said so out loud and a man of fifty-six said that two fanlights is a shape and not a finding and that the district has four documents nobody owns and neither of these is one of them.

She asked the man with the portfolio one question and he answered it and she said the other half, and nobody in that yard interrupted her, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-four, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I will tell you what this office prints and I will not tell you what is in it. An office may print a house and may not print a person, and if your Registry prints a holder then a holder is a name it has taken off a sheet and a name off a sheet is not a person who agreed to be there.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the rule is a good one and that it is four months old and has been said in this yard four times and once in a room, **and that a Registry and this office are the same kind of thing and that one of the two of them has said so out loud in front of nine people and that the other one has not and that neither of those is a reason to do anything today.**

The man with the portfolio said that his Registry did not say so and was not asked to, and gave no reason, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he gave no reason and that she is not going to put a number on it, **and that a stranger's reason or no reason is not a refusal to join two things and is not added to the thirteen and is not added to her own twelve and that this morning both of those are where they were on the thirty-first of March.**

He asked her, then, for the page in the drawer, and she said no in about four seconds and gave the reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-seven, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**You may look at anything in this office. You may not carry it out and you may not have a copy of a page and that is not about you and is about the hand on it.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who will not say where he got a name is not the same as a woman who will not give a page, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that they are the same, **and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and the two of them did not move this afternoon and the man who digs loam said the two sentences out loud on purpose and the clerk entered that he did and entered that it was not asked for.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was thirty-four miles off and was not in that yard, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the man with the portfolio did not say where he had the name from and was not asked twice, **and that the line in the ward market's own book where the name stood was read out in a yard on the twenty-second of the third month and has been empty since the thirty-first of it, and that a copy of that line is on a board of eleven and the board is in this yard and the line on it is empty too.**

A man of fifty-six said that a Registry came into this district on the second of April and read a name, and that the name came off this district's page on the thirty-first of March, and that the two facts are eleven inches and two days apart, **and that they are not joined, and that a man who joins them is a man who is guessing, and that a man who is guessing in a yard in front of nine people does not know that he is doing it.**

Then the man with the portfolio said what he was not going to say today, and he said it before anybody asked him, and it went in the minute in his own words and a man of about nineteen did not count it because nobody asked him to.

"**The lease names the place. This is not the lease. I am not going to say it today and I am not refusing to say it and both of those are true.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that this district has a column for a person who will not read a thing and that the count in it is nine and that this morning it is nine, **and that this district does not have a column for a person who will not name a place and has never had one and that the two are not the same refusal and that the registrar's own count of refusals to join two things is twelve and the district's is thirteen and neither of them moved this morning and this is not added to either.**

---

They put him in the six things at about half past two in the afternoon because that is what this yard does to a person who arrives in it, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked all six and a clerk of nineteen years wrote down what was said and did not write down what was not said.

He answered the first two and did not answer the third, and the third was the year, and a clerk entered that the year is a figure and a figure is not a toll and that he did not answer it and gave no reason, **and that a man who will not say a year is a different thing from a man who will not say a place and that the district has no column for the first one either and that it did not make one at half past two in the afternoon.**

He answered the fourth, which was the term, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The term is the term on the lease and I will not read you a term off a lease and I will read you a term off this sheet and you may write it down.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who will give you a copy of the part that is on his paper and not the part that is on the other paper is a man who knows exactly what the trouble is going to be, **and that this district has been doing that for four months with sheets of its own and is in no position to be shocked by it, and that a document which says what it does not know is honest and a document which says what it will not say is a door and that both of those sentences were said out loud in this yard on the twenty-seventh of December of last year and both of them were right then.**

He did not answer the fifth, which was the toll, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the reason nobody had been told and then said it anyway, which he does.

"**The toll is at the ninth place and the ninth place is the one holding on the delivery sheet that nobody can account for and I have been to that counter eleven times in twelve weeks and six days and asked about it and been refused five times with no reason, and this morning I am not saying those are the same and I am not saying they are not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that six refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given stand at six and are not a column and are not added to the thirteen, **and that a man of about thirty at a counter thirty-four miles east and a man of about forty-four with a portfolio came to this district in the same week and that the two of them have not been in this yard at the same hour and that the two are not joined and the clerk is not going to join them and the count of things this district does not have did not move this afternoon at five.**

The sixth thing was who he had come for and he answered that one before it was asked and the yard went quiet for about nine seconds.

"**I have not come for anybody. There is a man in this basin whose name I have and I did not get it here and I am not going to say where I got it and that is not a refusal, it is a door on my side.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who admits to having a door is a man who has been on the other side of one, and a man of fifty-six said that the two sentences are the same sentence and that the difference between a door and a lock is which side of it you are standing on, **and that a lock holds a road and a gate lets a road through and that this district has not settled which word it is using and did not settle it this afternoon and the man with the portfolio has used one of the two words four times and would not say which.**

The man with the portfolio did not answer the fifth thing either and the yard let it stand, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two were not answered and that not answered is not refused and that the record says not asked.

A man of fifty-six said that the sixth of April is when a man of about thirty-one comes home or does not, and that the yard is going to be here either way, **and that a district which has spent the morning being looked at by a stranger is not going to be told by a man of fifty-six what to think about it in a lane, and that this district has four documents nobody owns and one of them is a line in a public book with four names on it and a figure under the four names and that it is still three.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam asked the man with the portfolio where he was going to sleep, and he said the second house up the lane with a sign over the door that is not a sign, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked to say and said it, **and that a man who sleeps four hundred yards from a book a stranger can walk up to has not been given a room and has not been refused one and that a man who sleeps in this district for three days is a person in this district and is not entered anywhere and the district has no instrument for a person who is in it and not in a book.**

Nobody made one. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who is in a place and not in a book is the ordinary case and has been the ordinary case for eighteen months, **and that the district's instrument is a book and a book is a room and a room is a door, and that the whole of the second of April fits in that sentence and that he is not going to say it twice.**

**AND ON THE SECOND OF THE FOURTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR CAME UP THE LOWER TERRACE ROAD WITH A PORTFOLIO AND WENT PAST A YARD WITH NINE PEOPLE IN IT TO A DOOR WITH A FANLIGHT OVER IT, THE REGISTRAR OF THIS DISTRICT'S RECORDS OFFICE CAME INTO THE YARD ON HER OWN ACCOUNT AND REFUSED HIM A PAGE IN FOUR SECONDS AND GAVE THE REASON, AND HE SAID THAT HE HAD A NAME AND DID NOT SAY WHERE HE HAD GOT IT.**
