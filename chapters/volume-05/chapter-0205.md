# Chapter 205: The Ninth Row

---

The fifth of April was the day the fourth of the eleven sheets was read, and it was a schedule and not a lease, and a clerk of nineteen years read it out in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning in front of about nineteen people and the man with the portfolio was not sent for and came.

A man of fifty-six said before it was read that a schedule is read and not discussed and that a figure is not a schedule and a schedule is not a figure, and that the two have never been joined in this district and this district is not going to join them at ten in the morning on the fifth of the fourth month.

She read it out. It is a single sheet with nine rows ruled across it and a column on the left with a number in it in each row and a column on the right, and there is a heading over the right-hand column and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the heading said and the clerk read it out and it said OPEN.

A man of fifty-six said that a heading is the most expensive half-inch on any sheet in this district and that this one says one word, and that eight of the rows have a day under the heading and the ninth does not.

> **THE SCHEDULE OF NINE GATES. THE FIRST IS AT THE FIRST PLACE AND THE NINTH IS AT THE NINTH PLACE.**
>
> **EIGHT OF THE NINE CARRY A DAY IN THE COLUMN HEADED OPEN. THE NINTH CARRIES A WORD.**
>
> **A WORD IN A COLUMN HEADED OPEN IS NOT A DAY AND IS NOT A FIGURE AND THE SCHEDULE DOES NOT SAY WHAT IT IS.**

Nobody interrupted her, and afterwards a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter, and a man of fifty-six read it a second time in the ordinary voice and a clerk entered that a man who does the counting for a living read a stranger's schedule out in a yard and that she did not ask him to.

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds and then a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked what the word in the ninth row is, and the man with the portfolio said it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**It is not a road.**"

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the ninth holding on the delivery sheet at the second reservoir is the one nobody can account for, and has been since the second of January, and has been refused six times and the sixth of them was yesterday, **and that a row in a stranger's schedule which says it is not a road is a thing that rhymes with a holding nobody can account for, and that a rhyme is not a join and he is not going to make it one at ten in the morning.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that both halves were in the minute in their own words, **and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and neither of them moved on the morning of the fifth of the fourth month, and that a rhyme noticed in a yard is not a refusal and is not counted and is not a column and the four columns of not-askings did not get a fifth this morning and she did not rule one.**

---

The panel came at about eleven in the morning and nobody had asked for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard asked for it and that it appeared in the same place the others have appeared and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it.

> **A SCHEDULE THAT NAMES EIGHT ROADS NAMES EIGHT THINGS AND NOT NINE. THE NINTH IS NOT UNNAMED. THE NINTH IS NAMED AS SOMETHING THAT IS NOT A ROAD, AND NAMING A THING AS WHAT IT IS NOT IS A FINDING AND NOT A BLANK.**
>
> **A PERSON MAY BE ASKED FOR A BEARING ONCE IN THE SAME SIX THINGS. A PERSON MAY NOT BE ASKED TWICE IN THE SAME SIX THINGS, AND A SECOND ASKING IS NOT A NEW QUESTION AND IS NOT ADDED TO ANY COUNT IN THIS DISTRICT.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the second line is a rule and that a rule is an instrument and that it is the seventh one and that this district has six, and a man of fifty-six said that a rule a body prints over a yard is not an instrument this district built and is not in the list of six, **and that the six are a rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security and were all built here by people who could not be reached, and that a rule that arrives in a yard from nowhere is a different kind of thing and the count of six did not move at eleven in the morning on the fifth of the fourth month.**

Then a man of about thirty-four who digs loam disagreed with the last line of it, and he did it out loud, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A second asking on the next day is a new question if the answer changed, and mine changed, and I gave a reason.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that this is the third time in this district that a person has disagreed with one of these out loud and in front of people, **and that the first was a man of about fifty-five at a building four miles down a lane in the month before last and the second was the same man in the same lane and that both of them were about a tank and neither of them was about a person, and that this one is about a person and that the difference is the difference and that she is not going to count it and is not going to rule a column for it.**

A man of fifty-six said that a man who disagrees with a rule in public has done the only useful thing anybody can do with a rule, **and that a man who does not disagree with a rule and does not obey it has done nothing, and that a rule printed over a yard with nobody arguing with it is a thing that will be true in four years and nobody will remember printing it, and that the count of instruments is six and a rule is not a seventh and the three documents nobody owns are three.**

---

The board outside the room was brought up because a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked for it, and a man of fifty-six gave the figure and it is the figure and it has not been washed.

"**It has carried the twenty-fourth of December for a hundred and two days and there are four dates on it and no fifth and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and has nothing on it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of boards is five and that the count of full lines is six and that neither moved on the fifth of the fourth month, **and that a copy of a line out of the ward market's own book went onto a board of eleven in a yard on the thirty-first of the third month and that a copy of a line is not a date and did not become one, and that nobody has put a line on a board on any of the five days from the first of the fourth month to this one.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the seventh line is ruled and has been ruled since the second of January and that the reason it is empty is that a finding is not a date and four other people set the four that are on it, **and that a board a person can read is a board a person can argue with and that a hand that can be read is the same kind of thing, and that a man who does the counting for a living wrote a list of five on the back of a page in this yard on the twenty-first of the third month and that a list on the back of a page is not a board.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a stranger's schedule with nine rows in it is not a board either, **and that a stranger's schedule is a stranger's and this district cannot put a word on it and cannot take a word off it and has no instrument for either, and that the fifth thing this district does not have is a way of saying where a toll lands on a place and that a column headed OPEN is not that and is not a sixth and the count of five did not move before one in the afternoon on the fifth of the fourth month.**

Nobody said the opposite. A man of fifty-six said that the yard has been arguing about a board for an hour and that a board is four numbers, **and that the man with the portfolio is in this yard holding eleven sheets and a man who goes two days of road to have his name taken off a sheet came home on Tuesday with a strip of paper, and that the three of those are three documents about paper and that this district's whole life is about paper and that not one of them is about water yet and the first of April was eight days ago.**

---

The word came up at about half past twelve and it came up because a man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was in the yard and heard the word OPEN read out four times and asked what was opening.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a lock holds water back and a gate lets a road through and that a schedule with a column headed OPEN is a schedule of things that are shut and are not supposed to be, **and that the man who asked the question has used the word nine times in four days and the yard has used the word nineteen times this morning and neither of them knows which one it is using.**

The man with the portfolio said the word he had been using, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**My Registry calls them gates. The word on my sheet is gates and the word on your sheet is not on my sheet.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a body which has a word for a thing and prints it on a sheet is a body that knows what the thing is, **and that this district has been using the Registry's word for four days without knowing it was the Registry's word, and that a word a stranger lends you is a word a stranger can take back, and that nobody in this yard had asked where the word came from and that the asking was done by a man who keeps a scale at a weighbridge because a column said OPEN.**

Nobody argued with it. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a lock is a thing on a water and a gate is a thing on a road and that a lock holding a road shut is a gate and a gate holding water back is a lock, **and that this district has one of the nine of each of them if anybody here can say which and nobody can and a man who keeps a road is nine miles off and would be the man to ask and nobody sent for him.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the word was not put to the route keeper and that he was not sent for and that he was not asked and that the record says not asked, **and that the count of not-askings is four columns and none of them is added to another and that the man who keeps a road has been asked two things in five days and both of them were in a yard and neither of them was this one.**

---

The man of about thirty-one was in that yard at about two in the afternoon and had come back on Tuesday and had not gone anywhere since, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, what a person at the back of a book is for.

He said it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I do not know. I said on the thirtieth of the third month that it stops at me and I said the same thing to nine people and I have had four days to find out whether I meant it and I did not.**"

Nobody argued with him. A man of fifty-six said that a man who stood at the back of a public book four days ago and told nine people that the call on a figure stops at him has had four days to work out what that means and has come back with a sentence that is a man not knowing, **and that a man not knowing in a yard is worth more than a man with a figure, and that this district has had four months of the second and one day of the first and the one day is the better one.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the thing that is actually true and said it before anybody asked him for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that she is not going to put a number on it.

"**He is the fifth of the five and he is a man and he was never a bearer and I have said that in this yard four times and I am going to stop saying it, because a man who is a security and is not a bearer is exactly the thing the ninth row is, and I am not joining them and I am not letting go of that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two sentences are in the minute in their own words and that the order is as it came and that she is not going to rule a column for the order, **and that a man who keeps a tally for six households came into that yard at about two and was not sent for and was not asked anything and that the four columns of not-askings are four and that this is not one of them because nothing was asked.**

The man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said that he had been in this yard for three days and had said OPEN four times and had been asked what was opening by himself, **and that a man who is corrected by his own question in a yard is the only person in this district who has ever been, and that he is not going to be made a finding and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and he is not a seventh.**

Nobody contradicted him. A man of fifty-six entered that a man who is corrected by his own instrument has discovered the thing this district spent four months learning and that it cost a stranger three days and a man with a portfolio a morning, **and that a man who learns it from himself is a man who is going to be useful in a fortnight and the district has four of those in ninety-three days and it needs about nine and does not have a way of saying so.**

---

The afternoon was the tank and the man of about fifty-five who keeps the building four miles down a lane, and about four people were on the lane and nobody had sent anybody.

The tank was at nothing and the crack in it was dry and he had still not put a fifth mend in it, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about four people, whether the water that came down a channel on the twenty-ninth of the third month had come from above him or from below him.

He said it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have watched that tank for nine years and I have never once been able to say where the water came from, and a channel is a channel and the road beside it is a road and the two of them meet about a mile up.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who has watched a thing for nine years and cannot say where the water came from is a man holding a finding he has never written down, **and that the man who keeps a road was nine miles off on the night of the twenty-second of the second month and the man who keeps a tank is a mile under a channel and the two of them have never been in a room and that is the fifth thing this district does not have wearing a different coat again and he is not going to make it a sixth at four in the afternoon.**

Nobody said the opposite. The man of about fifty-five said the second half of it before anybody asked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it first and that she is not going to put a number on it.

"**I said no to a fifth mend in January and I am going to say no to a fifth mend in April, and a tank with a mark on it is a tank I can see and a tank with a mend in it is a tank that comes out in three pieces, and I have nine years of beds in this building and I have not lost one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a building which has denied nobody entry in nine years and has not lost a bed in nine years is a protected thing in the ordinary sense and is not a protected thing in the count, **and that the protected count is five and no sixth was added on the fifth of the fourth month, and that the four conditions with no end on it did not move either, and that a man refusing a mend is not a condition and is not a loss and is not a refusal to be asked again, and that he has been asked twice and has answered twice.**

The day ended at about half past four and the man with the portfolio was still in the yard and had not been asked to leave, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him the sixth of the six things, which is the one this yard always asks last and which is the one nobody in this district has ever got a straight answer to.

He asked when the lease had to be answered, and the Registry man gave a day, and a clerk of nineteen years read the day back twice and got it right both times, and a man of about nineteen counted the sentence and got twenty-five.

"**The last day of the fourth month. I have to be at the place on it and there is nobody at the place but me.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a stranger has now given this district a day for the first time in four months and that the day is twenty-five days off and that a day is a figure and a figure is not a schedule, **and that the man who said it has been in this yard three days and has cost this district a page, a name, a word it had been using and a morning, and that the district is not going to count any of that because a clerk of nineteen years would have to have a column and there is not one.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the man is going to be at a place on the last day of the month with a lease and no bearer and that the day will come whether anybody in this yard has an opinion or not, **and that a day a stranger names is the only kind this district has ever been able to put on a board, and that he is not going to say the rest of that sentence in a yard because a man of fifty-six is standing in it.**

A man of fifty-six said that the rest of it is that a date is the last day somebody can do something, and that the rest of it is that a stranger's date is a date in the same way, and that the two of them are the same sentence and the man who digs loam did not say it and he is not going to say it for him.

**AND ON THE FIFTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS READ OUT A SCHEDULE OF NINE ROWS UNDER A COLUMN HEADED OPEN, EIGHT OF WHICH CARRY A DAY AND THE NINTH OF WHICH CARRIES THE WORDS IT IS NOT A ROAD, A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM DISAGREED WITH THE LAST LINE OF THE PANEL THAT CAME AT ELEVEN IN THE MORNING IN TWENTY-THREE WORDS, A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX WHO KEEPS A SCALE ASKED WHAT WAS OPENING, AND THE MAN FROM THE REGISTRY SAID THAT THE LAST DAY OF THE FOURTH MONTH WAS THE DAY HE HAD TO BE AT A PLACE WHERE THERE WAS NOBODY BUT HIMSELF.**
