# Chapter 245: Eight Other Places

---

The seventeenth of the fifth month was grey and still and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and three of the sheets that were read out in that yard the day before were not in it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and forty-four days on the board and four hundred and sixty days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and seventy-four, and a hundred and thirty-five days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the next month is fifteen days off, and I read out one of these yesterday and I am not going to read out another one in this yard today.**"

The three sheets were on the table, and each of them had been in a different room when the names went under the line, and the fourth one from the day before was not among them, because it was in a room four hundred yards away in a book that could not be carried out of it, and a clerk entered that the first charter is in a room and not in this yard and that a room is a place where a sentence goes and is not a place a stranger can walk up to, and that this is the first of the nine that this district has made itself findable. and the clerk of nineteen years had carried all three back about four hundred yards in a coat and had entered the fact that she had carried them and had not been asked to and that a sheet carried four hundred yards in a coat is not a bearer and is not a figure about a person.

She read the second of them out because somebody asked her to, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got ninety-seven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The first one is five seasons. The second one is three. The third one is five. They are not the same sheet and they are not the same term and I did not write any of the three and I could not have written one of them without somebody telling me what the term was, and I did not suggest a single one of the three. A charter whose term comes out of the middle of a yard is a charter with a holder in it and this one has got five names under a line instead.**"

The fourth sheet was different and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said so before anybody asked him, and the count came to a hundred and seven.

"**The fourth one is one season. One. And the man who put his name under that line is seventy-one and he said the reason out loud with nobody standing in the room, which is that he does not know what there will be in this valley when he is not here to ask anybody, and a man who is seventy-one does not get promised five seasons by a yard of about nineteen people on the strength of a good morning. That reason is on the sheet in his own words and not mine, and a clerk has written it down and I have not read it since.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a term of one season and a term of five seasons are two terms and are not added together, that a man of seventy-one has not been taken advantage of by anybody and has not refused anything, and that the record about the reason says not asked and that a reason given out loud in a room with a man standing in the door is a thing somebody asked for and got.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households asked the one question he came for, in his own words and not in the six things, which was whether the six of his were one of the nine, and he was told no, and he said he had expected that and would like it written down that he had expected it, and then he said the other thing, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and twenty-four.

"**I keep a count of six households and one signed and two refused and three not asked again, and that count has not moved since the twenty-first of last month, which is twenty-six days. I asked whether my six were one of the nine because if they were then somebody is going to come up my lane in a fortnight with a piece of paper, and I would like to be able to tell them there is a yard four hundred yards away that already said no on their behalf, which would be a lie, so I asked instead. I am not one of your nine and neither are my six and I have not told my six a word about any of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who asks a question in order not to lie about it later is not a bearer of anything, that a refusal of a bearer is not a refusal of the eight and did not move them, that the eight did not move at about one in the afternoon on the seventeenth of the fifth month, and that the six have not moved on any of the twenty-six days since the twenty-first of last month.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam refused the join that had been available to him since the middle of the morning, standing at the end of the table with his right arm swinging, and the count came to a hundred and twenty-three.

"**There is a building four miles down a lane that has denied nobody entry in nine years and has nine years of beds in a second room and a tank with two lines of chalk on the inside of the brick, and somebody in this yard is going to want to put that building under a line in one of these eight because it is a place with people in it. It is not a settlement. It is one man and one woman and about nine years of not being turned out, and if anybody puts them under a line then a clerk is asking a question they have not been asked and writing down the answer they gave to a different question.**"

It went in that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve, and that neither of them moved at about eleven in the morning on the seventeenth of the fifth month.

Then the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came into that yard at about half past eleven with a fifth sheet in the inside of his coat, which he had not been asked to fetch and which he said had been given to him at the top of the road by a man he did not name, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was in the yard and that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing and that the record about the man at the top of the road says not asked.

There were no names under the line on that one.

A man of fifty-six said the thing about it, out loud, in the plain voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and forty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That is the ninth. Nine sheets and eight of them have got names under the line and the ninth one has not got any, and I am going to say the two things about that and I am going to stop at the second one on purpose. The first is that the people at that place have not been asked, and a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and the record says not asked. The second is that I am not going to say whether an empty line on a sheet from this month is the same shape of thing as an empty place on a page out of a cave, and anybody who wants to know can go and work it out and I am not going to work it out in a yard.**"

Nobody worked it out in a yard. The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was asked whether he would keep a copy of the ninth sheet, and said no in about four seconds, and gave a reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and eight.

"**A copy of a sheet with five names on it is a copy of a sheet. A copy of a sheet with nobody on it is a copy of an absence, and a man who hands a copy of an absence round this basin is a man who has made the absence into a thing that travels, and the day it travels somebody is going to fill it in. I will not do that one. I have refused two things in a month and I have refused this one and none of the three is one of the eight and I would like that written down three times.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move at about half past twelve in the afternoon on the seventeenth of the fifth month, and that the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road put a sheet into that yard that he was not asked to fetch and did not put his own name on it and is not a bearer of it.

The four remaining sheets were not in that yard and were not going to be, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said where they were in the plain voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and sixty-three.

"**The fifth is with a woman who keeps a goat. The sixth is in a village about nine miles the other way and a man there read it out in a room with about eleven people in it and wrote it out in his own words and sent me four words back down the road on a piece of paper with nothing else on it. The seventh and the eighth I have not seen and I am not going to describe them to you this morning because I have not been in the rooms they were made in and a man who describes a sheet he has not read in the room it was made in is doing the thing this yard spent four months stopping. You have four of the nine in front of you and there are four of the nine that this yard cannot produce, and I would rather say that out loud than have somebody find out in June.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a yard which cannot show four of the nine is not a yard that has refused four of the nine, and that the record about the two this district has not seen says not asked, and that a description of a sheet from a man who was not in the room it was made in would be a figure about the man and not about the sheet.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was not in that yard on that day and had gone out of it on the previous morning with a coat and a knapsack and nothing else, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody sent for him, that he was not asked a first thing, and that the record about the two days of road says not asked, **and that a man of fifty-six said that going is not arriving and that this district has never once given that man a day to be here on, and that nobody is going to sit at that gate and wait for him this time either.**

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about two with two buckets and put them down where she puts them, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing took one of them up the bank for her without being asked and without saying anything about it, and she let him and then she said one thing to nobody in particular and nobody counted it because nobody was asked to, and the clerk entered that she said it and that it went into the minute unnumbered.

She said that a bucket is a bucket and that it weighs about eleven pounds full and that she knows that to about a pound and has never been asked how she knows it, and that in nine years nobody in this basin has ever asked her what a bucket weighs, and that if anybody did ask her she would tell them and she would want it written down that she told them in her own words and not what somebody else had written down for her.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a weight a woman knows about her own bucket is not a rate and is not a figure about a person and is not a column, and that a refusal of a fifth column of not-askings is not what this morning was and that the four columns did not move, and that she let him, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him to and that the record says not asked, and that a bucket carried up a bank by a man who was not asked is not a figure about either of them and is not a job and is not a seventh instrument.

The day came down about half past five and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there twenty-four days, and the notice was still under the leg of that table with the ground gone hard under it now, and the first day of the next month was fifteen days off.

**AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH THREE MORE OF THE NINE WERE READ OUT AND THEIR TERMS WERE NOT THE SAME, BEING FIVE SEASONS, THREE SEASONS AND ONE SEASON WITH THE REASON FOR THE ONE GIVEN OUT LOUD BY A MAN OF SEVENTY-ONE, AND A FIFTH SHEET CAME DOWN THE ONLY ROAD IN THIS DISTRICT THAT CLIMBS WITH NO NAMES UNDER THE LINE ON IT AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT THE PEOPLE AT THAT PLACE HAVE NOT BEEN ASKED.**
