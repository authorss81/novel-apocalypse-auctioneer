# Chapter 244: The First Of Nine

---

The sixteenth of the fifth month was overcast and warm for the hour and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was not in it, having gone out of it at about six that morning with a coat and a knapsack.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody sent for him, that he was not asked a first thing, and that the record about where he went says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and forty-three days on the board and four hundred and fifty-nine days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and seventy-three, and a hundred and thirty-four days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the next month is sixteen days off, and there is a sheet of paper in my coat with five names on it and I have not read it out.**"

It came out of the inside pocket of a coat that had been in a yard every morning for a fortnight and it had been folded in four and it had gone soft at the folds, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put it on the table face down and then turned it round himself and did not say anything about doing that, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who turns a sheet face up is not a man who has agreed with it.

A clerk of nineteen years asked for it to be read, because a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and she read it in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them and read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **THIS IS ONE CHARTER OF NINE AND IT STANDS ALONE. THE OTHER EIGHT ARE IN EIGHT OTHER PLACES AND MAY NOT BE WRITTEN ON IT, AND A PERSON WHO HOLDS ONE OF THEM IS NOT A BEARER OF THIS ONE.**
>
> **IT IS FOR THE GROUND, THE DRAINS AND THE WATER OF ONE SETTLEMENT. THE NUMBER OF THAT SETTLEMENT AND THE NAME OF IT ARE NOT ON THIS SHEET, AND A HOUSE IS NOT A BEARER, AND A LIST OF HOUSES IS NOT A BEARER.**
>
> **THE BEARERS ARE THE FIVE NAMES UNDER THE LINE. A NAME GOES UNDER THE LINE ONLY IF THE PERSON IT BELONGS TO PUT IT THERE, IN THEIR OWN ROOM, WITH SOMEBODY STANDING IN THE DOOR, AND A NAME PUT UNDER THE LINE BY ANYBODY ELSE IS NOT ON THIS SHEET.**
>
> **THE TERM IS FIVE SEASONS. THE TERM BEGINS ON THE FIRST DAY OF THE NEXT MONTH AND IS COUNTED FROM THAT DAY AND NOT FROM THE DAY THE SHEET WAS SIGNED.**
>
> **ANY ONE OF THE FIVE MAY GIVE UP THE WHOLE OF IT ON THE FIRST DAY OF ANY SEASON AFTER THE FIRST, BY SAYING SO OUT LOUD TO ANY ONE OF THE OTHER FOUR. THE GIVING UP IS DONE ON THAT DAY. A GIVING UP IS NOT A REFUSAL AND IS NOT A COLUMN.**
>
> **A GIVING UP DOES NOT GIVE BACK A SEASON. NOTHING SOWN IS PULLED UP. THE COST OF A GIVING UP IS NOT MONEY. IT IS THAT THE ARRANGEMENTS ABOVE THAT SETTLEMENT GO BACK TO BEING HELD BY NOBODY'S AGREEMENT, AND THAT WORK ALREADY DONE IS NOT RETURNED, AND THAT THE SPENDING A GIVING UP DOES NOT RETURN IS NOT PRICED HERE BECAUSE THERE IS NO RATE IN THIS BASIN THAT TURNS A YEAR INTO COPPERS AND THERE NEVER HAS BEEN ONE. NOBODY OWNS THE WATER. A BEARER HOLDS A SHARE IN AN ARRANGEMENT, A SHARE IS NOT THE WHOLE OF IT, AND THE WHOLE OF IT IS NOT ANYBODY'S.**

Nobody in that yard had read that sheet before that morning, and one of the five names under the line was read out loud in a yard for the first time since it was written, and entered that a name said out loud once in a yard a stranger can walk into is a name a stranger can ask about, and that this is the first name this district has said out loud in a yard this month, and that the man who said it out loud said it because nobody else was going to.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the half of the cost of a giving up that nobody else in that yard was going to say, and the count came to a hundred and twenty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I am not one of the five and I do not live in any of their rooms and nobody has asked me about any of it, and I am the right person to say the other half of that sentence. A giving up does not cost money and it does not cost a season and the sheet says both of those, and the sheet is not wrong. It costs the work. There are about four hundred yards of drain round that house that have been dug this month by a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and by two other men, and the dig does not come back out of the ground because five people decided in one week that they would rather not.**"

Then he said the other thing, and the count came to a hundred and fifty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**And the other half of the cost is the half that cannot be put on a sheet, and I want it said out loud in a yard and not in a room. If those five give it up then whatever is being agreed about the water above four districts stops being agreed by them, and nobody else in this basin has agreed to anything yet, and so what happens above that water after a giving up is not nobody's business, it is everybody's, and everybody includes the people who are not in the five, and there is not one of them in this yard today who has been asked what they think about that. That is the cost. It is nine rooms of work and a hole in the middle of it, and this district has no way to pay a person who is not in a household and has not priced the spending and is not going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the cost of a right to withdraw was established in a yard by a man who is not a bearer and is not a householder of the settlement, and that no bearer was asked what a withdrawal would cost them and that the record about the five says not asked, and that a cost which cannot be priced is not a figure and is not a column and is not a fifth thing added to the five.

The registrar of this district's records office said the part that was hers, from about four feet inside the gate, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and forty-one, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I have not signed that sheet and I am not going to sign it, and it is not a small thing to say in a yard with about nineteen people in it, because a registrar who signs a charter is a registrar who has put her room behind somebody's arrangement. My rule of the twenty-first of last month says a book is kept in one room by one person and that person is the only one who may look in it. That rule is the only reason a notice four hundred miles away could be written, and it stands whether I sign this or not, and it stands whether the first of the next month comes or not, and if that notice takes effect in a fortnight then the books in that room are still in that room and still mine.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the first day of the next month is the first day of the first season on that sheet and the first day a notice four hundred miles away takes effect, and that those are two things and that a clerk is not going to write them in the same sentence, and that a man who is not going to join two things is not the same as a man who has never noticed them.

Then the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing asked, in front of about nineteen people, whether he might make a copy of the first charter, and was told that he could, and he said that he would want eleven coppers for it, and the man of fifty-six refused him and gave the reason, which was that he has said the same no six times and there is nothing in that sentence a seventh time would change, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that the eight did not move at about half past eleven in the morning on the sixteenth of the fifth month, and that a copy of a thing this district has written itself is not a bearer and is not a figure about a person and is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own.

A man of fifty-six gave the figure himself, without being asked, about the drain round that house, and said it came to about four hundred yards in three weeks with two men and a third man on about nine days, and then he said that that was a man counting his own unpaid work and not a clerk and that nobody was going to enter it as a figure about anybody, and a clerk entered that he had said so himself before anybody asked him to, which is a thing a man of fifty-six has done about a dozen times in four months.

He made the copy anyway, in his own hand, in about twenty minutes, for nothing, and put it on the table face down, and the clerk entered that he had done it for nothing and had not been thanked and did not ask to be, **and that the district is still not going to pay him and has now said so six times, and that the five things this district does not have are five and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and did not move at about twenty past twelve in the afternoon on the sixteenth of the fifth month.**

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the back of that yard for about nine minutes at about half past twelve and said one thing about the sheet before she went, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and five.

"**I have four houses standing empty and I offered them to this yard on the last day of last month and nobody took them and I am not asking again. I am not putting four empty houses under a line on a piece of paper that starts in a fortnight either, because a house with nobody in it is not a bearer of anything and if it was then this district would have had nine bearers in March and it had none. You want five names and I have given you none, and a clerk is going to write down that I gave you none.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who gives nothing is not a refusal, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that the eight did not move at about half past twelve in the afternoon on the sixteenth of the fifth month, and that her four houses still stand and were not added to the eleven of the woman of fifty-eight and are not going to be.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about one with two buckets and put them down where she puts them, and a clerk entered that nobody counted them and that a count of buckets is not a count of houses and is not a figure about a woman, and that a woman who was not asked this afternoon has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way and the record says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing that was in the room with everybody else before anybody could, in the plain voice, and the count came to a hundred and forty.

"**That is one and there are eight more, and I am going to say the whole method out loud this afternoon so that a clerk can write it down. Every one of the eight is asked in a room or a doorway by somebody who is not in it, in the words of the person who is in it, and every one of the eight gets a term of its own and not mine, and every one of the eight gets the right to give it up on any first day of a season, and the other eight sheets are not going to be in this yard, and if somebody asks me where the other eight are I am going to say that they are in eight other places and I hope somebody in those eight places is holding them.**"

Nobody argued with him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve, and that neither of them moved at about one in the afternoon on the sixteenth of the fifth month, and that nine sheets in nine places is not one sheet with nine places on it, and that a page with nine places ruled on it is the shape of a page that came out of a cave and that nobody in this district is going to make that shape again.

**AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH THE FIRST OF NINE LOCAL CHARTERS WAS READ OUT IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AND READ BACK, WITH FIVE NAMES UNDER A LINE AND A TERM OF FIVE SEASONS AND A RIGHT FOR ANY ONE OF THE FIVE TO GIVE THE WHOLE OF IT UP ON THE FIRST DAY OF ANY SEASON AFTER THE FIRST WITHOUT GIVING BACK A SEASON, AND THE COST OF GIVING IT UP WAS SAID OUT LOUD BY A MAN WHO IS NOT ONE OF THE FIVE AND WAS NOT ASKED BY ANY OF THEM.**
