# Chapter 243: Past The Turn

---

The fifteenth of the fifth month was clear and cold and about half past six in the morning, and the yard of Lot Seventeen was empty, and about nine people were on the lane behind the bank by ten past seven.

The three figures went out loud at the gate in the dark and the man of fifty-six read the board out after the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and got the other two right, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote them down by the light of the fanlight over a door four hundred yards away, which does not light anything, and she said afterwards that she had written them from memory and would not do it again.

"**A hundred and forty-two days on the board and four hundred and fifty-eight days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and seventy-two, and a hundred and thirty-three days separate the second of January and this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights was the twentieth of last month, which is twenty-five days back, and nobody in this yard knows whether that night happened and about nine of us are going to find out whether anybody can.**"

Nobody sent for anybody. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that out loud at the gate before they were through it, and a clerk entered it, and entered that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one.

The four miles out past the loams took about an hour and ten minutes at the pace of people who are not in a hurry and are not going to pretend they are not. The sun came off the top of the loams at about half past seven and the whole of that road was in front of them the whole way, and the last half mile of it is a cart track between two banks with a wire on the left of it that has not been tight since before anybody in this district was born. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road went past the field gate this time and did not turn back, and nobody said anything about that and nobody asked him why, and a clerk entered that the record about the field gate says not asked.

The top field of a farm whose legal owner is not in this district is on the left as the road starts to climb, and the fourth holding took water in the night of the nineteenth into the twentieth of last month and its channel had a foot of water in it on the twentieth and is down about a foot now, and a clerk entered that a sheet about a fourth holding and a field on a hill are two records about two things and are not joined.

The climb is about nine miles and it is the only road in this district that climbs. The last half mile of it is not a road at all and is a goat track along a shoulder with about two feet of drop on the left, and a wire runs along the top of that drop to keep a goat off, and the wire is not taut and has not been taut for longer than anybody up there can remember.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put his left hand on that wire at about half past ten to get past a man coming the other way with two buckets, and the wire took the skin off the inside of his wrist about the length of a thumb, and he did not stop and he did not say anything and he went on up the shoulder, and the man with the two buckets said nothing either and went down.

The road house at the top of the road is a building of two rooms with a slate roof and a plank door that has no hasp on it. The man of about forty-one who keeps it was standing outside it at about eleven with his stick, and he had not been sent for and had not been asked a first thing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both of those things before anybody said anything to him, and entered that this district has never once given that man a day to be here on and that it did not give him one this morning either.

The board that goes in the notch at the top lock stands on end against the north wall of that building, and it is about nine inches wide and about four feet long, and it has stood there every summer since before the man of about forty-one could reach the top of the frame.

Nobody had asked him a first thing and then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him one, in his own words, about who had put the board in the notch that morning, and the answer was counted, and it came to a hundred and fifty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I did not put it in and my father did not put it in and neither of us has ever put one in. There is somebody who comes in March and goes down again before the water is down and I do not know what he is called and I have not asked, and I am not going to ask now that you have all been standing on my shoulder, because the day I ask him is the day there is a man at the top of this road instead of a house, and this house has never had a man in it. He comes. He puts the board in. He goes. And in this basin that is the whole of what has ever happened on this water and nobody has ever been able to write it down, and the reason nobody has been able to write it down is that there is nobody to write.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has never been asked to be a keeper is not a keeper, and that a house is not a bearer and is not a holder, and that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on and that a name put on a house by a man who asked a question in his own doorway is the same kind of thing and is not a smaller one, **and that the record about the March man says not asked, and that the man of about forty-one has no day in this district and that nobody has given him one and that this district has never once given him one.**

Then they went about two hundred yards above the road house along the shoulder to the frame, and there were about nine of them on a piece of ground about the size of a room with a drop on two sides, and the wind came over the top of it and took their coats sideways.

The frame at the top is a frame of cast iron about four feet high and it is bolted to two pieces of oak let into the bank on either side, and the oak is older and darker than the oak at the loams and darker at the bottom where it stands in the water. The screw comes up through the middle of the frame to a handle about a foot and a half long, and the pin goes through the screw where the handle is pinned on, and the sill at the bottom is a stone about two feet wide with one notch cut in it.

**The board was in the notch.** It had been put in by somebody and it was wet to about a hand's breadth above the water and the water was standing above the board about a foot deep and no more, and the road below that frame was under about a foot of water at the place where the cart track comes out, and a man of about nineteen who counts stood on the shoulder and looked down at that for about two minutes and counted nothing, and a clerk entered that he counted nothing because there was nothing to count.

The man of about fifty-six said it out loud, in the ordinary voice, with his hand not on the frame, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and twenty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Four generations of people have kept this and not one of them is on a page and not one of them has ever been asked, and none of us is a keeper, and I want that written down before anybody starts. We are nine people standing on a shoulder in the cold looking at a piece of iron that a family put a plank into. If the four generations knew we were here they would not come out. That is not a complaint. It is the reason a man of about thirty-four could walk to one of these in a morning and did not, and he was not a coward about it, he was the wrong kind of person for it, and so am I.**"

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the finding, and he said it standing at the edge of the shoulder with his right arm across his body, and the count came to a hundred and fifty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**We have been for nine hours. We can say three things and we could not say the fourth one and I am going to say all four out loud. We can say the board is in. We can say the water is about a foot over the board and the road below is shut. And we can say that nobody in this basin is holding this, because there is nothing on it to hold. What we cannot say is whether the ninth of the nine nights happened on the twentieth of last month, and we cannot say it because a plank does not keep nights, and there is not one mark on this frame and no book and no man, and it is not anybody's job to remember it, and that is the first thing this district has ever found out by walking to a thing instead of saying a number out loud.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nine structures in the water above four districts have been standing open on eight of eleven nights, one a night, since the first of April, and that the ninth of the printed nine was the twentieth of last month, and that a record about those nine nights now says something was found and not who found it, **and that a thing a man could have done and did not do is not a refusal and is not a consent and is not a count, and that a plank is not a record and is not a column and is not an instrument this district built and not named and did not become a seventh of the six.**

The man of about nineteen who counts said one thing about the four minutes on that shoulder before anybody had said anything else, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and five.

"**I counted the nine of us going up the shoulder and I counted the nine of us coming off it and those are the two numbers I have got, and I did not count the board and I did not count the water and I did not count the four generations, because a man of about thirty-four stood about four feet from me this morning and told me that nobody has ever been able to write any of it down, and if I had put a figure on it this afternoon I would have been the first man in this basin who made one up.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man of about nineteen who declines to count a thing on the day he has counted everything else for forty days is a man who has understood the instrument and not the yard, and that the yard is the easier of the two.

A man of fifty-six said that the man who digs loam had not refused a join all day, and that he had listened to two refusals on the way up and had not made one, and that a day a man does not refuse is not a day he has agreed to anything and the record about it says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing got his arm above his head at about half past two to point at something on the far bank, and it took him two goes, and nobody counted it, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam did not offer to do it and neither did anybody else and the clerk entered that nobody offered and that the record about two goes says not asked.

They came down. It took about five hours and the light going, and the last half mile of the goat track was in the dark by the time they were off it, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road went down it in front of the nine of them with a lamp he had not been asked to carry and that nobody asked him about, and the wind had gone round to the north by then and came up the shoulder behind all of them.

They were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about half past seven and about nine people were still in it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing sat down on the step of the building at the end of the lane, and the woman of fifty-eight was there with the two buckets she had brought down at about five and had not gone back up with, and she looked at the inside of his left wrist for about two seconds and said nothing, and he said nothing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him about it and that the record about the wrist says not asked, **and that the five things this district does not have are five and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and did not move at about half past seven in the evening on the fifteenth of the fifth month.**

**AND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WALKED NINE MILES UP THE ONLY ROAD IN THIS DISTRICT THAT CLIMBS AND ABOUT TWO HUNDRED YARDS PAST THE TOP OF IT AND STOOD IN THE COLD ON A SHOULDER AND FOUND THE BOARD IN THE NOTCH AT ONE OF THE NINE, AND COULD SAY THAT THE ROAD BELOW WAS SHUT AND COULD NOT SAY WHETHER THE NINTH OF THE NINE NIGHTS HAPPENED ON THE TWENTIETH OF LAST MONTH, BECAUSE A PLANK DOES NOT KEEP NIGHTS.**
