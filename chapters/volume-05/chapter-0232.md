# Chapter 232: What A Pin Is For

---

There was ice in the trough at the foot of the bank on the fourth of the fifth month at about nine in the morning, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten, and there was a barrow at the gate with a thing on it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the figures and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and thirty-one days on the board and four hundred and forty-seven days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and sixty-one, and the twelfth of this month is eight days off, and there is a lock on a barrow at the end of this yard and nobody in this district has ever read one in a street.**"

It had been in the channel at the sluice end of the loams for longer than the man of about thirty-four who digs loam has dug beside it. It came out of that channel at about seven in the morning onto a barrow with two men on the shafts, and it is a frame of cast iron about four feet high with a screw through the middle of it and a long handle on the top of the screw and a pin through the screw where the handle meets it, and a sill at the bottom of it with a notch cut in the sill. It weighs about a hundred and ninety pounds.

It went up the bank in about eleven minutes with the gate shut behind the barrow and the man who digs loam at the near shaft and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing at the far one, and it was put down at the end of the trestle table, and the barrow was not a figure and is not one of the six instruments this district built and not named and did not become a seventh.

It was asked, and by a man of fifty-six in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, who had asked for it, and the answer was that the man of about thirty-four who digs loam had asked for it, because the lock stands four feet from where he has been standing for nine years and he is the only man in this district who knows where the two ends of that channel are.

He gave the reason and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I did not ask to have it carried and I asked to have it read. It has been in that channel every day I have dug beside it for nine years and I have never once known what the pin was for, and a man who digs beside a thing for nine years and does not know what the pin is for has been the wrong man for nine years, and that is on me and not on the iron.**"

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against read it, in front of about nineteen people, with his hand flat on a frame that was cold enough to hurt through the skin, and he read it in about two minutes and he read it well, and he was wrong.

"**This is a lock and it holds a road shut. What it holds with is the pin. The pin is the piece that takes the load. The frame is only a frame and the screw is only a way of lifting, and if the pin is out then the screw runs free and the weight of the water goes through the frame and out, and so a lock is a safe thing exactly as long as a man is near it, and it is not a safe thing on a night when nobody is near it.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, and the clerk entered that this is the third time in a fortnight that nobody in that yard has said anything at all, and that she is not going to make a figure of any of the three.

The man of fifty-six said it was not right, and he said it in the ordinary voice and without any pleasure in it at all, and he put his own hand on the frame next to the other man's hand, and the count came to a hundred and twelve, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**The pin takes nothing. I have been putting a stick through that pin to turn that screw with both hands in the water for nine years, about four thousand times, and if the pin took the load my hands would have gone under the water with the screw. It is there so the screw cannot turn on its own in the night. What holds that channel is the frame and the weight of the water sitting on the frame, and the screw is a lever and not a wall, and a man who puts his weight on that lever is lifting the whole weight of a channel on one thread of iron.**"

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the second half of it himself, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, in his own words, and he said the whole of it and he said what it cost, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and forty-seven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I was wrong about the pin and I am saying so in a yard with about nineteen people in it and I am not going to wait for one of you to find it. Here is the whole of what I was wrong about and it is this. A pin is not a wall, a screw is not a wall, a frame is not a wall, and a lock does not need a man to hold it and never did, and I have stood at the back of a book for two months telling anybody who would listen that a person at the back of a book is a thing that can be fastened to, and I have been right about that and wrong about this, and a yard is the only place in this basin where a man is allowed to find that out for nothing.**"

The clerk wrote it down and entered that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against has now said twice in one morning that he was wrong, and that neither saying is a figure and neither is a column, and that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the other half and he said it before the clerk had finished, and it was counted, and it came to ninety-seven.

"**That is a seventh, and I am putting it up myself, and I want the clerk to refuse it in about four seconds. This yard has just watched a man of twenty-one years say out loud in front of about nineteen people that the thing he knows best in the world is not what he said it was, and if this district writes that down as an instrument then in four months somebody will be thanked for it, and a thing this district builds and does not name is the one instrument in this basin that works.**"

The clerk refused it in about four seconds and was not asked to refuse it, and the six did not move, and the six are a rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security, **and a man admitting in public that he was wrong is not a seventh of them and did not become one at about eleven in the morning on the fourth of the fifth month.**

A man of fifty-six said that one of the nine who wrote a rule four hundred years ago sat down afterwards and wrote, in a second hand, that a man thanked for it has been made something else, and that the something is not a chair, and that a rule about being thanked and a rule about being wrong are the same shape of rule, and that the ninth place on the page in a cave is ruled and empty and is going to stay that way whatever this yard does with its mornings.

A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was not in that yard and the four houses she offered are still standing empty and were not added to eleven, and the clerk entered that, and entered that the conditions with no end on it are four and that the seat of a chair is not a fifth.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was asked, in front of about nineteen people, whether he would draw the lock so that the yard could keep a picture of it, and he said no in about four seconds and gave the reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-five.

"**I copy words off one thing onto another thing. I do not make a picture of a thing nobody owns, and a picture is a copy with nobody's name on it, and a copy of a lock would be a lock this district could point at and say that is the one, and there are nine of them and not one of them is ours.**"

The clerk entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and that the eight did not move at about half past twelve in the afternoon on the fourth of the fifth month, and that a picture is not an instrument this district built and not named and did not become a seventh of the six, and that the district is still not going to pay him and has now said so five times.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the second sheet with the chair printed on it out at about one in the afternoon for about nine people who had not been in the yard in the morning, and got nothing wrong, and the clerk entered that the second sheet has now been read out in that yard four times and that a sheet read out four times is still one sheet somebody else wrote, and that the chair standing at the end of the trestle table has been moved four feet once and has not been sat in by anybody on any of the days it has stood there.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about half past one with two buckets and put them down where she puts them, and nobody in that yard asked her anything about a barrow or about a hundred and ninety pounds, and the clerk entered that nobody asked her and that the record says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam put his right arm back into the sling at about half past eleven without being asked and without saying anything about it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing saw him do it and asked him about it in his own words, and the answer was counted and came to eighty-three.

"**That barrow went up a bank in about eleven minutes at the near shaft and I am fifty-eight pounds of a man and I was fine at the bottom of it and I am not fine now. Do not write that down as a complaint. Write down that a barrow with a hundred and ninety pounds on it goes up a bank at the near shaft and the man at the near shaft is the one who pays for it in the shoulder.**"

The clerk entered that a shoulder is not an instrument and is not a figure about a man and is not a column, and that the record about a body says what a body did and does not say what a body is worth, **and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and did not move at about half past eleven in the morning on the fourth of the fifth month.**

They put it back in the channel about two in the afternoon and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said at the gate that a thing that is in the water again and has been read in a street is not a different thing from a thing that has not been read, and that the ninth of the nine printed nights was the twentieth of last month, and that a day a district cannot say whether it happened is a day and not a figure, and that there is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and there never has been one.

**AND ON THE FOURTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH A LOCK CAME OUT OF A CHANNEL AT THE SLUICE END OF THE LOAMS ON A BARROW AND UP A BANK IN ABOUT ELEVEN MINUTES, AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST READ IT IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE AND SAID THE PIN HOLDS THE WATER AND WAS WRONG, AND THE MAN OF FIFTY-SIX PUT HIS HAND ON THE FRAME NEXT TO HIS AND SAID THE PIN TAKES NOTHING, AND HE SAID SO OUT LOUD AGAIN IN HIS OWN WORDS AND ASKED FOR NO NUMBER ON EITHER OF THE TWO.**
