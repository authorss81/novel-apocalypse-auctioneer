# Chapter 210: A Dray Going Up A Road

---

The tenth of April was the eighth day of a man with a portfolio and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the yoke was standing against the tailboard of a dray at the bottom of the lane in the open where anybody in four hundred yards could walk round it and look at it.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the figures out loud, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam gave them back a second time without being asked and got both of them right.

"**A hundred and seven days on the board and four hundred and twenty-three days on the train, and the days nobody has entered stand at a hundred and thirty-seven, and the last of the eight days in a column headed OPEN is tomorrow and this district has no instrument for tomorrow and did not build one on the second of January and is not building one today.**"

Nobody argued with it. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a day is not a route and a route is not a schedule and that the copy of a stranger's schedule is a copy and not a third one, **and that a clerk of nineteen years read those eight days out yesterday in one run of words and read them back and got them right twice and that a man of about twenty-three who copied them in fifty minutes and gave the copy away for nothing is not owed eleven coppers a week for that and is not owed them for the other thing either and nobody has asked him for either.**

The man of about thirty-one had put the yoke on the tailboard himself at about half past eight in the morning and had not put anything else on the cart, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the yoke is against the tailboard and not on it, and that it is a carrying yoke and not a draught yoke and is not the thing that would pull a dray, **and that it is on the cart because a man of about thirty-one said on the ninth of this month that he would not put it on a cart until the woman who owns it had seen it on a cart, and she saw it on the cart at about ten in the morning, and that is the whole of the reason it is there and the reason is not a use.**

---

She saw it at about ten in the morning and she stood at the bottom of the lane for about a minute and a quarter and she did not go up to it and she did not touch it and she did not say anything at all, and about nineteen people watched her not say anything and a clerk of nineteen years entered the not-saying and entered that nobody filled it.

The man of about thirty-one said one thing to her from about nine feet away and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**It goes up the road in the morning and I will bring it back down the same way and I will put it on those stones myself and I have not got a way to pay you for the two days and I am not going to pretend I have.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who says out loud in front of nineteen people that he has not got a way to pay a person has done the only thing this district has an instrument for, and that the district's instrument is a yard and a yard is a room, **and that a man who cannot say what a day of a thing is worth and a man who has not got a way to pay for two days of one are the same man and neither of them is anybody's bearer and the fifth of the five did not move at about ten in the morning on the tenth of the fourth month.**

Nobody picked it up. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the woman of fifty-eight had been asked for a round and had refused to price it and had been asked for four houses and had refused to price that and had now been told out loud in a yard that there is no money for two days of it, **and that she has now said the same shape of sentence three times in eleven days to three different men and that a woman who says the same true thing three times is not a woman who has run out of things to say and is a woman who has run out of people who can price one day, and that this is the fifth of the five and it is not a wage and it is not going to be.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against put her in the six things at about half past ten in the morning in front of about nineteen people, and it was the fifth time anybody had put anything to her in a yard and the third time it had been the same shape of question, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk of the ward market had asked her in the second month and the yard had asked her on the twenty-third of the third month and this is the third asking in a yard and is not a new question and is not added to anything.

He asked her whether she would come up the hill and walk the yoke down the bank herself, and she said no in four seconds and gave the reason, and a man of about nineteen counted the reason and got thirty-two, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**Then it is not mine to walk down. I have said for eleven days that I cannot say what a day of it is worth and this is a day of it.**"

Nobody picked it up. A man of fifty-six said that a woman who refuses a thing for a reason that is a figure she does not have is not refusing it because she does not want it and that the two are not the same refusal and neither of them is a refusal of the offer of four houses, **and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the seven refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and the seven are seven and are not an eighth and were not made larger by a woman in a yard saying a true thing about a figure she does not have.**

Then he asked the registrar of this district's records office for one thing, and it was not a name on a line, and she said yes in four seconds and gave the reason, and a man of about nineteen counted the reason and got sixty-one, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**Put the eight days in my book. A day a stranger has printed is a day somebody is going to be told about, and a day in a book is a day a person can be asked about, and this basin has never had a book in which a printed day could be found by anybody who wanted to find it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that eight days went into a book in that office at about eleven in the morning on the tenth of the fourth month and that a clerk of nineteen years read them back and got them right, and that they are days and not figures and not a schedule and not a toll, **and that the man who asked was not asked why he wanted them and that the not-asking is entered and that a man of fifty-six said afterwards that a man who asks a woman to put a stranger's days in her book has not asked her for anything in eleven days and that is the first of the two steps in a yard and it is not a declaration and the clerk entered the word and entered that she was not going to explain it.**

---

It came at about eleven in the morning the way the others have come, unasked and into the same place, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard had asked for it and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it.

> **A THING TAKEN FROM A PERSON WHO WAS NOT ASKED IS NOT A TOLL. A TOLL IS A THING A NAMED PERSON PAYS UNDER A STATED TERM. A YOKE-LOAD IS A JOB.**
>
> **A JOB TAKEN IS STILL A JOB, AND THE PERSON IT WAS TAKEN FROM IS STILL THE PERSON WHO DOES IT.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam read the last line out loud in the ordinary voice, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he read it and that nobody asked him to, and that he did not disagree with it, **and that this is the first of these that nobody in this yard has disagreed with and that she entered the fact and entered that she is not going to make anything of it and that a man of fifty-six said that a rule which agrees with something a man said in a yard is a rule this district will obey and cannot un-obey and that the man who said it does not want that and was not asked whether he wants it.**

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was in that yard and had not been sent for and had not been asked anything, and that the copy of the schedule is in the ward market's own book and that the man who made it has not been asked what a lock is, **and that a man who copies a thing he does not understand in fifty minutes and gives the copy away is the reason eight days were read out in a yard yesterday and is owed eleven coppers a week for it and there is no money in this district that anybody can pay anybody with and the fourth refusal for the same reason is not a fourth refusal.**

Then the man of about twenty-three said the thing himself, and it went in the minute in his own words and nobody counted it because nobody was asked to.

"**I copied eight days and I do not know what a lock is and I would like it recorded that I would have copied it just the same and that this is not a thing anybody is owed money for and that I have said so three times and I am not going to say it a fourth.**"

---

The eleventh of April came up out of a night with a hard frost on it, the same as the first, and the dray went up the road at about half past five in the morning with two people in it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk of nineteen years was at the bottom of the lane at about half past five and was not sent for and was there on her own account.

The man of about thirty-one was on the box and the man with the portfolio was beside him with the case under his arm and the yoke was lashed to the tailboard with about nine feet of line, and a clerk of nineteen years entered all three of those and entered that nobody watched it go except a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat at the third house up the lane from the scale, who was not sent for and was not asked anything and stood at her own door in the dark until it was round the bend, **and that this district does not have an instrument for a person who stands at a door at half past five in the morning and does not say why, and did not make one and is not going to.**

A man of fifty-six said that a dray going up a road with two people in it is the ordinary case and has been the ordinary case for eighteen months, **and that the yoke on the tailboard is not the ordinary case and that the whole of the difference between those two sentences is that one of them had an owner and the other one did not, and that the owner was asked yesterday whether she would walk it down the bank herself and said no in four seconds and was not asked whether it could go on a cart, and that a thing a person has been asked about and has not agreed to and that is being done anyway is a thing this district has done twice in two months, once in the second month with a round of about ninety houses and once this week with a piece of wood off a stone, and that the two are not joined and the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and the registrar's own count at twelve and neither of them moved at about half past six in the morning on the eleventh of the fourth month.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the last of it and he said it to the lane and not to anybody, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that she is not going to put a number on it.

"**Eight days in eleven days and one of them a night and the eighth one is tonight and the ninth row is not a road and has a word in it where the other eight have a day, and a dray is going up the only road that climbs with a thing off a woman's stones on the back of it, and the two of those are not joined and I am not going to join them in a lane at six in the morning, and I would like somebody to write down that I said I would not and said it anyway, in my own words.**"

Nobody wrote it down, because a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and said so about himself eleven times in four months, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he asked and that it was not written and that the asking is entered and that the not-writing is entered, **and that the count of things this district does not have is five and the count of instruments built and not named is six and the count of documents nobody owns is three and the protected count is five and no sixth was added and the conditions with no end on it are four and there is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and none of the seven of those moved at about half past six in the morning on the eleventh of the fourth month.**

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH, WHICH IS THE LAST DAY IN A COLUMN HEADED OPEN ON A SHEET A STRANGER DATED THE FIRST OF IT, A DRAY WENT UP A ROAD WITH TWO PEOPLE IN IT AND A YOKE ON THE TAILBOARD THAT NOBODY ASKED THE OWNER ABOUT, AND NOBODY WROTE DOWN THE ONLY THING THAT WAS SAID ABOUT IT.**
