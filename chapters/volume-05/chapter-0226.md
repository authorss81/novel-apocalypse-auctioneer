# Chapter 226: Where The Work Went

---

The twenty-seventh of April was the day after a man said a true thing in a yard, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the second sheet was on the trestle table with the chair standing beside it, and the strip of paper was still underneath.

The three figures went out loud and were read back off the board by the man of fifty-six, who got all three of them right, and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and the seat of a chair is not a fifth.

"**A hundred and twenty-four days on the board and four hundred and forty days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and fifty-four, and the last day of this month is three days off, and nobody has been asked to carry a ledger since yesterday morning.**"

Nobody had been asked, and the record says not asked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that, **and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and none of them has been ruled since the second of January and she did not rule one this morning and is not going to.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the half of it that nobody had said, and he said it before the yard had finished standing about, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The work did not stop. The work stopped for one man and went somewhere else, and it will go somewhere else again tomorrow, and the page that has his name in a column on it does not need a reason and does not need him and does not need anybody to have been asked. A column is a column. I have said that about three of them in a month and this is the third and I am saying it before somebody else counts it.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who has said the same shape of true sentence three times in a month has made a habit of it, and that a habit of a true sentence is still a true sentence, and that the four columns of not-askings did not move and that the six instruments did not move.

The registrar of this district's records office said the other half of it in the ordinary voice and did not wait to be asked, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and eight, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**The man of about nineteen at the ward market is not well four days a month and on those four days a ledger goes from this room to a counter at the end of a lane and back, and until the twenty-second of this month it went with a man of about thirty-one who was in the room. It has not gone since. It is a book of nineteen rows and four columns and it weighs about as much as a full bucket, and it is two hundred yards each way, and I am not going to have it carried by a man who has not been asked.**"

Nobody said they would take it. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud in front of about nineteen people that this yard was not going to ask anybody to carry it, and it was entered that he said it and that he was not asked to and that the clerk was not going to make a number of it, and that a yard that stops itself from asking has not refused anybody and cannot be counted either way.

The counter at the end of the lane is a counter in a room nine feet by eleven with a hatch in the wall of it, and the ledger stands on a table under that hatch with nineteen ruled rows and four columns and about nine pounds of paper in it, and the man of about nineteen who keeps that counter was not well on that day, and the table is four feet high.

The room has a stove in it and a chair and a jug of water and a window about the size of a hand with the shutter closed against the wind, and the ledger has been on that table since about ten in the morning and it is a quarter past two in the afternoon, and nobody in that room knew it was there except the man who was not well and had gone home at about eleven.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked down to the end of the lane at about half past two and looked at it through the hatch and did not go in, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he looked at it and that he did not go in and that she was told about it by a man of about thirty-four who digs loam and not by the man who mends fencing, **and that a room nine feet by eleven with a book on a table in it is not a page and is not a document and is not one of the three documents nobody owns, and that the three are three and did not move at about half past two in the afternoon on the twenty-seventh of the fourth month.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered the three figures of it and entered that nobody was in that room, **and that a book of nineteen rows is a book and not a bearer and not a figure about a person, and that a table four feet high is a table and is not one of the six instruments this district built and not named and is not a seventh.**

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat at the third house up the lane from the scale was in that yard and had not been sent for, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, whether she would carry a ledger two hundred yards each way on the days the man of about nineteen is not well.

She said it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-three, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I carried one on the twenty-second because it was in my arms and I did not know it was a job, and it was a Friday and I did not know that either, and I have not thought about it once since, and I am not going to say yes to it in a yard and I am not going to say no to it either, and I want it written down that nobody asked me and that I have not said no.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her one more thing about it, in front of about nineteen people, and whether the goat had been fed, and she said the honest answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-four, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**The goat was fed at six and it is fed at six and it does not know what day it is, and that is the whole of what I have said and I am saying it because a man in this yard keeps asking me things like I am a counter and I am not a counter and my name is not on anything.**"

The minute has it that a woman said a day of the week and that no weekday is attached to any date in this district, and that she was not going to correct her because she was not wrong about the carrying, **and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the record says not asked, and that it is the first time in four months that a person in this yard has asked for the not-asking to be written down, and that she wrote it down, and that a request for a thing to be written down is not a column and she did not rule a fifth.**

It came at about half past twelve and nobody had asked for it, and it arrived in the same place the others arrive in, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard asked for it and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it.

> **A NAME PRINTED AGAINST AN OFFICE IS A NAME SERVING AN OFFICE. A NAME SERVING AN OFFICE IS A FIGURE.**
>
> **A FIGURE SERVING AN OFFICE CAN BE ASKED WHAT THE OFFICE ASKED OF HIM, AND HE CAN ANSWER, AND THE ANSWER IS GOOD ENOUGH.**
>
> **A DISCLOSURE IS AN INSTRUMENT. A DISCLOSURE PREVENTS A THING BEING DONE WITHOUT A PERSON KNOWING.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam disagreed with the last of those three out loud and in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A disclosure is not an instrument, it is a man telling a thing once, and what it prevents is somebody being able to say afterwards that nobody knew, and it does not stop a page having a column in it with a name in it, and it does not stop the name being the next one. I said a true thing yesterday and it is on nobody's page and the ledger is still going to have to go two hundred yards each way.**"

It was entered that this is the first time since the first of April that one person has disagreed with two of these out loud, and that the man who did it is the man who did it the first time on the fifth of this month, and that a man who disagrees with a rule twice is not becoming a habit of disagreeing, he is two rules and two disagreements.

The woman of fifty-eight was in the yard at about two in the afternoon and had come down the bank at about one, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in his own words and not in the six things, what she thought about a name being on a page, and she said it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and six, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**A name said once in a yard is a name four hundred people have and a name said twice is a name a counter can ask for, and that is the rule of this yard and I learned it here and I said it on the sixteenth of this month about eleven doors of my own, and nobody has ever once used it about anybody but me. He has been in this yard three days and his name is being said in every yard in four districts and he is not mine and I am not going to be the woman who did it to him.**"

Nobody contradicted her. The man of fifty-six said that a woman of fifty-eight has taken a rule she made for herself and put it in front of a man she has met twice, and that a rule a person makes for herself and then uses against somebody else's harm is the only instrument this district has built and not named in four months, **and that the six are a rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security, and that a woman saying a true thing of her own about a man is not a seventh of them, and the clerk refused the seventh in about four seconds and was not asked to refuse it.**

The day came down and the ledger stayed on the table under the hatch, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam went up the bank at about four in the afternoon with his arm in a sling to carry a bucket he could not carry, and the woman of fifty-eight took the bucket off him and said nothing at all, and it was entered that she said nothing and that nobody asked her to and that a person who takes a bucket off a man and says nothing has not been asked and has not refused and the record says not asked.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF THE FOURTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM SAID THE WORK DID NOT STOP BUT WENT SOMEWHERE ELSE, A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A GOAT ASKED FOR THE NOT-ASKING TO BE WRITTEN DOWN, A PANEL SAID A DISCLOSURE PREVENTS A THING BEING DONE WITHOUT A PERSON KNOWING AND HE DISAGREED WITH IT OUT LOUD, AND A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT USED THE YARD'S OWN RULE ABOUT A NAME SAID TWICE TO PROTECT A MAN WHO IS NOT HERS.**
