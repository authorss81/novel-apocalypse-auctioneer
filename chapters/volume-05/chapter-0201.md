# Chapter 201: The First Of April

---

The first of April came up out of a night with a hard frost on it and the cold the second month had, and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about eight in the morning because a man of about thirty-one had gone out of it at first light with a stub about the size of a coin in the coat of him.

The month turned, and it was said once, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it and entered that the month before it was the third month and the month before that was the second month, **and that the year that began on the first of January is the eighteenth after the Long Fracture, and that the train on the siding came in on the eleventh of February of the year before that one, and that the year is in no chapter anybody has read and it is the one thing a man who works it out from the eleventh of February alone will get wrong.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked for the figure out loud, because he asks for figures out loud, and a man of fifty-six gave it and did not pretend to have arrived at it in front of anybody.

"**The board outside the room has carried the twenty-fourth of December for ninety-eight days and nobody has washed it, and the train on the siding had stood four hundred and thirteen days on the last day of the third month and four hundred and fourteen days today.**"

Nothing was said against it. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a month turning is not an event, **and that a clerk is not going to put a number on that, and that the count of boards did not move and the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and has nothing on it and is going to have nothing on it this month as far as anybody standing in this yard knows.**

The tank at the back of the building four miles down a lane was at nothing, which it had not been since the first of the second month, and it had gone down by itself in about nine days, and the man of about fifty-five who keeps that building had not drawn it down. **The crack in it is dry for the first time since the twenty-eighth of the second month and he has still not put a fifth mend in it, and he was asked in the six things whether he wanted to now that it was dry, and said no in about two seconds and gave no reason and a clerk entered that he gave no reason.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a man who has refused a mend five times over four months and has said no to a dry crack is a man with a position, and that the district has never had one of his about anything.

"**The count of things this district does not have is five and it did not move today, and the fifth of them has now been said out loud in this yard three times in eleven days by three people who did not say it at the same time.**"

Nobody argued with that. A man of fifty-six said that the three people are a man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, a woman of fifty-eight, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam, and that the three said three different sentences and that the three sentences are not a finding and are not a column, **and that a man who mends fencing has been keeping the count the way a man keeps a tally and that keeping a count is not the same as having a column and that the district has four columns of not-askings and no column for this and did not make one this morning.**

---

The yoke was still on the stones at the top of the hill and it had been there for eight days, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up the bank at about half past nine and looked at it and did not touch it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him to and that he did not.

She came up at about ten with no yoke and two empty buckets, and she was carrying the buckets because her hands were used to carrying something, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her in the six things, in front of about nine people, what she wanted, and she answered.

It had been put to her twice before, once in the second month and once on the twenty-third of the third month in front of nine people, and a man of fifty-six entered that this is the third asking and not a new question and is not added to anything.

She said it standing up and not loudly, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-four, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I have been on that hill nine years and I asked for the round to be somebody else's job in the second month and nobody did it, and now a man has offered me four houses of it and I cannot say what a day of it is worth, and I am not going to take a thing I cannot say the worth of.**"

The man who had offered was standing at the bottom of the bank with a hand flat on a scale frame, and he was the man of about thirty-six who keeps the scale at the sluice end of the loams, and he had offered the four houses on the morning of the thirty-first of the third month, and nobody had asked him why and he had not said.

A man of fifty-six said that a man of about thirty-six offered a woman a job and could not say what a day of it was worth, and that this is the fifth of the five things this district does not have coming out of the mouth of a person who weighs sacks for a living, **and that a way to pay a person who is not in a household is not a wage, and that a wage is a figure, and that a yoke-load is a job, and that a person who cannot say what a day is worth cannot take the job, and that the not-saying is not rudeness and he is not going to have it written down as rudeness.**

Nobody argued with it. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the offer stands and is not withdrawn, and the man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said that he had said it once and was not going to say it twice, **and that four houses out of about ninety is not a round and is not going to become a round, and that there is nothing the matter with the work and the whole of the matter is the figure and that he can weigh nine sacks a day and cannot weigh a day.**

The woman of fifty-eight said the rest of it in the yard, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-six, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**Then somebody is going to have to say what a day is worth, and nobody in this yard can, and I am fifty-eight and I am not going to be the one who guesses at it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman of fifty-eight is not a protected thing and is not one of the five things this district does not have and is not one of the four conditions with no end on it, **and that the protected count is five and no sixth was added today, and that the conditions count is four and did not move either, and that a woman with a yoke on a stone is not in any of the three lists and is not in the fourth one and was not put in one this morning.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the offer is the third thing anybody has tried on her in four months and that two of the other two were this district's fault, **and that a woman who has been asked what she wants three times and has said the same shape of sentence three times has not been asked a new question and is not owed one, and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.**

---

The cart came on the last day of the third month and the yard had said the first of the next month, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the yard was a day out and entered no reason for it, because nobody had one and a figure with no reason under it is a guess.

Six households at the sluice end of the loams and a sheet each, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the state of them in the yard without being asked, and it has not moved since the twenty-first of the third month.

"**One has signed and two have refused and three have not been asked again, and the three are not refusals and are not consents and are not a column, and this yard decided on the nineteenth of the second month not to decide about the three and has not gone back on it.**"

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households was at the back of that yard and had not been sent for, and he said that the third time a sheet comes down that lane a cart goes past his door before six in the morning, **and that a man who keeps a tally for six households is not a way of telling six households anything, and that he has been asked twice to be told what to do about the three and has not been told and is not asking again this morning.**

A man of fifty-six said that a man who is not asking is a man who has been refused, and the man of about forty-eight said that it was not, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that both halves were in the minute in their own words and that the registrar's own count of refusals to join two things is twelve and did not move and the district's is thirteen and did not move either, **and that a man who is not asking has not been refused and a man who is not asking is not a count of anything and the record says not asked.**

---

The court sat on the last day of the third month and its next day is whatever a clerk of the ward market names, and nobody has named one, and about nine people were asked in a yard on the morning of the first of April whether anybody had gone to the counter to be heard and nobody had.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether a court that cannot fetch a person could fetch the woman of fifty-eight, and a man of fifty-six said no and gave the reason in a sentence, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got twenty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A court can hear a figure and write it down and it cannot walk up a hill, and not asked is not refused, and the record says not asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who has not come to a thing has not refused the thing and cannot be counted either way, **and that the fifth line of the court's second sheet says the terms of a house thirty-four miles east are valid until the people inside them can be heard and the people inside them have not been heard and that this morning made the second half of that sentence shorter and did not make the first half of it any better.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was not in that yard and had gone out of it at first light and nobody sent for him and a clerk entered that he was not asked a first thing, **and that what was said between him and this district on the thirty-first of the third month is in a minute in her own words and is not a document this district can produce, and that a minute in a person's own words is not a fourth document nobody owns and the three did not move.**

Then the word came up, and it came up because a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked it and nobody else in that yard had thought about it since the nineteenth of October.

He asked whether the word steward was finished, and a clerk of nineteen years read out what is written in the ward market's own book on that line, which is a word and a date and a name, and the word is steward and the date is the thirty-first of March and the name is the man the figure of twenty-one years is against.

The date has passed. The season started this morning and it is the second half of the word and the entry does not say so and nobody has written it down, and the clerk entered it of her own accord and was not asked to and said so.

"**The first half was a word in a book and the second half is a season and the season started on the first of the fourth month and nobody has stood in a frame since the nineteenth of October and I am entering that this is not a delay for the third time in twenty-three weeks and nobody asked me to.**"

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a man who was named a steward by nine households on a night in October has spent the winter carrying somebody else's water, **and that a word with a season on the back of it is not finished because a date went past, and that the district has been saying for four months that a term ends a use without a man standing up and it does not finish a man.**

Nobody said the opposite. A man of fifty-six said that a man who has been named a steward and has not stood in a frame is a man with a title and a job and that this district has no word for that and did not make one, **and that the count of instruments this district has built and not named is six and the six are a rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security, and that a title with no job on it is not a seventh and was refused entry in this yard this morning and the count did not move.**

---

The lane four miles down from a lower terrace was dry for the first time since the seventeenth of the third month and about four people were on it, and a man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was at the end of it with a sheet in his hands that is not his.

The butt at the back of the brick building is at four days and it was at nine on the seventeenth of the third month and about four hundred yards away a tank came up within a hand's breadth of its lip, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the word for that is a word about a river and not about a thing anybody did, **and that a butt is a store and not a source and that a district which emptied a store to fill a tank has not moved the water, it has moved the days.**

The woman of about thirty-eight who keeps that building was on her own step and was not sent for and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked a first thing, and she said the thing herself and it was in twenty-four words the second time she has said it and she knew it was the second time.

"**If I tell a clerk of a house where my wall is then my wall is on his sheet, and I have a place and nobody's name is on it.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a person who keeps a building is not its bearer and is not a keeper of it and is not a party to a claim on a place in it, **and that a claim passed on the twenty-fourth of the third month on a sheet that did not name a building and did not name a family, and that a claim on a place that is not named is a claim that can be put on any place anybody in this basin cares to write it on.**

The man of about twenty-three read the sheet out in the lane to four people who cannot read, which he has done before, and this time he read the second line of it as well, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he read two lines where he has read one four times, **and that the second line is the day at the bottom of it and the second line is the twenty-ninth of December and it is not on the copy the family was given, and that he has still got no way to make a man at a counter take a name off a sheet and is not going to stand in a lane and pretend he has.**

Nobody argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that a man who reads out a second line in a lane is doing the district's work and is not being paid for it and was refused eleven coppers a week in the second month and refused again in the third month, **and that the same reason behind a second refusal is not a new refusal and the man is owed nothing and is owed a figure for what he does and there is still no money in this district that anybody can pay anybody with.**

---

The other thing on the first of April was said at about half past four in the afternoon and it was said by a man who keeps a road and is not a bearer of it, and nobody had sent for him either.

He had come up on foot from a road house at the second place and had walked nine miles to do it and had done that four times in five weeks, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him in the six things, in front of about nine people, who had opened his road.

He answered, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Somebody opened the road on the night of the twenty-second of the second month and I was nine miles off it, and it was not me, and I have not seen who it was, and I am not going to say a name for it.**"

Nothing was said against it for a moment. A man of fifty-six said that a route keeper has told this district what a road did on a night in the second month and has not been asked for a figure and has not given one, **and that a schedule is not a figure and a figure is not a schedule and the two have never been joined in this district, and that this afternoon is the first time anybody here has said out loud that a road was opened by a person who does not keep it.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a road opening is not a thing and a road being open is a thing, and that the whole of the difference between them is one night's work nine miles from this yard, **and that a person who opens a road and is not asked who opened it is not the same as a person who is not asked, and that this district has spent four months finding that out about a name on a page and is now finding it out about a road.**

The route keeper did not pick it up. A man of fifty-six entered that he was asked one question and gave one answer and that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of a road and has never been asked to be one, **and that the count of refusals to read is nine and this is not a refusal to read and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in the ledger for the next reading.**

A man of about thirty-one had been a day off on a road and nobody in this district knew which day he would be back and a clerk entered that he was not sent for and that nobody here went with him, and that the stub he took with him is about the size of a coin and has been folded and unfolded so often that it is coming apart at the folds, **and that the stub does not carry the twenty-ninth of December and that the day which decides the claim is on a sheet in a pocket two days of road away and that the entry about it made in the second month is unchanged.**

**AND ON THE FIRST OF THE FOURTH MONTH THE MONTH TURNED AND WAS SAID ONCE, THE BOARD OUTSIDE THE ROOM HAD CARRIED THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF DECEMBER FOR NINETY-EIGHT DAYS, THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD FOUR HUNDRED AND FOURTEEN DAYS, A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX OFFERED A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT FOUR HOUSES OF A ROUND AND COULD NOT SAY WHAT A DAY OF IT WAS WORTH, AND A ROUTE KEEPER SAID IN FORTY-FIVE WORDS THAT SOMEBODY HAD OPENED HIS ROAD ON A NIGHT IN THE SECOND MONTH AND IT WAS NOT HIM.**
