# Chapter 248: A Public Index

---

The twentieth of the fifth month was warm and still, and about nine people went up the road that climbs again at about half past five in the morning because the light in a barn with one window is better early, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody was sent for and that the record about the half past five says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures at the bottom of the lane with his left cuff buttoned over the scab on his wrist, and a clerk of about nineteen wrote them on the back of the same sheet.

"**A hundred and forty-seven days on the board and four hundred and sixty-three days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and seventy-seven, and a hundred and thirty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the next month is twelve days off, and we are going up to the same barn for the second morning in a row and I would like it on the record that I did not enjoy yesterday and I went.**"

They went up past the top field in the dark and the man of about forty-one who keeps the road house at the top of the road was outside his own door at about ten past seven with his stick, and he had not been sent for and he was not asked a first thing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both of those things and entered that this district has never once given that man a day to be here on and that nobody gave him one this morning either, and that a man who is standing outside his own door when eleven people go past in the dark is a man who was awake, and that a man being awake is not a man who was asked.

He came up the two hundred yards of shoulder with the party and stood at the edge of it for about four minutes and said nothing at all, and then he went back down to his own door, and a clerk entered that he had come and had not spoken and that the record about the four minutes says not asked.

In that barn about nineteen people were on the beaten earth by about nine and the covenant was on a board that two men had carried in from another building, and it had not been read out again and a clerk entered that a document that has been read out once and adopted and put up on a board is a document, and that the board is not the document.

The thing that happened at about half past ten was not on any sheet anybody had brought up the road, and a clerk of nineteen years entered before anybody spoke that she had been told about it by a man who had come down the road that climbs the day before, and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against had been told about it by the same man, and that neither of them had told anybody in this yard.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said what it was, in the plain voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and forty-three.

"**They have decided to put my name in a column in that book and to call me the man who convenes, and I am not going to stand in this barn and say I do not know what I think about it, because I do know. Four months ago I stood at the back of a book in this district and I told anybody who would listen that a person at the back of a book is a thing that can be fastened to. I was right about that then. I have been wrong about a pin and about a notch in a sill and I said so out loud both times in front of other people. This is the same thing happening to me from the other end and I am not going to be the man who is surprised by it.**"

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the part about the room, in the ordinary voice, and the count came to a hundred and twenty-eight.

"**I asked the nine questions. I want that said out loud in this barn before anybody writes anything, because the man you are putting in that column is not the man who knocked on nine doors, and I knocked on nine doors, and the man who went two days of road to a second place that had not answered is the man you are putting in the column. I am not asking for it to be changed. I have said out loud for four months that I am not going to be a founder, and a column is not a chair, and I have found out this morning that a column is a different thing from a chair and I have not had my hand on the sheet.**"

Nobody argued with him about it. A man of fifty-six said that a man who has said out loud that he does not want a thing and then let it go to somebody else has not lost anything, and that the six instruments this district built and not named are six, and that a column is not an instrument and did not become a seventh.

A man of about thirty-four who keeps a tally for six households said one thing about the column from the edge of the floor, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and twenty-four.

"**I keep six households in a book that nobody in this basin has ever read out loud, and the whole of what I have wanted for six months is for somebody who is not me to say the numbers in a room. So I am not going to be the man who stands up in this barn and objects to a man being named. I want it said that I said that, because in three weeks there is going to be somebody in this basin who decides that six households and a convener are the same kind of thing, and I would like there to be a line in the book saying I said no to that this morning before anybody thought of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who objects to a thing nobody has proposed yet has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the three weeks says not asked, and that the six households have not moved on any of the twenty-nine days since the twenty-first of last month.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the price of it, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and forty-nine.

"**Here is what it costs and it costs one thing and I am not going to dress it up as two. On the eighteenth of this month four hundred yards from where I am standing, a woman I have worked beside for eleven months wrote a rule that says a book is kept in one room by one person and that person is the only one who may look in it, and a body four hundred miles away wrote a notice in which that room is described and my name is not on it, and she stood in a yard and said out loud that not being named is not a comfort. Today my name goes in a column in a book a stranger can walk up to, and from this afternoon a stranger can walk into that yard and say his name instead of asking for a page.**"

"**And this yard's own rule is that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, and I wrote that rule into this district's practice in April, and I am not going to try to take it off me this morning, and if I tried I would be the first man in this basin to be given a thing by his own good sense and to want it back a week later. That is the whole of the price. I am not founding anything. I am going to be findable, and findable is a worse door than a page, and I know that because I spent four months building the page.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said the price of a thing out loud in a room before he took it has not consented to anything that is not on the sheet, and entered that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, and that he said so out loud in a barn in front of about nineteen people and did not ask anybody to take it off, and that a convener is a person and is not a holder and is not a bearer of the water and is not a founder.

Then the registrar of this district's records office signed a different portion of that covenant in that barn, in front of about nineteen people, and said what her portion was, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and thirty-nine.

"**I have signed the sixth line and not the other five, and the sixth line is the one about where the figures are written and who may read them, and I have signed it as the person who keeps the book and not as a bearer and not as a holder and not as a steward, and I want that read back in this room. My authority in that book is the authority of one woman in one room and it is not shared with the man whose name is in the column and it is not diminished by his being in it, and if the four places below the water sign their own parts on their own doors then this covenant does not have one holder and it does not have two either, and it has a room.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a registrar who signs one line out of six and says out loud which one is the only way anybody has ever known which line anybody was holding, and a clerk entered that the six lines are six lines and are not six figures about six people and are not a column of persons, and that a person who signs one thing is not a bearer of the other five.

They came down the road that climbs at about half past four and were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about seven, and about nine people were in it, and at about ten past seven a man stood at the gate and asked a question and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it before anybody answered it.

He asked whether the man who convenes was at that yard, and he said that he had been told the name and that he had come about four miles on the strength of the name and not on the strength of a page, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the man was standing about nine feet from the gate with a bucket in his hand and had come down the bank about two minutes before the stranger came up the lane.

He said he had come about a sheet of paper with a name on it and not for anything else, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in his own words and not in the six things, what he was going to do with a name, and the man said he was going to take it back down the road and put it in front of two other people and ask them whether they would pay a toll, and a clerk of nineteen years entered all of that in her own hand and entered that a person carrying a name out of this yard is a person and is not a bearer of a toll and is not a party of the fourth of four, and that nobody in that yard was asked whether they wanted to be put in front of two other people and that the record about that says not asked.

A man of fifty-six said that four strangers in four months is not a market, and that a yard that starts taking a name and handing back a page is a yard that is about four weeks away from being a counter of somebody else's, and that he had watched this yard get built out of a gate in five months and he was not going to help it get built out of a name, and a clerk entered that he said that and that nobody agreed with him and that the record about his objection says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a name in a column in a book a stranger may walk up to is a name a stranger can ask for at a gate, and that this is the first time in four months that anybody has come to that yard asking for a person instead of asking for a page, and that the man who was asked for answered from about nine feet away without being fetched, **and that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, and that the day the stranger came was the twentieth of the fifth month and it was not asked of anybody whether he should come.**

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against went up the bank at about ten past eight with a bucket in each hand and did not say anything to anybody about the man who had come to the gate, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he carried two buckets up a bank for the woman of fifty-eight and was not asked to and did not say why, and that a bucket carried up a bank by a man who has been made findable is still a bucket and is not a figure about either of them.

**AND ON THE TWENTIETH OF THE FIFTH MONTH A MAN OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS WAS PUT IN A COLUMN IN A BOOK A STRANGER MAY WALK UP TO IN A BARN ABOUT NINE MILES UP THE ONLY ROAD IN THIS DISTRICT THAT CLIMBS, HE SAID THE PRICE OF IT OUT LOUD BEFORE HE TOOK IT, A REGISTRAR SIGNED ONE LINE OUT OF SIX AND SAID SO OUT LOUD, AND AT TEN PAST SEVEN THAT EVENING A STRANGER STOOD AT A GATE FOUR HUNDRED YARDS AWAY AND ASKED FOR HIM BY HIS NAME.**
