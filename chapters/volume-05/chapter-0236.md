# Chapter 236: A Mark Is Not A Job

---

The eighth of the fifth month was the first day of the year with a sun in it that felt like one, and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning and the same nine were on a road four miles out past the loams at about half past one in the afternoon.

The figures went out loud in the yard before anybody left and the man of fifty-six read the board over the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing's shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and thirty-five days on the board and four hundred and fifty-one days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and sixty-five, and the twelfth of this month is four days off, and the fourth holding took water in the night of the nineteenth into the twentieth of last month, which is eighteen days ago, and about nine of us are going out and look at it.**"

Nobody sent for anybody. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud, standing at the gate, that the yard was going to walk out to the lock and that anybody who came was coming because they wanted to and not because they had been asked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that, and entered that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one.

The road out past the loams is about four miles and it goes up past the top field of a farm whose legal owner is not in this district, and the clerk entered that the fourth holding on the delivery sheet is that top field and that a sheet about a fourth holding and a lock at a channel are two records about two things and are not joined, and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and neither of them moved.

The sun came off the top of the loams at about half past eleven and the whole four miles of that road was in front of them the whole way, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked at the back and did not overtake anybody the whole way, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that party was asked to walk and that nine of them walked and that the record about the four miles says not asked.

The last half mile before the channel is not a road. It is a cart track between two banks with a wire on the left of it that has not been tight since before anybody in this district was born, and the water in the channel could be heard from about nine hundred yards out, and there is a smell off it in the afternoon that is a smell of wet iron and not of anything else.

Two of the nine had not come out past the top field and were not asked to and the record about the two of them says not asked, and one of the two of them is a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road, who walked out with them and turned back at the field gate and went home, and nobody asked him where he was going and he did not say.

The lock was in its channel where they had put it back on the fourth of this month, with the frame bolted down and about nine inches of water standing above the sill, and there were about nine people standing on the bank of a channel four miles from anybody's kitchen.

The frame is about four feet high and it is bolted to two pieces of oak let into the bank on either side, and the oak is older than anybody in that yard and darker at the bottom where it stands in the water, and the screw comes up through the middle of the frame to a handle about a foot and a half long, and the pin goes through the screw where the handle is pinned on, and the sill at the bottom is a stone about two feet wide with one notch cut in it. The board that goes in the notch is about nine inches wide and about four feet long and it is not there, because it is May, and the man of about forty-one said later that it stands on end against the north wall of a building nine miles up a road and has stood there every summer since before he could reach the top of the frame.

There was no wind in that channel at about half past one and the noise of the water going under the sill was the only noise for about four minutes, and nobody spoke for those four minutes, and the clerk entered that a man of about nineteen counted nothing in those four minutes because there was nothing to count and that he was not asked to count something instead.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against read the sill, and he read it in about two minutes, and he was wrong, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got ninety-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The sill has a notch cut in it and the notch is the record. That is the height the water is held to and everything below that notch is what a lock is for, and a notch cut in a sill is a mark somebody made to come back to, and the water that came in the night of the nineteenth into the twentieth stood up to that notch and not an inch past it, and a mark like that is a gauge and a gauge wants somebody to read it.**"

A man of about forty-one who keeps a road house at the top of the road was standing about four feet away and had been standing there since before any of them arrived, and he had not been sent for and had not been asked a first thing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both of those things before anybody said anything to him.

He said it was not right, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and he crouched down in the wet and put his thumb in the notch, and the count came to eighty.

"**That is not a mark of how high the water has been. That is where the board goes. There is one notch in that sill and there has been one since before my father walked this road, and it is a cut for the edge of a plank, and a plank goes in it and the water is held above the plank about a foot deep and no more, and the board is not there now because it is May.**"

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam got down into the water to look at the bottom of the sill while the man of about forty-one was still talking, and he was in it up to about the knee and out again in about two minutes, and the man of about fifty-six did not go in after him and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not go in either, and the clerk entered that two men did not go in and that nobody asked them to and that the record about the two of them says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the rest of it and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and three.

"**And I have walked past that notch four thousand times and I never once thought it was a record of anything, because it is a hole a thing fits in. A notch is not a gauge, and a gauge is not a notch, and a mark on a thing does not become a job by anybody deciding to read it, and this man of twenty-one years has now been wrong in front of me twice in six days, about nineteen people the first time and about nine of us the second, and both times he said so out loud before we found it.**"

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the second half of it himself, out loud, in front of about nine people standing on a bank four miles from a kitchen, in his own words, and the count came to a hundred and sixteen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I read a mark as a job. That is the whole of it and I am not going to dress it up as a discovery about water. There is one notch in that sill and it is a place a plank goes and I have spent two days building a gauge out of a hole a board fits in, and a man of about forty-one who walks past it four thousand times put his thumb in it and had it finished before I had finished saying it, and that is what nine years of digging is worth against two days of reading, and I would rather it was worth nothing than have it be worth that.**"

The clerk entered that a mark may not carry a job and that he has now read one as one, and that neither of the two sayings is a figure and neither is a column, and that the seven instruments this district built and not named are six, and that the seventh was proposed once, in a yard, on the fourth of this month, and refused once, and has not been proposed a second time since, and that a rule refused once and never again after that is a rule that has been learned and not a rule that has been dropped.

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam refused the join that had been available to him all afternoon, in the ordinary voice, standing in a channel with his arm in a sling, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and four.

"**There are two chalk lines on the inside of a brick tank four miles down a lane and there is one notch in a sill four miles the other way, and a mark on a tank is a mark a man drew with a piece of chalk in the dark and a notch in a sill is a hole a plank goes in, and I am refusing that join now while I am standing next to both of them, and it is the ninth of that join in this district and the number of times I have refused it is not part of it.**"

There was a smell off the channel in that afternoon that is a smell of wet iron and cold mud and nothing else, and it stayed on all nine of them for the walk back until about a mile in, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that nine men who have stood in a channel for about two hours smell of it for the rest of the day and that a body four hundred miles away does not know that and never will, and the clerk entered that and entered that a smell is not a finding about a place.

The man of about fifty-five who keeps a building would have liked to hear that and was not there and nobody sent word to him, and the clerk entered that a man who was not told was not asked and has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the two chalk lines and the crack between them says not asked.

The man of about forty-one who keeps the road house walked back up about a quarter past three with his stick and got away before the light and was gone over the turn at about half past seven, and the clerk entered that he went and that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing about going and that a day a man is on a road is a day he is here and is not a figure about anybody.

They came back at about half past four and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam was carrying his right arm in a sling and his left hand up over his eyes against a low sun on the last mile, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked on his left side for about nine minutes and did not say why and was not asked.

**AND ON THE EIGHTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WALKED FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS AND A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-ONE PUT HIS THUMB IN A NOTCH IN A SILL AND SAID IT WAS WHERE THE BOARD GOES AND NOT A MARK OF ANYTHING, AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE HAD READ A MARK AS A JOB, AND A MARK MAY NOT CARRY A JOB.**
