# Chapter 231: Four Places And A Word

---

The third of the fifth month came in clear and hard with the rain of the night gone out of the channel, and the yard of Lot Seventeen filled from about a quarter to ten.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures before the gate had properly stopped wanting a foot against it, and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and thirty days on the board and four hundred and forty-six days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and sixty, and a hundred and twenty-one days separate the second of January and this morning, and there is a thing on that table that was not there when I walked in.**"

There was a third thing on the trestle table. The slate with the copy of the page out of the cave was still lying under the second sheet with the chair printed on it, and on the top of that was a new sheet folded once across, and nobody in that yard had put it there. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the whole of that before anybody had thought to ask him for it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got forty-eight, and it went into the minute in his own words.

"**That table has now had three things put on it by somebody in another county and not one of them by anybody in this district, and I am saying that first because in about four minutes somebody is going to stand on all three of them at once.**"

Then he said the other half of it, which was the reason the sheet was worth a morning at all, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and the count came to ninety-two, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**That sheet has one word on it four times and the word is the one this district took off a stranger's schedule in the first week of the fourth month and has not said out loud in a yard since. The gate at the end of this yard is a gate and has been a gate for four months and I am not confusing the two. A body four hundred miles away can print what it likes and this district is not going to start saying it again because a sheet arrived.**"

Nobody picked that up. A man of fifty-six said that a word on a sheet is a word somebody else chose, and that a district which has given a thing its own word once does not lose the word by being handed a second one, and that the two gates in that sentence are two different objects and one of them is standing in this yard with a chair beside it and a foot of ice in the trough at the foot of the bank.

A clerk of nineteen years asked for the sheet to be read at about half past ten, and said out loud why she was asking, which is that a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one and nobody had asked it this morning. She read it in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them and read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **A BODY THAT KEEPS RECORDS OF HOLDINGS SENDS A TOLL ON A GATE TO EACH OF FOUR PLACES BY THE ROADS BELOW THE WATER.**
>
> **THE TOLL ON THE GATE IS ONE TOLL AND IT IS PAID BY THE PARTY OF EACH OF THE FOUR AND BY NOBODY ELSE. A GATE HOLDS A ROAD SHUT AND A ROAD BELOW A GATE IS A ROAD THAT IS SHUT.**
>
> **THE FOUR ARE NUMBERED AND NOT NAMED. THE FIRST OF THE FOUR. THE SECOND OF THE FOUR. THE THIRD OF THE FOUR. THE FOURTH OF THE FOUR. A PLACE IS THE FOURTH OF THE FOUR BY ITSELF UNTIL SOMEBODY SAYS IT IS NOT.**
>
> **THE TOLL IS PAID ONCE AND IS NOT PAID TWICE, AND A BODY THAT KEEPS RECORDS OF HOLDINGS IS NOT ASKED WHETHER A GATE HAS BEEN PAID.**

The clerk wrote down that a sheet of four lines printed by a body four hundred miles away and sent to a yard is a document somebody owns, and named the three this district does not own, the printed sheet at the fourth place, the notice about a party of nine who went thirty-four miles east, and a line in a public book with four names on it and a figure under the four names, and entered that the three did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the third of the fifth month and that a sheet from a body is not a fourth of them.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam stood about nine feet back from the table with his right arm across his body the way he had stood for a fortnight, and he said what the thing was, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A toll is a thing a named person pays under a term. That is what a toll is and that is the only thing a toll has ever been in this basin. This sheet has got four parties on it and not one person on it, and a party is not a person, and a toll that cannot be fastened to a person cannot be collected and cannot be refused either, and the record on all four of these is that nobody was asked.**"

The clerk entered that a party is not a bearer, that a party is not a holder, and that a place being the fourth of four by itself until somebody says it is not is not a finding about a place and is not a finding about a person, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one this morning and is not going to.

Then he refused the join everybody in that yard was already making, in the plain voice, and the count came to sixty-six, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**A toll on a gate and a chair over a charter are two instruments a body printed for two purposes about two different arrangements, and a thing that is a toll and a thing that is an offer cannot agree and cannot disagree, and I am refusing that join a sixth time in this district and a sixth is a sixth with six people watching it.**"

It went in that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve, and that neither moved at about eleven in the morning on the third of the fifth month, and that a second refusal of the same join is the same refusal and the number of days this district has spent on it does not make a new one.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing then did the thing he has done twice in a fortnight, which is to say a thing out loud before anybody else can join it up, and it was counted and came to seventy-nine, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**I am not going to be the man who decides that this district is the fourth of the four, and I am saying that in a yard with about nineteen people in it so that if somebody does decide it, it is on the page that a man noticed. A sheet that numbers four places and names none of them is a sheet that any yard in four districts can put itself in, and the putting is the toll.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who says out loud that he is not going to do a thing has not refused the thing, and that the sheet on that table is a thing nobody in this district was asked about, and that the record says not asked, **and that the count of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is eight and did not move at about a quarter past eleven in the morning on the third of the fifth month and the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and did not move and the two are not the same figure.**

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about eleven with a bucket in each hand and put them down where she has put them every morning since the seventeenth, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked up to her at the foot of the bank and stopped about nine feet short, as he always does, and asked her in his own words and not in the six things whether a toll on a thing that holds the water back is a toll on the woman who carries the water.

She gave the honest answer and it was counted and got seventy-two, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I carry water to eleven houses and I have never been asked to carry it for anybody's gate and I am not going to be asked now. If a body wants my two buckets it can come up this bank and say so to my face and I will put them down, and until it does there is a woman on this bank and not a party of the fourth of four.**"

The clerk entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that the eight did not move, and that a woman holding eleven houses of a round of about ninety and no figure on them at all is a person and not a party of anything, **and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and did not move at about eleven in the morning on the third of the fifth month.**

Nobody said thank you. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the half of it that nobody had said, and it was not counted because he had not asked for it to be.

He said the shoulder was going the other way. For a fortnight it had got no better and no worse and had been the same thing every morning, and since the second of this month he had been getting it up off the ground without the two goes the second had needed, and he did not know whether that was good news or whether it was a thing that gets worse later, and a man who announces a thing about his own body before anybody can ask him about it is doing the one thing this yard is worth more than the yard itself.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was at the far end of the trestle table when the sheet was read and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in his own words and not in the six things, whether he would make a copy of it. He said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I make copies of things this district owns. That is the whole of what I do and I have made four of them in a month and been paid for none of them. The moment I copy a thing nobody in this district has signed for, then this district owns it, and a district that owns a toll sheet is a district that is arguing about the toll, and I am not going to be the hand that does that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and that the eight did not move, and that a copy is a copy and is not a bearer and is not a figure about a person, and that the three documents this district does not own are three and did not move at about half past twelve in the afternoon on the third of the fifth month.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a tally for six households was in that yard for about six minutes in the middle of the morning and had not been sent for and was not asked a first thing, and the clerk entered that he was there and that the three households that have not been asked again are three and that nobody in that yard is going to ask him about them today.

The day came down hard about half past four. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam went out of the gate and up the bank with his right arm in the sling the woman of fifty-eight had lent him and had not been asked about, and the sheet with the four places on it was still on the trestle table, and the ninth place on the slate underneath it was still ruled and empty, and nobody had put a line on a board on any of the thirty-three days since the first of the fourth month.

**AND ON THE THIRD OF THE FIFTH MONTH A SHEET ARRIVED ON A TRESTLE TABLE THAT NOBODY HAD PUT IT ON, IT CARRIED A TOLL ON FOUR PLACES AND NOT ONE PERSON, A TOLL IS A THING A NAMED PERSON PAYS UNDER A TERM, AND THIS DISTRICT SPENT THE MORNING FINDING OUT THAT IT HAD BEEN SENT FOUR TOLLS TO FOUR PLACES THAT DID NOT SIGN FOR THEM.**
