# Chapter 240: Two Of Four

---

The twelfth of the fifth month was clear and the frost had gone out of the trough at the foot of the bank, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the first day of the next month was twenty days off.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and thirty-nine days on the board and four hundred and fifty-five days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and sixty-nine, and a hundred and thirty days separate the second of January and this morning, and this is the last day this block reaches and it is not the last day of anything else.**"

Then he did the thing he does, which is to put two documents into the same paragraph himself before anybody else can, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and twenty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The sheet on that table wants one toll paid by four places, and the notice under the leg of that table wants a record asked against four places, and two of the four have not answered, and this yard has held the fourth of the four since the third of this month and a house nine miles up has held it since the same morning. I have said twice in this yard that those two fours are not the same four, and I am saying it a third time this morning with both documents in the same breath, and if a clerk writes down that they are one four then a clerk has written down the only false sentence in this district's books this month.**"

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam refused the join in the ordinary voice, standing at the end of the trestle table with the sling still on, and the count came to ninety-seven, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**A toll and a rival record are two instruments with two purposes, and a notice that asks four places a question is not a sheet that takes four tolls, and a body that has put up a notice about a room is not a body that has asked a house for money, and if you cannot keep those two apart in one paragraph then this yard has started believing what it is given, and I have been the man who refuses that for nine days in front of about nineteen people and I am not stopping today.**"

It went in that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve, and that neither of them moved at about ten in the morning on the twelfth of the fifth month, and that a refusal with a bigger number of people in front of it is the same refusal and is not a tenth of anything.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in that yard and said the thing he had come to say, and he said it in the plain voice and not in a letter and not in a room, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Here is what I am going to do and it is two things and not nine. A body four hundred miles away has said two of its four have not answered. I am going to go and ask the two that have not answered, each of them separately, in their own doorway, about the thing they have not answered, and I am not going to take the other two with me and I am not going to ask one about the other, and I am not going up there with a sheet and a chair and a body that wants a man.**"

Nobody argued with him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the page out of the cave has nine places on it and eight names on eight of them and one rule saying that no person shall hold it alone, that the ninth place is ruled and empty, and that nobody has put a name in it in this district and is not going to, **and that nobody has put a line on a board on any of the forty-two days since the first of the fourth month, and that a sheet, a notice and a board are three different kinds of thing and a yard that treats them as one kind of thing is a yard that has stopped being able to tell them apart.**

A man of fifty-six said that a man who says he is going to ask two places separately and not one place about two things has said the opposite of everything the two documents on that table are for, and that a body four hundred miles away distributes a toll to four places at once and a man of twenty-one years proposes to do the opposite in a lane, and that there is no name in this basin for what a man does when he refuses to distribute.

The clerk entered that a body which has said two of its four have not answered has not asked this district a question, and that a district which has decided which two it is going to answer has not been asked that either, and that the record about both of those says not asked, and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against is not a bearer of a toll, is not a holder, is not a founder, is not a party of the fourth of four, and is not on the standing orders of the nine, **and that the count of things this district does not have is five and the fifth of the five is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and did not move at about ten in the morning on the twelfth of the fifth month.**

---

She came at about half past ten.

She came on foot and with no book under her arm. She was at the gate at about twenty past ten and the gate did not open for her because it does not open without a foot against the post, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put his own foot against it and did not touch her and was not asked to, and the clerk entered that the record about who opened that gate says not asked.

She did not come into the yard. She stood about four feet inside the gate and looked at the trestle table and the chair standing at the end of it and the ground under the leg where the notice had been, and nobody said anything to her about any of the three, and the clerk entered that she was in that yard for about six minutes and that nobody asked her a first thing and that she looked at three things and did not touch any of them.

Nine people went out past the loams at about eleven, and she walked at the back and not beside anybody, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked beside her for about half a mile and then fell in behind her and was not asked to and did not say why.

The road is about four miles and it goes up past the top field of a farm whose legal owner is not in this district, and the fourth holding took water in the night of the nineteenth into the twentieth of last month and its channel had a foot of water in it on the twentieth and was dry before that since the first of the second month, and it is down about a foot now.

The lock was in its channel where it had been put back on the fourth of this month, with the frame bolted down, and there was nothing standing above the sill and about two inches of water lying in the notch in it, and the level was the same as it had been on the eleventh and had not gone on any further in the day.

She stood on the bank of it for about a minute before she said anything, and nine people stood behind her and nobody came past her, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that party went on ahead of her and that nobody was asked to and that the record about the minute says not asked.

The man of about forty-one who keeps a road house at the top of the road was not there today and had not been sent for, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud that he was not there and that nobody knew where he was and that a man of about forty-one who came down nine miles on the fifth of this month is not a man anybody in this district has a day for, and the clerk entered that the next time is not fixed and that this district has never given him one.

Then she crouched down and put her thumb in the notch, the way a man of about forty-one had put his thumb in it on the eighth of this month, and the stone was wet and she had to take her hand off and dry it on her coat and do it again, and a man of about nineteen counted what she said and got a hundred and four, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**This is where the board goes. It is not a record of anything and I can see now why a person who reads for a living would want it to be a record, and it is a hole a plank fits in, and somebody cut it four hundred years before me, and my name is not anywhere near it and my room is not anywhere near it, and I wrote a rule that made my room findable and the rule was mine and I would not write it a second time differently, and that is the whole of what I came down here for.**"

Nobody said thank you to her for coming, and she did not wait to be thanked, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards, on the walk back, that he had walked beside her for the first half mile and then had not, and that he could not have told anybody in that yard why he had done it that way and had not tried.

They came back into the yard at about half past three and the notice was still under the leg of the trestle table and the chair was still at the end of it and the ground under the leg was still soft, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the notice out one more time for about nine people who had not heard it and got nothing wrong for the fifth time, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the notice has now been read out in that yard five times in seven days and that five is not five consents and is not a column, and that a notice read out five times in a yard a stranger can walk into is a notice this district has made its own without anybody deciding to.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a man who does not thank a person for walking four miles in order to stand next to a piece of iron has understood something about the piece of iron, and that the iron does not care and neither does the man.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the half of that which is about the pin, and it was counted, and it came to eighty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A rule this yard wrote this month about being wrong is a rule about being thanked, and one of the nine who wrote a rule four hundred years ago wrote the same sentence under it and underlined it twice, and I have known that since the twenty-ninth of last month and I have not said it out loud in this yard until this morning because I was the one who put the seventh instrument up and I did not want to be the one who took it back.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who put an instrument up in a yard and then said out loud that it is the same shape as a rule about being thanked is not withdrawing it, and that the seven instruments this district built and not named are six and did not become a seventh, and that the lock has been read in this district three times, that the first two of those readings were by a man who said out loud both times that he had been wrong about it, and that the third was by the person in this district who keeps the books and who had to take her hand off the stone and dry it on her coat before her thumb would go in the notch.

She said the other half of it before she got up, and it was counted, and it came to fourteen, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**You did not tell anybody at that yard that I said I would come.**"

"**No.**"

"**Good. I would not have come.**"

**AND ON THE TWELFTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH A REGISTRAR WALKED FOUR MILES AND STOOD NEXT TO A PIECE OF IRON AND PUT HER THUMB IN A HOLE A PLANK GOES IN, AND ON THE FIRST DAY OF THE NEXT MONTH A RIVAL RECORD TAKES EFFECT AGAINST FOUR PLACES OF WHICH TWO HAVE NOT ANSWERED, AND NINE PLACES ON A PAGE IN A CAVE STILL HAVE EIGHT NAMES ON THEM AND A NINTH PLACE THAT IS RULED AND EMPTY, AND NOBODY HAS PUT A LINE ON A BOARD ON ANY OF THE FORTY-TWO DAYS SINCE THE FIRST OF THE FOURTH MONTH.**
