# Chapter 249: Nine Walls

---

The twenty-first of the fifth month was grey and the yard of Lot Seventeen filled from about a quarter to ten, and there were five things on the trestle table where there had been three for nine days.

The three sheets that had been read out in that yard on the sixteenth and the eighteenth were still on the table and the notice was still under the leg of it and the slate with the three columns on it was leaning where it had been leaning for three days, and the fifth thing was the letter, and the chair at the end of the table had been standing there twenty-eight days.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and forty-eight days on the board and four hundred and sixty-four days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and seventy-eight, and a hundred and thirty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning, and that is twenty weeks and no days, and the first day of the next month is eleven days off, and there is a letter on that table with a man's name on it.**"

Then he said the first half of what was on the table before anybody had thought to ask him for it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That is a letter. It came from the body that wrote the sheet with the chair printed on it and the sheet with the four places numbered on it, and it came into this yard folded once and not by anybody who has been in this yard, and it is the first piece of paper in four months that has the name of a person on it, and his name is on it twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years asked for it to be read, because a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and she read it out in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them and read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **TO THE PEOPLE OF THE FOUR PLACES BY THE ROADS BELOW THE WATER.**
>
> **A CONVENANT HAS BEEN AGREED ABOVE YOU AND IT TAKES EFFECT ON THE FIRST DAY OF THE NEXT MONTH. THE COVENANT HAS NO FOUNDER. IT IS THE FOUNDER OF THIS THING TO SAY THAT IT HAS ONE, AND WE HAVE PUT HIS NAME UNDER IT IN A BOOK THAT ANY PERSON MAY WALK UP TO AND READ.**
>
> **THE MAN WHOSE NAME IS IN THAT BOOK IS THE MAN THIS DISTRICT FOUNDED IN THE MATTER OF THE WATER ABOVE IT, AND HE HAS BEEN ASKED FOR IT BY A PERSON WHO CAME FOUR MILES ON THE STRENGTH OF THE NAME, AND HE ANSWERED.**
>
> **HE MAY NOT TAKE THE NAME OFF. HE HAS SAID SO OUT LOUD AND HE HAS NOT ASKED US TO TAKE IT OFF. THE COLUMN IS WHERE A THING THAT HAS BEEN BUILT STANDS, AND A THING THAT HAS BEEN BUILT HAS A NAME ON IT, AND THE NAME IS NOT THE SAME AS THE THING.**
>
> **WE HAVE LOST THE IMMEDIATE VOTE. WE ARE NOT COMPLAINING ABOUT IT. WE ARE TELLING YOU THAT IT IS GONE, SO THAT NOBODY IN ANY OF THE FOUR PLACES CAN GO ON SAYING THAT A BODY FOUR HUNDRED MILES AWAY DECIDES WHAT HAPPENS TO THE WATER ABOVE THEM. IT IS NOT TRUE ANY MORE, AND THE ONLY THING THAT HAS REPLACED IT IS FOUR STEWARDS AND A DOOR.**
>
> **THIS LETTER IS GOING ON NINE WALLS. A WALL IS NOT AN ADDRESS. NOBODY IS TO WRITE A REPLY TO IT AND SEND IT BACK, BECAUSE THERE IS NOWHERE TO SEND IT TO, AND A REPLY THAT COMES BACK TO A BODY FOUR HUNDRED MILES AWAY IS A DOCUMENT THIS DISTRICT DID NOT WRITE AND DID NOT ASK FOR.**

Nobody in that yard said the word for about nine seconds, and then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing that was in the room with everybody else, in the plain voice, and the count came to a hundred and twenty-eight.

"**The word on that is one this district has spent four months refusing, and there are two ways to answer it. A man can stand up in a yard and say he is not one, and then he has said it in a yard a stranger can walk into, and then a stranger can come four miles and ask whether he really is not. Or he can say nothing, and then the word stands on a piece of paper that nine walls are going to have on them, and nothing is a thing a person can be asked about. I am not going to do the first one. I would like a clerk to write down that I said that and that I did not say the word.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who refuses to deny a word does not thereby claim it, and that a man who has not said a word has not said it, and that the record about the word says not asked, and that the three documents this district does not own are three and did not move, and that a letter from a body is a document somebody owns.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing about it, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and eighty-two.

"**That letter has my name on it twice and I put one of them there myself in a room yesterday with about nineteen people in it, and I have known since that room that it was going to come out in a piece of paper, and I have not slept particularly well and I am not going to say that in this yard either. What I am going to say is that a letter is a thing with no doorway in it. I have been in about nine doorways this month and I can tell you what came out of every one of them and none of them was this. A man of fifty-six asked me in a lane last month whether I would give a page to a person I had not met and I said I would think about it, and the honest answer was that I would not, and I still would not, and that is why this yard is a place a stranger can walk into and not a place a stranger can buy a page out of.**"

Nobody said the opposite. A man of fifty-six said that a man who has said out loud that a letter has no doorway in it has also said out loud that a yard has nine doorways in it, and that a thing with nine doorways and a thing with none are the whole of the difference between this district and a body four hundred miles away, and that nobody in that yard was going to answer a letter by writing a second one.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the other half of what he had been carrying since the third of this month, and the count came to a hundred and sixteen.

"**The fourth line of the offer has not been read out in this yard in five weeks and it is not going to be read out in this yard today, and there are nine people in this basin who have said no to reading it and there are nine, and one of them said no about a fortnight ago for the first time and the other eight said no before that, and the ledger in that office still has no date in it for the next reading, and the departure is still unspent, and I am not going to spend it on a day when a letter has arrived with a man's name on it twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the refusals to read are nine and did not move on the twenty-first of the fifth month, that there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading, and that a thing a man has not read out loud in five weeks has not become less unread because a piece of paper arrived this morning, and that the record about the departure says unspent.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing asked to be allowed to copy the letter, and was told that he could copy anything at all because a letter from a body is a document somebody owns and it is not one of the three this district does not own, and he made a copy in about twenty minutes in his own hand and asked for eleven coppers for it, and the man of fifty-six refused him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that the eight did not move, and that the district is still not going to pay him and has now said so seven times.

The registrar of this district's records office was in that yard and asked, in her own words and not in the six things, to read the notice out one more time for the people who had come in after eleven, and a clerk entered that she had asked and that nobody had asked her to.

She got through the first line of it and she came to the second line and stopped, and the second line is the one she wrote herself on the twenty-first of last month, and she stood at the end of the trestle table for about nine seconds and did not go on, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the whole of the notice out himself, and got nothing wrong, and it is the seventh time that notice has been read out in that yard.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the notice has now been read out in that yard seven times, and entered that the person who has kept the book in that room for nine years was not able to finish reading a four-line notice about her own room in front of about nineteen people, and that nobody in that yard said anything to her about it, and that the record about the nine seconds says not asked.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about one with two buckets and put them down where she puts them, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not carry them up that day and a clerk entered that nobody asked him to and that the record about the two buckets says not asked and that a man who carries a bucket on one day and not on another has not been asked a question on either day.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that yard for the whole afternoon and refused nothing at all, and a man of fifty-six said that a day a man does not refuse is not a day he has agreed to anything and the record about it says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road carried the letter up the road that climbs at about two in the afternoon, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put it into nine things that were not envelopes, and a clerk entered that a wall is not an address and that nine walls is not nine addresses and is not a figure about nine people, and that nobody in that yard was asked whether nine walls was the right number.

Then a panel arrived in that yard at about half past three that nobody had asked for, and the clerk entered that nobody in that yard asked for it and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it, **and entered that a panel is a rule and a rule is not a document and that the three documents this district does not own are three and a panel is not a fourth of them.**

> **A THING THAT HAS BEEN BUILT HAS A NAME ON IT, AND A NAME ON A THING IS A FIGURE. A FIGURE MAY BE ASKED A QUESTION BY ANY PERSON, IN ANY PLACE, ON ANY DAY, WITHOUT BEING ASKED FIRST. A FIGURE HAS NO WAY OF SAYING NO.**

A man of fifty-six disagreed with the third of those three out loud, in front of about nineteen people, and the count came to a hundred and twenty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That is the whole of the mistake and it is a small one and it costs a person. A figure is a thing. A thing cannot say no. A person can say no, and has been saying no in this yard since the first of April, and the reason a toll can be fastened to a person is exactly that the person can say no to it. The moment a thing with a name on it cannot say no, the name stops being a person and becomes a handle, and a handle is what a body four hundred miles away has been reaching for since before this district had a yard, and it has got one, and the one it has got is not a person.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who disagrees with a printed rule out loud in a yard is doing the thing this yard exists to make possible, and that a figure is not a person and a name on a figure is not a holder and is not a bearer and is not a consent, and that the word founder is on a piece of paper in this yard and is on nine walls and has been denied twice in this district's own books and is not a column, and that nothing anybody said this afternoon made the man whose name is on it a founder and nothing anybody said this afternoon took it off him either.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OF THE FIFTH MONTH A LETTER CAME INTO THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN FOLDED ONCE AND PUT THERE BY NOBODY, IT NAMED A MAN TWICE AND CALLED HIM THE FOUNDER OF A COVENANT THAT HAS NO FOUNDER, IT SAID IN ITS OWN WORDS THAT THE IMMEDIATE VOTE WAS GONE, AND IT WENT OUT ON NINE WALLS ALONG THE ONLY ROAD IN THIS DISTRICT THAT CLIMBS, AND NOBODY IN THAT YARD SAID THE WORD BACK.**
