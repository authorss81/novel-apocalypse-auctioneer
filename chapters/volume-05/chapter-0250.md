# Chapter 250: Two Lines On A Slate

---

The twenty-second of the fifth month was bright and warm for the hour and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there twenty-nine days and nobody had sat in it on any of the twenty-nine.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and then read one more out that was not on the board, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A hundred and forty-nine days on the board and four hundred and sixty-five days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and seventy-nine, and a hundred and forty days separate the second of January and this morning, and that is twenty weeks and no days, and the first day of the next month is ten days off, and there is nothing left in this yard to read out this month.**"

The man of fifty-six said the count of what the month had got, in the ordinary voice, and nobody asked him to, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and that she wrote it down and that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and the count came to a hundred and thirty-seven.

"**Nine charters and nine separate rooms and nine rights to give the whole thing up on the first day of any season after the first, and not one sheet of the nine in this yard, and four terms among the nine and three different lengths in them. Nine pieces of iron in the water and none of them on any of the nine and no person holding any of them. A covenant with a third column in it ruled and empty on its face because the people who would go in it have not been asked. And a letter on nine walls with a man's name on it twice that he put there himself. That is the month and I am not making anything out of it and I am not asking anybody to agree with it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered all nine of those things and entered that the notice has now been read out in that yard seven times, the first of them thirteen days before the sixth and the first of all of them seventeen days ago, and that seven readings is not seven consents and is not a column, and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and that neither of them moved on any of the ten days, and that the eight did not move, and that the four columns of not-askings are four, and that the six instruments built and not named are six, and that the three documents this district does not own are three, and that the five things this district does not have are five, and that the protected things are five and no sixth.

Then she entered the two that were on the table and not in the count, and they were in the same hand as the rest and they were not a paragraph.

She entered first that this district has four different ninths in it this month, being the ninth of the nine charters, the ninth of the eleven houses the woman of fifty-eight carries water to, the ninth of the nine printed nights, and the ninth place on the page out of the cave, and that they are four ninths and are not added to each other and are not a pattern, and that a man of fifty-six said that he was not going to count them again.

She entered that the fourth and fifth of the nine are in four other places, that this yard has not got the fourth in front of it this morning and has never had the fifth in front of it at all, and that the record about both says not asked, and that the ninth of the nine has no names under the line and the record about the people at that place says not asked, and that two of the four places a body four hundred miles away asked have now each been asked a question in their own doorway by a man who went on his own two feet and wrote down what he was told in their own words, and that a body four hundred miles away does not have to be told about either of them twice, and that nobody in this district is going to carry a page out of that office to prove it.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about eleven with two buckets and put them down where she puts them, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing carried one of them up without being asked and without saying anything, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a bucket is a bucket and is about eleven pounds full and is not a figure about either of them, and a clerk entered that nobody counted the buckets and that the record about the two of them says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said no before anybody asked him, and gave a reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and five.

"**I have four houses standing empty and I offered them to this yard on the last day of last month and nobody took them and I am not offering them again. You have put nine sheets out with nine rooms and nine doors and nine rights to walk out of, and there is not one door in any of the nine that a man could come and ask me for, and I am not going to be a tenth thing that this yard counts, and if a clerk writes down that I am in the count I will read it back at the counter myself.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says no before he is asked, with a reason, is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a man who keeps houses is not a clerk of a house, that the eight did not move at about half past twelve in the afternoon on the twenty-second of the fifth month, and that the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and did not move and the two are not the same figure.

The man of about fifty-five who keeps a building four miles down a lane sent nothing to that yard on that day and nobody sent anything to him, and a clerk entered that, and entered that there are two chalk lines on the inside of a brick tank with the water about a hand's breadth over the lip, and that a crack about the length of his hand is not a sixth protected thing, and that the nine years of beds are still in the second room and the water has not come over the step.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said one thing in the middle of the afternoon that the yard had been waiting nine days for without knowing it, and the count came to two hundred and six.

"**Nine sheets and a covenant and a column with my name in it and a word on nine walls, and I have been in this yard nine mornings out of ten of it saying the plainest thing I can find, and here is the plainest one I have got left. On the first of April there were nine things in the water above four districts and this district did not know whether they were open or shut, and there was nobody to ask, because a thing in the water is not a person. Today this district knows that the board is in the notch at one of them and that the road below it is shut and that nobody is holding it, and it still does not know whether the ninth of the nine nights happened. A month has bought us one lock out of nine and one sentence that cannot be got out of us. That is what a month of asking people in doorways has actually bought, and I would take it over a sheet with a chair on it, and I have said that in this yard before and I am saying it again because it has not been said since the twelfth.**"

Nobody thanked him for saying it, and he had not asked to be thanked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says a thing a second time because it has not been said since the twelfth is not making a habit of it and is not making a finding, and that a resemblance noticed a second time is not a finding and a third time is a habit, and that this is the second time and not the third.

---

At about half past five in the afternoon the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing carried a slate down about four hundred yards of lane and it went past the kitchen at the end of that lane, and he had carried it past there eleven times before and this was the twelfth, and no clerk in this district has written down on which days any of the eleven went past, and that was not repaired this month.

The registrar of this district's records office was in the kitchen doorway with her coat off and her sleeves turned up and a cloth over one shoulder, and the fanlight over the door of the room where the books are does not light anything, as it has never once lit anything, and she had a piece of chalk in her hand before he got to the step.

She wrote two lines on the side of the slate that faced him and did not say anything about what she had written while she was writing them.

The first line said that the fanlight over the door of her office had not lit anything in four months and that she had not had a man out to it because she had not wanted one. The second line said that nobody had ever asked her about it and that she was not going to tell him either, in that yard or in a room or in a lane, and she put the chalk down on the step.

He read it standing where he was. Then he said one thing, and it was not counted, because there was nobody there to count it and the record about it is in a minute in his own words and is not in a book.

"**I am not going to ask you why.**"

"**That is the second time you have not asked me something, and both of them are on this thing now, and you are not going to tell anybody what is written on it, and I am not going to be told, because if you are told it back you will get it slightly wrong, and then in six months one of us will be arguing about what I said on a day in the middle of that week, and I have watched people do that about smaller things than this.**"

"**That is fair.**"

"**It is not fair. It is convenient. I am telling you it is not fair because you have been fair about nine things this month and I do not want you to be fair about this one without being told that it is not.**"

---

A clerk of nineteen years was four hundred yards away and was not told about it until about seven. The record she entered came from one person and not from the other, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said, in his own words, that there were two lines on a slate and that he was not going to read them out, and that he had the chalk in his pocket, and she entered that a slate is not a book and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own and did not become a fourth of them, and that a sentence written on a slate and carried back down a lane by the man it was written to is not in any of the four places this district has written a thing in, and that a fourth place is not a fifth.

The day came down about half past six and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam went out of the gate with his right arm out of the sling, which was folded in the inside pocket of his coat, and the woman of fifty-eight has not asked for it back and has not said she wants it, and a clerk entered that she had not asked and that the record says not asked.

He went down to the sluice end of the loams and stood in the channel, and the water had come back up over the ten days and there was about a foot of it standing above the sill again, and the frame was holding it the way it holds it, which is by being a frame with the weight of the water sitting on it, and the screw was up through the middle with the handle on it and the pin through the screw, and there was no hand on any part of it, and there had not been one since he got there.

He stood in that water up to about the knee for about four minutes with his left arm hanging at his side and did not touch the frame, and said one thing out loud to nobody, and it was not counted because there was nobody there, and a clerk of nineteen years entered afterwards, from the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, what it was, and entered that a thing in the water does not need a person and that a person standing in the water next to a thing that does not need him is not holding it, and is not a bearer of it, and is not a column.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-NINTH DAY OF A CHAIR NOBODY SAT IN, AND SEVEN READINGS OF A NOTICE, AND NINE SHEETS IN NINE ROOMS AND ONE OF THEM WITH NOBODY UNDER THE LINE, AND A SLATE THAT WENT PAST A KITCHEN FOR THE TWELFTH TIME AND CAME BACK WITH TWO LINES ON IT THAT WERE NOT ABOUT THE WATER, A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR STOOD UP TO THE KNEE IN A CHANNEL AT THE SLUICE END OF THE LOAMS WITH HIS HAND ON NOTHING.**
