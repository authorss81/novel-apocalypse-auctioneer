# Chapter 203: The Renewal

---

The third of April was the day the Registry's sheet was read, and it was read by a clerk of nineteen years at about ten in the morning in the yard of Lot Seventeen in front of about nineteen people, and the man with the portfolio was not sent for and came.

A man of fifty-six said before it was read that a document is read out and not discussed, and that this district settled that on the twenty-ninth of December of last year and has not gone back on it, **and that a sheet brought here by a stranger is read the same way a sheet brought here by a neighbour is read and that the two being read the same is the only protection anybody in this yard has got this morning.**

She read it out in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them and nobody interrupted her, and afterwards a clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **A LEASE IS RENEWED. THE TERM ENDED ON THE LAST DAY OF THE SECOND MONTH AND THIS RENEWAL IS DATED THE FIRST OF THE FOURTH MONTH.**
>
> **THE USE IS A ROAD. THE TOLL IS PAID AT THE NINTH PLACE. THE HOLDER IS THE REGISTRAR-GENERAL, CAEL ORIN.**
>
> **THE PLACE IS NAMED ON THE LEASE. THIS IS NOT THE LEASE.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, which is longer than it sounds, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing everybody was thinking and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half of it, and the two said them at the same time in two sentences.

"**The term ended on the last day of the second month and this is the first of the fourth month and nobody has said why it was late.**"

"**It was not late. It was never late. Nobody knows when it was late because nobody has ever seen the day it was due on.**"

A man of fifty-six said that both of those are the same sentence said twice by two people who are worried about the same thing in different directions, and that a document which names a day it missed and does not name a day it was due on is a document that cannot be checked by anybody in this yard, **and that a term and a renewal and a date that is late and a date that is due are four different things and there is no column in this district for any of the four and the count of things this district does not have is five and did not move at ten in the morning on the third of the fourth month.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the clerk, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, what the place is.

She read the third line out again and then asked the man with the portfolio, and he said the thing he had said the day before, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The lease names the place and this is not the lease and I told you yesterday I was not going to say it and I am not saying it today either, and I have not refused and I am not going to refuse.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that this is the second time in two days that a person has said both halves of a refusal in the same breath, and entered that this district has a column for a person who will not read a thing and that the count in it is nine and is nine, **and that this district does not have a column for a person who will not name a place, and that it has not had one since the second of January and did not make one this morning, and that the two are not the same refusal and the second of them has no count at all and that is a hole and not a blank.**

Nobody argued with her about the word. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a hole in a form is a thing a person can put their thumb in and this one is, **and that the third form of a withdrawal notice printed in this district in the month before last carries its own hole on its face in twenty-nine words, and that a document which says what it will not say is a door, and that this district settled that on the twenty-seven of December and has now got one in a stranger's hand.**

There were ten other sheets in the case and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, in front of about nineteen people, whether this district was going to be read ten more documents by a stranger, and the man with the portfolio said the second half of it before the asking.

"**Eight of them are about places you have never heard of and I am not going to read them here, and the ninth is a schedule and the tenth is a lease, and neither of the two is for this district.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a stranger carried ten documents into a yard and one of them was read and nine were not, **and that this district has three documents nobody owns and that a document a stranger will not read out in a yard is a document the stranger owns and is not a fourth and did not become one this morning, and that the count is three and has been three since the seventeenth of September of last year.**

A man of fifty-six said that a man who will not read nine documents into a yard has done this district one kindness and that the district should be careful about how much of it it takes, **and that the fourth of the eleven is the schedule and a schedule is not a figure and a figure is not a schedule and the two have never been joined in this district, and that a man who is going to read a schedule in a yard tomorrow will be asked what it schedules and the answer will be a day and a day is not a figure.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the thing nobody in the yard had said, and he said it flat, and nobody picked it up.

"**The ninth place is the one holding on the delivery sheet at the second reservoir that nobody can account for, and a man of about thirty has been to that counter eleven times in thirteen weeks and been refused five times with no reason, and this sheet says the toll of a lease on a place with no name is paid at the ninth place, and I am not saying those are the same and I have said twice today that I am not saying that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are six and are not a column and are not added to the thirteen, **and that the sixth of them was given on the twenty-fourth of the third month and the man of about thirty has now been refused five times since the second of January, and that the refusal a Registry man gave this morning is the seventh and is not a clerk of a house and is not a column, and that a resemblance noticed twice is refused the same way it was refused the first time.**

---

The rest of the morning was the reading, and the reading was done twice, and a man of fifty-six read it out as well in the ordinary voice and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who does the counting for a living read a stranger's sheet out in a yard and that she did not ask him to.

The second line has three things on it and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing took them one at a time in the ordinary voice and did not put a figure on any of them.

The use is a road. The toll is paid at the ninth place. The holder is a person with a title and a name on a line in a stranger's document, and the name on it is Cael Orin, and it is the first time that name has been said out loud in this yard.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that a holder is a person and that a person who is named in a stranger's document four hundred yards from a book a stranger can walk up to is a person who can be found, **and that this district worked that out about itself on the twelfth of the third month and it has taken a stranger nineteen days to arrive at the same sentence and that a district which is the last to know a thing about itself is not a district anybody should be asking to hold anything.**

A man of fifty-six said that a man who says that in a yard on a Tuesday morning is talking about a road and not about a man, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that no weekday is attached to any date in this district and that the word was not a date and that she is not going to correct him because he is not wrong about the road.

Nobody said the name out loud a second time and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a name said once in a yard is a name four hundred people have, **and that a name said twice is a name a counter can ask for, and that a name said once and not written down is a name that four hundred people have and nobody can use, and that this district has one of those and has had it since the twentieth of December and did not know it was the good kind.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the name was said once and not written down and that the minute says a holder and does not carry the name, **and that a minute in the district's own hand that does not carry a name is not a copy of anything and is not one of the three documents nobody owns and did not become a fourth this morning.**

---

The lane at the sluice end of the loams had six doors in it and about four people were on it in the afternoon and a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households was on it and was not sent for.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the state of them without being asked and it is the state it was on the first of April and on the twenty-first of the third month, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the record of the six has not moved on any of the three days and that the six are not a column and are not a rate and are not added to anything.

"**One signed on the twenty-ninth of the second month. Two refused in the second month. Three have not been asked again and one of the three is a man who lives in a cart with a charcoal roof drawn beside his name, and a house came down that lane before six in the morning and the man in the cart was not in it.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who keeps a tally for six households has now been asked three times to be told what to do about the three and has not been told and that the asking is the house's and not this district's, **and that this yard decided on the nineteenth of the second month not to decide whether the three are going to be asked again and has not gone back on it and that not deciding is a decision and was entered as one at the time and is not to be entered again as if it were new.**

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat at the third house up the lane from the scale was in the lane and was one of the two that refused, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked a first thing on the third of the fourth month and that she has been in this district's yard three times now and has answered once.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, in front of about four people, what a refusal was, and she said it and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighteen, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I would rather be in the water with my name not written on anything than pay for it.**"

Nobody picked it up. A man of fifty-six said that a woman who has said that in a yard and in a lane has said the same thing twice and that a thing said twice is one thing said twice and not two things, **and that a toll is not a contract and a refusal of a contract is not a refusal of a toll and the two are not joined, and that she is in a pool of tolls with her name not written on anything and has been since the thirtieth of the third month and nobody asked her about the pool and the not-asking is entered.**

The offer of four houses was still standing and nobody mentioned it, and a man of fifty-six entered that the offer was made on the thirty-first of the third month and has not been withdrawn and has not been taken up, **and that a man who offered a woman four houses of a round on the last day of a month and a woman who did not take it is a man and a woman and two facts and not a decision that anybody in this lane made.**

---

The man of about thirty-one came home at about half past two in the afternoon on the third of April.
He had gone out of the yard at first light on the first, two days of road each way, and he was thinner and he had a strip of paper about the length of his hand in his coat and he put it on the trestle table face up without being asked and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked to put it there and was not asked where he had got it and is not going to be.

It is not the stub. The stub is about the size of a coin and is coming apart at the folds, and this is a strip about the length of his hand and it has a line ruled across it and a word at the top of it and nothing at all under the line.

A man of fifty-six said that a word and a ruled line and nothing under the line is the shape of a receipt and that a receipt is a document somebody owns and that this one is on a table in a yard with the man who was given it standing next to it.

The man of about thirty-one said the thing, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got fifty-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The counter took my name off the sheet. It took about nine seconds and nobody asked me anything and it cost nothing, and there is a strip of paper with my name on it that says the name came off, and I have not been released and I have been found.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man has been found and not released, and that the two are not joined, **and that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on and that what came off here is not the figure and is the name, and that the figure of twenty-one years came off this district's page on the thirty-first of March in the same line as the date and was not asked for by anybody, and that the two are two different removals eleven inches and two days of road apart and neither of them is a release and the two are not joined.**

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the man of about thirty-one has spent four days walking to a counter to have his own name taken off a piece of paper, and that a man who does that is a man who has believed that a name is a thing a person can get rid of, **and that this district told him on the twenty-second of the third month in a yard that the name cannot be taken off by the person it is put on, and that it turns out that it can, and that the two of those are not a contradiction and one of them is a counter two days of road away and one of them is a yard.**

The man of about thirty-one said the second half of that before anybody asked him for it, and the clerk entered that he said it first and that she is not going to put a number on it.

"**It can be taken off where they put it on. It cannot be taken off by me. That is what I went to find out and I found it out and I am not glad and I am not going to be asked what I am going to do with it.**"

A man of fifty-six entered that nobody asked him and that the not-asking is entered and that the count is where it was, **and that a man who goes two days of road to find out something and is not glad and is not going to say what he will do with it is a man and not a security and never was one, and that the fifth of the five posted securities is a man and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against is a different man and the two are not joined and have never been.**

---

The road a man keeps that somebody else opened on the night of the twenty-second of the second month runs past the fourth holding on the delivery sheet at the second reservoir, and the fourth holding is the top field of a farm four miles out past the loams and its legal owner has not been at the crossing in about four years.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that out loud in a yard on the afternoon of the third of the fourth month and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it and that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam was not asked to agree with it, **and that two things in this district have been on a page since the second of January and have not been joined, and that a man naming a third thing beside them is not joining them and is not stopping them, and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and neither of them moved at four in the afternoon of the third of the fourth month.**

The fourth holding has not taken a drop since the first of the second month and it is the one the man of about thirty-four who digs loam opened a sluice for on the night of the thirteenth of the third month, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a road he does not keep was opened nine miles from a holding he does not own, and that the two are a coincidence until somebody says otherwise and nobody in this district is going to be the one who says otherwise.

A man of fifty-six said that the district has a rule and the rule is the whole of this novel so far, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam did not pick it up.

"**A resemblance noticed a second time is not a finding and a resemblance noticed a third time is a habit, and this yard has refused two of them out loud in ninety-one days and I am not going to be the man who makes a third one, and the four of the nine holdings and the ninth of the nine holdings and a road that somebody opened in the night are three things and there is no column for three things.**"

The Registry man was still in the yard at about four in the afternoon and nobody had sent for him and he had not gone, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in the six things, in front of about nine people, what a Registry is for.

He said the second half of it before the asking, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did that twice in three days and that a man who answers the second half before the first half is a man who has been asked before.

"**A Registry holds records of holdings. That is all it does and it is not a small thing to do and it is not a thing you can do in a yard.**"

Nobody argued with it. A man of fifty-six said that a body which holds records of holdings and keeps them where a stranger cannot walk up to them has solved a problem this district has been losing at for four months, **and that the district solved it by opening the door and the Registry kept it shut and that the two of them have ended up in the same yard on the same afternoon and that neither of them is going to say what that is because a clerk of nineteen years is standing here with a book.**

**AND ON THE THIRD OF THE FOURTH MONTH A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS READ OUT A SHEET OF THREE LINES AND THE THIRD OF THEM SAID THAT THE PLACE IS NAMED ON THE LEASE AND THAT THIS IS NOT THE LEASE, THE HOLDER ON THE SECOND LINE IS THE REGISTRAR-GENERAL, CAEL ORIN, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE CAME HOME AT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON WITH A STRIP OF PAPER THAT SAYS HIS OWN NAME CAME OFF A SHEET AND SAID IN FIFTY-ONE WORDS THAT HE HAD BEEN FOUND AND NOT RELEASED.**
