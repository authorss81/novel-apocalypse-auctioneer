# Chapter 221: On The Table Again

---

The twenty-second of April was colder than that yard had been since the second month, and the gate would not open without a man putting a foot against the post, and about nine people were standing in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam had it open by about a minute past ten with his left hand flat on the post and his right arm held against his ribs, and he did not say a word about the arm in a yard, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said nothing and that she was not asked to make anything of it and that she was not going to.

He had put a spade into a wet bank at the sluice end of the loams at about half past six that morning, and the bank had gone out from under the blade about a foot and a half down, and he had felt it go in his shoulder and had carried on for about a quarter of an hour after that because there was a ditch to finish, and he told the yard about it before anybody had thought to ask him, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I put a spade into a wet bank at the sluice end of the loams at about half past six and I have not been able to lift a spade above my head since, and I dug for nine years, and I am telling this yard about it because nobody in this yard is going to find out by asking me how I am.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a man who announces a thing about his own body before anybody can ask him about it is doing the one thing that yard has which is worth more than the yard itself, and that an arm is not an instrument and did not become the seventh of them. The six instruments this district has built and not named are a rehearsal, a platform, a rail, a correction, a question and a security, **and the clerk refused a seventh in about four seconds and was not asked to refuse it, and the six did not move at about a quarter past ten in the morning on the twenty-second of the fourth month.**

The three figures went out of the yard the way they go every morning, out loud, because he asks for figures out loud, and the board was read back over his shoulder and both of the other two were right.

"**A hundred and nineteen days on the board and four hundred and thirty-five days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and forty-nine, and the last day of this month is eight days off, and there is a second sheet lying on the trestle table that was not there yesterday morning and none of the nine of us put it there.**"

Nobody claimed to have put it there. The only person who had stood at that table since the fourteenth of this month was a woman of fifty-eight, and she had put a bucket down beside it on the seventeenth and had not put it on it, and the strip of paper the man of about thirty-one brought down that day was lying under the second sheet, face up, where he had left it.

The trestle table has stood in that yard since the second month and nobody in this district owns it. The clerk entered that a table is not a document, and that the two sheets lying on it are both documents somebody owns, **and that the three this district does not own are the printed sheet at the fourth place, the notice about a party of nine who went thirty-four miles east, and a line in a public book with four names on it and a figure under the four names, and that the three did not move at about ten in the morning on the twenty-second of the fourth month and a second sheet is not a fourth of them.**

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked for the second sheet to be read at about half past ten, and said out loud why he was asking, which is that a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and that nobody had asked it this morning, and he was asking.

The clerk of nineteen years read it out in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them, and read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter. It is dated the eighteenth of this month, which is two days after the first letter came out of the same fold and was read in that yard, and it came down the lane in a bag with a delivery that nobody in the district put in the bag.

> **A BODY THAT WRITES TO A VILLAGE OFFERS A VILLAGE ONE SHEET, ONE HOLDER, ONE TERM, AND ONE NAME.**
>
> **THE ONE HOLDER IS TO SIT IN A CHAIR. THE CHAIR IS TO BE CALLED A FOUNDER'S CHAIR. THE TERM IS A TERM WITH NO END ON IT.**
>
> **THE NINE THINGS THAT HOLD BACK THE WATER WILL BE HELD BY THE ONE HOLDER FOR THE FOUR DISTRICTS UNDER THEM, AND THE OFFER WILL WORK FROM TOMORROW.**
>
> **AN ANSWER IS ASKED FOR BY THE LAST DAY OF THIS MONTH AND THE BODY SENDS ITS REGARDS AGAIN.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, which is the only time in eleven days that nobody in that yard has said anything at all, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the whole of what it was, and he was not asked for it, and no man counted it.

"**One sheet, one holder, one term with no end on it and one name, and the name is not on it. There is a place ruled for it the way there is a place ruled at the ninth on a board outside the room, and a person is a name, and this district does not put a name into a ruled place because somebody printed a line round it.**"

A man of fifty-six said that a place ruled for a name is the same shape of nothing as the seventh line at the ninth place, and that two shapes of nothing are two shapes and are not one finding. **He read out that the count of boards is five and the count of full lines is six, and that the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and has nothing on it and did not get anything on it this morning, and that nobody has put a line on a board on any of the twenty-two days from the first of this month to this one.**

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in the six things, in front of about nine people, whether a term with no end on it is a fifth condition in this district, and the clerk refused the entry in about four seconds and gave the reason and was not asked to refuse it.

"**A condition with no end on it is a thing a body can be held to, and this district has nobody in it to be held, and those are two different sentences, and there are four of the four and I did not rule a fifth this morning and I am not going to, and a term with no end on it printed on a sheet is an offer, and an offer is not a refusal, and this yard has not refused anything.**"

Nobody contradicted her. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a body which offers a thing with no end on it and says it will work from tomorrow has worked out that this district has nothing in it that can answer the offer, and has said so in writing, and that a man of fifty-six answered that a body does not need to work anything out about a village that has a book a stranger can walk up to.

He asked next, in the six things, in front of about nine people, what a chair is, and the man of fifty-six answered it in the ordinary voice, did not look at the sheet while he did it, and was counted and got forty-six.

"**A chair is a place a person is put, and a founder is a man somebody has already decided about, and the two of them together on one line of one sheet is a thing that does not need a man in the room to work.**"

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that a figure with a person to be put in it is not a figure, and that a chair with a name in it is a job nobody has been offered, and that the man who takes a job up is not the man it is handed to, and that this district has spent four months finding out that the two are not the same person and has not written it down anywhere yet.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale stood at the back of that yard for about ten minutes and then went out of the gate without saying anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was there and that nobody sent for her and that she was not asked a first thing, and that the four houses she offered on the last day of last month still stand and have not been taken up and were not added to eleven this morning and are not going to be.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about eleven with a bucket in each hand and put them down where she has put them every morning since the seventeenth, and she was in the yard for about a quarter of an hour, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked up to her at the foot of the bank and stopped about nine feet short and asked her, in his own words and not in the six things, whether a chair over a water is a thing she has an opinion about.

She said the honest answer and a man of about nineteen counted it and got seventy-five, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I have carried water up that bank for nine years and nobody has ever asked me once whether I would rather somebody else carried it, and there is a chair in that sheet and somebody is going to sit in it, and I know which of those two things I have been for nine years, and I am not going to say it in front of nine people on the second day of eleven houses.**"

She picked the two buckets up again and went out of the yard and up the bank, and it is about a hundred and forty steps from the gate to the top of it and she took them without stopping. The clerk entered that she did that and that nobody asked her to and that nobody followed her, **and that a woman holding eleven houses of a round of about ninety and no figure on them at all put two buckets down in a yard this morning and picked them up again and went out of it, and that the round of about seventy-nine is not coming back, and that about ninety is not a figure so that is not a subtraction.**

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam asked for one thing at about half past eleven, in front of everybody, it was the fourth time in eleven days, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got sixty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I would like that offer written down in my own words. I have asked three times in ten days and I have not been written down three times, and this is the fourth time, and I am telling you now that it is not going to happen, so that the not-happening is a thing this yard did and not a thing I did.**"

The man of fifty-six said that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself eleven times in four months, and that the asking is entered, and that he is not going to say it four times, and that the counting of how many times a man has said a thing is a count of a man and is not a finding.

Nobody wrote it down.

The day came down cold and clear about half past four and the gate wanted a foot against the post, and about nine people had been in that yard, and one of them had told it about his arm before it could think to ask him, and one of them had gone out of it with two buckets in her hands, and a sheet with a chair printed on it was still lying on a table under a piece of paper that a man had brought down a road and would not say where from.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF THE FOURTH MONTH A SECOND SHEET WAS LYING ON THE TRESTLE TABLE THAT NOBODY HAD PUT THERE, AND IT OFFERS ONE HOLDER AND ONE CHAIR AND A TERM WITH NO END ON IT AND A RULED PLACE FOR A NAME WITH NOTHING IN IT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM SAID HE COULD NOT LIFT A SPADE ABOVE HIS HEAD AND A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT WENT OUT OF THE YARD WITH TWO BUCKETS IN HER HANDS, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS REFUSED A FIFTH CONDITION AND A SEVENTH INSTRUMENT AND THE COUNTS ARE STILL FIVE AND SIX AND THREE.**
