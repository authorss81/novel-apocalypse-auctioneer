# Chapter 241: The Second Question

---

The thirteenth of the fifth month came in with a wind that had gone round in the night and was coming off the west, and about eleven people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there twenty days.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and forty days on the board and four hundred and fifty-six days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and seventy, and a hundred and thirty-one days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the next month is nineteen days off.**"

Then he said the other half of it, which was the reason a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A man of about forty-four walked two hundred yards down behind the bank this morning on his own two feet and sat down on the step of the building at the end of the lane, and nobody sent for him and nobody asked him to come, and nobody has asked that man a question since the eleventh of this month and I am going to ask him one now and write down what he says and not argue with a word of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered the first of those things before anybody in that yard opened their mouth, and entered that a person who walks into a yard has not been brought into it and can go out of it again whenever he likes, that the record about his coming says not asked, **and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to.**

The second question was put in his own words and not in the six things, in the middle of that yard, with the man of about forty-four still sitting on the step and about eleven people standing about nine feet back, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and twenty-four.

"**Last time I asked you in your own doorway whether your house would pay a toll on a thing that holds back the water, and you said no, and you said you did not know which of the four places you were and that you would answer a second question. Here is the second one. If a body four hundred miles away and a yard four hundred yards from your door were both writing down what happens to the water above this valley, and neither of them was asking you for a penny, would you want your own house's answer to be in there. I am not asking you to agree with anybody. I am asking whether you would want to be in it.**"

The answer came to a hundred and sixteen, and a man of about nineteen counted it and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I would. And I want it in my own words and not in yours, and I want it to say that I have got a well and a drain and a woman carries water to my door and that I have not been asked about one or the other of them by anybody in nine years. And I am saying the second thing as well, which is that I do not know what is in any of it, because nobody has ever come up this lane and told me, and if a clerk writes down that I want to be in it then she has to write down that I do not know what it is.**"

Nobody argued with him about either half. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has been asked a second question and has answered it has not thereby agreed to be a bearer of anything, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a man who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, **and that the eight did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the thirteenth of the fifth month.** She entered that the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and did not move, and that the two are not the same figure and are not added together.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam refused the join that had been available to him all morning, standing at the end of the trestle table with the sling still on his right arm, and the count came to a hundred and six.

"**A settlement is a place and a household is people, and a page with nine places ruled on it is the shape of the page that came out of a cave, and I am not going to help anybody make one of those this month. Nine doors, nine pieces of paper, nine people who have to walk to the ninth one. The day somebody writes all nine of them on one sheet is the day the ninth one is a ruled place with nobody standing at it, and I have held that page in my hand and I know what an empty ruled place looks like.**"

It went in that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve, and that neither of them moved at about eleven in the morning on the thirteenth of the fifth month, and that a tenth refusal of a join is the same refusal with a bigger number of people in front of it and is not a tenth of anything.

A man of fifty-six said that the man who digs loam has now said the same shape of true sentence about a page for the third time in six weeks, that a shape noticed a second time is not a finding and a third time is a habit, and that he was not going to enter it a fourth time, and then he said the half nobody had said.

"**Nine doors is the whole of it and there is no tenth thing, and the ninth door is the one that will go wrong, because the eighth will be a man who has already said he would answer a second time and the ninth will be somebody nobody in this yard has met, and this district has about nineteen days before the first of the next month and about nine doors and one road that climbs.**"

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about eleven with a bucket in each hand and put them down where she has put them every morning since the seventeenth, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in his own words and not in the six things, whether her eleven houses were nine doors or one, and she gave the honest answer and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and eleven.

"**They are eleven doors and I know the difference between a house and a place because I have been carrying water into both of them for nine years and only one of them has ever said thank you. You are not going to get nine out of me by asking me twice and you are not going to get one out of me by asking me once, and if you go up to a person in a house you go on your own two feet and you take nothing with you, and that is the only thing I know about it and it has not been asked of me twice yet.**"

The clerk entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a woman carrying water to eleven houses and holding no figure on any of them is a person and not a party of anything, and that the eight did not move at about eleven in the morning on the thirteenth of the fifth month.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was at the far end of the trestle table with his right hand flat on it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him whether he would copy a thing this district wrote itself out of nothing, and he said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-four.

"**I will copy a thing this district has agreed to, and that is a different thing from a thing this district has made up this morning. You have not got one yet. The moment you have got one, and it is your own, and it has got the names of the people in it that you put there yourself, then I will copy it in about twenty minutes and you can have it in my hand for nothing, the same as the last four.**"

The clerk entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that the eight did not move, that a copy is a copy and is not a bearer and is not a figure about a person, and that the three documents this district does not own are three and did not move at about half past eleven in the morning on the thirteenth of the fifth month, **and that a man who has made four copies for nothing in a month and refused a fifth and said why has not refused anything.**

Then he asked for a figure and gave one, out loud, in front of about eleven people, and nobody counted it because nobody had been asked to, and the clerk entered it in her own hand because a clerk enters a number that is said in a yard whether it is asked for or not.

He asked for eleven coppers a week, the same figure he asked for in the second week of last month, and the man of fifty-six said no out loud and gave no reason at all, and then the man of about thirty-one who digs loam said what that no is, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and twenty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A yard is a room with no roof and a man of about nineteen has been counting in it every morning since the second of April and nobody in this yard has ever paid any of us for one morning of it, and I am not making that a complaint. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and there is no sixth, and there is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and there never has been one, and a man who is not in a household is not going to be paid out of a yard that cannot pay itself.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the five did not move at about half past twelve in the afternoon on the thirteenth of the fifth month, and that eleven coppers a week is a figure about a man and is not a rate and is not a column and is not entered in one, and that the man who asked for it has now asked five times and the district has now said no five times, and that a figure somebody says out loud in a yard and nobody counts is not a count.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was not in that yard at any hour of that day and had gone out of it on the previous afternoon with a knapsack, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody sent for him, that he was not asked a first thing, and that the record about where he went says not asked, **and that a man who said in a yard on the twelfth that he would go and ask two places each in their own doorway has gone, and that a man of fifty-six said that going is not the same as arriving, and that this district has never once given that man a day to be here on.**

The day came down hard about half past four and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam went out of the gate and up the bank with his arm in a sling that is not his, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the gate and let him, and it was entered that he was let and that nobody asked him to be let and the record says not asked.

Then he turned round at the top of the bank and said the thing about the morning after, into the yard, where about five people could still hear him, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**In the morning about nine of us are going to walk up the road that climbs and past the turn to look at one of the nine, because a man of about thirty-four said in this yard on the tenth that he could have walked to one of them in a morning and did not, and I have decided he was right, and that it was a stupid thing to say out loud and a true one. Nobody is sending for anybody. If you want to come, come. If you would rather not, do not, and I am not going to write your name on anything about it.**"

**AND ON THE THIRTEENTH OF THE FIFTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WALKED TWO HUNDRED YARDS DOWN BEHIND THE BANK ON HIS OWN FEET AND WAS ASKED A SECOND QUESTION AND IT WAS THE SECOND ONE IN NINE YEARS, AND HE SAID HE WOULD WANT HIS OWN HOUSE'S ANSWER IN THERE AND ALSO THAT HE DID NOT KNOW WHAT WAS IN IT, AND THE RECORD ABOUT HIM HAS TWO HALVES IN IT AND NOT ONE COLUMN.**
