# Chapter 261: Nothing In The Column

---

The eleventh of the sixth month came in close and hot before nine and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there forty-nine days and nobody had sat in it on any of the forty-nine.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and sixty-nine days on the board and four hundred and eighty-five days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and ninety-nine, and a hundred and sixty days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is fifty-one days off counted off this morning, and that is nineteen days to the end of this month and the thirty-one of the month after it and the first of the month after that, and this is the eleventh, and a building with two doors in it is to be shut today by a person this yard has never asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the three figures were carried off the mouth of the man who gave them and not off a sheet, and that fifty-one is nineteen and thirty-one and one, and that the length of the month after next is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a clerk does not put a number of days and a number of months in one sentence without saying which is which.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said what the day was for, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fourteen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**We are going down the lane behind the bank this morning and we are going to stand in front of that building, and I am not going to read that sheet out a second time because it is in a coat in this yard and about nineteen people heard it on the eighth, and the second of its three lines says the building is to be shut by its keeper, and there is no keeper. I would like a man to hear that said out loud in daylight by somebody other than me, and I would like it said in front of the building and not in a yard a stranger can walk into.**"

A clerk entered that a man who says a thing out loud in daylight has not thereby asked anybody anything, and that the record about the coat says not asked, and that the sheet in question carries no name of a person and no name of a body and no name of a place, and that a building is not a place name.

---

The lane behind the bank is about two hundred yards long with a wire on the left of it that has not been tight since before anybody in this district was born, and a step of two stones outside one house with a plank door and a hasp and no handle at the bottom of it, and a third house up from the scale a woman keeps a goat, and at the near end of the lane the building with two doors, which is a room with a beaten earth floor, a front door with a handle and a curtain in it, and about nine feet back a plank door in the end wall with a hasp on the outside of it and a nail in the hasp instead of a staple.

The scale is under a lean-to at the foot of the bank, and the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, who has stood at that scale nine years and weighs about nine sacks a day, was at it when the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came down the lane at about half past ten. She had said on the seventh of this month, in a room with no name over the door and in front of about nine people, that she would sit at that scale on the eleventh of this month whether anybody was there or not. Nobody came to see her do it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was at that scale on the eleventh and that nobody sent for her, and that a woman who has said out loud that she will be somewhere on a day has signed nothing and is not a bearer of the eleventh, and that the record about the eleventh says not asked.

He asked her one question, in his own words and not in the six things, and she said no in about four seconds, and gave a reason, and the count came to a hundred and fifty-six.

"**No, and you did not ask me whether I would. You walked down here and you asked me to be the keeper of a building, and a keeper is a person who has been asked to keep a thing and has said yes, and you have got a piece of paper with an empty column under a rule about keepers, and you have come down this lane to put the nearest pair of hands in it. My hands are not that pair of hands. I have got four empty houses standing up this lane that this yard counted on the first of this month and I said no before I was asked, and I am not going to be a column on a sheet ten days after I said that, and if you write me on anything at all I will read it back at that counter and there will be two of us reading it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a scale is not a clerk of a house, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the eleventh of the sixth month, and that a person who has been asked a question and has answered it with a no and a reason has refused that one question and nothing else, and cannot be counted either way about anything else, and that a column printed empty on a sheet is a column and not a person, and did not become a person this morning.

A man of fifty-six said, in the ordinary voice and to nobody in particular, that on the eighth of this month he had told that yard that a printed rule is better than his own because his own needed somebody in the room to agree with it, and that this morning he wanted to add the other half of it, which is that an empty column is not a fault in the column, and that he had not expected to have to say that and had not expected anybody to hand him the chance.

---

At about eleven they were at the near end of that lane and about nine of them were standing in front of the building, and the plank door in the end wall had a hasp on the outside of it and a nail in the hasp instead of a staple.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing worked the hasp with his left hand and the nail did not move, and then with his right hand, and it did not move, and he said that in six weeks that nail has gone from tight to loose and that there is nothing in this district that is any use on a nail that has been in a wall since before he was born.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam went round the outside to look at the back of it, on the ground that a building with two doors is only shut if the second one is shut, and the end wall is about nine feet of brick with no window in it at all, and the ditch behind it is about two feet deep in the sixth month and going down into it is not a thing nine people can be asked to stand and watch.

He got down about two feet on his hands and knees and put his right hand flat on the mud at the bottom of it, and his right hand has not been much use to him since the afternoon of the second of this month, and about a minute afterwards he came up that bank backwards with the hand hanging at his side and said out loud, in the ordinary voice and to about nine people, that it is no use, and that he was telling them because he had said nothing about it for nine days and that nine days was too long a time to say nothing about a hand.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said that, and that nobody asked him a follow-up question, and that the record about the nine days says not asked, and that a hand that is not right is a hand and is not a figure about anybody and is not a finding and is not a reason for anything, and that the ditch behind that building is about four inches deep in the dry and about four feet in the wet and is not an exit and is not a route and is not a road and did not become a seventh instrument of the six.

Then the plank door came off with the flat of an axe in about two seconds and made the noise in the ditch that it made on the fifth of this month, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam held the board without being asked and neither of them said one word about it, and a clerk entered that a board off a wall is not a door and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own, and that nobody asked anybody whether they wanted that building shut and that the record about the shutting says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of the eleventh of the sixth month in one line, and it was that a building with two doors was to be shut by a keeper, and the column for the keeper was empty on the face of the sheet, and the sheet cannot be obeyed by anybody, and the building did not shut, and that a plank was off the back of that building in the middle of the morning and nobody in this district decided it, and that neither of those two things was decided.

The man of fifty-six said the sentence the day was going to be remembered by, and it was counted and came to a hundred and nine, and a clerk entered that nobody in that lane said it after him.

"**A year ago that building had one door and a curtain in it and everybody who went in went through the front. It has had a second door for about six weeks and nobody has ever once counted how many people went out of it, and today a piece of print with an empty column on it has shut nothing and opened everything, and I want the plainest sentence I can find for what that is and I have not got one. The column is empty. The building is not. That is all I have and I am not going to make a figure of it in a lane.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a sheet which names a job and prints the rule for the job and leaves the column for the job empty has found nobody and has said so in print, and that a mark may not carry a job and a printed line may not either, and that a keeper has never been on a page in this basin and there never was one and no keeper is named.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the board back on its nails in the end wall and could not get the nails in, because the holes had closed up in the wet, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam held it again and said nothing.

The front door of that building has a handle and it is not locked, and he turned it, and about nine people looked at about nine mats pushed up against the wall of it and a candle stub on a ledge, and nobody went further in. A man of about nineteen who counts counted the mats and got nine and then said out loud that a mat is not a figure about anybody and that he was not going to count anything else in there, and a clerk entered that he counted nine and stopped, and that the record about the rest of the room says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing, in his own words and not in the six things, whether he would go two miles and write down what was in that room, and he said no in about four seconds and gave a reason and the count came to seventy-four.

"**No. I am not going in there with a slate. There are about nine mats and a candle stub and not one thing over the door of it, and if I write it down then in a year somebody comes with a sheet and asks me where I got it from, and I will have to say the eleventh of the sixth month, and that is a day a man can be found on.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move, and that about nine mats and a candle stub are not a seventh instrument of the six and are not a copy and are not one of the three documents this district does not own.

About nine people slept in that room that night and were gone before six in the morning, and nobody sent for one of them and nobody fetched one of them, and the record about the nine says not asked.

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A SHEET THAT SAID A BUILDING WITH TWO DOORS WAS TO BE SHUT BY ITS KEEPER DID NOT SHUT IT, AND THE COLUMN FOR THE KEEPER WAS EMPTY ON THE FACE OF THE SHEET, AND ONE PERSON WAS ASKED TO BE THE KEEPER AND SAID NO WITH A REASON, AND A MAN WENT DOWN INTO THE DITCH BEHIND THE BUILDING AND CAME UP WITH A HAND THAT DOES NOT CLOSE AND TOLD ABOUT NINE PEOPLE SO.**
