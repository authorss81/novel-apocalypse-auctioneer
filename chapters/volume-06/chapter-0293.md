# Chapter 293: The Seventh Morning

---

The thirteenth of the seventh month was flat and warm and the light came off the wall inside the gate early, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right, and about nineteen people were standing about in that yard.

"**Two hundred and one days on the board and five hundred and seventeen days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and thirty-one, and a hundred and ninety-two days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is nineteen days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is eighty-four days back, and the table against that wall eleven miles off stood up for the seventh time this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nineteen is eighteen days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated and was not used this morning, and that a table which has stood up seven mornings is a table and not a habit, and that a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said in that yard on the eleventh of this month that a sixth morning is a figure nobody has asked for and a seventh is a habit, and that he said on the same day that he would come back into that yard on the eighteenth of this month and say which of the two the seventh one was, **and that nobody asked him to and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the seventh morning happened this morning and he was in that yard at about ten and said nothing at all to anybody, and that a man who says a thing on a day he named is not making a habit of saying it.**

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said one thing, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and thirty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**There is a figure on that sheet at that gatepost and there is a figure going to be at that table and I am not going to say the rest of that sentence in this yard, and I would like it entered that I have stopped in the middle of it in front of about nineteen people. The number on that sheet was arrived at by knocking on doors. The number at that table is going to be arrived at by people standing in the open in a field being counted at it. Those are two ways of arriving at a figure about people and I have done both of them, and I did the first one at about two in the morning on the night of the twenty-fourth of last month, to about nine people coming through one door in about two hundred yards of lane, and I did not know one of their names and I put a piece of paper in my coat and went home. I am going to stop here. I said in this yard on the third of this month that I am not going to be one of a number of anything and that was eight days ago and I am not saying anything new to you this morning. I am saying half of a thing and stopping, and a clerk can enter that I stopped.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who digs loam is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a count and is not a keeper of anything, and that he said the half of a sentence out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people and did not say the rest of it, and that **the record about the half he did not say says not asked**, and that four hundred and eleven on a sheet at that gatepost and seven mornings at a table eleven miles off are two figures and are not added together and are not a finding about each other, and that **no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved on the thirteenth of the seventh month**, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to say a thing has not refused to say it and cannot be counted either way, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and that the eight did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the thirteenth of the seventh month.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them said one thing back to that in that yard, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and eighty-two, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about and that about nine people heard it.

"**I have been in this basin twelve days and I have watched a man count a thing twice and I want to say what I have noticed, which is that the reason he stopped in the middle is that he has been the counted one. He counted about nine people coming through one door at two in the morning on the night of the twenty-fourth of last month and did not know one of their names. I have been at the other end of a thing for twelve days and I have not been counted by anybody yet, and the difference between those two states is not courage. It is that he did it first and I am doing it after. I am not going to say that it is the same and I am not going to say that it is different, and I am not going to be a man who comes down eleven miles of road in a seventh month and explains a man's stopping to about nineteen people, when the man who stopped has not explained it either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who puts tables up is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a table, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to explain another man has not agreed with him and has not disagreed with him and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not explaining says not asked, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and that the eight did not move again at about half past eleven in the morning on the thirteenth of the seventh month.

The man of fifty-six said one thing back to that and it was not counted, and a clerk entered that it was not counted and that nobody asked him what it was about and that the record about the not counting says not asked.

"**He is going to walk into this yard in about five days and say the other half of that, or he is not, and neither of those is a finding. What he has done this morning is the only thing in this district anybody has done about a figure in six weeks that I have not got a better version of. I have been standing in this yard for five months telling anybody who would stand still that a door is not a keeper and a wall is not an address and a table with four legs is a counter, and it turns out that is not a list of things that are wrong, it is a list of things that are incomplete. An incomplete thing can be finished by somebody who is not you. I am not going to be the man who is pleased about a sentence that stops halfway.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to be pleased about a thing has not consented to it and cannot be counted either way, and that a shape noticed a second time is not a finding, and that two men have now said a part of the same thing on two different days in that yard and that the two are two and are not added together and that the record about the second saying says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the bank with her scale under the lean-to at about half past eleven and said one sentence out loud to about four people who were standing at the end of the trestle table, and it was not counted, and a clerk entered that it was not counted and that nobody asked her for it.

"**I stood at that scale nine years and weighed about nine sacks a day and I have never weighed a day, and a man of about thirty-four has just told about nineteen people that he is not going to say the rest of a sentence, and I have never once in nine years had a figure of a person come out of that scale, and there is not one of those two things going to happen at that table either, whatever they print on it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a scale is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a figure and is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that four empty houses belonging to her stand empty and are not going to be offered again before the first day of the season after this one on her own statement of the twentieth of last month, and that a sack is not a day and a day is not a person and the two are not added together.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing came into that yard and put a piece of paper down on the end of the trestle table, and there were two lines of print on it and the chalk mark of a third one started, and it had been lying on the end of that table since the fifth of this month, and nobody had picked it up.

He said one thing about it, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to two hundred and twenty, and a clerk entered that he was asked for nothing and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

"**This is the piece of paper I got through on the fifth of this month and I could not do the third line of. It has been on the end of this table for eight days and about nineteen people have walked past it every one of them and nobody has picked it up, and I want to say that I have not minded that and that I have noticed that I have not minded it, and those are two different things. A man of about thirty-four refused a join of two documents on this table on that morning and gave a reason, and the reason was that putting two documents on one page makes them one thing a man can pick up and carry, and I have thought about that for eight days with a piece of my own on the table and it turns out he was talking about this as well. I cannot write a full line of anything and I have not been able to for three weeks, and this is a hand that stops, and a hand that stops is a hand and is not a refusal and is not a consent, and I would like that to stay on the page about this piece of paper for as long as the paper is here.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two lines of print on a piece of paper are two lines and are not a document and are not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that the two lines on that paper are the first two of the sheet of four lines of print and that the two are not a copy of the five lines of print as it stands and that the three are not added together, and that a hand that stops in the middle of a document is a hand and is not a refusal and is not a consent, and that nobody asked him for it and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and that a piece of paper with two lines on it is not a seventh of them and did not become one, and that the six did not move on the thirteenth of the seventh month.

She entered what was still standing on the thirteenth of this month, in the same hand and not as a paragraph. She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the hundred and four days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood eighty-one days and nobody has sat in it on any of the eighty-one, and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and that neither moved today and that no join was proposed today, and that the columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to, and that the rival record has now been in force forty-two days and has been read out in that yard seven times and that nobody has answered it.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was thirteen days old and the first day of the eighth month was nineteen days off, and a man who digs loam had said half a sentence in a yard and stopped in the middle of it on purpose and about nineteen people had heard him do it.

**AND ON THE THIRTEENTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM SAID OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN THAT THERE IS A FIGURE ON A SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST AND THERE IS A FIGURE GOING TO BE AT A TABLE ELEVEN MILES OFF, AND HE STOPPED IN THE MIDDLE OF THAT SENTENCE ON PURPOSE AND ASKED FOR IT TO BE ENTERED THAT HE DID NOT SAY THE REST OF IT.**
