# Chapter 259: The Bell In A Frame

---

The ninth of the sixth month was clear and hot by ten and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there forty-seven days and nobody had sat in it on any of the forty-seven.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and sixty-seven days on the board and four hundred and eighty-three days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and ninety-seven, and a hundred and fifty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning, and about nine of us are going four miles out past the loams this morning to look at a bell that is not going to be rung, and I have said that sentence out loud before we go so that a clerk can write it down as a thing nine people agreed to before they went and not as a thing nine people found out afterwards.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the phrase *not going to be rung* was said at the gate at about half past nine in the morning by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and that nine people heard it, and that nobody was asked to agree to it and that a man who says a thing out loud before he does it has not had anybody's consent to it, and that the record about the nine hearing it says not asked.

---

The yard is about four miles out past the loams and the frame in it is of oak about eleven feet high with two uprights let into the ground about four feet apart and a beam across the top, and the oak is older and darker than the oak at the loams and there is a second frame behind it about nine feet back that is newer and is holding nothing.

The bell hangs in the middle of it on a headstock of iron and it is about two feet across the mouth and it is cracked from the rim up about a third of the way on the side that faces the loams, and the clapper is there and the rope is not, and about three feet of rope is gone and the rest of it is a short stub of about nine inches hanging inside the beam where nobody standing on the ground can reach it.

The woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book stood under that frame for about two minutes and then said the thing about the rope, and it was counted and it came to a hundred and fifteen.

"**Three feet of that rope went in about nine years and about a foot of it went in the last one. Somebody took it. It was not cut, it was untied at the top and pulled down a bit at a time so that you could not see it from the road, and the nine inches that is left is the length that keeps a man from being able to say he has rung it by accident. Whoever took it did not want the bell gone. They wanted the bell to be there and to be unreachable, and that is a different thing and it is a thing somebody thought about for a long time.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a bell is not a keeper and not a bearer and not a figure about anybody, and that a rope that has been taken off a beam so that a bell cannot be rung is not a door and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own, and that the record about who untied it says not asked, and that a man who has never been asked to be a keeper of a bell is not a keeper of a bell.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half about the bell, and the count came to a hundred and twenty-seven.

"**A bell is a thing in a frame and a frame holds it up and a bell does not need a person. That is the first honest thing anybody in this district can say about a useful object in eighteen months and I have been waiting five months for a thing to say it about. But a bell is not a plank. A plank in a notch does not keep nights, and I do not know what a bell does and neither does anybody in this yard, and I have been in this basin eighteen years and I have never once seen it rung, and the two times it has been rung are on two walls about a mile apart and I have read both of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a bell is an instrument and not a keeper and that a bell has never been on a page in this basin and there never was one and no bell is named on any page, and that the two times the bell was rung are written on two walls in two houses and that neither wall is in this district's books and that the three documents this district does not own are three and the two walls are not a fourth and are not a fifth.

The two walls are set out here in full, and either of them must reproduce exactly if it is read out again in a room.

The first is on the gable end of a barn about a mile back toward the loams, and it is in whitewash, and the top of the last rung has gone and there is nothing above the first line of it.

> **RUNG. THE ELEVENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH OF THE FIFTEENTH YEAR. AT NIGHT. NOBODY CAME.**

The second is on the inside of a byre about a mile the other way, and it is in charcoal, and it is in a different hand, and it is four lines and the last one has been rubbed at.

> **RUNG ON A NIGHT IN THE MONTH AFTER THAT ONE.**
> **WE WENT DOWN THE LANE WHEN WE HEARD IT.**
> **WE CAME BACK UP THE LANE WHEN IT STOPPED.**
> **NAMES OF WHO HEARD IT —**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the first wall is a single hand and the second is a different hand and that the two are not one record and are not added together, and that a wall is not an address and is not a page and is not a book and that the eleven words under the rubbed line are rubbed, and that the record about the rubbed words says not asked, and that nobody in this district has been asked to say whether they were ever legible.

Then a panel arrived in that yard at about eleven in the morning that nobody had asked for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard asked for it and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it, and entered that a panel is a rule and a rule is not a document and is not a fourth and is not one of the six instruments this district built and not named.

> **A BELL IS A VOICE. A VOICE REACHES EVERYBODY WITHIN THE SOUND OF IT.**
>
> **A LIST OF WHO HEARD IT IS A LIST OF WHO EXISTED ON A NIGHT. A LIST OF WHO EXISTED ON A NIGHT IS THE FIRST HALF OF A FINDING.**
>
> **THE HALF THAT IS WORTH HAVING IS THE ONE WHERE NOBODY IS NAMED, AND A BELL THAT NAMES NOBODY IS A BELL THAT HAS NOT RUNG.**

**Nobody in that yard disagreed with it out loud.** A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody did, and entered that she had been in that yard, and entered that **the record about the disagreeing says not asked**, and entered that about nine people were standing under a frame of oak at four miles out past the loams and that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, the man of about thirty-four who digs loam, the man of fifty-six, the woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route and a man of about nineteen who counts were five of the nine and that she did not count the other four on purpose and that she is not going to be asked for them.

A man of fifty-six said out loud that he disagreed with the third of the three and then said nothing else at all, and a clerk entered that he said he disagreed and that he did not say the rest of it, and that a man who says he disagrees and does not say why has not converted a disagreement into a finding, and that the record about the not saying says not asked.

---

The registrar of this district's records office was not in that yard and was in the room where the books are, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came in with a shut door behind him at about four in the afternoon and she said that he could not have that door shut on her own room and that he shut it.

He wrote the panel down on the back of a sheet and put it on the table in front of her and she read it, and then she disagreed with the third of its three lines out loud, to him, and not to a room, and there was nobody there to count it and a clerk of nineteen yards away entered the whole of it afterwards, from him and not from her, and the record about the nine seconds and the not saying it out loud in a yard says not asked.

"**A bell that names nobody is not a bell that has not rung. It is a bell somebody has written a piece of paper about, and the paper is in a book four hundred miles away, and the paper has got a heading over the column, and everybody within the sound of it is in the column, and there is no third column on that page and nobody in this basin has ever been asked whether there should be one. You do not get a bell for nothing and nobody in this basin believes that, and a rule that says the half worth having is the half with nobody named in it is a rule written by somebody who has a page in front of him and is asking the rest of us to be pleased about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the registrar of this district's records office is not a bearer of any instrument and is not a holder and is not a steward and is not a party of anything, and that a registrar who disagrees with a printed rule in a room with one man in it has not made a finding and has not made a column, and that a rule about who gets named in a list of hearers is a rule about a page and not about a bell, and that **the bell was not rung on the ninth of the sixth month of the eighteenth year and the rope is about nine inches long and the record about the not ringing says not asked.**

The woman of about thirty-one refused a second thing that day in a doorway about nine yards up the lane behind the bank, and gave a reason, and a clerk entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a woman who keeps a route is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move at about five in the afternoon on the ninth of the sixth month.

She had been asked by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, in his own words and not in the six things, whether the route passes the frame. And she said no, and the reason she gave, entered by a clerk of nineteen years from the man who asked, was that if the route passed the frame then four generations of people would have known it, and that a frame in a yard four miles out past the loams that everybody knows about is a thing four generations have arranged to know nothing about, and that he did not ask her a second thing and that the record about his not asking a second time says not asked.

The day came down about half past seven and the eleventh of this month was two days off, and the woman of about thirty-one went up the lane before the light went, and a clerk entered that going is not the same as arriving and that this district has never once given that woman a day to be here on and did not give her one today.

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WALKED FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS AND STOOD UNDER AN OAK FRAME WITH A CRACKED BELL IN IT AND ABOUT NINE INCHES OF ROPE LEFT ON THE BEAM, AND A PANEL ARRIVED THERE UNASKED AND NOBODY DISAGREED WITH IT OUT LOUD, AND THE BELL WAS NOT RUNG, AND THE ONLY PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT WHO SAID OUT LOAD THAT SHE DISAGREED WITH IT WAS IN A ROOM FOUR HUNDRED YARDS AWAY WITH A SHUT DOOR AND ONE MAN IN IT.**
