# Chapter 290: Two Sides, One Gate

---

The tenth of the seventh month came in hot and clear and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen and the chair at the end of that trestle table had been standing there seventy-eight days and nobody had sat in it on any of the seventy-eight.

"**A hundred and ninety-eight days on the board and five hundred and fourteen days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and twenty-eight, and a hundred and eighty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-two days off counted off this morning, being twenty-one days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is eighty-one days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and eight days, and about a hundred and one days ago today, counting the first of the fourth month in, nobody put a line on a board.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that twenty-two is the last of ten figures counted off ten mornings, and that the ten of them are thirty-one, thirty, twenty-nine, twenty-eight, twenty-seven, twenty-six, twenty-five, twenty-four, twenty-three and twenty-two, and that the first of those ten was counted off the first of the seventh month and not off the thirtieth of the sixth.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them came through the gate with the hand-cart at about half past ten in the morning and tried to lift the second of the two trestle boards off the back of it, and he could not lift it with his right hand and he could not lift it with his left one, and he stood at the back of the cart with both hands under it for about nine seconds and did not get it up.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam, who had a hand out of a ditch behind a building with two doors on the fourth of this month and was helped out of it by a man with a shoulder, lifted it off the cart himself and put it against the outside wall and said nothing at all about doing it.

Then the man of about thirty-seven said one thing about his two hands, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and it was counted and came to a hundred and thirty-seven, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about.

"**I have carried a hand-cart eleven miles twice this month and I have lost the use of both hands doing it and I am not going to be able to set that table up on my own tomorrow or the day after. I am not asking this yard for anything. I am saying it here because I came down that road with a cart and a tent and I told this yard on the first of this month that I had not put a price on anything until somebody asked me twice, and I have been putting something on it every day since, and it turns out to be both of my hands, and I would like there to be one place in this district where a man says that before somebody works it out of him.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says out loud that his body is going to be the price of a thing is not a consent and is not a bearer and is not a party of anything, and that a body is not a figure about a man and two hands are not a price of anything, and that nobody in that yard was asked to carry anything and that the record about the carrying says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered what had not moved in ten days, and it went in the same hand as the rest and it was not a paragraph.

She entered that the things this district does not have are five and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and that the name of that fifth thing was said out loud in that yard on the third of this month by a man who has it to sell and that saying it did not make it, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and that a join of five lines and four lines on one page was proposed in that yard on the fifth of this month and was refused on the page by a man who gave a reason and that a join is a join and is not a seventh of them, and that a table on a cart and a copy in another man's hand and a panel and a sheet of four lines of print and a wall about four feet high and a man with a cart were entered as not a seventh, and that the six did not move.

She entered that the three documents this district does not own are three and that three documents arrived or were made in ten days and none became a fourth of them, and that the protected things are five and that no sixth was added and that an address is not a sixth and a lean-to is not a sixth and a table is not a sixth and a panel is not a sixth, and that the conditions with no end on it are four and that no fifth was proposed in ten days, and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and that neither moved on any of the ten days and that one join was proposed and refused, and that the refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading and the fourth line of the offer that has been on a table in this district for five months was not read out in this block.

She entered that the refusals of eleven coppers a week are nine and that they moved from eight on the third of this month because a refusal was made out loud in that yard in front of about nineteen people by a person who gave a reason, and that **the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight and did not move, and a refusal with a reason is not one of those eight, and a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and the nine and the eight are not the same figure and are not added together**, and that the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and that the seven and the eight and the nine are three different figures and are not added together.

She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the hundred and one days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood seventy-eight days and nobody has sat in it on any of the seventy-eight, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the eighty days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and that she entered that figure out of a ladder and did not ask him for it on any of the ten days of this block and that he said it on none of them, and that the boards in this district are five and the lines that are full are six and a seventh is ruled and empty at the ninth place, and that the columns of not-askings are four and that she did not rule one this month, and that there is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and there never has been one and that eleven coppers a week is a price a man named out loud on the third of this month and is not a rate.

She entered that the rival record has been in force thirty-nine days and has been read out in that yard seven times and that the eighth reading was refused out loud on the twenty-fourth of last month with a reason and that a refused reading is not a reading and that nobody has answered that record. And she entered that this district has five different ninths in it and that they are the ninth of the nine charters, the ninth of the eleven houses the woman of fifty-eight carries water to, the ninth of the nine printed nights, the ninth place on the page out of the cave, and the ninth of the ten days this block ran, and that they are five ninths and are not added together, and that a sixth was available to her this morning and she did not take it, and that a clerk who could have made a sixth and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing.

She entered that the man of about thirty at a counter is at the sixteenth time down the eleven mile road and the next is the seventeenth and that this district has never once given that man a day to be here on, and that the man of about forty-one who keeps a road house at the top of the road that climbs is not in this district on any of the ten days either, and that the man with the portfolio is not in this district and that nothing in that yard says where he is, and that a man of about forty-four with a case went out of that gate at about two in the afternoon on the nineteenth of last month with a list of the people in this district who are not in a household, and that the list is not on either of the two sheets that went into that yard this month and is not in this district and that nobody in this district has seen it and nobody has counted it, and that a man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them came down the eleven mile road on the last day of last month with a hand-cart and gave no name to anybody and was not asked for one.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the last thing of the ten days in a lane behind a bank at about half past seven in the evening, to about four people, and it was counted and came to two hundred and eighty-four, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it out loud and that a clerk was four hundred yards away and got it from him and not from her, and that the record about the four hearing it says not asked.

"**Two sides came at that gate in ten days and neither of them came to take anything out of it. One of them wanted to say out loud where a yard is so that a person who is not in a household could find a table, and the other one printed four lines and did not have a name on them and said that the place an offer is made in goes in the record of that place, and both of them are right and both of them want this yard. I have spent five months standing in this basin telling anybody who would stand still that a door is not a keeper and a wall is not an address and a table with four legs is a counter, and I said the last one of those wrong myself in a yard on the twenty-eighth of last month and I have not got that one right either, and I have not got this one wrong yet. A market is a place where somebody asks somebody a question. That yard has not asked anybody in it a question about what they want in ten days and two people have come to it and one of them has lost the use of both hands and neither of them has been refused by it, because a yard cannot refuse anybody, and that is not a thing to be proud of at the end of ten days. I do not know what that gate is yet. I know what it is not and I have said so out loud in a lane and I am not going to be the man who writes it down as something else.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a yard that is the address of two sides is a yard and not a market, and that a market is a thing where a person is asked a question, and that a yard that cannot refuse anybody has not refused anybody in ten days, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and that no seventh was proposed after the fifth of this month and the six did not move, and that a wrong count is not struck in this district and no count has been struck in this block and that the forty-one names said out loud in that yard on the seventeenth of last month are forty people and one of them is a person twice and that figure is not struck either, and that the record about the not striking says not asked.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was ten days old and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-two days off, and about four hundred and eleven people in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off this district were to be moved on or before it, and no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved in ten days.

**AND ON THE TENTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN SAID OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN THAT HE HAD LOST THE USE OF BOTH HANDS CARRYING A TABLE ELEVEN MILES, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN HAD BEEN ASKED TWICE IN TEN DAYS BY TWO SIDES TO BE AN ADDRESS AND HAD ANSWED NEITHER TIME, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID IN A LANE THAT A MARKET IS A PLACE WHERE SOMEBODY ASKS SOMEBODY A QUESTION AND THAT HE DID NOT KNOW WHAT THAT GATE WAS YET.**
