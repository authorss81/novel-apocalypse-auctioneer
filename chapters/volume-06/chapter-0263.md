# Chapter 263: Eleven Coppers A Week

---

The thirteenth of the sixth month came in hot before eight and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there fifty-one days and nobody had sat in it on any of the fifty-one.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and seventy-one days on the board and four hundred and eighty-seven days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and one, and a hundred and sixty-two days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is forty-nine days off counted off this morning, and there is a man standing at that gate with a case and I am going to open it.**"

The gate took three goes with the left hand on the first of this month and had taken four with a fifth on the right, and it took two this morning, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody counted the goes and that the record about the two says not asked.

Then a man of about forty-four came through it with a case about as long as his forearm and put the case on the trestle table, and he said what he was, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and seven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I broker. That is the whole of what I do and I am not going to dress it up, because there is a man in this yard who has been asking everybody to be careful what they call a thing for five months and I would rather be called it early. I put names into books for money. I have been doing it four years and I have never been in a place where a person said no to me, and I am telling you that in the first minute because it is cheaper for both of us than finding out at the end of a week.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says what he is in the first minute has not thereby been asked anything and has not been made a party of anything, and that a man with a case is not a counter and is not a keeper of a yard and is not a bearer of a toll, and that a yard a stranger can walk into is not a market and a man who walks into one is not the making of one.

Then the man of about forty-four asked them to read his card, and a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read it out in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them and read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **A NAME THAT IS IN NO BOOK CAN BE PUT IN ONE FOR A CUT OF THE FIRST QUARTER OF WHAT THE NAME BRINGS IN.**
> **A NAME BRINGS IN WHAT A PERSON WILL PAY A WEEK FOR THE USE OF SOMETHING THEY HAVE NOT GOT.**
> **I DO NOT ASK ANYBODY WHAT THEY ARE CALLED. SOMEBODY ELSE HAS TOLD ME AND I HAVE PAID FOR IT.**

Nobody in that yard said anything for about nine seconds. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the card is a document and that it belongs to the man who put it on the table, and that it is not one of the three documents this district does not own, and that it names no person and no place and carries one figure and one week, and that the third of the three lines is a sentence about a method and not about a person, and that the record about the somebody else says not asked, and that the rule of the counter, which is that a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, has just been used by a man who is not of this district to make his own offer public in about nine seconds.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, who has stood at that scale nine years at the sluice end of the loams and who weighs about nine sacks a day, said the fifth of the five things this district does not have out loud in that yard, and the man of about forty-four priced it, and a man of fifty-six refused it and gave a reason that is not the shape of the other eight, and the count came to a hundred and forty-eight.

"**A way to pay a person who is not in a household. I have heard that sentence in this yard eight times in five months and I have said no to it eight times, and every one of those eights was a man of this district offering eleven coppers a week for something he was already doing for nothing, and this morning it is a man from four hundred miles away offering a quarter of what a name brings in, and that is a different offer and I am not going to pretend it is the same one, and I am also not going to say yes to it, because a thing this district cannot pay for is a thing this district has not agreed to be charged for, and a man with a case does not need anybody's agreement and that is exactly what I object to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have was named out loud in that yard and is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and that it did not move, and that a refusal of a different shape is still one refusal of that fifth thing and is not a ninth of anything, and that the five are five, and that a rate turning a year into coppers is still none and there never has been one, and that a cut is not a rate, and that a rate is a thing a named person pays under a term, and that what the man of about forty-four is offering is a thing a man outside this district pays a man inside it and it names no term at all.

Then the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing, who has been asked for eleven coppers a week in this district eight times in five months and refused eight times, said yes, in about one second, and gave a reason, and it was counted and it came to a hundred and thirty-five.

"**Yes, and I will say out loud why, because if I do not say why then one of you will say it for me later and you will get it wrong. I have been refused eleven coppers a week eight times in this district and I have said yes to it in one second from a man with a case, and the difference is not the eleven coppers. It is that a man with a case has not spent five months asking me to do it. He has not made me a refusal first. I have been a refusal in this district for five months and I have got very good at it and it has never once cost me anything, and I would like to be paid for four months of being a refusal.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said yes to a thing he was refused by this district has not refused this district, and that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house, and that a person who says yes with a reason is not one of the eight and the eight did not move at about eleven in the morning on the thirteenth of the sixth month.

He wrote for about an hour on the end of that trestle table, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that what he wrote was a list of people in this district who are not in a household, and that a list of people who are not in a household is a document and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own because a man with a case owns it and paid for it, and that it is not a seventh instrument of the six and did not become one, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to, and that eleven coppers a week is a figure about a man and not a figure about the people on a list and is not a rate.

At about half past twelve his right hand cramped and he could not open it, and the man of about fifty-six took the pen out of his fingers and put it down on the table, and said nothing about it, and a clerk entered that a pen taken out of a hand by another man is not a bearer and is not a job and did not become a seventh instrument, and that nobody asked the man of about twenty-three anything about his hand and that the record about the hand says not asked.

---

In about nine feet of lane outside the office, about eight feet from the door of the room where the books are, at about half past three in the afternoon, the man the figure of twenty-one years is against offered the woman who keeps the books in that room one thing, and she refused in about four seconds, and there was nobody there to count either of them, and the whole of it is in a minute in one hand and is not in a book, and a clerk of nineteen years was four hundred yards away and entered it afterwards out of his hand and not out of hers.

He offered to stand in the yard of Lot Seventeen at ten o'clock the next morning, before the man with a case opened his case, and say out loud in daylight in front of about nineteen people what a man with a case is and what he came down eleven miles to sell.

"**No. The day a man with a case is a thing this yard is doing something about is the day he has been asked, and he has not been asked, and you saying his name in that yard does not ask him, it tells him that this yard has noticed him, and a man who knows he has been noticed is a man who has been asked whether he was told to come.**"

And he said one thing back, in the same hand and the same minute, and it was not counted and there was nobody there to count it.

"**That is the fifth refusal in a month and every one of them has been made by the only person in this basin who can make it, and I want to say the other half of it, which is that I offered to do a thing in that yard because I do not know how else to help you, and I have said that sentence in this lane before and you told me in June not to come and find you about the forty-one, and this is not the forty-one, and I am going to keep saying things in this lane until one of them is not refused, and I would rather you told me plainly which one to stop.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who refuses a man a thing in a lane has not refused the man and is not one of the eight, and that the eight did not move at about half past three in the afternoon on the thirteenth of the sixth month, and that a man who has said that he will keep offering has not acquired anything, and that the record about the next thing he is going to offer says not asked.

The man of about forty-four put his case down beside the trestle table at about four o'clock and asked for the end of it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave him the end of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the end of a trestle table is not a counter and that a corner of a yard is not an address and that a man with a case is not a keeper of anything, and that the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came round the back of the yard with his cart at about half past four so that it would not be standing in the lane, and that nobody sent him and that the record about the cart says not asked.

**AND ON THE THIRTEENTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR CAME INTO THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN WITH A CASE AND ASKED THE YARD TO READ HIS OWN CARD OUT LOUD, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE WHO HAD BEEN REFUSED ELEVEN COPPERS A WEEK EIGHT TIMES SAID YES IN ABOUT ONE SECOND, AND A MAN WHO HAD SPENT FIVE MONTHS ASKING EVERYBODY TO BE CAREFUL WHAT THEY CALLED A THING WAS USED TO MAKE AN OFFER PUBLIC IN NINE SECONDS.**
