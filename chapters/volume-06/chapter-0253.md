# Chapter 253: Four Miles Of Lane

---

The third of the sixth month was clear and cold for the hour and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there forty-one days and nobody had sat in it on any of the forty-one.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and sixty-one days on the board and four hundred and seventy-seven days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and ninety-one, and a hundred and fifty-two days separate the second of January and this morning, and there is a woman standing at the gate of this yard who has not been in it for eleven months and nobody sent for her.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was in the yard and that nobody sent for her and that she was not asked a first thing, and entered that the record about eleven months says not asked, and entered that a person who walks into a yard has not been brought into it and can go out of it again whenever she likes.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, who has stood at that scale nine years at the sluice end of the loams and who weighs about nine sacks a day, was at the far end of the trestle table with her sleeves turned up, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her, in his own words and not in the six things, whether she had slept, and she said she had not and that the ninth of the eleven houses she carries to has a woman in it who is being moved on the first of the month after next, and a clerk entered that a woman of fifty-eight who has said a thing out loud has not thereby been asked a question about it and that the record about the ninth of the eleven houses says not asked.

---

She came down the four miles in the dark and got in about a quarter to eight in the morning and stood about nine feet off the gate with a bundle about the size of a loaf on her shoulder, and she is about thirty-one and she keeps a route for people who are not in any book, and she is the only person in this basin who has ever found him without being sent, and she had not been down this lane in eleven months.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against came out of the gate at about half past eight and did not go within nine feet of her, and a clerk entered that he did not and that nobody asked him to and that the record about the nine feet says not asked.

He had a sheet in his hand. A clerk of nineteen years entered that it was folded in two and had been folded in two for three days and that it was not any of the nine local charters and that it was not the covenant and that it was not a copy of anything, and that a sheet that is not one of the nine and is not a copy of one of the nine is a new instrument and that the count of the instruments this district has built and not named is six and that a sheet with nothing on it yet is not an instrument.

He showed it to her at about half past eight with the fold still in it and asked her one question, in his own words and not in the six things, and she said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and seventy-four, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**Put my name under a line on it. That is what you have got in that fold and I have not even opened it and I do not need to. No. And the reason is not that I do not trust you and it is not that I have come four miles to be difficult at a gate. The reason is that a name under a line is a door, and a door is a thing a person comes to, and I have spent nine years making sure there is no door in this basin that has my name behind it. You wrote that rule yourself in April in a yard full of people, that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, and on the twentieth of last month a man came four miles on the strength of one and stood at that gate, and you told me about it in a lane and said findable is a worse door than a page.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who says no before she is asked, with a reason, is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a woman who keeps a route is not a clerk of a house, that the eight did not move at about half past eight in the morning on the third of the sixth month, and that a sheet with nothing on it yet is not a bearer and is not a figure about anybody and did not become a seventh instrument of the six.

Then he said the part that was his, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and eighty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I am not going to argue with you about the rule, because you have said it better than I said it and I said it first. Here is what I thought I was doing and I will say it out loud so that a clerk can write down that I said it. There are four hundred and eleven people on a sheet at a gatepost and the first day of the month after next is sixty days off, and the version of this that I can do in a week is a sheet with nine names under a line and one of the nine is you, and a name under a line means that nobody moves anybody on that settlement without five people saying so. That is not a small thing. I know what it is. I have spent five months building doors and I have just watched one of them cost me the last private thing I had, and I brought you one anyway, at six in the morning, down four miles, without asking you first what you were doing this month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said the price of a thing out loud before he has offered it has not consented to anything that is not on a sheet, and that a sheet with nothing written on it is not a page and is not a document and is not one of the three documents this district does not own, and that a man who convenes nine settlements has not convened one and that a door with a name behind it is not a settlement and is not a representation and is not nine.

Nobody argued with either of them. A man of fifty-six said that a man who has been made findable in a yard four hundred yards from his own gate and who has spent a morning building a door for somebody else because it is the only instrument he has got is a man who has learned a thing and has not yet learned what it is a thing about, and that he was not going to say the rest of it in a yard.

Then she said one thing about the number, in the plain voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and twenty-two.

"**I am not going to tell you how many of them there are and you are not going to ask me twice. If I told you and you wrote it down then there would be a piece of paper in this district with a number on it that a man in a shed somewhere got by asking me, and in nine years that number would be the only number anybody had, and the first time somebody wanted a number about us they would use that one and not go and knock. That is the whole trick and it has taken four generations and I am not the one who is going to undo it for a sheet with a fold in it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the record about four hundred and eleven says not asked, and that it says not asked from the person best placed in this basin to answer it and not from a stranger, and that a person who is not asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one this morning.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing offered to copy the sheet she had not opened, in his own hand, in about twenty minutes, for nothing, and she looked at him and said that a copy of a sheet with nothing on it is a copy of nothing and that he could keep his twenty minutes, and a clerk entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move.

The bundle about the size of a loaf was bread and a piece of chalk and two strings, and she put the bread on the trestle table and did not say where it came from and a clerk entered that nobody asked her and that the record about the bread says not asked, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing cut it in the yard and gave about nine people a piece and did not give her one and did not say why and a clerk entered that he did not and that the record about the ninth piece says not asked.

At about eleven she asked him to walk out of the yard with her, which he did, and they stood in the lane behind the bank about two hundred yards long with a wire on the left of it that has not been tight since before anybody in this district was born, and a clerk of nineteen years was not in that lane and entered afterwards, from one of the two of them and not from the other, that they were in it for about nine minutes.

What he said in it was not counted, because there was nobody there to count it and the record about it is in a minute in his own words and is not in a book.

"**I am not going to ask you how many there are. I am not going to ask you where the routes go. I am going to say one thing in a lane and then I am going to go and do something else with my morning, and if you want to answer it you can, and if you do not then nothing has happened in this lane and I would rather that than the other thing.**"

What she said back was also not counted, and a clerk entered that both of those sentences were entered in one hand and that the hand was his, and that a clerk who enters two people's sentences in one hand has entered a conversation and not a record, and that a conversation is not a document and is not one of the three documents this district does not own.

She was gone up the lane at about a quarter to twelve and nobody followed her, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came back into that yard at about half past eleven in the morning and said that going is not the same as arriving and that this district has never once given that woman a day to be here on and did not give her one this morning.

The day came down about half past seven. The chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there forty-one days and the fold in the sheet in the inside pocket of a coat had come out on its own, and a clerk entered that a fold coming out of a sheet is not a decision and is not a refusal and is not a consent and that the record about the fold says not asked.

**AND ON THE THIRD OF THE SIXTH MONTH A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO KEEPS A ROUTE FOR PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT IN ANY BOOK CAME DOWN FOUR MILES IN THE DARK AND STOOD NINE FEET OFF A GATE, AND HE ASKED HER FOR ONE NAME UNDER ONE LINE AND SHE SAID NO IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS AND GAVE AS THE REASON THAT A NAME UNDER A LINE IS A DOOR AND HE HAD SAID THAT HIMSELF IN A YARD A MONTH BEFORE.**
