# Chapter 295: Two Miles The Other Way

---

The fifteenth of the seventh month came in hot and the lane behind the bank had no shade in it at all by eleven, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**Two hundred and three days on the board and five hundred and nineteen days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and thirty-three, and a hundred and ninety-four days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is seventeen days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is eighty-six days back, and the table against that wall stood up for the ninth time this morning, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in any household and it has not been paid on any of the nine mornings that table has stood up on.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that seventeen is sixteen days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated and was not used this morning, and that a table which stands on a morning of a week is a figure about a morning of a week and is not a finding about anybody, and that **the fifth of the five things is still not paid and a man who named it out loud in that yard on the third of this month has it to sell and saying the name of it did not make it, and that a person who has stood at a table is not a person who has been paid and the two are not added together**, and that the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said in that yard on the eleventh of this month that a sixth morning is a figure nobody has asked for and a seventh is a habit, and that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said in that yard on the thirteenth that he was not going to say the rest of a sentence and did not say it, and that the record about the rest says not asked.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against left that yard at about ten in the morning and walked the two miles to the room with nothing over the door, where about nine people are, and there is a bench in it with eleven notches cut in the underside of the seat, and he did not go in.

He stood in the lane outside that door for about nine minutes. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a room with nothing over the door is not a counter and is not a market and that about nine people in a room is not a figure about anybody, and that the bench with the eleven notches was not looked at and that the record about the not looking says not asked.

---

Then he walked the two miles back and at about half past two in the afternoon he was sick behind the bank, on his own two feet, in the lane, about nine feet from where the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was standing, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not say anything about it then and did not say anything about it afterward, and the only reason a clerk of nineteen years four hundred yards away knows about it at all is that he was told one sentence about it that evening and entered it, and the record about the telling says not asked.

"**He had been on his feet since about seven this morning and had not eaten since six last night and he walked four miles and stood in a lane about nine minutes and came back, and he was sick behind the bank, and I was standing nine feet off him and I did not help him and I did not say anything to him about it and I am not going to say anything about it to you either except that it happened, because he did not ask me to keep it and I am not going to keep it for him.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a body is not a figure about a man and is not a price of anything and that a man who has not asked another man to hold a thing about him has not given it to him, and that the record about the not saying anything says not asked, and that nobody in this district has counted what a man did with his stomach on the fifteenth of this month.

At about half past four in the afternoon the man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood at the end of the trestle table in that yard and said one thing about the two miles, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to two hundred and thirty-eight, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about and that about nine people heard it.

"**On the second of this month a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat carried a copy of five lines up two miles to that room and had them read out to about nine people in it, and nobody asked her to carry them, and I have been in this district four days since then and I have thought about that in every one of them. Under the second of the printed lines as it stands on that table, that room is a place where a sheet has been read out, and therefore a place where an offer is made, and therefore a place that goes in the record of that place. I walked the two miles this morning. I stood in the lane about nine minutes. I am not going to walk in there and tell about nine people that they have become a place in a book four hundred miles off because of a thing a woman carried up on her own two feet on the second of this month, and I am not going to send anybody to do it either, and if that room becomes a place it is not going to be because a man who convenes nine settlements told the woman who keeps it that it had. She keeps that room and she is not a keeper of a place and I would like that in there in my own hand.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who convenes nine settlements is not a representative of a settlement that has not asked to be represented, and that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he is not going to walk into a room and tell nine people something is not a keeper of that room and is not a party of it and cannot be counted either way, and that a woman who keeps a goat is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a copy and is not a keeper of anything, and that **a copy is not a document and a copy that has been read out in a room is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own**, and that the three documents nobody owns are three and that the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart, who is not the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room and is not the man of about nineteen who counts, has been in that room and has seen the bench and has not said anything about the eleven notches, and that the record about the not saying says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the end of the trestle table at about half past five in the afternoon and said one thing about the second of the five lines, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and twenty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been twelve days in this basin and I have got a hand-cart and a tent and a table against a wall and two forearms, and this morning a man who convenes nine settlements stood in a lane two miles off for about nine minutes and did not go in, and I want to say the part of that which is mine and not his. That room has been a place since the second of this month because a woman carried a copy of five lines up two miles and had it read out in front of about nine people, and I wrote those five lines, and a man of about thirty-four told me in a yard on the second of this month that the fifth of them is not true and the second of them is a hand on somebody's shoulder, and he was right about both. So the room two miles off is a place because of a thing I wrote, and a yard four hundred yards from here is a place because a man read it out in it, and both of those happened without anybody asking, and I have got two things now and they are both mine and I have not got a third thing, and I am not going to stand in this yard and say that makes them a good thing.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who puts tables up is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a table and is not a keeper of a room with nothing over the door, and that the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room is not a person who is sent for and did not send for anybody, and that a copy is not a document, and that the three documents this district does not own are three and that neither a copy carried up two miles nor a sheet read out in a yard is a fourth of them, and that a man who has said out loud that two things are his is not a party of either of them and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them were at the top of the bank at about half past four in the afternoon, about six feet apart, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two of them were not in that yard and that about six people in it knew where they were and that nobody was sent.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing on the top of that bank, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to two hundred and ten-two, and a clerk entered that he said it about the two miles and that nobody at the top of that bank was going to be able to answer it.

"**I have been standing in this basin five months telling anybody who would stand still that a door is not a keeper, and this morning a man stood in a lane two miles off for about nine minutes and did not go in, and I have been thinking about that all day and I have got one thing about it and I do not think he would want to hear it. A door that nobody goes through is not a door that is closed. It is a door that has not been asked whether it is open. That woman in that room has not been asked this month, and she told a man on the eighth of this month not to ask her again this month, and that is a month and not a thing, and he has kept it, and he is not the only man in this basin who could turn up at that door tomorrow with a reason. I am not going to be the man who says that to him in a yard. I am saying it to you on the top of a bank where about six people cannot hear it, and I would like it entered that I said it up here and not down there.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a door and is not a keeper of anything, and that a door that has not been asked whether it is open is not a shut door and is not an open door and is not a record and is not a finding, and that a man who has said out loud that he would rather have said a thing on a bank than in a yard has not refused to say it in a yard and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not saying it in the yard says not asked, and that the columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was fifteen days old and the first day of the eighth month was seventeen days off, and a man who convenes nine settlements had stood in a lane two miles off and had not gone in, and nothing in that room had been told anything.

**AND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN WHO CONVENES NINE SETTLEMENTS WALKED TWO MILES TO A DOOR WITH NOTHING OVER IT AND STOOD IN THE LANE OUTSIDE IT ABOUT NINE MINUTES AND DID NOT GO IN, AND HE SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO BE THE MAN WHO TOLD NINE PEOPLE THAT THEY HAD BECOME A PLACE.**
