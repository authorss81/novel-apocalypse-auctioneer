# Chapter 286: The Woman Who Carries Water

---

The sixth of the seventh month was low and warm and the woman of fifty-eight was at the bank with two buckets at about ten in the morning when the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures, and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

"**A hundred and ninety-four days on the board and five hundred and ten days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and twenty-four, and a hundred and eighty-five days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-six days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is seventy-seven days back, and nobody in this district can say whether that night happened.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that twenty-six is twenty-five days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that two buckets were not counted and that nobody counted them.

Then the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them asked that yard one question, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he asked it in front of about nineteen people and that nobody was asked whether they wanted to hear it.

"**I am going to ask a question in this yard and I want to say first that I am asking it because I do not know the answer and not because I have got one, and I am asking it in daylight where a stranger can walk into because a question asked in a lane is a question with no answer on the record. Does anybody in this district carry water to a house in the ground.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything for about four seconds, and about four people in it turned round and looked at the woman of fifty-eight, who was at the bank with two buckets and had not asked to be looked at, and a man of about nineteen who counts counted the people who turned round and got four, and gave the number out loud, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he gave it and that a number a man of about nineteen counts in a yard in daylight in front of about nineteen people is a figure about four people looking round and is not a figure about the woman of fifty-eight and is not a finding, and that the record about the four looking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing about it, out loud, in about four seconds, and a clerk entered that he said it before anybody else in that yard had said anything.

"**Nobody is to answer that. Not you, not me, and not the man who asked it. A question about water in this district is not a question about water, it is a question about a house, and there is a house in this district that two people in this yard know about, and a man with a case found another one in nine minutes on the fourteenth of last month because a woman at a house on that lane told him where it was, and I am not going to be in a yard this morning while this basin works out which lane that is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house, and that a man who says out loud that nobody is to answer a question has not refused the question and cannot be counted either way, and that the columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to, and that the record about the not answering says not asked.

And the woman of fifty-eight answered it, out loud, in about four seconds, on her own two feet, before anybody asked her a second time, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had not been asked and that the record about her not being asked says not asked.

Then she said the rest of it in the ordinary voice and it was counted and came to two hundred and five, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard was able to answer her.

"**Yes. I have carried water to eleven houses for nineteen years and the ninth of those eleven is a house in the ground and it is in the shadow of a building I can see two ends of from a rise a mile out past the loams, and the woman in it is not one of the four hundred and eleven on that sheet at your gatepost and she is not on any sheet, and she asked me for a second bucket in the second week of last month and I put a second bucket there on the third of that month. I am saying this now because four of you have just turned round and looked at me, and being looked at is worse than saying it, and I would rather be a woman who carried water to a house than a house that nobody in this district can say anything about. The woman in that house is not on the list of the people who are to be moved. She has never been asked a question by anybody in nineteen years. I have asked her about a bucket twice. I have never asked her anything else and I am not going to today.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the ninth of the eleven houses the woman of fifty-eight carries water to is a house in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off, is not one of the four hundred and eleven, is not on any sheet, and is not on the list of the people who are to be moved, and that **no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved on the sixth of the seventh month**, and that the woman of fifty-eight is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a bucket and is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a woman who carries water to a house is not a woman who keeps it, and that a person who has said out loud that she is not going to ask somebody something has not refused to ask and cannot be counted either way.

Then the thing came into that yard with nothing else and nobody asked for it.

> **A FIGURE MADE BY COUNTING THE PEOPLE WHO CAME TO A PLACE IS A FIGURE ABOUT THE COMING AND NOT ABOUT THE PEOPLE.**

A man of fifty-six disagreed with it out loud in about four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he disagreed with it and that nobody had asked him to and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

"**That is the fifth sentence of mine that has been said back to me in twenty days and I have not said it once. I have said a version of it four times in five months and I have never once said those words, and I have watched a man of about thirty-one who digs loam say the true thing about a table with four legs and say it in a yard in the middle of the afternoon so that nobody found out in six months, and I have never once been able to do that. I do not think that is wrong. I think it is the fourth time this month that something in this district has been right and been said by somebody who is not me, and I am not going to stand in this yard and take it off the shelf it is on.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a panel does not create a consent and does not make a person a bearer and does not make a house into a party and does not turn a right to give something up into a refusal and does not decide who hears a bell, and that a man who has disagreed with one has not refused it and cannot be counted either way, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and that this is not a seventh of them and did not become one, and that the six did not move on the sixth of the seventh month.

At about half past four in the afternoon the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the top of the bank with the man of fifty-six, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two of them were not in that yard and that about six people in it knew they were at the top of the bank and that nobody was sent and that the record about the sending says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven said one thing about the ninth of the eleven houses, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and fifty-nine, and a clerk entered that he said it about forty feet from a step of two stones outside the second house up that lane and that the record about the distance says not asked.

"**I am not going to ask about that house again. I asked about houses this morning from a yard in front of about nineteen people and four of them turned round and looked at a woman of fifty-eight, and she answered me herself in about four seconds, and she has carried water to that house for nineteen years and it is not on any sheet, and I have got a sheet of my own with a fifth line on it that says a person cannot be uncounted again. If that house is on the list of the people who are to be moved then it is on the list of a body that counts by knocking on doors, and a house in the ground does not answer a door, and that is not a thing I can fix with a table and a week. Write it in your own book if you want to. I am not going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who puts tables up is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a house and is not a bearer of a list, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to ask about a house again has not refused the house and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking again says not asked, and that the ninth of the eleven houses the woman of fifty-eight carries water to is a house in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off this district, is not on any sheet in this district, and that no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved on the sixth of the seventh month.

The man of fifty-six said one thing back and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record about the not counting says not asked.

"He has just told me the thing about a bell that I have been trying to say to this yard for two weeks and he does not know it, and he has said it to me on the top of a bank in about four seconds. A house in the ground does not answer a door. A table is a door with a roof on it. And I am not going to be the man who says the rest of it to him at half past four in the afternoon in the seventh month."

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has said out loud that a table is a door with a roof on it has not made a finding and cannot be counted either way, and that a bell does not knock and a table does not knock either, and that the record about the not saying the rest of it says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered what was still standing, which were the things that had not moved, in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph. She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the ninety-seven days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood seventy-four days and nobody has sat in it on any of the seventy-four, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the seventy-six days since the twenty-first of the fourth month and that she did not ask him and that a man of about forty-eight who says of himself that he is fifty-one is not going to be asked a second time, and that the boards in this district are five and the lines that are full are six and a seventh is ruled and empty at the ninth place, and that the protected things are five and that no sixth was added today and that an address is not a sixth and a lean-to is not a sixth and a sheet of print is not a sixth, and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight and did not move today, and that the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month and is a hundred and four days back this morning, and that the rival record has now been in force thirty-five days and has been read out seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of last month and it is unanswered.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was six days old and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-six days off, and a woman of fifty-eight carried two buckets back up the bank and was not asked anything else by anybody in this district that day.

**AND ON THE SIXTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN ASKED THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN OUT LOUD WHETHER ANYBODY IN THIS DISTRICT CARRIES WATER TO A HOUSE IN THE GROUND, AND FOUR PEOPLE LOOKED ROUND AT A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT, AND SHE ANSWERED IT HERSELF IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS, AND SAID THAT THE WOMAN IN THAT HOUSE IS NOT ON ANY SHEET AND IS NOT ON THE LIST OF THE PEOPLE WHO ARE TO BE MOVED, AND NO NUMBER ON THAT SHEET MOVED.**
