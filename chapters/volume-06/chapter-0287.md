# Chapter 287: The First Morning

---

The seventh of the seventh month was bright and already hot at nine, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen and one of them had not slept.

"**A hundred and ninety-five days on the board and five hundred and eleven days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and twenty-five, and a hundred and eighty-six days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-five days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is seventy-eight days back, and the first day of the eighth month is the day on that sheet at that gatepost and it is twenty-five days off, and there is a table against a wall about eleven miles off this morning that was not there yesterday.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that twenty-five is twenty-four days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a day a man is not in this district is not a day he has been here.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came through the gate at about half past ten in the morning and said one thing about what it had cost him, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and eighty-seven, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about and that he said it before anybody asked him.

"**I took his hand-cart and his table and his tent out to that wall on the fifth of this month and I brought it back yesterday afternoon, and that is two days of my cart, and I had a load of stone in on the sixth that I did not take, and the stone was paid for and the man who paid for it has it by another road and I do not know whether that is going to cost me the road after next. I have not been paid for the two days and he did not offer and I did not ask, because when I saw that wall I understood there is not going to be anything to be paid for, there is going to be a table on it. I would like it entered that I am a man who drives a cart and that I am not one of the things I am talking about, and that I drove eleven miles twice for nothing and that nobody in this district has asked me to and that I am not going to be asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who drives a cart is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a table and is not a keeper of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he hauled a thing for nothing has not been given a thing and has not refused one, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a day a man is not in this district is not a day he has been here and that he was not in this district on the fifth and the sixth of this month and that those are two days off this district's figure and not two days on it.

Then the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat, at the third house up that lane from the scale, said one thing to about four people at the foot of her own gate, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record came from the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and not from her, and that she said it before anybody asked her and that the record about her not being asked says not asked.

"**My gate has not been off its post since I was a girl and I have never once seen it work and I have never once needed it to. That man has got a gate in a wall four feet high on the far side of eleven miles of road and it is not on a hinge, and I want it said out loud in a yard that a gate that is not on a hinge is a gate somebody can be counted coming through, and I am not going to say that twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a goat is not a keeper of a gate and is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of anything, and that a person who has said a thing out loud once and refused to say it twice has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way.

Then the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said one thing about the road, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and eighty-four, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about.

"**I have kept four miles of that road eleven years and I walked out to that wall on the fifth of this month behind his cart and I walked back on the sixth, and a road does not go where a sheet says it goes. There is a turning off the eleven mile road about a mile before that wall and it is not on the sheet and there is nothing on it saying a table is down it, and on the fifth there was one cart on it going out and nothing coming back, and on the sixth there was nothing going out at all. I am not saying that is a finding. I am saying that a man who puts a table up eleven miles off and does not put a mark on the turning has made a thing that can be found by the man who made it and by nobody else, and a hand-cart has gone out of this district once this month and come back once and not one of those two goes had a turning marked on it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and has never been asked to be one, and that a turning that is not on a sheet is a turning and not an address, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about eleven in the morning on the seventh of the seventh month.

Then the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them said the figure about the first morning, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it in about four seconds and that nobody in that yard was going to be able to do anything with it.

"**I set that table up against the wall at about eight o'clock this morning and I was there until ten and there was one person standing at it, and I do not know who it was and I did not ask and I am not going to describe him or her to this yard, because if I describe one person on the first morning then this district has got a figure about a person and the whole of what I came down eleven miles of road for is that it does not. One person. At eight in the morning on the first morning. I am not going to say one more thing about it today and I would like this yard not to make a finding out of one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who stood at a table on the morning it was first set up has not agreed to anything and has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about that person says not asked, and that the man of about thirty-seven asked for it not to be described and was not asked to ask.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing read the four lines of the printed sheet out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nine feet of lane outside the office, to about four people, and the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was standing there and had not asked him to.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about twenty-three had been asked for the four lines by nobody, that the woman who keeps the books in the room four hundred yards from that gate was four feet away with her hand on the door and did not stop him, and that the record about her not stopping him says not asked, and that a clerk of nineteen years was four hundred yards off in a yard and did not hear it until about seven.

"**I cannot copy these. I could not copy the five lines on the second of this month either and I got two of them and that is all I have got. My hand has not been right since the thirteenth of last month and it is not going to be right this month, and I have been the man in this district who copies things since the second month of the year before last, and there is nobody else in these nine settlements who will do it, and I have been saying that to nobody. I am reading them out because a man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart read five lines back in that yard yesterday and got nothing wrong, and he reads them for the yard, and I cannot write them for anybody, and a thing that is only ever read by one man in a yard is a thing that stops when that man is ill.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a document and is not a keeper of anything, and that a man who reads a thing aloud is not a copy of it and is not a second copy of it and is not a document, and that a document that is only read and never written is not one of the three documents this district does not own and is not a fourth of them, and that a voice is not a figure about a man and is not a bearer and is not a price of anything, and that the record about his not being asked says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven said one thing back to him and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record about the not counting says not asked.

"Then I will come to you every morning of a week and you will read them out in that lane, and there will be about four people here by the second week and they will hear it from you and not from me, and that is the only version of this thing I have got that does not stop when I stop. I am not going to say thank you for it. You have not done it for me and I have not asked you and I would rather it stayed that way, and I would like it entered that a man of about twenty-three reads four lines out loud in a lane on the seventh of the seventh month because his hand has gone and not because anybody sent for him."

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to thank a man for a thing has not refused the thing and has not consented to it and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not saying thank you says not asked, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and that a man reading four lines aloud in a lane is not a seventh of them and did not become one, and that the six did not move on the seventh of the seventh month.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of the things that had not moved, in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph. She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the ninety-eight days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood seventy-five days and nobody has sat in it on any of the seventy-five, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the seventy-seven days since the twenty-first of the fourth month and that she did not ask him, and that the refusals of eleven coppers a week are nine and the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight and neither moved today, and that the rival record has been in force thirty-six days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud on the twenty-fourth of last month with a reason and it is unanswered.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was seven days old and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-five days off, and about four hundred and eleven people in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off were to be moved on or before it, and one person had stood at a table against a wall at about eight o'clock that morning and had not been asked anything and was not going to be.

**AND ON THE SEVENTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE WHO DRIVES A CART SAID OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN THAT HE HAULED A HAND-CART AND A TABLE ELEVEN MILES TWICE FOR NOTHING AND LOST A LOAD OF STONE HE HAD BEEN PAID FOR, AND A TABLE WENT UP AGAINST A WALL FOUR FEET HIGH ON THE FAR SIDE OF THE ELEVEN MILE ROAD, AND ONE PERSON STOOD AT IT AT ABOUT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND WAS NOT DESCRIBED TO THAT YARD BY ANYBODY.**
