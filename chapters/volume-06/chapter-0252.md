# Chapter 252: The Ones Who Answered

---

The second of the sixth month was grey and still and about eleven people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there forty days and nobody had sat in it on any of the forty.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and sixty days on the board and four hundred and seventy-six days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and ninety, and a hundred and fifty-one days separate the second of January and this morning, and the sheet that was on that gatepost yesterday is in a coat about nine feet from where I am standing and it has not been read out a second time and it is not going to be.**"

Then he said the other half of the morning, which was why about eleven people were in that yard, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fourteen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**We are going to work out where four hundred and eleven came from, and we are going to do it in the open and in daylight and with about eleven people watching, and I am not going to knock on one door to find out. If somebody in the shadow of that thing wants to tell this yard how the number was got, they can come down this lane on their own two feet, and if they do not, then this yard's figure about them is nothing, and not nothing is a thing you write down and it is not a number and it is not a consent and it is not a refusal.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the record about the four hundred yards of lane and about the two hundred behind the bank says not asked, and that nobody has been sent for, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam did the sum out loud about half past ten and nobody had asked him to and the count came to a hundred and seventeen.

"**You cannot get four hundred and eleven by knocking. You can get four hundred and eleven by knocking at a great many doors and finding out how many of them open, and that number would be small, and if the number that comes back is large then the people doing the knocking have been asking somebody how many people are behind the doors that did not open. Which means somebody opened. Which means somebody in the shadow of that Hall answered a question about their own neighbours, and either they were asked nicely and they answered, or they were not asked and they answered anyway, and the sheet does not say which and does not say who.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the second line of a printed sheet is a finding about a method and not about a person, and that a person who answered a question about their own neighbours has not thereby been entered anywhere and is not on any page in this district and is not a figure about this yard, and that the record about whoever answered says not asked.

Then a man of about nineteen who counts, who is in that yard every morning and is nobody's clerk, said the thing about what he had done with the whole morning and it was counted and came to a hundred and forty.

"**I counted four things yesterday and I am going to say what they were and then I am going to stop. I counted nineteen people in this yard at ten. I counted the nail in the gatepost, which is one. I counted the buckets the woman of fifty-eight put down at the foot of the bank at about four, which is two. And I counted the words on that sheet, which are three lines. That is everything I can see from here and none of it is four hundred and eleven, and I did not go and look for the rest, and I am not going to, because looking for the rest means walking into the ground in the shadow of that thing and asking people how many of them there are, and that is the knock again, only slower.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about nineteen who counts has declined to count a thing for the second time in two months and that the first was on a shoulder in the cold in the last month when there was nothing to count, and that this time there was a number in front of him and he still did not count it, and that a man who declines to count the number on a sheet has not refused the sheet and is not one of the eight, and that a man who keeps a count is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move at about eleven in the morning on the second of the sixth month.

A man of fifty-six said that a man of about nineteen who will count a nail and a pair of buckets and a line of print and will not count a population is the only instrument in this yard that has not been made into a figure about somebody by the fact that it is useful, and that he was not going to say anything else about it, and a clerk entered that he said that and that the record about his not saying anything else says not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam went down to the sluice end of the loams at about half past two in the afternoon, because a man of fifty-six had said in that yard in the morning that a plank in a notch does not keep nights and that nobody had been to look at the sill since the twenty-second of last month, and nobody sent for him and a clerk entered that he went on his own two feet and that the record about the going says not asked.

The water was about a foot above the sill again and the frame was holding it the way it holds it, and he got down the bank on the stones and into about a foot and a half of it and put his right hand flat on the sill under the water and moved it about nine inches along the stone and then nine inches back.

He was in that water for about two minutes and came out and could not close his right hand for the rest of the afternoon, and he said nothing about it, and at about half past five he could not get it closed on the handle of a pail, and the woman of fifty-eight took the pail off him and did not ask him anything about it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him about it and that the record about the two minutes and the pail says not asked.

A clerk entered that the sill is about two feet wide with one notch cut in it and that a hand on a sill is not a hand on a lock and that nobody in this district is holding anything, and that the man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in the water next to a thing that does not need him and that is the same sentence as the one entered on the twenty-second of last month and that a clerk is not going to enter the same sentence a second time without saying that she is entering it again.

The man of about fifty-five who keeps a building four miles down a lane sent nothing to that yard on that day and nobody sent anything to him, and a clerk entered that, and entered that there are two chalk lines on the inside of a brick tank with the water about a hand's breadth over the lip, and that a crack about the length of his hand is not a sixth protected thing, and that the nine years of beds are still in the second room and the water has not come over the step and that nobody has asked for a fifth mend in four months and counting.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came through that yard at about four on his way to somewhere else and stopped, and a clerk entered that nobody sent for him and that he was not asked a first thing and that the record about the stopping says not asked, and he said one thing and it was counted and came to a hundred and ten.

"**I have been asked one question in this district since the first of the month that is now last month and I have given one answer, and I am not going to be asked a second one this morning by anybody. I have got four miles of road in front of me and one thing to do at the end of it and neither of those is a sheet, and if this yard wants to know how many doors there are in the ground in the shadow of that Hall then it can come and walk them with me, and I will not write them down and neither will you.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and has never been asked to be one, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a man who keeps a road is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move at about four in the afternoon on the second of the sixth month.

Then the registrar of this district's records office, who has kept the book in that room for nine years and who wrote a rule about it on the twenty-first of the month before last, said the part about the word last month, from about four feet inside the gate, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and twenty-eight, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**Yesterday morning the words last month and the month before last in this district meant the fourth month and the third month, and since six yesterday morning they have meant the fifth and the fourth, and the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is the last day a person in this basin gave a refusal with no reason and no clerk of a house in it, is two months back and sixty-nine days. I have written the change into the standing page in my own hand and not into a book anybody may walk up to, and a clerk of nineteen years has entered that the usage of this district turned with the month, and that is a fact about words and is not a finding about anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a rule about what a phrase means is not a document and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own and did not become a fourth of them, and that a phrase turning with a calendar is not a consent and is not a refusal and is not a column, and that the four columns of not-askings did not move, and that the man of about thirty at a counter is at the sixteenth time down the eleven mile road and the next is the seventeenth and that this district has never once given that man a day to be here on and did not give him one this morning.

The day came down about half past eight, which was late for the sixth month, and the trough at the foot of the bank was still about four inches over its lip and had been since the second week of last month, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did not try the gate again, and a clerk entered that he did not and that nobody asked him not to.

**AND ON THE SECOND OF THE SIXTH MONTH ABOUT ELEVEN PEOPLE IN A YARD A STRANGER CAN WALK INTO SPENT THE DAY WORKING OUT THAT FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN CANNOT BE ARRIVED AT BY KNOCKING AND MUST HAVE BEEN TOLD TO SOMEBODY, AND A MAN WENT DOWN INTO A FOOT AND A HALF OF WATER AT THE SLUICE END OF THE LOAMS AND PUT HIS HAND ON A SILL AND COULD NOT CLOSE IT FOR THE REST OF THE AFTERNOON, AND A REGISTRAR WROTE DOWN THAT THE WORD LAST MONTH MEANT SOMETHING DIFFERENT THIS MORNING THAN IT DID YESTERDAY MORNING AND CALLED IT A FACT ABOUT WORDS AND NOT A FINDING.**
