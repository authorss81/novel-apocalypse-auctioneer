# Chapter 282: Five Lines

---

The second of the seventh month came in hot off the ground and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table in the yard of Lot Seventeen before that yard was full, and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right, and about nineteen people were standing about in the heat.

"**A hundred and ninety days on the board and five hundred and six days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and twenty, and a hundred and eighty-one days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is thirty days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is seventy-three days back, and a man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them came through that gate at half past ten yesterday morning with a hand-cart and a tent rolled on it and is standing at the end of this table with five lines on a sheet.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who puts tables up is not a keeper of a table and is not a bearer of a sheet and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that the record about his not being asked says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years asked for the sheet to be read, because a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and the man of about thirty-seven asked for it in the ordinary voice at about half past ten in the morning and nobody asked on his behalf, and a clerk entered that he asked for it and that the record about the asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read it out in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them.

> **A TABLE UNDER A LEAN-TO ON THE FAR SIDE OF THE ELEVEN MILE ROAD WILL BE OPEN ON ANY MORNING OF A WEEK TO ANY PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT WHO IS NOT ENTERED IN ANY HOUSEHOLD.**
>
> **ELEVEN COPPERS A WEEK IS PAID TO ANY PERSON WHO STANDS AT THAT TABLE ON ANY MORNING OF A WEEK AND IS COUNTED AT IT.**
>
> **A PERSON WHO STANDS AT THAT TABLE IS NOT ASKED WHAT THEY ARE CALLED AND IS NOT ENTERED IN ANY BOOK IN THIS DISTRICT.**
>
> **THE ELEVEN COPPERS COME OUT OF WHAT THE NINE HOUSES IN ONE LANE IN THIS DISTRICT PAY, AND NINE HOUSES HAVE PAID FOR FIVE MONTHS.**
>
> **A PERSON WHO HAS STOOD AT THAT TABLE ONCE CANNOT BE UNCOUNTED AGAIN.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet names no person and no body and no place, and that an offer is a document and is not a lot and is not a charter, and that neither a lot nor a charter nor an offer creates a consent or makes a person a bearer or makes a house into a party or turns a right to give something up into a refusal, and that **the fourth line of any offer that has been on a table in this district for five months has not been read out in this yard and was not read out this morning, and this sheet has five lines and is not that offer, and the refusals to read are nine and did not move and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading.**

Then the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart, who is not the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room with nothing over the door and is not the man of about nineteen who counts, read it back, and he got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was asked to read it back and was not asked whether he agreed with it and that reading a thing back is not agreeing to it.

Not one of the about nineteen people in that yard said a word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that none of them did and that the record about the nineteen saying nothing says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing about the fifth of those five lines, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and fifty-two, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard was able to answer him.

"**That fifth line is not true. It cannot be made true either, and I am not going to stand in this yard and pretend there is a version of a table you can be uncounted from. I have been in this basin fifty months building documents and the only thing I have ever wanted out of one is that it says what it does not know, and this sheet knows. It says it knows. A person who has stood at that table once cannot be uncounted again, and it is on the face of the sheet, and it is the only line on it that is about the person instead of about the paying, and whoever wrote it meant it. A count of the people who come to a place is a count of the people who come. That is not a flaw in that line. That is what a count is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house, that a man who has said out loud that a line of a document may not be true has not refused the document and is not a party of it, and that the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about eleven in the morning on the second of the seventh month.

And the man of about thirty-seven agreed with him out loud, in the same yard, on the same morning, in about four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he agreed and that he said why.

"**I wrote that line on purpose and I would like it read out again this morning so that everybody in this yard has heard it twice. If I had left it off, then in five months a man with a book would come and say nobody told me, and he would be right, and you would be right, and I would be the man who hid it in a hole. You can be uncounted out of a room. You cannot be uncounted out of a morning you were standing in the open in. Put my name on it. I have not got a name for this district and I am not going to be asked for one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says out loud that he wrote a line on purpose has not thereby become a party of the document and has not acquired a standing in it, and that a person who has not been asked for a name has not refused to give it and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the three documents this district does not own are three and that a sheet brought into a yard on a hand-cart and read out in front of about nineteen people is a document this district has seen the whole of and is not a fourth of them.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing about the fourth of those five lines, and it was counted and came to a hundred and fifty-six, and a clerk entered that he said it in about four seconds and that nobody in that yard was going to be able to put it anywhere today.

"**Nine houses in one lane in this district have paid for five months and not one of those nine houses has ever been asked by anybody what the five months was for, and I do not know yet and I am not going to say in this yard that I know. What I am going to say is that a pool of nine houses is the fifth of the five things this district does not have put in a shape that fits it exactly, and that this district has been offered this before by a man with a case and it was fitted to the same hole, and that man is not in this district and has not been since the nineteenth of last month, and I would like whoever is keeping count of shapes in this basin to enter that this one came up the eleven mile road on its own and nobody sent it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says a thing arrived on its own has not thereby acquired a holder of it, and that nobody in that yard sent for the man of about thirty-seven and that the record about the not sending says not asked, and that a shape that is fitted to a hole is not the same shape as the thing that made the hole, and that the two are not joined.

---

At about one in the afternoon the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came into that yard and said one thing to the man of about thirty-seven and not to the yard, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record came from the man of about thirty-seven and not from him.

"**The second of your five lines is the only line on that sheet that is doing anything. The rest of it is furniture. A table is a table, a week is a week, nine houses paying for five months is nine houses paying for five months. But counted at it. That is the one that is a hand on somebody's shoulder. I have got a hand that has not closed since the eleventh of last month and I know exactly what a count at a table feels like from the other side of it, because I have been the one doing it. I did it to about nine people coming through one door out of about two hundred yards of lane at two in the morning on the night of the twenty-fourth of last month, and I did not know a single one of their names, and I put a piece of paper in my coat and went home. You have written that down on a sheet in daylight where a stranger can walk up and read it, and you are right to, and I would like somebody in this yard to say out loud that I have not got an argument against the second of those five lines and that I have got one against the fifth.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who digs loam is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a count and is not a keeper of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he has not got an argument against a line of a document has not consented to that document and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not arguing says not asked, and that the second and the fifth of those five lines are two lines and the third of them is that a person who stands at that table is not asked what they are called, and that three lines are three lines and are not added together.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of the things that had not moved on the second of the seventh month, in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph. She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the ninety-three days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood seventy days and nobody has sat in it on any of the seventy, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the seventy-two days since the twenty-first of the fourth month and that she did not ask him, and that the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is eight and that it did not move, and that the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and that the seven and the eight are not the same figure and are not added together, and that the rival record has now been in force thirty-one days and has been read out in that yard seven times and that the eighth reading was refused out loud on the twenty-fourth of last month with a reason and a refused reading is not a reading and nobody has answered that record.

At about half past four in the afternoon the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat, at the third house up that lane from the scale, carried a copy of those five lines up two miles to a room about nine feet by fourteen with nothing over the door, so that about nine people in there could hear what had been said in the yard, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked her to and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room did not send for her and is not a person who is sent for.

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was two days old and the first day of the eighth month was thirty days off, and four hundred and eleven people in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off this district were to be moved on or before it, and no number on that sheet moved this month.

**AND ON THE SECOND OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A SHEET WITH FIVE LINES ON IT WAS READ OUT IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN BY A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING AND READ BACK BY A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE WHO DRIVES A CART AND GOT NOTHING WRONG, AND THE FIFTH OF THE FIVE LINES SAID THAT A PERSON WHO HAS STOOD AT THAT TABLE ONCE CANNOT BE UNCOUNTED AGAIN, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN SAID OUT LOUD IN THE SAME YARD THAT HE WROTE THAT LINE ON PURPOSE.**
