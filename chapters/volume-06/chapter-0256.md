# Chapter 256: A Second Book

---

The sixth of the sixth month was warm and low and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there forty-four days and nobody had sat in it on any of the forty-four.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and sixty-four days on the board and four hundred and eighty days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and ninety-four, and a hundred and fifty-five days separate the second of January and this morning, and there is a second book in a room four hundred yards from where I am standing and I have not been in the room and have not been asked to be.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a second book is a book and that a book is a book and that the two are not the same thing, and that a rule about books is not a document and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own and did not become a fourth of them.

---

The room where the books are is about nine feet by eleven with one table and one shelf and one door, and the fanlight over that door does not light anything, as it has never once lit anything in four months, and nobody has ever asked the registrar of this district's records office about it and she is not going to be asked about it here.

The first book is on the table and it has been on the table for nine years, and it is a bound ledger about two inches thick with a strap over it, and it sits at the left-hand end of a table about four feet long. The second book is a bound ledger of the same make and it is about three-quarters of an inch thick, and it is on the shelf, on its side, with a second chair turned up on top of it so that nobody coming in at a hurry can knock it off.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a second book that has to have a chair turned over it to stay on the shelf is a book that its keeper expects somebody to want, and that a person who expects somebody to want a book has not been asked what the wanting is for, and that the record about the chair says not asked.

She has kept the book in that room for nine years. She wrote the rule about it on the twenty-first of the month before last and it has not been rewritten since, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the rule is in her own hand and is on a page in that room and is not in any book a stranger may walk up to, and that a rule in a room is not a public instrument and was never one.

A man of fifty-six said in the yard on the second of this month that a rule which is in one room and is in one hand cannot be looked at by anybody, and that a rule which cannot be looked at by anybody cannot be argued with by anybody, and that he had been saying that for a month and that a month was nine days in the yard and nobody had done anything about it, and a clerk entered that he said it and that nobody answered him and that the record about his not being answered says not asked.

The registrar came down to that yard at about half past two in the afternoon and said what she had done, in the plain voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and twenty-three, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I have opened a second book. It is in the same room and it is on the same table and it is in my hand and nobody else has looked in it and nobody else will look in it, and I have done the thing I told this yard on the sixteenth of last month I would not do, which is put my room behind somebody's arrangement. I am not going to stand here and be praised for it and I am not going to be told I have broken a rule, because I have broken it, and I wrote it, and I would like it written down in one line that I knew what I was doing at about five this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who has said out loud that she has put her own room behind an arrangement has not thereby become a bearer of it and is not a holder and is not a steward and is not a party of anything, and that the record about what she knew at five in the morning says not asked, and that a second book is not a page and is not a column and is not a figure about anybody.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came to the door of that room at about three in the afternoon and stood in it and did not go in, and a clerk of nineteen years was four hundred yards away and entered the whole of it afterwards, from one of the two of them.

He asked her for the number of names in it, and she said no in about four seconds, and gave a reason, and there was nobody there to count it and a clerk entered that the sentence was entered in the hand of the man who asked and that a sentence entered in one person's hand is a conversation and not a record, and that the conversation was not in a book.

"**No. And it is my own rule and not yours and not his, and I wrote it on the twenty-first of the month before last, and it says a book is kept in one room by one person and that person is the only one who may look in it. You are not that person. You have been in nine doorways in nine days and every one of them was somebody else's rule about somebody else's room, and I have watched you learn that in about four minutes each time, and there is nothing in that which makes you the person who may look in this one.**"

Then she said the other half of it, and it was entered in the same hand, and it was about nine seconds and it was not counted.

"**And I want to say the second thing in this doorway and not in a room, because a room is where a sentence goes and this one is not going in a room. At the end of last month I would have given you that number in about four seconds and you would have written it on the back of a sheet and taken it down a lane and said it in a yard, and four days ago a woman stood at a gate and told you that a number is the only thing anybody has ever come for, and you wrote that down, and I have read what you wrote down, and I am not going to be the second person in your book this week who hands you a number and calls it help.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who refuses a man a figure in her own doorway has not refused the man and is not one of the eight and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight and did not move at about three in the afternoon on the sixth of the sixth month, and that a registrar is not a clerk of a house and that a figure a person keeps to herself is not a column and is not a rate and is not one of the five things this district does not have.

Nobody in the yard knew what had been said in that doorway and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody knew and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to, and that a man who is not going to be told what is in a room is not a man who has been refused a room and can go away and come back.

---

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about four with two buckets and put them down where she puts them and nobody counted them, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing carried one up and said nothing about it, and a clerk entered that a count of buckets is not a count of houses and is not a figure about a woman and that the record about the two buckets says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam, whose right hand was still not closing properly on a cold handle and who had not mentioned it since the second of this month, said the half of the day that nobody had wanted to say, and it was counted and came to a hundred and thirty.

"**A first book in one room by one person is the reason a notice four hundred miles away could be written without anybody's name on it. A second book in one room by one person is the reason four hundred and eleven people on a piece of paper do not have to be asked anything this month. Both of those are true and they cannot both be a rule. One of them is a rule about what a record is for and one of them is a rule about what a person is worth, and this district has spent five months refusing to say which is which, and somebody in that room has just said it out loud in about nine seconds and did not have to be asked twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said a thing in about nine seconds has not thereby agreed with the person who said it, and that a rule about what a person is worth is not a rate and there is no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and there never has been one, and that the record about the second book says not asked and that the record about who knew at five in the morning says not asked.

A man of fifty-six said that the man who digs loam had now said the same shape of true sentence about a record for the second time in two days, and that a shape noticed a second time is not a finding, and that he was going to leave it there and was not going to say what happens the third time because a clerk who writes down what happens the third time has entered a habit and not a finding.

The man of about nineteen who counts said one thing about the second book and it was counted and came to a hundred and twenty-one.

"**I am not going to try to find out what is in it and I am not going to try to find out how many names are in it, and I want that entered as a thing I said and not as a thing I did. There are nine people in this yard and I have counted all nine this morning and I could count the same nine again at about eleven this evening and they would be nine, and that is the whole of what I know about this yard, and I would rather say that out loud in a yard than be a man in a book somewhere with a number in him that I did not put there.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about nineteen who counts nine people in a yard is not a clerk of a house and is not one of the eight and that the eight did not move, and that a man who declines to count a second thing this block is not declining twice for a reason and that a clerk is not going to make a rule of it.

The day came down about half past six and the sixth month was six days old, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the sixty-seven days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of the trestle table was still standing there, and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against had not gone into a room four hundred yards away.

**AND ON THE SIXTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH THE REGISTRAR OF THIS DISTRICT'S RECORDS OFFICE OPENED A SECOND BOOK IN THE ROOM WHERE THE BOOKS ARE AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT SHE HAD PUT HER OWN ROOM BEHIND SOMEBODY'S ARRANGEMENT, AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST ASKED HER AT HER OWN DOOR FOR THE NUMBER OF NAMES IN IT AND SHE SAID NO IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS ON HER OWN RULE OF THE TWENTY-FIRST OF THE MONTH BEFORE LAST, AND THE RECORD OF THAT DOORWAY IS IN ONE PERSON'S HAND AND IS NOT IN A BOOK.**
