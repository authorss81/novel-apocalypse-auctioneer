# Chapter 269: A Bucket And A Step

---

The nineteenth of the sixth month was clear and still and about six people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there fifty-seven days and nobody had sat in it on any of the fifty-seven.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and seventy-seven days on the board and four hundred and ninety-three days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and seven, and a hundred and sixty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning, and that is twenty-four weeks and no days, counted off the second of January and not off a page, and the first day of the month after next is forty-three days off counted off this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a hundred and sixty-eight days is twenty-four weeks and no days and that it is a round figure and that a round figure is a figure about a calculation and is not a figure about anybody, and that a clerk who prints a round number because it is round has not found anything.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the sixty days since the twenty-first of the month before last, being one signed, two refused and three not asked again, and that he has not said that figure since the seventh of this month and that a clerk did not ask him for it this morning, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about eleven the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came through that gate and said one thing, and it was counted and came to a hundred and fifty-five, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard argued with him.

"**On the fifteenth of this month I walked my four miles and told the nine houses on it that there was a man with a case at this yard buying lists of people who are not in a household, and not one of the nine asked me for it, and I wrote that down as nine houses saying nothing, and I want it taken back off the page this morning. One of the nine went and found him. He told me about it in about four seconds at the second gate and he was not ashamed of it and he was right not to be, because he has a woman in that house who is not in a household and eleven coppers a week is eleven coppers a week, and I stood in his yard on the fifteenth and told him about a list without ever once asking him whether his house was on one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and has never been asked to be one, and that a man who has walked four miles telling nine houses a thing and has been told by one of them that it was the wrong thing to tell has not made a finding and has not entered a habit, and that the record about the second gate says not asked, and that a house is not a party of anything and is not a bearer and is not a figure about a person.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart, who had been hauling for a man with a case since the thirteenth of this month and had not been asked to, sent nine sacks down to the scale at about eight that morning and did not take any money for them, and that a man who hauls a thing for somebody else all day carries it and is not a bearer of it and is not one this morning either, and that the record about the nine sacks says not asked.

At about two in the afternoon the man of about forty-four with a case put the case under his arm and left the slate on the end of the trestle table with nothing on it, and said that he was going back down eleven miles and that he would not be at this table again, and nobody in that yard said anything about it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he came through that gate on the thirteenth of this month and went out of it at about two this afternoon, and that a stay counted without the day of arrival is five days, being the fourteenth to the eighteenth, and that nobody counted him either way and that the record about the five says not asked, and that a man who is not in this district is not a day he has been here, and that this district has given that man seven days in ten and has not asked him one question that was not about what he was selling.

Then a clerk entered the other half of the morning, which is that a man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was in that yard at about half past three, and that he has been asked for eleven coppers a week in this district eight times in five months and has been refused eight times, and that a man with a case said yes to it on the thirteenth of this month in about one second, and that the arrangement was finished on the eighteenth, and that the eight did not move.

---

At about six in the evening the woman of fifty-eight came down the bank with two buckets and put them down where she puts them, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing carried one of them up, and then he asked her the same question for the fifth time, in his own words and not in the six things, which was whether the ninth of the eleven houses she carries water to is one of the four hundred and eleven on the sheet at the gatepost.

She put the second bucket down on the step and sat down on the step of two stones, which is a step outside the second house up that lane and which nobody in this district has ever seen her sit on, and she said the first of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said it in thirteen words and looked at him while she said it.

> **I SAID IT BECAUSE YOU ASKED ME FIVE TIMES AND I AM FIFTY-EIGHT**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who carries water to eleven houses is not a clerk of a house, and that a person who has been asked a question five times and has answered it on the fifth has not refused anything before the fifth, and that the record about the four times she did not answer says not asked, and that this is the first question anybody in this district has asked that woman that anybody in this district has had an answer to.

Then she said the rest of it, and it was counted and came to a hundred and eighty, and a clerk entered that nobody counted the buckets and that nobody said anything while she said it.

"**No. The woman in the ninth of those houses is not one of the four hundred and eleven, and I have known that since the fourth of this month and I have not said it to anybody, and you are going to go down that lane tonight and tell people I said it, and I cannot stop you and I would not stop you if I could. In the second week of last month she stopped me at that gate and asked me whether she could have a second bucket, and I said I would see, and there was a second bucket on the third of last month. A person who asks for a second bucket is a person who has answered a question. That is what your four hundred and eleven is made of, and it is not a lie and it is not a mistake, and I have carried water to that house for nineteen years and I would not want her on a list because of a bucket, and I am telling you the same thing twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who has said out loud that a thing she feared is true has not thereby agreed to the thing and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the second bucket says not asked, and that the ninth of the eleven houses the woman of fifty-eight carries to is the one the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale named out loud in a yard on the third of this month without being asked, and that a thing said by a person who was not asked on the third of this month and by a person who was asked on the nineteenth are two different kinds of thing and are not added together, and that no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved this evening.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing that had been in him since about half past six, and it was counted and came to a hundred and thirty-one, and a clerk entered that the man of about fifty-six was in the lane and heard it and said nothing.

"**I have asked her five times and I told a clerk on the fourth of this month that I was going to go on asking and I did not say it out loud, and I have not wanted it since the fourth, and I want that entered in one line because I am about to go down that lane and say it to about nine people and I would rather it were on the page that I did not want it. I did not want it on the day after forty names were said in a yard. I have wanted it every morning since and I have not known what to do about that, and now I know what to do about it and it is a bucket on a step.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has got the answer he has been asking for on the worst day there was for it is not relieved, and that the record about what he did with it afterwards says not asked, and that a man who asks questions in this district and carries a bucket up a bank afterwards is not a keeper of a bucket and is not a bearer of anything.

Then a man of fifty-six said one thing, and it was counted and came to a hundred and five, and a clerk entered that the woman of fifty-eight did not say anything back to it and that she was not asked to.

"**Four hundred and eleven is a count of people who were asked a question and answered it, and every one of those four hundred and eleven wanted something when they answered. That is not a discovery and I am not going to make one out of it in a lane. It is a thing I have been wrong about for six weeks and I did not know I was wrong about it, and the woman sitting on that step is the reason I know now, and I have been in this basin fifty-six years and I have never once asked what a person was asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has said out loud that he was wrong about something for six weeks has not withdrawn anything anybody else is holding, and that the record about the six weeks says not asked.

The day came down about half past eight and the sixth month was nineteen days old. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing carried the second bucket up the bank and put it where she puts it, and nobody counted the buckets, and a clerk entered that nobody counted them, and that a count of buckets is not a count of houses and is not a figure about a woman of fifty-eight who has been asked one question in five months and answered it on the fifth time.

**AND ON THE NINETEENTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH THE WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT SAT DOWN ON A STEP OUTSIDE THE SECOND HOUSE UP THAT LANE, WHICH NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAD EVER SEEN HER DO, AND SAID THAT THE WOMAN IN THE NINTH OF THE ELEVEN HOUSES SHE CARRIES TO IS NOT ONE OF THE FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN, AND THAT THE WOMAN ASKED FOR A SECOND BUCKET IN THE SECOND WEEK OF LAST MONTH, AND THAT A PERSON WHO ASKS FOR A SECOND BUCKET IS A PERSON WHO HAS ANSWERED A QUESTION.**
