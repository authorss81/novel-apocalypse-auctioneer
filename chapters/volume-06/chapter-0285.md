# Chapter 285: Two Sides, One Address

---

The fifth of the seventh month was hot before nine and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and nobody in that yard had said the word address.

"**A hundred and ninety-three days on the board and five hundred and nine days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and twenty-three, and a hundred and eighty-four days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-seven days off counted off this morning, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and three days, and nobody in this yard has been asked to move that figure and it did not move anyway.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that twenty-seven is twenty-six days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a figure nobody has been asked for is a figure and is not a finding.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the answer to the question the whole of that yard had been asking since the first of this month, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and eighty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A market that is the address of both sides cannot be the address of either side, and I am going to say that out loud in this yard today and then I am going to spend the rest of the month finding out what there is instead, because I have been in five months of this basin telling people what a door is for and this is the first morning I have not been able to tell this yard where to put one. A man comes down eleven miles of road with a table in a hand-cart and wants a place to say where it is, and the reason he wants it is that the people he wants to pay cannot be found at a door. A body four hundred miles off prints four lines and says the place an offer is made in goes in the record of that place, and the reason it wants that is that it has four hundred and eleven people to move on or before the first of the eighth month and it has counted them by knocking on doors. Those two things are pulling at the same gate from opposite ends. If this yard says the word address out loud, then the people this district cannot count are the easiest people in the basin to find, and if this yard does not say it, then the man of about thirty-seven has got no way to be found by anybody who needs him and he is eleven miles off with a tent. I have got no third thing. I have got two things and a gate and I am not going to stand in this yard and pretend I have found a third.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who convenes nine settlements is not a representative of a settlement that has not asked to be represented, and that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he has got no third thing has not refused either of the two and cannot be counted either way about either of them, and that the record about the two being pulled says not asked.

A man of fifty-six said one thing back, and it was counted and came to two hundred and twenty-three, and a clerk entered that he said it in the ordinary voice and that nobody in that yard was going to be able to answer it.

"**I have stood in this yard for five months and watched it become the place where things are said out loud, and it got there because people brought sheets here and asked for them to be read, and every time one of those has happened somebody has been asked a question. A man asked for five lines to be read on the second. A man asked for four lines to be read on the fourth and somebody stopped him. A man stood in a doorway in a lane behind a bank on the night of the twenty-fourth of last month because this yard had asked him to stand in it, and about nine people came through a front door two hundred yards up that lane and a man of about nineteen counted them and would not give the figure to a clerk. A market is a place where a person is asked a question. The only questions anybody has asked in that yard in ten days have been about what that yard is. It is the best room in this district and it is not a market and it is not going to become one by being wanted, and the two people pulling at that gate are not the problem. The problem is that the gate has never had a question in it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has said out loud that a yard is not a market has not refused the yard and cannot be counted either way, and that no public market is built out of one reader and a sheet at a gate in ten days is not a market.

Then the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them proposed the join, and it was refused on the page.

He wanted the five lines of his own sheet and the four lines of the printed sheet put on one page, in this yard, in front of about nineteen people, and read out together, so that anybody standing in that gate could see the two of them at once and work out for himself what the offer was and what the rule about it was.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two documents joined on a page are a join and not a comparison, and that the refusals to join two things in this district are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve, and that neither figure moved at about eleven in the morning on the fifth of this month, and that a join is a join and that a fifth refusal of one join is one join with five days of people watching it, and that the record about the watching says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said no to it in about four seconds and gave a reason, and the count came to a hundred and thirty-six, and a clerk entered that he is not a clerk of a house and that the eight did not move.

"**No. Those two came from two ends of eleven miles of road and putting them on one page does not make them agree, it makes them one thing that a man can pick up and carry, and I have watched a man carry a sheet down a road in this basin and I know what happens to it. And I will say the other half so that nobody in this yard thinks I am against it. If those two are on one page then everybody who stands at that table is standing under both of them at once, and the man of about thirty-seven has not thought about what that does to the people he is trying to pay, and neither has the man four hundred miles off, and neither of them is in this yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and has never been asked to be one, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given.

Then the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was asked to make the copy, and he got through two lines of it and could not do the third, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody sent for him and that the record about the sending says not asked, and that a hand that has not closed since the thirteenth of last month is a hand and is not a refusal and is not a consent.

He put the piece of paper down on the end of the trestle table with the two lines on it and the chalk mark of the third one started, and he said one thing about it, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and thirty, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what he was talking about.

"**I wrote three lines of a sheet with four lines on it in this yard on the twenty-eighth of last month and the fourth of them was done by a man with a bad hand because I could not do it, and nobody has said anything to me about that since and I have not asked anybody to. I have got two lines of these four and I cannot get to the third and I have been about three weeks not being able to do a full line of anything, and I would like it entered that I offered to do it this morning before anybody asked me, and that nobody asked me, and that I am not going to be able to do the rest of it this month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a copy and is not a keeper of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he cannot do a thing this month has not refused that thing and cannot be counted either way, and that the columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to.

And the man of about thirty-seven said that he did not want the copy, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it in about four seconds and that he said why.

"**I did not come eleven miles for a piece of paper. I came for a place where a person can be found and a figure that says they were there, and I can write that on anything. If I cannot have an address then I will stand at a wall four feet high with a table behind it every morning of a week like a man selling apples, and the nine houses in that lane will pay me and the people will come, and the second of those four lines that came in here on the fourth of this month is still true whether this yard says a word or not, and I would rather be findable that way than not be findable at all.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that he will do a thing on his own terms has not bound anybody to the terms, and that a wall about four feet high with a table behind it is not an address and is not a page and is not a document, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and that a join is a join and is not a seventh of them and that a copy is a copy and is not a seventh of them and that the six did not move today.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the ledger of what had not moved, in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph. She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the ninety-six days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood seventy-three days and nobody has sat in it on any of the seventy-three, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the seventy-five days since the twenty-first of the fourth month and that she did not ask him, and that the refusals of eleven coppers a week are nine and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight and that neither moved today and that the two are not the same figure.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was five days old and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-seven days off, and about nineteen people had been in a yard a stranger can walk up to and the only thing anybody in it had been asked this month was what it was.

**AND ON THE FIFTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN WHO CONVENES NINE SETTLEMENTS SAID OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN THAT A MARKET THAT IS THE ADDRESS OF BOTH SIDES CANNOT BE THE ADDRESS OF EITHER SIDE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD REFUSED A JOIN OF FIVE LINES AND FOUR LINES PUT ON ONE PAGE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE COPIES FOR NOTHING GOT THROUGH TWO LINES OF IT AND COULD NOT DO THE THIRD.**
