# Chapter 273: Nine Inches

---

The twenty-third of the sixth month was low and warm and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there sixty-one days and nobody had sat in it on any of the sixty-one.

Nothing had been said in that yard yet and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures, and the man of fifty-six read the board out from over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and eighty-one days on the board and four hundred and ninety-seven days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and eleven, and a hundred and seventy-two days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is thirty-nine days off counted off this morning, and about nine of us are going four miles out past the loams this morning to get nine inches of rope down off a beam, and I have said that sentence out loud before we go so that a clerk can enter it as a thing nine people agreed to before they went and not as a thing nine people found out afterwards.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the phrase *nine inches of rope* was said at that gate at about ten in the morning by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and that nine people heard it, and that nobody was asked to agree to it and that a man who says a thing out loud before he does it has not had anybody's consent to it, and that the record about the nine hearing it says not asked.

A clerk entered that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood sixty-one days, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the sixty-three days since the twenty-first of the month before last, and that the record that came into force on the first of this month has been in force twenty-two days and has been read out seven times and has not been answered, and that a refused reading is not a reading.

---

The yard is about four miles out past the loams and the frame in it is of oak about eleven feet high with two uprights let into the ground about four feet apart and a beam across the top, and the oak is older and darker than the oak at the loams, and there is a second frame behind it about nine feet back that is newer and is holding nothing.

The bell hangs in the middle of it on a headstock of iron and it is about two feet across the mouth and it is cracked from the rim up about a third of the way on the side that faces the loams, and the clapper is there, and about three feet of rope is gone and the rest of it is a stub of about nine inches hanging inside the beam where nobody standing on the ground can reach it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the three things a bell has to have before this district will call a sounding safe, in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph, and entered that all three of them are absent in this district this morning, and that the first of them is that a bell that is rung records everybody who hears it for one night, and that the second of them is that a bell rung when there is nothing to answer it cannot be rung again for a month, and that the third of them is that a community has to provide a shelter and a way to take the record off, and that a bell is not a figure and a bell is not a sixth of the five protected things in this district and a person standing in a doorway is not a sixth of the five either.

Then the man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the two walls out loud so that about nine people standing under a frame of oak could hear them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that they are set out here in full and that either of them has to reproduce exactly if it is read out again in a room.

The first is on the gable end of a barn about a mile back toward the loams, and it is in whitewash, and the top of the last rung has gone and there is nothing above the first line of it.

> **RUNG. THE ELEVENTH OF THE THIRD MONTH OF THE FIFTEENTH YEAR. AT NIGHT. NOBODY CAME.**

The second is on the inside of a byre about a mile the other way, and it is in charcoal, and it is in a different hand, and it is four lines and the last one has been rubbed at.

> **RUNG ON A NIGHT IN THE MONTH AFTER THAT ONE.**
> **WE WENT DOWN THE LANE WHEN WE HEARD IT.**
> **WE CAME BACK UP THE LANE WHEN IT STOPPED.**
> **NAMES OF WHO HEARD IT —**

He said what those two walls say when they are put next to each other, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it in fifteen words, and that the fifteen words are these.

> **EITHER THE RULE IS NOT A RULE OR ONE OF THOSE TWO HANDS IS WRONG.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the first of those two wall-handlers wrote on it that nobody came, and that a bell rung with nobody coming to it is a bell rung for nothing, and that the second of them wrote that it was rung on a night in the month after that one, and that a month after the eleventh of the third month of the fifteenth year runs to the eleventh of the fourth month of the fifteenth year and the night the second of them wrote about is inside it, and that this district has not worked out which of those two things is true and is not going to work it out this morning, and that a clerk who is not going to work it out is not a clerk who has found nothing.

---

They got the nine inches down off the beam at about twenty past ten in the morning and it took about twenty minutes, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody sent for a ladder and that the record about the ladder says not asked, and that a rope is not a document and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went up the second frame, which is the newer one about nine feet back that is holding nothing, and he went up it with his right hand because his left hand has not been right since the first of this month, and his left hand had nothing to hold, and he got along the top of that frame about four feet and put his right arm over the beam of the first one and got hold of the stub of rope and pulled.

The headstock of iron is where the bell hangs and it is about two feet below that beam, and he brought the inside of his left forearm down the side of it on the way back, from the wrist to the elbow, and took the skin off it in one strip, and he did not stop on the frame and he did not say anything about it, and he came down the second frame with the nine inches of rope in his own hand.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the nine inches of rope went into the hands of a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and that he is not a bearer of it and is not a keeper of it and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that the woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book was not in that yard and had not been there and does not come to that frame, and that an arm is not a figure about anybody and that the strip of skin off it is not a finding and is not a reason for anything, and that nobody in that yard saw it happen and that the record about the nine seeing it says not asked.

Then he said one thing about it out loud, to about nine people, and it was counted and came to a hundred and twenty-two, and a clerk entered that he said it about eleven minutes after it happened and that nobody had asked him.

"**That is the second time this month something has come off that frame and taken a piece of me with it, and I am saying it in this yard and not in a lane because I said on the fourteenth of this month that I would rather everybody knew that about me on a day I said it than have it worked out on some later day by somebody who was not me. It is not a rope and it is not a bell. It is a strip of skin the length of my forearm and I cannot get a fist round anything with that hand this evening and I have got to pull a rope with something in about two days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says out loud that his body is going to be the price of a thing before the thing happens is not a consent and is not a bearer and is not a party of anything, and that the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the twenty-third of the sixth month, and that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house.

Then a man of fifty-six said the thing about the rope that the woman of about thirty-one had said about it in that same yard on the ninth of this month, and a clerk entered that he said it without having been asked and that the record about his not being asked says not asked.

"**Somebody untied that rope at the top and pulled it down a bit at a time so that you could not see it from the road, and the nine inches that is left is the length that stops a man being able to say he rang it by accident. Whoever did that did not want the bell gone. They wanted the bell to be there and to be out of reach, and that is a different thing, and it has been there for nine years, and it is the only decision about that bell anybody has ever made and it was made by somebody who is not on a page and never was.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a keeper has never been on a page in this basin and there never was one and no keeper is named, and that a decision somebody made nine years ago about a rope is not a job and is not a bearer and did not become an instrument this district built and did not make a seventh of the six, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and a seventh was proposed twice in two days and refused on the page both times and the six did not move.

---

They came back up the four miles in the middle of the afternoon because the light was not going to hold, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road, who is not the tally keeper and is not the man who digs loam and is not the man who mends fencing, was at the top of the bank where the road ends and said one thing to about four people, and it was counted and came to a hundred and forty-four, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about.

"**I have kept four miles of that road for eleven years and there is a gate on it about a mile before the place where the ground stops, and I have told this yard about that road three times this month and not one of you has ever asked me the other end of it. What I want said this afternoon is that about nine people walked past my gate today going to a bell and about nine people walked back past it, and both lots of nine were the same nine, and there is not one other thing in this district where that is true. I would like it entered that I said it on my own two feet and that nobody sent for me and that I am not a bearer of that road and have never been asked to be one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a clerk of a house, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about one in the afternoon on the twenty-third of the sixth month, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not a bearer of a thing is not a bearer of it and is not a holder of it and is not a steward of it and is not a party of anything.

At about half past four the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat was at the foot of her own gate at the third house up that lane from the scale, with a kid about the size of a dog against her leg, and she said one thing to the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing as he went past, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted, and that the record came from him and not from her, and that the record about her not having been asked says not asked.

"**You are going to be holding something with that arm on the night and I have watched you not hold a bucket with that hand for a fortnight. There is a rope about nine inches of it on a beam in a yard four miles out and you have had it in your hand this morning and I could see from the gate that you were not going to say anything about it, so I am saying it now before you, and I am not saying it to be thanked, and I am not going to say it twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a goat is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a goat and is not a bearer of a rope and is not a keeper of anything, and that a person who has said a thing out loud once and refused to say it twice has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way.

The day came down about half past seven and the ninth of the nine printed nights was sixty-four days back and a strip of skin about the length of a forearm was drying on a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing in a lane behind a bank, and nobody in this district had told anybody that they were going to be told anything about it, and the record says not asked.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-THIRD OF THE SIXTH MONTH NINE INCHES OF ROPE CAME DOWN OFF A BEAM FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS AFTER THREE FEET OF IT HAD GONE IN NINE YEARS, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR TOOK THE SKIN OFF THE INSIDE OF HIS LEFT FOREARM TO THE ELBOW ON A HEADSTOCK OF IRON ON THE WAY DOWN AND SAID SO OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD ELEVEN MINUTES LATER, AND A MAN READ TWO WALLS OUT LOUD UNDER A FRAME OF OAK AND SAID THAT EITHER THE RULE IS NOT A RULE OR ONE OF THOSE TWO HANDS IS WRONG.**
