# Chapter 289: One Thing, Asked Once

---

The ninth of the seventh month was dull and warm and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen and one of them counted the heads at the gate and got nine and gave the figure out loud without being asked.

"**A hundred and ninety-seven days on the board and five hundred and thirteen days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and twenty-seven, and a hundred and eighty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-three days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is eighty days back, and nobody in this district can say whether that night happened, and about a hundred days ago today, counting the first day in, nobody put a line on a board.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that twenty-three is twenty-two days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a head counted at a gate in daylight is a figure about a gate and is not a figure about the nine people and is not a finding.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the hundred days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood seventy-seven days and nobody has sat in it on any of the seventy-seven, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the seventy-nine days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and that she did not ask him for the figure this morning and will not ask him for it this month, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight and did not move today, and that the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month and is a hundred and seven days back this morning, and that the rival record has now been in force thirty-eight days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of last month and it is unanswered.

Then the man of about forty-eight who keeps that tally came down the lane behind the bank at about half past ten in the morning with his book under his arm, on his own two feet, and went past that yard without stopping, and about four people in that yard said good morning to him and he said good morning back and said nothing else, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a count of six households is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a book and is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a figure a man will not repeat is not a refusal and is not a consent.

At about eleven in the morning about nine people at the gate of the yard of Lot Seventeen asked the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them how many people had stood at his table on the two mornings it had been up, and he said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and the count came to a hundred and ninety-eight.

"**No, and I am going to say the reason before I am asked twice, and the reason is that a figure of two mornings is a figure about two mornings. On the first morning there was one person at eight o'clock. I have not been back out there since and I am not going to until the end of the week, and I do not know what is at that table at about eight o'clock this morning and neither do the four people who watched one person stand at it. If I tell this yard a number about the first two mornings then this yard will have a number, and in a month the number will be about a person, because that is what numbers do in this basin, and on the third of this month a woman who keeps a scale in this district said the word rate out loud in that yard before anybody asked her for it, and I have been in this basin nine days and I had not thought of it, and I would like somebody here to notice that she weighs about nine sacks a day and has never weighed a day.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who puts tables up is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a figure and is not a keeper of a table, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about eleven in the morning on the ninth of the seventh month, and that a figure of two mornings is a figure about two mornings and is not a figure about anybody, and that a woman who carries water to eleven houses is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a figure.

---
The woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book came down four miles in the afternoon and went back up them at about half past five, and about nine people were in the room where the books are with the door shut, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in about nine feet of lane outside that door with a man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not in this district on any of the ten days of this block except this one afternoon, and that a day she is not here is not a day she has been here, and that the record about the rest says not asked.

He did not ask her in that room. He asked her in that lane, in the ordinary voice, about nine people inside the door heard most of it and a clerk four hundred yards away in a yard was not told about it until about seven.

"**One thing, and then I am not going to come at you with a second one this month, and I told her in a room yesterday afternoon that I was not going to let that go either, and I am telling you the same thing in a lane because I do not want it in a room. On the sixth of this month a man asked this yard whether anybody in this district carries water to a house in the ground and four people turned round and looked at a woman of fifty-eight, and she answered it herself. A man of about thirty-seven set a table up against a wall about eleven miles off on the seventh of this month at about eight in the morning and there was one person at it. I want to know whether anybody in this district who is not in a household is standing at that table. That is the whole of what I am asking and I am asking you and not him, and I would rather you said no than that you took a week about it.**"

And she said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and the count came to two hundred and sixty-nine, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked one thing and that nobody in that lane asked her a second one, and that the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about half past four in the afternoon on the ninth of the seventh month.

"**No, and I am going to tell you why in about nine seconds and then I am going up those four miles and I am not coming down them again this month. You have asked me for this three times since the first of last month and I have said no three times and I have never given you a reason, and I am going to give you one now because you have finally asked me for one thing instead of a general favour, and I would like it entered that that is why I am answering. I keep lines. That is all I do. I walk them at night in fours and fives and I have walked them for eleven years and there is a wire on the left of one of them that has taken the skin off a shin twice. If I tell you who is standing at a table, then in eleven years somebody can work out who walks a line. That is the whole answer and it is not about you and it is not about that table. You are asking me for a figure about the people and I can only give you a figure about the lines, and the two are not the same, and I am not going to stand in a lane and hand you the second one dressed up as the first. And I am not going to say anything else, and I am not going to tell him either, and I would like both of you to enter that you did not get anything out of this lane.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a route is not a keeper of a route and has never been asked to be one, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that a line is not a document and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own, and that a woman who has said out loud that she is not coming back down a road has not refused the road and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not coming back says not asked, and that nobody in that lane and nobody in that room was given a sentence that settles anything.

And the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing back and it was not counted, and about nine people inside the door heard it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it in about four seconds and that the minute is in his own hand.

"**Then I enter that I got nothing, and I want it in your version too because I am not going to be the only one carrying that. There is a man with a table against a wall eleven miles off and a sheet of four lines of print saying that any offer to pay a person not in a household is a figure about people not in a household, and the number of people in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off is to be completed before the first of the eighth month, and that is twenty-three days off, and there is one person in this district who could tell me whether the thing I am looking for is standing at a table or not, and she has said no three times and a reason today, and I am not going to ask her a fourth time and I am not going to find another way round her, and I would like that entered as a thing I have said out loud in a lane to a man of about thirty-seven who will not remember it by the end of the week.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says out loud in a lane that he is not going to look for another way round a refusal has not refused the refusal and has not consented to it and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who has been refused nine things in five months has acquired nothing by any of the nine and is not a party of any of them, and that the figure of the things a man has been refused moved from eight to nine at about half past four in the afternoon on the ninth of this month, and that the eight of the eighth of this month is the woman who keeps the books in the room and the nine of this afternoon is the woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route, and that that nine and the nine refusals of eleven coppers a week are two different nines and are not the same figure and are not added together, and that the record about the nine says not asked.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was nine days old and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-three days off, and about four hundred and eleven people in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off were to be moved on or before it, and a woman of about thirty-one went back up four miles on her own two feet and told nobody what she had said in that lane.

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO KEEPS A ROUTE CAME DOWN FOUR MILES AND WENT BACK UP THEM, AND A MAN ASKED HER FOR ONE THING IN A LANE AND SHE REFUSED IT IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS AND GAVE HIM A REASON SHE HAD NEVER GIVEN ANYBODY, AND SAID THAT SHE HAS WALKED HER LINES FOR ELEVEN YEARS, AND NOBODY IN THAT LANE WAS GIVEN A SENTENCE THAT SETTLES ANYTHING.**
