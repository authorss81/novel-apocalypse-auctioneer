# Chapter 284: The Second Side

---

The fourth of the seventh month was still and warm and the sheet of four lines of print came into the yard of Lot Seventeen on the back of the same hand-cart at about half past ten in the morning, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and ninety-two days on the board and five hundred and eight days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and twenty-two, and a hundred and eighty-three days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-eight days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is seventy-five days back, and there is a sheet on that hand-cart with four lines of print on it and no name on it and no seal on it, and it is the second thing that has come into this yard in three days from outside this district.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a sheet is a document and is not a copy, and that a sheet with no name and no seal on it is a document that has found nobody, and that a document that names a body which does not exist has found nobody, and that neither creates a consent nor makes a person a bearer nor makes a house into a party, and that the three documents this district does not own are three and that this is not a fourth of them, because a sheet brought into a yard on a hand-cart in front of about nineteen people is a document this district has seen the whole of.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them asked for it to be read out in that yard, and about four people had not got to the end of the gate before the woman who keeps the books in the room four hundred yards from that gate came up the bank and said one thing to the lane and not to the yard, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record came from the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and not from her.

"**Do not read it in that yard. Ask him to read it in a room with a door and nine people in it who have chosen to be there, or do not read it at all this month, and I will not tell him which, because I am not going to be the reason a sheet gets said out loud in a place where everybody who lives within nine hundred yards of it is standing.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar is not a keeper of a yard and is not a bearer of a gate and is not a party of anything, and that a woman who has said out loud that she will not tell a man which of two things to do has not refused either of them and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not saying says not asked.

And the man of about thirty-seven did not have it read out, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had asked for it and had not had it read out, and that a sheet that is not read out has not been refused and is not a reading and cannot be counted either way, and that asking a yard to read a thing is not asking the yard to agree to it.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the four lines of it out loud himself, in the ordinary voice, standing about four feet back from the trestle table, not as a thing read out in that yard but as a thing he had read, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that distinction and that the four lines are these.

> **AN OFFER TO PAY A PERSON WHO IS NOT ENTERED IN ANY HOUSEHOLD IS A FIGURE ABOUT PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT ENTERED IN ANY HOUSEHOLD.**
>
> **THE PLACE AN OFFER IS MADE IN IS ENTERED IN THE RECORD OF THAT PLACE.**
>
> **A PERSON WHO IS PAID WEEKLY BY ANYBODY IS A PERSON WHO CAN BE FOUND.**
>
> **THE FIGURE OF THE PEOPLE IN THE GROUND IN THE SHADOW OF A BUILDING ELEVEN MILES OFF THIS DISTRICT IS TO BE COMPLETED BEFORE THE FIRST DAY OF THE EIGHTH MONTH.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that it names no body and names no person and names no place, and that the second of its four lines is a rule about an address and that an address is not a bearer and a wall is not an address, and that a document that names a job that does not exist has found nobody and this one names none, and that the fourth of its four lines names a figure and a day that are both already on a sheet nailed to a gatepost in that yard, and that no number on that sheet moved on the fourth of this month.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said what those four lines were, out loud, to about nineteen people, and it was counted and came to two hundred and seventeen, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard was able to answer him.

"**That is the same shape of thing as three other sheets in this district and it is not the same thing, and I want the difference said out loud. The printed sheet at the fourth place, the notice about a party of nine who went thirty-four miles east, and a line in a public book with four names on it and a figure under the four names are three documents this district does not own, and I have said for five months that a document which names nothing is the only safe kind there is. This sheet names no person and no body and it is the fourth of that shape and it is the worst of the four, because the other three do not have a second line in them. This one says the place an offer is made in goes in the record of that place. On the first of this month a man asked this yard for its address and this yard did not say the word. Somebody four hundred miles off has now put on paper that the address is not optional. That is not a threat. Nobody has threatened this yard this month. That is worse, because a threat can be refused and a printed rule about where a thing happens cannot.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a printed rule and is not a party of it, and that the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about eleven in the morning on the fourth of the seventh month, and that a man who says out loud that a thing is worse than a threat has not refused the thing and cannot be counted either way.

Then a clerk of nineteen years entered, in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph, that this yard has now been asked twice in ten days by two sides to be an address, that on the first of this month a man of about thirty-seven asked it for itself, and that on the fourth of this month a sheet of four lines of print says that the place an offer is made in goes in the record of that place, **and that a yard that is the address of two sides is a yard and not a market, and that a market is a thing where a person is asked a question, and that this yard has not asked anybody in it a question about what they want there, and that is the whole of what a market is, and that neither side that came to that gate has been asked one, and that no public market is built out of one reader and a sheet at a gate in ten days is not a market, and that the record about the two askings says not asked.**

---

At about half past two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-one who digs loam went down the lane behind the bank to the near end of it and looked at the ditch behind the building with two doors.

It is about two feet deep in the sixth month and it was about three feet deep on the fourth of the seventh, and the plank door in the end wall of that building opens on it, and the nail in the hasp of that door did not move on the eleventh of last month and nobody in this district touched it this month.

He got down into it and dug out about four feet of it with a spade he had carried down the lane on his shoulder, and he was in the bottom of it for about three hours, and at the end of the three hours he could not get himself out of it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, whose right shoulder has not been what it was since the first of this month, stood at the top of it and put a hand down and had him up by the arm.

Neither of them said anything about it for about nine minutes afterwards, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the nine minutes are not a refusal and are not a consent and that the record about them says not asked, and that a ditch is not a document and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own.

Then the man of about thirty-one who digs loam said one thing about the water, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and seventy-one, and a clerk entered that he said it at the top of the lane and not in the yard and that nobody asked him what it was about.

"**There is about a foot of water in the bottom of that ditch this afternoon and there was about two inches in it on the eleventh of last month, and nobody in this district has put a mark on it and nobody has to, because it is on the side of a building that has two doors in it and the second of them is a plank on the end wall with a nail in a hasp, and a plank does not keep nights and it does not keep water either. I am not saying that building is going to be shut. Nobody has asked me whether I think that and nobody is going to. I am saying that the ditch went up about ten inches in twenty-three days and the month turned on the first of this month, and the man who mends fencing got me out of it with that shoulder and I am not going to be the one who forgot that by the end of the summer.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who digs loam is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a ditch, and that a foot of water in a ditch in twenty-three days is a figure about a ditch and not a finding, and that nobody asked him whether he thinks that building is going to be shut and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this afternoon.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the figures that had not moved, in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph. She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the ninety-five days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood seventy-two days and nobody has sat in it on any of the seventy-two, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the seventy-four days since the twenty-first of the fourth month and that she did not ask him, and that the conditions with no end on it are four and that no fifth was proposed this month, and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and that neither moved today and that no join was proposed today, and that the rival record has now been in force thirty-three days and has been read out in that yard seven times and that its eighth reading was refused out loud on the twenty-fourth of last month with a reason and it is unanswered, and that the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is eight and did not move today.

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was four days old and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-eight days off, and about a foot of water was standing in a ditch behind a building with two doors, and a sheet with four lines of print on it and no name on it was rolled against the outside wall of that building beside a curtain on a nail, and the record about both of those says not asked.

**AND ON THE FOURTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A SHEET OF FOUR LINES OF PRINT WITH NO NAME AND NO SEAL CAME INTO THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN SAYING THAT THE PLACE AN OFFER IS MADE IN IS ENTERED IN THE RECORD OF THAT PLACE, AND IT WAS NOT READ OUT IN THAT YARD, AND THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN HAD NOW BEEN ASKED TWICE IN TEN DAYS BY TWO SIDES TO BE AN ADDRESS AND HAD ANSWED NEITHER TIME.**
