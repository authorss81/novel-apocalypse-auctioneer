# Chapter 280: A Wrong Sheet

---

The thirtieth of the sixth month was low and warm and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there sixty-eight days and nobody had sat in it on any of the sixty-eight, and this morning was the last morning of the sixth month.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and neither of those two men is a keeper of anything.

"**A hundred and eighty-eight days on the board and five hundred and four days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and eighteen, and a hundred and seventy-nine days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is thirty-two days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is seventy-one days back, and nobody in this district knows whether that night happened, and about a quarter past nine this morning a man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came out of the room four hundred yards from that gate having read two pieces of paper in a book in there that nobody had asked him to read.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that thirty-two is no days to the end of this month and the thirty-one of the month after it and the first of the month after that, and that the length of the month after that is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man who walks into a room a stranger may walk up to and reads what is in a book in it has not been asked anything and has refused nothing and cannot be counted either way.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the things that had not moved in ten days, and they were in the same hand as the rest and they were not a paragraph.

She entered that the things this district does not have are five and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and that it is not paid and not priced, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and that a public entrance was proposed on the twenty-second of this month and entered on the page as not a seventh and that the six did not move, and that a board with a finger's width at the bottom of it for a name and a stool outside a door were proposed in the same morning of the twenty-second and both were refused on the page, and that the three documents this district does not own are three and that four documents were made or arrived in ten days and none became a fourth of them, and that the protected things are five and no sixth was added and that a bell is not a sixth and a person standing in a doorway is not a sixth.

She entered that the conditions with no end on it are four, and that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and that neither moved on any of the ten days and that no join was proposed in this block, and that the refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading and the fourth line of the offer was not read out in this block, and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight and that eight people gave one with a reason in ten days and not one of them is a clerk of a house.

She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the ninety-one days since the first of the month before last, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood sixty-eight days and nobody has sat in it on any of the sixty-eight, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the seventy days since the twenty-first of the month before last, and that the boards in this district are five and the lines that are full are six and a seventh is ruled and empty at the ninth place, and that the columns of not-askings are four and a fifth was proposed on the twenty-second of this month on a board and refused on the page, and that there is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and there never has been one, and that the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and that the seven and the eight are not the same figure and are not added together, and that the rival record has now been in force twenty-nine days and has been read out in that yard seven times and that the eighth reading was refused out loud on the twenty-fourth of this month with a reason and a refused reading is not a reading and nobody has answered the record.

She entered that this district has five different ninths in it and that they are the ninth of the nine charters, the ninth of the eleven houses the woman of fifty-eight carries water to, the ninth of the nine printed nights, the ninth place on the page out of the cave and the ninth of the ten days the block before this one ran, and that they are five ninths and are not added together, and that a sixth was available to her this morning and she did not take it, and that a clerk who could have made a sixth and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing.

She entered that the man of about thirty at a counter is at the sixteenth time down the eleven mile road and the next is the seventeenth and that this district has never once given that man a day to be here on, and that the man of about forty-one who keeps a road house at the top of the road that climbs is not in this district on any of the ten days either, and that the man with the portfolio is not in this district and that nothing in this yard says where he is, and that a man of about forty-four with a case is not in this district and went out of that gate on the nineteenth of this month with a list, and that nobody has seen the list and nobody has counted it and that the record about the list says not asked.

Then the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart, who is not the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room with nothing over the door and is not the man of about nineteen who counts, and who hauled nine sacks down to the scale on the morning of the fourteenth of this month for a man with a case and took no money for them, said one thing in that yard, and it was counted and came to two hundred and forty-two, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard argued with it.

"**I have read two pieces of paper. I did not ask to and nobody stopped me and I am not a stranger and I am not a clerk and I do not keep anything except a cart. The first of them says a bell was rung and about nine people went into a building and put their own names on a piece of paper, and it says that there is a person in this district who heard that bell and is not in that piece of paper and is not named on it. The second of them says the building is what makes a sounding safe, and that a sounding is safe if every person who heard it went into that building or was asked why they did not. I am a man who hauls sacks. I am telling you what those two say when you put them side by side. The second one is not true, and the first one is the reason the second one is not true, and both of them are in a book in this district that anybody can walk in and read, and the second one is the one that is going to be picked up by somebody who is not any of you, and I would like it entered that the man who said it is a man who drives a cart and that he is not one of the things he is talking about.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who drives a cart is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a lot and is not a party of the first of those two documents or the second of them, and that a man who has read a document and said out loud what it says is not a bearer of it and is not a keeper of it and has not been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that the record about his not having asked to read it says not asked.

A man of fifty-six said one thing, and it was counted and came to a hundred and eighty-five, and a clerk entered that a man of fifty-six said it twice in three days and that the second time is not a habit and is not a finding and that the record about the twice says not asked.

"**A yard is a room with no roof and a yard is where a thing gets said out loud, and everything this district has found out in five months it found out in a yard, and a yard is the worst possible place to find out that a document you are about to publish is wrong. I have been wrong about four hundred and eleven for six weeks and a woman of fifty-eight sat down on a step outside the second house up that lane on the nineteenth of this month and told me, and I said so out loud in that lane the same evening, and nobody argued with me about it and nobody has asked me about it since. This morning the two pieces of paper in that book are in the same state I was in on the nineteenth of this month, and there is a man of about twenty-nine in this yard who has just said so out loud in a sentence, and I am going to let his sentence stand and I am not going to add anything to it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has said out loud that he is letting another man's sentence stand has not endorsed it and has not refused it and cannot be counted either way.

---

At about six in the evening, in the doorway of the room where the books are, the woman who keeps the books in that room said one thing to the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, and there was nobody in that doorway who had been asked to count it.

"**I told you in about nine feet of lane on the twenty-sixth of this month that I was not going to put my own hand on either of your two shapes, and I have written one of them out in my own hand from the first word to the last, and I have put my own hand under the other one at about five this afternoon, and a man of about thirty-four read it out, and I did not take my hand back off it, and that is the whole of what has happened between us in twenty days. I want to say one more thing and then I am going to shut this door and it is going to be shut for about nine hours. There is no room in this district that nobody can walk into and I said that to you in about nine feet of lane on the eighteenth of this month and I have not stopped saying it, and what I have done since is put my own hand to two pieces of paper in a book that anybody can walk in and read, and I do not know what to call that, and I am not going to call it anything tonight.**"

And he said one thing back, and it was not counted either, and the minute is in his hand and is not in a book.

"**I am not going to call it anything either, and I am not going to say thank you, and I have not thanked you once in twenty days and you have not once asked me to, and I would like both of those to stay the way they are for a while longer than a month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not a steward and is not a party of anything, and that two people who have said out loud that they are not going to call a thing anything have not refused it and have not consented to it and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the twenty days says not asked.

The day came down about half past seven and the sixth month was thirty days old and has no more days in it that anybody in this district has counted, and the first day of the month after next is thirty-two days off.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the last thing of the ten days in a lane behind the bank at about half past seven in the evening, to about four people, and it was counted and came to two hundred and eighty-nine, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it out loud and that a clerk was four hundred yards away and got it from him and not from her and that the record about the four hearing it says not asked.

"**We put two pieces of paper into a book in this district this month and one of them is a hole with a reason printed under it and the other one is a charter that says a bell in a frame four miles off was safe, and it was not safe, and the reason it was not safe is a child of about eight who nobody in this district has asked anything in his life, and a house in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off that a woman of fifty-eight has carried water to for nineteen years and that is not on the list of anybody who is to be moved, and neither the child nor the woman in that house can be put anywhere in this basin without somebody making them people who were asked. So there is a charter in a book in this district that a stranger may walk up to and read and it is wrong on its face, and I want that said in a lane by a man who mends fencing before anything else is said about it, and here is the rest of it and I have not got it written down anywhere. A wrong sheet that a stranger can walk up to and read is a worse thing than no sheet. No sheet is an absence and a man in a yard can say an absence is a lie and be wrong about it. A wrong sheet is a thing a stranger can pick up, and point at, and carry down a road, and we put it there ourselves, and I am not going to be the man who takes it back out of that book.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a wrong count is not struck in this district and no count has been struck in this block, and that the forty-one names said out loud in that yard on the seventeenth of this month are forty people and one of them is a person twice and that figure is not struck either, and that a sheet a stranger may walk up to and read is not an address and is not a page and is not a keeper of anything, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and a seventh was not proposed in ten days after the twenty-second and the six did not move, and that the record about the not striking says not asked.

**AND ON THE THIRTIETH OF THE SIXTH MONTH TWO PIECES OF PAPER WERE IN A BOOK FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE WHO DRIVES A CART CAME OUT OF THAT ROOM HAVING READ BOTH OF THEM AND SAID THAT THE SECOND OF THEM WAS NOT TRUE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID IN A LANE THAT A WRONG SHEET THAT A STRANGER CAN WALK UP TO AND READ IS A WORSE THING THAN NO SHEET, AND SAID THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO BE THE MAN WHO TAKES IT BACK OUT OF THAT BOOK.**
