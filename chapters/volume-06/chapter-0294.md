# Chapter 294: Nobody Is Sent

---

The fourteenth of the seventh month was bright and the wind came up the lane behind the bank in the middle of the day and took the heat off for about an hour, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**Two hundred and two days on the board and five hundred and eighteen days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and thirty-two, and a hundred and ninety-three days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is eighteen days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is eighty-five days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and twelve days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that eighteen is seventeen days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated and was not used this morning.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in that yard from about ten until about half past two, and he said almost nothing in it, and at about eleven he walked to the end of the lane behind the bank and stood there about nine feet from a gate he did not go through for about four minutes, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not go through it and that the door he did not go to is four hundred yards from where he was standing, and that he was not asked to stand there and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then he came back into the yard and said one thing about it, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to two hundred and eighty-eight, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about and that the woman who keeps the books in the room four hundred yards from that gate was not told any of it that day.

"**On the eighth of this month I asked that woman to put two sheets into the first of the two books in her room, in front of about nine people, and she said no in about four seconds and gave me a reason that was better than mine, and at the end of it she told me not to come and ask her again this month, and I said I would not, and this morning the second of that printed sheet says a place where a sheet has been read out is a place where an offer is made, and the third of it says the place an offer is made in is entered in the record of that place, and the record of that place is a book in a room four hundred yards from that gate with a door on it. I did not go to that door this morning and I am not going to go this month, and I did not send the man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing to it with a sheet under his arm, and I did not send the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart to it with a cart. Nobody is going to be sent to that door by me in the seventh month. I am not doing that because I think she was wrong on the eighth. She was not wrong on the eighth. I am not doing it because she told me a month and not a thing, and a month runs out on a day, and the seventh month runs out on the thirty-first of it, and I have got the rest of it in front of me and so has she.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who convenes nine settlements is not a representative of a settlement that has not asked to be represented, and that a refusal of the eighth of this month is in the record and stands in the record, and that a man who has kept to a thing he was told to keep to cannot be counted either way, and that the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about half past eleven in the morning on the fourteenth of the seventh month, and that six days separate the eighth of this month and this morning and nobody in this district has asked either of those two people anything since.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was at the foot of the trestle table at about half past twelve and said one thing before anybody asked him for it, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record about the not counting says not asked.

"**Nobody has asked me for a copy of that five-line sheet and I want it entered that nobody asked me, because I know what I am going to say and I would rather have said it this morning than been asked in a week's time. I cannot copy it. My hand has not closed since the thirteenth of last month and I could not get through the third of four lines on the fifth of this month in front of about nineteen people, and I have not been able to do a full line of anything in three weeks. I read those five lines out loud in the lane outside that office every morning of a week and I can do that with a mouth. If anybody in this district wants that sheet in a second place they are going to have to send a person who can still write, and there is not one in these nine settlements, and I am not going to pretend to be one for another day to keep somebody from being disappointed.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of anything, and that a hand that has not closed is a hand and is not a refusal and is not a consent, and that a voice in a lane is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and that **a copy of a document is not a document and the five lines of print are not a second of the five lines on a man's own sheet, and the two are not added together.**
Between about one and about half past two that yard was the quietest it had been in a week. The wind came and went at the top of the lane and nobody shut a gate, and the sheet of five lines of print lay on the end of the trestle table with its fifth line uppermost, and about four people read it and stood about it afterwards without saying anything to each other, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the low wall along the lane and let about four of them ask him the same question about the second of the five lines and gave the same answer four times.

The sheet of five lines of print had been on the end of that trestle table since about half past twelve and about nine people in that yard walked up to it and read it, and a man of about nineteen counted how many of them read it to the end and got nine and gave the number out loud, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he gave it and that a number a man of about nineteen counts in daylight in front of about nineteen people is a figure about nine people reading a thing to the end and is not a figure about the sheet and is not a finding, and that the record about the nine says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing about that, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to two hundred and twenty-nine, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about.

"**On the eighth of this month I stood in about nine feet of lane outside a door four hundred yards from where I am standing and a woman told me one sentence at that door, and I have not been back to that door and I am not going to be back to it this month. This afternoon there is a sheet on the end of a table and about nine people in this yard have read it to the end without anybody standing in a doorway, and I am not going to stand in this yard and say that is better, because I have got no standing to say it and I have said on the eleventh of this month that I would not sit in that chair and I am not going to be the man who has an opinion about where a sheet ought to live four days after somebody has refused him a place to put one. I want the nine entered and I want it entered that a yard is not a book, and that a sheet a stranger can walk up to in a yard is a different thing from a page a stranger can walk up to in a room, and that neither of them is a person and that I do not know which of the two I would rather have.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a yard is not a book and is not a room and is not a market and is not a counter, and that a document read in a yard has not been refused and is not a reading, and that asking nine people to read a thing is not asking them to agree to it, and that the record about a man not knowing which of two things he would rather have says not asked.

She entered what had not moved on the fourteenth of this month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody put a line on a board on any of the hundred and five days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood eighty-two days and nobody has sat in it on any of the eighty-two. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the eighty-four days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she did not ask him, and he was in that yard on the ninth of this month and said good morning and nothing else. Four conditions with no end on it and no fifth proposed today. Nine refusals to read, the departure unspent, and there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading. **The fourth line of the offer that has been on a table in this district for five months was not read out today and was not read out on any of the ten days this block reaches, and the five lines of the man's own sheet and the five lines of print are not that offer.**

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households came down the lane behind the bank at about half past two in the afternoon with his book under his arm, on his own two feet, and stopped at the gate of that yard for about nine seconds and looked at the sheet of five lines lying on the end of the trestle table from about nine feet away, and about four people in that yard said good morning to him, and he said good morning back, and he went on up the lane, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him anything and that a man who keeps a count of six households is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a book and is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a figure a man will not repeat is not a refusal and is not a consent, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was fourteen days old and the first day of the eighth month was eighteen days off, and a sheet of five lines of print was lying on the end of a trestle table in a yard where about nineteen people could walk up and read it, and nobody had been sent to a door four hundred yards away by anybody.

**AND ON THE FOURTEENTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN WHO CONVENES NINE SETTLEMENTS WALKED TO THE END OF A LANE BEHIND A BANK AND STOOD ABOUT FOUR HUNDRED YARDS SHORT OF A GATE HE DID NOT GO THROUGH, AND HE SENT NOBODY TO THAT DOOR, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD THAT NOBODY HAD ASKED HIM FOR A COPY OF THE FIVE LINES AND THAT HE COULD NOT COPY THEM.**
