# Chapter 266: A Room With A Roof

---

The sixteenth of the sixth month was bright and close and about six people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there fifty-four days and nobody had sat in it on any of the fifty-four.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and seventy-four days on the board and four hundred and ninety days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and four, and a hundred and sixty-five days separate the second of January and this morning, and there are six of us in this yard and the sheet about a copy of anything is on that gatepost, and I am going to stand in this gate for about nine minutes before I say anything else and I would like everybody to let me.**"

A man of about nineteen who counts counted the people in that yard at ten and got six and said out loud that a smaller number in a yard is not a smaller yard and is a figure about six people and not about a yard, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said that and that he was not asked for it and that the record about the six says not asked.

He stood in the gate for about nine minutes and did not say anything, and the yard got quieter rather than louder, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what he was doing and that the record about the nine minutes says not asked, and that a yard is a room with no roof and that a yard in which a thing cannot be said is a room with a roof, and that the roof on this one is a sheet on a gatepost and a man with a case at the end of a trestle table.

At about half past ten he said it in a lane behind the bank to about four people instead, and his voice was not good after standing in a gate for nine minutes trying to use it, and it was counted and came to a hundred and sixty-two, and a clerk entered that it was the sentence he had come to that yard to say and that a sentence said in a lane is a sentence and not a weaker one.

"**I have had a thing to say in that yard since yesterday and I got as far as the gate and I stood in it for about nine minutes and I could not do it, and I want it entered that I could not, because I have spent five months being the man who asks the questions in this district and I have never once failed to ask one, and this morning I failed in front of six people. I am asking it in about four feet of lane instead and it is the same question and it is going to cost the same, and there is a man at the end of that table who buys lists of people who are not in a household, and this district has had a second book of its own since the sixth of this month, and I want to ask in daylight, out loud, does anybody in this district know what is in it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked that question in a lane to about four people at about half past ten in the morning on the sixteenth of the sixth month, and that the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was not one of the four, and that a person who was not asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the four says not asked, and that a question asked in four feet of lane and not in a yard is a question and not a smaller one, and that nobody said anything in that lane and that the record about the nothing says not asked.

---

Then a panel arrived in that yard at about eleven that nobody had asked for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard asked for it and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it, and entered that a panel is a rule and a rule is not a document and is not a fourth and is not one of the six instruments this district built and not named.

> **A PRICE PAID TO A PERSON WHO IS NOT IN A HOUSEHOLD IS A WAY OF PAYING A PERSON WHO IS NOT IN A HOUSEHOLD.**
> **A WAY OF PAYING SOMEBODY IS A WAY OF FINDING OUT WHO IS NOT IN A HOUSEHOLD.**
> **THE TWO ARE ONE INSTRUMENT AND THE SECOND ONE IS THE ONE THAT GETS USED.**

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, who has stood at that scale nine years and weighs about nine sacks a day, disagreed with the first of those three out loud and in front of about six people, and the count came to a hundred and seventy-three.

"**The first of those three is wrong and I will say why in front of the six of you. Eleven coppers a week to a man who copies for nothing is not a way of paying a person who is not in a household. It is a way of paying a man who is not in a household for the thing he was already doing for nothing, and that is a lower price for the same work and a higher price for the same man, and a man who has been sold at a discount for five months knows exactly which of those two it is. The second of the three is right, and I have been saying it since the man with the case put that card on this table, that the man at the end of that table is the price and the trade is the find, and nobody wanted to hear it from a woman at a scale, and now there is a rule about it nailed to a gate.**"

Nobody disagreed with the second of those three or with the third of those three, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody did, and entered that the record about the disagreeing says not asked, and entered that the man of about thirty-four who digs loam was not in that yard this morning and that nobody sent for him and that the record about his not being there says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who disagrees with a printed rule out loud in a yard is doing the thing this yard was made to make possible, and that a woman who keeps a scale is not a clerk of a house and is not one of the eight and that the eight did not move at about eleven in the morning on the sixteenth of the sixth month, and that a person who disagrees with a rule out loud on a day when a sheet on a gatepost says that one copy of anything entered in that yard is to go to a body four hundred miles away has taken a risk, and that nobody asked her to take it, and that the record about the risk says not asked.

Then the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing said the thing he had been carrying since the seventh of this month, in the ordinary voice and to about six people, and it was counted and came to a hundred and forty-five.

"**On the afternoon of the seventh of this month, at about half past three, I was in the room where the books are because I had gone to ask about a page, and nobody sent me and nobody asked me a first thing, and the second book was on the shelf with the chair turned up on top of it and it was open. I did not read it. I want that said in this yard before anybody says it for me, because there is a man at the end of that table who is going to ask me to write things down, and I would rather this yard knew what I am than have it worked out later. I did not read it and I have not stopped seeing it and I do not know what a man is supposed to do with that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about twenty-three was in that room on the afternoon of the seventh and that nobody sent for him and that the record about the open book says not asked, and that a man who has said in a yard that he did not look has not thereby been trusted and has not thereby been distrusted, and that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house and is not one of the eight and that the eight did not move, and that two people were in that room on the seventh of this month and neither of them was asked and that the two are not added together.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the woman who keeps the books in that room was not in that yard on the sixteenth and that nobody sent for her, and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was not in that yard either, and that it is the first of eleven days in this month that either of them has not stood in it, and that a clerk did not ask either of them why and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about fifty-six was standing at the end of that trestle table while the panel was being disagreed with and said nothing at all, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said nothing, and that a man who says nothing in a yard is not agreeing and is not disagreeing, and that the record about his not saying anything says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that about three feet of rope is gone off a beam four miles out past the loams and about nine inches of it is left inside the beam, and that nobody in this district has asked for it to be put back, and that the bell is not rung and was not rung today and the record about the not ringing says not asked.

At about six in the evening the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked the four hundred yards and stood in the doorway of the room where the books are, and the woman who keeps the books in that room opened the door and did not shut it again, and there was nobody in the lane to count either of them, and the whole of it is in a minute in one hand and is not in a book, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it afterwards out of his hand and not out of hers.

He said that he had asked in a lane behind the bank that day whether anybody in this district knew what was in the second book, and that four people had not answered him, and that he was asking her because there is a man at the end of a trestle table who buys lists.

"**I know what is in it. That is all I am going to say about it and I want it written down that I said the first half of that sentence and not the second half, and that you did not ask me for the second half, and that if you ask me for it I will say no, and that a person who has said I know and has not said the number has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and I would like all three of those clauses in the minute in your hand and not in mine.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who keeps a book in one room by one person is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not a party of anything, and that a registrar who says in a doorway that she knows what is in a book she keeps has not consented to anything and has not refused anything, and that the eight did not move at about six in the evening on the sixteenth of the sixth month.

The day came down about half past seven, and the first day of the month after next was forty-six days off counted off that morning, and a clerk entered that a yard in which six people can say a thing out loud in a lane but not in the yard is a yard, and not a market, and not a counter, and that the record about what it is says not asked.

**AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A MAN STOOD IN A GATE FOR ABOUT NINE MINUTES AND COULD NOT SAY A THING HE HAD COME TO SAY AND SAID IT IN A LANE INSTEAD, AND A PANEL ARRIVED IN THAT YARD UNASKED AND A WOMAN WHO KEEPS A SCALE DISAGREED WITH THE FIRST OF ITS THREE LINES OUT LOUD, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE SAID IN THAT YARD THAT HE HAD BEEN IN THE ROOM WHERE THE BOOKS ARE ON THE SEVENTH OF THIS MONTH AND HAD NOT LOOKED.**
