# Chapter 255: Two Doors

---

The fifth of the sixth month was clear and hot by eleven and about nine people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there forty-three days and nobody had sat in it on any of the forty-three.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and sixty-three days on the board and four hundred and seventy-nine days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and ninety-three, and a hundred and fifty-four days separate the second of January and this morning, and that is twenty-two weeks and no days, and I counted it off the second of January and not off a page.**"

Then he said what the yard was for, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and twenty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**There is a building about two hundred yards down the lane behind the bank at the near end of it with one room in it and about nine people in it most nights, and it has two doors, and the second one is a plank door at the back that opens on a ditch. About nineteen of us slept in that room in the night of the second and were gone before six this morning, and I did not send for one of them and I did not fetch one of them, and I am not going to put a figure on it, and I am going to say out loud the one thing I know about that building and then I am going to stop talking about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who slept in a room has not been entered anywhere and that a room is not a settlement and is not a household and is not a party of the first column or the second or the third, and that the record about the second and the third of this month says not asked, and that the protected things in this district are five and no sixth was added to them and that a building with two doors is not a sixth of anything.

---

The building is one room with a beaten earth floor and a front door that has a handle and a curtain in it, and about nine feet back there is a plank door in the end wall with a hasp on the outside of it and a nail in the hasp instead of a staple, and when that plank door is off its hinges it comes off in about two seconds and goes down flat in the ditch behind.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had been in that room in the night and said one thing about it, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and forty-seven.

"**The second door is not a door. It is a piece of board with a nail in it and it comes off in about two seconds and it makes a noise like a duck and it opens on a ditch that goes down to about four feet of water in the wet and about a foot in the dry, and everything I have spent five months building is a way of getting a person into a room, and that is not what that room is for. That room is for getting a person out of it without anybody seeing them go out of it. I have been in about nine doorways in nine days and I have not once thought about the back of the building, and I have been wrong about five months of work and I am saying it here before somebody else has to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a door at the back of a building that opens on a ditch is not a keeper and is not a bearer and is not a figure about anybody, and that a man who has said out loud that he has been wrong about five months of work has not withdrawn the work and has not lost anything anybody else is holding, and that the record about the five months says not asked, and that a man who is not going to be thanked for it did not ask.

A man of fifty-six said that a man who has been wrong about the front of a building for five months and has found it out at the back of one at four in the morning in the middle of a wet month is the first man in this basin who has learned a thing from a building instead of from a book, and that a yard is the easiest place in this basin to learn that in and that the yard is nearly always the wrong place to say it in.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went round the outside of that building at about twenty past twelve to look at the back of it, and the end wall is about nine feet of brick with no window in it at all, and the plank door is not so much on that wall as in a hole cut for it and left.

He worked the hasp with his left hand and could not get his fingers round the nail, and it took him about nine seconds and he did not say anything about it, and he got it up with his right hand, and the board came off in about two seconds and made a noise in the ditch behind like a duck and went down flat in about four inches of water.

He stood looking at it for a while and then he put it back, and it took him about two goes to get the nails in because the nail holes had swollen in the wet and had closed up about a sixteenth of an inch, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came down that lane at about half past twelve on his way to somewhere else and held the board for him without being asked, and neither of them said one word about it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a knot tied by one man's hand for another man is not the second man's knot and is not a job and is not a figure about either of them and did not become a seventh instrument of the six.

A clerk entered that a board off a wall and a board back on a wall is not a door and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own, and that a ditch about four inches deep in the dry and about four feet in the wet is not an exit and is not a route and is not a road, and that nobody asked the woman who keeps that room what the ditch goes to and that the record about the ditch says not asked.

Then a panel arrived in that yard at about half past eleven that nobody had asked for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard asked for it and that she wrote down what it said and not what anybody thought of it, and entered that a panel is a rule and a rule is not a document and is not a fourth and is not one of the six instruments this district built and not named.

> **A BUILDING WITH TWO DOORS IS A BUILDING THAT CAN BE EMPTIED. A BUILDING WITH ONE DOOR IS A BUILDING THAT CANNOT.**
>
> **THE PEOPLE WHO GO IN THROUGH THE SECOND DOOR WERE NOT ASKED AND HAVE NOT REFUSED AND CANNOT BE COUNTED EITHER WAY. THEY ARE IN THE BUILDING AND THEY ARE NOT IN THE FIGURE.**
>
> **A FIGURE THAT DOES NOT INCLUDE EVERYONE IN THE ROOM IS A FIGURE OF THE ROOM AND NOT OF THE PEOPLE.**

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam disagreed with the third of those three out loud, in front of about nine people, and the count came to a hundred and forty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The second one is our own rule and our own rule is right and I would not change a word of it. The third one is asking a figure to be a thing about people, and a figure cannot be a thing about people however you word it, and there is no wording of the third line that makes a number know who was in the room. You can put all nineteen of them in that figure or none of them and it is the same number standing in the same place, and the difference is not in the figure. The difference is in whether anybody asked them, and this yard has spent five months being the only place in this basin where anybody asks anybody, and it just occurred to me that we have been doing it in the front of the building.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who disagrees with a printed rule out loud in a yard is doing the thing this yard exists to make possible, and that a figure is not a person and a figure has no way of saying yes or no and that a number is not a column of people, and that the three documents this district does not own are three and a panel is not a fourth of them, and that nothing in that yard asked anybody to be in that building or out of it and that the record about the nineteen says not asked.

Nobody picked it up. A man of fifty-six said that the man who digs loam had disagreed with a printed rule in a yard for the second time in two months and that the second time was in a barn and the third time was in a yard and that the pattern was not going to be entered because a pattern is a figure about people and this yard does not do those.

Then the registrar of this district's records office said one thing from about four feet inside the gate, and it was counted, and it came to a hundred and twenty-three.

"**That building is not in my book and it is not in any book in this district and it is not going in one, and I want it said in this yard this morning that I have been asked about it once in nine years by a man who wanted to know whether a person could be put under a line for sleeping in it, and I said no, and he did not put one there, and that is the whole of the history of that building in this district. I am not a keeper of it. I have never been asked to be and if I was asked tomorrow I would have to think about it for longer than this morning has.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who has said out loud that she is not a keeper of a building has not refused a building and cannot be counted either way, that the record about the nine years says not asked, and that **a keeper has never been on a page in this basin and there never was one and no keeper is named.**

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing asked for eleven coppers a week, the same figure he has asked for since the second month, and a man of fifty-six refused him and gave the reason, which was that a yard is a room with no roof and this district still has no way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house, that the eight did not move at about two in the afternoon on the fifth of the sixth month, and that the district has now said no eight times and that a figure somebody says out loud in a yard and nobody counts is not a count.

He copied nothing that afternoon and a clerk entered that he did not, and entered that he was not asked to and that the record about his not copying says not asked.

The day came down about half past seven and the sixth month was five days old, and the plank door at the back of a building about two hundred yards down a lane was back on its hinges with a nail in the hasp, and a clerk entered that it was and that nobody said when it went back and that the record about the nail says not asked.

**AND ON THE FIFTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A MAN SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD THAT HE HAD BEEN WRONG ABOUT FIVE MONTHS OF WORK AND THAT HE HAD FOUND IT OUT AT THE BACK OF A BUILDING, AND A PANEL ARRIVED IN THAT YARD UNASKED AND WAS DISAGREED WITH BY THE MAN WHO DIGS LOAM IN THE THIRD OF ITS THREE LINES, AND NOBODY ASKED THE NINETEEN PEOPLE WHO SLEPT IN THAT ROOM ANYTHING AT ALL.**
