# Chapter 296: Ten Mornings

---

The sixteenth of the seventh month was the hottest day of that week and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right, and he read them out from about four feet back instead of from the end of the table.

"**Two hundred and four days on the board and five hundred and twenty days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and thirty-four, and a hundred and ninety-five days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is sixteen days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is eighty-seven days back, and the table against that wall stood up for the tenth time this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that sixteen is fifteen days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated and was not used this morning, and that ten is a round figure and that a round figure is a figure about a calculation and is not a figure about anybody, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood eighty-four days and nobody has sat in it on any of the eighty-four.

The woman of fifty-eight was at the bank with two buckets at about half past ten and stayed about an hour and went up the lane, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two buckets were not counted and that nobody counted them.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the end of the trestle table with his hand-cart against the wall inside the gate and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and he said one thing about the tenth morning, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to three hundred and nine, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about.

"**Ten mornings. I have been out there ten mornings since the seventh of this month and the tenth of them is the first one that is a number with a shape to it and I would like it entered that I noticed that and that I am not going to do anything about it. I am not going to take that table down. I said in this yard on the twelfth of this month that I would not and I have had four days to think about it and I have thought about it and that is where I have got to. A man who puts a table up every morning of a week is saying something with his body about that table. I cannot do it with my body. A man of about thirty-four carries it eleven miles on a shoulder and I get it off him with two forearms, and on the morning I cannot be there it is going to be a board against a wall with nobody putting it up, and that is a different thing from what I came down eleven miles of road to do, and I have not got a way of telling anybody in this basin the difference except to say it out loud here this afternoon and have a clerk write it down. I have a tent on that cart and I have had it rolled up since the thirtieth of last month and there is a field about two hundred yards across with a ditch at the far end of it and I am not going to put the tent up, because the first of my five lines says a table under a lean-to and a tent is not a lean-to and I would rather that line stay wrong on its face than have me make it true sideways.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who puts tables up is not a keeper of a table and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, **and that the eight did not move at about half past four in the afternoon on the sixteenth of the seventh month**, and that a tent is not a lean-to and a lean-to is not a wall four feet high and the three are not added together, and that a tent rolled on a hand-cart is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and that the six did not move on the sixteenth of the seventh month.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud in that yard for the second time in five days, standing about four feet back from the trestle table in the middle of the afternoon, because a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat had asked him to and had been asked nothing about it, and about halfway through the fourth of them he stopped, and sat down on the end of the trestle table, and sat there about nine minutes, and the chair at the end of that table had stood eighty-four days and nobody had sat in it on any of them.

He got up and finished the fourth and read the fifth, and then he said one thing about sitting down, in about four seconds, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that about nineteen people saw him sit down and that the record about the nineteen seeing it says not asked.
He was not the only one who had sat down in that yard that afternoon. Two women at the low wall along the lane had been sitting on it since about eleven, and about four people who had come for the reading stood about twenty feet off with nothing to do and ended up on the wall as well, and nobody at the end of that table said anything to any of them about it.

"**That is the second time this month I have come off that shoulder in front of about nineteen people, and it is the first one I have said out loud in a yard before it happened rather than after, and I have sat down on the end of a table and not in that chair because I am not going to be the first man to sit in that chair in this district, and I would like the two of those entered as two different things, because they are.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who sits down in a yard is not a keeper of a table and is not a bearer of a chair, and that a chair at the end of a trestle table is a chair and a table is a table and the two are not added together, and that a man sitting on the end of the table has not sat in it.

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the bank under the lean-to with her scale at about half past four in the afternoon, and about four people had come to be weighed, and she said one thing while she was doing it that nobody asked her for, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record about the not counting says not asked.

"**There are four houses of mine standing empty and they are not going to be offered again before the first day of the season after this one, and I said that out loud in a yard on the twentieth of last month and I have not said it since. A woman came to this scale this afternoon with a boy about four years old and she stood there while I weighed the sack and she said that she had heard there was a table standing eleven miles off that pays a person and she asked me whether I thought a person ought to take it, and I said I did not know, and then I said the thing I am going to have written down, which is that I have four empty houses and I am not going to put a woman who asks me that in one of them, and that is not an answer to her question and it is the only answer I have.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a house that stands empty is not a protected thing and did not become a sixth of them, and that the protected things in this district are five and that no sixth was added today, and that a woman who has said out loud that she does not know is not a refusal and cannot be counted either way, and that a person weighed at a scale is not a figure about anybody and that a sack is not a day.

At about half past five in the afternoon the man of about thirty-four who digs loam went down the lane behind the bank to the near end of it and stood at the top of the ditch behind the building with two doors and looked at it for about a minute without going down into it.

It was about three feet deep on the fourth of this month and there had been about a foot of standing water in the bottom of it that afternoon, and the plank door in the end wall of that building opens on it, and the nail in the hasp of that door had not moved on the eleventh of last month and nobody in this district has touched it in this block.
From the top of it the far end of the field looked the way it had looked for eleven days, which is to say like a field, and the standing water at the bottom of the cut was about a foot of it and clear enough to see the shape of the ditch in it. He stood at the top of it and did not go down, and there was nobody in that lane to see him not go down, and about a quarter past six he walked back up the lane past the bank on his own two feet with his right arm hanging.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a ditch is not a document and is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a man who stood at the top of a ditch for about a minute and did not go down into it has not refused the ditch and cannot be counted either way, and that nobody asked him whether he thinks that building is going to be shut and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this afternoon.

She entered the rest of what had not moved on the sixteenth of this month, in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody put a line on a board on any of the hundred and seven days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the eighty-six days since the twenty-first of the fourth month and she did not ask him for the figure. The refusals of eleven coppers a week are nine and the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight and neither moved today and the two are not the same figure. The documents this district does not own are three, and a sheet of five lines of print has been read out in that yard twice and is not a fourth of them, and the protected things are five and a tent is not a sixth and a figure of mornings is not a sixth and a place is not a sixth.

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was sixteen days old and the first day of the eighth month was sixteen days off, and a man had sat down on the end of a trestle table in front of about nineteen people and had not sat in the chair at the end of it.

**AND ON THE SIXTEENTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN SAID OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO TAKE HIS TABLE DOWN AND THAT A TENT WAS NOT A LEAN-TO AND THAT HE WOULD RATHER THE FIRST OF HIS FIVE LINES STAYED WRONG ON ITS FACE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAT DOWN ON THE END OF THE TRESTLE TABLE IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE AND DID NOT SIT IN THE CHAIR AT THE END OF IT.**
