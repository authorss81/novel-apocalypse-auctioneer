# Chapter 278: The Lot About A Room

---

The twenty-eighth of the sixth month came in bright and hot before nine and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there sixty-six days and nobody had sat in it on any of the sixty-six.

The three figures were on the end of that trestle table before the heat got into the yard, given by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and eighty-six days on the board and five hundred and two days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and sixteen, and a hundred and seventy-seven days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is thirty-four days off counted off this morning, and nobody has put a line on a board on any of the eighty-nine days since the first of the month before last, counting the first day in.**"

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing wrote it out longhand on a sheet about as wide as a page at the end of that trestle table at about ten in the morning, in his own hand, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he wrote it because he had been asked to and that nobody sent for him and that the record about the asking says not asked.

He had not been able to speak above a whisper since the twenty-fifth of this month and he could not hold a pen now either. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he got as far as the third of the four lines and that the fourth of the four lines is in the hand of the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and that a hand that finishes a sentence is not a bearer of the sentence and is not a keeper of it and did not become an instrument this district built and did not make a seventh of the six.

A clerk of nineteen years asked for it to be read, because a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked for it in the ordinary voice, and he read it out in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them and read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **A BELL IN AN OAK FRAME FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS WAS PULLED AND RANG ON THE NIGHT OF THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THIS MONTH AT ABOUT TWO IN THE MORNING.**
>
> **ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WENT INTO THE BUILDING AT THE NEAR END OF THE LANE BEHIND THE BANK AND WROTE THEIR OWN NAMES ON A PIECE OF PAPER ON A TABLE OUTSIDE IT, AND THAT PIECE OF PAPER IS IN A BOOK FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM THAT GATE.**
>
> **THIS IS A RECORD OF A ROOM AND NOT A RECORD OF A NIGHT. THE RECORD THAT BELL MADE IS NOT A PAPER. NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAS SEEN IT AND NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT IS ABLE TO TAKE IT OFF.**
>
> **THERE IS A PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT WHO HEARD THAT BELL AND IS NOT IN THAT PIECE OF PAPER AND IS NOT NAMED ON THIS SHEET AND HAS NOT BEEN ASKED ANYTHING BY ANYBODY.**

Nobody in that yard said anything for about nine seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet names no person and no place and no body, and that a lot in this district is an instrument and a document and that neither creates a consent and neither makes a person a bearer and neither makes a house into a party, and that a lot does not turn a right to give something up into a refusal, and that the nine seconds are not a consent and that the record about them says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a sheet that is made in a yard and read out in it and is on its face about a hole in itself is a document and is not a copy, and that the three documents this district does not own are three, and that this is not a fourth of them, because a document a man in this district wrote in his own hand in a yard in front of about nineteen people is a document this district owns, and that the piece of paper in the coat of a man of about thirty-four is a document in this district and is not one of the three either, and that the list of people in this district who are not in a household that a man of about forty-four took out of this district on the nineteenth of this month is not on either sheet and nobody in this district has seen it and nobody has counted it.

Then a man of fifty-six said the thing about the sheet, out loud, to about nineteen people, and it was counted and came to a hundred and ninety-three, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard was able to answer him and that the record about their not answering says not asked.

"**That is a hole with a reason printed under it and I want the yard to hear me say that I know exactly what shape that is, because a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said a thing about that shape in a room on the twenty-seventh of this month and I was in that room when he said it, and it is the third column of a covenant in a book a stranger may walk up and read four hundred yards from that gate, and that column has been ruled and empty since the nineteenth of the month before last because the people who would go in it have not been asked. This is a different hole. That one was left by nobody asking. This one is printed on purpose with the reason underneath it, and a clerk in this district said on the eighth of this month that a hole printed on a sheet is not the same shape of thing as a hole left there by nobody asking, and she was right, and I have thought about it for twenty days and I have still not got an answer.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a clerk who is not going to answer a thing is not a clerk who has found nothing, and that the record about the twenty days says not asked.

At about half past one in the afternoon the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came into that yard and said one thing about the table outside the building with two doors, and it was counted and came to a hundred and eighty-seven, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was for.

"**A table with four legs against a wall outside a door, a piece of chalk, and a piece of paper, and a man in the doorway to take the paper off. That is a counter. I have spent five months saying that this yard is not a counter and that a sheet at a gate in twenty days is not a market and that a room with about nine people in it and no name over the door is not a counter, and on the night of the twenty-fourth a man of about thirty-four stood in a doorway in a lane behind a bank and took a piece of paper off a table with four legs and put it in his coat, and that is a counter. A man taking a thing off a table on a night is a counter and not a door, and I have not got an argument against that and I have not got an argument for it either, and I am saying it in this yard in the middle of the afternoon so that nobody finds it out in six months.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a table with four legs is not a counter and is not a market and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own, and that a man who takes a thing off a table is not a keeper of it and is not a bearer of it and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a counter is a thing where a person is asked a question, and that the person at that table on the night of the twenty-fourth was not asked anything and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon the woman who keeps the books in the room four hundred yards from that gate came out into the lane in front of the office with the sheet about as wide as a page in her hand and read it once herself, standing up, in the ordinary voice, with nobody in that lane who had been asked to count her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet is to go into the first of the two books on that shelf and not into the second one, and that the second book has been open on that shelf since the sixth of this month, and that nobody in that lane has read it, and that the record about the not reading says not asked.

"**I have read that four times this afternoon and the third of its four lines is the one I have not been able to put down. It says the record that bell made is not a paper, and that nobody in this district has seen it and nobody in this district is able to take it off. I have kept a book in one room by one person for nine years and the reason that room is safe is that I know exactly what is in it. That sentence says out loud, in a yard, on a piece of paper, that this district has a thing in it that it cannot look at and cannot get out of. I have been in that room since before anybody in this district had a word for what I do, and I have never once had to say a thing like that about the room I keep. I am not putting it in that book tonight. I am going to write one more piece of paper tomorrow and I am going to read it out in that room and I would like a person who is not me to be in the room when I do.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who keeps a book in one room by one person is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not a steward and is not a party of anything, and that a registrar who has read a document out loud in a lane has not entered it anywhere and that the record about the entering says not asked, and that a registrar who has said out loud that she is not going to put a thing in her book tonight has not refused the thing and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not refusing says not asked.

And the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing back in that lane, and it was not counted, and the minute is in his hand and is not in a book.

"**Then I will be in the room, and I will be the only one in it who is not of this district's records office, and I will not write anything and I will not say anything about what is in that room, and if anybody in this basin asks me afterwards what was said in there I will say that a room with a shut door in it is not a room anybody may walk up to and that is the whole of my answer.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a conversation in a lane is not a document and did not become a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a man who has said out loud that he will not say what was said in a room has not refused the room and is not a keeper of it and is not a bearer of it and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything.

At about half past five in the afternoon the woman of fifty-eight came down the bank with two buckets and put them down where she puts them and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing carried one of them up, and she said one thing to the lane and not to him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record came from him and not from her.

"**I have carried water to eleven houses for nineteen years and I have been asked one question in all that time and I answered it on the nineteenth of this month and I have not slept properly since. I want it said out loud in a yard where strangers walk in that I do not know whether I went into that building on the night of the twenty-fourth, and I am not going to be asked, and anybody who works that out and writes it down is going to be writing down a thing about a woman of fifty-eight and not about a night.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who carries water to eleven houses is not a clerk of a house, and that a person who has said out loud that she does not know something has not refused to know it and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the two buckets were not counted and that nobody counted them.

The day came down about half past eight. The sixth month was twenty-eight days old, the first day of the month after next was thirty-four days off, and a sheet about as wide as a page with four lines on it in two hands was carried four hundred yards into the room where the books are at about half past six that evening by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and it was not in the book that night and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the record about the not putting it in says not asked.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A LOT WITH FOUR LINES ON IT IN TWO HANDS WAS WRITTEN OUT LONGHAND IN A YARD AND READ OUT AND READ BACK, AND THE FOURTH OF ITS FOUR LINES SAYS OUT LOUD THAT THERE IS A PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT WHO HEARD THAT BELL AND IS NOT IN THAT PIECE OF PAPER AND IS NOT NAMED ON IT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD THAT A TABLE WITH FOUR LEGS AGAINST A WALL WITH A MAN IN THE DOORWAY TAKING A PIECE OF PAPER OFF IT IS A COUNTER.**
