# Chapter 288: A Room With A Shut Door

---

The eighth of the seventh month was close and still and the three figures went out of the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning in the usual way, given by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and read off the board over his shoulder by the man of fifty-six, and about nineteen people were in that yard and none of them knew what was going to happen four hundred yards from it at four o'clock that afternoon.

"**A hundred and ninety-six days on the board and five hundred and twelve days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and twenty-six, and a hundred and eighty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-four days off counted off this morning, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and six days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that twenty-four is twenty-three days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning.

---

About nine people were in the room where the books are at about four in the afternoon with the door shut, and it is about nine feet by eleven with one table, one shelf, two books on that shelf, a chair, a door, and a fanlight over the door that lights nothing and has not lit anything in four months, and a clerk of nineteen years was four hundred yards away in a yard and was not told about it until about seven, and the record came from the man the figure of twenty-one years is against and not from the woman who keeps that room.

He said the room out loud before he said anything else, because he had learned that in this district last month, and about nine people heard him say it and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it in about nine seconds.

Then he made the offer, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and seventy-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I want to put both of those sheets into the book in this room. Not the second book, the first one, the one a stranger can walk in and read. The five lines and the four lines, both of them, with the day on them, exactly as they were read out in that yard. And I want the reason I am asking printed on the face of it in your hand, the same way you put the reason under that lot about a room on the twenty-ninth of last month, and I have brought the sheet for it and it is blank. Here is what I think it buys. That sheet is an attempt to pay the people in this district who are not in any household, and the attempt is wrong, and the fifth of its five lines says so out loud and the man who wrote it said out loud that he wrote it on purpose. A wrong thing that is on the record of this district is a thing a body four hundred miles off has to read before it does anything about it. Right now the second of those four lines is a rule nobody can point at, because there is no record anywhere in this basin of an offer having been made, and a rule about a place where nothing has ever happened has never been tested by anything. Put it in and it is tested. Put it in and a person who comes to that table in a season with the same shape of offer has got something to point at, and so does the woman who has to stand at it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a lot and a charter and an offer and a sheet of print are four different shapes of thing and are not added together, and that none of them creates a consent and none of them makes a person a bearer and none of them makes a house into a party, and that a man who has said out loud in a room that he wants a thing put in a book has not thereby been given the book and has not acquired a standing in it.

And she said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and the count came to two hundred and ninety-four, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked one question in that room on the twenty-seventh of last month and this was not it, and that nobody in that room said anything while she said it.

"**No. And I am going to say the reason in this room and not in that yard, and I want it written down that I have said it and not that I have been argued with. I have kept that book in this room nine years and I know exactly what is in it, every line, in the order the lines are in, and the reason that room is safe is not the shut door. The shut door is nothing. The reason that room is safe is that I know what is in it. If the fifth of those five lines goes on a page in that book with a day on it, then this basin has written down, in a place a stranger can walk up to, that eleven coppers a week was offered to people who are not in households on a morning in the seventh month, and the fifth line says a person who stood at that table once cannot be uncounted again. So the day goes in the book and the figure goes in the book and the third of those five lines, which is the only line on that sheet worth having, which says a person who stands at that table is not asked what they are called, is undone by the second sheet we would put in beside it. You would be publishing the morning. I am not refusing you because it is your book. It is my book, it is my hand, and it is my room, and I have been the only person in it for nine years and I am not going to be the person who made it the room a stranger comes into to find out who was poor in the seventh month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who keeps a book in one room by one person is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not a steward and is not a party of anything, and that a keeper of a room is not a thing she is and has never been asked to be, and that a woman who has refused a thing has refused that one thing and nothing else and cannot be counted either way about anything else, and that the columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to, and that the record about the not saying says not asked, and that the chair at the end of the trestle table in the yard of Lot Seventeen has stood seventy-six days and nobody has sat in it on any of the seventy-six, and that nobody has put a line on a board on any of the ninety-nine days since the first of the fourth month counting the first day in, and that the rival record has now been in force thirty-seven days and has been read out seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason and it is unanswered, and that the ninth of the nine printed nights is seventy-nine days back and nobody in this district can say whether that night happened.

And he did not take it back, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not take it back and that a man who has not withdrawn an offer has not bound anybody to it and has not acquired anything by having made it, and that the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about four in the afternoon on the eighth of the seventh month and that a man who has been refused eight things in five months and has not taken one of them back has acquired nothing by the eight.

He said one thing back and it was not counted, and the minute is in his hand and is not in a book, and about nine people in that room heard it.

"**Then I am not going to take it back and I am not going to let it go, and in five days it will be on the end of that trestle table in a yard a stranger can walk into whether you put it in that book or not, and it will be on the wall behind that yard and on nine other walls in four other settlements if I have to carry it there myself, and I am not asking you for anything this afternoon. I am telling you what is going to happen so that you cannot say afterwards that nobody told you. And I am not going to say in this room that you are wrong, because you are not. You have just told me the price of my own idea in a sentence and you were in that room when I learned it, and I have been wrong in this basin for five months in bigger ways than this one, and the last time I was wrong about a shape of a thing a man of about thirty-four told me about it in a yard and he was right and I did not do it for a fortnight.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a room that he is not going to say that another person is wrong has not agreed with her and has not refused her and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not taking it back says not asked, and that the second book on that shelf has been open since the sixth of last month and was not read this afternoon and nothing was entered in it, and that the record about the not reading says not asked.

And she said one thing back and it was not counted either, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that neither of the two sentences was counted and that nobody in that room had been asked to count them.

"**Then it will be in a yard and not in a book, and that is not the same thing and it is not nothing. I have not put my hand to it. I have not refused your hand. I am going to tell you the one thing I can do about a room, because I have not said it in five weeks and it is true. There is no room in this district that nobody can walk into, and I have not stopped saying that, and I said it to you in about nine feet of lane on the eighteenth of last month, and you shut this one for an hour and a half this afternoon and about nine people were in it and a clerk four hundred yards off did not know until about seven, and that is the whole of what a shut door buys. Put your two sheets in the yard. And do not come and ask me again this month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two people who have said out loud that they are not going to call a thing anything have not refused it and have not consented to it and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who has been told not to ask again this month has been asked to do something and has not been given anything, and that neither of them said a declaration and the record about the eight says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was in about nine feet of lane outside that door at about half past five in the afternoon and had been told nothing, and the woman who keeps the books in that room came out and told him one thing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record came from her and not from him and that the record about his not asking says not asked.

"**Two sheets and a blank one, and he has not asked you for anything out loud and you have not said yes to anything out loud, and about nine people were in that room and I have just been told one sentence of it at the door. I have spent five months in this basin saying that a document which says what it does not know is honest, and you have spent nine years keeping a book by knowing exactly what is in it, and those are the same sentence, and he has walked in there with the third version of it and asked you to make it a fourth. I am not going to say whether he was right. I was in that yard on the second when he had five lines read out and I have not heard the case for putting them in a book and I am not going to say he was wrong until somebody reads it to me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a book and is not a keeper of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to say whether another man was right has not agreed with him and has not disagreed with him and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not saying says not asked, and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight and did not move on the eighth of the seventh month.

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was eight days old and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-four days off, and about nine people had been in a room with a shut door in it four hundred yards from a yard a stranger can walk into, and two of them had not said anything that either of them was going to call anything.

**AND ON THE EIGHTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN ASKED THE WOMAN WHO KEEPS THE BOOKS IN THE ROOM FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM THAT GATE TO PUT TWO SHEETS INTO THE BOOK A STRANGER MAY WALK UP TO AND READ, AND SHE REFUSED IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND SAID THAT IT WAS HER BOOK AND HER HAND AND HER ROOM AND THAT HE WOULD BE PUBLISHING A MORNING, AND HE DID NOT TAKE THE OFFER BACK.**
