# Chapter 283: The Fifth Thing

---

The third of the seventh month was close and low and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came into the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning with a copy of those five lines rolled inside his coat and about four people walked out from the top of the bank with him, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table before any of them got to it.

"**A hundred and ninety-one days on the board and five hundred and seven days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and twenty-one, and a hundred and eighty-two days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-nine days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is seventy-four days back, and a man went out to the far side of the eleven mile road yesterday on his own two feet and came back last night, and the chair at the end of this table has stood seventy-one days and nobody has sat in it on any of the seventy-one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that twenty-nine is twenty-eight days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a day a man is not in this district is not a day he has been here, and that a man who walks eleven miles and comes back has not been in a household and has not been anywhere else and the record about the not sending says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam put the rolled sheet on the end of that trestle table and unrolled it and said what he had found, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and ninety-eight, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was for.

"**The fifth of those five lines names a lean-to on the far side of the eleven mile road. There is no lean-to on the far side of the eleven mile road. There is a wall about four feet high and a gate in it that is not on a hinge and a field behind that wall about two hundred yards across with a ditch at the far end of it, and the ditch is about three feet deep in the seventh month where it was about two feet deep in the sixth, and I have put my hand down in it and there is about a foot of mud in the bottom of it that was not there a month ago. I walked there and back in about eleven hours on two legs and I dug for about an hour at the wall and I am telling you this yard what that cost before I say anything else, because I have got a hand that has not closed since the eleventh of last month and I had it in a ditch yesterday for an hour and I could not get it out the way I got it in.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a body is not a figure about a man and that a hand in a ditch is not a price of anything and is not a consent, and that the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning on the third of the seventh month.

Then the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them named the price out loud in that yard, to about nineteen people, in about four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had been asked for it once and had said in this yard on the first of this month that he would not put a price on it until somebody asked him twice, and that this was the second time and that nobody had asked him on anybody's behalf.

"**Eleven coppers a week, and it is the same eleven coppers a man with a case paid a man of about twenty-three in this district on the thirteenth of last month, and I have heard that figure on four days of road and I have not been given a reason for it and I am not going to give one. I am saying it here because I said on the first that I would not, and because a price said out loud in a yard is a price somebody can refuse and a price in a letter is not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that eleven coppers a week is a price a man has named out loud and is not a rate in this basin, and that there is still no rate in this district that turns a year into coppers and there never has been one, and that a price is a thing a named person pays under a term and a party is not a person and a figure is not a person and has no way of saying no.

And the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and the count came to a hundred and eighty-eight.

"**No. And I want it entered that I was the worst man in this district for that to have been said in front of me this morning, because I have got a hand that cannot hold a pen shut and I am the last person in this basin who ought to be the man who says the thing about a table and a count. I am saying it anyway, because that is the fourth time I have said something in a yard that my own body made hard, and I have stopped counting those. The fifth of those five lines says a person who has stood at that table once cannot be uncounted again. You are not offering me eleven coppers. You are offering me eleven coppers and a morning I have been in, and there is not a door in this basin I can go out of afterwards, and I have been in about four of them in five months and I am not going to be in another one, and I am not going to be one of a number of anything at a table either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the refusals of eleven coppers a week in this district are nine, and that this is the ninth, and that it moved from eight this morning because a refusal was made out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people by a person who gave a reason, and that **the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight and did not move, and a refusal with a reason is not one of those eight, and a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and the nine and the eight are not the same figure and are not added together, and neither figure is a ninth or a tenth of the other.**

Nobody in that yard said a word for about nine seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the nine seconds are not a consent and are not a refusal, and that nobody in that yard was asked what they were waiting for.

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, who has stood at that scale nine years and weighs about nine sacks a day, said one word out loud that nobody had asked her for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said it before anybody asked her and that the record about her not being asked says not asked.

> **RATE.**

She said it twice, which she had not done before in this yard, and then she said the rest of it in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to two hundred and eighteen, and a clerk entered that nobody argued with her about it.

"**I have said one word out loud in this yard twice in five weeks and both times it was a piece of furniture and both times somebody had already put one in the ground. This one is not a piece of furniture and I would like it entered that I said it before it was asked for. Eleven coppers a week is not a price. A price is a thing you are told and a rate is a thing that is already true of you whether anybody says it or not, and if that man stands a table up eleven miles off and counts the people who come to it, then in four months there is no such thing as a person in this district who is not in a household, because there will be a figure every week about how many of them there are and it will not be a price anybody agreed to. I weigh about nine sacks a day and I have never weighed a day. I have four empty houses standing up that lane and I said on the twentieth of last month they are not going to be offered again. I am not standing at a table to be one of a figure and I am saying so before anybody asks me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a scale is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a figure, and that a woman who has said a word twice has not thereby made a finding and that the record about the second saying says not asked, and that the columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to.

Then a man of fifty-six said one thing about the fourth of those five lines and the nine houses, and it was counted and came to a hundred and sixty, and a clerk entered that it was the second time in three days that he had said a figure out loud and that nobody in that yard had a figure back for him.

"**Nine houses in one lane paying for five months. I have been in this basin fifty-six years and I know nine houses in a lane when I am told about them and I am not going to say out loud in this yard which ones, because the man who wrote that line out did not put them on a sheet and neither am I. What I will say is that four hundred and eleven is a count of the people who answered a door, and if there is a table eleven miles off that counts the people who come to it, then in about five months somebody four hundred miles away is going to hold two counts of the same people and the two will not be the same figure, and neither of them will be a lie, and there will be no way on this side of either one of them to say which of them is about people.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who says out loud that he will not name nine houses has not refused to name them and cannot be counted either way, and that no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved on the third of the seventh month.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered the rest of the things that had not moved, in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph. She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the ninety-four days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood seventy-one days and nobody has sat in it on any of the seventy-one, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the seventy-three days since the twenty-first of the fourth month and that she did not ask him and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the boards in this district are five and the lines that are full are six with a seventh ruled and empty at the ninth place, and that the rival record has been in force thirty-two days and has been read out seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud on the twenty-fourth of last month with a reason and it is unanswered.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was three days old and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-nine days off, and a copy of a sheet with five lines on it was rolled inside a coat belonging to a man of about thirty-four standing at the end of that trestle table, and a curtain was on a nail in the end wall of a building with two doors, and the record about both of those says not asked.

**AND ON THE THIRD OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN NAMED ELEVEN COPPERS A WEEK OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM REFUSED IT IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS WITH A REASON, AND THE REFUSALS OF ELEVEN COPPERS A WEEK ARE NINE, AND THE REFUSALS WITH NO REASON A CLERK OF A HOUSE HAS GIVEN ARE EIGHT, AND THE TWO ARE NOT THE SAME FIGURE.**
