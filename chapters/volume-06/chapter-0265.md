# Chapter 265: One Copy Of Anything

---

The fifteenth of the sixth month was low and warm and about eleven people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there fifty-three days and nobody had sat in it on any of the fifty-three.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and seventy-three days on the board and four hundred and eighty-nine days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and three, and a hundred and sixty-four days separate the second of January and this morning, and there is a sheet on this table that was not on it at half past six yesterday evening and nobody put it there.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet was face up on the trestle table and had not been there the evening before, and that nobody put it there, and that a sheet which arrives in a yard is a document somebody owns and is not one of the three this district does not own and did not become a fourth of them.

A clerk of nineteen years asked for it to be read, because a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read it out in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them and read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **A YARD IN WHICH THINGS ARE READ OUT IS A YARD IN WHICH THINGS ARE KEPT.**
> **ONE COPY OF ANYTHING ENTERED IN IT IS TO GO TO THE BODY THAT SENT THIS SHEET.**
> **THE COLUMN FOR THE PERSON WHO KEEPS THAT YARD IS EMPTY ON THIS SHEET AND WAS EMPTY WHEN IT WAS PRINTED.**
> **NOBODY HAS TO BE ASKED FOR THIS. IT IS A LINE OF PRINT AND NOT A QUESTION.**

Nobody in that yard said anything for about nine seconds. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet carries no name of a person and no name of a body and no name of a place, and that it prints a column and says in print that the column is empty, and that the fourth of its four lines says out loud that nobody has to be asked, and that the sheet that came into this yard on the eighth of this month also had a job in the middle of it and a hole at the bottom of it, and that this one has added a sentence about the hole, and that a clerk who has now had two sheets inside eight days with a hole at the bottom of them is going to keep having them and is not going to make a figure of it.

A clerk entered that a column for the person who keeps a yard is not a column of not-askings, and that the columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to, and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that a sheet which says in print that nobody need be asked is not a person asking, and that a yard going quiet for about nine seconds is not a refusal and is not a consent, and that the record about the nine seconds says not asked.

Then a man of fifty-six said the sentence he is going to have to live with for the rest of the month, and it was counted and came to a hundred and fifty-five, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard said it after him and that the record about who agreed with it says not asked.

"**On the eighth of this month I stood in this yard and said that a printed rule is better than mine because mine needed somebody in the room to agree with it and that one does not need anybody anywhere, and I said it like a man who has won something. I have been pleased about it for seven days. I have been pleased about it while a man with a case sat at that end of that table and I have been pleased about it while a man wrote down a list of people who are not in a household, and being pleased about it is the reason both of those things could happen in this yard and go on happening in it, and I would like somebody to write down that a man of fifty-six said that out loud in front of about eleven people and did not make a figure of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has admitted out loud that he was pleased about a thing is not a finding and is not a reason for anything, and that the record about the seven days says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-one who digs loam said the other half of the morning, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and sixty-nine.

"**Two sheets in seven days and not one name on either of them, and I want to say in this yard that the second of those two is the best piece of printing anybody has put up in this basin in my lifetime, and I want to say it without anybody standing up afterwards and telling me I have said a stupid thing. I have spent five months in this yard saying that a document which names nothing is the only safe kind there is, and I was right about that in the same way a man is right about the shape of a hole. That first sheet shut a building by naming a job. This one has taken the person out of the job altogether and printed the reason on the face of it, and a man four hundred miles away has worked out that it does not need anybody at all, and I have got no answer to that and I have not got one this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam has said a thing out loud in a yard and that his right hand is still not right after the ditch behind the building with two doors on the eleventh of this month, and that a man who says a thing out loud has not agreed with the person who printed it, and that the record about his not having an answer says not asked, and that the record about what is in a yard in this district is a record about a yard and not about anybody.

---

At about half past three the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households came into that yard on his own two feet and put a book on the trestle table face down as he does, and a clerk of nineteen years asked him for his figure, which she had not done in five months, and he said no and gave a reason, and the count came to a hundred and thirty-nine.

"**No, and here is the reason and it is a new reason and I am telling you that it is new. I have said that figure four times this month and I said on the first of it that I would say it once and not again and I have gone past my own rule twice, and I am not going past it a fifth time in a yard where a man with a case is sitting at the end of the table buying lists of people who are not in a household. A figure of mine in a yard with that man in it is a figure of mine in his list, and he does not have to ask me a second time, and I am not going to be a man who has to be asked twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the six households of that man have not moved on any of the fifty-five days since the twenty-first of the month before last, being one signed, two refused and three not asked again, and that the figure is his and that a clerk did not get it out of him this afternoon and has it from the four days he did say it on, and that a figure a man will not repeat is not a refusal and is not a consent, and that a man who keeps a count of six households is not a clerk of a house and is not one of the eight, and that the eight did not move at about half past three in the afternoon on the fifteenth of the sixth month.

And the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road, who keeps four miles of that road and is not the tally keeper and is not the man who digs loam and is not the man who mends fencing, walked the whole of it in the middle of the afternoon on his own two feet and told the nine houses on it, in his own words and not in the six things, that there is a man with a case at the yard of Lot Seventeen buying lists of people who are not in a household, and not one of the nine asked him to.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and has never been asked to be one, and that a man who walks four miles in an afternoon to tell nine houses a thing they did not ask him for has not been paid and has not been asked to be paid and is not a bearer of anything, and that the record about the nine houses saying nothing says not asked, and that a refusal to say thank you is not a refusal and is not one of the eight and did not move the eight.

At about five the man of about forty-four put a slate on the end of that trestle table and a piece of chalk beside it and wrote nothing on the slate, and about four people asked him in turn what the slate was for, and he said it was for a person to come and put a name on. Then he counted eleven coppers out onto the end of that table in front of about eleven people, one at a time, and a man of about nineteen who counts counted them and got eleven.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a slate on a table is not a page and is not a document and is not a copy and is not a fifth instrument, and that a person who puts a name on a slate in a yard a stranger can walk into has not been asked to and has not refused, and that the record about anybody who has put a name on it says not asked, and entered that eleven coppers a week is a figure about a man and not a rate, and that a rate is a thing a named person pays under a term, and that there is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and there never has been one, and that a figure somebody says out loud in a yard and nobody counts is not a count, and that this one was counted.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing took the second sheet out to the gatepost at about half past five and nailed it there at about the height of a man's shoulder, and the first sheet that had been on that gatepost, which is the one about four hundred and eleven people, is not on it because it has been in the inside pocket of a coat since the first of this month. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a gatepost with a nail in it is not a book and is not a page and is not an address, and that a gatepost with two different things nailed to it inside ten days is a gatepost, and that a man who has read a sheet out loud in a yard and then nailed it to a gatepost has not turned that yard into a place, and that the record about whether this yard is a place says not asked.

The day came down about half past seven. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a yard a stranger can walk into is now an address that two sides want, the first being a man with a case who has been given the end of a trestle table and the second being a body four hundred miles away that has printed an empty column for the person who keeps it, and that a sheet at a gate in ten days is not a market and that a man with a slate is not a counter and that neither of those is the yard, and that the record about what the yard is says not asked.

**AND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A SECOND SHEET CAME INTO THE YARD IN THE NIGHT WITH FOUR LINES ON IT AND THE FOURTH OF THE FOUR SAID OUT LOUD THAT NOBODY HAS TO BE ASKED, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT HE HAD BEEN PLEASED ABOUT A PRINTED RULE FOR SEVEN DAYS WHILE A MAN BOUGHT A LIST, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-EIGHT REFUSED TO SAY A FIGURE THAT WAS HIS BECAUSE HE HAD WALKED PAST HIS OWN RULE TWICE ALREADY THIS MONTH.**
