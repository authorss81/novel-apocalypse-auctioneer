# Chapter 300: The Fourteenth Morning

---

The twentieth of the seventh month came up white and still and the air in the yard of Lot Seventeen was already warm at eight, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and about nineteen people were in that yard, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there eighty-eight days and nobody had sat in it on any of them.

"**Two hundred and eight days on the board and five hundred and twenty-four days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and thirty-eight, and a hundred and ninety-nine days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twelve days off counted off this morning, being eleven days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is ninety-one days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and eighteen days, and the table against that wall stood up for the fourteenth time this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that twelve is the last of ten figures counted off ten mornings, and that the ten of them are twenty-one, twenty, nineteen, eighteen, seventeen, sixteen, fifteen, fourteen, thirteen and twelve, and that the first of those ten was counted off the eleventh of this month and not off the tenth of this month, which is the last day in the prose of the ten before them.

The yard filled the way it had filled every morning of that block. By eleven the shade off the east wall had come round far enough to reach the trestle table, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had put his back to the sun and about nine people were reading over his shoulder, and the sheet of five lines of print was still on the end of that table where a hand-cart had put it down on the twelfth of this month, the corners gone soft from being picked up and put down again.

She entered that the instruments this district has built and not named are six, and that eight things have been put in front of her in the ten days of this block and entered as not a seventh of them: a tent rolled on a hand-cart, a table left standing against a wall, a figure of mornings, a sheet of five lines of print, a fifth line of that sheet which is wrong on its face, a copy nobody asked him for, a habit, and a place. She entered that no seventh of them was proposed on any of the ten days and that the six did not move on any of them, and that a thumb laid on a line is not a signature and a queue is not a counter, and the two are not added together.

Then a man at the far end of that table asked the yard, in the ordinary voice and not to anybody in particular, whether anybody in this district had been paid anything for standing at a table eleven miles off this morning. Nobody answered him for long enough that he asked it again, and a woman by the gate said out loud that nobody has been paid, and the man said all right and went back to the fifth line.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in any household, and that its name was said out loud in that yard on the third of this month by a man who has it to sell, and that saying it did not make it, **and that on the fourteenth morning of that table the fifth of the five had still not been paid, and that twelve days are left, and that a clerk has entered the number of days without entering what is going to happen in them.**

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam put his right hand up to about the height of his shoulder in that yard at about eleven in the morning, and could not get it above that, and said one thing about it out loud, and it was counted and came to two hundred and twenty-seven, and a clerk entered that about nineteen people saw him do it and that he had done the same thing on the eleventh of this month and that nobody in that yard said anything about either morning.

"**That arm has not gone above the shoulder on any of the twenty days of this month and I am going to say the rest of it now because I have been carrying it since the eleventh of this month and I am not going to be the man who finds out on the twenty-first morning at about eight o'clock that I cannot carry a board eleven miles. I have carried that table out four times this month. I cannot do it on the twenty-first and I cannot do it on the twenty-second, and there is nobody else in this district who can, and the man it belongs to cannot either, and I have not told him that this morning and I am not going to tell him this afternoon and I would like that entered as two things about me and not as a thing about him. So there is going to be a board against a wall about four feet high eleven miles off this district tomorrow morning at about eight o'clock, and it is going to be there because two men put it down on the eleventh of this month, and nobody is going to put it up, and that is not anybody's fault and it is not a refusal and it is not a market and it is not a count of anything.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who digs loam is not a keeper of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he cannot do a thing tomorrow has not refused it and cannot be counted either way, and that a body is not a figure about a man and an arm is not a price of anything, and that the record about the not telling him says not asked, and that a table is not a document and the two are not added together.

At about one the heat came up off the floor of that yard and about half of it went and sat down on the low wall along the lane, and the man of fifty-six stayed where he was and read the board over his shoulder twice more for the people who had not heard it in the morning, and got it right both times. The second time, a man asked him why he reads a figure out at a gate when there is a clerk four hundred yards off with all of it in a book, and he said that a figure somebody says out loud is a figure and a figure nobody says out loud is a figure in a book, and that the difference between those two was the whole of what he had ever done at that gate.

She entered the rest of what had not moved in ten days in the same hand and not as a paragraph, and she did it in nine lines rather than the whole of it, because a clerk enters a thing where the thing happened and nine of the twelve had already gone in on the day they happened. Three documents this district does not own. Five protected things. Four conditions with no end on it. Thirteen refusals to join two things and the registrar's own count of twelve. Nine refusals to read. Five boards and six lines full. Four columns of not-askings. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week. Eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given. Seven refusals about the ninth holding. Twelve figures, and not one of them moved in ten days. **She entered that the fourth line of the offer that has been on a table in this district for five months was not read out on any of the ten days of this block, and that the five lines on the man's own sheet and the five lines of print are not that offer, and that a refused eighth reading of the rival record is not one of the nine, and that a tent is not a sixth protected thing and a habit is not a sixth and a place is not a sixth and a copy is not a sixth.** She entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the eight and a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that the seven and the eight and the nine are three different figures and are not added together.

She entered then the figures that are about days rather than about people. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and eleven days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood eighty-eight days and nobody has sat in it on any of the eighty-eight, and a man sat down on the end of that table on the sixteenth of this month and not in the chair, and those are two different things. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the ninety days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that figure out of a ladder and did not ask him for it on any of the ten days of this block, and he said it on none of them, and he is a man of about forty-eight and not thirty-four and has said of himself that he is fifty-one. There is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and there never has been one, and eleven coppers a week is a price a man named out loud and is not a rate. The rival record has been in force forty-nine days and has been read out in that yard seven times, and the eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of last month, and nobody has answered it.

She entered that **no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved on any of the ten days of this block, and that the figure on it is four hundred and eleven, and that it was arrived at by counting the people who answered a door, and that two men in that yard have said out loud in ten days that there is a figure on that sheet and there is a figure at that table, and that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the half of that sentence on the thirteenth and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the half of it on the nineteenth, and that neither of them said the rest, and that the record about the halves that were not said says not asked.**

She entered that this district has five different ninths in it, and that the ninth of the ten days this block ran fell on the nineteenth of this month, and that the other four are the ninth of the nine charters, the ninth of the eleven houses a woman of fifty-eight carries water to, the ninth of the nine printed nights and the ninth place on the page out of the cave, which is ruled and empty and was not filled; **that they are five ninths and are not added together, and that a sixth was available to her this morning and she did not take it, and that a clerk who could have made a sixth and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing.**

The men who are not in this district went into the book in three lines and not a paragraph. The man of about thirty at a counter is at the sixteenth time down that road and the next is the seventeenth, and this district has never given him a day to be here on. The man of about forty-one who keeps a road house at the top of the road that climbs has no day in this district and never had one. The man with the portfolio is not in this district and nothing in that yard says where he is. **A man of about forty-four with a case went out of that gate at about two in the afternoon on the nineteenth of last month with a list of the people in this district who are not in a household, and that list is not on the offer and not on the five lines of print and not in this district, and nobody in this district has seen it and nobody has counted it.**

And a man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them came down the eleven mile road on the thirtieth of last month with a hand-cart and a tent rolled on it and gave no name to anybody, and has not been asked for one. **A woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book has not been in this district on any of the ten days of this block, and nobody in this district has said one word about her in any of the ten, and the record about the not saying says not asked.**
About half of that yard was gone by four. The sheet of five lines of print was still on the end of the trestle table with about four people reading it, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had been asked twice that afternoon what a queue for a table would do when there was no table to queue for and had given the same answer twice, which was that he did not know and would not guess in a yard.

And then the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them said the last thing of the ten days in that yard at about half past four in the afternoon, to about nineteen people, and it was counted and came to two hundred and seventy, and a clerk entered that a clerk was four hundred yards away and got it from a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and not from the woman who keeps that room, and that the record about the four hearing it says not asked.

"**I came down eleven miles of road on the thirtieth of last month with a hand-cart and a tent rolled on it, and on the first of this month I asked this yard for its address and this yard did not say the word, and I have not asked it since. On the second a man read five lines of my own out in this yard and a man read them back and got nothing wrong, and the fifth of those five is not true and I wrote it there on purpose. On the seventh I put a table against a wall eleven miles off and one person stood at it at about eight in the morning and I have not described that person to this district and I am not going to. On the twelfth a sheet came into this yard with five lines on it instead of four and the second of them was about a place where a sheet has been read out, and nobody in this district asked for that line, and that is the only thing that has happened in ten days that nobody here asked for. A table is standing against that wall and it is going to be standing there tomorrow morning at eight and nobody is going to put it up. I have got no third thing. I said in this yard on the fifth of this month that I had got no third thing and I have not got one this afternoon either, and I have stopped saying that as though saying it were a way of doing something about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says out loud that he has stopped saying a thing as though saying it were a way of doing something about it has not refused the thing and has not consented to it and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not saying says not asked, and that a yard that is the address of two sides is a yard and not a market, and that a market is a place where a person is asked a question, and that that yard has now not asked anybody in it a question about what they want in any of the ten days of this block and that nobody in it has been refused by it, because a yard cannot refuse anybody.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was twenty days old and the first day of the eighth month was twelve days off, and about four hundred and eleven people in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off this district were to be moved on or before it, and no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved in ten days, and a table was standing against a wall about four feet high eleven miles off with nobody going to put it up in the morning, and a book was four hundred yards away with a shut door on the room it is in and nobody had been asked to write in it.

**AND ON THE TWENTIETH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM SAID OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN THAT HE COULD NOT CARRY A TABLE ELEVEN MILES ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OR THE TWENTY-SECOND, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN SAID OUT LOUD IN THE SAME YARD THAT HE HAD GOT NO THIRD THING AND THAT HE HAD STOPPED SAYING THAT AS THOUGH SAYING IT WERE A WAY OF DOING SOMETHING ABOUT IT, AND TWELVE DAYS WERE LEFT AND A CLERK ENTERED THE NUMBER OF DAYS WITHOUT ENTERING WHAT WAS GOING TO HAPPEN IN THEM.**
