# Chapter 264: What She Would Take

---

The fourteenth of the sixth month was bright and still and about eleven people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there fifty-two days and nobody had sat in it on any of the fifty-two.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and seventy-two days on the board and four hundred and eighty-eight days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and two, and a hundred and sixty-three days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is forty-eight days off counted off this morning, and a man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart took nine sacks off the end of that table at about six this morning for a man with a case and nobody asked him to and he says he is not going to be asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart is a man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart and is not a man of about nineteen who counts and is not a woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room with nothing over the door, and that the three of them are three people in three trades inside two miles of each other in this district, and that the trade is the identifier and never carries the identification alone, and that a man who hauls a thing for somebody else carries it and is not a bearer of it and has not been asked to be one.

---

At about two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked the two miles to a lane with no name on anything and a building on it, on his own two feet, and nobody sent for him, and a clerk entered that the record about the walking says not asked.

The room behind that building is about nine feet by fourteen and there is nothing over the door. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room has kept it for four years and there were about nine people in it and nobody raised their voice. There is a bench about nine feet long with about eleven notches cut in the underside of the seat at intervals that are not even, and they are not a calendar and are not a count of nights and are not a count of people, and the woman who keeps that room has said so out loud before anybody asked her.

He told her the third of the three lines on the card of the man of about forty-four, in his own words and not in the six things, and she put her piece of chalk down on the bench and picked it up again and said one thing about it, and it was counted and came to a hundred and fifty-five.

"**I said no to that on the seventh of this month before I was asked and I gave you the reason and you wrote it down, and the reason was that if the fifth of your five things was going to be the fifth of mine then somebody would come and ask me what I would take for it. He has come. He has not come to me, which is the part I did not think of. A man at a house on this lane told him there was a room behind the building where people slept some nights, and she told him because nobody has ever told her that room is worth keeping, and I am not blaming her and I am not going to, and I have been sitting here since four o'clock this afternoon working out that everything I said on the seventh was right and none of it was any use.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a room is not a clerk of a house, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that the eight did not move at about four in the afternoon on the fourteenth of the sixth month, and that a house that has told a man with a case where a room is has not been entered anywhere, and that the record about that house says not asked, and that refusing a man is not the same as stopping a list and that the two are not added together.

She refused the man of about forty-four himself in about four seconds when he came to that lane at about half past six, and gave a reason, and the count came to a hundred and thirty-nine.

"**No, and I will not tell you which nine of them sleep here, and I will not tell you how many of them there are, and I am not going to give you the lane, and there is a bench in this room with eleven cuts in the underside of it and you can have the bench. The list you are holding was made before I knew you were on this lane, and I have found that out tonight, which means that everything I am saying now is too late to be worth anything to you and is worth something to the nine, and that is the only arithmetic in this room and I would like it noticed that I have been doing arithmetic for four years and this is the first sum of it that came out wrong.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move, and that a bench with eleven notches cut in the underside of the seat is not a copy and is not a list and is not a fifth document and is not a seventh instrument of the six, and that eleven notches are eleven and are not a figure about anybody, and that the record about the nine says not asked.

---

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat, who is at the third house up the lane from the scale and who has kept a goat for eleven years, said no to a third thing before she was asked, at about half past six in the evening at the foot of her own gate, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing wrote the words down in a clerk's hand and read them back to her and she said she had got them, and she said them once and said she was not going to say them twice, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said them in nineteen words.

> **I HAVE SAID NO TO A MAN WITH A CASE AND HE IS NOT A MAN WITH A SLATE**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a goat is not a clerk of a house, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about half past six in the evening on the fourteenth of the sixth month, and that a woman who has kept a goat for eleven years and has held a bucket and a rope and a kid and nothing heavier than about nine pounds since the spring is not a bearer of a list and is not one of the eight.

At about seven, in the lane behind the bank, the woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book said no to a third thing in about four seconds, and gave a reason, and the count came to a hundred and thirty-four.

She had come down four miles in the afternoon, and a clerk entered that she had come on her own two feet and that nobody sent for her and that she had not been in that district for eleven months a fortnight ago and has not been asked a first thing since, and that going is not arriving and that this district has never once given that woman a day to be here on.

He had asked her, in his own words and not in the six things, whether anybody who keeps a route in this district is on a list of people who are not in a household.

"**No, and I will tell you what I am not going to tell you, which is that if a man is hauling sacks for a man with a case then that man is a person who is not in a household by the ordinary meaning of those words, and if his name is on a list then a route is a thing somebody can buy, and I have spent nine years making sure there is nothing in this basin that can be bought that walks. You have had two men asking me questions this month and I have said no to both, and this is the third, and I would like a clerk to write down that I said no in about four seconds and that I did not have to think about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a route is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a route and has never been asked to be one, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about seven in the evening on the fourteenth of the sixth month, and that the man of about forty-four was refused out loud three times in two days in three trades, and that a man refused in three trades in two days is not a man this district has agreed anything with, and that he is a man outside this district and is not a clerk of a house and is not one of the eight.

The wire on the left of that lane took about two fingers off the inside of her left shin in the same place it took them on the fourth of this month, and she said nothing about it, and she did not limp, and a clerk entered that she said nothing and that the record about the wire says not asked, and that a cut is not a figure about anybody and is not a finding and is not a reason for anything.

---

Back in the yard at about half past eight the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing about his own hand, in the ordinary voice and to about eleven people, and it was counted and came to a hundred and seventy, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him a follow-up question.

"**This left hand has not been right since the first of this month. It took me four goes and a fifth to get that gate open on the first and it took me about nine seconds to get my fingers round a nail in a hasp on the fifth and I have not said a word about it to anybody in fourteen days because a man who mends fencing in this district does not go about with his hand out. I am saying it now because a man with a case is buying lists of people who are not in a household, and I have spent five months being the man in this yard who asks the questions, and a man who asks the questions and cannot hold what he is asking about is a man somebody else can use, and I would rather everybody knew that about me on the fourteenth of this month than have it worked out on some later day by somebody who was not me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he said that, and that a hand that is not right is a hand and is not a figure about anybody, and that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not one of the eight, and that the eight did not move, and that the record about the nine days says not asked, and that a man who has said out loud that he can be used is not the same as a man who has been used and that the two are not added together.

The day came down about half past nine, which was late, and the man of about forty-four with a case was still at the end of the trestle table with a slate on it and nothing written on the slate, and a clerk entered that a slate with nothing on it is not a page and is not a document and is not a copy and did not become a fourth of the three documents this district does not own.

**AND ON THE FOURTEENTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WALKED TWO MILES TO A ROOM WITH NOTHING OVER THE DOOR BECAUSE A WOMAN AT A HOUSE ON THAT LANE TOLD HIM WHERE IT WAS, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A GOAT SAID NO TO A THIRD THING AND WOULD NOT SAY IT TWICE, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO KEEPS A ROUTE REFUSED A THIRD THING IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS, AND A MAN SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD THAT HE HAS BEEN WRONG ABOUT HIS OWN HAND FOR NINE DAYS.**
