# Chapter 275: The Sheet On The Table

---

The twenty-fifth of the sixth month came up grey and cool after a night that had been close, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there sixty-three days and nobody had sat in it on any of the sixty-three.

Three figures went out of that yard the way they have gone out every morning of this month, from the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and the man of fifty-six had the board over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and eighty-three days on the board and four hundred and ninety-nine days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and thirteen, and a hundred and seventy-four days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is thirty-seven days off counted off this morning, and the bell was rung about two in the morning on the night of the twenty-fourth and about nine people in that lane were inside a building with two doors by about nine minutes after it started.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a bell that has been rung records everybody who hears it for one night, and that this district does not know the figure of that night and has never known it, and that a figure this district does not have is not a figure about anybody and is not a finding.

A clerk of nineteen years asked for the sheet to be read, because a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked for it in the ordinary voice at about ten in the morning, and a clerk entered that he asked for it and that nobody asked on his behalf.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing read it out at the end of the trestle table in about nine minutes. It is a piece of paper about as wide as a page folded in four and unfolded, and there are nine names on it in nine different hands, and one of the hands is a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale and one of the hands is a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat, and he read all nine out loud in the ordinary voice of a man who is not reading well, and he read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a piece of paper with nine names on it in nine hands is a document and is not a copy, and that the three documents this district does not own are three and that this is the fourth thing to be made or to arrive in this district in twenty-five days, and that a sheet in a coat in a lane is not a document nobody owns, and that a document a person in this district wrote in their own hand and a person in this district is carrying is not a document nobody owns, and that a list of people in this district who are not in a household that a man of about forty-four took out of this district on the nineteenth of this month is not on that sheet and is not one of these three and nobody in this district has seen it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a name on a piece of paper in a yard a stranger can walk into is a name, and that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, and that nine people wrote their own names in their own hands and that nobody wrote anybody else's and that a person who has not written on it has not refused it and cannot be counted either way and the record about them says not asked.

He could not speak above a whisper for the rest of that day and for the day after. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he read nine names out loud in about nine minutes in that yard and that his voice went at about the sixth of them and that he finished the other three in a whisper, and that he was asked nothing about it, and that a voice is not a figure about a man and is not a bearer and is not a price of anything.

Then the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room with nothing over the door, which is about two miles the other way and has a bench about nine feet long in it with about eleven notches cut in the underside of the seat at intervals that are not even, said one thing out loud in that yard, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said it in sixteen words, and that the sixteen words are these.

> **THERE IS A CHILD IN THAT ROOM AND HE HEARD IT AND HE DID NOT COME.**

Nobody in that yard said anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody did, and that the record about the nineteen saying nothing says not asked.

Then she said the rest of it, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to two hundred and ten, and a clerk entered that a clerk of nineteen years was in that yard and heard all of it.

"**I went back up that two miles at about half past three in the morning and he was sitting up on that bench in the dark and he was not crying and he was not asleep, and I have kept that room four years and about nine people sleep in it most nights and he is one of the nine and he is about eight years old and he has never been asked anything by anybody in this district in his life. Nobody told him there was a bell. I did not go back down that two miles to fetch him because it is two miles and it is dark and I am not a person who is sent for. I am telling this yard because a piece of paper came off a table four miles from here this morning and it is about a room, and a child heard a bell in this district last night and he is not in that room in the way that paper means, and I am not going to say what he is called and there is a man standing at that table who copies things and I would like him to hear me say that before anybody asks him to write something.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a child of about eight in a room with nothing over the door has not been asked anything by anybody in this district and cannot be counted either way and the record about him says not asked, and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that both of those sentences are the same sentence and that a clerk is entering it twice on purpose and is not making a figure of it, and that a child is not a clerk of a house and is not one of the eight and is not a party of anything and is not a bearer and is not a figure about anybody.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing said one thing in a whisper, and it was counted and came to a hundred and thirty-four, and a clerk entered that he said it in a whisper and that a man of about nineteen counted it in a whisper and that the whisper did not change the figure.

"**I said yes to eleven coppers a week in one second on the thirteenth of this month and wrote a list of people in this district who are not in a household in about an hour and my hand has not been right since the day I started. I am not going to write that child's name down anywhere and I am not going to ask him whether I may. Nobody in this district has ever asked him anything, and the second anybody does, he stops being a person nobody has asked and starts being a person who was asked, and this district found out on the seventeenth of this month what asking a person does to a person, and I have been the one who said it out loud in that yard twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month, and that a man who has said out loud that he will not do a thing has not refused a person and has not consented to anything, and that the record about the child says not asked.

A man of fifty-six said the thing the morning was for, and it was counted and came to a hundred and fifty-three, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard was able to answer him and that the record about their not answering says not asked.

"**We have a piece of paper with nine names on it in nine hands and it is a true piece of paper. It is a record of a room. Last night a bell rang four miles off and everybody inside the sound of it is in the record the bell made, and nobody in this district has seen that record and cannot make it and has not the standing to make it, and what we have is a piece of paper about who came through one door out of about two hundred yards of lane. I am not saying the piece of paper is bad. I am saying that a record of a room and a record of a night are two different things and the difference between them is sitting on a bench in a room two miles off with its name on it and the name of it belongs to it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a bell is not a document and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own and did not become a fourth of them, and that the third thing a bell has to have before this district will call a sounding safe is a shelter and a way to take the record off, and that this district built a shelter and put a man in a doorway and had a piece of paper taken off a table in four goes and put in a coat, and that what was taken off was a record of a room and not the record the bell made, and that the six instruments this district has built and not named did not move on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month and a seventh was not proposed.

At about four in the afternoon the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came into that yard and put a piece of paper about as wide as a page on the end of that trestle table and unfolded it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was the same piece of paper folded the same way in four, and that a man who has put a piece of paper on a table in a yard a stranger can walk into has not thereby made it a document anybody may walk up and read, and that the record about who may read it says not asked.

The day came down about half past seven. The sixth month was twenty-five days old, the first day of the month after next was thirty-seven days off, a piece of paper with nine names in nine hands was face up on the end of a trestle table in a yard, and about nine people slept in a room at the near end of a lane behind a bank and were gone before six in the morning of the twenty-sixth, and the record about the nine says not asked.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A PIECE OF PAPER WITH NINE NAMES ON IT IN NINE HANDS WAS READ OUT IN A YARD BY A MAN WHO COULD NOT SPEAK ABOVE A WHISPER BY THE SIXTH NAME, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE WHO KEEPS A ROOM WITH NOTHING OVER THE DOOR SAID OUT LOUD THAT THERE IS A CHILD OF ABOUT EIGHT IN THAT ROOM AND HE HEARD THE BELL AND HE DID NOT COME, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT A RECORD OF A ROOM AND A RECORD OF A NIGHT ARE TWO DIFFERENT THINGS.**
