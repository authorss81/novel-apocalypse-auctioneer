# Chapter 297: Nobody Has Been Asked

---

The seventeenth of the seventh month did not cool until about six in the evening and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**Two hundred and five days on the board and five hundred and twenty-one days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and thirty-five, and a hundred and ninety-six days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is fifteen days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is eighty-eight days back, and the table against that wall stood up for the eleventh time this morning and the chair at the end of this table has stood eighty-five days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that fifteen is fourteen days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated and was not used this morning, and that a table which has stood up eleven mornings is a table and eleven mornings is a figure about a table, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and that eleven mornings is not a seventh of them and did not become one.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the end of the trestle table at about half past ten in the morning and said one thing about the two documents in that yard, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and forty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have got two documents in this basin and both of them are mine and I want them held apart from each other, because on the second of this month a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road refused a join of them in this yard and he was right and I have not said so out loud since. The first is five lines I wrote, and it says a table is open to any person in this district who is not entered in any household, and it pays eleven coppers a week to anybody who stands at it, and it says that a person who stands at it is not asked what they are called, and the fifth of the five is a sentence about counting that is not true and I put it there on purpose. The second arrived in this yard on the twelfth of this month from four hundred miles off and it has a line in it that nobody asked for about a place where a sheet has been read out. Those are two documents. Neither of them has a person in it. I have not put them on one page and I am not going to, and I would like that entered, because before the first of the eighth month somebody is going to ask me which one of them is mine and I am not going to have an answer that depends on the day.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a join is a join and that the refusals to join two things in this district are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and that neither moved at about half past eleven in the morning on the seventeenth of the seventh month, and that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he will not put two documents on one page has not refused either of them and cannot be counted either way, and that a refusal of one join is one join and that the record about the not joining says not asked, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and that a refusal of a join is not a seventh of them and did not become one, and that the six did not move on the seventeenth of the seventh month.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was at the end of the trestle table at about half past twelve in the afternoon and said one thing about reading a thing back, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and ten-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I read things back in this yard and nobody has ever asked me why and I have never said. It is because the rule of the counter says a thing read back is read back from the top and not from the word, and that rule is not about accuracy, it is about a man in a yard being able to say afterwards that the second reading and the first reading were the same thing. I read the five lines on the second of this month and got nothing wrong. I am going to get something wrong one day, in daylight, in front of about nineteen people, and I am saying now, at about half past twelve, before I have, that I am not going to be the man who starts at the word. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam counted about nine people coming through one door on the night of the twenty-fourth of last month and did not know one of their names, and a man of about twenty-three cannot write a full line of anything and says so out loud, and I am the man who reads it back twice. Those are three different things this basin has and none of them is a way of counting a person.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who drives a cart is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a document and is not a keeper of anything, and that the rule of the counter is that a thing read back wrong is read back again from the top and not from the word, and that a man who has said out loud that he is going to get something wrong has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and that a voice is not a copy and is not a second copy and is not a document, and that the record about the not asking him why says not asked, and that a head counted at a gate is a figure about a gate and is not a figure about a person, and that a person who stood at a table is not a person who has been paid and the two are not added together.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing in that yard in the middle of the afternoon, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got three hundred and fifty, and it went in the minute in his own words, and a clerk entered that he said it in front of about nineteen people and that nobody in that yard had been asked whether they wanted to hear it.

"**I am going to say the whole of the position out loud in this yard this afternoon because I have been carrying it about for five days and carrying it about is how a man arrives at a gate with a version of something. There is a sheet on that trestle table with five lines on it and the second of them says a place where a sheet has been read out is a place where an offer is made, and the third of them says the place an offer is made in is entered in the record of that place, and the fourth of them says the figure of the people in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off is to be completed before the first day of the eighth month, and that day is fifteen days off this morning. The fourth of those lines is about four hundred and eleven people and that number has not moved on that gatepost since the first of the sixth month and nobody in this district has been asked to move it and I am not going to ask anybody to move it. Here is the whole of the position. The only record of this place is a book four hundred yards away in a room with a door on it, and there is one person in this district who can put anything into that book, and on the eighth of this month she told me not to come and ask her again this month, and I said I would not and I have not and I am not going to. So there is a printed line about this place and a book about this place and a woman with the only hand that may write in it, and she has not been asked, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and I am saying that out loud in a yard because before the first of the eighth month somebody is going to want a thing in that book and there is nobody but her.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who convenes nine settlements is not a representative of a settlement that has not asked to be represented, and that a man who says out loud in a yard that there is nobody but a person who has not been asked has not refused on her behalf and has not consented on her behalf and cannot be counted either way, and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that **no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved on the seventeenth of the seventh month and that four hundred and eleven is four hundred and eleven and that eleven mornings is eleven mornings and that the two are two figures and are not added together and are not a finding about each other**, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of fifty-six said one thing back and it was counted and came to a hundred and forty-seven, and a clerk entered that he said it in the ordinary voice and that nobody in that yard was going to be able to do anything with it.

"**He has just said out loud in front of about nineteen people that before the first of the eighth month somebody is going to want a thing in that book, and he does not know who, and neither do I, and neither does she, and the first half of that sentence is the part I have been trying to say to this yard for three weeks and it has come out of his mouth this afternoon on a day he is not supposed to be saying anything. I am not going to take it off him. A man who says the true thing by accident in the wrong week has still said it, and if anybody in this yard wants to be angry with him about the manner of it they can come and be angry with me first and I will tell them the same thing.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to take a thing off another man has not agreed with him and has not disagreed with him and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not taking it off says not asked.

---

Then the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart read the five lines back at about half past four in the afternoon for the second time in five days, because a man of about thirty-four had read them out in that yard in the morning and about four people at the end of the trestle table had asked for them to be read back, and he got the fourth of the five lines wrong, and stopped, and went back to the first of the five and read all five again from the top, and got them right, and about nineteen people watched him do it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the rule of the counter is that a thing read back wrong is read back again from the top and not from the word, and that a man who gets a document wrong in front of about nineteen people has not refused it and has not agreed with it and cannot be counted either way, and that he was asked to read it back and was not asked whether he agreed with it, and that a man who drives a cart is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a document and is not the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps a room with nothing over the door and is not the man of about nineteen who counts.

She entered the rest of what was standing on the seventeenth of this month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the hundred and eight days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the eighty-seven days since the twenty-first of the fourth month and that she did not ask him, and that the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and that the seven and the eight and the nine are three different figures and are not added together, and that the rival record has now been in force forty-six days and has been read out in that yard seven times and that the eighth reading was refused out loud on the twenty-fourth of last month with a reason and that a refused reading is not a reading and that nobody has answered that record.

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was seventeen days old and the first day of the eighth month was fifteen days off, and a man who drives a cart had read five lines of print twice in a yard and about nineteen people had watched him start again from the top.

**AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN WHO CONVENES NINE SETTLEMENTS SAID OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN THAT THE ONLY RECORD OF THAT PLACE IS A BOOK FOUR HUNDRED YARDS AWAY AND THAT THE ONLY PERSON WHO MAY WRITE IN IT HAS NOT BEEN ASKED AND THAT BEFORE THE FIRST OF THE EIGHTH MONTH SOMEBODY IS GOING TO WANT A THING IN IT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE WHO DRIVES A CART GOT A WORD WRONG READING THE FIVE LINES BACK AND READ THEM AGAIN FROM THE TOP.**
