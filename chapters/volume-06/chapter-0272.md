# Chapter 272: A Public Entrance

---

The twenty-second of the sixth month came in hot and about eleven people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there sixty days and nobody had sat in it on any of the sixty.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and eighty days on the board and four hundred and ninety-six days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and ten, and a hundred and seventy-one days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is forty days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is sixty-three days back.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that forty is eight days to the end of this month and the thirty-one of the month after it and the first of the month after that, and that the length of the month after that is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that nobody put a line on a board on any of the eighty-three days since the first of the month before last, counting the first day in.

Then he said what the day was for, in his own words and not in the six things, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and eighty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Yesterday a man in this yard agreed to stand in a doorway of a building with two doors in a lane behind a bank and said that the reason he agreed is that the door has to be findable in the dark. A man in a doorway who has not been found in the dark is not a man in a doorway, he is a man standing in a wall, and I have built nine doorways in five months and I have never once gone and stood in any of them at two in the morning to find out whether they could be found. I am going down that lane at about half past five this afternoon and I am going to take the curtain off the front door of that building and hang it on the nail it came from, and after that anybody in this district may walk into that building at any hour of any night and nobody will ask them a single question, and that is the whole of the public entrance and it is not a thing you can nail up.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a curtain off a nail is not a door and is not a page and is not a document and is not a copy and is not one of the three documents this district does not own, and that the front door of that building has had a handle on it and a curtain in it since a man of about twenty-six built it, and that a building with two doors is not a sixth of the five protected things and is not a sixth of anything.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing proposed the first thing, and it was refused on the page.

He wanted a board. He wanted it fixed to the jamb of the front door of that building at about the height of a man's shoulder, with four lines on it and a space at the bottom of it about the width of a finger, and he wanted it to say that anybody may go in and that nobody will be asked who they are and that whoever takes the paper off the table by the door will put their own name in that space.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a board is not a book and is not a page and is not an address, and that a mark may not carry a job and a printed line may not either, and that a line on a board at the height of a man's shoulder with a space at the bottom of it for a name is a fifth column of not-askings, and that the columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to, and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way and the record about the space says not asked.

He said one thing back about it, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and twenty-seven, and a clerk entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and that the eight did not move.

"**Then take the space off it and the board goes up. Four lines is four lines and this district has been putting four lines on things since the eighth of this month and it has not been asked to be quiet about it. What I want off that board is the line with the finger's width at the bottom of it, because a man does not need a finger's width to find a door in the dark and a man does need a finger's width to be written on in front of about eleven people, and I have been on the end of a trestle table with a man's slate and I am not going to be on the end of a trestle table with a door's.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said that out loud has not thereby been given anything and that a man who has been on the end of a trestle table with a slate is not a bearer of a slate and is not a keeper of a yard and is not one of the eight, and that a fourth line of print on a board at a door is a line of print and not a question and is not a consent and is not a bearer of anything.

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, who had stood at that scale nine years and weighed about nine sacks a day, proposed the second thing, and a clerk entered that she proposed it before anybody asked her and that the record about her not being asked says not asked.

She wanted a stool put outside that door for the man who was going to stand in it, on the ground that a man standing in a doorway from two in the morning until something stops is a man who will be on his feet about nine hours and that this district has at least one of those already.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a stool outside a door for a man in that door is a chair and that the chair at the end of the trestle table in that yard has stood sixty days and nobody has sat in it on any of the sixty, and that a district that has one chair nobody sits in does not want a second one, and that a stool is not a protected thing and is not a sixth of the five and did not become an instrument this district built and did not make a seventh of the six.

She disagreed with that out loud, in about four seconds, and gave a reason, and the count came to a hundred and fifty-eight.

"**I have said one word out loud in that yard today and it was not asked for, and the word was stool, and I would like it entered that a woman who keeps a scale and weighs about nine sacks a day is the person in this district most likely to be standing in a doorway tonight and not the man you have all decided on, and I am not going to stand in it because I have said so out loud, and if you want to put a stool out there then put it out there and let the man who is standing in it decide whether he wants to sit on it, because a stool put out for a man by somebody else is the same shape of thing as a column printed empty on a sheet four hundred miles away and I am tired of this district finding out what those are the hard way.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a scale is not a clerk of a house, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about eleven in the morning on the twenty-second of this month, and that a person who has said out loud that a thing is the same shape as another thing has not made a finding about either of them and that the record about the stool says not asked.

---

About half past five that afternoon about nine people went down the lane behind the bank, which is about two hundred yards long, with a wire on the left of it that has not been tight since before anybody in this district was born, past the lean-to where the scale is and past the third house up from it where a woman of about thirty-four keeps a goat, to the near end of it.

The curtain came off the front door of that building and went back on the nail in the end of the end wall it had come off, and it took about four minutes, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing did it and nobody helped him and nobody asked him to.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the front door of that building is not locked and was not locked before this afternoon either, and that a curtain in a door has never been the thing keeping anybody out of that building, and that a handle on a door and a person in it is the whole of the difference and that a curtain is a draught and is not a rule.

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the thing about the entrance, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people at the foot of that lane, and it was counted and came to a hundred and fifty-one, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was for.

"**A public entrance is not a thing you build. It is a thing that is not there. There is one door in that building at the end of this lane that a person can walk through at any hour without saying who they are, and there has been one since the second of April, and this district has spent five months building nine doors and has not noticed that the one that is open is the one nobody designed. If I stand in it on the night we have not chosen yet, then that door is a public entrance on that night, and on the morning after it is a door with a curtain on a nail again, and a thing that is there on one night and not on the next is not an instrument this district has built and named and it is not a seventh of the six.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a public entrance on a building with two doors was proposed in that lane at about ten minutes to six in the evening on the twenty-second of the sixth month and that a clerk entered at the point it was proposed that it is not a seventh instrument of the six, and that the six did not move on the twenty-second of the sixth month, and that a person standing in a doorway is not holding the door and is not a bearer of it and is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything.

They carried a table out of the building and put it against the outside of the wall beside the front door, and a man of about nineteen who counts counted the legs of it and got four and said out loud that four legs is not a figure about anybody and that he was not going to count anything else on it, and a clerk entered that he counted four and stopped, and that the record about the rest says not asked.

---

At about half past seven that evening, in about nine feet of lane outside the office, about eight feet from the door of the room where the books are, the man the figure of twenty-one years is against asked the woman who keeps the books in that room for one thing, and she said yes in about four seconds, and then said a second thing that he had not asked for.

"**Can the paper that comes off that table go in the book in that room. Not out of the room. In. The book a stranger may walk up to and read, the one that has the covenant in it with the third column ruled and empty on its face, and the one with my name in a column on the second page of it. I want the paper that comes off that table to be in that book and I want the reason it is not a complete paper to be printed on the face of it, and I want it printed in your hand and not in a clerk's.**"

"**Yes. Put it in the book and put the reason on the face of it, and yes I will do it in my hand, and I want you to understand that I have never once in nine years put a sentence of my own in that book, and that I said on the twentieth of this month that I wanted to have had nothing to do with what you are going to do, and that I said it in a kitchen doorway to you, and that what I am going to do now is the opposite of that and I am doing it anyway and I would like it written down that I said on the twentieth that I wanted nothing to do with it and that on the twenty-second of this month I have put my own hand on a piece of paper about a bell.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who keeps a book in one room by one person is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not a steward and is not a party of anything, and that a person who has said yes in about four seconds has granted one thing and refused nothing, and that a sentence in a registrar's own hand in a book a stranger may walk up to is a sentence a person put there, and that a sentence in a clerk's hand in that book is a sentence anybody can say a clerk put there because nobody asked her to.

And he said one thing back, and it was not counted, because there was nobody in that lane who had been asked to count it.

"**Then we are both standing in this. I did not ask you for your hand. I asked for the book and I asked for a reason printed on the face of a piece of paper, and I had a shape in mind where a clerk writes the reason down and the clerk is not in it, and you have taken the shape apart and put your own name in the middle of it, and there is no version of what I asked for that does not have you in it now.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has been refused five things in a month by two women and has said out loud that he will go on offering has not acquired anything by being granted the sixth, and that the record about the six says not asked, and that a registrar is not a bearer of a room and a keeper of a room is not a thing she is and has never been asked to be.

The day came down about half past eight. The sixth month was twenty-two days old, a curtain was on a nail at the end of a lane behind a bank, a table with four legs was against a wall outside a door, and about nine people slept in that room that night and were gone before six, and nobody sent for one of them and nobody fetched one of them, and the record about the nine says not asked.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF THE SIXTH MONTH A BOARD WITH FOUR LINES ON IT AND A FINGER'S WIDTH AT THE BOTTOM OF IT FOR A NAME WAS PROPOSED AT THE DOOR OF A BUILDING WITH TWO DOORS AND REFUSED ON THE PAGE, AND A STOOL WAS PROPOSED AND REFUSED, AND A PUBLIC ENTRANCE WAS ENTERED AS NOT A SEVENTH INSTRUMENT OF THE SIX, AND A REGISTRAR PUT HER OWN HAND ON A PIECE OF PAPER ABOUT A BELL AFTER HAVING SAID ON THE TWENTIETH OF THE MONTH THAT SHE WANTED TO HAVE HAD NOTHING TO DO WITH IT.**
