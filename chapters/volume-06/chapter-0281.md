# Chapter 281: An Address

---

The month turned in the night and nobody in this district was awake for it except a clerk of nineteen years in a room about nine feet by eleven, who wrote one line and put the pen down.

By about ten in the morning there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there sixty-nine days and nobody had sat in it on any of the sixty-nine, and the yard had not been asked whether it was anything.

The three figures came off the board on the gatepost, which carries the twenty-fourth of December and has not been washed, given by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right, and neither of those two men is a keeper of anything.

"**A hundred and eighty-nine days on the board and five hundred and five days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and nineteen, and a hundred and eighty days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is thirty-one days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is seventy-two days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and ninety-nine days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered the derivation of the thirty-one, because a figure somebody says out loud in a yard is not a figure until somebody says where it was counted from. She entered that it is thirty days to the end of the seventh month, because this morning is the first of the seventh month and one day of it is gone, plus the first of the eighth, **and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning and is not to be used on any of the ten days this block reaches.**

Then she entered the other thing, which was the whole reason she had been awake, and she entered it in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph.

She entered that from this morning *last month* means the sixth month and *the month before last* means the fifth month, and that the third month is three months back and is to be called that or called by its own name, and that this district has turned a word twice in nine weeks and did it twice in the night both times, and that a relative month is a figure about words and is not a figure about a date. And she entered that the phrase this district has used for the day on that sheet at that gatepost since the first of last month, *the first day of the month after next*, **no longer means what it meant on the first of the sixth month, because from the seventh month the month after next is the ninth, and the day on that sheet is the first of the eighth, and nobody in that yard is to use that phrase again this month.** And she entered that a man of fifty-six said in a lane on the sixteenth of last month that a man who is going to go on asking a thing is a man a clerk is about to make a habit of, and that she is not going to, and that the record about her not going to says not asked.

Then the stranger came through the gate, which takes two goes, at about half past ten in the morning with a hand-cart and a tent rolled on the back of it.

He is a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them, and he came down the eleven mile road on the last day of last month and slept one night at a farm about a mile out past the loams and did not say where he had come from before the road, and he gave no name to anybody in this district and nobody asked him for one, and a clerk entered that he was not asked and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who puts tables up is not a keeper of a table and is not a party of anything and is not one of the five things this district does not have, and that a hand-cart and a tent rolled on the back of it are not a document and are not a page and did not become a fourth of the three documents this district does not own.

He stood about four feet back from the trestle table and looked at the board on the gatepost with the two sheets on it for a while, and then he said the thing he had come for, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and forty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I am not going to stand in this yard and tell you what I sell, because I have not set anything up yet and I do not know whether I will. I am going to tell you why I am standing in it. There is a thing this district does not have and I have found out what it is in four days on the road and I am going to say the name of it out loud in a yard a stranger can walk into, and then I am going to go away and let you decide what to do about it. I have been down eleven miles of road and up four of another one looking for a place where a person who is not in a household can be found and paid something for standing in the open, and there is one place in this district where that can happen and it is this yard, and it is not because of anything you have done. It is because this is the only address in this basin that a stranger can walk up to. I am not asking this yard to be a market. I have read what a man who digs loam said in it about markets and I think he is right. I am asking this yard to say out loud where it is, once, so that a person who cannot be found at a door knows one place to come.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything for about nine seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the nine seconds are not a refusal and are not a consent, and that the record about them says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him one question, and it was counted and came to ninety-six, and a clerk entered that he asked it before anybody else in that yard had opened their mouth and that the record about the opening says not asked.

"**Say the name of the thing. You have been in this yard about nine minutes and you have not said it and I have not asked you and I am asking you now, in front of about nineteen people, because if you go out of that gate and down eleven miles of road and come back in a season with a table up, then this yard will have heard nothing, and the only thing you are asking of it is a name said out loud, and I would rather it heard the name than the shape.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house, that a question is a question and not a line of print, and that the columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to.

And the man of about thirty-seven said it, in about four seconds, in the ordinary voice, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it without being asked a second time and that the record about the second not asking says not asked.

"**A way to pay a person who is not in a household. I have been down eleven miles of road and up four of another one for four days and I have been told about five things this district does not have and that is the fifth of the five of them, and I did not know until this morning that it was the fifth, and I have not put a price on it yet and I will not until somebody asks me twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the things this district does not have are five and that the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and that it is not paid in this district and is not priced in this district, and that a man who has said a name out loud in a yard four hundred yards from a room a stranger may walk up to has not entered it anywhere and has not been asked to and the record about his not entering says not asked, and that an address is not a bearer and a wall is not an address and a yard a stranger can walk up to is not an address until somebody in it says the words, and that nobody in that yard said them.

Then a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road, who is not the tally keeper and is not the man who digs loam and is not the man who mends fencing, came down off the bank at about eleven in the morning and said one thing, and it was counted and came to a hundred and eighty-six, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about.

"**I have kept four miles of that road eleven years and there is a gate on it about a mile before the place where the ground stops, and I walked down to the far side of the eleven mile road yesterday and back and I did it in a day on two feet, and the whole of what I have to say about this is that a yard is a good address for a person who already lives in the district and a useless one for a person who does not, because a person who is not in a household does not know which of the nine settlements you are in, and you have told me you have been down four days of road and you have not once said which of them you came out of. I am not saying no. I am saying that a yard is not an address, it is a place, and it is a place in a district of nine, and you have brought eleven miles of road behind you and you have not brought a way of naming where.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and has never been asked to be one, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about eleven in the morning on the first of the seventh month, and that a man who says a yard is a place and not an address has not refused the yard and cannot be counted either way about it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the things that had not moved in the night, in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph. She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the ninety-two days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood sixty-nine days and nobody has sat in it on any of the sixty-nine, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the seventy-one days since the twenty-first of the fourth month and that she did not ask him for the figure and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the boards in this district are five and the lines that are full are six and a seventh is ruled and empty at the ninth place, and that the columns of not-askings are four, and that there is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and there never has been one.

---

The man of about thirty-seven put the hand-cart against the wall inside that gate at about half past eleven in the morning and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing took the other end of it, because the gate takes two goes and the ground behind the post is soft, and on the second of the two the man of about thirty-four could not take the weight on his left hand at all, because the skin is not back on the inside of it from the wrist to the elbow and has not been since the twenty-third of last month, so he went up the bank with the cart on his right shoulder instead.

He put it down on the flat by the trestle table and stood with his right hand still up on it for about four seconds, and then he took his hand off, and about four people in that yard watched him do it.

He said one thing about it, out loud, in about four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record came from him and not from the man of about thirty-seven.

"**That is the second thing this month that has come off that shoulder. I cannot take a weight on that side of me and I am not going to be able to for a while, and I am saying it here and now and not on some later day, because that hand got into this in the first place on a day I was going up a frame with nothing in my left hand, and I would like this yard to have one place in it where a fact about my body is said the same day the body does it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says out loud that his body is going to be the price of a thing before the thing happens is not a consent and is not a bearer and is not a party of anything, and that a body is not a figure about a man and a shoulder is not a price of anything, and that the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about half past eleven in the morning on the first of the seventh month.

The man of about thirty-seven said that he would be at that gate again in the morning with something written on it, and that he would ask for it to be read out, and that he was not asking anybody in that yard to agree to it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said in a yard that he will ask for a thing to be read out has not asked the yard to agree to it and that the record about the agreeing says not asked.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was one day old and the first day of the eighth month was thirty-one days off, and a hand-cart and a tent rolled on the back of it were against the wall inside the gate of a yard a stranger can walk up to, and nobody in that yard had said the word address out loud, and the record about the not saying says not asked.

**AND ON THE FIRST OF THE SEVENTH MONTH EVERY RELATIVE MONTH IN THIS DISTRICT CHANGED MEANING IN THE NIGHT, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT THE PHRASE THIS DISTRICT HAD USED FOR THE DAY ON THAT SHEET NO LONGER MEANS WHAT IT MEANT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN WHO PUTS TABLES UP FOR ANYBODY WHO WILL USE THEM CAME THROUGH A GATE THAT TAKES TWO GOES AND ASKED THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN FOR ITS ADDRESS, AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT THE FIFTH OF THE FIVE THINGS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE IS A WAY TO PAY A PERSON WHO IS NOT IN A HOUSEHOLD.**
