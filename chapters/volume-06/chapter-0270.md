# Chapter 270: The Fourteenth Time Past

---

The twentieth of the sixth month came in hot and still and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there fifty-eight days and nobody had sat in it on any of the fifty-eight.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and seventy-eight days on the board and four hundred and ninety-four days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and eight, and a hundred and sixty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is forty-two days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights was the twentieth of the month before last, which is sixty-one days back, and nobody in this yard knows whether that night happened.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the twentieth of the fourth month is the month before last as of this morning, and that the third month is two months back and is to be called that, and that a relative month is a figure about words and is not a figure about a date and did not become a fifth column of the four.

Then the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing said the thing he had been carrying since the eighteenth, and it was counted and came to a hundred and fifty-two, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard said it after him.

"**Seven days ago a man with a case sat at the end of that table and I said yes to eleven coppers a week in about one second, and I told this yard at the time that the difference was not the eleven coppers, and I want to say the rest of that sentence now because I did not say the rest of it then. He stopped on the eighteenth, in four seconds, in front of eleven people, and my hand has not been right since the day I started, and I have not been paid since the fifteenth, and there is no man in this district who owes me a penny and no man outside it who does either. I am not asking anybody for anything. I wanted it in a minute in front of about nineteen people and I have got it and I am going to sit down now.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house and is not one of the eight, and that the eight did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the twentieth of the sixth month, and that a man who has been paid once by somebody outside this district can afterwards be got in a yard for nothing, and that this district spent five months asking people questions on the assumption that a person who is asked is a person who is free, and that the assumption is wrong and was wrong on the first of this month and nobody found it out until the seventeenth.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the things that had not moved in ten days, and they were in the same hand as the rest and they were not a paragraph.

She entered that the things this district does not have are five and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, that a man with a case offered the fifth of those five on the thirteenth of this month and it was refused out loud, that a refusal of a shape the other eight have not got is one refusal of that fifth thing and is not a ninth of anything, and that the fifth is not paid and not priced, that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and a seventh was proposed in ten days and refused on the page, that the three documents this district does not own are three and a fourth was made in ten days and it belonged to a man with a case, that the protected things are five and no sixth was added and a building with two doors is not a sixth, that the conditions with no end on it are four, that the refusals to join two things are thirteen and the registrar's own count is twelve and that neither moved on any of the ten days, that the refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading and the fourth line of the offer was not read out in this block, and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight and that seven people gave one with a reason in ten days and not one of them is a clerk of a house, and that there is still no rate in this basin that turns a year into coppers and there never has been one.

She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the eighty-one days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of the trestle table has stood fifty-eight days and nobody has sat in it on any of the fifty-eight, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the sixty days since the twenty-first of the month before last, and that the rival record has been in force nineteen days and has been read out seven times and nobody has answered it.

She entered that this district has five different ninths in it and that they are the ninth of the nine charters, the ninth of the eleven houses the woman of fifty-eight carries water to, the ninth of the nine printed nights, the ninth place on the page out of the cave and the ninth of the ten days the block before this one ran, and that they are five ninths and are not added together and are not a pattern, and that a sixth was available to her this morning and she did not take it, and that a clerk who could have made a sixth and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing.

She entered that the man of about thirty at a counter is at the sixteenth time down the eleven mile road and the next is the seventeenth and that this district has never once given that man a day to be here on, and that the man of about forty-one who keeps a road house at the top of the road that climbs is not in this district on any of the ten days either, and that the man with the portfolio is not in this district and that nothing in this yard says where he is, and that the bell four miles out past the loams has about nine inches of rope left on the beam and was not rung in this block, and that the ninth place on the page out of the cave is ruled and empty and was not filled in any of the ten chapters of this block.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the far end of the trestle table at about eleven with her sleeves turned up and told the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing that her four empty houses were still standing and were not going to be offered again before the first day of the season after this one, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she said it before she was asked and that a woman who keeps houses is not a clerk of a house, and that the four houses were not added to the eleven of the woman of fifty-eight, and that nobody in this yard asked her why.

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam put his right hand flat on the end of the trestle table to turn the board over and could not get his fingers under the edge of it, and he did it with two fingers of the same hand a minute later, and a clerk entered that a man whose hand has not been right since the ditch behind the building with two doors on the eleventh of this month has not been asked about it by anybody in that yard and that the record about the two fingers says not asked.

---

At about half past five the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing carried a slate down about four hundred yards of lane and it went past the kitchen at the end of that lane, and he had carried it past there thirteen times before and this was the fourteenth, and no clerk in this district has written down on which days any of the thirteen went past, and that was not repaired this month either.

The registrar of this district's records office was in the kitchen doorway with her sleeves turned up and there was no chalk on the step, and the fanlight over the door of the room where the books are does not light anything, as it has never once lit anything in four months.

He wrote one line on the side of the slate that faced her and put it down on the step and neither of them said anything about what was on it while he was writing it, and a clerk of nineteen years was four hundred yards away and was not told about it until about seven, and the record came from the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and not from her, and she entered that a slate is not a book and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own and did not become a fourth of them, and that a sentence written on a slate and carried back down a lane by the man it was written to is not in any of the four places this district has written a thing in, and that a fourth place is not a fifth, and that the man who wrote it said it in twenty-four words and that nobody counted it and that the twenty-four words are these.

> **TOMORROW I AM GOING TO ASK A MAN TO STAND IN A DOORWAY. HE WILL SAY NO. I AM GOING TO ASK HIM TWICE.**

And she said one thing about it, and it was not counted, because there was nobody there to count it and the minute is in the same hand and is not in a book.

"**I know what that is. I have kept a room with a shut door in it for nine years and I know exactly what a man sounds like when he is about to ask somebody to stand in a doorway and be the one who is standing there. Do not do it for me. Do it for the forty people whose names were said in a yard on the seventeenth, and if it goes wrong I want to have had nothing to do with it, and I want that written down as a thing I asked for and not as a thing you decided.**"

And he said one thing back to that, and there was nobody in that kitchen who had been asked to count it.

"**I am not going to tell you what I am going to do tomorrow. I have said about nine things out loud in that yard in twenty days and I have said none of them to you, and that is going to keep being true, and I would rather you knew that now than found it out on the twenty-second.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who has said out loud that she wants to have had nothing to do with a thing has not refused to be asked about it and cannot be counted either way, and that a registrar is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not a steward and is not a party of anything, and that a man who has asked for help in a kitchen doorway and been refused in a kitchen doorway has not been helped and has not been refused, and that the record about the fourteenth time the slate went past says not asked.

Then she said one more thing and it was not about a door or a slate or a book.

"**There is bread on the board and it will be gone by seven.**"

And he said one thing back, and it was not counted either.

"**I will come back for it at seven.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that neither of them said whether he would, and that the record about the seven o'clock says not asked, and that a thing said in a kitchen doorway about bread is not a document and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own and did not become a fourth of them.

The day came down about half past seven. The sixth month was twenty days old and the first day of the month after next is forty-two days off, and a plank is back on the end wall of a building of one room at the near end of a lane behind a bank with a nail in a hasp that did not move on the eleventh of this month, and two books are in one room four hundred yards from that gate, and a slate with nothing on it is on the end of a trestle table, and about nine people slept in that room on the night of the twentieth and were gone before six.

**AND ON THE TWENTIETH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE SAID OUT LOUD IN A YARD THAT A MAN WITH A CASE STOPPED IN FOUR SECONDS ON THE EIGHTEENTH AND THAT HIS HAND HAS NOT BEEN RIGHT SINCE THE DAY HE STARTED, AND A SLATE WENT PAST A KITCHEN FOR THE FOURTEENTH TIME AND CAME BACK WITH TWENTY-FOUR WORDS ON IT, AND A REGISTRAR SAID IN A KITCHEN DOORWAY THAT SHE WANTED TO HAVE HAD NOTHING TO DO WITH IT, AND NEITHER OF THEM SAID ANOTHER WORD ABOUT A DOORWAY AFTER THAT.**
