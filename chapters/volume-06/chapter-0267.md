# Chapter 267: Forty-One

---

The seventeenth of the sixth month came in hot before seven and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there fifty-five days and nobody had sat in it on any of the fifty-five.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and about nine of the nineteen had come because he had said in a lane behind the bank on the day before that he had one question and was going to ask it in that yard at ten in the morning, and nobody had been asked whether they wanted to hear it.

"**A hundred and seventy-five days on the board and four hundred and ninety-one days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and five, and a hundred and sixty-six days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is forty-five days off counted off this morning, and there are about nineteen of you in this yard because I told four people in a lane yesterday that I had one question, and I have got it, and I am going to ask it at ten, and I would like whoever is going to answer it to stand up when I have finished asking it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that about nineteen people were in that yard and that nine of them had come on the strength of a sentence said in a lane and that nobody had been asked whether they wanted to hear a question, and that a question asked in a yard is asked in a yard a stranger can walk into.

At ten he asked it, in his own words and not in the six things, and it was counted and came to a hundred and eight, and a clerk of nineteen years was in that yard and heard it and did not stop it and entered afterwards that she did not.

"**There is a second book in the room four hundred yards from this gate. It has been open since the sixth of this month. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam counted forty-one lines on its two open pages at four in the morning on the seventh of this month and was not asked to and did not ask, and he said the figure out loud in this yard on the tenth and this district has had a figure of forty-one about itself for seven days. I am not asking anybody for a number. I am asking whether anybody in this district knows what is in it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked that question in that yard in front of about nineteen people at ten in the morning on the seventeenth of the sixth month, after asking it in a lane to about four people the day before, and that nobody in that yard stopped him, and that a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and that a man who asks the question is the man who opened the door, and that a clerk is not going to enter those as two things.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing stood up. He stood up on the end of the trestle table by the man with the case, and he said the forty-one names out loud in the ordinary voice, in about nine minutes, and a man of about nineteen counted the names and got forty-one, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he counted them and that nobody asked him to and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a man of about nineteen who counts a thing when there is a number in front of him is not a counter of people and is nobody's clerk.

"**The first of the forty-one is a woman who came to the room where the books are on the eighth of this month and asked whether her daughter would be put under a line. The second is a man who came on the ninth and asked for nothing at all and said he would come back in a season. I am not going to say whose they are. I am going to say that I wrote them down on the seventh of this month in the afternoon in the room where the books are, and I wrote them from what I had seen, and I did not ask anybody whether I might.**"

The registrar of this district's records office was in that yard and she did not stop him and she was not asked to, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was four feet off the trestle table for about nine minutes and that a person standing four feet off a page is not deciding what the page says, and that a person standing next to a lock is not holding it and a person standing next to a page is not reading it, and that she was asked a first thing that morning by nobody, and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way.

Then she said one thing, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and four, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard answered her.

"**I wrote forty-one names on a page in one room at about five in the morning on the sixth of this month and I did not read it out and I did not ask one of them whether I might, and this morning a man stood up on that table and read it out in a yard a stranger can walk into, and neither of us was asked. I want both of those in the minute in my own hand. I am not going to stop him now. There is nothing in this yard that would let me stop him and I have looked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who has said out loud in a yard that she did not stop a thing has not thereby consented to the thing and is not a bearer of it and is not a holder and is not a steward and is not a party of anything, and that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, and that the forty-one names in that yard are now figures about this district and are not forty-one people, and that a person whose name is in a figure in a yard has not been entered anywhere and has not refused anything.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against, who had not stood in that yard on the day before, said one thing about it, and it was counted and came to a hundred and eleven, and a clerk entered that he said it to the yard and not to the man who had asked the question.

"**A man can be findable by a name in four miles. I have proved that. What I have not proved and what I have just watched happen in front of about nineteen people is the other half of it, which is that a yard will hold anything you put in it if you say it out loud in it, including a thing that was never meant to be in a yard, and there is no column anywhere in this district that will hold me responsible for that. Do not thank me and do not blame him either, and I am not going to say anything else about it in this yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who convenes nine settlements is not a representative of a settlement that has not asked to be represented, and that a man who has said out loud that he was not going to say anything else has not thereby made a finding, and that the record about the not saying anything else says not asked.

The man of about fifty-six said the price of it out loud, once, and it was counted and came to ninety-six, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard was able to answer him and that the record about their not answering says not asked.

"**Forty-one names went out loud in that yard this morning for nothing because a man standing in it asked a question. This district has spent five months finding out that asking is a door, and we found it out the expensive way, which is that a door works in both directions and a man who is bought once by somebody four hundred miles away is afterwards got in a yard for nothing. Nobody in this basin can price that and I have been in this basin fifty-six years and I have never met anybody who could.**"

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing he had to say about his own part in it, and it was counted and came to a hundred and thirty-six.

"**I asked that question. In a lane yesterday to four people and in this yard this morning to about nineteen, and the first time I got nothing and the second time I got forty-one names, and the difference between those two things is not the yard and is not the hour and is me, standing up in front of more people, and a man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart is not the reason and a man with a case is not the reason. Nobody is to tell me it was the broker. I opened it. I have spent five months telling this district that a document which names nothing is the only safe kind there is and I asked a question in a yard this morning until somebody answered it in front of everybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that, and that nobody in that yard argued with him about it, and that a man who has said out loud that he opened a thing is not a finding and is not a reason for anything, and that the record about the four in the lane says not asked.

The man of about forty-four with a case, who had been sitting at the end of that trestle table for nine minutes without moving, said one sentence. It was not counted, because he did not ask for it to be and nobody in that yard was standing in a place to count.

"**That is not a list. I have been doing this four years and I could not sell that in any of them, and I have been sitting at this table since the thirteenth waiting for something I could sell.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who is not of this district is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of anything and is not one of the eight, and that a thing said in a yard is not a document and is not a copy and did not become a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a man with a case has said out loud in a yard that what was said in it is not a list and that the record about that says not asked.

---

At about half past four the woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book came down four miles in the afternoon, because a man had come down her route in the morning and had not been one of hers, and she stood in the lane behind the bank and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing told her what had happened in the yard and then asked her one thing.

She said it in about four seconds and it was counted and came to a hundred and thirty-seven, and it was not a refusal and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not a refusal.

"**I have walked four miles to say one thing and I am not going to say the thing you would like. You read forty-one names out in a yard this morning, and there are people in this district who are going to go home tonight and work out which of them they are, and two of them are women I have stood next to on a road in the dark, and I did not know their names then and I have not asked since, and that is the whole of what I have come to say. I am not going to say any more of it and you are not going to ask me any more of it, and if you do, I will go back up those four miles and I will not come down again.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book was in that lane at about half past four in the afternoon on the seventeenth of the sixth month, and that nobody sent for her and that she was not asked a first thing, and that a person who has said a thing out loud in a lane and has not said a second thing has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and that she is not a bearer of a route and is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be one.

She went back up the four miles before the light went and a clerk entered that going is not arriving and that this district has never once given that woman a day to be here on.

The registrar of this district's records office went back the four hundred yards at about half past five and could not get her own door open, and it took her about nine seconds, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was standing in the lane and did not touch the door. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a hand that would not close on a pen at four in the morning on the seventh of this month and a hand that would not close on a door at about half past five on the seventeenth are the same hand, and that the second time is worse than the first, and that nobody asked her about either and that the record about the nine seconds says not asked, and that a registrar who keeps a book in one room by one person is not a keeper of a room and has never been asked to be.

The day came down about half past eight. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two open pages of one book in one room four hundred yards from that gate had carried forty-one lines on the seventh of this month and that forty-one names were said out loud in that yard on the seventeenth, and that a number and a list have both been said out loud in a yard a stranger can walk into inside seventeen days of this month, and that a yard in which two numbers about the same people can be said next to each other is the first thing anybody in this district has built in two months, and that nobody in this district decided to build it and that the record about who decided says not asked.

**AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED ONE QUESTION IN A YARD AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE STOOD UP ON THE END OF A TRESTLE TABLE AND SAID FORTY-ONE NAMES OUT LOUD IN ABOUT NINE MINUTES IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE, AND THE REGISTRAR OF THIS DISTRICT'S RECORDS OFFICE WAS FOUR FEET AWAY FOR THE WHOLE OF IT AND DID NOT STOP HIM, AND A MAN SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE HAD OPENED IT.**
