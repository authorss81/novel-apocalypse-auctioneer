# Chapter 268: Forty People

---

The eighteenth of the sixth month was bright and hot by nine and about eleven people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there fifty-six days and nobody had sat in it on any of the fifty-six.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and seventy-six days on the board and four hundred and ninety-two days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and six, and a hundred and sixty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is forty-four days off counted off this morning, and forty-one names were said out loud in this yard yesterday and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam went into the room where the books are at six this morning and I have not been in it and have not been asked to be.**"

At about six in the morning the man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in the room where the books are, and the registrar of this district's records office was in it with him, and his right hand has not been much use to him since the afternoon of the second of this month and could not turn the corner of a page, and she held the book, and neither of them said anything about that. He counted the lines on the two open pages again, in daylight, with his own finger, and got forty-two, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he counted them and that nobody asked him to count them and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

He said the finding out loud in that room and then said it out loud again in a lane four hundred yards away to about four people, and it was counted and came to a hundred and sixty-nine, and a clerk entered that he said the same sentence twice and that the two are the same sentence and not two findings.

"**Forty-two lines and forty names, and one of them is a person twice. There is a name on the left-hand page at the bottom and the same name on the right-hand page at the top, in the same hand, with a quarter of an inch of space after it, because the hand that wrote it ran out of room at the bottom of a page and carried it over. That is the whole of the difference between the two figures and the difference is a quarter of an inch of space, and I counted forty-one lines on the seventh of this month at four in the morning with my own finger and I was not asked to and I did not ask, and I counted forty-two this morning with the same finger on the same book, and the arithmetic was right both times and the figure was wrong both times, and a man who gets a number right twice in eleven days by counting lines has been counting lines.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a line with a person's name on it is not a person, and that a count of lines is not a count of people, and that a book a person keeps to protect people has in it a hand that copied a name across a page break, and that the number of people whose names were said out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the seventeenth of this month is forty and not forty-one, and that forty is a figure about a yard on one day and not a figure about anybody, and that the record about the quarter of an inch says not asked.

---

At about half past ten the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing tried to write a thing on the end of that trestle table and could not hold the pen, and the man of about forty-four with a case counted eleven coppers out onto the table and said in about four seconds that the arrangement was finished, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of about twenty-three had been paid eleven coppers a week on the fifteenth of this month and had not been paid on the eighteenth, and that a man who is not paid is not a bearer of anything and had not been one on the fifteenth either, and that the eight refusals a clerk of a house has given did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the eighteenth of the sixth month, and that a refusal a man makes of somebody outside this district is not a refusal this district made.

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat, who is at the third house up the lane from the scale, came down the lane at about eleven and the man of about fifty-six carried a pail off the man of about thirty-one who digs loam without being asked and without saying anything, and a clerk entered that a pail taken off a man by another man is not a job and is not a bearer and did not become a seventh instrument of the six, and that a man whose hand has not been right for sixteen days has not been asked about it in sixteen days and that the record about the sixteen says not asked.

---

At about two in the afternoon, in about nine feet of lane outside the office and in front of about nine people, the man the figure of twenty-one years is against offered the woman who keeps the books in the room four hundred yards away one thing, and she refused it in about four seconds, and both of them said it out loud where about nine people could hear it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that lane argued with her afterwards and that the record about the nine hearing it says not asked.

"**On the twentieth of this month, in daylight, in that yard, I will say out loud that there is a second book in a room four hundred yards from that gate, and that it is kept by the person who keeps the books in that room, and that it holds people who came and asked for something. I will not say one name and I will not say a number. And then a body four hundred miles away that has printed an empty column for a person can send you a line of print about it instead of sitting there guessing what is in a room, and that is the safest shape a thing like that can have and I have thought about it for a day and a night.**"

"**No. The moment I say what is in that room in a yard, that room is a room anybody may ask about, and there are forty people in this district who have told one person one thing each and nobody else one thing, and the only reason they told me is that I am the person who keeps a book that nobody may read. You are asking me to spend that in a yard on the ground that it will protect them. It will. And so will everything else about it, and I have been that person for nine years and I have never once had to stand in a yard about it, and I am not going to start this month.**"

Then he said one thing, and it was counted and came to fifty-three, and a clerk entered that nobody in that lane said it after him.

"**Then say it in a room. That is the whole of what I have got and it is a small thing to have got and I am saying it in front of about nine people because I have run out of ways of saying it to you in about nine feet of lane.**"

And she said one thing back, and it was not counted, because there was nobody in that lane who had been asked to count anything, and the minute is in the same hand and is not in a book.

"**There is no room in this district that nobody can walk into. That is the other half of what I have just said and you did not hear the other half, because you only wanted the first one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who refuses a man a thing in front of about nine people has not refused the man, and that a registrar is not a clerk of a house and is not one of the eight and that the eight did not move at about two in the afternoon on the eighteenth of the sixth month, and that a registrar who has said out loud that she will not spend her room is not a bearer of it and is not a keeper of it and is not a holder and is not a steward and is not a party of anything, and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was about two hundred yards off up the lane while that was said, and he did not come, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not come and that nobody told him about it and that the record about his not coming says not asked.

Then the man of about fifty-six said the sentence, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people in about nine feet of lane, and it was counted and came to ninety-seven, and a clerk entered that nobody in that lane was able to answer it.

"**There are three places in this district now where a thing about a person is said out loud and every one of them is a place a stranger can walk into to. In five months this yard has been the one place in the basin that asked people questions, and it has taken me until the seventeenth of this month to work out that asking a person something and asking a person something in front of about nineteen people are not the same act. They are not the same act because one of them has a witness.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that the record about the three places says not asked, and that three is a figure about three places and is not a figure about anybody.

The registrar of this district's records office went back the four hundred yards at about half past four and had to stop twice on the way, once at the end of the yard wall and once at her own gate, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody walked with her and that the record about the two stops says not asked, and that a woman who has been on her feet since six in the morning in a room and a lane and a yard is not a figure about anybody and is not a finding and is not a reason for anything.

At about four in the afternoon the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about twenty-three, in his own words and not in the six things, what the forty people in that room would want said about yesterday, and the man of about twenty-three said that he did not know and that he had not asked them.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who does not know has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about what forty people would want says not asked, and that a clerk who wrote down what forty people would want would be doing the thing a body four hundred miles away did on the first of this month with a number and a door, and that she was not going to.

Then at about half past four, at the top of the bank, the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the woman of fifty-eight the same question for the fourth time, and she did not answer him, and a clerk entered that the record about the not answering says not asked.

The day came down about half past eight and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the sixth month was eighteen days old, and that the bell four miles out past the loams was not rung and the record about the not ringing says not asked, and that a yard in which a person can be found by a number said out loud in it is a yard a stranger can walk into, and that the record about who is a stranger says not asked.

**AND ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A MAN COUNTED FORTY-TWO LINES IN DAYLIGHT IN A ROOM AND FORTY NAMES, AND SAID THAT THE ARITHMETIC HAD BEEN RIGHT TWICE IN ELEVEN DAYS AND THE FIGURE HAD BEEN WRONG TWICE, AND IN ABOUT NINE FEET OF LANE IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINE PEOPLE A REGISTRAR REFUSED A MAN WHO OFFERED TO SAY THE SHAPE OF WHAT SHE KEEPS WITHOUT SAYING ONE NAME, AND HE ASKED HER TO SAY IT IN A ROOM AND SHE SAID THERE IS NO ROOM IN THIS DISTRICT THAT NOBODY CAN WALK INTO.**
