# Chapter 271: The Twenty-First

---

The twenty-first of the sixth month came in hot under a low cloud and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there fifty-nine days and nobody had sat in it on any of the fifty-nine.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and seventy-nine days on the board and four hundred and ninety-five days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and nine, and a hundred and seventy days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is forty-one days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights was the twentieth of the month before last, which is sixty-two days back, and nobody in this yard knows whether that night happened.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that forty-one is nine days to the end of this month and the thirty-one of the month after it and the first of the month after that, and that the length of the month after that is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that the twentieth of the fourth month is the month before last as of this morning and that the third month is two months back and is to be called that, and that a relative month is a figure about words and is not a figure about a date.

She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the eighty-two days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood fifty-nine days, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the sixty-one days since the twenty-first of the month before last, and that the record that came into force on the first of this month has now been in force twenty days and has been read out in that yard seven times and has not been answered.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said what the slate had said, in his own words and not in the six things, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and seventy-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**On the twentieth of this month I told a woman in a kitchen doorway that today I was going to ask a man to stand in a doorway, that the man would say no, and that I was going to ask him twice. That was twenty-four words on a slate and I have not added to them. I am asking in this yard at ten in the morning in front of about nineteen people and not in a lane, and if it goes wrong this yard will know about it before anybody else in this basin does. I am asking the man of about thirty-four who digs loam to stand in the doorway of the building at the near end of the lane behind the bank from about two in the morning on a night we have not chosen yet, until whatever it is we are listening for stops, and then to take off whatever is put on the table by that door, put it inside his coat, and not say where he has put it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says a thing out loud in a yard a stranger can walk into has not thereby asked anybody for anything, and that the record about the twenty-four words on the slate says not asked, and that a slate is not a book and is not a page and is not one of the three documents this district does not own and did not become a fourth of them, and that a night this district has not chosen yet is a figure about words and is not a figure about a day.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam, who is not the man who mends fencing and who counted forty-two lines in a room on the eighteenth of this month with a right hand that could not turn a page corner, said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and the count came to a hundred and thirty-two.

"**No. I have got a hand that does not close and I have had it since the afternoon of the second of this month and I have said it out loud in this yard twice and in a ditch in front of about nine people. Standing in a doorway from two in the morning until a thing stops is not a thing a man does with a hand. It is a thing a man does with his feet and his back. And a man who cannot hold a pen shut is a man who cannot hold a sheet flat, and taking a thing off a table and folding it is the second of those and not the first, and you asked me for the second one inside the first thing you said.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who digs loam is not a clerk of a house, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about ten in the morning on the twenty-first of the sixth month, and that a person who has been asked a question and has answered it with a no and a reason has refused that one question and nothing else and cannot be counted either way about anything else, and that a hand is not a figure about anybody.

Then he asked him the same question a second time, in the same words, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had said in a kitchen doorway on the twentieth that he was going to do it twice, and that a man who says out loud in a doorway that he is going to ask a man twice has not been asked to by anybody, and that the record about the second asking says not asked.

"**Then I am asking you a second time and I am using the same words, because the first time I put a thing in it that was mine and not yours and you heard it. Stand in that doorway. I am not going to tell you what for. If I tell you what for then you will agree to a task and not to a night, and a night is the whole of what I am asking for. When a person comes out of that lane in the dark they are wet and they are frightened and they have got about four seconds to work out whether that door is for them or a trap, and the only thing that will tell them in four seconds is somebody standing in it. I am not going to put a board up saying the door is open. I am going to put a man in it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man standing in a doorway is not holding the door and is not a bearer of it and is not a keeper of it and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a door does not need a person and that the reason a person is wanted in that doorway on this district's own account tonight is a reason about four seconds and not about the door.

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said yes, and said what it cost, and the count came to two hundred and seventeen.

"**Yes, and I am going to tell you what it costs and it is not my hand. If I stand in that doorway then in about four hours somebody in this district is going to have to be able to find that door in the dark from four miles out, and every person who comes out of that lane is going to come out of it past a man, and a man in a doorway who has been asked to stand there is a man who has been asked for something. This district has spent five months finding out what asking a person does to a person, and it found out on the seventeenth of this month the expensive way, and I am not going to stand in a door on behalf of a thing this district has not said out loud in daylight. You will stand in that yard at ten in the morning on a day that anybody in this yard chooses, and you will say out loud that you are going to ring a bell four miles out past the loams, and you will say out loud that a bell takes the names of everybody who hears it, and you will say both of those before anybody is asked to be in that doorway.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything for about nine seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the nine seconds are not a refusal and are not a consent, and that a condition attached by a person to a thing they have said yes to is a term and not a refusal, and that a term nobody in that yard was asked about says not asked.

A man of fifty-six said one thing about it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it in ten words, and that the ten words are these.

> **I WANT THE YARD TO KNOW BEFORE THE DOORWAY KNOWS.**

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the other half of what a man of fifty-six had said, out loud, to about nineteen people, and the count came to a hundred and fifty-nine, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard argued with him.

"**A condition is the best thing anybody has put in front of me in a month and I want it written down that I have said so. I have spent five months in this yard asking this district to be careful what it calls a thing, and I have spent five months being wrong about five months of work, and this morning a man told me that he would stand in a doorway for this district on the condition that the man who sent him says it out loud in this yard first, and that is the price of a door in the plainest words I have heard them in. He has not been bought. Nobody has offered him anything. He has cost himself something and he has named the something and I would like it in the minute that a man who names his own price is not the same as a man who has been given one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who names his own price has not made a finding and has not entered a habit, and that the record about the four people who said nothing in that yard says not asked, and that a refusal to say thank you is not a refusal and is not one of the eight and did not move the eight.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, in his own words and not in the six things, whether anybody in that yard had anything to say about a bell before tomorrow, and nobody answered him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asking a yard whether it has anything to say has asked about nineteen people a question, and that a person who answers it is a person who was asked, and that nobody answered and that the record about the nineteen saying nothing says not asked.

The day came down about half past seven. The sixth month was twenty-one days old, the first day of the month after next was forty-one days off, and a building of one room at the near end of the lane behind the bank had two doors and a plank back on the end wall of it and a nail in a hasp that did not move on the eleventh, and about nine inches of rope was still inside a beam four miles out past the loams that nobody standing on the ground can reach.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OF THE SIXTH MONTH A MAN ASKED A MAN TO STAND IN A DOORWAY IN A YARD A STRANGER CAN WALK INTO AND HE SAID NO IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS, AND WAS ASKED THE SAME QUESTION A SECOND TIME IN THE SAME WORDS AND SAID YES, AND SAID THAT THE MAN WHO SENT HIM WOULD HAVE TO STAND IN THAT YARD IN DAYLIGHT AND SAY OUT LOUD THAT HE WAS GOING TO RING A BELL, AND THAT THE BELL TAKES THE NAMES OF EVERYBODY WHO HEARS IT.**
