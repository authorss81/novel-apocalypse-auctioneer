# Chapter 274: The First Safe Sounding

---

The twenty-fourth of the sixth month was hot and close and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there sixty-two days and nobody had sat in it on any of the sixty-two.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures before anybody had put anything down, and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and eighty-two days on the board and four hundred and ninety-eight days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and twelve, and a hundred and seventy-three days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is thirty-eight days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is sixty-five days back, and I am going to say the next thing standing where I am and nobody is going to move me.**"

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood on the ground at the end of the trestle table with his arms down and said it in the ordinary voice to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and fifty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**In the yard of that frame four miles out past the loams there is a bell about two feet across the mouth of it and it is cracked from the rim up about a third of the way, and there has been nine inches of rope left inside a beam about eleven feet off the ground since somebody took three feet of it away a bit at a time so that you could not see it from the road. Tonight I am going to have that nine inches tied to something a man can hold, and I am going to pull it, and the bell is going to ring. A bell that is rung records everybody who hears it for one night. Everybody. Not the people who come to this yard. Not the people I have built a door for. Everybody inside the sound of it, and this district does not know yet how far that is and neither do I, and I am saying this out loud in daylight in front of about nineteen people so that nobody in this yard can say afterwards that they did not know. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing is going to hold the rope. I am not going to be the one holding it. I have asked a man to stand in a doorway and he has said yes and I have said yes back to him, and that is the other half of tonight, and this is the half that is about all of you.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sentence was said in a yard a stranger can walk into at about ten in the morning on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and that about nineteen people heard it, and that nobody was asked whether they wanted to hear it, and that a man who says out loud what he is going to do has not had anybody's consent to it and that the record about the nineteen hearing it says not asked.

A clerk entered that a bell is not a figure and is not a bearer and is not a keeper and is not one of the three documents this district does not own, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and a seventh was proposed twice in two days and refused on the page both times, and that a bell four miles out past the loams is not a seventh and the six did not move on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month.

A man of fifty-six said one thing about it, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and nobody counted it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody counted it and that the record about the not counting says not asked.

He said that he had lived in this basin fifty-six years and had heard that bell twice, and that both times he had been in a field, and that both times he had gone and looked at it, and that neither time had anybody told him anything. He said that he was not afraid of tonight. He said that he was afraid of the morning after and that he was not able to write down what he was afraid of, and that a man of fifty-six who cannot write down what he is afraid of ought to be careful about being the first person in this district to say a sentence like that in a yard.

And then he said the rest of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it in sixty-one words, and that the sixty-one words are these.

> **I AM GOING TO SAY IT ANYWAY. FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN PEOPLE WERE COUNTED ON A SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST BY KNOCKING ON DOORS, AND A BELL TAKES NAMES OF EVERYBODY WHO HEARS IT AND DOES NOT KNOCK, AND I WOULD LIKE SOMEBODY WHO IS NOT ME TO WORK OUT IN THE NEXT TEN DAYS WHETHER THOSE ARE THE SAME ACT.**

Nobody in that yard said anything for about nine seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a yard going quiet for about nine seconds is not a refusal and is not a consent, and that the record about the nine seconds says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the record that came into force on the first of this month was not going to be read out again in that yard, and a man of fifty-six asked him why not, and he gave a reason, and the count came to a hundred and forty.

"**Because a yard in which things are read out is a yard in which things are kept, and that is the second of the four lines on the sheet nailed to that gatepost since the fifteenth of this month, and I have read seven times out of that yard a record that has been in force twenty-three days and that none of us has answered, and I am not going to read it an eighth time at a quarter past ten in the morning on the day we are ringing a bell four miles off. If anybody in this yard wants to hear it read out then say so now and I will read it, and if nobody says so then nobody in this district has asked for an eighth reading and I am not going to hand them one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refused reading is not a reading and that the readings of that record in that yard are seven, and that a man who refuses to read a thing out loud has not refused the thing and cannot be counted either way about it, and that the record about nobody saying so says not asked, and that that record is not the fourth line of any offer and the fourth line of any offer has not been read out in this district this month.

---

About half past five that afternoon a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing tied nine inches of a rope that has been inside a beam for nine years onto the end of a length of new rope about two feet long that the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart brought down to the end of the lane behind the bank in the middle of the afternoon without being asked, and he tied it with a knot in his right hand because his left hand has the skin off the inside of it from the wrist to the elbow, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a knot tied by a man for himself is not a job and is not a bearer and did not become an instrument, and that the record about the cart says not asked.

A sheet of paper about as wide as a page was folded in four and laid flat on the table with four legs that stands against the outside wall of that building beside the front door, and a piece of chalk was put beside it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud, to about nine people, that anybody who wanted to be on that paper after the bell stopped could write their own name on it and nobody else and nobody would stop them and nobody would be asked why, and that anybody who did not want to be on it did not have to give a reason and would not be asked for one.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a blank piece of paper on a table outside a door is not a page and is not a document and is not a copy and is not one of the three documents this district does not own, and that a piece of paper on which a person may write their own name and nobody else's is not a column of people and is not a bearing and is not a consent, and that a person who has not written on it has not refused it and cannot be counted either way and the record about them says not asked.

About nine people slept in that room that night and were gone before six in the morning of the twenty-fifth and nobody sent for one of them and nobody fetched one of them, and the record about the nine says not asked.

---

At about half past one in the morning the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came down the lane behind the bank from the far end of it and stood in the doorway of the building with two doors, and he was not asked to be there and nobody met him on the road, and a clerk entered that a man standing in a doorway is not holding the door and is not a bearer of it and is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything.

At about two o'clock in the morning the bell began.

It is a cracked bell and it does not sound like a bell. It sounds like somebody running the flat of a hand along the rim of a pan, and it does not stop and start, and it goes on for longer than the people in that lane expect, and about two hundred yards up the lane from the one the man was standing in, the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was in a house with the door shut and a blanket over her shoulders and did not come out of it while it rang.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the woman who keeps that scale was not out of her own house while that bell was going and that nobody sent for her and that the record about her not being sent for says not asked, and that a person who is in a house with the door shut has not been asked anything and has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way.

The last person through the front door of the building with two doors was a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road, who had been at the top of the bank and had come down, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that it was about nine minutes from the first sound to that man, and that he had said it out loud to the lane and that nobody had counted it but him, and that a man standing still in the dark with a hand that does not close is the worst person in this basin to time anything, and that a figure about nine minutes given by that man is a figure about nine minutes and is not a figure about this district.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about nineteen who counts stood in that lane from about two in the morning and counted the people who came through that front door and got a number, and that he would not give the number to a clerk, and that he said out loud that a number a man counts in the dark at the end of a lane is a figure about a man counting and not about the people, and that he had said on the eleventh of this month that a mat is not a figure about anybody and that a person in a doorway at two in the morning is not a figure about anybody either.

The bell stopped. For about nine seconds after it stopped nobody in that lane said anything, and then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came out of her own door with the blanket still over her shoulders and stood in it, and said one thing out loud, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record about the nine seconds says not asked.

"**I have stood at that scale for nine years and I have never once heard it, and I have been inside that door the whole of it with the blanket on, and I have been standing in it since it stopped and I do not know yet whether to shut it.**"

Then about nine people wrote their own names on a piece of paper on a table outside a door, one at a time, in the ordinary hand of each of them, in the dark, and nobody wrote anybody else's name and nobody asked anybody what they were called, and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat wrote hers and then stood about four feet back and watched the others do it and did not say anything to anybody about what she was doing.

And then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam took the paper off the table.

It took him four goes. He put the fingers of his right hand on the near edge of it and the paper went about nine inches across the boards and stopped, and he did it again and it went about four inches and stopped, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a hand that has not closed since the ditch behind that building on the eleventh of this month cannot lift a sheet of paper off a table and that this was said out loud in that lane by the man himself in the ordinary voice and that nobody asked him about it.

On the third go he got two fingers under it and it came up off the boards and it went on the ground at his feet, and he picked it up off the ground with two fingers, and folded it in four the way it had been folded, and put it inside his coat.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who takes a thing off a table is not a bearer of it and is not a holder of it and is not a keeper of it and has not been asked to keep it, and that what is inside a coat in a lane at about twenty past two in the morning is not a book and is not a page and is not a document and did not become a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a person who carries a thing is not a keeper of it and that the record about where it was put says not asked.

A man of about fifty-six said one thing at the end of that lane, out loud, to about nine people, and it was counted and came to eighty-eight.

"**It worked. Nine minutes and everybody in that lane was inside a building with two doors and nobody was hurt and nobody has been entered in anything, and I have been in this basin fifty-six years and I have never once seen a thing work. Now go to bed, all of you, because in about four hours this district is going to have to say out loud what it did tonight and most of what it is going to say is going to be for the first time.**"

The day came down about half past three in the morning and the twenty-fourth of the sixth month was the day that bell was rung in and about nine people had come into a building with two doors and had written their own names on a piece of paper on a table outside it and a sheet about as wide as a page was inside a coat belonging to a man of about thirty-four whose hand has not closed since the eleventh, and a strip of skin the length of a forearm was drying on a man of about thirty-four at a frame four miles out past the loams.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A BELL FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS WAS PULLED AND RANG FOR ABOUT NINE MINUTES AT ABOUT TWO IN THE MORNING, AND EVERYBODY IN THE LANE BEHIND THE BANK WENT INTO A BUILDING WITH TWO DOORS, AND ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WROTE THEIR OWN NAMES ON A PIECE OF PAPER ON A TABLE OUTSIDE IT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR TOOK IT OFF THE TABLE IN FOUR GOES AND PUT IT INSIDE HIS COAT.**
