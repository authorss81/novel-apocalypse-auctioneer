# Chapter 276: Two Shapes Of A Lot

---

The twenty-sixth of the sixth month was clear and windy and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there sixty-four days and nobody had sat in it on any of the sixty-four.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and eighty-four days on the board and five hundred days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and fourteen, and a hundred and seventy-five days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the month after next is thirty-six days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is sixty-seven days back, and there are two shapes of a piece of paper on that table and I would like this yard to have heard both of them before anybody signs either one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the sixty-six days since the twenty-first of the month before last, being one signed, two refused and three not asked again, and that the figure is his and that a clerk did not ask him for it this morning and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against put both of the shapes out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and sixty-seven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The first shape is a lot about the night. It is going into the book four hundred yards from that gate that a stranger may walk up and read, in the hand of the person who keeps that book, and it says that a bell was rung at about two in the morning on the night of the twenty-fourth, four miles out past the loams, and it says the names of everybody in this district who heard that bell. Not the people who came through one door out of about nine hundred yards of lane. Everybody who heard it. And that includes a child of about eight who is two miles the other way, and the child has to be in it, because a lot about a night that leaves out a person who was in the night is not a lot about the night, it is a lot about the people who could be written down. The second shape is a lot about the room. It says the bell was rung, it says nine people came into a building with two doors, it says nine names in nine hands, it says on its own face that it is a record of a room and not a record of a night, and it says that there is a person in this district who heard that bell and is not on it, and it describes that person and does not name that person, and it says that the record the bell made is not a paper and this district has never seen it and cannot take it off.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that both of those shapes are lots and that a lot in this district is an instrument and a document and that neither creates a consent and neither makes a person a bearer and neither makes a house into a party and neither turns a right to give something up into a refusal, and that two shapes of a lot are not a vote and that nobody in that yard was asked which of them they wanted and that the record about the nineteen says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing argued for the first shape, out loud, and it was counted and came to two hundred and sixty-two, and a clerk entered that nobody in that yard argued with him about the second shape being the safer one.

"**I have spent five months in this yard telling anybody who would stand still that a document which names nothing is the only safe kind there is, and I have said it so many times that I have stopped hearing it, and I heard it on the eighth of this month from a man of fifty-six saying that a printed rule was better than his own and I have been ill about it for nine days. A lot about the room is a hole with a reason printed under it. That is the exact shape of the third column of the covenant in the book a stranger may walk up and read, and that column has been ruled and empty since the nineteenth of the month before last because the people who would go in it have not been asked, and a hole printed on a sheet is not the same shape of thing as a hole left there by nobody asking, and those are the words of a man of about twenty-four years ago in this district and I am not going to pretend I have forgotten them. I want the child's name in that book. He is eight. He will be forty when somebody could take it off him, and I have watched a name go into a yard in this district on the seventeenth of this month and I would rather it went in on a page that somebody can be angry at than stay off it for the rest of his life because four grown people were frightened.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the twenty-sixth of this month, and that a hole printed on a sheet is not the same shape of thing as a hole left there by nobody asking, and that the two are not joined and were not joined in that yard this morning.

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, who had stood at that scale nine years and weighed about nine sacks a day, said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, and it was counted and came to a hundred and eighty, and a clerk entered that she said it before she was asked and that the record about her not being asked says not asked.

"**You are both talking about a child and neither of you has been in the room. I have four empty houses standing up that lane and I said on the twentieth of this month that they are not going to be offered again before the first day of the season after this one, and the reason is that a house is not a household and a household is not a person and a person is not a column, and a man of about twenty-three stood up on the end of that table on the seventeenth of this month and said forty-one names out loud and every one of those people found out about it from a stranger, and that was eight days ago. You want the child's name in a book in this basin. I want to know what happens to him in ten years, because ten years is a long time to be a thing a stranger may walk up and read, and there is nobody in this district who could tell me, including the man asking the question.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a scale is not a clerk of a house and that a woman who has said a thing before being asked has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and that a woman who keeps houses is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a house and is not a keeper of anything.

---

At about half past three in the afternoon the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households came into that yard on his own two feet and put a book on the end of the trestle table face down as he does, and the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing asked him to hold the page open while he measured a thing, and he held it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a count of six households was asked to hold a page and that nobody asked him whether he would, and that a man who holds a page is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a book and is not a keeper of anything, and that the record about the holding says not asked.

He held it for about an hour. At about half past four in the afternoon he stood at the top of the bank with a figure on a piece of paper in his own hand and held it out at the length of his own arm and could not bring it into focus, and he said so out loud to about four people, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it in seventy-four words before he said anything else, and that the seventy-four words are these.

> **I AM NOT GOING TO BLIND MYSELF OVER A PIECE OF PAPER. HOLDING A PAGE IS NOT READING A PAGE AND I HAVE BEEN ASKED TO HOLD ONE AND I HELD IT FOR AN HOUR BECAUSE A MAN WITH A BAD HAND ASKED ME TO, AND I CANNOT SEE A FIGURE AT THE LENGTH OF MY OWN ARM THIS EVENING, AND I AM TELLING THIS BANK THAT INSTEAD OF WALKING DOWN IT WITH IT.**
>
> **MY SIX HAVE NOT MOVED ON ANY OF THE SIXTY-SIX DAYS AND I AM NOT GOING TO BE A MAN WHO READS A FIGURE OUT OF A LOT ABOUT A NIGHT.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure a man will not repeat is not a refusal and is not a consent, and that the six households are one signed, two refused and three not asked again, and that the man who keeps them has not moved them and has not been asked to move them and that a man of forty-eight who says of himself that he is fifty-one is not going to be asked a second time, and that his eyes are his and are not a figure about anybody.

---

About eleven in the morning the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road, who is not the tally keeper and is not the man who digs loam and is not the man who mends fencing, came through that gate and said one thing about a figure of four hundred yards, and it was counted and came to a hundred and ninety-two, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about.

"**Nine minutes. I walked my four miles this morning before I came down here and I have been the only man in this district walking towards that bell since it went. It is four miles out past the loams and I have walked it in a quarter of an hour and a quarter of an hour is about as long as it takes a person to get a coat on and get a child up and get out of a door, and that is the whole of what I came to say. A sheet of paper cannot do that. I have kept a road for eleven years and I have never once said that a road is a person and I am not saying it now. I am saying that a thing four miles off and a door two hundred yards from me are about a quarter of an hour apart on foot in the dark, and that anything this district is going to write down tonight is going to be read by somebody who is not in it and who will not be a quarter of an hour away from it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and has never been asked to be one, and that a man who walks four miles before he comes into a yard has not been sent and that the record about the walking says not asked, and that a quarter of an hour is a figure about two distances and is not a figure about anybody.

---

At about half past six in the evening, in about nine feet of lane outside the office, about eight feet from the door of the room where the books are, the woman who keeps the books in that room said one thing to the man the figure of twenty-one years is against, and there was nobody in that lane who had been asked to count either of them.

"**I said on the twenty-second of this month that I would put my own hand on a piece of paper about a bell, and I have not done it and I am not going to do it tonight. I want you to hear the reason in a lane and not in that yard, because in that yard everything I say is a thing about nineteen people and I have spent nine years keeping myself to one. I can write the second of your two shapes in my hand. I can write the first of your two shapes in my hand as well, and that is not what I am refusing. What I am refusing is to be the hand that puts a child of about eight into a book in this basin and then goes back to being the person who keeps it, and I have told you on the twentieth of last month that I would not be told a thing back, and I am not going to start tonight.**"

And he said one thing back, and it was not counted, and the minute is in the same hand as hers and is not in a book.

"**Then I will not do it in your hand. I will write the second shape out longhand in this yard on the twenty-eighth of this month in front of about nineteen people and read it out and read it back, and then I will bring it to that door and you will put your own hand underneath mine, and if you will not then we will find out what a lot is without a registrar's name at the bottom of it, and I do not know what that is either, and I am telling you tonight so that you have got a night to think about it in and not four hours.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who keeps a book in one room by one person is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer and is not a holder and is not a steward and is not a party of anything, and that a registrar who has said out loud that she will not put her hand to a thing has not refused the thing and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who has been refused five things in a month and granted the sixth and been refused a seventh has not acquired anything by any of the seven, and that the record about the night to think about it in says not asked.

The day came down about half past eight and the wind went round to the north, and two shapes of a lot were lying on the end of a trestle table in a yard a stranger can walk into and nothing had been signed and nothing had been chosen and no paper had gone anywhere, and the record about the not choosing says not asked.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH TWO SHAPES OF A LOT WERE PUT OUT LOUD IN A YARD BY A MAN WHO CONVENES NINE SETTLEMENTS, AND A MAN WHO MENDS FENCING SAID THAT THE ONE WITH THE HOLE IN IT IS THE SAME SHAPE AS THE THIRD COLUMN OF THE COVENANT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-EIGHT HELD A PAGE OPEN FOR AN HOUR AND THEN COULD NOT SEE A FIGURE AT THE LENGTH OF HIS OWN ARM, AND NOTHING WAS SIGNED.**
