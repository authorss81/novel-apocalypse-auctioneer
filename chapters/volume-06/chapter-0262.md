# Chapter 262: The Same Question Twice

---

The twelfth of the sixth month came in low and grey and about eleven people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there fifty days and nobody had sat in it on any of the fifty.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and seventy days on the board and four hundred and eighty-six days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred, and a hundred and sixty-one days separate the second of January and this morning, and that is twenty-three weeks and no days, and the first day of the month after next is fifty days off counted off this morning, and the record that came into force on the first of this month has now been in force eleven days and nobody in this yard has answered it and nobody is going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the three figures were carried off the mouth of the man who gave them, and that a hundred and sixty-one is twenty-three weeks and no days counted off the second of January and not off a page, and that fifty is twenty and thirty-one and one, and that a record being in force and unanswered is not a smaller thing than a record not being in force and unanswered.

Then the man of fifty-six said the part about the yard, and it was counted and came to a hundred and twenty-five, and a clerk entered that nobody said it after him.

"**Six of you. There were nineteen in here on the first of this month and eleven on the fourth and there are six this morning and I have not seen one person walk out of that gate carrying anything, and I am telling you that a yard with nine fewer people in it is not a yard with a different opinion in it, it is a yard where nine people have gone somewhere else to say a thing, and I do not know where they have gone to and I am not going to ask, and I am not going to stand in here being pleased about it because being pleased about a quiet yard is how a yard becomes a room with a roof.**"

A clerk entered that a clerk does not count the people in a yard as a figure about anybody and that a man of about nineteen who counts has not been asked for the number and that the record about the six says not asked.

---

About half way up the lane behind the bank, at about half past four in the afternoon, the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the step of two stones and asked the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road one question, in his own words and not in the six things, about how many doors there are in the ground in the shadow of the Hall, and the man who keeps a road said that he had been asked one question in this district since the first of the month that is now last month and had given one answer on the second of this month, and that a second time is a different question or it is the same question asked twice, and he did not know which and he was not going to find out by being asked again.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and has never been asked to be one, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a man who keeps a road is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move at about half past four in the afternoon on the twelfth of the sixth month, and that the number of doors in the ground in the shadow of the Hall is still not a figure about anybody and the record about it says not asked.

Then a clerk entered a thing that had been entered out of what the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said afterwards and not out of having been asked: that he had come down that lane on the way back from the four miles and had seen a man standing about nine hundred yards out on the eleven mile road with a case in his hand, and that it was a man of about forty-four, and that he had not gone out to him, and that nobody sent him out and nobody sent for the man, and that the record about the case says not asked.

---

At about two in the afternoon the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came down the lane to the lean-to where the scale is and asked the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, in his own words and not in the six things, whether she was going to tell him anything else about the ninth of the eleven houses, and she said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and the count came to a hundred and fifty-six.

"**On the third of this month I said out loud in that yard that there is a woman in the ninth of the houses she carries to who is being moved on the first of the month after next, and nobody asked me a question about it, and that is the only reason it is worth anything to anybody. If you ask me a question now then what I say next is an answer, and an answer is a thing with a person attached to it, and I have spent nine years at a scale not being a thing with a person attached to it. You have come down here and made me an answer. I would like it written down that I said no and that I would have liked not to have been asked, and that the two of those are not the same and that I am not going to pretend they are.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a scale is not a clerk of a house, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about two in the afternoon on the twelfth of the sixth month, and that the record about the ninth of the eleven houses says not asked, and that this is the third thing that question has cost somebody this month, the other two being the woman of fifty-eight and the man who asked it, and that a clerk who has now entered that three people have been touched by one question is not going to enter how many times it is asked.

---

At about six in the evening the woman of fifty-eight came down the bank with two buckets and put them down where she puts them, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing carried one of them up, and then he asked her the same question he had asked her on the fourth of this month, in his own words and not in the six things, which was whether the ninth of the eleven houses she carries water to is one of the four hundred and eleven on the sheet at the gatepost.

She looked at him for about as long as it takes to lift a bucket and she went up the bank with the empty one and she did not answer him.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that this is the second time the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing has asked that question and that nobody sent him to ask it, and that a person who has been asked a question and has not answered it has not refused it and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the ninth of the eleven houses says not asked, and that the sheet at that gatepost does not say which nine houses and does not have to, and that a clerk who is about to say that a man is going to go on asking a thing is about to make a habit of a man and is not going to.

A man of fifty-six said one sentence about it that was not counted, because there was nobody in that lane who had been asked to count it, and the record about that sentence came from the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale and is not the woman of fifty-eight and is not a clerk of a house.

"**He is going to ask her on the sixteenth and on the eighteenth and on the nineteenth and on every day after that until one of us is dead or she tells him, and the thing he does not know is that he is spending something of his own to do it, and the thing he does know is that nobody in this district is going to stop him, and those two things together are the whole of him this month.**"

---

In about nine feet of lane outside the office, about eight feet from the door of the room where the books are, at about half past seven in the evening, the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said a thing and the woman who keeps the books in that room said a thing back, and there was nobody there to count either of them, and the whole of it is in a minute in one hand and is not in a book, and a clerk of nineteen years was four hundred yards away and entered it afterwards out of his hand and not out of hers, and entered that a conversation is not a record and is not a document and did not become a fourth of the three documents this district does not own.

He offered to sit in that room four hundred yards away for the four nights of this month, so that she would not be at a desk at four in the morning again with her head on her arm, and he said it plainly and he said the reason too, and she refused in about four seconds and gave a reason.

"**No, and the reason is the rule you have just said out loud to me, and I wrote it in April. One person looks in that book. If the one person is you then it is not a rule, it is a preference, and a preference can be argued with at four in the morning by a man who has been awake since two, and I have been in this room for nine years and I have never once had to be argued with, and I am not starting this month.**"

And he said one thing back, in the same hand and the same minute.

"**That is the fourth time in a month you have refused me something and every one of the four has been right, and I am not going to argue with it, and I am also not going to stop offering, and those are two different things and you know which of them is worse.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a registrar who refuses a man a thing in a lane has not refused the man and is not one of the eight and that a registrar is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move at about half past seven in the evening on the twelfth of the sixth month, and that the man of the figure of twenty-one years is against is a Convener and is not a founder and is not a holder and is not a bearer of a toll and is not a party of anything, and that a man who has been refused four times in a month and has said he will go on offering has not acquired anything and the record about the fifth offer says not asked.

The day came down about half past eight. The sixth month was twelve days old and the building at the near end of the lane behind the bank slept about nine people in it, and the nail in the hasp of the plank door was where it had been on the eleventh, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart went past that lane at about nine with a cart and did not stop.

**AND ON THE TWELFTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED THE WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT THE SAME QUESTION HE ASKED HER ON THE FOURTH AND SHE DID NOT ANSWER HIM, AND A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WITH A CASE WAS SEEN STANDING ON THE ELEVEN MILE ROAD, AND A REGISTRAR REFUSED A MAN WHO OFFERED TO SIT IN HER ROOM FOUR NIGHTS RUNNING BECAUSE A RULE SHE WROTE HERSELF WOULD HAVE BECOMED A PREFERENCE.**
