# Chapter 298: Fourteen Days

---

The eighteenth of the seventh month came in low and grey and by about ten the air in the yard of Lot Seventeen had the weight of the day still in it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right, and about nineteen people were there.

"**Two hundred and six days on the board and five hundred and twenty-two days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and thirty-six, and a hundred and ninety-seven days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is fourteen days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is eighty-nine days back, and the table against that wall stood up for the twelfth time this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that fourteen is thirteen days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated and was not used this morning, and that **this is the first morning on which the day on that sheet at that gatepost is a fortnight off or less**, and that a clerk could have written a fortnight on the board this morning and did not, and wrote fourteen, because a fortnight is a name for a shape and fourteen is a count off a day, **and that a round figure is a figure about a calculation and is not a figure about anybody**, and that the record about the not writing a fortnight says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came through the gate at about half past ten, having been out to the far side of the eleven mile road and back that morning, and he said one thing in that yard, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and fifty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**On the eleventh of this month I said in this yard that a sixth morning at that table is a figure nobody has asked for and a seventh morning is a habit, and that I was not going to be the man who came back here and said which of the two the seventh one was, and I have come back here on the eighteenth of this month because I said the eighteenth and I have said it. It was a habit. I have got four miles of that road and I walk it every day of a week and I am not going to tell this yard that makes the seventh morning small, because it does not. I went out there this morning and I sat down in the ditch at the far end of that field for about nine minutes at about half past eight and I could not get up the first time and I got up the second time, and that is what I have to say about the seventh morning, and I am saying it to about nineteen people in daylight because that is what this yard is. A habit and a figure are the same thing after a month and there is no test in this district that tells them apart, and I have looked for one for six days and I have not found it, and I am not going to be the man who pretends that not finding one is the same as there not being one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and has never been asked to be one, and that a habit is not a consent and is not a figure and cannot be counted either way, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and that the eight did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the eighteenth of the seventh month, and that a man who has come back into a yard on a day he named has not been asked to and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that **a man who cannot get out of a ditch the first time has not refused the ditch and cannot be counted either way**, and that a ditch is not a document and a habit is not a document and the two are not added together.

Then the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat, at the third house up that lane from the scale, said one thing at the foot of her own gate at about half past twelve in the afternoon, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that nobody asked her for it and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

"**I carried a copy of five lines up two miles to a room with nothing over the door on the second of this month because there were about nine people in it and none of them had heard what had been said in a yard four hundred yards away, and nobody asked me to carry it and nobody paid me and I would carry it again tomorrow if somebody wanted it carried. That is all. I am not going to say anything else about it and I would like it written down that I have not said anything else about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a goat is not a keeper of a gate and is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a copy and is not a keeper of anything, and that a copy is not a document and did not become a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a woman who has said out loud that she would carry a thing again has not agreed to carry it again and has not refused to and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not saying anything else says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and the man of fifty-six were at the top of the bank at about half past four in the afternoon, about six feet apart, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two of them were not in that yard and that about six people in it knew where they were and that nobody was sent.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud on the top of that bank and it was counted and came to a hundred and seventy-nine, and a clerk entered that he said it in the ordinary voice and that nobody at the top of that bank was going to be able to answer it.

"**I have been a man who builds documents for fifty months in this basin and the whole of what I have ever wanted out of one is that it says what it does not know, and this morning a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road stood in a yard and told about nineteen people that he could not tell a habit from a figure, and I want to say what I think that is, and it is not encouraging. A document that says what it does not know is honest and it stops somebody. A man who says he cannot tell a habit from a figure is also honest and it does not stop anybody at all, because there is nothing in it to stop anything. I have spent fifty months on the first kind and I do not know what the second kind is for, and I have not got a better version of it this evening, and I am a man who reads things out loud in yards and I have got nothing to read.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a document and is not a party of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he has got nothing to read has not refused to read and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking him for a better version says not asked, and that the columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to, and that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that two men on the top of a bank are not a meeting and did not become one.

The woman of fifty-eight was at the bank with two buckets at about half past three in the afternoon and stayed about forty minutes, and she said one thing to nobody in particular while she was filling the second of them, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted, that two buckets were not counted, that nobody counted them, and that the record about the not counting says not asked.

"**Fourteen days. I have carried water to eleven houses for nineteen years and I am not going to stand at this bank this afternoon and count any of them and I am not going to say the figure out loud, and there is a man of about thirty-four who digs loam in this district who could count them in about four minutes if anybody asked him, and nobody is going to ask him, because I would not answer and because a house in the ground does not come out and stand at a bank. I am fifty-eight years old and I have said in a yard on the sixth of this month that the woman in that house is not on the list of the people who are to be moved, and I am not going to say it again, and I have said it today, and that is twice, and I would like it entered that it was twice and not once.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman of fifty-eight who carries water to eleven houses is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a figure and is not a keeper of a house and is not a keeper of anything, and that no figure about those eleven houses was produced on the eighteenth of the seventh month, and that a house in the ground is not one of the four hundred and eleven on the sheet at that gatepost and is not on any sheet in this district, and that the ninth of the eleven is one of the five different ninths and that the five are not added together, and that a woman who has said a thing twice and would like that entered has not asked to say it a third time and cannot be counted either way, and that no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved on the eighteenth of the seventh month.

She entered the rest of the eighteenth of this month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the hundred and nine days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood eighty-six days and nobody has sat in it on any of the eighty-six, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the eighty-eight days since the twenty-first of the fourth month and that she did not ask him, and that the documents this district does not own are three and that one document came into that yard on the twelfth of this month and did not become a fourth of them, and that no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved on the eighteenth of the seventh month and that four hundred and eleven is on it and that it is the figure the fourth of the five lines of print says is to be completed before the first of the eighth month, and that a figure which is to be completed and a figure which has been completed are two different states, **and that nobody in this district has asked anybody which of the two that figure is in, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.**

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was eighteen days old and the first day of the eighth month was fourteen days off, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road had come back into a yard on a day he had named seven days before and said that the seventh morning at a table eleven miles off was a habit.

**AND ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH THE DAY ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS FOR THE FIRST TIME A FORTNIGHT OFF OR LESS, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD CAME BACK INTO THE YARD ON A DAY HE HAD NAMED AND SAID THAT THE SEVENTH MORNING AT THAT TABLE WAS A HABIT, AND SAID THAT A HABIT AND A FIGURE ARE THE SAME THING AFTER A MONTH AND THAT HE HAD LOOKED FOR A TEST FOR SIX DAYS AND HAD NOT FOUND ONE.**
