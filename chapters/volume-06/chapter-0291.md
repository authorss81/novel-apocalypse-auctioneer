# Chapter 291: A Table a Man Cannot Set Up

---

The eleventh of the seventh month was already hot at nine and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and ninety-nine days on the board and five hundred and fifteen days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and twenty-nine, and a hundred and ninety days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-one days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is eighty-two days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and nine days.**"

She entered the derivation of the twenty-one before she entered anything else, because a figure somebody says out loud in a yard is not a figure until somebody has said where it was counted from. Twenty-one is twenty days to the end of the seventh month, because this morning is the eleventh of it and eleven of its thirty-one days have gone, plus the first of the eighth. **The length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning and is not to be used on any of the ten days this block reaches.** And then she entered the whole of the ladder, in the ordinary voice, because a clerk who has ten mornings of a figure in front of her and writes down one of them has not written down a ladder: twenty-one, twenty, nineteen, eighteen, seventeen, sixteen, fifteen, fourteen, thirteen, twelve, and the first of those ten was counted off this morning and not off the tenth of this month, which is the last morning of the ten before it, and the last of the ten is the count off the twentieth of this month.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the end of the trestle table with his hand-cart still against the wall inside the gate, and he said one thing about his own two hands before anybody asked him for it, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the record about the not counting says not asked.

"**I cannot set that table up on my own. I have not been able to since the morning of the tenth and I am not going to be able to tomorrow or the day after, and I am saying it here at about ten in the morning this morning instead of saying it at that wall at about eight, because if I do not say it in this yard then somebody finds out at that wall, and a person is told things at a wall in the morning a good deal worse than they are told in a yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who puts tables up is not a keeper of a table and is not a bearer of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that his body is going to be the price of a thing is not a consent and is not a party of anything, and that **a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and the eight did not move at about ten in the morning on the eleventh of the seventh month**, and that a man who has said a thing before he was asked it has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was at the bank at about half past ten and said nothing whatever to anybody, and got the far end of the trestle board up onto his right shoulder, and went down the eleven mile road on his own two feet with a board about six feet long on that shoulder and the other nine hundred yards of his life.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked eleven yards with him as far as the gate, which takes two goes, and then went back into the yard and was there for the rest of the morning.

---

At about two in the afternoon the board was on the shoulder of the man of about thirty-four who digs loam inside a gate that is not on a hinge, about two feet of grass and a field about two hundred yards across, and he could not get it down off the shoulder, because his right arm has not gone above the level of that shoulder since the first of this month, and he stood under the end of it with his face against the wood for about nine seconds. The man of about thirty-seven got his two forearms under the other end of it, because his hands do not close on anything, and the two of them brought it down between them onto the grass.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two men put one table down and that neither of them could have put it down alone, and that the nine seconds are not a consent and are not a refusal, and that the record about the nine seconds says not asked, and that two bodies on one end of a board are not a party of anything and are not entered together anywhere.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came through the gate on his way back from the far side of the eleven mile road and said one thing about the mornings, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was counted and came to a hundred and forty-seven, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him what it was about.

"**I walked out to that wall this morning before I came through here and that table has stood up on five mornings, and I have not got a figure of anybody who stood at it and I am not going to have one this morning either. I want to say one thing about the five and then I am going to stop. A sixth morning is a figure nobody has asked for. A seventh morning is a habit. Those are two different shapes and I am not going to be the man who comes back in here on the eighteenth of this month and tells this yard which of the two the seventh one was, because a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road has got four miles of that road to walk and no business turning five mornings into a finding on the way past.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure entered this morning is five mornings and that it is a figure about a table and not about anybody who stood at it, and that the man of about thirty-seven refused to give a figure about the people at that table on the ninth of this month with a reason and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that the eight did not move at about half past three in the afternoon on the eleventh of the seventh month, and that a man who says out loud that he is not going to be the man who enters a thing has not refused to enter it and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not entering says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came through the gate at about half past five in the evening and put his hand up to about the height of his own shoulder and could not get it any higher than that, and said one thing about it out loud, and it was counted and came to a hundred and eighteen, and a clerk entered that about four people in that yard saw him do it and that none of them said anything.

"**That arm has not gone above the shoulder on any of the ten days since the first of this month and I am telling this yard on the eleventh because on the twelfth it will be a thing I have not mentioned today. I am not asking for anything. I am saying that I am the only man in this district who can put that board on that shoulder and I am going to keep being the only man in this district who can put that board on that shoulder for as long as my arm behaves, and that is not a figure anybody should write down, it is a man telling a yard what he has got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who digs loam is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a table and is not a keeper of anything, and that two men said two things about the same arm on the same afternoon in a yard and that neither of them was said to the other, and that the record about the not saying it to the other says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them asked the man of about thirty-four who digs loam one question in that yard at about a quarter past five in the afternoon, and nobody else in that yard heard it, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam answered it out loud, and it was counted and came to two hundred and ten-eight, and a clerk entered that he was asked one question and that nobody in that yard was asked to be present for it.

"**Can you do it again tomorrow. That is the whole question and I am not going to dress it up. I cannot carry anything and there is a board six feet long and it has got to be eleven miles from where it is now, and there is nobody in these nine settlements who can do it but you, and I would rather ask you in a yard in front of about nineteen people than find out at that wall at eight in the morning. And I want to say the other half of it before you answer, which is that I know what a man says when a man is going to say yes, and you have not said it, and I am not going to let you say it out of being the man who says it. I have got a tent on that cart and a wall four feet high and a field about two hundred yards across, and the first of my five lines says a table under a lean-to, and there is no lean-to, and there is going to be a table under nothing at all, and a man who digs loam is the only thing standing between that and a sentence somebody could say out loud about me in four hundred miles.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he knows what a man is going to say has not been given a thing and has not refused one, and that a man who was asked one question and answered it has not consented to anything and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who puts tables up is not a keeper of a table and is not a bearer of an answer, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and that a question about a shoulder is not a seventh of them and did not become one, and that the six did not move on the eleventh of the seventh month.

She entered the rest of what had not moved on the eleventh of the seventh month in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph. She entered that nobody put a line on a board on any of the hundred and two days since the first of the fourth month counting the first day in, and that the chair at the end of that trestle table has stood seventy-nine days and nobody has sat in it on any of the seventy-nine, and that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the eighty-one days since the twenty-first of the fourth month and that she did not ask him and that a man of about forty-eight who says of himself that he is fifty-one is not going to be asked a second time. She entered that the refusals of eleven coppers a week are nine, and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are eight, and that the refusals about the ninth holding are seven, and that the nine, the eight and the seven are three different figures and are not added together. She entered that the rival record has now been in force forty days and has been read out in that yard seven times and that the eighth reading was refused out loud on the twenty-fourth of last month with a reason and that a refused reading is not a reading and that nobody has answered that record.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was eleven days old and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-one days off, and a table was standing against a wall about four feet high eleven miles off this district that two men had put down and neither of them could have put down alone.

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN SAID OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN THAT HE COULD NOT SET HIS OWN TABLE UP ON HIS OWN AND HAD NOT BEEN ABLE TO SINCE THE TENTH, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM CARRIED IT ELEVEN MILES ON HIS OWN SHOULDER AND COULD NOT GET IT DOWN AT THE FAR END, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD SAID THAT A SIXTH MORNING AT THAT TABLE IS A FIGURE NOBODY HAS ASKED FOR AND A SEVENTH IS A HABIT, AND HE WAS NOT GOING TO BE THE MAN WHO ENTERED WHICH OF THE TWO IT WAS.**
