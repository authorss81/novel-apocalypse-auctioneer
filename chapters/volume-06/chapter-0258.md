# Chapter 258: A Line Of Print

---

The eighth of the sixth month was overcast and warm and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there forty-six days and nobody had sat in it on any of the forty-six.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and sixty-six days on the board and four hundred and eighty-two days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and ninety-six, and a hundred and fifty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning, and a sheet came into this yard in the night with three lines on it and the middle one of the three is a job.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet was on the trestle table face up and had not been there at half past six the evening before, and that nobody put it there, and that a sheet that arrives in a yard is a document somebody owns and is not one of the three this district does not own and did not become a fourth of them.

A clerk of nineteen years asked for it to be read, because a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read it out in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them and read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **THE BUILDING AT THE NEAR END OF THE LANE BEHIND THE BANK, WHICH HAS TWO DOORS, IS TO BE CLOSED ON THE ELEVENTH DAY OF THIS MONTH.**
>
> **IT IS TO BE CLOSED BY ITS KEEPER.**
>
> **A KEEPER IS A PERSON WHO HAS BEEN ASKED TO KEEP IT AND HAS SAID YES. THERE IS NO COLUMN FOR A PERSON WHO HAS NOT BEEN ASKED AND THE COLUMN FOR THE ONE WHO HAS IS EMPTY.**

Nobody in that yard said anything for about nine seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet was printed with its own hole in it, that the hole is on the third of the three lines and not the second, and that a hole printed on a sheet is not the same shape of thing as a hole left there by nobody asking, and that a sheet that carries a job and a rule about the job and an empty column for the job is a document that has found nobody and has said so in print.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked who wrote it, in his own words and not in the six things, and nobody knew, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet carries no name of a person and no name of a body and no name of a place, and that a piece of paper with three lines on it and nothing else on it is the exact shape of the page that came out of a cave with nine places ruled on it, and that the difference between the two is which side of the door it came in by.

He said the other half of that himself and it was not counted, because there was nobody who had been asked to count and the record about it is in a minute in his own words and is not in a book, and a clerk entered that the minute exists and that the sentence is in it and that the sentence is four hundred yards from the office it is about.

"**Five months I have spent telling anybody who would stand still that a document which names nothing is the only safe kind there is. That is on a nail at that gate with a job in the middle of it and an empty column at the bottom of it and not one word on it about who wants the door shut, and it is the best piece of printing anybody in this basin has put up in my lifetime, and I am not going to pretend to this yard that I have not noticed what that does to everything I have said.**"

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the thing about the second line and the count came to a hundred and sixty-three.

"**It is to be closed by its keeper. That is the whole of the threat and it is not a threat about a door, it is a threat about a person, and it has gone looking for a person in the only place a document can look, which is a column, and the column is empty and the sheet says so. In this basin there has never been a keeper of anything on any page and there never was one, and I have been in this yard nine mornings out of ten since the second of April saying the plainest thing I can find, and the plainest thing I can find this morning is that somebody four hundred miles away has just written down a job and a rule for filling it and has left it empty, and that is the first time in six months that anybody has written down a way of moving a person that does not need the person.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a mark may not carry a job, and that a printed line may not carry a job either, and that a document which names a job that does not exist has found nobody and that the record about the keeper says not asked, and that a keeper has never been on a page in this basin and there never was one and no keeper is named.

Then a man of fifty-six said the half of it that was about the shape, and nobody had asked him, and it was counted and came to a hundred and thirteen.

"**I have been in this yard since the second of April refusing to let a document do a job that a person has not been asked to do, and I have said it a hundred times in five months and every time I have said it a person has nodded and gone on doing what they were doing. This morning a body four hundred miles away has written my sentence down and printed it and put an empty column under it, and it is better than mine, and the reason it is better is that mine needed somebody in the room to agree with it and that one does not need anybody anywhere.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six has said that his own rule is worse than a printed one and that this is the second time in two months that he has said a thing out loud that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said better first, and that a shape noticed a second time is not a finding and that a clerk is not going to enter what happens the third time.

---

About nine people went out to the rise about a mile out past the loams in the middle of the morning, because the cloud on that side of the valley was thin, and nobody sent for anybody and a clerk entered that they went on their own two feet and that the record about the going says not asked.

The Hall is about eleven miles off in a straight line, in the country above the water, and it is a long low building with a roof on it that is not a roof you can cover a house with and a wall on the end of it that does not come down to the ground.

They stood on that rise for about twenty minutes and a man of about nineteen who counts counted the two of them that can be counted, which are the two ends of it and the notch in the middle of the roof, and he counted nothing else and said so, and a clerk entered that he counted two things and declined the rest, and that the eleven miles is a figure about a distance and not about a building and is not a toll and is not a rate.

A man of fifty-six stood at the front of that rise and looked at it for about nine minutes without saying anything and then said one sentence, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and thirty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been in this basin fifty-six years and I have never once been closer to that building than this rise, and I am not going to be now, and I would like somebody to write down that the reason is not that I am frightened of it. The reason is that everything this district has done since the second of April has been a way of asking a person a question, and a person on the end of a question is a person who has been asked, and I have not asked anybody anything in eleven miles and neither has anybody else in this party, and if I go any further I will be standing on a road in front of a door doing the thing that got a sheet on a gatepost.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and that he said it in a field about a mile out past the loams with about nine people standing behind him, and entered that nobody asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing whether he was right and that the record about his not being asked says not asked, and entered that a man who is frightened of a building and a man who is frightened of a question are two different men and that this district has only had the second kind so far and does not know which of them a man standing on a rise is.

Nobody in that party went any nearer and a clerk entered that nobody asked them to and that the record about the not going says not asked, and entered that a building eleven miles off that a person can see the two ends of from a rise in this district has never had anybody from this district inside it, and that this district has no figures at all about what is in it, and that the sheet at that gatepost on the first of this month is the first document anybody anywhere has given this district about it, and that the record about the sheet says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said one thing at the top of that rise and it was counted and came to a hundred and three.

"**I have kept four miles of that road for eleven years and there is a gate on it about a mile before the place where the ground stops, and everybody in this basin thinks the gate is the far end of the road. It is not. The road goes on past the gate for another seven miles and I have not walked the last of it in six years and I am not going to today, and nobody has ever asked me what is at the end of it, and I would like that written down as a thing nobody has asked me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and has never been asked to be one, that a road is not a document and is not a figure about anybody, and that the record about the seven miles and the six years says not asked, and that the four columns of not-askings in this district are four and she did not rule one and is not going to.

Back in the yard, a clerk of nineteen years entered the two people who are not in this district and cannot be given a day, and entered that the man of about forty-one who keeps a road house at the top of the road that climbs is standing outside his own door this morning, as he was on the fifteenth and the twentieth of last month, and was not asked a first thing, and that this district has never once given that man a day to be here on and gave him none this morning either, and entered that the man of about thirty at a counter is at the sixteenth time down the eleven mile road and the next is the seventeenth and that no chapter in this district has ever given that man a day.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about five with two buckets and put them down where she puts them and nobody counted them, and a clerk entered that nobody counted them and that the record about the two buckets says not asked and that the ninth of the eleven houses she carries to is a house in the ground in the shadow of the Hall and that the sheet at the gatepost does not say that and does not have to.

The day came down about half past six and the eleventh of this month was three days off, and a clerk entered that it is the eleventh and not the twelfth and that it was counted off this morning and not off a sheet.

**AND ON THE EIGHTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH A SHEET CAME INTO THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN IN THE NIGHT WITH THREE LINES ON IT, AND THE SECOND OF THE THREE SAID THAT A BUILDING WITH TWO DOORS IS TO BE CLOSED BY ITS KEEPER, AND THE THIRD OF THE THREE PRINTED THE RULE FOR THE JOB AND AN EMPTY COLUMN FOR THE PERSON WHO HAS BEEN ASKED, AND ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WENT A MILE OUT PAST THE LOAMS AND STOOD ON A RISE AND LOOKED AT A BUILDING ELEVEN MILES OFF AND WENT NO FURTHER.**
