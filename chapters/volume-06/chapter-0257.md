# Chapter 257: A Room With No Name Over The Door

---

The seventh of the sixth month was hot by nine and about eleven people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there forty-five days and nobody had sat in it on any of the forty-five.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and sixty-five days on the board and four hundred and eighty-one days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and ninety-five, and a hundred and fifty-six days separate the second of January and this morning, and there are about nine people in a room about two miles the other way this evening and I am going to walk there on my own two feet and I am not going to take anybody with me and I am not going to come back with a list.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody sent for anybody and that the record about two miles and about the walking says not asked, and entered that a person who walks into a room has not been brought into it and can go out of it again whenever she likes.

---

The room is behind a building about two miles the other way on a lane that has no name on anything, and it is about nine feet by fourteen, and there is nothing over the door. Not a board, not a paper, not a mark, not a line of charcoal and not a line of stones, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that on the page after the fact and entered that a door with nothing over it is not an instrument of this district and is not a bearer and is not a party of anything and is not a seventh of the six.

There were about nine people in it. The woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room, who has kept it for four years and who was in it on the night of the second, was sitting on a bench against the far wall with a piece of chalk and a slate on her knees, and she is not the woman of fifty-eight and is not the woman who keeps a scale and is not the woman who keeps a route and is not the woman who keeps a goat, and a clerk entered that four women keep four things inside four hundred yards of each other in this basin and that none of the four is a clerk of a house.

What that room trades in is three things and a clerk entered the three and did not enter a fourth. It trades where about nineteen people slept last night. It trades which lane is under water where. And it trades the fact that a person has been in a place, said to about four other people and not written down.

The first of the three is said by pointing. There is a bench, and the bench is about nine feet long, and on the underside of the seat there are about eleven notches cut in the wood at intervals that are not even, and the notches are not a calendar and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room said out loud, before anybody asked her, that they are not a calendar and are not a count of nights and are not a count of people and that a clerk is welcome to look at them.

The second of the three is said by standing up. Nobody in that room raised their voice at any point between about nine in the evening and about half past ten, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not in the room and that she knows that from the woman of about twenty-nine and not from being there, and that the record about the volume of a room says not asked.

The third of the three is the only one that costs anything, and it is said by a person taking a risk on somebody else's behalf, and the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps that room said the thing about it, and it was counted and came to a hundred and fourteen.

"**I do not keep the nine. I keep the room and the bench and the piece of chalk and the eleven notches on the underside of the seat, and nine people tell me where they slept last night and I say it back to whoever needs it and nobody writes it. If I wrote it down then in four months somebody would come with a sheet, and if somebody comes with a sheet then the notches go, and if the notches go then I have got a room with no name over the door and nothing else at all, and a room with nothing else at all is a room somebody else can have.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a room is not a clerk of a house, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that the eight did not move at about half past nine in the evening on the seventh of the sixth month, and that eleven notches cut in the underside of a bench are not five things and are not six and did not become a seventh instrument of the six and are not a column of not-askings and did not become a fifth column of the four.

What it cannot trade is a week of somebody's work, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that out loud in that room at about half past nine in the evening, and the woman of about twenty-nine said the rest of it, and it was counted and came to a hundred and seventy-three.

"**You are going to stand in my room and say the fifth thing this district does not have, and you are going to say it as though saying it out loud fixes it. A way to pay a person who is not in a household. I have had that hole since I was fifteen. I have watched four people go into the water and come out of it worse in front of me and I have had nothing to give them and I have had a bench and a slate and a piece of chalk and about nine people who do not sleep. No. And the reason I am giving you is that the five things this district does not have is a sentence a man reads out in a yard, and I have got a room with no name over the door, and if the fifth of your five is going to be the fifth of mine then somebody is going to come and ask me what I would take for it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a room is not a clerk of a house, that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that the eight did not move at about half past nine in the evening on the seventh of the sixth month, and that a bench and a slate and a piece of chalk are not five things and are not six and did not become a seventh instrument of the six.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing had come with him on his own two feet and had not been asked and was not asked a first thing, and a clerk entered that he was in the room and that nobody sent for him.

He offered to copy the list of the nine houses in that room, in his own hand, in about twenty minutes, for nothing, and she said no before he had finished the sentence and gave a reason, and it was counted and came to a hundred and nine.

"**No, and I will not even let you write the nine down first, because a list of nine houses in your hand is a list of nine houses that can be read, and a list that can be read is the only thing anybody in this basin has ever come for. You have been refused eight times in this district and you made the copies anyway, and I have watched you do it, and I would do it too, and that is exactly why you are not to have this one. I am not refusing you because you would sell it. I am refusing you because you would not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that the eight did not move, and that a refusal given in a room with about nine people in it is a refusal in a room and not a refusal in a yard, and that a yard a stranger can walk into is not the only place a sentence can be said and that this district has been wrong about that for five months.

A man of fifty-six, who had walked the two miles on his own two feet and had not been asked to and had not said why, said one thing about what a room with no name over it is, and it was counted and came to a hundred and twenty-two.

"**It is the only public instrument in this basin with no address on it, and I have been a man who reads figures out loud in a yard for eleven years and I have never once been in a room where there was nothing over the door, and I have been in about nine doorways in nine days and I thought that made me somebody who had seen both sides of a thing. I have not. A yard with nothing over the door is a counter of somebody else's the moment a stranger hears about it, and a room with nothing over the door is not, and the difference between the two is four hundred yards of road and about nine months.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that he was wrong has not withdrawn what he built and that the record about the nine months says not asked, and that a room with no name over a door is not a counter and is not a market and is not a chapter, and that a chair a man of about fifty-six is not in is not a chair anybody is in.

---

He came back down the two miles at about half past eleven at night and about four people were still in that yard, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam was not one of them because he had been at the room where the books are since about half past three in the afternoon, on his own two feet, to look at a sill that is not in that room, and he said afterwards that he had gone to see whether a person was in it and not to look at anything.

A clerk of nineteen years entered, out of what he said afterwards at about half past eleven at night and not out of having been there, that he had been in that room at about four in the morning on the seventh of this month, which is six hours before the yard was standing, and that he was not asked a first thing and that nobody sent for him, and that the registrar of this district's records office was asleep at the desk in that room with her head on her arm, and that her right hand was on the table with a pen in it and that the hand would not close on the pen when he put his own hand over it and asked her to close it, and that it stayed like that for about nine seconds and then she woke up and closed it and did not say anything about it for the rest of the day.

A clerk entered that a hand that will not close on a pen is a hand and is not a figure about anybody and is not a finding and is not a reason for anything, and that a person who is asleep at a desk in a room with a shut door in it has not agreed to anything that happens in that room after she wakes up, and that the record about the nine seconds says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households came into that yard at about half past four that same afternoon, before the light went off the top of the bank, and said that his six have not moved in forty-seven days, which is one signed, two refused and three not asked again, and he said it once and looked at the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing while he said it and neither of them said anything else about it, and a clerk entered that a figure a man keeps about six households is his and is not a figure about the yard and is not a column and is not one of the eight, and that a figure said out loud on the seventh of this month is the seventh of this month's figure and not the eighth's, and that the eighth of this month is tomorrow and has not been counted off by anybody.

The day came down about half past eight and the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing had made no copy of anything that day, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he made none, and entered that a man of about forty-four came down the eleven mile road in the second week of last month with a case, and did not come into that yard, and did not come back, and that nobody sent for him and that the record about the case says not asked.

**AND ON THE SEVENTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH ABOUT NINE PEOPLE WERE IN A ROOM WITH NOTHING OVER THE DOOR AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-NINE WHO KEEPS THAT ROOM REFUSED THE FIFTH OF THE FIVE THINGS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE OUT LOUD, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE WHO COPIES FOR NOTHING WAS REFUSED A LIST OF NINE HOUSES IN HIS OWN HAND BECAUSE HE WOULD NOT HAVE SOLD IT, AND A REGISTRAR ASLEEP AT A DESK AT FOUR IN THE MORNING COULD NOT GET HER HAND TO CLOSE ON A PEN FOR ABOUT NINE SECONDS.**
