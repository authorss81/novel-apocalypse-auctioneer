# Chapter 251: Three First Days

---

The first of the sixth month came in warm and low with a wind off the west that had gone round in the night, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten in the morning, and the chair at the end of the trestle table had been standing there thirty-nine days and nobody had sat in it on any of the thirty-nine.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**A hundred and fifty-nine days on the board and four hundred and seventy-five days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at a hundred and eighty-nine, and a hundred and fifty days separate the second of January and this morning, and that is twenty-one weeks and three days, and three things started this morning and a clerk is not going to write them in one sentence.**"

Then he said the three things, and a man of about nineteen counted them and got a hundred and thirty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The first day of the first season on the first of the nine charters. The first day a record set against four places four hundred miles away is in force. And the first morning on which the words last month and the month before last in this district mean two different months than they meant yesterday, because last month is now the fifth month and the month before last is now the fourth month and the third month is two months back and is to be called that. That is all three of them and I have said them in three sentences and not in one, and a man of fifty-six told this yard a fortnight ago that a clerk is not going to put the first two of those in the same sentence and I have not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered all three of those, and entered that she had written the day on a slate and not in a book because a book is in a room four hundred yards away and the day is in a yard a stranger can walk into, and that a slate is not a book and is not a page and did not become a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a clerk who has entered the same sentence three months running has entered a habit and not a finding, and that the third month is two months back as of this morning and was the month before last yesterday.

Then somebody at the back asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing to read the rival record out one more time for the people who had come in after eleven, which is what he has done seven times in seventeen days, and he said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and twenty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**It is in force. That is what in force means, and it has been in force since about six this morning, and reading it out to a yard a stranger can walk into is not answering it and is not refusing it and is not asking anybody anything, and I have done it seven times in seventeen days for a yard that wanted to hear a piece of paper, and I am not going to do it an eighth time on the morning it starts being true. If somebody wants it read, they can read it. It is under the leg of that table and it has been for a month and it has taken the shape of the leg.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the rival record has now been read out in that yard seven times and that the seventh was on the twenty-first of last month and that the seventh was eleven days back, and that a refusal to read a thing out a second time with a reason is not one of the refusals to read and that the refusals to read are nine and did not move at about eleven in the morning on the first day this record was in force, and that there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading and that the departure is unspent, and that a notice being in force and unanswered is not a smaller thing than a notice not being in force and unanswered and is in one respect a larger one.

Nobody argued with him about it. A man of fifty-six said that a man who has spent a month making a yard's own paper into this district's business and who stops on the morning it costs something to keep doing it has understood the difference between a record and a habit better than anybody else in the yard, and that he was not going to make anything of it.

Then a man of fifty-six said the count of the morning out loud, in the ordinary voice, and nobody asked him to, and a clerk entered that he said it and that she wrote it down and that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and the count came to a hundred and forty-six.

"**A day is not a page and a page is not a day. The first season of the first charter began at six this morning and I do not know what that is worth and neither does anybody in this yard, and a record four hundred miles away became true at the same hour, and neither of those two facts has a name in this district yet and I am not going to supply one. What I will say is that the day a thing is in force and the day a season starts are two days that happen together and they are not the same day, and that this yard has spent five months finding out that two things can stand next to each other without being joined, and I am going to keep doing that until somebody with more sense than me stops me.**"

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, who has stood at that scale nine years at the sluice end of the loams and who weighs about nine sacks a day, was at the back of that yard and said no before anybody asked her, and gave a reason, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and forty-six.

"**I have four houses standing empty and I offered them to this yard on the last day of the month before last and nobody took them and I am not offering them again, and I said the same thing nine days ago and I am saying it again because a season started at six this morning and I would like it in your book that I said it before the season and not after it. You have got nine sheets out in nine rooms and not one door in any of the nine that a woman at a weighbridge can walk through, and I am not going to be a tenth thing this yard counts. If a clerk writes down that I am in the count I will read it back at the counter myself, and this time there will be two of us reading it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who says no before she is asked, with a reason, is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, that a woman who keeps houses is not a clerk of a house, that the eight did not move at about half past eleven in the morning on the first of the sixth month, that the refusals about the ninth holding are seven and did not move and the two are not the same figure and are not added together, and that her four houses still stand and were not added to the eleven of the woman of fifty-eight and are not going to be.

---

It was found about a quarter to twelve, and nobody found it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that before anybody said anything about it.

It was a sheet of paper about the size of a sheet of paper, nailed to the gatepost of that yard at about the height of a man's shoulder, and the nail was driven in fresh and there was no mud on it, and about nine people were standing about nine feet off it looking at the back of it.

Somebody turned it round. A clerk of nineteen years asked for it to be read, because a yard reads a document when somebody asks it to read one, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read it out in one run of words with a stop at the end of each of them and read it back afterwards and got nothing wrong, which is the rule of the counter.

> **THE PEOPLE LIVING IN THE GROUND IN THE SHADOW OF THE HALL, WHOSE NAMES HAVE NEVER BEEN ENTERED, ARE TO BE MOVED.**
>
> **THERE ARE FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN OF THEM. THAT NUMBER WAS ARRIVED AT BY COUNTING THE PEOPLE WHO ANSWERED A DOOR.**
>
> **THEY ARE TO BE MOVED ON OR BEFORE THE FIRST DAY OF THE MONTH AFTER NEXT. THE NUMBER IS NOT TO BE ENTERED AGAIN AND IS NOT TO BE ASKED FOR A SECOND TIME.**

Nobody in that yard said anything for about nine seconds. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet names one building and no place and no person, and that a building is not a place name and that the sheet says the Hall and does not say which Hall, and that a nail in a gatepost is not a keeper and that whoever drove it in is not on the sheet and is not in this district and has never been asked about it.

Then the man of about thirty-one who digs loam, who came down the bank with his right arm out of a sling that is not his and has been out of it since the fourteenth of last month, said the thing about the number and the count came to a hundred and thirty-seven.

"**Four hundred and eleven, and they got it by knocking. That is the whole of the sentence and there are two halves of it and both of them are wrong in different directions. A number arrived at by counting the people who answered a door is a count of the answerers and not a count of the people, and I have been in nine doorways in nine days and I know exactly what a person looks like on the other side of one, and what they look like is a person who has opened it. The people in the shadow of that thing did not open. That is the entire method they have got and it is nine years old and it is the only thing standing between them and a piece of paper, and somebody knocked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a count of the people who answered a door is not a count of the people, and that the difference between a number made by knocking and a number made by walking is not a figure and is not a column and did not become a fifth instrument of the six, and that the six did not move at about twenty past twelve in the afternoon on the first of the sixth month, and that the five things this district does not have are five and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household.

The man of fifty-six worked out the day out loud and nobody had asked him to and a clerk entered that he did it and that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk, and the count came to a hundred and one.

"**The first day of the month after next, from this morning. This month has thirty days and the month after it has thirty-one, and thirty and thirty-one is sixty-one, and I counted it off this morning and not off a page. So it is the first day of the eighth month, and it is sixty-one days off, and I have been in this basin eighteen years and I have never once known what happens on the first day of a month that nobody in this yard was told about, and I am not going to guess at it in a yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the day was derived from the first of the sixth month and not from the sheet, and that a clerk does not put a number of days and a number of months in the same sentence without saying which is which, and that the first of the sixth month is the day a record set against four places became in force and that the first of the eighth month is a day this district has been told about once and has never been asked anything.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went at the gate to take the sheet down so that a stranger could not walk into it in the dark and put it in a coat, and the gate would not open, and it took him four goes with his left hand and a fifth with his right, and a clerk entered that nobody counted the goes and that the record about four goes and a fifth says not asked.

He said afterwards that the ground behind that post had gone soft over the winter and had not gone hard again and that four inches of rain had gone into the trough at the foot of the bank in the second week of last month and had not come out of it, and that a gate that will not open is a gate that is not an instrument of anything and is a piece of wood, and that he was not going to say more about it in a yard about it.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about four with two buckets and put them down where she puts them, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing carried one of them up and said nothing, and a clerk entered that nobody counted the buckets and that a count of buckets is not a count of houses and is not a figure about a woman, and that a bucket of hers is about eleven pounds full and that nobody has ever asked her.

Then she asked, standing at the top of the bank with her other bucket in her hand, whether anybody had counted the four hundred and eleven, and nobody had, and a man of about nineteen said that he had stood about four feet off that post for about two minutes and had counted nothing, and a clerk entered that he counted nothing because he could not count the people who were not in front of him, and that a man of about nineteen who declines to count a thing on a day when a number is nailed to a gatepost in front of him is a man who has understood the instrument, and that the record about his two minutes says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the two things that were on the table and not in the count, and they were in the same hand as the rest and they were not a paragraph.

She entered first that this district has five different ninths in it now and not four, being the ninth of the nine charters, the ninth of the eleven houses the woman of fifty-eight carries water to, the ninth of the nine printed nights, the ninth place on the page out of the cave, and the ninth of the ten days this block runs, and that they are five ninths and are not added to each other and are not a pattern, and that a clerk who counted four of them last month and counted five of them this month has not found anything and has done the thing a clerk does.

She entered second that the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally for six households, who is at the sluice end of the loams about two hundred yards from the scale and who is not the man of fifty-six and is not the man who digs loam, came into that yard at about half past four and put a book on the trestle table face down and said that his six have not moved in forty-one days and that he was not going to say it a fifth time in a month, and that the figure is his and that a clerk did not ask him for it and wrote it down after he had said it, and that a man who keeps a count of six households is not a clerk of a house and is not one of the eight.

The day came down about half past seven and the sixth month was one day old, and the chair at the end of the trestle table was still standing there, and the nail hole in the gatepost was still there with nothing in it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the sheet folded once in the inside pocket of a coat.

**AND ON THE FIRST DAY OF THE SIXTH MONTH A RECORD SET AGAINST FOUR PLACES FOUR HUNDRED MILES AWAY BECAME IN FORCE AND WAS NOT ANSWERED, AND THE SEVENTH TIME IT HAD BEEN READ OUT IN THAT YARD WAS ALSO THE LAST TIME, AND THE WORDS LAST MONTH AND THE MONTH BEFORE LAST MEANED TWO DIFFERENT MONTHS THAN THEY HAD MEANT THE DAY BEFORE, AND A SHEET NAMING ONE BUILDING AND NO PLACE AND NO PERSON WAS FOUND NAILED TO A GATEPOST AT THE HEIGHT OF A MAN'S SHOULDER, AND IT SAID THERE WERE FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN OF THEM AND THAT THE NUMBER WAS ARRIVED AT BY COUNTING THE PEOPLE WHO ANSWERED A DOOR, AND THEY ARE TO BE MOVED ON OR BEFORE THE FIRST DAY OF THE MONTH AFTER NEXT, WHICH IS SIXTY-ONE DAYS OFF THIS MORNING.**
