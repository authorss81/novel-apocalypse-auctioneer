# Chapter 299: A Figure About Coming

---

The nineteenth of the seventh month was grey and close and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing gave the three figures and the man of fifty-six read the board over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**Two hundred and seven days on the board and five hundred and twenty-three days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and thirty-seven, and a hundred and ninety-eight days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is thirteen days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is ninety days back, and the table against that wall stood up for the thirteenth time this morning and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and seventeen days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that thirteen is twelve days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated and was not used this morning, and that a table which has stood up thirteen mornings is a table, and that a man of fifty-six said in that yard on the fifth of this month that a market is a place where a person is asked a question, and that this yard has not asked anybody in it a question about what they want there on any of the days of this month, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came through the gate at about half past ten in the morning and said one thing about a fortnight, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and twenty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I said in this yard on the eighteenth of this month that a habit and a figure are the same thing after a month and that I had looked for a test for six days and had not found one. I have been thinking about it since and I have got a worse answer than the one I gave you and I am going to say it out loud rather than keep it. A fortnight is not a shape. It is a number, and the number is fourteen, and I can get to fourteen on any morning of a week by counting, and I counted it this morning on the way here off the eleventh of this month. And I stood at the gate of this yard a moment ago and there were about nineteen people in it and none of them knew what the number was, and I knew it, and that is the whole of what a fortnight has ever been. There is nothing in that which is about anybody in there. I have walked four miles of that road for eleven years and I know the difference between a road that people walk and a number that counts them, and I do not know why I cannot say which one of the two I have been talking about for a fortnight.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of the road and has never been asked to be one, and that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he has got a worse answer than the one he gave has not withdrawn the first answer and cannot be counted either way, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a head counted at a gate is a figure about a gate and is not a figure about the nineteen people and is not a finding, and that the record about the nineteen not knowing says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing in that yard, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and thirty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I am going to say a thing about figures this afternoon and I am going to stop in the middle of it on purpose, and a man who digs loam did the same thing in this yard six days ago and stopped, and I have thought about his doing it every day since, and I am not going to pretend that a man who does a thing a second time has found a second thing. Here is the whole of it. A figure about how many mornings a table has stood up is nothing. It is a figure about a table. On the day a week turns over, that figure stops being a figure about a table and starts being a figure about a week, and a figure about a week is a figure that can be put beside another figure about a week, and then it is two, and then somebody four hundred miles off can hold them one under the other and see whether the second one is doing what the first one did. That is what a weekly figure is. It is not a spy. It is arithmetic with a memory. There is a figure on a sheet at that gatepost four hundred yards from where I am standing, and it was arrived at by counting the people who answered a door, and I am going to stop there.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a figure, and that he said the half of a sentence out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people and did not say the rest of it, and that the record about the half he did not say says not asked, and that a man who digs loam said the same shape of half-sentence in that yard on the thirteenth of this month and that **a shape noticed a second time is not a finding, and a man who stops in the middle of a sentence twice in six days in a yard is not making a habit of it and has not been asked to stop**, and that a figure about a week is a figure about a week and is not a figure about anybody, and that four hundred and eleven and thirteen are two figures and are not added together, and that the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about half past eleven in the morning on the nineteenth of the seventh month.

Then the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them said one thing back and it was not counted, and a clerk entered that it was not counted and that about four people in that yard heard it.

"**A figure about a week is arithmetic with a memory and he has said that in a yard in front of about nineteen people, and I have had that in my hands since the seventh of this month and I did not have the words for it. I am not going to say what I am going to do about it. A man puts a table up on a morning of a week and counts what is at it, and the fourth of my five lines says the people standing at it are not asked what they are called, and I wrote that line and a man told me in this yard that the other two lines undo it, and he was right, and I have been getting the third line to hold on its own for thirteen mornings and it has not. I would like somebody in this yard to notice that I have not said what I am going to do about a figure about a week, and that the reason I have not is that I do not know, and that a man who does not know is not a party of anything.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says out loud that he does not know has not refused and has not consented and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who puts tables up is not a keeper of a table and is not a bearer of a figure about a week, and that a figure of mornings is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and that the six did not move on the nineteenth of the seventh month.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the end of the trestle table at about half past two in the afternoon and said one thing about a figure about a week, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and ten-seven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have got a tent rolled on a hand-cart and I have had it rolled up since the thirtieth of last month and there is a field about two hundred yards across with a ditch at the far end of it, and a man of about thirty-four has told about nineteen people in this yard this afternoon that a figure about a week is arithmetic with a memory, and I have been carrying a tent for thirteen days because I did not know what I was going to do with a field. A tent is not a lean-to and I said that on the sixteenth of this month and I am not going to unsay it. But I am going to put it up against that wall on the twentieth or the twenty-first of this month, on the ground that a table standing in the open in the seventh month is a thing a person will stand at for as long as their legs last, and that is not a reason anybody can print on a sheet, and it is the only reason I have got, and I would like a clerk to enter that the reason a man puts a tent up is not a finding and is not a figure and is not about anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who puts tables up is not a keeper of a tent and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a tent is not a lean-to and a lean-to is not a wall four feet high and a field is not a document, and that a tent is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a man who has said out loud that a reason of his own is not a finding has not made a finding and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about half past four in the afternoon the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing read the five lines of print out loud in about nine feet of lane outside the office, to about four people, and a man of about thirty-seven was standing there and had not asked him to.

He got through the first of them and the second and the third and he lost his voice on the fourth of them, in the middle of the word eleven, and finished the fourth and read the fifth out loud with almost no sound in it, and then he stood in that lane for about nine seconds and said one thing, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that a clerk was four hundred yards away in a yard and did not know about it until about seven.

"**I could not finish that one on the fourth line and I finished it anyway and I have not got anything above a whisper now and I will not have got anything above a whisper until the morning of the twenty-first. I read five lines out loud in this lane every morning of a week because a man with a table eleven miles off asked me to in the seventh month and because I cannot write them any more, and I would like it entered that a voice is not a copy and is not a second copy and is not a document, and that a man who reads a thing for a fortnight is not a keeper of it and has not been given it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a document, and that a hand that has not closed and a voice that has gone are two things about a man and not two figures about anybody, and that a voice in a lane is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and that nobody sent for him and the record about the not sending says not asked.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was nineteen days old and the first day of the eighth month was thirteen days off, and a man who reads things for a living in this district had lost his voice in about nine feet of lane outside a room and had finished the sentence anyway.

**AND ON THE NINETEENTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN THAT A FIGURE ABOUT A WEEK IS ARITHMETIC WITH A MEMORY AND STOPPED IN THE MIDDLE OF A SENTENCE ON PURPOSE, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE WHO COPIES FOR NOTHING READ THE FIVE LINES OUT ALOUD IN A LANE OUTSIDE THE OFFICE AND LOST HIS VOICE ON THE FOURTH OF THEM AND FINISHED IT ANYWAY.**
