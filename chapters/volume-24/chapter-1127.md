# Chapter 1127: The Four Hundred And Eleventh Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And Four Figures Came Off That Wall Twice And The Flat Answer Went To The Stone

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and that figure has been on that sheet since before the middle of the winter and nobody in this basin has done a thing with it. That figure is eight hundred and sixty-six days old.

That board carries one thousand and twenty-five marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and forty-one days and not one wagon has come off it in all of them. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and fifty-five days. One thousand and sixteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was not run, and it is not a lot and not open this morning. Seven hundred and thirty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this ground stands at. Seven hundred and thirty-five days is how long that rule said out loud in that ground has stood. Eight hundred and five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and forty-four days past a printing it never made.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and eight days back. Six hundred and eighty-four marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and seventy marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and it stands on nothing in this basin. Those four documents have never been got into this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places in this basin and in no fifth. Four conditions are set out in this district and nothing has been set at the end of any of them. Fourteen things is what this basin has made and there is nothing outside those fourteen that has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen have never been in this district at all and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is at sixty-six lines and it took nothing onto it this morning. No first asking is on that page and none is on any other paper in this district. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing ever written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and forty-one mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and ninety-one mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and eleven mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and tenth morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. On the seven hundred and sixty-sixth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and sixty-five of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and fifty-second of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and sixty-seventh morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundredth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

Nothing has ever been written at the back of that lot book and it lies open along those boards this morning with the back of it bare as it has always been. One page of that book carries five lines ruled across it, and the column on the page after that one holds a single name under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and it stands empty and nothing has ever been asked about it in this district. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in their own carriers' hands and not one of them came down it today. That record has stood three hundred and twenty-six days. There were about nine people stood on that ground at ten to eight, and it has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and fifty-sixth afternoon running and one word was all there was in that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it has been paid and it is spent, and none of it is left in this district. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district and none of them has ever been read out loud anywhere in it.

---

He was at that wall at about a quarter to eight with the sun just coming over the top of that bank and about four feet of that stone still cold under his hand.

What he wanted off that morning was all four of them again at about four, and his own hand to stay on that stone where he puts it.

---

He reads them off the top down, and that is not a preference of his. There is no other order in which four figures on a wall stand the right distance apart for a man with one hand on that stone and his feet on the ground.

The top one costs him. It is the oldest paint on that wall and the sun has been on it longer than he has been reading it, and what is left of it is thinner than what is left of the other three, and a man who reads that one with the sun on it will read it wrong and will not know that he has read it wrong.

---

He had his hand flat on the stone under the lowest one, the way he does it, and he read the top one, and the second, and the third, and the fourth.

He got all four.

"You four," he said out loud to that stone. "I will have you both times today and I am not going to need one person in this district to tell me what any of you is for."

Then the flat answer went to that stone, and it was the three hundredth time running.

"That is three hundred mornings of saying something to this stone and I have not missed one of them, and the two of you have been up there twice today and both of you have come off that wall clean, and my hand has been lying flat on this stone since the sun came over the top of that bank, and I have put no question to anybody in this district about any part of it and I am not going to start at the top of that hill, and you keep what I put on you and that is the whole of the trade I have."

Nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about any of it.

---

The sun came up the face of that bank and got onto his hand by degrees.

The boy said thirty at ten to eight off those boards and nothing came down that lane behind it. The man of thirty-eight was at about four feet off the near end of that first table from about half past nine with his cloth in his hand until about four, and nobody in that yard said one word to him there. The woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan about four feet off the near end of that second table from about half past twelve, and she said nothing out loud that morning.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight put that number of his into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he has for it, and about four voices in that yard took it up behind him out of step with one another, and nobody in that yard said one word out loud about any of it.

There was nothing turning on that frame at the near end of that first table and the turner was stood at it from about six and a half with his heel off it and nobody in that yard started it, and he said nothing out loud this morning.

---

At about two the man of about thirty-four came along that wall on his way back to his own boards and stood at the other end of it with the cloth over his arm.

"You have got that stone under your hand every morning of that run," he said.

"I have."

"Then you have got it colder than the rest of it."

"It is not cold this morning."

The man of about thirty-four stood there for a while and then he went on down his own boards with the cloth still over his arm, and neither of them asked the other one for anything.

---

That is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert, and nobody in this basin has ever been up over the far end of it.

---

At about four his hand went back up flat under the lowest one and he took them off that wall again from the top down and he got all four.

"You four, and that is the last of you for today."

Then the flat answer went to that stone after them, in other words than the ones he had used at a quarter to eight, and it was the three hundredth time running.

"That is the three hundredth of them and I have had the two of you twice today and got the two of you twice today, and this stone has had my whole hand on it since before the light was on this ground, and there is not one person in this district who has ever asked me where the four of you are kept or what I do with what comes off you, and there is not going to be one, and that is the trade and the whole of it."

Nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about any part of that.

---

The clerk came down those boards at about four with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud, and then she wrote the four hundred and eleventh morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and took no line for it.

That day's boards came off the cart face down at about half past four and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders with the light still on them, and about nineteen people went up with them.

He was still at that wall with his hand lying flat on the stone under the lowest one of those four when the last of them had gone up that slope, and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that they have not said what that wall is for, and not one of them said it this morning.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it and it took no line and it carried no sixty-seventh. No page came down that lane and the thirty behind it are the thirty that were behind it this morning. Those three came through this district and not one of them has been read out loud in it. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and there is nothing in this basin to set it on. That gatepost sheet will go on carrying four hundred and eleven in the morning and in the morning after that.

He read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and four figures off it at about four and he got all four both times, and what he said to that stone after them was the three hundredth time running, and nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about any of it.
