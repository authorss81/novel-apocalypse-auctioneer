# Chapter 1124: The Four Hundred And Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And A Piece Went Into A Corner At About Ten And Came Out Of It Inside A Minute

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nothing has ever been done with that figure by anybody in this basin. That figure is eight hundred and sixty-three days old.

That board carries one thousand and twenty-two marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and thirty-eight days and not one wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and fifty-two days. One thousand and thirteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was never run, and it is not a lot and it is not open this morning. Seven hundred and twenty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard has stood at. Seven hundred and thirty-two days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Eight hundred and two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and forty-one days past a printing it was never in.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and five days back. Six hundred and eighty-one marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and sixty-seven marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing here for it to sit on. Those four documents have never been in this district at all. Three of those lines can be read in four places here and not one of them is in a fifth. Four conditions are set out in this district and there is no end standing on any of them anywhere here. What this basin has made comes to fourteen things and no other thing has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen have never arrived in this district and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is at sixty-six lines this morning and it took no line onto it. There was a reason said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-eight mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and eighty-eight mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and eight mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and seventh morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. On the seven hundred and sixty-third night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and sixty-two of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and forty-ninth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and sixty-fourth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and ninety-seventh time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with the back of it bare, and it has lain open along those boards with the back of it bare for as long as anybody in this district has been keeping count of anything. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book and the column on the page after that one has a single name in it under a heading that still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and it is empty. Behind that lane there are thirty pages and every one of them is in the hands of their own carriers, and nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and twenty-three days. About nine people were on that ground at ten to eight, and that ground has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and fifty-third afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two and nobody here has ever gone up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it has been paid and it is spent and none of it is left in this basin. Three of those first thirty-three lines came through this district, and no voice in this district has ever read one of them out loud.

---

She was at those boards in the dark with the book open against the rail and the pen in the holder, and she wrote nothing at all, because there was nothing yet to write.

What she wanted off that morning was to know, before her hand went near any part of it, whether that morning had anything on it at all.

---

She has two forms of words.

One of them is for a morning when nothing has gone out loud in that yard that goes at the back of that book, and it is the common of them and she has had it ready since she was twenty-six. The second of the two is for a morning when something has, and she has not had to use it once in fifteen years.

---

At about ten the man of about thirty-four was at the near corner of his own table.

She was at the bottom of that slope with her back to it and the light in her eyes off those boards, and she had a clear view of the whole of that table because nothing stands between that ground and those boards.

He got the piece off the boards at the far end of that table, where it had lain since the light came, with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of the table.

He set that square cut end into the near corner of his own top, down into the corner where the two boards of that frame come together, and he pushed on the end of it with the heel of his hand.

It went in the whole way.

The near corner of that top came up off the hollow of grit and that table stood level on its two trestles, and the daylight that had been standing under that corner all morning was not there any more.

---

Then he took his hand off it and stood looking down the length of that table, and she could see the whole length of that top and there was no light standing under any part of it.

Inside a minute he took it out again.

It came out of that corner without any trouble. The near end of that top went down onto the hollow as soon as it was gone, and the daylight came back under that corner about the width of a finger, and the twist came back into the top of that table.

He laid that piece on the boards at the far end of his own table with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of it, and he got his cloth and went back to the far half of that table.

Nobody in that yard said one word about any part of that.

Nobody asked him why.

Nobody thanked him for it, and nobody at the top of that bank said anything about it either.

---

She wrote nothing at that moment and she wrote nothing about it at any other moment, and there is not one line on that page and not one word in her own hand anywhere this morning about a piece of wood going into a corner of a man's own table.

She was not asked anything about it either. Nobody at those boards said one word to her about it and nobody at that table said one word to her about it.

---

The boy was at those boards at ten to eight and gave that lane count out loud before the clerk's hand had gone anywhere near any part of it. It was thirty. Nothing came down that lane behind it.

He came down that slope at about half past nine, put his bag against the foot of that trestle and stood at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand until about four, and those four feet are the four feet where a man can work without being spoken to, and nobody in that yard has ever asked him what they are.

The woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan about four feet off the near end of that second table from about half past twelve with the basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud, and nobody has ever asked her what she weighs things for.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight put that number of his into about four hundred yards of air, and behind him about four voices in that yard took it up out of step with one another, and nobody in that yard put one word out loud about any of it.

The wheel at the near end of that first table was standing with nothing on it, and the turner was at that frame from about six and a half, and his heel never went near that casting that day, and nobody in that yard started it.

---

At about four the man of fifty-six had his hand up on that low wall and the four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and ninety-seventh time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him a question about any part of it.

It is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert, and nobody in this district has ever been given the far end of that.

---

At about four she came down that row of boards with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud.

"The four hundred and eighth morning," she said. "Nothing has gone out loud at the back of that book and it has taken no line and I have the one form of words. Thirty came off that lane count at ten to eight and no page came down behind it. That is all I have and that is all I am going to have."

Then she wrote the four hundred and eighth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

What she wrote went onto that page over a thin place in the paper about four inches in from the foot of it, and sat a hair lower than the lines above it, and she has never once made an allowance for that in fifteen years and she did not make one this morning.

---

That day's boards came off the cart with their faces to the ground at about half past four and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders while the light was still on the eleven feet of that slope, and about nineteen people went up with them.

She went up behind the last of them with that book shut under her arm, and about four people were standing about at the top of that bank, and not one of them said anything to her about that morning.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it and it took no line for anything and it did not carry a sixty-seventh. Nothing came down that lane and it is at thirty pages behind it as it always has stood. Those three came through this district and nobody in it has heard one of them read. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. That count of seven months is standing on nothing in this basin and has done from the first of it. That gatepost sheet will go on carrying four hundred and eleven in the morning and the morning after.

He put that piece into the near corner of his own top at about ten and it went in and that table stood level, and inside a minute he took it out and put it back on the boards at the far end of his own table, and nobody asked him why.
