# Chapter 1125: The Four Hundred And Ninth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And A Woman At Her Own Scale Said One Figure Of Her Own Out Loud

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and that gatepost has carried that figure through every morning this basin has had. It is eight hundred and sixty-four days old.

That board carries one thousand and twenty-three marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and thirty-nine days and nothing has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and fifty-three days. One thousand and fourteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was not run, so it is not a lot this morning and not open either. Seven hundred and twenty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard still stands at. Seven hundred and thirty-three days is how long that rule said out loud in this yard has stood. Eight hundred and three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and forty-two days past a printing it was never asked for.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and six days back. Six hundred and eighty-two marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and sixty-eight marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months is counted in this district and nothing in this basin will hold it. Those four documents have never been got into this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places here and not in a fifth place anywhere in this basin. Four conditions stand set out here and there is no end set upon any one of them. What this basin has made comes to fourteen things and nothing else has ever been made in it anywhere here. Five of those fourteen never got as far as this ground and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page stands at sixty-six lines and nothing at all went onto it this morning. There is no first asking on that page or on anything else in this district. One reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for a reason, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-nine mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and eighty-nine mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and nine mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and eighth morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and sixty-fourth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and sixty-three of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and fiftieth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and sixty-fifth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and ninety-eighth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

Nothing has ever been written at the back of that lot book and it lies open along those boards with nothing on the back of it this morning. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book and the column on the page after that one carries a single name in it under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and it has stood empty every morning of that run. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in the hands of their own carriers and not one of them came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and twenty-four days. There were about nine people on that ground at ten to eight, and that lot has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and fifty-fourth afternoon running and one word was all that afternoon came to. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two, and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it has been paid and spent and none of it is left in this basin. Three of the first thirty-three lines on that back page came through this district, and no voice in this district has read any of them aloud.

---

She came down that bank at half past seven with the cloth over her arm and the basket on the other one, and she has come down that bank at that hour for nine years.

What she wanted off that morning was that pan to be still when she put a figure out loud on it, and the light still on those boards when she did it.

---

There were four things on that pan before the middle of the day and a fifth that went down at about five to twelve and stayed where it was.

The wind came at that scale in a way it does not come at the boards, and it wanted the bar and she let it want it twice before she let it settle. She put the cloth over her own arm when her arm wanted doing, and she looked at the face of that bank more than she looked at her own pan.

---

At about half past twelve she turned about a foot and a half on her own feet so that the light off those boards came across the front of her instead of behind her, and she said her figure out loud, once, in the voice she has used at that hour for nine years.

About nineteen people were on that ground within hearing of that voice.

Nobody answered her.

Nobody thanked her.

Nobody in this yard has ever put one question to her about what she weighs things for, or where any figure she says comes from, or what she does with the answer to it.

---

She put the pan down off its cock and went back to her basket and took the cloth off her arm and folded it over her hand.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air, and about four voices in that yard took it up after him out of step with one another.

She heard that go across that ground and she did not answer it either, and there was no more of that than there was of her own.

---

Nobody in that yard has ever been up the far end of about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

---

The rest of that morning she had to herself, which is most of a morning at that scale, and about two the man of about thirty-four went past her with the cloth over his arm and neither of them said one word to the other one.

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and ninety-eighth time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him anything about any part of it.

At about four the clerk came along that row of boards with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud, and then she wrote the four hundred and ninth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and took no line for it.

About half past four the light came off those boards and that day's boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank, on about nine pairs of shoulders, with about nineteen people going up with them.

She knotted the cloth over her scale, put the basket on her arm and stood about four feet off the near end of that second table while the last of them went, and that near rail was standing on the near side of it with nothing against it.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it and it took no line at all and it did not carry a sixty-seventh. Nothing came down that lane and the thirty behind it are where they have always been. Those three came through this district and none of them has been read out in this district. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and nothing in this basin will ever take that count. That gatepost sheet will be carrying the same four hundred and eleven on it in the morning, and the day after that.

She said one figure of her own out loud at about half past twelve in about nineteen people's hearing and not one person in that yard said a word back to her. That page took no line on it this morning.
