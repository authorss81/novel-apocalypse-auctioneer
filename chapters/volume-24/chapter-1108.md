# Chapter 1108: The Three Hundred And Ninety-Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And A Gap In The Middle Of The Figure

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nothing in this basin has ever been done with it. That figure is eight hundred and forty-seven days old.

That board carries one thousand and six marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and twenty-two days and no wagon has come off it. Nobody has entered anything at that rail for one thousand and thirty-six days. Nine hundred and ninety-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid was open four hundred and fifty days, it was not run, and it is not a lot and not open this morning. Seven hundred and eleven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and sixteen days is how long that rule said out loud in there has stood. Seven hundred and eighty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and twenty-five days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and eighty-nine days back. Six hundred and sixty-five marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and fifty-one marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and it is on nothing this basin can put a hand on. Four of those documents have never once been in this district. Three of them are readable in four places here, and nothing in a fifth place in this district has any of them on it. Four conditions are set out in this district with no end set on any of them. Fourteen is what this basin has made and nothing else has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen never arrived here at all, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page stands at sixty-five lines and it took no line this morning. There is no first asking on that page and none on anything else either, and nobody is able to recover it. On the one hundred and eighty-second morning a reason was said out loud in that yard, and nothing on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, and it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-two mornings and the near rail on the near side of it three hundred and seventy-two mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank three hundred and ninety-two mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and ninety-first morning and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and forty-seventh night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and forty-six of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and thirty-third of those mornings and all four were got. That is the two hundred and forty-eighth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and eighty-first time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

Nothing has ever been written at the back of that lot book and there is nothing there this morning, and it lies open along those boards. One page of that book has five lines ruled across it and the column on the page after it carries a name, under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and it stands empty and nobody has ever been asked to fill it. Thirty pages are behind that lane in their own carriers' hands, and nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and seven days. That ground has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and about nine people were stood on it at ten to eight this morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones for the three hundred and thirty-seventh afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Neither of those two has ever been given to anybody in this district, and nobody here has ever been up to either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it is spent, and there is nothing of it left in this district. Three of the first thirty-three lines on that back page came through this district, and none of them has ever been read aloud in this district.

---

She came in at half past seven with the book under her arm and the pen in the holder and she was fifteen years into the doing of that.

What she wanted off that morning was to get that number onto that page without the ink going where it always goes.

---

There is a band in that paper four inches in from the foot of the back page where the page has gone thin.

She has known about it for years. She has never once made any allowance for it and she has never once gone over a figure twice to get it down heavier, and she was not going to start this morning.

She could have got a number onto that page clean by sitting a half inch higher up the page and letting the thin of the paper fall between one part of the figure and the next. She has never done it on one morning in fifteen years.

---

She came down that row of boards at about four with the pen still in the holder and read that morning out loud, the way she reads it, before her hand went near any part of it.

"The three hundred and ninety-second morning," she said. "Nothing came out of that yard today that anybody answered, and there is nobody in it that was thanked for anything. A man of fifty-six had his hand up on that wall at about a quarter to eight and got four figures off it, and had it up again at about four and got the same four off it, and his hand was on that stone in between. A man stood at his own table from about half past nine until about four with a cloth in his hand and did not say one word out loud to anybody. A woman put a bar on her own pan at about half past twelve, four feet off the near end of that second table. There was a boy at those boards at ten to eight and he said a number, and nothing came after it. A man put his number out into about four hundred yards of air at about half past one and about four voices took it up after him, out of step with one another. The turner stood at the near end of that first table all day. A load that is two days in one went up that bank off one cart at about half past four while the light was still on that ground."

She got to the end of that, and there was nothing after it for her to go over.

---

About four people on those boards stood still through the whole of it with a foot on a board and nobody arranged that.

Then she took the pen out of the holder and wrote the three hundred and ninety-second morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

---

The gap came out in the middle of it where it always comes out.

The ink spread either side of the thin of the paper and put the two halves of that figure a little further apart than a figure ought to be, and there it was under the last line in her own small careful hand, and that morning's number with a hole in the middle of it.

She looked at that for a while.

She has known about that band of paper for years and she has never once corrected for it and she did not do so this morning. She put the pen back in the holder and did not do one thing about it.

---

Nobody in this district has ever asked her why she reads that morning out loud, and nobody has ever asked her to say it again, and nobody has ever asked her what a line on that page is for.

---

About half past four the light came off those boards and about four of that nineteen said the goodnight and the day's boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders.

She went up behind the last of them with the book shut under her arm and the pen in her pocket, and at the top of that bank about four people were standing about and not one of them said anything to her about any part of that day.

---

The covers came over that page at sixty-five lines and it took no line today. That lane brought nothing down it today and it stands thirty pages behind it. Three of those lines came through this district, and the reading of them is unheard in it. That fifth of that five stands at five and nobody has paid it. Seven months is counted here and nothing at all in this basin will hold that. Four hundred and eleven will be on that gatepost sheet in the morning, same as it is now.

She read the three hundred and ninety-second morning out loud at about four and then wrote it under the last line with the gap in the middle of it that is always in the middle of it.
