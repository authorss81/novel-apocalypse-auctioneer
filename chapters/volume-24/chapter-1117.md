# Chapter 1117: The Four Hundred And First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And One Line Went Onto The Back Of That Lot Book Carrying The Morning And Not The Reason

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this basin has ever put that figure to a use. That figure is eight hundred and fifty-six days old.

That board carries one thousand and fifteen marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and thirty-one days and no wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and forty-five days. One thousand and six days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot this morning. Seven hundred and twenty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and twenty-five days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Seven hundred and ninety-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and thirty-four days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and ninety-eight days back. Six hundred and seventy-four marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and sixty marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing in this basin for it to sit on. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places here, and there is no fifth place in this basin with any of them on. Four conditions are set out in this district and no end is set upon any of them. Fourteen is what this basin has made, and there has never been anything else made in it. Five of those fourteen never came here at all, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

A reason was said out loud once from the top of that bank this morning, and it did not come down in anybody's mouth after it. One line went onto the page at the back of that lot book, and that line carries this morning and not what was said in it. She was not thanked for it and nobody answered her. That page stood at sixty-five lines from the first board up to the last one off and it stands at sixty-six lines.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-one mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and eighty-one mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and one mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundredth morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and fifty-sixth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and fifty-five of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and forty-second of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and fifty-seventh morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and ninetieth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

Nothing has ever been written at the back of that lot book, and that book lies open along those boards. Five lines are ruled across one of its pages, and the column on the page after that one carries a single name in it under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and it has stood empty every morning. Behind that lane there are thirty pages and every one of them is in the hand of the person who brought it, and it brought nothing down it this morning. That record has stood three hundred and sixteen days. There were about nine people on that ground at ten to eight, and that ground has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and forty-sixth afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Neither of those two distances has ever been handed to anybody in this district, and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight, it has been paid and it is spent, and nothing of it is left in this district. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district, and none of them has ever been read out loud in it.

---

She was at those boards before the light with the book open against the rail and the pen in the holder, and she wrote nothing at all, because there was nothing yet to write.

What she wanted off that morning was to know, before her hand went near any part of it, whether the morning had anything on it at all.

---

She has two forms of words.

One of those two forms is for a morning when nothing has gone out loud in that yard that goes at the back of that book, and it is the common of them and she has had it ready since she was twenty-six. The other one is for a morning when something has, and she has not had to use the second one in fifteen years.

---

At about half past ten a voice came off the top of that bank.

She was at the bottom of it, at the near end of the second table, with her back to the slope and the light in her eyes off those boards. What came down to her was a voice going out flat the way a voice does when a person puts a thing out loud so that it carries, and she got every bit of the carrying of it and none of the rest of it.

She did not get a word of it.

She stood there with the pen in the holder and did not get one word of it.

---

Nobody at the foot of that bank heard it.

Nobody at the foot of that bank answered it.

Nobody has ever said to anybody that they heard it, and nobody has ever asked her whether she heard it.

---

Then the morning went on being a morning.

At ten to eight the boy was at those boards with his hands in his pockets and said thirty, and not one page came down that lane behind it.

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight came down that slope and went out to about four feet off the near end of that first table, and he was still at about those four feet at about four, and nobody put one word to him at any point of it.

About four feet off the near end of that second table the woman who keeps a scale had her own pan and her basket down beside her from about half past twelve, and she said nothing out loud that day.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air, and about four voices in that yard took it up behind him out of step with one another.

---

The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at the far end of that ground all that morning and it stood there for the four hundredth morning.

Nobody in that yard asked that man about that figure and nobody asked the clerk about it either, and the figure went in neither of their mouths and onto neither of their hands, and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that they have not said what the count of a table is for.

---

At about four she came down that row of boards with the pen still in the holder and read that morning out loud before her hand had been near any part of it.

"The four hundred and first morning," she said. "A voice was put out loud from the top of that bank at about half past ten and it came down to me at the near end of this table and I did not get a word of it, and one line goes on at the back of this book, and that line carries this morning and leaves out whatever it was that got said up there, and I have given all of that to you before I have put the nib down."

She stopped there and there was nothing after it for her to go over.

---

Then she wrote the four hundred and first morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and below her own hand she took one line.

What she has of it is that something was said up there, and the sound of a woman's voice saying it, and a line on that page. If she were asked to put the words of it together again she would find she had never had them, and nobody in this district is going to ask her.

The line went over the worn place in that paper about four inches in from the foot of the page and it did not sit level with the lines above it. She has never once made an allowance for that in fifteen years and she did not make one this morning.

Nobody thanked her.

Nobody at the foot of that bank said one word to her about any part of that morning.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and ninetieth time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him anything about any part of it.

The frame at the near end of that first table had nothing turning on it, the turner was at it, his heel was off it, nobody in that yard started it, and he said nothing out loud this morning.

---

The light came off those boards at about half past four and that day's boards came down off the cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them.

She went up behind the last of them with the book shut under her arm, and at the top of that bank about four people were standing about, and not one of them said anything to her about that morning.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it, having stood at sixty-five when that morning began, and it took that one line and no other. That lane brought nothing down it today and it stands at thirty pages behind it as it always has. Three of those first thirty-three lines have come through this district, and nobody in this district has heard one of them read. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid and it was not paid this morning. Seven months is counted in this district, and there is nothing in this basin to hold it. That gatepost sheet will be carrying the same four hundred and eleven on it in the morning as it is now.

One reason went out loud from the top of that bank at about half past ten and did not come down, and one line went onto that page carrying the morning and not the reason, and nobody answered her.
