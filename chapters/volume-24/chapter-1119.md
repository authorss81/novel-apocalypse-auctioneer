# Chapter 1119: The Four Hundred And Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And A Man At About Four Feet Off The Near End Of That First Table Worked From Half Past Nine Until About Four

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this basin has ever taken that figure off that sheet and done anything with it. That figure is eight hundred and fifty-eight days old.

That board carries one thousand and seventeen marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and thirty-three days and no wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and forty-seven days. One thousand and eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, it was not run, and it is neither a lot nor open this morning. Seven hundred and twenty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and twenty-seven days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Seven hundred and ninety-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and thirty-six days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred days back. Six hundred and seventy-six marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and sixty-two marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and it is on nothing in this basin. Those four documents have never once been inside this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places here and there is no fifth place in this basin with any of them on it. Four conditions stand set out in this district and nothing is set at the end of any of them. Fourteen things is what this basin has made and nothing outside those fourteen has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen never got in here at all, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page took no line this morning and it is at sixty-six lines. There is no first asking on that page and there is none on any other paper in this district either, and nobody here is able to recover one. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, and it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-three mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and eighty-three mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and three mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and second morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and fifty-eighth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and fifty-seven of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and forty-fourth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and fifty-ninth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and ninety-second time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

Nothing has ever been written at the back of that lot book and it lies open along those boards this morning with the back of it as bare as it has always been. There are five lines ruled across one page of that book, and the column on the page after that one has a name in it under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and it stands empty and nobody has been asked to fill it. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in their own carriers' hands, and not one of them came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and eighteen days. About nine people were stood on that ground at ten to eight, and that lot has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and forty-eighth afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Those two distances have never been given to anybody in this district and nobody here has ever gone up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll of ninety-eight came to that figure, it has been paid and it is spent, and there is none of it left. Three of the first thirty-three lines on that back page came through this district, and no voice in this district has ever read one of them out loud.

---

He came down that slope at about half past nine with the bag over his shoulder and got a block plane and a mallet and a piece of board out of it, and set the bag against the foot of the trestle behind him.

What he wanted off that morning was to get the whole of that length cut and trued before the light came round off the near end of that table and put his eye out, and for about those four feet to still be about four feet at about four.

---

Those four feet between him and the near end of that first table are the four feet where a man can work without being spoken to, and that is the whole of what they are for.

Nobody has ever asked him anything. He has never asked anybody in this district for a bed or for a wage or for an office, and nobody at the top of that lane has ever offered him any of those three, and nobody there has ever offered him anything else either.

About four people at the top of that bank have said since that they have not said what those four feet is, and none of them said it this morning.

---

He did not hurry the cut.

He took a shaving off it with the plane the way it comes off, which is in one curl, and he put the plane down on its side and picked it up again and went along it with the grain and not against it. He got his eye down the length of it twice to see whether it was straight, and the second time he did not need to take the cut off again.

He said nothing out loud at any point of that.

---

At ten to eight the boy was stood at those boards with his hands in his pockets and said thirty, and nothing came down that lane behind it.

At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan about four feet off the near end of that second table with her basket down beside her and said nothing out loud that day.

Half past one came and the man of about forty-eight put his number out into about four hundred yards of air, and about four voices in that yard took it up after him out of step with one another.

He had the plane down on its side at that hour. He picked it up again after it had gone off the face of that bank and went on along that length as though nothing had gone out.

---

At the foot of that low wall the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of it as he is every morning, and nobody has ever asked him a question about any part of it and nobody did this morning.

There is a figure that goes with that morning and it is a nine hundred, and it stands on that page with the other figures and nobody in that yard said it out loud and nobody looked up at it.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and ninety-second time running, and nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about any of it.

The clerk came down that row of boards at about four with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud, and then she wrote the four hundred and third morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and she took no line for it.

---

He got the last of that length finished a little before four and wiped the board off with the flat of his hand and stood with the plane in his hand for a while with the light going up the face of that bank behind him.

The light came off those boards at about half past four and that day's boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up with them.

He went up that slope at the end of it and the man of about thirty-four was the last of those men off that ground, and the foot and about four inches of sound pallet was on the boards at the far end of his own table with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of it.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it, and it took no line on it and it did not carry a sixty-seventh. Nothing has come down that lane and it stands at thirty pages behind it. Three of those first thirty-three lines have come through this district and none of them has been read out in it. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted here and nothing in this district will hold it. Four hundred and eleven will be on that gatepost sheet in the morning, same as it is carrying it now.

He worked at about four feet off the near end of that first table from about half past nine until about four and said nothing out loud and nobody in that yard asked him one thing.
