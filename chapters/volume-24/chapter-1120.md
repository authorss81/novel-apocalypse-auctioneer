# Chapter 1120: The Four Hundred And Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And That Table Sat Level From About Ten Until About Four

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this basin has ever made anything out of that figure. That figure is eight hundred and fifty-nine days old.

That board carries one thousand and eighteen marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and thirty-four days and no wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and forty-eight days. One thousand and nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was not run, so it is not a lot and not open this morning. Seven hundred and twenty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and twenty-eight days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Seven hundred and ninety-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and thirty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and one days back. Six hundred and seventy-seven marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and sixty-three marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing in this basin that count is set on. Those four documents have never been in this district. Those three lines are readable in four places here, and there is no fifth place in this basin with any of them on it. Four conditions are set out in this district with no end put upon any one of them. This basin has made fourteen things and it has never made anything else. Of those fourteen, five never came here, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page stands at sixty-six lines and it took no line on it this morning. There is no first asking on that page and there is none on anything else in this district either, and nobody here is able to recover one. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, and it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-four mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and eighty-four mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and four mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and third morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and fifty-ninth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and fifty-eight of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and forty-fifth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and sixtieth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and ninety-third time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

The back of that lot book is bare, as it has always been, and it lies open along those boards. Five lines are ruled across one of its pages and the column on the page after that one carries a single name under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and nothing has ever been in it. Thirty pages are behind that lane in the hands of the people who brought them, and it brought nothing down it today. That record has stood three hundred and nineteen days. That lot has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and there were about nine people on it at ten to eight.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and forty-ninth afternoon running and one word was all there was in that afternoon. Neither of those two has ever been given to anybody in this district and nobody here has ever gone up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight, it has been paid and it is spent, and none of it is left anywhere in this basin. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district, and none of them has ever been read out loud in this district.

---

He came down that slope at about a quarter to eight with the cloth over his arm, and the light was just off the top of that bank and lying along the whole length of that ground.

What he wanted off that morning was his own table squared and the whole length of that top along with the cloth before the near end of it went with the sun.

---

He put it up the way he puts it up and squared the top with the back of his heel, and the near end of it stood a quarter of an inch low on that hollow of grit at the front of the foot of the near trestle.

He got the cloth and started at the far end.

---

At about ten he stopped and went and got the piece off the boards at the far end of that table, where it had lain in the open since the light came up, with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of it.

He set that square cut end into the near corner of his own top, right down into the corner where the two boards of that frame come together, and he pushed on it with the heel of his hand.

It went in the whole way.

The near corner of that top came up off that hollow and that table stood level on its two trestles. The daylight that had been standing under that corner all morning was not there. The twist came out of the top of it.

---

He put the cloth down under that near corner and worked along the top from the near end back toward the far.

The near two feet went first, because that is the part of that table a man does not work, and they went without the board dipping anywhere under his hand.

He got the whole length of that top done inside two hours.

---

The man of about thirty-two had the far end of that table and a wedge in his hand from about half past eight until the light went off those boards.

Neither of them said one word to the other one at any point of that day.

---

At ten to eight the boy was at those boards with his hands in his pockets and said thirty, and no page came down that lane behind it.

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight came down that slope and stood at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand until about four, and nobody said one word to him there.

From about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan about four feet off the near end of that second table, and she said nothing out loud that day.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight put his number out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for it, and about four voices in that yard took it up behind him out of step with one another.

There was nothing turning on that frame at the near end of that first table, and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel off it, and nobody in that yard started it, and he said nothing out loud this morning.

---

At about two he got up off that near corner and stood back and looked down the length of that table for a while.

Then he got on with the cloth again.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and ninety-third time running, and nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about any part of it.

At about four the clerk came down that row of boards with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud, and then she wrote the four hundred and fourth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and she took no line for it.

---

At about four he took the piece out of that corner.

It came out without any trouble at all. The near end of that top went down onto the hollow of grit as soon as it was gone, and the daylight came back under that corner about the width of a finger, and the twist came back into the top of that table where it had been at about eight that morning.

He put that piece back on the boards at the far end of his own table with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of it, and he took the cloth off that top and folded it over his arm.

---

Nobody in that yard said one word about it at any point of that day.

Nobody asked him why it came out.

Nobody thanked him for it, and nobody said anything about it at the top of that bank either, and about four people went up that bank that afternoon and not one of them said anything about it.

---

About half past four was when the light came off those boards, and that day's boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up with them.

He was the last of those men off that ground, and the near end of his own table was a quarter of an inch low on that hollow, and the foot and about four inches of sound pallet was lying on the boards at the far end of that table where anybody walking past could see it.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it, and it took no line on it and it did not carry a sixty-seventh line. That lane brought nothing down it and it stands at thirty pages behind it as it always has stood. Three of those first thirty-three lines came through this district and no voice in this district has read any of them out loud. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months has been counted in this district and no thing in this basin will hold it. That gatepost sheet will be carrying four hundred and eleven in the morning, as it is now.

He put that piece into the near corner of his own top at about ten and it stayed in until about four and that table sat level the whole of that time, and at about four he took it out and put it back on those boards, and nobody in that yard said one word about it at any point of that day.
