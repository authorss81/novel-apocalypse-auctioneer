# Chapter 1141: The Four Hundred And Twenty-Fifth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And One Reason Went Out Loud From Those Stones At About Four

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and nothing in this district has ever been done with it. That figure is eight hundred and eighty days old.

That board carries one thousand and thirty-nine marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and fifty-five days without a wagon ever coming off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and sixty-nine days. One thousand and thirty days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and was not run. It is not a lot on this ground and it is not open here this morning. Seven hundred and forty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line this yard stands. That rule said out loud in here has stood seven hundred and forty-nine days. It has been eight hundred and nineteen days since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and fifty-eight days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and twenty-two days back. Six hundred and ninety-eight marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and eighty-four marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and no thing here carries the count. Those four documents have never come to this district at all. Three of those lines are readable in four places in this basin and in no fifth place. Four conditions are set out in this district and not one of them has an end on it. Fourteen things is everything this basin has made, and there has never been a fifteenth of anything made in it. Five of those fourteen have never come here, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

One reason went out loud from those stones at about four, and it did not come down in anybody's mouth after it. Nobody answered the man who said it and nobody thanked him. One line went onto the page at the back of that lot book, and that line carries this morning and not what was said in it. That page stood at sixty-six lines from the first board up to the last one off. It stands at sixty-seven lines.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and fifty-five mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there four hundred and five mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-five mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and twenty-fourth morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and eightieth night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and seventy-nine of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and sixty-sixth of those mornings, and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and eighty-first morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and fourteenth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with the back of it bare. Five lines are ruled across one page of it. The column on the page after that one holds one name under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form, and it is empty. Behind that lane there are thirty pages and every one of them is in the hands of the people who brought it down. Nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and forty days. About nine people were on that ground at ten to eight, and that ground has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and seventieth afternoon running, and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two distances, and nobody here has ever got to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it has been paid and it is spent. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district, and nobody in this district has heard one of them read.

---

He came onto that ground in the afternoon with his barrow and his short-handled spade, and he got the loam off the top of that strip where it had been lying since the last time, and there is a band of clay about a foot down under it that he wants before the light comes off that bank, because clay that is wet this evening is clay he does not have to carry twice.

What he wanted off that afternoon was the strip down to the clay and his own back off that bend of it.

---

At ten to eight the boy was at those boards with his hands in his pockets and gave that lane count out loud, and not one page came down it behind the number.

About half past nine the man of thirty-eight came down that slope and went out to about four feet off the near end of that first table, and he stayed at about those four feet until about four, and nobody put one word to him at any point of it.

From about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had her own pan up about four feet off the near end of that second table, with her basket down beside her, and nothing at all came out of her mouth that day.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight put that number of his out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for it alone, and about four voices in that yard had it behind him out of step with one another, and he stood in the middle of that with his hands at his sides and let them have it.

The frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it, and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel off it, and the turner said nothing out loud at any hour of that day.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, and he was asked nothing.

---

At the far end of that ground the man of about thirty-two had his own top across his own trestles and was going along the far half of it with his cloth.

---

At about four he stood up off that clay with his back going and put one thing out loud into that yard, the way a man puts a thing out loud when he wants it to go the width of the ground instead of the width of a spade.

He said it once.

Nobody answered him.

Nobody thanked him.

He did not say it again, and he did not put one other word out loud at any hour of that day, and it did not come down in anybody's mouth after it. By that evening there was nobody in this district he could have put it to again.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another, and the flat answer went to that stone after them. It was the three hundred and fourteenth time running for it, and nobody in this district has ever asked him anything about any part of it.

The clerk came down that row of boards with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud before her hand had gone near any part of it. Then she wrote the four hundred and twenty-fifth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and under her own hand she took one line.

---

The light came off those boards at about half past four, and that day's boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them.

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it, and nobody in this basin has ever been given that far end.

He went up behind the last of them and left his spade lying flat on the clay where he had stood it.

---

That page stood at sixty-seven lines when the covers came over it, and it carries no sixty-eighth. That lane brought nothing down it, and it is where it was that morning. Those three came through this district and have never been read out loud in it. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and nothing on this ground will take that count. That gatepost sheet will be carrying the same four hundred and eleven on it in the morning and in the morning after that.

One reason went out loud from those stones at about four, and one line went onto that page carrying the morning and not the reason. Nobody answered him and nobody thanked him.
