# Chapter 1122: The Four Hundred And Sixth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And Nothing Was Turning On That Frame And The Turner Was Stood At It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and that sheet has been on that gatepost through everything this basin has done and everything it has not done. It is eight hundred and sixty-one days old now.

That board carries one thousand and twenty marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and thirty-six days and not one wagon has come off it in any of them. Nobody has entered anything at that rail for one thousand and fifty days. One thousand and eleven days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, was never run, and is not a lot and not open this morning. Seven hundred and twenty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard is. Seven hundred and thirty days is how long that rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Eight hundred days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and thirty-nine days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and three days back. Six hundred and seventy-nine marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and sixty-five marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months a clerk has counted in this district, and not one thing in this basin will hold that count down. Those four documents have never been got inside this district. Three of those lines stand in four places in this basin and in no fifth place at all. Four conditions stand set out in this district and not one of them has an end set after it. Fourteen is what this basin has made and nothing outside those fourteen has ever come out of it. Five of those fourteen have never come as far as this district and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is at sixty-six lines this morning and it took nothing onto it. What the first asking of anybody was is not on that page and is not on any other paper here. On the one hundred and eighty-second morning a reason was said out loud in that yard. Nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-six mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and eighty-six mornings, and the man who fitted that rail is not to be thanked. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and six mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and fifth morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. On the seven hundred and sixty-first night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and sixty of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and forty-seventh of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and sixty-second morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and ninety-fifth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

Nothing has ever been written at the back of that lot book and it lies open along those boards with nothing on the back of it. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book and the column on the page after that one holds a single name under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and it stands empty in front of everybody in that yard this morning. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in the hands of their own carriers and not one page came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and twenty-one days. About nine people were on that ground at ten to eight this morning and it has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and fifty-first afternoon running and one word was all that afternoon came to. Neither of those two has ever been handed to anybody in this district, and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it is paid and it is spent and none of it is left anywhere in this basin. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district and no voice in this district has ever read one of them out loud.

---

He was at that frame at about six and a half with the stove already in a good while, and the casting had been cold since before the light came and he had his hand on it to prove it.

What he wanted off that morning was the whole of that load off that cart and up that slope before the light went off the boards, and no stop on the way in.

---

There is a groove cut across the face of that casting about the width of two fingers and the belt sits down in it, and there are two marks on that casting that he made himself and there has never been a third.

He went at the groove with the length of wire he keeps for it, a foot and a half of it with a hook bent in one end, and the wire came up the whole width of that groove and there was nothing in it at all.

He put the wire back inside his coat where it lives.

---

At about half past eight the light came over the top of that bank and lay along the whole length of that ground.

The man of about thirty-four was already at his own table at the far end of it. The man of about thirty-two had the far end of that first table and a wedge in his hand. Neither of them said one word to the turner about anything, and the turner said nothing out loud to either of them.

---

There is a figure that belongs to this morning and it is an eight hundred, and it belongs to the first day of the eighth month, and it stands on that block with the rest of them the way every one of those figures stands on the morning it sits on.

Nobody in that yard said it out loud and nobody at the top of that bank looked down at it.

---

The boy said thirty off those boards at ten to eight, before the clerk had that book open, and nothing came down that lane behind the number.

He was standing at about four feet off the near end of that first table from about half past nine with a cloth in his hand and nobody spoke to him in those four feet before about four, and those four feet are the four feet where a man can work without being spoken to.

She was at her own pan from about half past twelve, four feet off the near end of that second table, with the basket down beside her, and she put nothing out loud in that yard this morning.

Half past one came round and that man put his number out into the air over that ground in the voice he keeps for it alone, and it went off the face of that bank, and about four voices behind him in that yard had it a moment later and not one of them together.

---

There are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank at the ground of that yard, and nobody in this district has ever been up over the far end of that.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and the four came off the top of it one after another, and what the man of fifty-six said to that stone after them was the two hundred and ninety-fifth time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him anything about any part of it.

She came along those boards at about four, gave the morning out loud with the pen still in the holder, and wrote the four hundred and sixth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand. She took no line for it.

---

About half past four the light left those boards and that day's stack came off the cart with its face down and went up the slope on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up with it before that ground was empty.

He stood at that frame and watched the last of them go up, and nothing was turning on that frame, and he had not put his heel on it once in all of that day.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it and it took no line on it. Nothing came down that lane and the thirty behind it are the thirty that were behind it this morning. Those three came through this district and no voice in this district has read one of them out. That fifth of that five is five and nobody here has ever paid it. Seven months is counted in this district and there is nothing here that will hold it. That gatepost sheet will have the same four hundred and eleven on it in the morning, as it has now.

Nothing was turning on that frame and the turner was stood at it from about six and a half until that ground was empty, and he said nothing out loud that morning, and nobody asked him anything.
