# Chapter 1139: The Four Hundred And Twenty-Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And He Went Up That Slope At About Two With Four Boards On His Shoulder

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this basin has ever done a thing with that figure or with that sheet. That figure is eight hundred and seventy-eight days old.

That board carries one thousand and thirty-seven marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and fifty-three days and not one wagon has come off it in that whole time. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and sixty-seven days. One thousand and twenty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, was not run, and it is not a lot here this morning and it is not open here this morning. This yard is seven hundred and forty-two days behind the figure on the second line of that book. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and forty-seven days. It has been eight hundred and seventeen days since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and fifty-six days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and twenty days back. Six hundred and ninety-six marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and eighty-two marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing whatever in this basin that will take that count. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places here and there is not a fifth place in this basin that has any of them on it. Four conditions are set out in this district and this district has never written the end of any one of them. Fourteen things is the whole of what this basin has made and there has never been a fifteenth. Five of those fourteen never got as far as this district, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is at sixty-six lines and no line went onto it at any point of that morning. There is no first asking on that page, and there has never been one on any paper in this district. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing put on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and fifty-three mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there four hundred and three mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-three mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and twenty-second morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and seventy-eighth night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and seventy-seven of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and sixty-fourth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and seventy-ninth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and twelfth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards and the back of it is bare. There are five lines ruled across one page of that book and the column on the page after that one holds one name in it under a heading which still says whoever. On that form a second space is ruled and nothing has ever been put in it. Behind that lane there are thirty pages and every one of them is in the hands of the people who brought it, and nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and thirty-eight days. About nine people stood on that ground at ten to eight and it has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones that afternoon for the three hundred and sixty-eighth afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody here has ever been given either of those two distances, and nobody has ever been up over the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and was paid and it is spent. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district and no voice in it has read one of them out loud.

---

He was at those boards at about ten to eight with his hands in his pockets, which is where he says that number every morning he is on that ground.

What he wanted off that morning was that number said out loud before the clerk's hand had gone anywhere, and a stack he could look at all day, and half past one to come round.

---

"Thirty," said Orren Pike. "And there is not one thing behind that this morning and I am not saying a word after it."

He said that part out loud as well.

Then the stub of pencil went behind his ear and he went back down to the near end of that stack with his hands in his pockets again.

---

The man of thirty-eight was at his four feet off the near end of that first table from about half past nine until about four with the cloth in his hand, and nobody in that yard said anything to him in any of it.

From about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had her own pan up about four feet off the near end of that second table with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that morning, and no one has ever asked her what she weighs things for.

At the far end of that ground the man of about thirty-four had his own table up and was going along it with a cloth, and the foot and about four inches of sound pallet was lying on the boards at the far end of that table, square cut end pointing at the near edge of it.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel off it, and nobody in that yard started it that day.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was asked nothing at any hour of it.

---

He got that day's boards off the cart at about half past nine and squared the stack off against those boards with the side of his boot, and the man of about thirty-two came down past him with a shingle in his hand and stopped at that end of that stack.

"Square it off those boards and not off yourself."

"I am squaring it off those boards."

"You were squaring it off yourself a minute ago."

"I am squaring it off those boards now."

Not one word of that was about anything on that ground except that stack, and the boy said about that much on any morning.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had that number of his out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for it and for nothing else.

About four voices in that yard had it behind him out of step with one another, and he stood in the middle of that with his hands at his sides and let them have it.

There is an order in that boy's head for the way those four voices come out after that number, and he has never said it out loud to anybody in this district, and nobody has ever asked him for it and nobody has ever looked to see whether he has it right.

He stood at the near end of that stack and listened to all four of them come out that day, and he did not say one word about any of it, then or afterwards, and there is nobody in this district he could have said it to.

---

At about two that afternoon he went up that slope with four boards on his shoulder, and he was not at that stack again that day and he did not come down that slope again that day.

Nobody in that yard said one word to anybody about where he had gone or about when he would be back, and he had said nothing out loud to anybody about going up.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the three hundred and twelfth time running for it.

The clerk read that morning out loud at about four with the pen in the holder and wrote the four hundred and twenty-third morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it and nobody in this basin has ever been given that far end.

---

At about half past four the light came off those boards and that day's boards came off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up with them, and the boy was not one of them.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it and it took no line and carried no sixty-seventh. Nothing came down that lane and it stands at thirty pages behind it as it has stood through that whole of it. Those three came through this district and nobody here has read one of them out loud. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and nothing in this basin is set to hold it. That gatepost sheet will go on carrying the same four hundred and eleven in the morning and the morning after.

He said thirty at ten to eight and nothing behind it, and no page came down that lane that day, and that lane count is where it was that morning. He went up that slope at about two and nobody in that yard said one word about it.
