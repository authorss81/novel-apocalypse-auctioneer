# Chapter 1144: The Four Hundred And Twenty-Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And He Carried That Piece The Length Of His Own Table And Put It Down In The Near Corner Of That Top

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and no person in this district has ever put it to any use at all. That figure is eight hundred and eighty-three days old.

That board carries one thousand and forty-two marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and fifty-eight days, and not one wagon has come off it in any of them. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and seventy-two days. One thousand and thirty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and was never run. It is not a lot on this ground and it is not an opening on this ground this morning. Seven hundred and forty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and fifty-two days. Eight hundred and twenty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and sixty-one days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and twenty-five days back. Seven hundred and one marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and eighty-seven marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing whatever on this ground for it to sit on. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places here, and there is no fifth place in this basin with any of them on it. Four conditions are set out in this district, and every one of them is still without an end on it. Fourteen things is everything this basin has made and no fifteenth has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen never came to this district, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page took no line at any hour of that morning, and it stands at sixty-seven lines. That page carries no first asking, and no paper in this district has ever carried one. There was a reason said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning. Nothing put on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and fifty-eight mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there four hundred and eight mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-eight mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and twenty-seventh morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and eighty-third night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and eighty-two of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and sixty-ninth of those mornings, and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and eighty-fourth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and seventeenth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

The back of that lot book is bare, and that book lies open along those boards. Five lines are ruled across one page of it, and the column on the page after that one carries one name in it under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form, and it stands empty. Thirty pages are behind that lane and every one of them went back up with the person who carried it down, and nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and forty-three days. About nine people were on that ground at ten to eight, and it has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and seventy-third afternoon running, and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody here has ever been handed either of those two distances, and nobody in this district has ever got over the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it is spent. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district and nobody has ever heard one of them read out loud in it.

---

He came down that slope at about twenty past seven with the cloth over his arm and his own bin under the other arm. The light was lying along the whole of that ground while the near end of his own top was still in the shadow off that bank.

What he wanted off that morning was the whole of that top along with the cloth, and his own bin shut, before the light came off those boards.

---

He put his own table up and squared that top with the back of his heel.

The near end of that top stood a quarter of an inch low on the hollow of grit at the foot of the near trestle, and the top of it is twisted and not dipping. He put his hand flat on that near corner and pushed on it once, and it is not loose and it is not square.

---

He got the cloth and started at the far end of that top and came back toward the middle of it, and the cloth went along that half of it without the board dipping anywhere under his hand.

At about a quarter to ten he stood with the cloth over his arm and the sun coming up off that bank behind him.

---

At about ten he went to the far end of his own table and picked that piece up where it had lain since the light came.

He carried it the length of his own table and set that square cut end down into the near corner of his own top, into the corner where the two boards of that frame come together. He leaned on the end of it with the heel of his hand until it was all the way in.

It went in the whole way.

The near corner of that top came up off that hollow of grit and that table stood level on its two trestles, and the daylight that had been standing under that corner all morning was not standing there any more.

---

There were about nineteen people in that yard, and not one of them said one word about it.

Nobody asked him what that piece was for.

Nobody thanked him for it.

He took his hand off it and stood a while looking down the length of that table.

There was no light standing under any part of that top. It is twisted and it is not dipping, and it is a table, and there is nothing in it but the top.

Then he got the cloth off his arm and started at the far end again and came all the way back, and he did the near two feet as well. The cloth went along them without the board going down anywhere under his hand.

---

At ten to eight the boy had said thirty at those boards and had said no figure after it out loud, and that lane brought nothing down behind it.

About half past nine the man of thirty-eight came down that slope and went out to about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand, and he was there until about four, and no one in that yard said a word to him.

About half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had her own pan up at the near end of that second table with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud at any hour of that day.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight put his number out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for that and nothing else, and about four voices in that yard had it behind him out of step with one another, and nobody in that yard put one word out loud about any part of it.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it, and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel off it. Nobody in that yard started it at any hour of that day.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, and he was asked nothing at any hour of it.

---

At about two that cart came through the gap and the near wheel went down into the place it goes down at the second bend, and that table did not move at the near end. He did not stop with his cloth to look at that.

At about three he put his hand flat on that top twice, the near half and then the far half, to see whether it was still where he had left it.

---

The hand went up on that low wall at about four and four figures came off it one after another, and the flat answer went to that stone behind them. It was the three hundred and seventeenth time running for it, and nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about any part of it.

The clerk read that morning out loud at about four with the pen in the holder and wrote the four hundred and twenty-eighth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

---

At about half past four the light came off those boards, and that day's boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them, and that piece was in the near corner of his own top when the light went off those boards.

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it, and no one in this basin has ever been given that far end.

He went up behind the last of them with his bin under his arm.

---

That page stood at sixty-seven lines when the covers came over it, took no line onto it and carried no sixty-eighth. That lane brought no page down it today, and it stands at thirty pages behind it as it always has. Those three came through this district and nobody in this district has read one of them out loud. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and there is nothing here to take that count. That gatepost sheet will be carrying that same four hundred and eleven on it in the morning and in the morning after.

That table sat level from about ten until the light came off those boards, and he got the near two feet of that top along with the cloth while it did, and not one person in that yard said one word to him about any part of that day.
