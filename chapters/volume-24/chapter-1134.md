# Chapter 1134: The Four Hundred And Eighteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And He Squared His Own Top With The Back Of His Heel And Left The Near Two Feet Alone

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and no person who has ever lived in this basin has done one thing with that sheet. That figure is eight hundred and seventy-three days old.

That board carries one thousand and thirty-two marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and forty-eight days without a wagon coming off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and sixty-two days. One thousand and twenty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and was never run, and it is neither a lot nor open on this ground this morning. This yard is seven hundred and thirty-seven days behind the figure on the second line of that book. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and forty-two days. It has been eight hundred and twelve days since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and fifty-one days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and fifteen days back. Six hundred and ninety-one marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and seventy-seven marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and nothing in this basin will take that count. Those four documents have never been in this district at all. Three of those lines are readable in four places here and no fifth place in this basin carries any of them. There are four conditions set out in this district and not one of them has ever been finished. Fourteen things is the whole of what this basin has made and there has never been a fifteenth made in it here. Five of that fourteen have not come as far as this ground, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is at sixty-six lines and it took nothing onto it in that morning. The first asking is on no paper in this district, and that page is one of the papers it is not on. On the one hundred and eighty-second morning a reason was said out loud in that yard, and whatever was written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was not a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and forty-eight mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there three hundred and ninety-eight mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and eighteen mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and seventeenth morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and seventy-third night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and seventy-two of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and fifty-ninth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and seventy-fourth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and seventh time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards and the back of it has always been bare. Five lines are ruled across a page of that book, and the column on the page after that one carries one name under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form and it stands empty in front of everybody here. Behind that lane there are thirty pages, every one of them in the hands of the people who brought them, and nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and thirty-three days. About nine people stood on that ground at ten to eight, and that lot has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones that afternoon for the three hundred and sixty-third afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Neither of those two has ever been given to anybody in this district, and nobody has ever been up over the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it has been paid and is spent. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page three came through this district, and nobody in this district has read one of them out loud.

---

He came down that slope at about twenty past seven with the cloth over his arm, and that day's cart was already out on the flat above the gap because there was a load coming in at about two and it does not wait for anybody.

What he wanted off that morning was the far half of that top along with the cloth and his own bin shut, because a cart coming in over that gap at about two puts a shake through that ground that a man with a trestle under his hand can feel in his teeth.

---

He put his table up the way he puts it up and squared the top with the back of his heel.

The near end of that top stands a quarter of an inch low on the hollow of grit at the foot of the near trestle, and the top of it is twisted and not dipping, and both of those have been so at about eight on every morning he has come down that slope.

He put his hand flat on that near corner and pushed on it once, the way a man pushes to find out whether a thing is loose.

It is not loose and it is not square.

---

The foot and about four inches of sound pallet lay out on those boards at the far end of that table with the square cut end pointing at the near edge, in the way of anybody coming the length of that yard.

Grit out of his own bin had got into the pores of that square end in the night, and he got it off with the back of his thumbnail before he set his hand on it, and then he left the piece where it was.

---

He got the cloth at the far end of that top and came back with it as far as the middle of that board, and the board did not go down anywhere under his hand, because that half of it stands over ground nobody in this district can remember moving.

Those two feet at the near end are a different thing entirely, and he does not put his weight on them, and he did not put his weight on them that morning.

---

At ten to eight the boy said thirty at those boards and said no figure after it, and that lane brought nothing down behind it.

From about half past nine the man of thirty-eight was standing at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand until about four, and nobody spoke to him in that whole time.

From about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had her pan up about four feet off the near end of that second table with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that morning.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for it, and about four voices in that yard took it up behind him out of step with one another, and nobody in that yard put one word out loud about any of it.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel off it, and nobody in that yard started it.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall all morning and was asked nothing at any hour of it.

---

At about ten he and the man of about thirty-two took the far edge of that first table between them and went at it for about twenty minutes with a block and a chisel.

"Your ground is packed today."

"It is packed because your cart came in over the same place twice yesterday."

"Your near wheel finds the same hole at the second bend every time and that hole is getting deeper."

"Wedge the leg before you put anything on it, and then it does not matter what it drops."

"Then do it at about ten and not at about eleven."

Not one word of that was about the piece on the boards at the far end of that other table, and neither of them looked at it while the other had his hands full.

---

About two the cart came through the gap and the near wheel went into the same place at that second bend on the way in, and the shake went through that ground, and his own table moved about the width of a thumbnail at the near end where the grit is hollowed out.

He watched the boards come off it and he did not say anything about that either.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the three hundred and seventh time running for it.

The clerk came down that row of boards at about four with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud and wrote the four hundred and eighteenth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it, and nobody in this basin has ever been given that end of it.

---

About half past four the light came off those boards and that day's boards came off that cart with their faces to the ground and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up with them.

He was on that ground after the last of them had gone up, and that piece was lying on the boards at the far end of his own table where anybody walking past could see it, and he folded the cloth over his arm and shut his own bin and stood at the near end of his own table a while with his hand flat on the near corner of it.

Nobody in that yard said one word about any of that.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it and it took no line on it and it did not carry a sixty-seventh. No page came down that lane and it stands at thirty pages behind it as it always has stood in that run. Those three came through this district and not one of them has been read aloud in it. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and nothing in this basin will hold that count. That gatepost sheet will be carrying four hundred and eleven tomorrow and the morning after that.

He worked the far half of his own top with the cloth and left the near two feet alone, and the near end of that table was a quarter of an inch low on that hollow when the light went.
