# Chapter 1116: The Four Hundredth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And A Man With A Count Said It Out Loud At About Half Past Two

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this basin has ever done anything at all with that figure. That figure is eight hundred and fifty-five days old.

That board carries one thousand and fourteen marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and thirty days and no wagon has come off it in any of them. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and forty-four days. One thousand and five days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was not run, so it is not a lot and it is not open this morning. Seven hundred and nineteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and twenty-four days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Seven hundred and ninety-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and thirty-three days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and ninety-seven days back. Six hundred and seventy-three marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and fifty-nine marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing in this basin that will hold it. None of those four documents has ever been got inside this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places in this basin, and there is no fifth place in it that has any of them on it. There are four conditions set out in this district and not one of them has ever had an end put to it. Fourteen is what this basin has made, and nothing outside those fourteen has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen never came into this district, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is at sixty-five lines this morning and it took no line onto it. There is no first asking on that page and there is none on any other paper here, and nobody in this district is able to produce one. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one. It is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and thirty mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and eighty mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and ninety-ninth morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and fifty-fifth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and fifty-four of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and forty-first of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and fifty-sixth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and eighty-ninth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

There is nothing at the back of that lot book and there never has been, and that book lies open along those boards this morning. One page of that book carries five lines ruled across it, and the column on the page after that one holds a name under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form, and it has stood empty this morning as it stands every morning. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in their own carriers' hands and nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and fifteen days. There were about nine people stood on that ground at ten to eight and it has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and forty-fifth afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it has been paid and spent and nothing of it is left anywhere in this basin. Three of the first thirty-three lines on that back page came through this district, and nobody in it has ever heard one of those three read.

---

He came in at about eight with the little book in the inside pocket of his coat and he got the morning's ground onto that paper the way he has got it onto that paper for a long time, and half past one is the hour and it has always been the hour.

What he wanted off that morning was for that number to go out at half past one and be taken up behind him the way it is always taken up, and for the day's entry to be shut before the light went off those boards.

---

At about ten to eight the boy was at those boards with his hands in his pockets and said thirty, and nothing came down that lane behind it.

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight was at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand, and he stayed at about those four feet until about four, and nobody put one word to him there.

At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan, four feet off the near end of that second table, with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that day.

Nothing was turning on that frame at the near end of that first table, and the turner was at it from about six and a half, and he did not put his heel on it, and nobody in that yard started it, and he said nothing out loud this morning.

---

At about half past one he took that number out into about four hundred yards of air.

He said it in the voice he keeps for it, which is not the voice he uses for anything else and is neither a shout nor low. Then about four voices in that yard took it up behind him, out of step with one another, the way they always do, and it went off the face of that bank.

He stood there and let them have it.

Nobody in that yard put one word out loud about it.

---

At about half past two he said a second thing, and he said it in the ordinary voice, out on that ground where anybody standing at those tables could get it.

"Four hundred mornings a man has stood at that foot of that bank," he said. "That is mine and it is entered after it, and I have said it before it goes in."

That is four hundred, and nobody at those tables or anywhere else on that ground said one word out loud about it, and nobody standing on those boards had any idea what had just been said down there.

Nobody answered him.

Nobody put one question to him about it, and none of the four voices that had taken his number up out of step a half of an hour before came back for this one.

---

About four the clerk came down that row of boards with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud before her hand went near any part of it, and then she wrote the four hundredth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and took no line for it.

She has read a morning number out loud for a good many years and she has never once changed what she does on a morning when the figure is round, and this one was read the same as the rest of them.

About four people on those boards stood still through the whole of that with a foot on a board and nobody arranged it.

Nobody on those boards looked up.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and eighty-ninth time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him a question about any part of it.

The man of about thirty-four was at the far end of that first table with his cloth over his arm, and the foot and about four inches of sound pallet was lying on the boards at the far end of his own table with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of it.

---

At about half past four the light came off those boards and that day's boards came down off the cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up with them.

He shut the little book, put it back into the inside pocket of his coat, and went up that bank with the last of them.

---

That page stood at sixty-five lines when the covers came over it and it took no line and it did not carry a sixty-sixth. No page came down that lane today, and it stands at thirty pages behind it as it stood yesterday. Three of those first thirty-three lines came through this district and no voice in this district has read any of them out. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and there is nothing in it that will carry that. That gatepost sheet will be carrying four hundred and eleven in the morning, same as it is carrying it now.

He put his number out at half past one and about four voices took it up out of step with one another, and at about half past two he said a count of four hundred out loud on that ground, and nobody in that yard looked up at any of it.
