# Chapter 1129: The Four Hundred And Thirteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And A Boy At The Near End Of That Stack Watched A Piece Go Into A Corner And Come Out

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and no person in this basin has ever done one thing with that figure on that sheet. That figure is eight hundred and sixty-eight days old.

That board carries one thousand and twenty-seven marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and forty-three days and no wagon has come off it in any of them. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and fifty-seven days. One thousand and eighteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, was not run, and it is not a lot nor open on this ground this morning. Seven hundred and thirty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands on. Seven hundred and thirty-seven days is how long that rule said out loud in that yard of this district has stood. Eight hundred and seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and forty-six days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and ten days back. Six hundred and eighty-six marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and seventy-two marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and nothing at all in this basin will take that count. Those four documents have never been in this district at all. Three of those lines are readable in four places in this basin and not in any fifth place in it. Four conditions are set out in this district and not one of them has ever had an end put to it. Fourteen things is the whole of what this basin has made, and no fifteenth has ever come out of it. Five of those fourteen have never come to this district and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is at sixty-six lines and nothing was taken onto it this morning. That page has no first asking on it and nothing else in this district has one either. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning has ever been a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and forty-three mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and ninety-three mornings, and the man who fitted that rail is not to be thanked. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and thirteen mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and twelfth morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. On the seven hundred and sixty-eighth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and sixty-seven of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and fifty-fourth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and sixty-ninth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and second time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with the back of it bare, and it has lain open along those boards with the back of it bare since before the oldest of them came to that ground. Five lines are ruled across one of the pages of that book and the column on the page after that one has one name in it under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form and it stands empty in front of everybody in that yard. Behind that lane are thirty pages and every one of them is in the hand of the person who brought it, and nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and twenty-eight days. About nine people were on that ground at ten to eight, and that lot has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, the way it is every morning of that run.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and fifty-eighth afternoon running and one word was all there was in that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two, and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and not one word of it has ever been said.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it has been paid and it is spent, and none of it is left anywhere in this district. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district and no voice in this district has read one of them aloud.

---

He was at those boards at about ten to eight with his hands in his pockets, which is where he has said that number every morning he has stood at that end of that stack.

What he wanted off that morning was for that number to be said before the clerk's hand had gone anywhere, and for the stack to come off the cart while the light was still on that ground.

---

"Thirty," said Orren Pike. "And there is nothing behind it this morning and I am not saying one word after it."

He said that out loud as well, which he does.

There is an order in his head for the way those four voices come out after that number at half past one, and nobody in this district has ever asked him for it and nobody in this district has ever looked to see whether he has it right. He did not say it out loud this morning and he said out loud that he was not saying it out loud.

Then the stub of pencil went behind his ear and he went back down to the near end of that stack.

---

The cart came through the gap at about two and the near wheel went into the same place at the second bend on the way in.

He was at the near end of that stack from about half past two, and that is where he stands when the boards come off.

---

At about ten he was at that end with his hand on the top board of that stack and he saw the man of about thirty-four go and get the piece off the boards at the far end of his own table.

He watched him carry it the length of that table and set the square cut end into the near corner of his own top, right down into the corner where the two boards of that frame come together, and push on the end of it with the heel of his hand.

It went in the whole way, and that table came up level on its two trestles, and the light that had been standing under that corner all morning was not standing there any more.

Then the man stood looking down the length of that table for a while, and then he took the piece out of that corner again, and the near end of that top went straight back down onto its hollow and the light came back under that corner about the width of a finger.

He laid the piece on the boards at the far end of that table with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of it and got his cloth and went back to the far half of that table.

Nobody said one word about any of that.

Nobody asked him anything about any of that.

---

At about four feet off the near end of that first table the man of thirty-eight was at his length of board with his cloth in his hand and nobody in that yard went near him.

From about half past twelve she had her own pan up about four feet off the near end of that second table with the basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud all that day.

About half past one the number went out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice that man keeps for it, and about four voices in that yard took it up after him out of step with one another.

That frame was standing with nothing turning on it and the turner was at it with his heel off it, and nobody in that yard started it, and nobody put one question to him about it either.

---

There are about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert, and nobody in this basin has ever been given either end of that.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the three hundred and second time running.

At about four she read that morning out loud from those boards with the pen still in the holder and put the four hundred and thirteenth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand afterwards, and she took no line for it.

About half past four, and the light was off those boards and the day's stack was off the cart face down and up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, with about nineteen people going up beside it.

He went up with the near end of that stack, on the boards, the way he goes up, and the foot and about four inches of sound pallet was lying on the boards at the far end of that man's own table when he went past it, and he did not stop for it and nobody has ever asked him about it.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it, took no line onto it and carried no sixty-seventh. No page came down that lane and it is at thirty pages behind it. Those three came through this district and not one of them has been read out in this district. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and nothing in this basin will take it. That gatepost sheet will be carrying the same four hundred and eleven on it in the morning and the morning after that.

He watched a piece go into the near corner of that man's own top at about ten and that table sit level, and then he watched him take that piece out again inside a minute and lay it on the boards at the far end, and nobody said one word about it.
