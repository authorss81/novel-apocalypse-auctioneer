# Chapter 1138: The Four Hundred And Twenty-Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And The Woman Of About Fifty-Two Stood In Four Feet Of Sun With Nothing In Her Hands

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and nothing in this basin has ever been done with that figure. That figure is eight hundred and seventy-seven days old.

That board carries one thousand and thirty-six marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and fifty-two days without a wagon ever coming off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and sixty-six days. One thousand and twenty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, was not run, and it is not a lot on this ground and it is not open here this morning. This yard is seven hundred and forty-one days behind the figure on the second line of that book. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and forty-six days. It has been eight hundred and sixteen days since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and fifty-five days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and nineteen days back. Six hundred and ninety-five marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and eighty-one marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing in this basin that will take that count. Those four documents have never been in this district at all. Three of those lines can be read in four places in this basin and in no fifth place in it. Four conditions have been set out in this district since before that book was opened, and no end is written on any of them. Fourteen is the whole of the making in this basin and no fifteenth has ever been made in it. Five of that fourteen never came here, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is at sixty-six lines and no line went onto it at any hour of that morning. That page carries no first asking of anybody, and no other paper in this district does. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and not one line written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning has ever been a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and fifty-two mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there four hundred and two mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-two mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and twenty-first morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and seventy-seventh night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and seventy-six of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and sixty-third of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and seventy-eighth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and eleventh time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards, the back of it bare, and it has lain open there with the back of it bare for as long as anything in this district has been kept count of. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book and the column on the page after that one carries one name in it under a heading which still says whoever. That form carries a second ruled space and it stands empty in front of everybody here. Behind that lane there are thirty pages and every one of them is in the hands of the people who brought it down, and not one of them came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and thirty-seven days. That ground had about nine people on it at ten to eight and that lot has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones that afternoon for the three hundred and sixty-seventh afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two, and nobody has ever been up past the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight, was paid, and is spent. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district and no voice here has read one of them out loud.

---

She came down that bank at about half past ten with nothing in her hands, because the frame and the bag she had brought down that morning are up at the top of that bank behind a stone and there was nothing left in them that she wanted to carry.

What she wanted off that morning was four feet of sun on that ground and as much of it as the light would give her.

---

There is about four feet of sun lying on that ground between about half past ten and about four on a morning like this one, and it moves across that ground the whole time like something walking.

She found the four feet where they were, at the near end of that second table and a little off it, and she stood in the middle of them and she did not move out of them.

---

Nobody said good morning to her.

Nobody said one word to her at any hour of that morning, and not one person in that yard asked her anything, and not one person in that yard came over to where she was standing.

At ten to eight the boy had said thirty at those boards and said no figure after it out loud, and he came down that slope at about half past nine and went past her at about four feet' distance and he did not put his head down for her.

He came out at about half past nine and put himself at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand, and he stayed at those four feet until about four, and nobody in that yard spoke to him in any of it.

The woman who keeps a scale had her own pan up about four feet off the near end of that second table from about half past twelve with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that morning, and nobody has ever asked her what she weighs things for.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had that number of his out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for that alone, and about four voices in that yard had it behind him out of step with one another, and nobody in that yard put one word out loud about any of it.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel off it all day, and nobody in that yard started it, and nobody put one question to him about it.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall the whole morning and was asked nothing.

---

She had the form folded in four in the inside of her coat and she had it there at ten to eight and she had it there at about four.

There is a second space ruled on that form and it was empty in front of her the whole of that morning and empty in front of every person in that yard, and nobody asked her to fill it, and she did not offer it to anybody.

Nobody has ever asked her what a thing on that form is for either, and nobody in this district has told her what the heading over that column says.

---

By about one the sun had come off the near end of that second table and onto her boots, and she moved with it, four paces at a time, the way you move with anything that is going.

She went as far as the boards and then she came back, because the far side of that ground has no sun on it at all before the light goes, and she was not going to stand in the one place where there was none of it.

About two that cart came through the gap and the near wheel went into the same place at the second bend on the way in, and the whole of that ground moved about the width of a thumbnail under her feet, and she stood still through it and nobody in that yard said anything to her about that either.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the three hundred and eleventh time running for it.

The clerk read that morning out loud at about four with the pen still in the holder and then wrote the four hundred and twenty-second morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it and nobody in this basin has ever been given the far end of that either.

About half past four the light went off those boards and that day's boards came off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders with about nineteen people going up beside them.

The four feet of sun went with the light, the way it does, and she went up that bank behind the last of that day's boards with her hands empty and nobody in that yard saying one word to her about it.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it and it took no line onto it, and it carried no sixty-seventh line. Nothing came down that lane and it stands at thirty pages behind it as it has stood through that run. Those three came through this district and none of them has been read out loud in it. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and nothing whatever in this basin will take it. That gatepost sheet will be carrying four hundred and eleven in the morning and the morning after.

She stood in four feet of sun with nothing in her hands, nobody said good morning to her, the second space on that form was empty the whole of that morning, and that page took no line for any of it.
