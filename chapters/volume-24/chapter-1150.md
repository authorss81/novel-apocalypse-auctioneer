# Chapter 1150: The Four Hundred And Thirty-Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And That Yard Was Empty After The Last Of The Others Had Gone Up That Bank

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and no person in this basin has ever done a thing with that figure or with that sheet. That figure is eight hundred and eighty-nine days old.

That board carries one thousand and forty-eight marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and sixty-four days and not one wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and seventy-eight days. One thousand and thirty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, was not run, and it is not a lot on this ground and it is not open here this morning. Seven hundred and fifty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard is. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and fifty-eight days. Eight hundred and twenty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and sixty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and thirty-one days back. Seven hundred and seven marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and ninety-three marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is no thing whatever on this ground for it to be sitting on. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places here, and there is not a fifth place in this basin with any of them on it. Four conditions are set out in this district, and not one of them has ever had an end put to it here. Fourteen things is everything this basin has made and no fifteenth of anything has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen are not in this district, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

Nothing went onto that page at the back of that lot book at any hour of that morning, and it stands at sixty-seven lines, and there is no total of those lines anywhere in this district. There is no first asking on that page and there has never been one on any other paper in this district. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning. Nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was a line for one, so it is counted nowhere. And no figure has been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and sixty-four mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there four hundred and fourteen mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-four mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and thirty-third morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and eighty-ninth night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and eighty-eight of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and seventy-fifth of those mornings, and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and ninetieth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and twenty-third time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with the back of it bare, and it has lain open there with the back of it bare since before the oldest of them came to that ground. Five lines are ruled across one page of it, and the column on the page after that one holds a single name in it under a heading which still says whoever. That form carries a second ruled space and it stands empty. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in the hands of the people who brought them down, and nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and forty-nine days. About nine people stood on that ground at ten to eight, and that ground has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and seventy-ninth afternoon running, and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been handed either of those two distances, and nobody here has ever been up over the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it is spent. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page three came through this district, and nobody in this district has read one of them out loud in it.

---

He came down that slope at about twenty past seven with the cloth over his arm and his own bin under the other arm, and the light was lying along the whole of that ground while the near end of his own top was still in the shadow off that bank.

What he wanted off that morning was the whole of that top along with the cloth and his own bin shut before that light went off those boards.

---

He set his own table up and squared that top with the back of his heel.

There was a length of pale wood let into the near corner of that top, where the two boards of that frame come together, and that top was not down at that corner.

He got the cloth and started at the far end of that top and came all the way back, and the cloth went along the whole of it without the board going down anywhere under his hand.

---

At ten to eight the boy was at those boards with his hands in his pockets and gave that lane count out loud, and he said nothing behind it at all, and that lane brought nothing down it.

About half past nine the man of thirty-eight came down that slope and went out to about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand.

About half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had her own pan up at the near end of that second table with her basket down beside her, and nothing at all came out of her mouth at any hour of that day.

The man of about thirty-two had his own top across his own trestles at the far end of that ground.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight put his number out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for that and nothing else, and about four voices in that yard had it behind him out of step with one another, and he stood in the middle of that and let them have it.

Nobody asked the man of about sixty-four anything at the foot of that low wall.

---

At about two the turner put one thing out loud where anybody at that stack could get it.

"That stack wants its bottom two rows turned before it goes up, and the ones on top of them want to be in an order that is not the order they are stacked in, and the light off that bank is not going to"

He stopped there and went back to that frame with nothing turning on it and his heel off it.

Nobody asked him anything about that.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall, and four figures came off the top of it one after another, and the flat answer went to that stone behind them. It was the three hundred and twenty-third time running for it.

The clerk read that morning out loud at about four with the pen in the holder and wrote the four hundred and thirty-fourth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

---

At about half past four that light came off those boards, and that day's boards came off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them, and that yard was empty after that.

That lane count is thirty, and nothing came down that lane.

The frame stands at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it, and the turner is standing at it.

The woman who keeps a scale is standing at about four feet off the near end of that second table, with the cloth knotted over her scale and her basket on her arm.

The man of thirty-eight is standing at about four feet off the near end of that first table with the cloth in his hand.

He is standing at the far end of his own table with the cloth in his hand. The foot and about four inches of sound pallet is laid into the near corner of that top, and that table is sitting level.

---

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it, and nobody in this basin has ever been given that far end at all.

That page at the back of that lot book stood at sixty-seven lines when the covers came over it, and it carried no sixty-eighth, and none of those sixty-seven lines is in the lot book. Those three came through this district and nobody in this district has ever heard one of them read. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and there is no thing on this ground for it to sit on. That sheet at that gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and nobody in this basin has done anything with it.
