# Chapter 1135: The Four Hundred And Nineteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And Nine Boards Went Over On A Cart Face Down

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and nothing in this basin has ever been done with it. That figure is eight hundred and seventy-four days old.

That board carries one thousand and thirty-three marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and forty-nine days and not one wagon has come off it in that time. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and sixty-three days. One thousand and twenty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, was not run, and is not a lot on this ground and is not open here this morning. This yard stands seven hundred and thirty-eight days behind the figure on the second line of that book. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and forty-three days. It has been eight hundred and thirteen days since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and fifty-two days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and sixteen days back. Six hundred and ninety-two marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and seventy-eight marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing whatever in this basin that will take it. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places in this basin and in no fifth place in it. Four conditions are set out in this district and no end has ever been put to any of them. Fourteen is the whole of the making in this basin and there has never been a fifteenth made in it. Five of those fourteen never came to this district at all, and the fifth of those five is unpaid.

That page is at sixty-six lines and it took no line onto it that morning. Whatever the first asking was, it is on no paper in this district, and that page is one of the papers. One reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing that was ever written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was a line for a reason, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and forty-nine mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there three hundred and ninety-nine mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and nineteen mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and eighteenth morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and seventy-fourth night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and seventy-three of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and sixtieth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and seventy-fifth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and eighth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards, the back of it bare, and it has lain open along those boards with the back of it bare since before the oldest of them came to that ground. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book and the column on the page after that one holds a single name in it under a heading which still says whoever. On that form there is a second space ruled, and it is empty. Behind that lane there are thirty pages and every one of them is in the hands of the people who brought it down, and no page came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and thirty-four days. Nine people, more or less, were on that ground at ten to eight, and it has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones that afternoon for the three hundred and sixty-fourth afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two distances, and nobody has ever been up over the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it is paid and it is spent. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district and nobody in this district has read one of them aloud.

---

He was at that frame at about six and a half with the stove still in and the belt off it and lying flat on those boards with its damp end turned under, and there is a straight edge on the top rail of that frame that has been on that rail long enough to have taken the colour of the rail.

What he wanted off that morning was that day's boards turned the right way on the cart before they came off it, because a man who is not there to turn them turns nothing and says nothing about it.

---

At about seven he put his hand on that casting and it took the heat out of it, and he took his hand off and put it back about a minute later and it took it again.

There is a groove in that casting about the width of two fingers where the belt sits, and he went into it with the wire he carries and what came up out of it was nothing, and he put the wire away and put his hands in his coat pockets, one in each of them, and left them there.

---

At about eight the light came over the top of that bank and lay the whole length of that ground.

Nothing went on that frame and it turned nothing, and his heel was somewhere else all that morning, and nobody in that yard came over to it, and nobody has ever put one question to him about any part of it.

The man of about sixty-four came down to the foot of that low wall at about half past eight and stayed at it, and nobody said one word to him then or at any other hour of that day, and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that they have not said what that wall is for.

---

That light goes the length of that ground and up that slope to a man on the top of it, and nobody in this district has ever been that man.

At ten to eight the boy said thirty at those boards and said no figure after it out loud.

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight was out at his four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand until about four, and not one person in that yard said anything to him in that whole time.

The woman who keeps a scale had her own pan up about four feet off the near end of that second table from about half past twelve with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that morning.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for that, and about four voices in that yard took it up behind him out of step with one another, and nobody in that yard put one word out loud about any of it.

---

At about ten he got as far as the front of the one sentence he carries, and he has never once been past the front of it, and nobody in that yard has ever stood close enough to him to know that he gets that far.

Then he let it go back where it always goes back to, and put his hand on that frame again with nothing turning on it.

---

At about two he said one thing out loud at the near end of those boards, where the man working that stack would get it and nobody else in that yard would.

"A board that has stood out in this air wants turning before it goes on a stack, and a man who puts one down face up in that sun is a man who will hear about it at the second bend."

Nobody asked him anything about that.

Nobody asked him whether it was going up, or who was going to turn them, or what a man was to do about a board he had not turned himself, and nobody asked him why that frame at the near end of that first table was not turning on a morning like any other.

---

At about two that cart came through the gap and he was at it before the near wheel had finished dropping into that place at the second bend, and he turned that day's boards over where they lay, one after another, and there were about nine of them and he did all nine, and he did not say a word to anybody while he was doing it.

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the three hundred and eighth time running for it.

The clerk read that morning out loud at about four with the pen in the holder and then wrote the four hundred and nineteenth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it, and the far end of that has never in this basin been given to anybody.

---

About half past four the light came off those boards and that day's stack came down off that cart with their faces to the ground and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up with them.

He stood at that frame and watched the last of them go up, and nothing was turning on it, and he had not put his heel on it once in that whole day.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it, it took no line onto it, and it carried no sixty-seventh line. Nothing came down that lane and it is at thirty pages behind it as it has stood for that whole run. Those three came through this district and nobody in it has ever heard one of them read. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and nothing in this basin is set to take it. That gatepost sheet will still be carrying four hundred and eleven in the morning and the morning after that.

He said one thing out loud at about two about boards and not about that frame, and he had one thing to say that morning, and being left alone is not the same thing as having nothing to say.
