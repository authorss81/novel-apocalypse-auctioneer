# Chapter 1111: The Three Hundred And Ninety-Fifth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And A Foot Of Sound Pallet Lying In The Open On His Own Table

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and it has been sitting on that nail through every morning this basin has had since. That figure is eight hundred and fifty days old.

That board carries one thousand and nine marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and twenty-five days and nothing has come off it. Nobody has entered anything at that rail for one thousand and thirty-nine days. One thousand days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, it was not run, and it is not a lot this morning. Seven hundred and fourteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and nineteen days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Seven hundred and eighty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and twenty-eight days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and ninety-two days back. Six hundred and sixty-eight marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and fifty-four marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months a clerk has counted in this district and there is nothing in it that figure sits on. Not one of those four documents has ever been brought inside the edge of this district, and none of them is anywhere inside it now. Three of those lines are readable in four places in this basin and not in a fifth. Four conditions are set out here and none of them carries an end. Fourteen things is what this basin has made. Five of those fourteen never arrived, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

Nothing at all went onto that page this morning, and the number of lines on it this morning is sixty-five. There is no first asking on it and there is none on any other paper here, and nobody in this district is able to produce one. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and that page was not able to take a line for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning came, so that nothing on it earlier than that was ever a line for one. It is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-five mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and seventy-five mornings, and the man who fitted that rail is not to be thanked. A man has stood at that foot of that bank three hundred and ninety-five mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and ninety-fourth morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and fiftieth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and forty-nine of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and thirty-sixth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and fifty-first morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and eighty-fourth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with nothing at the back of it, as it has always been. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book and the column on the page after that holds one name under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form and it is standing empty. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in the hands of the people who brought them, and not one of them came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and ten days. About nine people were stood on that ground at ten to eight, and it has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and fortieth afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Neither of those two has ever been handed to anybody in this district and nobody here has ever gone up to either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight, and it has been paid and spent, and nothing of it is left in this basin. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three have come through this district, and nobody in it has ever heard one of them read.

---

He came to that wall at about half past six because the light gets along the bottom of it before it gets anywhere else, and he wanted the near four feet of that stone to have lost the cold out of it before he put his hand on it.

What he wanted off that morning was to be along the whole length of that wall and back at the near end of it with the sun still off the top of that bank.

---

There is a dry growth at the foot of that wall where the water coming down off the bank above it has run for years, and it has been at that foot of that wall as long as anybody can remember being at that foot of it.

He has a blade for it and he keeps the blade blunt on purpose. A sharp blade finds the stone under a root and takes a bite out of it, and a bite out of the stone at the foot of that wall cannot be put back, and he would rather the growth came back in the spring than have a mark on that stone that was his.

It comes back in the spring. It has come back every spring.

---

He worked along from the near end with the blade in his right hand and his left hand flat on the top of the stone above it, and he did not hurry any part of it, and the frost came off the top of the stone under his palm by degrees.

Nobody has ever asked him what that growth is for.

Nobody has ever asked him anything.

---

That yard is in view from where he was, the whole round of it, from the near end of the first table to the place where the boards go up the bank. About four people at the top of that bank have said since that they have not said what that wall is for, and none of them said it this morning either.

On the boards at the far end of the man of about thirty-four's own table there was a foot and about four inches of sound pallet lying in the open, square cut end pointing at the near edge of that table, where anybody walking past could see it.

The dust had begun to settle on the square cut end of it the way it does on everything else on that ground that is left lying.

---

There is a thousand days on this morning, and the morning it stands on is the three hundred and ninety-fifth. Nobody said that figure out loud in that yard and nobody looked up at it, and nothing else about that morning was any different because of it.

There was another thing on that ground this morning that nobody in that yard looked at either, and that one is not written down anywhere at all. It is lying in the open at the far end of that table with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of it.

He looked at both of them from where he was, and neither of them was looking back.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six came along that wall from the near end with his hand already up and took the four figures off the top of it, one after another, and the man of sixty-four went on with the blade about eight feet further along and let him have it.

He had been at that wall at that hour for as long as he had been at that wall, and the two of them had never once said good morning.

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight came down that slope and went out to about four feet off the near end of that first table and worked his way along it with a cloth in his hand and said nothing out loud to anybody.

The turner was at that frame with nothing turning on it, and his heel was off it, and nobody in that yard had come over and started it. He said nothing out loud this morning.

---

At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale set up about four feet off the near end of that second table with her basket down beside her and did not say a figure out loud.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight put his number into about four hundred yards of air and about four voices in that yard took it up after him out of step with one another.

It did not reach that wall. It has never reached that wall.

He has never made out a figure said at that hour in that yard and he is sixty-four and he is not going to start.

---

At about four the hand went up on that wall again and the same four figures came off the top of it, and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and eighty-fourth time running, and nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about any of it.

At about four the clerk came down that row of boards with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud before her hand went near any part of it, and then she wrote the three hundred and ninety-fifth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and took no line for it.

---

The light left those boards at about half past four, and that day's boards came down off that cart with their own faces to the ground and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them.

He was still at that wall when the last of them had gone up the slope.

---

The covers came over that page at sixty-five lines and it did not take a line and it did not carry a sixty-sixth. That lane brought nothing down it today, and the thirty behind it this morning are the thirty that were behind it yesterday. Three of those first thirty-three lines have come through this district and nobody in it has heard one of them read. That fifth of that five is five and nobody in this district has paid it. Seven months has been counted here and nothing in this basin will hold it. That gatepost sheet will be carrying the same four hundred and eleven on it in the morning.

He got along that wall from the near end to the far end and back with the sun still off the top of that bank, and nobody in that yard asked him one word about it.
