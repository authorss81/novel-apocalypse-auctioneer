# Chapter 1126: The Four Hundred And Tenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And The Two Of Them At The Far Edge Of That First Table From About A Quarter To Ten

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and no person in this basin has ever done one single thing with that figure. It is eight hundred and sixty-five days old now.

That board carries one thousand and twenty-four marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and forty days and no wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and fifty-four days. One thousand and fifteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was not run, so it is neither a lot nor open this morning on this ground. Seven hundred and twenty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book that yard stands at. Seven hundred and thirty-four days is how long that rule said out loud on this ground has stood. Eight hundred and four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and forty-three days past a printing made somewhere else.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and seven days back. Six hundred and eighty-three marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and sixty-nine marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months is counted in this district and no thing in this basin will hold that. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines stand in four places in this basin and there is no fifth place in it with any of them on it. Four conditions stand set out in this district and not one of them has an end standing after it. Fourteen is what this basin has made and there is not one thing outside those fourteen in it. Five of those fourteen have never come in here and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is at sixty-six lines and it took no line this morning. No first asking is on that page and none is on anything else in this district. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and no line written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and forty mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and ninety mornings, and the man who fitted that rail is not to be thanked. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and ten mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and ninth morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and sixty-fifth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and sixty-four of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and fifty-first of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and sixty-sixth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and ninety-ninth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with nothing on the back of it, and it has lain open along those boards like that since before anybody in this district can remember. Five lines are ruled across one of the pages of that book and the column on the page after that one carries one name under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form and it stands empty in front of everybody in that yard this morning. There are thirty pages behind that lane in the hands of the people who brought them, and not one of them came down it today. That record has stood three hundred and twenty-five days. About nine people were standing on that ground at ten to eight and it has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and fifty-fifth afternoon running and one word was all there was in that afternoon. Neither of those two distances has ever been given to anybody in this district, and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it has been paid and it is spent, and none of it is left anywhere in this basin. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district, and not one of them has ever been read out loud in this district.

---

He came down that slope at about half past seven with his own top under his arm and had it across the two trestles before the light was off the top of that bank.

What he wanted off that morning was his own top squared, the cloth along it, and the far edge of that first table with him on it for a couple of hours with the man who works that end.

---

The far end of that first table is the end that gets used. The near end stands against the frame and the man of thirty-eight works four feet off it with his own length of board.

He got a wedge under the far foot of the near trestle at about ten and knocked it home with the heel of his hand, and then he went along the top with the cloth from the far end back toward the middle.

---

The man of about thirty-four came along with his own cloth and stood at that far end and put his hand flat on the top of it.

"That far edge has a dip in it about the length of your hand," he said, "and it is not that the frame is bent."

"It is the foot of that trestle gone down into its own bed."

"You packed that bed level in the spring with the toe of your boot."

"I packed it level. That is not the same as it staying level."

"Put a shingle under that foot and it will hold until the first rain."

"It will hold until somebody picks it up again. Everything on this ground holds until somebody picks it up."

---

"Get your cloth across the grain on that top," the man of about thirty-four said. "You will raise the dust off it and then you will be cleaning the same board twice before the light goes."

"I have had a piece of felt on that end of that table in my bag since the spring and you know I have."

"You have not had it on. You have had it in your bag."

---

At about ten the boy said thirty from those boards and said nothing else after it, and the lane behind that number stayed as it is.

At about half past ten the cart came through the gap at the second bend with the near wheel dropping into the same place it always drops into, and about four people at the boards got the near end of that stack.

"There is a place in the ground at that bend where that wheel goes down about the width of a finger," said the man of about thirty-four. "Take the near end on that side and it will come up that slope."

"I have taken the near end on that side every morning of that run."

"And it has not come up crooked once."

---

That light lies on about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and nobody in this district has ever been up over the far end of that.

---

At about half past eleven they came off that far end together, and every word either of them had said in that time belonged to the ordinary business of that ground.

---

He was at about four feet off the near end of that first table from about half past nine with his cloth in his hand and nobody went near him at all that morning.

She was at her own pan about four feet off the near end of that second table from about half past twelve with her basket down beside her and the cloth knotted over her scale, and she said nothing out loud that morning, and nobody in that yard said one word to her about it.

At half past one the man of about forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air and about four voices in that yard had it off him out of step with one another, and nobody at those tables stopped for any part of it.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table all morning with nothing turning on it, and the turner had his hands on the cold iron of it and his heel somewhere else entirely, and nobody came over and put anything on it.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and ninety-ninth time running, and nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about any of it.

At about four the clerk came down those boards, said that morning out loud with the pen in the holder, and wrote the four hundred and tenth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand. She took no line for it, and she has never once changed what she does on a morning when a figure in it is round.

About half past four and the light came off those boards and that day's stack came off the cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders and about nineteen people went up with it.

He was the last man off that ground, and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that they have not said what a table in that yard is for, and not one of them said it this morning.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it, took no line onto it and did not carry a sixty-seventh. Nothing came down that lane and it is at thirty pages behind it as always. Those three came through this district and no voice in this district has read one of them out loud. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and no thing in this basin will ever hold it. That gatepost sheet will have the same four hundred and eleven on it in the morning.

The two of them worked the far edge of that first table from about a quarter to ten until about half past eleven and talked about the ordinary business of that ground, and every word of that talking belonged to that ground.
