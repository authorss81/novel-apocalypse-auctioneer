# Chapter 1128: The Four Hundred And Twelfth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And His Own Top Across The Two Trestles And A Stack Of Boards At The Near End Of It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and not one person in this basin has ever done a single thing with that figure. That figure is eight hundred and sixty-seven days old.

That board carries one thousand and twenty-six marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and forty-two days and no wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and fifty-six days. One thousand and seventeen days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and it was never run, and it is not a lot and not open this morning. Seven hundred and thirty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard is behind. Seven hundred and thirty-six days is how long that rule said out loud in this district has stood. Eight hundred and six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and forty-five days past a printing this basin never asked for.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and nine days back. Six hundred and eighty-five marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and seventy-one marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months is what a clerk has counted in this district, and there is nothing in this basin that will hold that count down. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places here and in no fifth place in this basin. Four conditions are set out in this district and no end has been put on any of them. Fourteen is what this basin has made and not one thing outside those fourteen is in it. Five of those fourteen have never come here at all and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page took nothing onto it this morning and it is at sixty-six lines. There is no first asking on that page and there is none on any other paper here. One reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing that was written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and forty-two mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and ninety-two mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and twelve mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and eleventh morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and sixty-seventh night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and sixty-six of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and fifty-third of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and sixty-eighth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and first time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards and nothing has ever been written at the back of it. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book and the column on the page after that one carries one name under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and it has stood empty this morning and every morning before it. There are thirty pages behind that lane in their own carriers' hands, and not one of them came down it today. That record has stood three hundred and twenty-seven days. There were about nine people on that ground at ten to eight this morning and it has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and fifty-seventh afternoon running and one word was all that afternoon had in it. Those two have never been handed to anybody in this district, and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight, it has been paid and it is spent, and none of it is left anywhere in this district. Three of those first thirty-three lines came through this district and not one of them has ever been read out loud in this district.

---

He came down that slope at about twenty to seven with his own top under his arm and had it across the two trestles before the light was off the top of that bank.

What he wanted off that morning was that top squared and the cloth along it, and the near end of it got before the sun came round off that bank and put his eye out at it.

---

The middle of that top came up in one pull. He set it down flat, squared it against the frame with the back of his heel, and went along it with the cloth, and he did that four times over.

There were five lengths of his own top off those trestles by about nine, and each one of them went down flat where he had put it.

---

The boards he works from lie in a stack at the near end of his own frame with their undersides up while the lengths are off the trestles being dressed, and the light comes across that stack from the far end of the ground.

There are five boards in that stack with a row of notches cut across their undersides.

That row is the same width of a hand in from the edge on every one of the five and at the same angle and to the same depth, and the edge that made it went the length of all five in one pull.

There is nothing on the underside of any other board in that stack.

He did not look at those five while he worked that morning, and he has told nobody in this district that they are there, and he has not worked out what that row is for, and nobody in that yard has ever asked him about it.

---

The boy gave that lane count out loud from those boards at ten to eight and it was thirty, and the thirty did not move, and nothing came down that lane in the whole of that day.

At about four feet off the near end of that first table the man of thirty-eight was stood all that morning with a board and a plane in his hands, and those four feet were his and nobody in that yard said one word to him there.

At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale was out at her own pan with the basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that morning, and nobody in that yard put one question to her about any of it.

About half past one, and that man's number went out into about four hundred yards of air, and about four voices in that yard carried it after him, none of them in step with any other.

The frame at the near end of that first table had nothing turning on it and the turner was at it, and he had not put his heel on it, and nobody in that yard came over and started it.

---

There is about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and nobody in this district has ever been up over that wall and along that slope to see where it ends.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another, and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the three hundred and first time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him anything about any part of it.

About four, and the clerk gave that morning out loud along that row of boards and wrote the four hundred and twelfth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and no line went onto that page for it.

The light came off those boards at about half past four and that day's boards went down off the cart face down and up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went with them.

He went up with the first of them with the five lengths of his own top under his arm, and the stack with the undersides of it up was at the near end of that frame when he left it.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it and it took no line and no sixty-seventh went onto it. No page came down that lane and it stands where it stands. Those three came through this district and none of them has ever been read out in this district. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and nothing here will ever hold it. That gatepost sheet will be carrying four hundred and eleven in the morning and the morning after that.

He put his own top across the two trestles and squared it with the back of his heel and went along the top of it with his cloth, and five boards in that stack have a row of notches cut across their undersides, and he did not look at them.
