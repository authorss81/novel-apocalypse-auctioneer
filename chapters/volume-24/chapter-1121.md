# Chapter 1121: The Four Hundred And Fifth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And His Own Table Stood Out Of Level At The Near End And He Worked The Far Half Of It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nothing in this basin has ever been done with that sheet. That figure is eight hundred and sixty days old.

That board carries one thousand and nineteen marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and thirty-five days without a wagon coming off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and forty-nine days. One thousand and ten days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, it was never run, and it is neither a lot nor open this morning. Seven hundred and twenty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and twenty-nine days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Seven hundred and ninety-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and thirty-eight days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and two days back. Six hundred and seventy-eight marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and sixty-four marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and no thing in this basin has that count set on it. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places in here and there is no fifth place in this basin that has any of them on it. Four conditions are set out in this district and nothing in this district puts an end to any one of them. This basin has made fourteen things and there has never been a fifteenth made in it. Five of those fourteen never came as far as this ground and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is at sixty-six lines and it took nothing onto it at any point in that morning. There is no first asking on that page and there is none on any other paper in this district. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing that was ever written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was a line for a reason, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-five mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and eighty-five mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and five mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and fourth morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and sixtieth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and fifty-nine of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and forty-sixth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and sixty-first morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and ninety-fourth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards and the back of it is bare, as it has always been. Five lines are ruled across one of its pages and the column on the page after that one carries one name under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and nothing has ever been put in it. Behind that lane there are thirty pages and the people who brought them are holding every one of them, and not one of them came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and twenty days. About nine people were on that ground at ten to eight, and that lot has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and fiftieth afternoon running, and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been up the far end of about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight, it has been paid and it is spent, and there is not one piece of it left in this basin. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district, and no voice in this district has ever read any one of them out loud.

---

He came down that slope at about twenty past seven with the cloth over his arm, and the light was already lying along the whole length of that ground while the near end of his own table was still in the shadow off that bank.

What he wanted off that morning was the far half of that top along with the cloth before the sun came round and put his eye in at the near end of it.

---

He put the table up the way he puts it up and squared the top with the back of his heel.

The near end of that top stood a quarter of an inch low on the hollow of grit at the foot of the near trestle, and the top of it is twisted and not dipping, and both of those have been the same at about eight on every morning he has come down that slope.

He put his hand flat on the near corner and pushed on it once, the way a man pushes to find out whether a thing is loose. It is not loose and it is not square.

---

The foot and about four inches of sound pallet was lying on the boards at the far end of that table with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of it, where a man walking the length of that yard has to pass it.

Grit out of his own bin had worked into the pores of that square end overnight, and he got it off with the back of a thumbnail before he set his hand on it, and then he left it where it was.

---

He got the cloth and started at the far end.

He worked the far half of that top from the far end back toward the middle, and the cloth went along it without the board dipping anywhere under his hand, because that half of that table is over ground that has not moved since anybody can remember. The near two feet is the other matter entirely, and he does not lean on them, and he did not lean on them this morning.

---

At ten to eight the boy was at those boards with his hands in his pockets and said thirty, and that lane brought nothing down behind it.

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight came out and stopped at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand, and he was at those four feet until about four, and not one person in that yard said anything to him in any of it.

From about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had her own pan about four feet off the near end of that second table with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud this morning.

At about half past one that man of forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for that and for nothing else, and about four voices in that yard took it up behind him out of step with one another.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it, and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel off it, and nobody in that yard started it, and he said nothing out loud this morning.

---

About four, and the man of fifty-six put his hand up on that stone and took four figures off the top of it in the order he takes them in, and then he gave that stone his four words, which was the two hundred and ninety-fourth time running.

There is nobody in this district who has ever put one question to him about any part of that, and nobody has ever asked him where the four of them are kept.

At about four the clerk came down that row of boards with the pen in the holder, read that morning out loud, and then wrote the four hundred and fifth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

---

About half past four the light came off those boards and that day's boards came down off that cart with their faces to the ground and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up with them.

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it, and nobody in this basin has ever been given the far end of that either.

He was on that ground after the last of them had gone up, and that piece was lying on the boards at the far end of that table where anybody walking past could see it, and he folded the cloth over his arm and stood at the near end of his own table a while with his hand on it.

Nobody in that yard said one word about it.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it, and it took no line that morning and it did not carry a sixty-seventh. No page came down that lane and the thirty behind it are the thirty that were behind it yesterday. Those three came through this district and no voice in this district has read any of them out loud. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and nothing in this basin is built to hold it. That gatepost sheet will go on carrying four hundred and eleven tomorrow and the morning after that.

He worked the far half of his own top with the cloth and left the near two feet alone, and the near end of that table was a quarter of an inch low on that hollow when the light went, and nobody in that yard said one word about it.
