# Chapter 1112: The Three Hundred And Ninety-Sixth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And A Woman Of About Fifty-Two Came Down That Bank With A Frame And A Bag

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and nobody has ever taken it off that nail and used it for anything at all. That figure is eight hundred and fifty-one days old.

That board carries one thousand and ten marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and twenty-six days without a wagon ever coming off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and forty days. One thousand and one days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid was open four hundred and fifty days, and it was not run, and it is neither a lot nor open this morning. Seven hundred and fifteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and twenty days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Seven hundred and ninety days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and twenty-nine days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and ninety-three days back. Six hundred and sixty-nine marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and fifty-five marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing in this district it sits on. Those four documents have never once been got inside this district. Three of those lines can be read in four places in this basin, and there is no fifth place in it with any of them on it. Four conditions are set out in this district and there is no end set on any one of the four of them. Fourteen things is what this basin has made and it has made nothing else. Five of those fourteen things never got here at all, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is carrying sixty-five lines and it took nothing onto it this morning. The first asking of anybody in this district is not written on that page and it is not written on anything else either, and there is no way back to it. On the one hundred and eighty-second morning a reason was said out loud in that yard, and nothing on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-six mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and seventy-six mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank three hundred and ninety-six mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and ninety-fifth morning and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. On the seven hundred and fifty-first night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and fifty of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and thirty-seventh of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and fifty-second morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and eighty-fifth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

The back of that lot book is bare and always has been, and that book lies open along those boards this morning. Five lines are ruled across one of its pages, and on the page after that one there is a column with a single name in it under a heading that still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form and it stands empty and has stood empty all morning. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in the hands of those who brought them and nothing came down it today. That record has stood three hundred and eleven days. There were about nine people stood on that ground at ten to eight, and that lot has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and forty-first afternoon running and one word was all that afternoon came to. There is nobody in this district who has ever been handed either of those two, and nobody here has ever gone up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll of ninety-eight is paid and spent and none of it is left anywhere in this basin. Three of the first thirty-three lines on that back page came through this district, and nobody in it has ever heard one of those three read aloud.

---

He came down that slope at about a quarter to eight and had his own table up and squared by about eight, and the top of it was twisted and not dipping and the near end of it stood a quarter of an inch on grit that came out of his own bin.

What he wanted off that morning was to get the whole length of his own top along with the cloth before the man of about thirty-two came to that end of it, and to have his own table up that bank with the first load.

---

The foot and about four inches of sound pallet lay where it had lain every morning of that run, on the boards at the far end of his own table, with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of it.

He went along the top with the cloth from the far end toward the near, over sound ground the whole way, and he did not put his hand out for anything.

---

At about eleven a woman came down that bank with a frame in one hand and a bag over the other shoulder, and she came down it the way a person comes down a bank carrying a thing she does not want scratched, with the bottom edge of the frame held clear of the ground.

The frame is wooden and about the size of a door off, and there is a rebate cut round the inside of it an inch and a bit deep, and nothing has ever been in that rebate, and the corners of it are worn through where the wood has been lifted out and put back a good many times.

She put the frame down against the leg of his table at about four feet off the near end and stood it up against the leg, and she set the bag down on the ground beside it.

She asked nothing at that table.

---

Nobody at that ground has ever put one question to her and she has never put one to anybody in that yard.

She came down that bank, she brought a frame and a bag, and nobody has ever found out from her what either of them is for, and that has been the arrangement for as long as she has been doing it.

---

She took a form out of the bag and looked at it and put it back in the bag, and then a while after that she took it out again.

The form has two spaces on it. The first one has something in it. The second one is ruled and blank, and it was blank there in the sun in front of her for the best part of an hour with the light on it.

She did not put anything in it.

Nobody at those boards said one word to her about it, and nobody at that table said one word to her about it, and the man of about thirty-four worked his own top with his cloth and did not look at the form for longer than a working man looks at anything.

---

The whole shape of a paper with two spaces on it tells you what belongs in the second one, and there is nothing to decide about that. That is the whole of the difficulty of it, and it is not that she had nothing to put there.

About four people at the top of that bank have said since that they have not said what a frame with a rebate in it is the frame of, and not one of them said it this morning.

---

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight was at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand, and nobody spoke to him there.

At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan about four feet off the near end of that second table with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud, and the woman with the frame did not come over to her.

The man of about forty-eight put his number out into about four hundred yards of air at about half past one, and behind him about four voices in that yard took it up out of step with one another. Nobody in that yard put one word out loud about it.

The frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it and the turner was at it, and his heel was off it, and he said nothing out loud this morning and nobody asked him anything.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it, and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and eighty-fifth time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him a question about any part of it.

At about four the clerk came down that row of boards with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud, and then she wrote the three hundred and ninety-sixth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and took no line for it.

---

At about half past four the light came off those boards and that day's stack came down off the cart with the faces of it down and went up that bank. About nineteen people went up it with them.

He was the last of the men off that ground, and the foot and about four inches of sound pallet was on the boards at the far end of his own table with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of it, and the frame was standing against the leg of that table with nothing ever in its rebate.

---

That page stood at sixty-five lines when the covers came over it and it took no line. That lane brought nothing down it today, and it stands where it has stood, at thirty pages behind it. Those three came through this district unread and always have been. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and there is nothing here that will carry it. That gatepost sheet goes on carrying the same four hundred and eleven in the morning.

She came down that bank with a frame and a bag and asked nothing, and the second space on that form stood empty in front of her the whole of the time she was standing there.
