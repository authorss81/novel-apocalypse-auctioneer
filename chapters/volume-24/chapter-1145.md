# Chapter 1145: The Four Hundred And Twenty-Ninth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And He Got His Own Heel Under That Near Corner And Stood There With His Weight On It

That sheet at that gatepost carries four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this district has ever been made one word about it. That figure is eight hundred and eighty-four days old.

That board carries one thousand and forty-three marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and fifty-nine days, and not a wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and seventy-three days. One thousand and thirty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long that bid stood open before it was not run, and it is not a lot on this ground and it is not open here. Seven hundred and forty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard is. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and fifty-three days. Eight hundred and twenty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and sixty-two days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and twenty-six days back. Seven hundred and two marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and eighty-eight marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing here for it to be counted on. Those four documents have never come to this district. Three of those lines can be read in four places here, and there is no fifth place in this basin that has any of them on it. Four conditions are set out here, and no end has ever been written to any one of them. Fourteen things is everything this basin has made and there has never been anything made in it besides. Five of those fourteen have never been on this ground, and that fifth of the five stands at five and is unpaid.

That page took no line at any hour of that morning, and it stands at sixty-seven lines, and it took no line at any hour of the day before this one either. No first asking is on that page, and no other paper in this district ever has had one. One reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning. Nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and fifty-nine mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there four hundred and nine mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-nine mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and twenty-eighth morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and eighty-fourth night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and eighty-three of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and seventieth of those mornings, and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and eighty-fifth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and eighteenth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with the back of it bare, and it has lain open there for longer than the oldest of them has been coming to that ground. Five lines are ruled across one page of it. The column on the page after that one holds one name in it under a heading which still says whoever, and the second space ruled on that form is empty. Behind that lane there are thirty pages, and the hands they are in are the hands that brought them, and this morning brought no page down it. That record has stood three hundred and forty-four days. About nine people were on that ground at ten to eight, and it has been a public lot there since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and seventy-fourth afternoon running, and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Neither of those two distances has ever been handed to anybody in this district, and there are about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it.

The toll came to ninety-eight, and it is paid and spent and none of it is here. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district and nobody in it has ever read one of them out loud.

---

He came through that gap at about two with a load on the cart that had no business being carried slowly, and the near wheel went down into the same place it goes down at the second bend every time that wheel comes in off that lane.

What he wanted off that afternoon was that load off the cart and up that bank with the light still on those boards, and his near wheel through that bend without the whole thing stopping dead in it.

---

Everything in that yard was already where it was.

The boy had said thirty at those boards at ten to eight and had said no figure after it out loud. The man of thirty-eight was at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand and nobody had said one word to him. The woman who keeps a scale had her pan up about four feet off the near end of that second table with her basket down beside her, and she had said nothing out loud. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it, and the turner was at it with his heel off it.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air, and about four voices in that yard had it behind him out of step with one another.

---

At about half past ten the man of that table at the far end of that ground had come out from behind it with his boot and got his own heel in under the near corner of his own top. He stood there with his weight on that heel for a while.

There was a length of pale wood let into that corner, and it was there before he put his heel under it, and it was there after he took the heel out again. He did not put one hand into that corner and pull anything out of it.

He did not say one word out loud about it, and there was nobody in that yard to say one word to him about it, and nobody said one word to that man about it either.

---

At about two the turner put one thing out loud where anybody standing at that stack could get it.

"The bottom of that load wants the faces of them up going up that slope, and the ones at the back want it in an order that is not the order they are stacked in, and whoever set them has never—"

He stopped there and went back to what he had at that frame.

Nobody asked him anything about that.

---

He got his near wheel through that bend with a man on that wheel at the second one, and the load stayed where it was stacked. He did not stop at the far end of that ground, and he did not look at that corner again.

---

About half past four the light came off those boards, and those boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them.

The light lies along about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank on a morning like that one, and nobody in this district has ever been up over the far end of that.

Nobody in that yard said one word to another about any part of it.

The man of that table at the far end went up behind the last of them and came back down that slope at the end of that day, and that table was standing level behind him and there was no light under the near corner of it.

---

That page stood at sixty-seven lines when the covers came over it and it carried no sixty-eighth line on it. Nothing came down that lane on that day, and the thirty behind it this morning are the same thirty that were behind it yesterday. Those three came through this district, and nobody in it has heard one of them read out loud. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months has been counted in this district, and there is nothing in this basin built to hold it. That gatepost sheet will be carrying that same four hundred and eleven in the morning and in the morning after.

He came through that gap at about two and said nothing out loud at any hour of that day, and about nineteen people went up that bank that afternoon, and not one of them said one word about any of it.
