# Chapter 1132: The Four Hundred And Sixteenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And She Said That Morning Out Loud With The Pen Still In The Holder Before She Wrote It Down

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and no one has ever done anything with that figure or with that sheet. It is eight hundred and seventy-one days old.

That board carries one thousand and thirty marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and forty-six days without one wagon ever coming off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and sixty days. One thousand and twenty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid was open four hundred and fifty days and was not run, and no part of it is a lot here or is open here this morning. The figure on the second line of that book is seven hundred and thirty-five days ahead of this ground. That rule said out loud in this yard has stood seven hundred and forty days. It has been eight hundred and ten days since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and forty-nine days beyond a printing it was never part of.

The ninth of the nine printed nights sits nine hundred and thirteen days back from this morning. Six hundred and eighty-nine marks have been cut off that board. Along the edge of that second table there are six hundred and seventy-five marks in chalk.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing in this basin that it will sit on. Those four documents have never come to this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places in this basin and are in no fifth place. Four conditions are set out in this district, and no one has ever written an end on any of them. Fourteen is the whole of what this basin makes, and nothing else has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen never arrived here, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page carries sixty-six lines and nothing went onto it in that morning. The first asking is not on that page and is on no other paper in this district. There was a reason said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing ever written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was a line for a reason, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and forty-six mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there three hundred and ninety-six mornings, and there is nothing to thank the man who fitted it for. A man has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and sixteen mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and fifteenth morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and seventy-first night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and seventy of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and fifty-seventh of those mornings, and every one of the four was got. That is the two hundred and seventy-second morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and fifth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

The back of that lot book is bare as it has always been and that book lies open along those boards this morning. Five lines are ruled across one page of it and the column on the page after that one has one name in it under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and nothing has been put in it. There are thirty pages behind that lane in the hands of their own carriers, and not one of them came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and thirty-one days. About nine people were stood on that ground at ten to eight, and that lot has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and sixty-first afternoon running, and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two distances, and nobody here has ever gone up over the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and has been paid and spent. Three of the first thirty-three lines on that back page came through this district and not one of them has ever been read out loud in it.

---

She was at those boards in the dark with the book open against the rail and the pen still in the holder, because the pen comes out of the holder after a thing has been said and not before one.

What she wanted off that morning was to say that morning out loud in her own voice before her hand went near any part of that page, and she has wanted that every working day for fifteen years and it has never once been a question anybody put to her.

---

She has two forms of words and she had the first of them ready by the time the light came over the top of that bank.

The second of the two is for a morning when something has gone out loud at the back of that book, and she has never once had to use it in fifteen years.

This morning was the common of them, and she has had it ready since she was twenty-six.

---

At ten to eight the boy was at those boards and gave the lane count out loud before her hand had gone anywhere near any part of it.

"Thirty," he said, "and nothing behind it."

After that the stub of pencil went behind his ear and he went back down to the near end of that stack.

---

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight was at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand until about four, and nobody in that yard spoke to him in any part of that time.

The woman who keeps a scale had her own pan up about four feet off the near end of that second table from about half past twelve with her basket beside it, and she said nothing out loud this morning and no one has ever put a question to her about what she weighs things for.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had that number of his out into about four hundred yards of air, and four voices in that yard had it behind him out of step with one another, and no one in that yard put one word out loud about any of it.

The frame at the near end of that first table stood with nothing turning on it all day and the turner stood at it from about six and a half, and his heel was nowhere near that casting at any hour of it, and nobody in that yard started it, and nobody put one question to him about it.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall the whole of that morning and he was asked nothing at any point of it.

---

At about four the hand went up on that stone and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that wall after them, and it was the three hundred and fifth time running for it.

She had that book's own figures in her hand before she said anything, and she had said it to herself twice on the way down that row of boards, because fifteen years is long enough for a voice to go quiet on a slope.

---

At about four she read that morning out loud at the near end of those boards with the pen still in the holder.

"The four hundred and sixteenth morning," she said. "There has been nothing out loud at the back of that book, so that page has taken no line and I am on the common of my two forms of words. The boy gave that lane count out loud at ten to eight and there was nothing behind it. There is nothing else and there will be nothing else."

Then she took the pen out of the holder and put the four hundred and sixteenth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

What she wrote went onto that page over the thin place in the paper about four inches in from the foot of it, where the ink has never taken properly in fifteen years, and it sat a hair lower than the lines above it, and there was a gap in the middle of it about the width of a hair.

She has never once made an allowance for that band and she did not make one this morning.

---

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it and nobody has ever been given that far end of it in this basin.

About half past four the light went off those boards and that day's stack came down off the cart with its faces to the ground and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up with it.

She went up behind the last of that day's boards with that book shut under her arm, and about four people were standing about at the top of that bank, and not one of them said anything to her about that morning.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it and it took nothing onto it and it did not carry a sixty-seventh line. Nothing came down that lane and it is at thirty pages behind it as it has stood for the whole of that run. Those three came through this district and nobody in this district has ever heard one of them read. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and no thing in this basin will take it. That gatepost sheet will go on carrying four hundred and eleven tomorrow and in the morning after that.

She said that morning out loud before she wrote it, and she has said every morning out loud before she wrote it for fifteen years, and the gap in the middle of the number on that page is where the paper has gone thin and she has never once corrected for it.
