# Chapter 1123: The Four Hundred And Seventh Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And A Number Went Out Into The Air Over That Ground At About Half Past One

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this basin has ever taken that figure off that sheet and done one thing with it. That figure is eight hundred and sixty-two days old.

That board carries one thousand and twenty-one marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and thirty-seven days and no wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and fifty-one days. One thousand and twelve days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, it was not run, and it is not a lot and it is not open this morning. Seven hundred and twenty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands at. Seven hundred and thirty-one days is how long that rule said out loud on that ground has stood. Eight hundred and one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and forty days past printing it never made.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and four days back. Six hundred and eighty marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and sixty-six marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and it is on nothing at all, not on a wall and not on a page. Those four documents have never once been in this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places in this basin and not in a fifth place in it. Four conditions stand set out in this district and nothing is set at the end of any one of them. Fourteen is the whole of what this basin has made and nothing outside those fourteen has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen never came inside this district at all and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

Nothing went onto that page this morning and it is at sixty-six lines. No first asking is on that page and none is on any other paper in this district either. A reason went out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-seven mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and eighty-seven mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and seven mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and sixth morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and sixty-second night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and sixty-one of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and forty-eighth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and sixty-third morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and ninety-sixth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

The back of that lot book has nothing on it and never has had, and that book lies open along those boards this morning. Five lines are ruled across one of its pages and the column on the page after that one holds a single name in it under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and it stands empty this morning as it stands every morning. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in the hands of their own carriers and nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and twenty-two days. There were about nine people on that ground at ten to eight and that lot has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and fifty-second afternoon running and one word was all there was in that afternoon. Neither of those two has ever been given to anybody in this district, and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it is paid and spent and nothing of it is left in this basin. Three of the first thirty-three lines on that back page came through this district, and not one of them has ever been read out loud in this district.

---

He came in at about eight with the little book in the inside pocket of his coat and got the morning's ground onto that paper the way he has got it onto that paper for a long time.

What he wanted off that morning was that number to go out at half past one and be taken up behind him the way it is always taken up, and the day's entry shut before the light went off those boards.

---

He writes what the ground is and not what it means.

Two men at a table at the far end of it, one table at the near end with a frame on it and nothing on the frame, a woman with a pan four feet off the near end of the second one, a man at the boards with a book open against the rail, a man thirty-eight at about four feet who works and is worked beside, and a boy who comes down the slope and goes back up it.

That is the ground. It goes onto that paper the way it goes onto that paper every morning and nobody has ever asked him to read any part of it out.

---

The boy said thirty at ten to eight off those boards and said nothing after it, and no page came down that lane behind that number.

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight was out at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand until about four, and nobody said anything to him there.

From about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had four things on that pan before the hour of half past one, and she said no figure out loud that day.

---

At about half past one he put that number of his out into about four hundred yards of air.

He said it in the voice he keeps for it, which is neither a shout nor a low voice and is not the voice he uses for anything else there is. Then about four voices in that yard had it behind him, out of step with one another, the way they always have it, and it went off the face of that bank.

He stood there and let them have it.

Nobody in that yard put one word out loud about it.

---

At about two the turner said one thing out loud at the near end of that first table, where anybody standing at those boards could get it.

"There is a place in the ground at the second bend where the near wheel drops about the width of a finger, and the man on the bottom of that stack puts his weight on the other rail when he comes over it, and if he does not put it on the other rail then the boards go down on his foot and not on the ground."

Nobody put one question to him about any part of that, and nobody in this district has ever asked him one thing.

---

It is about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and nobody in this district has ever been up over the far end of that.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another, and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and ninety-sixth time running.

Nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about it and nobody ever will.

At about four the clerk came down those boards with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud before her hand had been near any part of it, and then she wrote the four hundred and seventh morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and took nothing onto that page for it.

---

At about half past four the light came off those boards, and that day's boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them.

He shut the little book, put it back into the inside pocket of his coat, and went up that slope behind the last of them with the book shut against his chest.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it, it took no line onto it and it did not carry a sixty-seventh. No page came down that lane today and it is at thirty pages behind it. Those three came through this district and not one of them has been read out in it. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. That count of seven months is on nothing and it has been on nothing from the first morning it was made. Four hundred and eleven will be on that gatepost sheet in the morning, and it is on it now.

He put his number out at half past one into about four hundred yards of air and about four voices took it up behind him out of step with one another, and nobody in that yard said one word out loud about it.
