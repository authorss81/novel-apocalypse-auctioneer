# Chapter 1118: The Four Hundred And Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And The Whole Of That Stack Went Up That Bank While The Light Was Still On The Eleven Feet

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this basin has ever done one thing with it. That figure is eight hundred and fifty-seven days old.

That board carries one thousand and sixteen marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and thirty-two days and no wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and forty-six days. One thousand and seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

The bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, was never run, and is not a lot on this ground. Seven hundred and twenty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and twenty-six days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Seven hundred and ninety-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and thirty-five days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and ninety-nine days back. Six hundred and seventy-five marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and sixty-one marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing in this basin that will hold that count. None of those four documents has ever been inside this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places here and there is no fifth place in it with any of them on. Four conditions are set out in this district and no end has been put on any one of them. Fourteen things is what this basin has made and nothing else has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen never got inside this district, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

No line went onto that page this morning and it stands at sixty-six lines. There is no first asking on that page and there is none on anything else in this district either, and nobody in this district is able to produce one. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, and it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-two mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and eighty-two mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted that rail. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and two mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and first morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. On the seven hundred and fifty-seventh night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and fifty-six of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and forty-third of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and fifty-eighth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and ninety-first time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

Nothing has ever been written at the back of that lot book and it lies open along those boards with the back of it bare. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book and the column on the page after that one carries a name under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form and it stands empty in front of everybody in that yard. There are thirty pages behind that lane in the hands of the people who brought them, and not one of them came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and seventeen days. There are about nine people on that ground at ten to eight, and that lot has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and forty-seventh afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. There is nobody in this district who has ever been given either of those two, and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight, it has been paid and it is spent, and none of it is left in this basin. Three of the first thirty-three lines on that back page came through this district, and no voice in this district has ever read one of those three aloud.

---

He was at those boards with his hands in his pockets at about ten to eight, which is when he has said that number every morning he has stood at that end of that stack.

What he wanted off that morning was for that number to be said before the clerk's hand had gone anywhere, and for the stack to go up that bank while the light was still on the ground.

---

"Thirty," said Orren Pike. "And there is nothing behind that this morning and I am not saying anything else after it."

He said that out loud as well, which he does.

There is a figure he has found and nobody in this district has ever asked him for it and nobody in this district has ever looked at it to see whether it is right, and this morning he did not say it out loud, and he said out loud that he was not saying it out loud.

Then he put the stub of pencil behind his ear and went back down to the near end of that stack.

---

The cart came through the gap at about two and the near wheel went into the same place at the second bend on the way in, the way it goes.

He was at the near end of that stack from about half past two, and that is where he stands when the boards come off, and the light was still lying along the whole of that ground and up the eleven feet of that slope.

---

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight came down that slope and stopped at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand, and he was still at about those four feet at about four, and nobody said one word to him there.

From about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan about four feet off the near end of that second table, with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that day.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for it, and about four voices in that yard took it up after him out of step with one another.

Nothing was turning on that frame at the near end of that first table, and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel off it, and nobody in that yard started it, and he said nothing out loud this morning.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and ninety-first time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him anything about any part of it.

The clerk came down that row of boards at about four with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud, and then she wrote the four hundred and second morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and she took no line for it.

---

At about half past four that day's boards came off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and the light was still on the eleven feet of that slope and every one of those boards went up while it was.

About nineteen people went up that bank with them.

He went up with the near end of that stack, on the boards, the way he goes up.

---

The foot and about four inches of sound pallet was lying on the boards at the far end of the man of about thirty-four's table when he went past it, square cut end pointing at the near edge of that table, and he did not stop for it and nobody has ever asked him about it.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it, and it took no line and it did not carry a sixty-seventh. Nothing came down that lane today and it stands at thirty pages behind it. Three of those first thirty-three lines have come through this district and not one of them has been read out in it. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months has been counted here and there is nothing in this basin that will hold it. That gatepost sheet will have four hundred and eleven on it in the morning.

He said thirty at those boards before the clerk's hand had moved and he said no figure after it, and the whole of that stack went up that bank while the light was still on the eleven feet.
