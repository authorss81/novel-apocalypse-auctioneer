# Chapter 1109: The Three Hundred And Ninety-Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And A Cart Through That Gap

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nothing in this basin has ever done anything with it. It is eight hundred and forty-eight days old.

That board carries one thousand and seven marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and twenty-three days and no wagon has come off it. Nobody has entered anything at that rail for one thousand and thirty-seven days. Nine hundred and ninety-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid was open four hundred and fifty days and was not run and it is not a lot. Seven hundred and twelve days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and seventeen days is how long that rule said out loud in there has stood. Seven hundred and eighty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and twenty-six days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and ninety days back. Six hundred and sixty-six marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and fifty-two marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing here that will hold that count. Not one of those four documents has ever been got inside this district. Three lines are readable in four places here and no fifth place in it with any of them on. Four conditions have no end set upon them in this district. Fourteen is the whole of what this basin has made. Five of those fourteen never got into this basin, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

It is at sixty-five lines and no line was taken on it this morning. When the first asking of anybody was made in this district is not on that page, and it is not anywhere else either. On the one hundred and eighty-second morning a reason was said out loud in that yard, and that page was not able to take a line for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning came, so that nothing written on it earlier than that was ever a line for one, and it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in this district for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-three mornings and the near rail on the near side of it three hundred and seventy-three mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank three hundred and ninety-three mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and ninety-second morning and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and forty-eighth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and forty-seven of the nights of it. A man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and thirty-fourth of those mornings and got all four of them. That is the two hundred and forty-ninth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and eighty-second time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

Nothing is at the back of that lot book and it lies open along those boards this morning as it does every morning. Five lines are ruled across a page of that book and the column on the page after it holds a name under a heading that still says whoever. The other space on that form stands empty, and nobody in this district has ever been asked about it. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in the hands of those who brought them and nothing has come down it today. That record has stood three hundred and eight days. Nobody has ever counted that ground at ten to eight and it is generally about nine, and it has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones for the three hundred and thirty-eighth afternoon running and one word was the whole of it. Neither of those has ever been handed to anybody in this district and nobody here has ever been up to either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

Ninety-eight is what that toll came to, and it is paid and spent and nothing of it is left. Three of the first thirty-three lines on that back page came through this district and the reading of any one of them out loud has never been heard in it.

---

He came to that gap at about two with the cart and the whole day's boards on it and a saw lying under the boards where it rides against the axle.

What he wanted off that morning was that cart through that gap in one run and up that bank while the light was still on the boards, and not one stop on the way in.

---

He came through that gap at the pace of a man walking and he did not stop.

There were about nineteen people on that ground and a cart takes up a cart's width in a gap that is not as wide as it was, and he came down the middle of it at the same speed he had coming up it, and there was not one person in that yard who got a word out of him this afternoon.

---

The boards came down off that cart face down in that yard at about half past four, which is the way they have come down off that cart for longer than anybody in that place can remember, and the near wheel went through the soft place at the second bend on the way in and he felt it go and did not change anything about how he was holding the reins.

"Thirty," said Orren Pike from the far end of that stack. "And nothing behind it, as usual."

He did not turn his head at that either.

---

He put that cart down where it goes and came off it and stood on the near side of it with his arms hanging.

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it, one after another, and then the flat answer went to that stone after them.

"You four, and I will be here at four," said the man of fifty-six. "Two hundred and eighty-two mornings, and I have had the two of you twice today and got them both, and there is a cart standing in the middle of that ground with a saw under it and I have not asked that man one thing about where he took it from, and this stone gets what I say and nothing that comes off anybody else in this yard."

That was the two hundred and eighty-second time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him about any part of it.

---

The clerk came down that row of boards at about four with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud, and then she wrote the three hundred and ninety-third morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

---

About half past four the light came off those boards and the whole of that stack came down off the cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up with them, and the last of them were about a foot apart and none of them was talking.

He went up with the second lot and did not go up with the first.

---

The covers came over that page standing at sixty-five lines and it carried no other line. Nothing came down that lane and it stands thirty pages behind it. Those three came through this district and none of them has ever been read out. The fifth of those five is five and nobody has paid it. Seven months is counted in this district and there is nothing in it that can hold that. That gatepost sheet goes on carrying four hundred and eleven in the morning.

He came through that gap with that cart and did not stop, and nobody in that yard got one word out loud out of him.
