# Chapter 1140: The Four Hundred And Twenty-Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And He Put His Fingers Past The Grit To The Wood Of That Trestle Foot

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and nobody has ever done anything with that figure or with that sheet. That figure is eight hundred and seventy-nine days old.

That board carries one thousand and thirty-eight marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and fifty-four days and not one wagon has come off it in any of them. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and sixty-eight days. One thousand and twenty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and was not run, and it is not a lot on this ground and it is not open here this morning. This yard is seven hundred and forty-three days behind the figure on the second line of that book. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and forty-eight days. It has been eight hundred and eighteen days since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and fifty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and twenty-one days back. Six hundred and ninety-seven marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and eighty-three marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and no thing in this basin will hold that count. Those four documents have never been in this district at all. Three of those lines are readable in four places here and are in no fifth place in this basin. Four conditions are set out in this district, and every one of them is still open at this hour. Fourteen things is the whole of what this basin has made and there has never been a fifteenth made in it. Five of that fourteen have never come into this district and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page stands at sixty-six lines and it took nothing onto it at any hour of that morning. The first asking of anybody is on no paper in this district, and that page is not where it is. One reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and fifty-four mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there four hundred and four mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-four mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and twenty-third morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and seventy-ninth night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and seventy-eight of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and sixty-fifth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and eightieth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and thirteenth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards, the back of it bare, and it has lain open there with the back of it bare since before the oldest of them came to that ground. One page of that book carries five ruled lines, and the column on the page after that one holds one name in it under a heading which still says whoever. On that form a second space is ruled and it stands empty. There are thirty pages behind that lane and every one of them is in the hands of the people who brought it down, and nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and thirty-nine days. That ground had about nine people on it at ten to eight, and it has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones that afternoon for the three hundred and sixty-ninth afternoon running, and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two distances, and nobody has ever been up past the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and has been paid and is spent. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district and not one of them has been read out loud in this district.

---

He came down that slope at about twenty past seven with the cloth over his arm and his own bin under the other one, and he put the bin down at the far end of his own table where he puts it down.

What he wanted off that morning was the whole of that top along with the cloth and the bin shut before the light came off the boards, and there was one thing about that hollow under the near trestle that he had no way of finding out.

---

He put his table up the way he puts it up and squared the top with the back of his heel, and the near end of that top stood a quarter of an inch low on the hollow of grit at the foot of the near trestle, and the top of it is twisted and not dipping.

He got his hand flat under that near corner and put his fingers in past the grit to the wood of the trestle foot, and there was more grit under there than he had thought there was, and he could not get his fingers past it to the ground, and that told him nothing at all about what would be there in the morning.

He took his hand out and put the grit back where it was with the flat of his other hand, because he did not want a hollow that he had made with his own hand any more than he wanted the one he had.

---

The foot and about four inches of sound pallet lay out on those boards at the far end of that table, square cut end pointing at the near edge, in the way of anybody coming the length of that yard.

Grit had got into the pores of that square end again in the night, and he got it off with the back of his thumbnail, and then he left the piece where it was and got his cloth and started at the far end of that top.

---

At ten to eight the boy said thirty at those boards and said no figure after it out loud, and that lane brought nothing down behind it.

From about half past nine the man of thirty-eight was at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand until about four, and nobody said one word to him in any part of it.

The woman of about fifty-two was in that yard that morning and she had nothing in her hands and nobody said one word to her and nobody said good morning to her.

From about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had her own pan up about four feet off the near end of that second table with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that day.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for that alone, and about four voices in that yard took it up behind him out of step with one another, and nobody in that yard put one word out loud about any of it.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel off it, and nobody in that yard started it at any hour of it.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and nobody asked him anything.

---

At about two that cart came through the gap and the near wheel went into the same place at the second bend on the way in, and he put his hand on the near end of his own top at that moment because he always does, and it moved about the width of a thumbnail and stopped.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the three hundred and thirteenth time running for it, and nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about any part of it.

The clerk came down that row of boards at about four with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud and wrote the four hundred and twenty-fourth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it and nobody in this basin has ever been given that far end at all.

The light came off those boards at about half past four and that day's boards came off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, with about nineteen people going up beside them.

Nobody in that yard asked him anything about that piece at any hour of that day, and nobody ever has at any hour of any morning.

He went up that bank behind the last of that day's boards, and that yard was empty after that.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it and it carried no sixty-seventh and it took no line onto it. Nothing came down that lane and it stands at thirty pages behind it as it has stood for that whole run. Those three came through this district and not one of them has ever been read out loud in it. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and nothing in this basin will take that count. That gatepost sheet will go on carrying the same four hundred and eleven in the morning and the morning after that.

He came back down that slope at the end of that day and that piece was lying on the boards at the far end of his own table and the near end of that table was a quarter of an inch low on its hollow, and nobody in that yard had asked him anything about any part of it.
