# Chapter 1149: The Four Hundred And Thirty-Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And He Went Along The Near End Of That First Table With The Cloth And Nobody Put One Word To Him

There is four hundred and eleven on the sheet at that gatepost and nobody in this basin has ever done anything with it. That figure is eight hundred and eighty-eight days old.

That board carries one thousand and forty-seven marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and sixty-three days, and no wagon has come off it in any of them. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and seventy-seven days. One thousand and thirty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, was not run, and it is not a lot here and is not an opening here this morning. Seven hundred and fifty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and fifty-seven days. Eight hundred and twenty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and sixty-six days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and thirty days back. Seven hundred and six marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and ninety-two marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing at all here for it to sit on. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places here, and there is no fifth place in this basin that has any of them on it. Four conditions are set out in this district, and this district has never written an end to one of them. Fourteen things is the whole of what this basin has made and no fifteenth of anything has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen have never come as far as this district and the fifth of them is unpaid.

Nothing went onto that page at the back of that lot book at any hour of that morning or of the morning before it, and it stands at sixty-seven lines. That page has never held a first asking, and no other paper in this district has either. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and sixty-three mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there four hundred and thirteen mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-three mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and thirty-second morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and eighty-eighth night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and eighty-seven of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and seventy-fourth of those mornings, and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and eighty-ninth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and twenty-second time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with the back of it bare, and it has lain open there with the back of it bare for longer than that yard has had a rail in it. Five lines are ruled across one page of it, and the column on the page after that one carries a single name in it under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form, and it stands empty in front of everybody in that yard. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in thirty different pairs of hands, and not one of them came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and forty-eight days. About nine people were on that ground at ten to eight. That ground has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and seventy-eighth afternoon running, and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been handed either of those two distances and nobody here has ever been up over the far end of either of them, and there are about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it is spent and it has been paid. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district and nobody has read one of them out loud in it.

---

He came down that slope at about half past nine with his cloth in his hand and went out to about four feet off the near end of that first table. Those are the four feet where a man can work without being spoken to, and that is the whole of what those four feet are for.

What he wanted off that day was the near end of that top along with the cloth, and nobody at all.

---

He worked at the near end of that first table with the cloth and with the back of his thumbnail on the arris of a board that had come up from that stack in the morning. The light came along the ground at the foot of that wall and then went back up the face of that bank, and he was still at about those four feet when it did.

Nobody in that yard put one word to him at any hour of that day, and nobody has ever asked him anything at any hour of any morning, and nothing at the top of that bank has ever been offered to him by anybody.

---

At ten to eight the boy had said thirty at those boards and had said no figure after it out loud.

From about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had her own pan up at the near end of that second table, with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that day.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for that and nothing else, and about four voices in that yard had it behind him out of step with one another, and he stood in the middle of that and let them have it.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it, and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel nowhere near it. The turner said nothing out loud at any hour of that day.

Nothing was put to the man of about sixty-four at the foot of that low wall.

---

At the far end of that first table the man of about thirty-four had his own bin shut and his own top along it. That corner has a length of pale wood let into it, and that top is not down at that corner.

He went along his own two feet at the near end of it with the cloth, and the cloth went along them.

---

At about four he stopped, and at about half past four the light came off those boards, and that day's boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders.

About nineteen people went up that bank that afternoon, and nobody in that yard said one word about any of it.

He did not go up with them. He stood at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand and watched the last of them go over the top of that bank.

---

There is about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and nobody here has ever got to the far end of it.

That page stood at sixty-seven lines when the covers came over it and it took no line onto it. That lane stands at thirty pages behind it as it always has, and nothing came down it today. Those three came through this district and not one of them has ever been read out loud in it. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and nothing in this basin is set to take it. That gatepost sheet will go on carrying the same four hundred and eleven in the morning and in the morning after.

He worked at the near end of that first table from about half past nine until about four, and about nineteen people went up that bank that afternoon, and not one of them said one word about any of it.
