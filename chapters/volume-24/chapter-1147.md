# Chapter 1147: The Four Hundred And Thirty-First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And One Figure Went Out Loud At About Half Past Twelve At Her Own Scale

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and nobody in this district has ever had a use for it. That figure is eight hundred and eighty-six days old.

That board carries one thousand and forty-five marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and sixty-one days and no wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and seventy-five days. One thousand and thirty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid was open four hundred and fifty days and it was never run, and no lot in this ground came out of it, and it is not open here. Seven hundred and fifty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard is. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and fifty-five days. Eight hundred and twenty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and sixty-four days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and twenty-eight days back. Seven hundred and four marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and ninety marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing here it is counted on. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places here, and there is not a fifth place in this basin that has any of them on it. Four conditions are set out in this district and no end has ever been put to any of them. Fourteen things is everything this basin has made, and there has never been anything made in it besides that. Five of those fourteen have never come to this district, and that fifth of the five is unpaid.

Nothing went onto that page at the back of that lot book at any hour of that morning, and it stands at sixty-seven lines, and it is not going to carry a sixty-eighth. That page has no first asking on it, and there is not one on any other paper in this district. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was a line for one, so it is counted nowhere. No figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and sixty-one mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there four hundred and eleven mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and thirty-one mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and thirtieth morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and eighty-sixth night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and eighty-five of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and seventy-second of those mornings, and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and eighty-seventh morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and twentieth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with the back of it bare, and it has lain open there for longer than anybody now coming to that ground has been alive. Five lines are ruled across one page of it, and the column on the page after that one holds one name in it under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form, and it stands empty in front of every person in that yard. Behind that lane there are thirty pages, and every one of them is in the hands of whoever brought it down, and that lane stayed empty all day. That record has stood three hundred and forty-six days. About nine people stood on that ground at ten to eight. It has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and seventy-sixth afternoon running, and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Neither of those two distances has ever been handed to anybody in this district, and nobody here has ever been up over the far end of either of them, and there are about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it is spent. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page three came through this district, and nobody in this district has ever heard one of them read.

---

He was at those boards at about ten to eight with his hands in his pockets, which is where he gives that number every morning he is on that ground.

What he wanted off that morning was that number out loud before the clerk's hand had gone anywhere, and a stack he could look at all day, and half past one to come round.

---

"Thirty," said Orren Pike, and then he said that there was nothing behind it that morning, out loud as well, and the stub of pencil went behind his ear.

Not one page came down that lane behind any of it.

---

About half past nine the man of thirty-eight came down that slope and went out to about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand, and nobody said one word to him.

From about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had her own pan up at the near end of that second table, with her basket down beside her.

---

At about half past twelve she put a figure of her own out loud, once, in her own voice, at her own scale.

It went out of that yard the way her figures go out. It was not loud and it was not meant for anybody in particular, and it was hers.

He was at his stack at the near end of those boards, and he did not look up for it.

---

About two minutes after it, the man of thirty-eight put a figure of his own out loud, once, at about four feet off the near end of that first table.

He was at his stack at the near end of those boards, and he did not look up for that one either.

That page took no line for either of them.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had that number of his out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for it and nothing else, and about four voices in that yard had it behind him out of step with one another, and he stood in the middle of that with his hands at his sides and let them have it.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it, and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel away from it. The turner said nothing out loud at any hour of it.

Nobody said one word to the man of about sixty-four at the foot of that low wall.

---

At the far end of that ground the man of about thirty-two had his own top across his own trestles, and the man of about thirty-four had his own bin shut at the far end of his own table. A length of pale wood is let into the near corner of that top, and that top is not down at that corner.

---

At about four the hand came up on that low wall, and four figures came off the top of it one after another, and the flat answer went to that stone after them. It was the three hundred and twentieth time running for it.

The clerk read that morning out loud at about four with the pen in the holder and wrote the four hundred and thirty-first morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

---

The light came off those boards at about half past four, and that day's boards came off that cart face down and went up that bank on nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them.

There is about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and nobody in this district has ever got to the far end of it.

---

That page stood at sixty-seven lines when the covers came over it and it took no line onto it and it carried no sixty-eighth. That lane stands at thirty pages behind it, and nothing came down it today. Those three came through this district and nobody has read one of them out loud in it. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and no thing on this ground will take that count. That gatepost sheet will be carrying the same four hundred and eleven in the morning and in the morning after.

One figure went out loud from her own scale at about half past twelve, and a second figure went out loud about two minutes after it at about four feet off the near end of that first table, and that page took no line for either.
