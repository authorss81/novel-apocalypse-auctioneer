# Chapter 1113: The Three Hundred And Ninety-Seventh Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And He Put That Piece Into The Near Corner At About Ten And Took It Out Again Inside A Minute

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this basin has ever done one single thing with that sheet. That figure is eight hundred and fifty-two days old.

That board carries one thousand and eleven marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and twenty-seven days and no wagon has come off it in all of them. Nothing has ever been entered at that rail for one thousand and forty-one days. One thousand and two days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, was not run, and is not a lot this morning. Seven hundred and sixteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and twenty-one days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Seven hundred and ninety-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and thirty days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and ninety-four days back. Six hundred and seventy marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and fifty-six marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and it is on nothing whatever. None of those four documents has ever been in this district. Three of those lines can be read in four places here and there is no fifth place in this basin with any of them on it. Four conditions are set out in this district and no end is set on any one of them. Fourteen things is what this basin has made and nothing has ever been made in it that is not one of those fourteen. Five of those fourteen things never came as far as this ground, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page is carrying sixty-five lines on it and it took nothing onto it this morning. There is no first asking on that page and there is none on anything else in this district either. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in this yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-seven mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and seventy-seven mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted that rail. A man has stood at that foot of that bank three hundred and ninety-seven mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and ninety-sixth morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and fifty-second night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and fifty-one of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and thirty-eighth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and fifty-third morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and eighty-sixth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with the back of it bare, which is the way it has always been. Five lines are ruled across one page of it and the column on the page after that one carries a name under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form, and nothing has ever been put in it. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in the hands of the people who brought them, and not one of them came down it today. That record has stood three hundred and twelve days. About nine people were standing on that ground at ten to eight, and that lot has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and forty-second afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Neither of those two has ever been given to anybody in this district, and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it is paid and spent and none of it is left. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district, and not one of them has ever been read out loud in it.

---

He came down that slope at about a quarter to eight with the cloth over his arm, and the light was already lying along the whole length of that ground.

What he wanted off that morning was his own top squared and the near two feet of it got along with the cloth, which is the two feet he does not work, because the ground under the near end of that table went down years ago and was filled in again badly and a man who leans on that end is a man working a table that is going somewhere.

---

He put it up the way he puts it up and he squared the top with the back of his heel, and the near end of it was a quarter of an inch low on that hollow of grit, and that was how it was at about eight and how it would be at about four.

At about half past nine he started at the far end with the cloth and worked down the table, and the near two feet were the last part of it and he did not lean on them.

---

At about ten he stopped and stood with his hand on the frame at the near end and looked at that corner for a while.

Then he went and got the piece off the boards at the far end of that table, where it had lain all morning in the open with the square cut end pointing at the near edge of the table.

---

He put that square cut end into the near corner of his own top, down into the corner where the two boards of that frame come together, and he pushed on the end of it with the heel of his hand.

It went in the whole way.

The near corner of that top came up off that hollow of grit and the table stood level on its two trestles, and the daylight that had been standing under that corner all morning was not there any more, and the twist that has been in that top for years came out of it as though somebody had taken a hand off it.

---

He took his hand off it and stood looking down the length of that table.

A table that sits level can be worked the whole length of it.

He got the cloth under the near corner first, because that is the end he had never been able to do, and then he worked the length of the top from the near end back toward the far, and the cloth went along it without the board dipping under it anywhere.

---

Inside a minute he took it out again.

It came out of that corner without any trouble at all. The near end of that top went down onto the hollow of grit as soon as it was gone, and the daylight came back under that corner about the width of a finger, and the twist came back into the top of that table where it had been when he started the day.

He put that piece back on the boards at the far end of his own table with the square cut end pointing at the near edge, and he got his cloth and went back to the far half of that table, which is the half that is over ground that has not moved since anybody can remember.

That table was out of level for the rest of that morning.

---

"You are holding more light under that near corner now than you were holding at about eight."
"I am holding the same light."
"You were holding the width of a thumbnail at about eight," said the man of about thirty-two, who was at the far end of that table with a wedge in his hand. "That is the width of a finger."
"Then it has shifted on the hollow."
"Nothing has shifted. I have had my thumb on that hollow while you worked."
"Get on with your own end."
"I am on my own end. I have been on it since about eight."

Then the man of about thirty-two went along his own top with his cloth and neither of them said one word about anything else for the rest of that morning.

Nobody asked him why.

Nobody thanked him for anything.

---

At ten to eight the boy was stood at those boards with his hands in his pockets and said thirty, and not one page came down that lane behind it.

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight was at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand until about four, and nobody said one word to him there.

The woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan about four feet off the near end of that second table from about half past twelve, and she said nothing out loud that day.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air, and about four voices in that yard picked it up after him out of step with one another.

The frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it and the turner at it from about six and a half, and he did not put his heel on it and nobody in that yard started it, and he said nothing out loud this morning.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another, and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and eighty-sixth time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him a question about any part of it.

The clerk came down those boards at about four with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud, and then she wrote the three hundred and ninety-seventh morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and she took no line for it.

---

At about half past four the light came off those boards, and that day's boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up with them.

He was the last man off that ground, and the near end of his own table was a quarter of an inch low on that hollow, and the piece was lying on the boards at the far end of it where it lies.

---

Sixty-five lines is what that page stood at when the covers came over it, and it did not take a line and it did not carry a sixty-sixth. Nothing came down that lane and it is thirty pages behind it. Three of those first thirty-three lines have come through this district and not one of them has been read. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and there is nothing in it that holds that. That gatepost sheet will be carrying four hundred and eleven in the morning as well.

He put that piece into the near corner of his own top at about ten and it went in and the table stood level, and inside a minute he took it out again and put it back on those boards.
