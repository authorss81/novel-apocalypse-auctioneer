# Chapter 1106: The Three Hundred And Ninetieth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And The Four Feet Where A Man Can Work

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and it has been sitting there for everything this basin has done and nobody has taken it off the nail. It is eight hundred and forty-five days old.

That board carries one thousand and four marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and twenty days with no wagon off it. Nobody has entered anything at that rail for one thousand and thirty-four days. Nine hundred and ninety-five days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and was not run and it is not a lot and not open. Seven hundred and nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and fourteen days is how long that rule said out loud in there has stood. Seven hundred and eighty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and twenty-three days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and eighty-seven days back. Six hundred and sixty-three marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and forty-nine marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing in it that will hold that. Those four documents have never been inside this district. Three lines are readable in four places here and not in a fifth. Four conditions are set out in this district with no end upon any of them. Fourteen things is what this basin has made and nothing outside those fourteen. Five of those fourteen never got in here and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

Nothing went onto that page this morning and it took no line, and it is still at sixty-five lines. There is no way back to the first asking made in this district, and it is on nothing. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for a reason, and it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in this yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and twenty mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and seventy mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank three hundred and ninety mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and eighty-ninth morning and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and forty-fifth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and forty-four of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and thirty-first of those mornings and all four were got. That is the two hundred and forty-sixth morning running with nobody having asked him anything, and this morning was the two hundred and seventy-ninth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

There is nothing at the back of that lot book and there never has been, and it lies open along those boards. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book, and the column on the page behind that one has a name in it under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form and it stands empty and nobody in this district has ever been asked about it. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in the hands of those who carried them, and nothing has come down it today. That record has stood three hundred and five days. On that ground at ten to eight there were about nine people, and the lot there has been public since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones for the three hundred and thirty-fifth afternoon running and one word was the whole of it. Neither of those two distances has ever been given to anybody in this district, and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight and every part of it is spent. Three of those first thirty-three lines came through this district and there is no voice in it that has read any of them out.

---

He came out at about half past nine and got his cloth out of his coat and worked his way to about four feet off the near end of that first table.

What he wanted off that morning was to finish the last length of it before the light went off those boards, and for about those four feet to still be about four feet at about four.

---

He does not hurry that and he does not talk through it.

Those four feet between him and the near end of that first table are the four feet where a man can work without being spoken to, and that is the whole of what they are for, and there is nothing else that yard has ever offered him and he has never once asked that yard for anything else.

Nobody has ever asked him anything either. Not that day and not on any other day, and he has never asked anybody for a bed, or for a wage, or for an office, and nobody at the top of that lane has ever offered him one.

---

At ten to eight the boy was at those boards with his hands in his pockets and said thirty, and nothing came down that lane behind it.

At about ten the turner came along that ground and said one thing out loud about the boards, to the ground and not to anybody.

"Whatever is on that cart goes up while the light is on that slope and not one minute after, and the near wheel is still finding the soft place at the second bend, so it goes up slow and it goes up once."

Nobody asked him anything about that or about anything else. He was at that frame and it was his frame, and that was the whole of the arrangement between him and that yard this morning.

---

At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan about four feet off the near end of that second table with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that day.

The man of about forty-eight put his number out into about four hundred yards of air at about half past one, in the voice he keeps for it, and about four voices in that yard took it up after him out of step with one another.

He heard the whole of that without turning round, the way he has heard the whole of it on a good many mornings, and he did not put his cloth down at any part of it.

---

The man of about thirty-four was at the far end of that first table with his own top on it and a foot and about four inches of sound pallet lying on the boards at the far end of that table, square cut end pointing at the near edge, and the two of them did not exchange one word all morning.

Neither did anybody else.

---

He got the last length finished at about a quarter to four and wiped the cloth down both sides of what he had done and stood with it in his hand for a while.

About four feet of that yard was a different kind of quiet, and about four people at the top of that bank had said since that they had not said what it was, and none of them said it this morning either.

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another, and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and seventy-ninth time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him about any part of it.

---

The clerk came down that row of boards at about four with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud, and then she wrote the three hundred and ninetieth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

She has read a morning number out loud for four hundred mornings and more, and this one was round the way one of them in ten is, and she read it the same as the rest of them, and nobody on those boards looked up at it.

---

About half past four the light came off those boards and the day's boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank, and about nineteen people went up with them.

The two who did not go were him and the woman with the cloth knotted over her scale and her basket on her arm about four feet off the near end of that second table.

---

It stood at sixty-five lines when the covers came over that page and it did not carry another line. That lane has brought nothing down it today and it is thirty pages behind it. Three of those first thirty-three lines came through this district unread. The fifth of that five stands at five and nobody has paid it. Seven months is counted here and nothing in it will carry that. Four hundred and eleven will be on that gatepost sheet in the morning and the day after.

The four feet were still four feet at about four, and nobody in that yard asked him one thing about them.
