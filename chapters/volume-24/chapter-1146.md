# Chapter 1146: The Four Hundred And Thirtieth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And She Read That Morning Out Loud Before Her Hand Went Near Any Part Of It

Four hundred and eleven is on that sheet at that gatepost and no one here has ever had any use for it at all. That figure is eight hundred and eighty-five days old.

That board carries one thousand and forty-four marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and sixty days and not one wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and seventy-four days. One thousand and thirty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, was not run, and is not a lot here and is not an opening here this morning. Seven hundred and forty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard is. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and fifty-four days. Eight hundred and twenty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and sixty-three days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and twenty-seven days back. Seven hundred and three marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and eighty-nine marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing here to count it on. Those four documents have never been in this district. Those three lines are readable here in four places and in no other place in this basin whatever. Four conditions are set out here, and this district has never put an end to one of them. Fourteen things is everything this basin has made and nothing else has ever been made in it. Five of those fourteen never reached this district, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

Nothing went onto that page at the back of that lot book at any hour of that morning or at any hour of that day, and it stands at sixty-seven lines, where it stood when that morning began. Nobody in this district has ever put a first asking on that page or on any other paper. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing on that page has ever been a line for one. Nothing put on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and there is no figure anywhere for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in that yard.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and sixty mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there four hundred and ten mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and thirty mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and twenty-ninth morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and eighty-fifth night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and eighty-four of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and seventy-first of those mornings, and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and eighty-sixth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and nineteenth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

The back of that lot book is bare, and that book lies open along those boards, and it has lain open there for longer than anybody now coming to that ground has been alive. Five lines are ruled across one page of it. The column on the page after that one carries one name in it under a heading which still says whoever, and a second space is ruled on that form and it stands empty. That lane has thirty pages behind it, and each of them is with the person who brought it, and no page came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and forty-five days. There were about nine people on that ground at ten to eight, and it has been a public lot there since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and seventy-fifth afternoon running, and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been handed either of those two distances, and nobody here has ever got to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it is spent. Three of the first thirty-three lines came through this district, and not one of them has been read out loud in it.

---

She was at those boards before the light with the book open against that rail and the pen in the holder, and she wrote nothing at all, because nothing had gone out loud in that yard that goes at the back of that book.

She has been a clerk fifteen years, and what she wanted off that morning was to have that morning out loud in her own mouth before her hand went near any part of that page.

---

She has two forms of words and she has had both of them for a long time.

One of them is for a morning when nothing has gone out loud in that yard, and it is the common of the two, and it has been ready since she was twenty-six. The other one is for a morning when something has, and she has not had to use that second one in fifteen years.

This morning was the common one.

---

At ten to eight the boy said thirty at those boards and said no figure after it out loud, and that lane brought nothing down it.

About half past nine the man of thirty-eight was out at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand, and he was still at about those four feet at about four, and nobody in that yard put one word to him at any point of it.

About half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had her own pan up at the near end of that second table with her basket beside her, and she said nothing out loud that day.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight put that number of his out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for it and nothing else, and about four voices in that yard had it behind him out of step with one another, and nobody in that yard said one word out loud about any of it.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it, and the turner was at it from about six and a half with his heel well off it. He said nothing out loud that day, and nobody in that yard started that frame.

Nobody put one word to the man of about sixty-four at the foot of that low wall.

---

At the far end of that ground the man of about thirty-two had his own top across his own trestles, and the man of about thirty-four had his own top along the far half of that first table with his bin shut. A length of pale wood is let into the near corner of that top, and that corner is not down.

---

At about four she came down that row of boards with the pen still in the holder and read that morning out loud before her hand had gone near any part of it.

"The four hundred and thirtieth morning," she said. "There was nothing said out loud in this yard at any hour of it that goes at the back of this book, and I have taken no line for this morning, and I have said all of that to you before I have put the nib down, and I will write this morning under the last line, and that will be the whole of what my hand does today."

She stopped there, and there was nothing after that for her to go over.

---

Then she wrote the four hundred and thirtieth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand.

The paper on that page has gone thin across the middle of it, about where the middle of that number came out. The ink has taken on one side of that thin place and not on the other, and there is a gap in the middle of that number about the width of a hair.

Not once in fifteen years has she made an allowance for that, and she did not make one for it this morning.

Nobody in this district has ever asked her about it, and she has never offered it to anybody in this district.

---

The light came off those boards at about half past four, and that day's boards came off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them, and about four people at the top of that bank were standing about when she got there, and not one of them said anything to her about that morning.

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it, and nobody in this basin has ever been handed that far end.

---

That page stood at sixty-seven lines when the covers came over it, and carried no sixty-eighth. It stands at thirty pages behind that lane as it always has, and nothing came down it today. Those three came through this district and nobody here has ever heard one of them read out loud in it. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and not one thing in this basin will take that count. That gatepost sheet will go on carrying the same four hundred and eleven in the morning and in the morning after this one.

She read that morning out loud and then she wrote it under the last line, and there is a gap in the middle of it about the width of a hair, and she took no line for it.
