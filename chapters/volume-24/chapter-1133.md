# Chapter 1133: The Four Hundred And Seventeenth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And A Man At The End Of That Second Table Said One Thing And Nobody Answered Him

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and nothing that any person in this basin owns has ever been done about it. That figure is eight hundred and seventy-two days old.

That board carries one thousand and thirty-one marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and forty-seven days and no wagon has come off it in that whole time. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and sixty-one days. One thousand and twenty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and was never run, and it is not a lot here and is not open here this morning. This ground is seven hundred and thirty-six days behind the figure on the second line of that book. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and forty-one days. It has been eight hundred and eleven days since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and fifty days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and fourteen days back. Six hundred and ninety marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and seventy-six marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and nothing in this basin will hold that count. Those four documents have never been in this district at all. Three of those lines are readable in four places in this basin and there is no fifth place in here that has any of them on it. Four conditions stand set out in this district, and there is no end standing on any of them anywhere in it. Fourteen things is the whole of what this basin has made and no fifteenth has ever come out of it here. Of those fourteen, five never arrived in this district, and the fifth of that five stands unpaid.

That page is at sixty-six lines and it took no line onto it in that morning. No first asking of anybody is written on that page or on any other paper in this district. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and no line on that page written before the two hundred and eighth morning was a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and forty-seven mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there three hundred and ninety-seven mornings, and there is no thanks due to the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and seventeen mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and sixteenth morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and seventy-second night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and seventy-one of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and fifty-eighth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and seventy-third morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and sixth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with the back of it bare, and it has lain open there with the back of it bare for as long as anything in this district has been kept count of. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book and the column on the page after that one holds one name in it under a heading which still says whoever. That form has a second space ruled on it and it is empty. Thirty pages are behind that lane and every one of them is in the hands of the people who brought it down, and nothing came down that lane today. That record has stood three hundred and thirty-two days. About nine people were on that ground at ten to eight, and it has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones that afternoon for the three hundred and sixty-second afternoon running and one word was all that afternoon held. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two, and nobody has ever been up over the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it was paid and it is spent and none of it is left anywhere in this district. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page three came through this district, and not one of them has ever been read out loud in this district.

---

He came down that slope at about half past nine with his cloth over his arm and stopped at about four feet off the near end of that first table, which is the place a man can stand at without being spoken to, and there is nothing in this district that has ever asked him why he stands there.

What he wanted off that morning was to get through the four lengths he had on that board before the light came round far enough to make him look at the near end of every one of them twice.

---

At ten to eight the boy said thirty at those boards and said no figure after it out loud, and the clerk wrote that morning down at about four and took no line for it.

The man of about thirty-four was at the far end of that ground with his own table up and the cloth in his hand, and the foot and about four inches of sound pallet was lying on the boards at the far end of that table, square cut end pointing at the near edge of it.

The woman who keeps a scale had her pan up about four feet off the near end of that second table from about half past twelve with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that morning.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight put his number into about four hundred yards of air, and about four voices in that yard took it up behind him out of step with one another, and nobody in that yard put one word out loud about any of it.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it, and the turner was at it, and his heel was off it, and nobody in that yard started it.

---

At about half past eleven the man at the end of that second table stood up off the stool he has sat on at that end for fifteen years and put his hand flat on the far edge of that top, the way a man does when he is going to say something and not when he is going to move it.

"There is one place on that top where the grain has gone soft under my hand," he said. "It is the only soft place on it and I have never told a living soul where it is."

Then he sat down on that stool again and went on with what he was doing at that end of that table.

Nobody answered him.

Nobody asked him where it was, or whether it was still soft, or what he did about it, and nobody said one word back to him about any part of that for the rest of that day, and he said nothing else out loud at any point of it.

---

The man of thirty-eight heard all of that from about four feet off the near end of that first table, because that yard is not a large yard and the wind was off that bank that morning.

He had a board under his hand and a cloth in the other one and there was nothing in that sentence that needed anything said back to it, and he did not say anything, and he did not put his cloth down.

---

At about four the man of fifty-six had his hand up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the three hundred and sixth time running for it, and nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about any part of it.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall through all of it and was asked nothing.

The clerk read that morning out loud at about four with the pen still in the holder and then wrote the four hundred and seventeenth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand, and she took no line for it.

There is about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it and nobody in this basin has ever been given that end of it either.

---

About half past four the light went off those boards and that day's boards came down off that cart with their faces to the ground and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders, and about nineteen people went up beside them.

He finished his four lengths, and he had a fifth length ready on the bench at about four and he did not start it, and the bench was still there at about half past four with the length on it.

---

That page stood at sixty-six lines when the covers came over it, and it took no line onto it and carried no sixty-seventh. No page came down that lane and it stands at thirty pages behind it as it has stood for the whole of that run. Those three came through this district and nobody here has ever read one of them out loud. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and no thing in this basin is built to take it. That gatepost sheet will go on carrying that same four hundred and eleven in the morning and in the morning after that.

A man said one thing at about half past eleven about his own table, and nobody in that yard answered him, and nobody has asked him about it since, and that page took no line for it.
