# Chapter 1114: The Three Hundred And Ninety-Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And Three Things Said Out Loud Beside A Frame With Nothing Turning On It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and it has been on that sheet through everything this basin has made and everything it has failed to make. That figure is eight hundred and fifty-three days old.

That board carries one thousand and twelve marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and twenty-eight days and not one wagon has come off it. Nobody has entered anything for that rail for one thousand and forty-two days. One thousand and three days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days without ever being run, and it is not a lot this morning. Seven hundred and seventeen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and twenty-two days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Seven hundred and ninety-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and thirty-one days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and ninety-five days back. Six hundred and seventy-one marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and fifty-seven marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing here for that count to be set on. Those four documents have never once been got into this district. Three of those lines stand in four places in this basin and in no fifth place in it. Four conditions are set out in this district, and there is no end upon any one of them. Fourteen is the whole of what this basin has made, and it has never made anything else. Five of those fourteen things never arrived in this basin, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

Nothing went onto that page this morning, and the number of lines on it is sixty-five. There is no first asking on that page, and there is none on any other paper in this district, and nobody here is able to recover it. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, and it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in this yard for the mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-eight mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and seventy-eight mornings, and the man who fitted that rail is not to be thanked. A man has stood at that foot of that bank three hundred and ninety-eight mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and ninety-seventh morning and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and fifty-third night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and fifty-two of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and thirty-ninth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and fifty-fourth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and eighty-seventh time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

Nothing has ever been written at the back of that lot book, and that book lies open along those boards this morning with nothing on that page of it. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book, and the column on the page after that has a single name in it under a heading which still says whoever. A second space is ruled on that form, and nothing has ever been put into it. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in their own carriers' hands and nothing came down it today. That record has stood three hundred and thirteen days. About nine people were on that ground at ten to eight, and that lot has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and forty-third afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been given either of those two and nobody here has ever gone up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight, it has been paid and it is spent, and none of it is left in this district. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three came through this district, and no voice in this district has ever read one of them out.

---

He was at that frame at about six and a half and the stove had been in a good while, and that casting had been cold since before the light came.

What he wanted off that morning was the whole of that day's load off that cart and up that bank with the light still on the boards, and not one stop on the way in.

---

There is a groove cut across the face of that casting about the width of two fingers, and the belt sits down in it, and the two marks he made himself on either side of it are the only two marks on that casting he has ever made.

He keeps a length of wire for that groove, a foot and a half of it with a hook bent in one end, and he went at the groove with it at about seven this morning and there was nothing in it.

Not grit. Not packing. Nothing at all, and the wire went down the whole width of it and came out clean at the other side.

He put the wire back in his coat.

---

At about seven he came along that ground and said the first thing out loud to nobody in particular, which is the way he says things.

"That stack on that cart is two days in one and the near wheel is still going into the same place at the second bend. It goes up slow and it goes up once, and the man on the bottom of it at that bend takes his weight on the other rail and not on the rail he is stood next to, and that is the whole of what I know about the second bend this morning."

Nobody put one question to him about any part of it.

---

At about ten he said the second thing, at the near end of those boards, where anybody standing at that stack could get it.

"The boards go up while the light is on them and not one minute after it. That is not me being careful, that is the hour. Half past four is where it has been for thirty-one years and it is not going to be somewhere else this morning, and if anybody at the top of that bank wants to stand up there waiting on me they can stand up there until the light is off that ground, because I am not walking a stack of boards up a bank in the dark to save somebody half an hour."

He put his hands in his pockets and looked at the face of that bank for a while.

---

At about two he said the third one, and it was about the ground at that bend and not about the boards at all.

"There is a stone in the ground at that second bend about the size of a closed fist and it comes up under the near wheel every single time and it has been coming up under it since before I was stood on this bank. A man does not feel it through the sole of a boot. He feels it come up through the spokes and he thinks it is the load. It is not the load. It is a stone, and it will be that stone this afternoon, and it does not get softer by the third time you go over it."

Nobody asked him a question about that either.

---

At about ten that morning he got to the front of the one sentence he carries.

He gets to the front of it most mornings. He has never once been past the front of it, and he has never told anybody in that yard that he gets as far as the front, and he stood there this morning with the words of it as far as they ever get and the casting cold under his hand.

Then he let it go where it always goes back to.

Being left alone is not being silent. He said three things out loud in that yard before the light went off those boards and every one of them was about the boards or the bend or the hour, and there was no question put to him for any of them, and there was no question put to him about any of the rest of it either.

---

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight came down that slope and stopped at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand, and nobody spoke to him there before about four.

The woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan about four feet off the near end of that second table from about half past twelve, with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud that day.

The man of about forty-eight had his number out into about four hundred yards of air at about half past one, and about four voices in that yard took it up after him out of step with one another.

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and eighty-seventh time running, and nobody in this district has ever asked him anything about any part of it.

---

The clerk came down that row of boards at about four with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud, and then she wrote the three hundred and ninety-eighth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and she took no line for it.

The light went off those boards at about half past four, and that day's boards came down off that cart with their faces down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them.

He stood at that frame and watched the last of them go, and nothing was turning on it, and he had not put his heel on it once in all of that day.

---

That page stood at sixty-five lines when the covers came over it and it took no line and did not carry a sixty-sixth. That lane brought nothing down it, and it stands at thirty pages behind it. Those three came through this district and no voice in it has read any of them out. That fifth of that five is five and nobody has paid it. Seven months is counted here and there is nothing in this basin that will hold it. The same four hundred and eleven will be on that gatepost sheet in the morning.

He said three things out loud in that yard today, and nobody asked him one question about any of them.
