# Chapter 1142: The Four Hundred And Twenty-Sixth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And He Said One Thing Out Loud At About Two And Stopped Before The End Of It

That figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and no one in this district has ever read it as anything else. That figure is eight hundred and eighty-one days old this morning.

That board carries one thousand and forty marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and fifty-six days, and no wagon has come off it in any of them. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and seventy days. One thousand and thirty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long that bid stood open, and it was not run, and it is not a lot on this ground. It is not open here this morning either. Seven hundred and forty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard is. That rule said out loud in that yard has stood seven hundred and fifty days. Eight hundred and twenty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and fifty-nine days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is nine hundred and twenty-three days back. Six hundred and ninety-nine marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and eighty-five marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing in this basin it is counted on. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines can be read in four places here, and there is not a fifth place in this basin that has any of them on it. Four conditions are set out here, and this district has never written an end to one of them. Fourteen things is everything this basin has made, and there has never been anything else made in it. Five of those fourteen never got as far as this ground, and that fifth of those five stands at five and is unpaid.

Nothing went onto that page at the back of that lot book at any hour of that morning, and it stands at sixty-seven lines, which is where it stood when that morning began. There is no first asking on that page, and there never was one on any paper in this district. One reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and no line on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for a reason, so it stands in no count anywhere, and no figure has ever been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and fifty-six mornings. That near rail on the near side of it has stood there four hundred and six mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-six mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the four hundred and twenty-fifth morning, and it is neither that second table nor that near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and eighty-first night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and eighty of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and sixty-seventh of those mornings, and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and eighty-second morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the three hundred and fifteenth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

That lot book lies open along those boards with the back of it bare, and it has lain open there with the back of it bare for longer than the oldest of them has been coming to that ground. Five lines are ruled across one page of it. The column on the page after that one carries one name in it under a heading which still says whoever. That form carries a second ruled space, and there is nothing in it. Behind that lane there are thirty pages, and every one of them is in the hands of the people who brought it down, and this lane brought nothing down itself today. That record has stood three hundred and forty-one days. There were about nine people on that ground at ten to eight, and it has been a public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and seventy-first afternoon running, and one word was the whole of that afternoon. Nobody in this district has ever been handed either of those two distances, and nobody has ever been up over the far end of either of them, and there are about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert at the end of it.

That toll came to ninety-eight and it is spent. Of those first thirty-three lines three came through this district, and nobody in it has heard a single one of them read out loud.

---

He was at that frame at about six and a half, and the casting was cold enough at that hour to take the heat out of a hand in about as long as it takes to count to ten.

He has been in that yard thirty-one years, and what he wanted off that morning was that day's load off that cart and up that bank with the light still on those boards.

---

Nothing was turning on it, and nothing had been turning on it for a long while, and he had his heel somewhere else in that yard and kept it there.

He put the flat of his hand on that casting twice, once at about seven and once after the light came over, and it was cold both times, and warmer the second of them than it had been the first.

The belt that turns that frame lies flat on those boards off its grooves. The two marks he has made on either side of that groove are the only marks he has ever made on it, and nobody in this district has ever asked him about them.

---

By about eight the light had come over the top of that bank and was lying along the ground at the foot of that wall. That ground is about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and nobody here has ever got to the far end of it.

---

The boy was at those boards at ten to eight with his hands in his pockets and gave that number out loud, and no page came down that lane behind it.

About half past nine the man of thirty-eight went out to about four feet off the near end of that first table with the cloth in his hand, and he was at about those four feet until about four, and nobody said anything to him in any part of that.

From about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale had her pan up about four feet off the near end of that second table, with her basket down beside her, and she said nothing out loud at any hour of that day.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight had that number of his out into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for it and for nothing else, and about four voices in that yard had it behind him out of step with one another.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall all that morning, and nobody put a word to him.

---

At the far end of that ground there were two men at two tables, and the near end of one of those tops stood a quarter of an inch low on the hollow of grit at the foot of its near trestle. The man who owned it had his own bin shut and a cloth in his hand.

---

At about ten he got as far as the front of the one sentence he carries.

He gets to the front of that one most mornings, and he has never once been past the front of it, and he has never once stood in that yard and told anybody that he gets as far as the front.

Then he let it go back where it always goes back to, and he put his hand on that frame again with nothing turning on it.

Nobody leaving him alone is the same thing as him having nothing to say, and he had one thing to say that morning, and he said it at about two where anybody standing at that stack could get it.

---

"The far end of that stack wants turning before it goes up, and the boards on the bottom want it face up going up that slope or they will come up that slope in a wind that has not been asked for, and a man at the top of that bank who wants them the other way can come down here and take the bottom of it himself and put his shoulder under it at the second bend and—"

He stopped there, and he had a thing to do with his hands, and what he was going to say after that bend is still where it has always stood.

Nobody asked him anything about that.

Nobody at the top of that bank has ever come down and put a shoulder under anything of his.

---

The hand went up on that low wall at about four, and four figures came off the top of it one after another, and the flat answer went to that stone behind them. It was the three hundred and fifteenth time running for it.

The clerk read that morning out loud at about four with the pen still in the holder, and she wrote the four hundred and twenty-sixth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand. She took no line for it.

That day's boards came down off that cart face down at about half past four and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them, and that ground was empty after that.

He stayed at that frame while the last of them went up, and nothing turned on it, and his heel was off it for the whole of that day.

---

That page stood at sixty-seven lines when the covers came over it and it carried no sixty-eighth. That lane has thirty pages behind it and has had them through that whole of it. Those three came through this district, and no voice here has ever read one of them out loud. That fifth of that five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and no thing at all in this basin will take it. That gatepost sheet will be carrying the same four hundred and eleven on it in the morning and in the morning after.

He said one thing out loud at about two about boards and about the second bend, and it was not about that frame, and he did not get to the end of it, and nobody asked him anything.
