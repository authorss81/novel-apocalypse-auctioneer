# Chapter 1115: The Three Hundred And Ninety-Ninth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And Five Boards In One Stack With A Row Of Notches Cut Across Their Undersides

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and that sheet has been on that gatepost longer than anybody in that basin has been putting anything on it. That figure is eight hundred and fifty-four days old.

That board carries one thousand and thirteen marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and twenty-nine days and no wagon has come off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and forty-three days. One thousand and four days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days, it was not run, and it is not a lot and not open this morning. Seven hundred and eighteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and twenty-three days is how long that rule said out loud in here has stood. Seven hundred and ninety-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month, and that body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and thirty-two days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and ninety-six days back. Six hundred and seventy-two marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and fifty-eight marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing in this basin that the count is set on. Those four documents have never been in this district. Three of those lines are readable in four places here and in no fifth place in it. Four conditions stand set out in this district with nothing set at the end of any of them. Fourteen is the whole of what this basin has made and there is nothing else that it has made. Five of those fourteen never arrived in this district at all, and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

That page stands at sixty-five lines and nothing was taken onto it this morning. There is no first asking on that page and there is none on anything else in this district either, and nobody in this district is able to recover one. On the one hundred and eighty-second morning a reason was said out loud in that yard, and nothing written on that page before the two hundred and eighth morning was ever a line for one, so it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and twenty-nine mornings and that near rail on the near side of it three hundred and seventy-nine mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at that foot of that bank three hundred and ninety-nine mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and ninety-eighth morning, and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and fifty-fourth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and fifty-three of the nights of it. Four figures came off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and fortieth of those mornings and all four of them were got. That is the two hundred and fifty-fifth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. This morning was the two hundred and eighty-eighth time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

Nothing has ever been written at the back of that lot book and it lies open along those boards with the back of it as bare as it has ever been. Five lines are ruled across one page of that book and the column on the page after that one has a name in it under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space ruled on that form, it stands empty, and nobody has ever been asked to fill it. Behind that lane there are thirty pages in the hands of the people who brought them, and not one of them came down that lane this morning. That record has stood three hundred and fourteen days. About nine people were stood on that ground at ten to eight, and it has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones this afternoon for the three hundred and forty-fourth afternoon running and one word was all that afternoon came to. Those two have never been given to anybody in this district and nobody here has ever been up to the far end of either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight, it is paid and spent, and none of it is left in this district. Three of the first thirty-three lines on that back page came through this district, and not one of them has ever been read out loud in it.

---

He came down that slope at about half past seven with his own top under his arm, and he had it across the two trestles before the light was off the top of that bank.

What he wanted off that morning was to have that top off those trestles in two-foot lengths and squared before the near end of it started to go with the sun, and to have the pieces laid down flat in the order they came off.

---

The middle of that top came up in one pull. The near two feet did not, and had not for years, because the ground at that end of that frame was opened up and filled in again badly and a two-foot length there has to be persuaded rather than pulled.

He set it down and squared it against the frame with the back of his heel, and he went along the top of it with his cloth, and he did that four times over, and there were five lengths of his own top off those trestles by about nine.

---

The boards he works from are in a stack at the near end of his own frame with their undersides up while they are off the trestles being dressed, and the light comes across them from the far end and there is no way to stand over that stack and not see what is on the undersides of them.

Five boards in that stack have a row of notches cut across their undersides.

They are the same width of a hand in from the edge on every one of the five. They are at the same angle. They are the same depth. And they were cut in one go, because the edge that made them went the length of all five in a single pull and the tear at the end of the fifth one is the same tear as at the start of the first.

There is nothing on the undersides of any other board in that stack.

---

He has told nobody in this district about those five boards.

He has not worked out what they are for.

Nobody in that yard has ever asked him about them, and he has never offered, and there is no part of that morning that turns on them and no part of it that turns on the fact that they are there.

---

At ten to eight the boy was at those boards and said the lane count before the clerk's hand had gone anywhere, and it was thirty, and nothing came down that lane behind it.

At about half past nine the man of thirty-eight came out and stood at about four feet off the near end of that first table with his cloth in his hand, and nobody said one word to him there before about four.

The woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan from about half past twelve, about four feet off the near end of that second table, and she said nothing out loud that day.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight put his number out into about four hundred yards of air, and about four voices in that yard took it up behind him out of step with one another, and nobody in that yard put one word out loud about it.

That frame stood at the near end of that first table with nothing turning on it, and the turner was at it from about six and a half and kept his heel off it, and nobody in that yard started it, and he said nothing out loud this morning.

---

At about four the hand went up on that low wall and four figures came off the top of it one after another and the flat answer went to that stone after them, and it was the two hundred and eighty-eighth time running, and nobody in this district has ever put one question to him about any of it.

At about four the clerk came down that row of boards with the pen in the holder and read that morning out loud before her hand went near any part of it, and then she wrote the three hundred and ninety-ninth morning under the last line in her own small careful hand and she took no line for it.

---

The light came off those boards at about half past four, and that day's boards came down off that cart face down and went up that bank on about nine pairs of shoulders. About nineteen people went up with them.

He went up with the first of them, with the five lengths of his own top under his arm.

---

The covers came over that page at sixty-five lines and it took no line and did not carry a sixty-sixth. Nothing came down that lane and it stands at thirty pages behind it. Those three came through this district and no voice in it has ever read one of them out. That fifth of that five is five and is unpaid. Seven months is counted in this district and there is nothing in this basin that will hold it. Four hundred and eleven will be on that gatepost sheet in the morning, and the day after that.

Five boards in that stack have a row of notches cut across their undersides, and he has told nobody about them, and nobody in that yard asked him.
