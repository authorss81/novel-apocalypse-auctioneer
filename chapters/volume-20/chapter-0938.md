# Chapter 0938: The TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SECOND Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month Nobody Said A Reason And Nobody Said No And The Man Of About Thirty-Two Put His Table Up About Four Feet Along From The Second Table And Squared It With The Back Of His Heel And Said Nothing At All And The Clerk Did Not Read The Morning Out Loud And Named It On The Page In Her Own Hand Without A Line And The Page Carries Forty-Five Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and seventy-seven days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and thirty-six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and fifty-two days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and sixty-six days. Eight hundred and twenty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and forty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and forty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and sixteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and nineteen days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and fifty-five days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and ninety-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and eighty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and fifty-two mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and fifty-one. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and two mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and twenty-two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and twenty-first morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries forty-five lines and it did not carry a forty-sixth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and sixty-third of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and seventy-seventh night of that run, having slept on five hundred and seventy-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and sixty-seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the seventy-eighth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and thirty-seven days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning, the morning was not read out loud, and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The turner had his wheel going with a piece of ash in it and he turned the ash out once, looked at it, and put it back in. The man who digs loam was at the north end on the same strip. The man of about thirty-four stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with his hands behind his back and said nothing out loud at all this morning, and he was looked at by about nine people and he did not know it.

And the man of about thirty-two put his table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel, the way he squares it, and got his apron on, and went along the top of it with the cloth, and he said nothing at all this morning. Nobody asked him anything and he said nothing and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man puts a table up every morning about four feet along from the second table and squares it and this morning was the first morning in four that he put it up and did not say one word out loud, and that about four of them have not said what the four mornings have come to.

The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and worked at the near end of the first table and said nothing out loud at all this morning, and the clerk sat at about four feet off the near end of the second table with the front of that book open and said nothing out loud to anybody this morning either.

And the morning went on in that yard the way it goes. About nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank and stood about four feet off the near end of the second table and looked at the front of that book and went away again, and not one of them said a word out loud, and about four of them have said since that a yard which had a reason in it yesterday has a morning this morning with nothing at all in it, and that about four of them have not said what a yard has on a morning like that.

At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale came down that bank with her scale in a piece of cloth and weighed a bar of iron out of a basket and wrote the figure on the edge of a board of her own with a bit of chalk and said nothing to anybody, and nobody said anything to her about it either.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

She wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it and about four of them have said since that she did not read it out loud this morning, and that about four of them said she did not read it out loud on the morning behind this one either, and that about four of them have not said whether the reading out loud belongs to a morning with something in it.

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and the boards went down the way they go down.
