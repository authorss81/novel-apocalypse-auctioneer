# Chapter 0948: The TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SECOND Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Woman Who Keeps A Scale Said Out Loud That The Reason Is On The Form And Not On The Page At The Back Of That Book And Nobody Moved The Form And Nobody Was Asked To Fill The Space On It And The Page At The Back Carries Forty-Six Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and eighty-seven days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and forty-six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and sixty-two days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and seventy-six days. Eight hundred and thirty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and fifty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and fifty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and twenty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and twenty-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and sixty-five days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and five marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and ninety-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and sixty-two mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and sixty-one. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and twelve mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and thirty-two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and thirty-first morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries forty-six lines and it did not carry a forty-seventh this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and seventy-third of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and eighty-seventh night of that run, having slept on five hundred and eighty-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and seventy-seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the eighty-eighth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and forty-seven days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning was read out loud before it was written, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them and put his hand flat on the stone afterwards and left it there a while.

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said a figure out loud, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved.

"Four hundred and thirty-one," he said, at the near end of the first table.

And about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them said it after him out of step with each other, and nobody asked him what it was a figure of. The turner had his wheel going. The man who digs loam was at the north end. The man of about thirty-two put his table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel and put his apron on.

And there is a form on those boards at about four feet off the near end of the first table, under the clean page, and it came down that lane in a hand, and it is folded across the middle with the fold going the wrong way for the writing on it, and the space on it is a different space on a different piece of paper from the column with the one name in it in the lot book, and it is empty as well. Nobody in that yard moved that form this morning and nobody lifted the clean page off it and nobody put a hand on it.

The man of about thirty-four stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table and said nothing out loud at all this morning. The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and worked at about four feet off the near end of the first table and said nothing out loud at all this morning. The clerk said nothing out loud to anybody from about ten until about two, and she had the front of that book open and she looked at the lot book and then at her own two hands and then at the lot book again, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk not write anything for four hours.

---

And at about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale said it, once, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and she said it to the boards and not to the clerk.

"That reason is on the form and it is not on the page at the back of that book," she said. "The form came down that lane in a hand and the page at the back never did. That is where it is, and I am not going to say it again."

---

And nobody in that yard asked her to say it again, and nobody asked her how she knew it was on the form, and nobody asked the clerk to move that form off those boards, and nobody asked her to fill the space on it, and nobody asked anybody else to fill it, and the clean page did not get lifted off it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that about four of them were standing close enough to hear a woman name a thing that was lying in the open on those boards, and that about four of them have not said what happens to a thing that is named out loud where it is lying.

---

And the clerk said nothing out loud to anybody about any of it. She looked at the woman and then she looked at the front of that book and she did not say one word back, and the woman who keeps a scale went back to her basket and weighed the bar of iron and wrote the figure on the edge of a board of her own with a bit of chalk and said nothing else out loud for the rest of the morning, and the page at the back of that lot book did not take a line for it.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall, and he was not asked one question, which is the eighty-eighth morning running of that.

"The two hundred and thirty-second morning," she said. "No reason was said out loud in this yard this morning and nothing is going on the back of that book, and that is the whole of it."
