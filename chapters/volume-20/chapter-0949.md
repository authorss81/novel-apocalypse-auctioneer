# Chapter 0949: The TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-THIRD Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Of Fifty-Six Said The Four Off That Wall Twice And Said His Four Words At About Four And Nobody Asked Him Anything Out Loud And The Page At The Back Carries Forty-Six Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and eighty-eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and forty-seven. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and sixty-three days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and seventy-seven days. Eight hundred and thirty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and fifty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and fifty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and twenty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and thirty days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and sixty-six days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and six marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and ninety-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and sixty-three mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and sixty-two. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and thirteen mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and thirty-three, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it were entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and thirty-second morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries forty-six lines and it did not carry a forty-seventh this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and seventy-fourth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and eighty-eighth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and eighty-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and seventy-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the eighty-ninth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and forty-eight days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning was read out loud before it was written, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and the man of fifty-six was already at the wall when they came down, because he is always at the wall when they come down.

---

At about a quarter to eight he said the four off that wall, once, in the ordinary voice, at the foot of it, and he got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone that stands under that wall and left it there a while, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard all four of them and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said them after him out of step with each other, and nobody said one word to him.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has said the four off that wall twice a morning for the better part of a year got all four of them this morning before about nine people had got their coats off, and that about four of them have not said how he knows the order they go in.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said so out loud, and nobody said anything back to him. The turner had his wheel going. The man who digs loam was at the north end. The man of about thirty-two put his table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel and put his apron on.

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the woman who keeps a scale has her board propped up where she can see the wall from where she weighs things, and that about four of them have not said what a woman who weighs bars of iron wants to see a wall for, and that about four of them have not said why she set the figures out of the sight of it and can still see the wall past the edge of it.

---

The man of about thirty-four stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with his hands behind his back and said nothing out loud at all this morning. The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and worked at the end of the first table and said nothing out loud at all this morning. The man who made the stone came down the bank about ten with his hands in his pockets and stood about nine feet off the wall for a while and then went back up that bank, and he said nothing out loud to anybody.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man came down that bank this morning, stood about nine feet off a wall for a while with his hands in his pockets and went back up it again, and that about four of them have not said what he came down for.

---

At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale came down that bank with her scale in a piece of cloth and set it up at about four feet off the near end of the second table and weighed a bar of iron out of a basket and wrote the figure on the edge of a board of her own with a bit of chalk and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she weighed three this morning where she has weighed two on the mornings before this one and that about four of them have not said where the third one went.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall, and he was not asked one question, which is the eighty-ninth morning running of that.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall again and got all four of them, and he said them in the same order as he had said them at a quarter to eight and he said them at the same speed, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and twenty-second time running.

---

And nobody asked him anything, and nobody asked him why he had come back to the wall, and nobody asked him what the four are a figure of, and nobody asked him to say them again a different way, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stood about where they were and let him put his hand flat on that stone and leave it there a while, and about four of them have said since that a man put his hand flat on a stone at about four o'clock this morning and left it there longer than he usually leaves it, and that about four of them have not said how anybody in that yard would know that from where they were standing.

---

And then the clerk read the morning out loud, and she read it before she wrote anything, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nineteen people at the foot of that bank, and she read it once.

"The two hundred and thirty-third morning," she said. "No reason was said out loud in this yard this morning and nothing is going on the back of that book, and that is the whole of it."

---

And she wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it on a page that has forty-six lines on it and has not got a forty-seventh, and nobody thanked the man of fifty-six for saying the four off that wall twice and nobody thanked the clerk.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and the boards went down the way they go down, and the second table stood at the foot of that bank where it stands and the near rail was on the near side of it.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the man of fifty-six has now gone at the foot of that wall twice a morning for the six hundred and seventy-fourth morning and that nobody has ever asked him one thing about either time, and that about four of them have not said what a man does with two goes a morning that nobody watches the end of.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries forty-six lines and it did not carry a forty-seventh this morning and not one of the forty-six is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and forty-eight days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and forty-eight days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a man who says four figures off a wall twice and then says he does not know what any of them is for is not a man who has said a reason, and the page at the back of that lot book is at forty-six lines and the lane count is thirty and it did not move.
