# Chapter 0934: The TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTEENTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Of About Twenty-Nine Who Drives The Cart Came Through The Gap In The Bank And Did Not Stop And Nobody In That Yard Said One Thing About It And The Clerk Said Nothing Out Loud To Anybody From About Ten Until About Twelve And The Page At The Back Carries Forty-Four Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and seventy-three days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and thirty-two. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and forty-eight days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and sixty-two days. Eight hundred and twenty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and thirty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and forty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and twelve days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and fifteen days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and fifty-one days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and ninety-one marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and seventy-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and forty-eight mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and forty-seven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and ninety-eight mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and eighteen, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and seventeenth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries forty-four lines and it did not carry a forty-fifth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and fifty-ninth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and seventy-third night of that run, having slept on five hundred and seventy-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and sixty-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the seventy-fourth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and thirty-three days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning, the morning was read out loud before it was written, and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them and put his hand flat on the stone afterwards and left it there a while, and nobody said one word to him.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him. The turner had his wheel going with a piece of ash in it. The man who digs loam was at the north end. The man of about thirty-two put his table up about four feet along from the second table and put his apron on and did not look at anybody. The man of about thirty-four stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table and said nothing out loud at all this morning.

---

The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine and went to the near end of the first table and worked at the end of it with his short-handled thing, and the clerk sat at about four feet off the near end of the second table with the front of that book open and did not say anything out loud to anybody.

---

And at about half past nine the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came through the gap in the bank with a load of stone on it and the shafts up on his hip, and he slowed in the gap the way he slows in a gap, and he did not stop, and he went through that yard and out the far side of it with the stone rattling behind him, and he did not go up the lane.

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at him go through and then went on with what they were doing, and nobody in that yard said one word about it, not the man of thirty-eight, not the turner, not the clerk, not the man of about thirty-four, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a cart went through that yard this morning at about half past nine and nineteen people watched it go and not one of them said a word about it afterwards, and that about four of them have not said what a yard does with a thing it has stopped talking about.

---

The turner's wheel did not change speed. The man who digs loam opened the same wet strip at the north end and put the spade in and out of the same place. The man of about thirty-two went along the top of his own table with the cloth. The man of about thirty-four looked at the boards four times and once at the far side of the gap, where the cart had gone, and then he looked at the boards again.

---

And from about ten until about twelve the clerk said nothing out loud to anybody. About nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank and stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table and looked at the front of that book and one of them said *is there anything on it* and the clerk looked up and did not answer and the one of them went away again, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk who said nothing for two hours this morning answered a question with her face, and that about four of them have not said what was on the clerk's face.

---

And the man of thirty-eight worked at the end of his table through all of it, and at about twenty past eleven he turned round and looked at the gap in the bank and then went back to the end of his table, and nobody saw him do it except about four people who have said since that he looked at the gap in that bank about four hours after a cart went through it, and that about four of them have not said what a man looks at.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and seventh time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And then the clerk read the morning out loud, and she read it before she wrote anything, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nineteen people at the foot of that bank, and she read it once.

"The two hundred and eighteenth morning," she said. "A man went through this yard this morning and did not stop, and no reason was said out loud in this yard this morning, and nothing is going on the back of that book, and that is the whole of it."

---

And she wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it on a page that has forty-four lines on it and has not got a forty-fifth, and nobody thanked anybody for anything this morning.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and the boards went down the way they go down.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a cart came through that yard on a morning earlier in this stretch and stopped in it and said the only thing it had, and that this morning it came through and did not stop, and that nobody in that yard said anything about the difference on either morning, and that about four of them have not said what the difference was.

The page at the back of that lot book carries forty-four lines and it did not carry a forty-fifth this morning and not one of the forty-four is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and thirty-three days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and thirty-three days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a man who comes through a yard and does not stop is not a man who has said why.
