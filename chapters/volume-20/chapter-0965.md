# Chapter 0965: The TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-NINTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Of About Thirty-Two Says One Thing Out Loud Once About His Own Table And Nobody In That Yard Answers Him And The Page At The Back Carries Fifty-Two Lines And It Did Not Move And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and sixty-three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and seventy-nine days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and ninety-three days. Eight hundred and fifty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and sixty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and seventy-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and forty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and forty-six days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and eighty-two days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and twenty-two marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four.

Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything. No reason was said out loud in that yard this morning, so the page at the back did not move, and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, the count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move, and the five things are still not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and seventy-nine mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and seventy-eight. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and twenty-nine mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and forty-nine, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and forty-eighth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries fifty-two lines and it did not carry a fifty-third this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and ninetieth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his six hundred and fourth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and ninety-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the one hundred and fifth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and sixty-four days, and the page at the back took no line this morning. The morning was read out loud once before it was written. Nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard the whole of it.

The light found the eleven feet and the boards went up, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and put his hand flat on the stone afterwards and left it there while the rest of that yard came down the bank behind him.

---

At about ten to eight the boy said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and said no figure after it and said out loud that he was saying no figure. He said it to the boards and not to the man who was counting marks along the edge of the second table, and the man counting them did not look up.

---

The man of about thirty-two set his own table up about four feet along from the second table, squared it with the back of his heel, and put his apron on. That table of his own has now stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and forty-eighth morning, and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail.

---

And at about half past ten, standing at the near end of his own table with the cloth in his hand, he said it out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, and he said it to the table and not to anybody.

"This is the fifth table I have made this year and I set it up this morning on the same ground I set it up on yesterday," he said. "I square it with the heel of my boot and I go along the top of it with a cloth, and that is what I do with it. Nobody asked me what I do with it. There is nothing in it."

---

And nobody in that yard answered him. Nobody asked him what he meant by the fifth table, nobody asked him how long it takes him to make one, nobody said good morning to him at that point, and nobody thanked him, and the turner's wheel went on turning and the man who digs loam went on at the north end and the man of thirty-eight did not look over from the first table.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man said a thing about a table of his own and nobody asked him one word back, and that about four of them have said he said it the way a man says the price of something, and that about four of them have not said what a price gets said out loud for at the bottom of a bank.

At about one, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, the man of thirty-eight looked up. He looked up for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, at about four feet off the near end of that other table and nobody else, and then he put his eyes back on the piece in his hand and carried on with it.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man working at the near end of that table looked up at about one this morning for the length of a count, and that about four of them have not said what he looked up at, and that about four of them did not see him do it and had it from somebody else.

---

The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with her scale in a piece of cloth and set it up about four feet off the near end of the second table. She weighed a bar of iron out of a basket, wrote the figure on the edge of a board of her own with a bit of chalk, said nothing to anybody, and went up that bank at about one.

---

The number the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said went out at about half past one into about four hundred yards of air, and nobody in that yard wrote it down. About four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him, all four of them at once, and none of them said it the way he had said it. He put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and left it lying there until the turner had gone a full wheel round, and then went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall, and he was not asked one question, which is the one hundred and fifth morning running of that.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and thirty-eighth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And the clerk read the morning out loud, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, once, and about nineteen people at the foot of that bank heard every word of it, and it was read before she wrote.

"The two hundred and forty-ninth morning," she said. "Nothing went on the back of that book this morning. Something was said out loud in this yard this morning and I am not going to say anything about it, because I have not been asked to and because I have nothing to say about it."

---

Then she wrote the morning under the last line on that page in her own hand and did not take a line for it. About nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it on a page that carries fifty-two lines and has not got a fifty-third.

---

By about half past four the light had gone off the boards, and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard had been said by about four people out of about nineteen. About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up. The boards went down the way they go down, and the second table stood where it stands and the near rail was on the near side of it.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man put a thing out loud in that yard this morning and that about four of them have said it was about a table and about nothing else, and that about four of them have not said what a yard does with a thing a man says about his own work, and that about four of them have said the page at the back of that book is where it was this morning, and that about four of them have not said whether anybody at those boards thinks the two of those go together.

The page at the back of that lot book carries fifty-two lines and it did not carry a fifty-third this morning and not one of the fifty-two is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and sixty-four days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and sixty-four days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning, no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back did not move, and a man who puts tables up said one thing about his own table this morning and nobody has to write it down and nobody has to be thanked for it either, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a man looking up at about one is not an asking and is not an answer, and the page at the back of that lot book is at fifty-two lines and the lane count is thirty and it did not move.
