# Chapter 0962: The TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIXTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Woman Of About Fifty-Two Comes Down That Bank And Says One Thing Out Loud Once About Herself And Nobody In That Yard Answers Her And The Page At The Back Carries Fifty-Two Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and sixty. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and seventy-six days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and ninety days. Eight hundred and fifty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and sixty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and seventy days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and forty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and forty-three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and seventy-nine days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and nineteen marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four.

Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything. One reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back of that lot book took one line for it, and the line carries the morning and not the reason. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, the count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move, and the five things are still not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and seventy-six mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and seventy-five. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and twenty-six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and forty-six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and forty-fifth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries fifty-two lines and it did not carry a fifty-third this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and eighty-seventh of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his six hundred and first night of that run, having slept on six hundred of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and ninety-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the one hundred and second morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and sixty-one days, and the page at the back took one line this morning and the morning was read out loud once before it was written. Nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard that and nothing else.

---

The light found the eleven feet earlier than it had the morning before, and the boards went up on it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall, got all four of them, laid his hand flat on the stone afterwards and left it there a while, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything about any of it.

---

At about ten to eight the boy said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said out loud that he was saying no figure, and then he went and stood at the end of the first table with his back to the boards and looked at the ground at his feet.

---

The woman of about fifty-two came down that bank at about a quarter to ten with nothing in her hands at all. She stopped about nine feet back from those boards in about four feet of sun and stood there facing the tables with her arms down, the way a woman stands when she has walked down a hill and has not come down it for anything in particular.

And at about ten she said it out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, standing where she was standing, and she said it to the tables and not to anybody.

"I have come down that bank every morning since before the frost came off the ground and I have not stopped coming down it," she said. "I am not coming down it for anybody in this yard and I am not going to start. I am going up it again in a minute and that is all of it."

---

And nobody in that yard answered her, and nobody asked her to say it a second time, and nobody asked her where she had come down that bank from, and nobody thanked her, and the turner's wheel went on turning and the man who digs loam went on opening the same wet strip at the north end and the man of fifty-six did not turn round.

---

And at about half past ten a man who works at the far end of the first table said good morning to her across about nine feet of ground. She did not answer him. She stood in the sun where she was standing for another half hour and then went up that bank, and she did not say good morning to anybody either.

---

The man of thirty-eight was at about four feet off the near end of the first table from about half past nine and he was about nine feet off her when she said it. He did not stop, he did not put the piece down, and he did not look over, and he said nothing out loud at all this morning.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman said a thing out loud at the foot of that bank and a man working about nine feet off her carried on with the piece in his hand, and that about four of them have not said whether he heard her, and that about four of them have said she came down that bank with nothing in her hands this morning the same as she came down it with nothing in her hands the morning before.

---

That second table was moved about a foot further along the ground at about nine by two men who did not speak to each other while they did it, and the near rail went with it on the near side and came to rest against the same ground. The man at the end of that table put his own boot on the wedge under the leg that rocks, left it there a while and sat down again.

---

The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with her scale in a piece of cloth and set it up about four feet off the near end of the second table. She weighed two bars of iron out of a basket, wrote the figure on the edge of a board of her own with a bit of chalk, said nothing to anybody, and did not stay past about one.

---

The number the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said went out at about half past one into about four hundred yards of air with nothing in front of it, and nobody in that yard wrote it down. About four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other. He put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards, said it a second time to himself where nobody could hear it, and then went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall, and he was not asked one question, which is the one hundred and second morning running of that.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and thirty-fifth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk read the morning out loud. She read it once, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and she read it before she wrote anything on it, and about nineteen people at the foot of that bank heard all of it.

"The two hundred and forty-sixth morning," she said. "One reason was said out loud in this yard this morning and it is going on the back of that book on one line, and the line carries the morning and not what was said. That is the whole of it and there is nothing else on that page this morning."

---

And she wrote one line on the page at the back of that lot book, in her own hand, under the last line, and it carried the morning and it did not carry what the woman of about fifty-two had said. About nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it, and nobody in that yard said the sentence again afterwards to see whether it came out the same way.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen. About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it, and about four of them put a hand on the edge of those boards on the way past. The boards went down the way they go down, the second table stood where it stands and the near rail was on the near side of it.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman said one thing out loud at the foot of that bank this morning and that about four of them have not said what she will do with it, and that about four of them have said she has been coming down that bank a great many mornings and has never once said anything at the bottom of it, and that about four of them have not said what changed this morning and about four of them have not said that anything did.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries fifty-two lines and not one of the fifty-two is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and one line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and sixty-one days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and sixty-one days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning, one reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and one line was written on that page carrying the morning and not the reason, and the second space on that form was empty the whole morning and the woman who could have put something in it went up that bank with nothing in her hands, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a line on that page is not a payment of the fifth of the five and is not an answer to anybody, and the page at the back of that lot book is at fifty-two lines and the lane count is thirty and it did not move.
