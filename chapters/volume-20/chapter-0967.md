# Chapter 0967: The TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIRST Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month An Appointed Administrator Fifty-Two Nineteen Years Appointed Comes Down That Bank Once Says One Reason Out Loud Once Is Answered With No And Does Not Come Back Down It And The Page At The Back Carries Fifty-Three Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and sixty-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and eighty-one days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and ninety-five days. Eight hundred and fifty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and seventy days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and seventy-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and forty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and forty-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and eighty-four days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and twenty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and ten marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four.

Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning. One reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back of that lot book took one line for it, and the line carries the morning and not the reason. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, the count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move, and the five things are still not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and eighty-one mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and eighty. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and thirty-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and fifty-one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and fiftieth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries fifty-three lines and it did not carry a fifty-fourth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and ninety-second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his six hundred and sixth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and ninety-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the one hundred and seventh morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and sixty-six days, and the page at the back took one line this morning and the morning was read out loud once before it was written. Nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and nobody said anything back.

The light came onto the eleven feet and the boards went up, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and laid his hand flat on the stone afterwards, and nobody said one word to him about any of it.

---

At about ten to eight the boy said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure and said out loud that he was saying no figure, and went and stood at the end of the first table.

---

At about ten an appointed administrator, fifty-two, nineteen years appointed, the third of the nine oldest keys, came down that bank with nothing in her hands at all. Nobody had told her anything before she got there and nobody met her at the bottom of it. About nine people at the top of eleven feet looked over at her and about four of them said good morning to her and she said good morning back to those four.

And then she stopped about four feet off the near end of the second table and said one question out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, and she said it to the boards.

"Is anybody going to be asked anything this morning?"

---

And a man who works at the far end of the first table said, in the ordinary voice, "No." And nobody said anything else. Nobody put her right and nobody said a word over his, and nobody asked her another thing, and the man who said no went back to the far end of that table and picked his piece up off it.

---

And at about twenty past ten she said one reason out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, standing where she was standing, and she said it to the boards and not to anybody.

"I have been an appointed administrator for nineteen years and I am the third of the nine oldest keys there are," she said. "For nineteen years I have gone into rooms I was sent to and read what was written down in them and gone out again, and nobody has ever sent me into this yard. I came down that bank this morning to stand at the bottom of it, and that is the whole of what I came down it for, and I am going up it again in a minute."

---

And nobody in that yard answered her, and nobody asked her a second thing about any part of it, and nobody thanked her, and nobody gave her a chair or a cloth or a place to stand other than the ground she was standing on, and the second space on that form was empty in front of her for as long as she was at those boards and nobody asked her to put anything in it.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman with keys came down that bank and said two things out loud and then went up it again, and that about four of them have not said what a person does to a yard by coming down it once, and that about four of them have said she did not come back.

---

She went up that bank at about half past ten. Nobody went after her with anything and nobody went up the bank with her at the same time as she went up it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her go up it and then went back to what they had been doing.

---

And the man who had said no at the far end of the first table worked at that table for the rest of this morning and said nothing out loud at all again after about ten. And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has said a word out loud at those boards on a great many mornings said one word this morning and then did not say anything else for the rest of the morning, and that about four of them have not said what the one word was for.

---

The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with her scale in a piece of cloth and set it up about four feet off the near end of the second table. She weighed two bars out of a basket, wrote the figures on the edge of a board of her own, said nothing to anybody, and went up that bank at about one.

---

The number the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said went out at about half past one into about four hundred yards of air, and two people on the stones at the foot of that wall heard it, and nobody in that yard wrote it down. About four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him, two of them a half count behind the other two. He put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall, and he was not asked one question, which is the one hundred and seventh morning running of that.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and fortieth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And the clerk read that morning out loud in the ordinary voice, once, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and about nineteen people at the foot of that bank heard every word of it before she wrote any of it down.

"The two hundred and fifty-first morning," she said. "One reason was said out loud in this yard this morning and one line goes on the back of that book, and the line carries the morning and not what was said. Nobody has been thanked and nobody has been given anything this morning and that is the whole of it."

---

And she wrote one line on the page at the back of that lot book, in her own hand, under the last line, and it carried the morning and it did not carry what the woman with the keys had said, and not one of the fifty-three lines on that page is in the lot book.

---

At about half past four the light came off the boards, and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen, and nobody said it twice. About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up. The boards went down the way they go down, and the second table stood where it stands and the near rail was on the near side of it.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that nineteen years is a long time to go into rooms nobody sent you into, and that about four of them have not said what anybody is supposed to do at the bottom of a bank on the strength of it, and that about four of them have said nobody at those boards offered her one thing and nobody at the top of that lane has offered anybody in that yard one thing this morning.

The page at the back of that lot book carries fifty-three lines and not one of the fifty-three is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and one line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and sixty-six days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and sixty-six days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning, one reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and one line was written on that page carrying the morning and not the reason, and an appointed administrator with the third of the nine oldest keys was at the bottom of that bank for about half an hour and was not thanked and was not given anything and was not asked a second thing, and a man of thirty-eight was asked nothing this morning by anybody including her, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a woman going up a hill she came down is not a page coming down that lane, and the page at the back of that lot book is at fifty-three lines and the lane count is thirty and it did not move.
