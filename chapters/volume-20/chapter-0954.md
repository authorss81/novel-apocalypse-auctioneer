# Chapter 0954: The TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Woman Of About Fifty-Two Came Down That Bank And Stood In The Sun And Nobody Said Anything To Her And Nobody Says A Reason And Nobody Says No And The Page At The Back Carries Forty-Eight Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and ninety-three days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and fifty-two. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and sixty-eight days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and eighty-two days. Eight hundred and forty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and fifty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and sixty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood.

Six hundred and thirty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and thirty-five days back and no night has been named. Four hundred miles off, a body is five hundred and seventy-one days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and eleven marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and ninety-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and sixty-eight mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and sixty-seven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and eighteen mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and thirty-eight, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and thirty-seventh morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries forty-eight lines and it did not carry a forty-ninth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and seventy-ninth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and ninety-third night of that run, having slept on five hundred and ninety-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and eighty-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the ninety-fourth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and fifty-three days, and the page at the back did not take a line this morning. The morning was read out loud before it was written. Nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards and said no figure after it, and he said out loud that there was no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and nothing else.

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them and put his hand flat on the stone afterwards and left it there a while, and the sun came over the top of that bank a good while after that and laid about four feet of itself along the ground at the foot of it.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him. The turner had his wheel going with a piece of ash in it. The man who digs loam was at the north end. The man of about thirty-two put his table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel and put his apron on and did not look at anybody.

---

The man of about thirty-four stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with his hands behind his back. The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and went to about four feet off the near end of the first table and worked at the end of it, and the piece he was on sat out of the sun and he moved it back into it without saying anything.

---

And at about a quarter to eleven the woman of about fifty-two came down that bank with nothing in her hands at all, and she came down it the way a person comes down a bank when she is not carrying anything, and she stopped about nine feet back from the boards in the middle of the four feet of sun that had come over the top of the bank, and she stood there.

---

And she stood in that sun for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall and then she stood there for a good deal longer than that, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a woman of about fifty-two stand in about four feet of sun at the foot of that bank with her hands at her sides and her bag not with her, and not one of them said one word to her.

---

Nobody asked her what she was standing there for and nobody asked her how long she meant to stand in it and nobody said good morning to her, and the turner's wheel went on turning and the man who digs loam went on shovelling and the man of thirty-eight worked at the end of his own table about nine feet of ground off her and neither of them looked over, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman came down that bank this morning with nothing in her hands and stood in the sun and was not spoken to by anybody in that yard, and that about four of them have not said what a person stands in the sun for when nothing is going on.

---

There is a form on those boards at about four feet off the near end of the first table, under the clean page, and the second space on it was empty the whole of this morning, and she stood about nine feet back from those boards with her back to the slope and she did not look at that form, and nobody asked her to fill it and nobody asked anybody else to fill it.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman stood about nine feet back from those boards for the better part of a morning and there was a piece of paper lying on them the whole time, and that about four of them have not said whether she knew it was there, and that about four of them have not said why a person comes down a bank with nothing in her hands when she came down it last with a net and a shuttle.

---

She went back up that bank at about twenty past two and she did not stop on the way and nobody said one word to her on the way up either, and the man at the end of the second table got up off his stool and went round the end of that table and looked at the wedge under the leg that rocks and left it alone and sat down again.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall, and he was not asked one question, which is the ninety-fourth morning running of that.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and twenty-seventh time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

When the light had gone off the boards the clerk read that morning out loud, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and about nineteen people at the foot of that bank heard it, and she read it once and she read it before she wrote anything.

"The two hundred and thirty-eighth morning," she said. "No reason was said out loud in this yard this morning and nothing is going on the back of that book, and nobody asked her for anything else."

And she wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it on a page that has forty-eight lines on it and has not got a forty-ninth.

At about half past four the light went off the boards, and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen. About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up. The boards went down the way they go down. The second table stood at the foot of that bank where it stands, and the near rail was on the near side of it.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the woman of about fifty-two came down that bank with nothing in her hands this morning and went back up it with nothing in her hands and said nothing to anybody and was not spoken to by anybody, and that about four of them have said the man at the near end of the first table worked about nine feet of ground off her for two hours and did not turn round, and that about four of them have not said what either of the two of them was doing there, and that about four of them have not said whether two people in one yard who say nothing to each other are saying something.

The page at the back of that lot book carries forty-eight lines and it did not carry a forty-ninth this morning and not one of the forty-eight is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and fifty-three days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and fifty-three days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a woman who comes down that bank and stands in the sun and says nothing is not a woman who has said a reason, and the page at the back of that lot book is at forty-eight lines and the lane count is thirty and it did not move.
