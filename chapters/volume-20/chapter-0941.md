# Chapter 0941: The TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FIFTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Ten The Man Of About Thirty-Four Said One Thing Out Loud About The Morning Before And Nobody Answered Him And The Page At The Back Carries Forty-Five Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and eighty days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and thirty-nine. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and fifty-five days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and sixty-nine days. Eight hundred and thirty days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and forty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and forty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and nineteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and twenty-two days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and fifty-eight days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and ninety-eight marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and eighty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and fifty-five mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and fifty-four. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and five mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and twenty-five, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and twenty-fourth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries forty-five lines and it did not carry a forty-sixth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and sixty-sixth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and eightieth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and seventy-nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and seventieth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the eighty-first morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and forty days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning was read out loud before it was written, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them and put his hand flat on the stone afterwards and left it there a while, and nobody said one word to him.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said a figure out loud, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved.

"Seven hundred and six," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them said it after him out of step with each other, and nobody asked him what it was a figure of, and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen has said a figure out loud on some of the mornings of this stretch and has said nothing out loud on others and has said so out loud when he has said nothing, and that about four of them have not said why he keeps the two apart.

---

The turner had his wheel going with a piece of ash in it and stopped it twice to pick the ash out with the point of his own knife. The man who digs loam was at the north end on the same strip and had a barrow of loam standing on edge beside him that he was not going to move until the middle of the morning. The man of about thirty-two put his table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel and put his apron on and did not look at anybody.

---

The man of about thirty-four stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table from about nine with his hands behind his back and said nothing out loud for the first hour of it. The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing, the way he comes down, and went to about four feet off the near end of the first table and worked at the end of it, and the two of them were about four hundred yards apart and neither of them went over to the other.

---

And at about ten the man of about thirty-four said it, once, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and he said it to the boards and not to anybody, and what he said was about the morning before this one and not about the work he does with his hands.

"I said a thing out loud in this yard on the two hundred and twenty-first morning," he said, "and I have not said it since and I am not going to say it again. Nobody has asked me for it and I am not asking anybody for anything this morning. What I have been doing since the light came up is standing at this table thinking about that morning and not about this one, and I have got no better at it than I was yesterday, and that is the whole of what I have got this morning."

---

And his hands came down off his back and went into his pockets and stayed there. Nobody in that yard answered him. Nobody asked him to say it again and nobody asked him what the morning before this one was to him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stood in that yard and not one of them opened their mouth, and the turner's wheel went on turning and the man who digs loam went on shovelling and the man of about thirty-two went along the top of his own table with the cloth and the clerk's pen did not move.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man said one thing out loud in that yard this morning about the morning behind this one and about nothing else, and that about four of them have said that a man who has said a thing once in that yard and has had a clerk write the morning down has spent the days since going over the writing of it and not over the saying of it, and that about four of them have not said how a man stops going over it.

---

The man of thirty-eight worked at the end of his own table through all of it and did not look over at the second table once, and he said nothing out loud at all this morning from about nine until about four, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the two men in that yard who stand about four hundred yards apart have between them now said three things out loud between them, and that about four of them have not said what the distance is for.

---

The man at the end of the second table got up off his stool at about eleven, went round the end of that table to put a wedge under the leg that rocks, came back and sat down again and said nothing out loud at all this morning. About four people at the top of eleven feet watched a man put a wedge under a table leg and did not go and help him, and about four of them have said since that the leg has rocked since before the middle of the winter and that about four of them have not said why it is left to the man who sits at that end.

---

At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale came down that bank with her scale in a piece of cloth and set it up at about four feet off the near end of the second table and weighed a bar of iron out of a basket and wrote the figure on the edge of a board of her own with a bit of chalk and said nothing to anybody, and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall, and he was not asked one question, which is the eighty-first morning running of that.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and fourteenth time running, and nobody asked him anything, and the man of thirty-eight was at the near end of the first table when he said it and did not turn round.

---

And then the clerk read the morning out loud, and she read it before she wrote anything, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nineteen people at the foot of that bank, and she read it once.

"The two hundred and twenty-fifth morning," she said. "No reason was said out loud in this yard this morning and nothing is going on the back of that book, and that is the whole of it."

---

And she wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it on a page that has forty-five lines on it and has not got a forty-sixth, and nobody thanked the man who said the thing about the morning before this one and nobody thanked anybody for anything on any of these mornings.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and the boards went down the way they go down, and the second table stood at the foot of that bank where it stands and the near rail was on the near side of it.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man of about thirty-four has now said a thing out loud in that yard twice in twenty mornings and both of the things were about himself, and that about four of them have said the page at the back of that book has stood at forty-five lines for four mornings and has not got a forty-sixth, and that about four of them have not said what a yard is at after a morning on which the only thing anybody said out loud was about the morning before.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries forty-five lines and it did not carry a forty-sixth this morning and not one of the forty-five is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and forty days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and forty days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a man who says a thing about the morning behind him is not a man who has said why, and the page at the back of that lot book is at forty-five lines and the lane count is thirty and it did not move.
