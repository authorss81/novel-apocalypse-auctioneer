# Chapter 0955: The TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-NINTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Half Past Ten A Man Who Was Not Standing Next To A Morning Said A Reason Out Loud About That Morning And One Line Carries The Morning And Not That And Nobody In That Yard Can Find Out Afterwards Whether It Was So And The Page At The Back Carries Forty-Nine Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and ninety-four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and fifty-three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and sixty-nine days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and eighty-three days. Eight hundred and forty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open.

Five hundred and fifty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and sixty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and thirty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and thirty-six days back and no night has ever been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and seventy-two days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and twelve marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and ninety-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four.

Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning. One reason was said out loud in that yard this morning, and one line went on the page at the back of that lot book for it, and that line carries the morning and not the reason. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move, and the five things are still not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and sixty-nine mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and sixty-eight. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and nineteen mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and thirty-nine, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and thirty-eighth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries forty-nine lines and it did not carry a fiftieth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and eightieth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and ninety-fourth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and ninety-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and eighty-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the ninety-fifth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

One hundred and fifty-four days is what the record has stood. The page at the back took one line this morning, and the morning was read out loud before it was written. Nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book. The second space on that form is still empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at about ten to eight and said no figure after it, and he said out loud that there was no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

The boards went up on the light, once it had found the eleven feet, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got them all, laid his hand flat on the stone afterwards and left it there a while, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about it.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him. The turner had his wheel going and had a tin of oil stood up on the end of his own bench where he could reach it without stopping. The man who digs loam was at the north end. The man of about thirty-two put his table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel and put his apron on.

---

The man of about thirty-four stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with his hands behind his back and said nothing out loud at all this morning. The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and worked at about four feet off the near end of the first table and said nothing out loud at all this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man has been at the near end of that table for the whole of a morning and has not looked up the bank once.

---

And at about half past ten the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came through the gap in that bank with a load on the cart and the shafts up on his hip, and he came into the gap the way he comes into a gap, and he stopped, and the cart stood still in that gap for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

---

And at about half past ten he said it, once, in the ordinary voice, from where he stood with the shafts against his hip, in about nineteen people's hearing, and what he said was about a morning he was not standing next to and not about the work he does with his hands.

"I was not in this yard on the morning of the one hundred and ninety-first morning," he said. "I am away from this yard three mornings out of ten and I know what that yard is like on the morning you are not in it. I have got one thing to say about that morning and I am saying it now and once, and then I am taking my load out the far side. I am not going to say it a second time and I am not going to come back in about it."

---

And nobody in that yard asked him whether it was so, and nobody asked him how he knew what that morning was, and nobody answered him, and nobody thanked him for stopping, and the turner's wheel went on turning and the man who digs loam went on shovelling and the man of thirty-eight went on working at the end of his own table and did not turn round, and the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall again at about four and did not look over at the gap.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a line can go on the page at the back of that book from a man standing in a gap in a bank saying a thing about a morning he was not standing next to, and that the morning is the only thing the line carries, and that nobody in this yard can find out afterwards whether it was so.

---

And nobody in that yard answered that, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man drove out of the far side of that yard at about a quarter to eleven this morning and did not go up that lane, and that about four of them have not said what he went out of that yard to do, and that about four of them have not said whether the man at the near end of the first table heard what he said.

---

The woman who keeps a scale was at the foot of that bank at about half past twelve, her scale in a piece of cloth, set up about four feet off the near end of the second table. She weighed two bars of iron out of a basket, put the figure on the edge of a board of her own in chalk, and said nothing to anybody.

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number into that yard at about half past one, into about four hundred yards of air, and nobody wrote it down. About four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other. He put his hand flat on his own coat and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and there was no sentence in front of that number.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall, and he was not asked one question, which is the ninety-fifth morning running of that.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and twenty-eighth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And the clerk read that morning out loud afterwards, in the ordinary voice, standing at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and about nineteen people at the foot of that bank heard her, and she read it once and she read it before she wrote anything.

"The two hundred and thirty-ninth morning," she said. "One reason was said out loud in this yard this morning and it is being written on the page at the back of that book, and the reason is not going on it, and that is what went on that page this morning."

---

And she wrote one line on the page at the back of that lot book, in her own hand, and the line carries the morning and not the reason, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk write one line for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and that page carries forty-nine lines and not one of the forty-nine is in the lot book and not one of them says what the man with the cart said in the gap at about half past ten.

---

Nobody thanked the man who said the reason and nobody thanked the clerk who wrote the line. At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and the boards went down the way they go down.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the page at the back of that lot book has carried forty-nine lines since yesterday afternoon and that the forty-ninth carries the morning and not the reason, and that about four of them have said the man who said the reason this morning was not standing next to the morning he said it about, and that about four of them have not said what a line on that page is going to be worth to anybody in this basin.

The page at the back of that lot book carries forty-nine lines and it did not carry a fiftieth this morning and not one of the forty-nine is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and fifty-four days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and fifty-four days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and one reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and one line was written on that page carrying the morning and not the reason and not the question, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a man saying a thing out loud about a morning he was not standing next to has said a reason and not a report, and the page at the back of that lot book is at forty-nine lines and the lane count is thirty and it did not move.
