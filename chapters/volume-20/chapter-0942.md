# Chapter 0942: The TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SIXTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of Thirty-Eight Went Up That Lane In The Afternoon With Nothing In His Hand And Came Back Down It With Nothing In His Hand And Said He Did Not Ask On Purpose And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move And The Page At The Back Carries Forty-Five Lines

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and eighty-one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and forty. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and fifty-six days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and seventy days. Eight hundred and thirty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and forty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and fifty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and twenty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and twenty-three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and fifty-nine days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and ninety-nine marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and eighty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and fifty-six mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and fifty-five. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and twenty-six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and twenty-fifth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries forty-five lines and it did not carry a forty-sixth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and sixty-seventh of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and eighty-first night of that run, having slept on five hundred and eighty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and seventy-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the eighty-second morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and forty-one days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning was read out loud before it was written, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them and put his hand flat on the stone afterwards and left it there a while, and nobody said one word to him.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and nobody said anything back to him. The turner had his wheel going. The man who digs loam was at the north end. The man of about thirty-two put his table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel and put his apron on.

---

The man of about thirty-four stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table from about nine and said nothing out loud at all this morning. The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and went to about four feet off the near end of the first table and worked at the end of it, and he worked at it without stopping until about one, which is longer than he has worked at it in one stretch for a good while, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that about four of them noticed the length of it and about four of them have not said why they noticed.

---

At about one he put his hand down off the table top and wiped it on his coat and went up the lane with nothing in his hand, and he went up it at the pace a man goes up a hill when he has not decided to come back down it in any particular hurry, and nobody at the top of that lane offered him anything and he did not ask for anything, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a man go up that hill and about four of them have said since that about four people in that yard have gone up that hill in three months and about four of them have not said what a person goes up it for.

---

And while he was gone the turner worked his wheel through a bar of iron and the man who digs loam put three barrowloads of loam on the north strip and stood on the spade with his boot, and the man of about thirty-two went along the top of his own table with the cloth twice and once with his own thumb along a groove, and the woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with her scale in a piece of cloth and weighed a bar of iron out of a basket and wrote the figure on the edge of a board of her own with a bit of chalk and said nothing to anybody.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it, and he looked up that lane once while he was saying it and about four of them have said since that about four people in that yard looked up that lane at the same moment this morning.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall, and he was not asked one question, which is the eighty-second morning running of that.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and fifteenth time running, and nobody asked him anything, and nobody at the foot of that bank said anything about the man who was up that lane.

---

And then the clerk read the morning out loud, and she read it before she wrote anything, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nineteen people at the foot of that bank, and she read it once.

"The two hundred and twenty-sixth morning," she said. "No reason was said out loud in this yard this morning and nothing is going on the back of that book, and that is the whole of it."

---

And she wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it on a page that has forty-five lines on it and has not got a forty-sixth, and nobody thanked the clerk and nobody thanked the man who was not in that yard.

---

At about half past four, while the boards were coming down, the man of thirty-eight came back down that hill from the top of that lane with his hands showing and nothing in either of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet saw him come down it, and he stopped at the bottom of the bank where the light had just gone off the boards and said it once, in the ordinary voice, in about nine people's hearing, and he said it to the boards.

"I did not ask on purpose," he said. "There was nobody at the top of that lane to ask and there was nothing up there this afternoon to put in a hand, and I have come back down it with nothing in my hand, and I am not going to say a thing in this yard a second time because nine miles is nine miles in either direction."

---

And nobody in that yard answered him and nobody asked him why he had gone and nobody thanked him for going and nobody thanked him for coming back, and he was not given a bed and he was not given a wage and he was not given an office and he did not ask for any of the three, and the boards came down the way they come down and the second table stood at the foot of that bank where it stands, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man went up a hill with nothing and came down it with nothing and said so out loud at the bottom of it, and that about four of them have not said what the two trips were for.

---

And the page at the back of that lot book had the morning written on it in a clerk's own hand without a line on it before he said anything at the bottom of that bank, and it did not take a line after he had said it either, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the lane count is a count of pages and that a person going up that lane is not a page coming down it and that about four of them have not said whether the count was ever a count of anything else.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries forty-five lines and it did not carry a forty-sixth this morning and not one of the forty-five is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a person going up that lane and coming back down it is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and forty-one days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and forty-one days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a man who goes up that lane with nothing in his hand is not a man who has said why, and the page at the back of that lot book is at forty-five lines and the lane count is thirty and it did not move.
