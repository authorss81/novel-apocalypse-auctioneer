# Chapter 0970: The TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOURTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Woman Who Keeps A Scale Says One Reason Out Loud Once About Herself And Not About A Page And One Line Goes On The Page At The Back Carrying The Morning And Not The Reason And Nobody Was Thanked And The Page At The Back Carries Fifty-Four Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and sixty-eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and eighty-four days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and ninety-eight days. Eight hundred and fifty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and seventy-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and seventy-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and forty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and fifty-one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and eighty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four.

Nobody put anything to the man of fifty-six out loud this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything. One reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back of that lot book took one line for it, and the line carries the morning and not the reason. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, the count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move, and the five things are still not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and eighty-four mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and eighty-three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and thirty-four mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and fifty-four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and fifty-third morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries fifty-four lines and it did not carry a fifty-fifth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and ninety-fifth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his six hundred and ninth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and ninety-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the one hundred and tenth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and sixty-nine days, and the page at the back took one line this morning and the morning was read out loud once before it was written. Nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard the whole of that and nobody said anything back to him.

At about ten to eight the boy said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and said no figure after it and said out loud that he was saying no figure, and then he stood at the end of the first table with his hands behind him.

At about nine that second table was carried a foot further along the ground by two men who did not speak to each other while they did it, and the near rail came with it on the near side and came to rest against the same ground. The man at the end of that table got up, went round the end of it and set his own boot on the wedge under the leg that rocks, and sat down again.

---

The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and worked at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and he said nothing out loud at all this morning, and nobody in that yard asked him anything out loud at any point from the boards going up to the light going off them.

---

The man of about thirty-two put his own table up about four feet along from the second table, squared it with the back of his heel and went along the top of it with the cloth. The man who digs loam was at the north end and said nothing out loud to anybody all morning. The turner's wheel went on turning.

---

The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with her scale in a piece of cloth and set it up about four feet off the near end of the second table. She weighed a bar of iron out of a basket and wrote the figure on the edge of a board of her own with a bit of chalk and said nothing to anybody, and she went on standing at that scale.

---

And at about half past one she said it out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, standing at her own scale at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and she said it to the boards and not to anybody.

"Nine years I have stood at the foot of this bank with a scale in a cloth and I have weighed for every man at these tables and for men off the bank whose names I do not know," she said. "In nine years not one of them has ever told me what the thing was before I put it on. I am not asking anybody for anything. That is what I have got this morning and I am going to put the cloth over the scale."

And nobody in that yard answered her, and nobody asked her to say it a second time, and nobody thanked her, and nobody asked her what she weighs things for, and the turner's wheel went on turning and the man who digs loam went on at the north end and the man of thirty-eight worked at the near end of the first table and did not turn round.

He put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and waited about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and then went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it.

"The two hundred and fifty-fourth morning," she said. "One reason was said out loud in this yard this morning and one line goes on the back of that book, and the line carries the morning and not the reason. Nobody has been thanked for it and nobody has been given anything for it, and that is where this stretch of mornings ends."
