# Chapter 0963: The TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-SEVENTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month Nobody In That Yard Says A Reason And Nobody Says No And The Clerk Does Not Read That Morning Out Loud And Names It In Her Own Hand Without A Line And Nobody Asked Her Why And The Page At The Back Carries Fifty-Two Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and two days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and sixty-one. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and seventy-seven days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and ninety-one days. Eight hundred and fifty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and sixty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and seventy-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and forty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and forty-four days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and eighty days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and twenty marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four.

Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything. No reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and no reason was said no, so the page at the back did not move, and the clerk named the morning on it in her own hand without a line. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, the count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move, and the five things are still not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and seventy-seven mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and seventy-six. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and twenty-seven mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and forty-seven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and forty-sixth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries fifty-two lines and it did not carry a fifty-third this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and eighty-eighth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his six hundred and second night of that run, having slept on six hundred and one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and ninety-second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the one hundred and third morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and sixty-two days, and the page at the back took no line this morning. The morning was not read out loud. Nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard the count and about four of them heard him say that, and nobody said anything back to him.
