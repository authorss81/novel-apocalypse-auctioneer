# Chapter 0933: The TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTEENTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Of About Thirty-Four Asked Out Loud In About Nineteen People's Hearing Whether The Reason The Man Of Thirty-Eight Gave On The One Hundred And Eighty-Second Morning Is On The Page At The Back Of That Lot Book And He Said No And He Gave No Reason For The No And A No Takes No Line And The Clerk Read The Morning Out Loud And Wrote Nothing And The Page Carries Forty-Four Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and seventeen, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The record has stood one hundred and thirty-two days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning, the morning was read out loud before it was written, and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

"Is the reason you gave on the one hundred and eighty-second morning on the page at the back of that book?"

And the man of thirty-eight stopped working at the end of his table and looked at him across about four hundred yards of that yard, and he answered in the ordinary voice, once, and he answered with one word.

"No."
