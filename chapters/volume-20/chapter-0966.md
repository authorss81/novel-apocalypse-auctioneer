# Chapter 0966: The TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTIETH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Who Digs Loam Is At The North End And The Woman Of About Fifty-Two Is At About Four Feet Off The Near End Of The First Table And Neither Of Them Says Anything Out Loud This Morning And The Page At The Back Carries Fifty-Two Lines And It Did Not Move And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Seven hundred and five days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and sixty-four. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and eighty days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and ninety-four days. Eight hundred and fifty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and sixty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is.

Five hundred and seventy-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and forty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and forty-seven days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and eighty-three days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and twenty-three marks have been cut off that board, and there are five hundred and nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four.

Nobody put anything to the man of fifty-six out loud this morning, and nobody asked the man who digs loam anything out loud this morning either. No reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and nobody said no, so the page at the back did not move, and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, the count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move, and the five things are still not paid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and eighty mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and seventy-nine. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and thirty mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and fifty, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and forty-ninth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries fifty-two lines and it did not carry a fifty-third this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and ninety-first of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his six hundred and fifth night of that run, having slept on six hundred and four of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and ninety-fifth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the one hundred and sixth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and sixty-five days, and the page at the back took no line this morning. The morning was read out loud once before it was written. Nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, said no figure after it, and said out loud that he was saying no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The boards went up when the light had found the eleven feet, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, laid his hand flat on the stone afterwards, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything about it.

---

At about ten to eight the boy said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then said no figure and said out loud that he was saying no figure, and went to the near end of the first table and stood there looking down the ground.

At about nine the man who digs loam was at the north end with his barrow on edge beside him and opened the strip he has been opening, and he put the spade in and out of the same place all morning and said nothing out loud to anybody at any point of this morning.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man digs at the north end of that yard every morning and that about four of them have not said what is being dug for or what is going to be done with it, and that about four of them have said he has never once asked anybody at those boards for anything he could have got by digging.

---

At about eleven the woman of about fifty-two came down that bank with nothing in her hands at all, and she did not stop in the sun this morning. She came down to about four feet off the near end of the first table and stood there with her arms down, at the corner of it, and she stayed there until about half past one.

---

The man of thirty-eight was working at about four feet off the near end of that same table, nine feet along from the corner she was standing at. He did not stop. He did not look over. He said nothing out loud at all this morning, and nobody at that table said good morning to the woman and she said nothing to anybody.

And at about half past twelve she put her right hand out without looking down and moved a piece of iron that was lying on the corner of that table about nine inches along the top and set it down out of the way, and then she put her hand back down at her side and stood where she was standing.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman stood at the near end of that table for about as long as a man of fifty-six takes to say four figures off a wall, and that about four of them have said she moved one thing on it while she was standing there, and that about four of them have not said what it is like to have a person at the end of your table for an hour and a half and not saying anything at all.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the man at the north end and the woman at the near end of that table have never said a thing out loud in that yard on the same morning, and that about four of them have not said whether that is because one of them is never at the same end of the yard at the same hour as the other, and that about four of them have not said whether either of them has ever said anything out loud at all.

---

At about quarter past twelve the man of about thirty-two put his own table up about four feet along from the second table, squared it with the back of his heel, and went along the top of it with the cloth and said nothing. The second table was carried a foot further along that ground at about nine by two men who did not speak to each other while they did it, and the near rail went with it on the near side.

---

The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with her scale in a piece of cloth and set it up about four feet off the near end of the second table and stood at it a while before she put anything on it. She weighed two bars out of a basket, wrote the figures on the edge of a board of her own with a bit of chalk, said nothing to anybody, and went up that bank at about one.

---

The number the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said went out at about half past one into about four hundred yards of air, and nobody in that yard wrote it down. About four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other. He put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall, and he was not asked one question, which is the one hundred and sixth morning running of that.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and thirty-ninth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And the clerk read that morning out loud in the ordinary voice, once, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and about nineteen people at the foot of that bank heard every word of it, and it was read out before she put her pen into it.

"The two hundred and fiftieth morning," she said. "Nothing is going on the back of that book this morning and the morning goes on it in my hand. Read it in this voice or do not read it, it is the same page either way, and I would rather say that out loud than have the yard guess at it."

---

Then she wrote the morning under the last line on that page in her own hand and did not take a line for it. About nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it, and the second space on that form was empty the whole of this morning and nobody in that yard asked anybody to fill it.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen. About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up. The boards went down the way they go down, and the second table stood where it stands and the near rail was on the near side of it.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the woman of about fifty-two went up that bank at about half past one this afternoon and that about four of them have not said why she came down it at about eleven instead of at about a quarter to ten, and that about four of them have said it made no difference to anything that is written down anywhere, and that about four of them have not said what it made a difference to.

The page at the back of that lot book carries fifty-two lines and it did not carry a fifty-third this morning and not one of the fifty-two is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and sixty-five days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and sixty-five days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning, nobody asked the man who digs loam anything this morning, no reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back did not move, and a woman standing at the end of a table for an hour and a half saying nothing is not a line on that page and is not a payment of the fifth of the five, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and being at the same end of the yard as somebody for an hour is not an asking, and the page at the back of that lot book is at fifty-two lines and the lane count is thirty and it did not move.
