# Chapter 0935: The TWO HUNDRED AND NINETEENTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month About Nine People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Have Said Since That Nobody In That Yard Has Asked Anybody Anything Out Loud And Not One Person In That Scene Said It And The Man At The End Of The Second Table Said One Thing And Was Answered By Nobody And The Clerk Read The Morning Out Loud And Took No Line And The Page At The Back Carries Forty-Four Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and seventy-four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and thirty-three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and forty-nine days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and sixty-three days. Eight hundred and twenty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Six hundred and thirteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and sixteen days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and fifty-two days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and ninety-two marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and seventy-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and forty-nine mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and forty-eight. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and ninety-nine mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and nineteen, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and eighteenth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries forty-four lines and it did not carry a forty-fifth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and sixtieth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and seventy-fourth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and seventy-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and sixty-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the seventy-fifth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and thirty-four days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning, the morning was read out loud before it was written, and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The turner had his wheel going with a piece of ash in it. The man who digs loam was at the north end on the same strip. The man of about thirty-two put his table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel and did not look at anybody.

Not one person in that yard said that out loud this morning. It was said up there where the light finds the eleven feet, and it was said afterwards, and it did not come down.

The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and worked at the near end of the first table all morning and said nothing out loud at all this morning. The man of about thirty-four stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with his hands behind his back and said nothing out loud at all this morning.

And at about half past ten the man at the end of the second table said one thing, in the ordinary voice, at his own end of that table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and he said it once and he did not raise it.

"I have stood at this end of this table since the morning the ground under it was made," he said. "And not one person in this yard has ever told me out loud what I am standing here for. That is all. I am not asking for it. I am saying it and then I am going back to the end of it."

---

And nobody in that yard answered him. Not the turner and not the man of thirty-four and not the man of thirty-eight and not the clerk, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stood in that yard and about four of them have said since that the man at the end of the second table has said one thing out loud this morning that is only about himself, and that about four of them have not said what a man does with a thing he says about himself when nobody answers him.

---

He went back to the end of the table and he worked at it for the rest of the morning and nobody said one word to him about what he had said, and nobody asked him to say it again, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man at the end of a table has now said three things out loud in two hundred and nineteen mornings and that about four of them have not said what has come out of the first two.

And then the clerk read the morning out loud, and she read it before she wrote anything, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nineteen people at the foot of that bank, and she read it once.

"The two hundred and nineteenth morning," she said. "A thing was said out loud in this yard this morning that was not a reason, and no reason was said out loud in this yard this morning, and nothing is going on the back of that book, and that is the whole of it."
