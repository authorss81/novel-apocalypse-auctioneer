# Chapter 0953: The TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVENTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Ten The Man Who Made The Stone Said Out Loud Once That He Made The Stone The Man Of Fifty-Six Puts His Hand Flat On And That The Only Thing Anybody In That Yard Has Ever Said About It Is Nothing And One Line Carries The Morning And Not That And The Page At The Back Carries Forty-Eight Lines And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and ninety-two days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and fifty-one. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and sixty-seven days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and eighty-one days. Eight hundred and forty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and fifty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and sixty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and thirty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and thirty-four days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and seventy days past a printing it did not make.

Five hundred and ten marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and ninety-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and one reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and the page at the back of that lot book took one line for it this morning and the line carries the morning and not the reason, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before that morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and sixty-seven mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and sixty-six. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for two hundred and seventeen mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and thirty-seven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and thirty-sixth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries forty-eight lines and it did not carry a forty-ninth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and seventy-eighth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and ninety-second night of that run, having slept on five hundred and ninety-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and eighty-second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the ninety-third morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and fifty-two days, the page at the back took one line this morning and the morning was read out loud before it was written, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them and put his hand flat on the stone afterwards and left it there a while, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the stone under that wall has been put on for as long as this yard has been standing and that about four of them have not said who set it.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him. The turner had his wheel going and put the point of his own knife into the wheel well twice to get the ash out of it. The man who digs loam was at the north end. The man of about thirty-two put his table up about four feet along from the second table and squared it with the back of his heel.

---

The man of about thirty-four stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with his hands behind his back and said nothing out loud at all this morning. The man of thirty-eight came down the slope at about half past nine with his hands showing and worked at the near end of the first table.

---

And the man who made the stone came down that bank about ten with his hands in his pockets and stood about nine feet off that wall and looked at the stone under it for a while, and then he took his hands out of his pockets, and he said it once, in the ordinary voice, at about nine feet off that wall, in about nineteen people's hearing.

"I made that stone," he said. "I cut it and I dressed it and I set it under that wall with my own hands and I have never said so out loud in this yard before. I have stood at this yard on a great many mornings and I have never once said that, and the reason I have never once said it is that the only thing anybody in this yard has ever said about it is nothing. Nobody has ever said a word to me about that stone and nobody has ever said a word to anybody else about it either, and I have been the man who made it and I have watched a man put his hand flat on it twice a morning, and I am not going to say this a second time."

---

And nobody in that yard asked him for a second thing about that stone, and nobody asked him how he knew that the only thing anybody had ever said about it was nothing, and nobody thanked him for making it, and nobody answered him, and the turner's wheel went on turning and the man who digs loam went on shovelling and the man of about thirty-two went along the top of his own table with the cloth, and the man of thirty-eight worked at the end of his own table and did not turn round.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man came down that bank this morning and said out loud that he cut a stone and set it under that wall, and that about four of them have said he said it about a stone and not about a morning, and that about four of them have not said what a thing is doing under a wall that nobody has ever asked about.

---

And then the man who made the stone went back up that bank at about a quarter past ten and went on with what he had come down for, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he said one thing at the foot of that wall this morning and went back up the same hill inside a quarter of an hour, and that about four of them have not said whether he has said anything else on the way up.

---

At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale came down that bank with her scale in a piece of cloth and set it up at about four feet off the near end of the second table and weighed a bar of iron out of a basket and wrote the figure on the edge of a board of her own with a bit of chalk and said nothing to anybody.

---

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and he said no sentence before it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall, and he was not asked one question, which is the ninety-third morning running of that.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the hundred and twenty-sixth time running, and nobody asked him anything, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who put his hand flat on that stone this afternoon had heard a man say out loud this morning that he made it, and that about four of them have not said what either of those two men would say about the other.

---

And then the clerk read the morning out loud, and she read it before she wrote anything, in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nineteen people at the foot of that bank, and she read it once.

"The two hundred and thirty-seventh morning," she said. "One reason was said out loud in this yard this morning and it is being written on the page at the back of that book, and the reason is not going on it, and that is the whole of it."

---

And she wrote one line on the page at the back of that lot book, in her own hand, and the line carries the morning and not the reason, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk write one line for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and that page carries forty-eight lines and not one of the forty-eight is in the lot book and not one of them says a word about a stone.

---

Nobody thanked the man who said the reason and nobody thanked the clerk who wrote the line and nobody thanked the man of fifty-six for putting his hand flat on that stone twice. At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and the boards went down the way they go down.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man made a stone this yard is standing on and has never said so, and that about four of them have said he said it once at about ten this morning and then went back up that bank, and that about four of them have not said what a stone under a wall is for and about four of them have not said what a line on a page is for, and that the two of those are not the same question and that nobody in that yard asked whether they were.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries forty-eight lines and it did not carry a forty-ninth this morning and not one of the forty-eight is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and fifty-two days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and fifty-two days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and one reason was said out loud in that yard this morning and one line was written on that page carrying the morning and not the reason and not the question, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and a man who says out loud that he made a stone nobody has ever asked about has said a reason and not a request, and the page at the back of that lot book is at forty-eight lines and the lane count is thirty and it did not move.
