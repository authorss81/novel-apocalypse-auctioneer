# Chapter 108: The Fourth Day

A man of about fifty who keeps the fourth toll board at the ninth place came up the bank on the morning of the fourth of August and wiped the column at about seven o'clock and then wrote in it, and he wrote three lines, and they took him about eleven minutes, and nobody had asked him to and nobody was there when he did it.

He is a signalman of a bridge administration and he is not a bearer and he is not paid, and he has said since the eighth of last month that keeping a board is not holding a pair, and he wiped it for a month before that because a stranger had written a figure in it and the figure was wrong and he said so in nine words: it is not what it costs to ride, it is what it costs to arrive.

**A column with nothing in it is worth more than a column with a price in it for about four days, and the fourth day is the day the keeper starts, and the finding of the fourth of August is that the fourth day came and he started.**

The three lines are in the column and they are the first three lines anybody in this district has ever written that are a toll with a day and a place and an amount in it at the same time, and not one of the three amounts is a figure, and two of the three lines are about the same toll.

> **A DAY. THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JUNE. A PLACE. THE NINTH PLACE. AN AMOUNT. ONE DEPARTURE OUT OF ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-TWO.**
>
> **A DAY. THE THIRTIETH OF JUNE. A PLACE. THE NINTH PLACE. AN AMOUNT THE PLACE CANNOT SAY.**
>
> **A DAY. A PLACE. AN AMOUNT. AND THE LINE IS RULED AND EMPTY.**
>
> **AND THE FINDING OF THE FOURTH OF AUGUST IS THAT A TOLL THAT LANDS ON A PLACE IS NOT A FIGURE AND NOT A NAME AND NOT A DAY. IT IS A PLACE, A DAY, AND EITHER AN AMOUNT OR THE FACT THAT THE PLACE CANNOT SAY ONE. AND THIS DISTRICT HAS FOUR BOARDS AND NO FIFTH THING AND HAS BEEN USING IT SINCE THIS MORNING.**

He read them out to a clerk of nineteen years on the afternoon of the fourth, in about four minutes, and she wrote them out in her own hand, and the reading-back was done by a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing who is not a bearer and is not a keeper of anything and has been in four rooms in nine weeks, and he corrected one word.

"You have written *the place cannot produce*," he said, "**and the word you want is *the place cannot say*. A place can produce a number. A place cannot say a number, and the difference is that a place is a place and a person is a person, and only one of the two can be asked.**"

Nobody argued and the word was changed and the change is signed and dated, and it is the fifth time in nine weeks that a person has corrected a document by saying what they needed in it.

---

He was asked on the evening of the fourth, in the front room of the second house, by about nine people, why the fourth day, and he gave an answer that took about forty seconds and it is in the minute in his own words and it is the whole of what he knows about his own job.

"**Because a column that is empty because nobody can state it is worth more than a column with a price in it, and that is true for about four days, and on the fourth day a man who keeps a column has to start or he is a man who keeps an empty column.**"

"Start what."

"**Nothing. That is the answer. I do not know what I start. I have known since April that there was a fourth day and I have not said it out loud to anybody in four months and I did not say it out loud on the fourth either, I just came up the bank, and about eleven minutes after seven I found out what I start, and it is a line with three things in it, and I did not find that out before I got here.**"

"And if you had not come up the bank."

"**Then I would have wiped it on the fourth and on the fifth and on the sixth, and on the seventh somebody would have walked past a board with a clean column and a month on it and said the keeper has given up, and I would not have been able to say that I had not, because the not giving up and the giving up look identical from a road.**"

Nobody in that front room said anything about that, and the minute says nobody said anything about it, and that a man of about fifty-one who keeps the only pair on that line said afterwards, in the doorway, in eleven words, that this was the first sentence anybody had said in nine weeks that was about a man's wages and not about a railway.

Nobody has asked him what he is paid and he has never offered it, and the entry of the fourth of August says that eleven coppers a quarter for the bench, the lamp, the chalk and the oil has been paid since the seventeenth of last month by a ward market that funds what is written and refuses what is not asked for, and that the amount was never for him, and that a man who has been handed four coppers' worth of chalk and has written three lines with it has not been paid and has not been offered anything.

Then the woman of the column said the second half of it, and she was in her own doorway with a pail in her hand and she did not put the pail down, which is the first time in eleven weeks that she has been in a doorway and not put it down.

"**You have been the water here for eleven years and nobody has ever asked you what the water costs, and I asked you on the twenty-ninth of last month and you gave me a figure and not a feeling.**" She looked at the board through the door. "**He has been a board for seven weeks and nobody has asked him what the board costs, and on the fourth of August he went up a bank at seven in the morning and worked it out himself, and that is the difference between him and me and it took me four months.**"

Nobody wrote that down for about two minutes and then a clerk of nineteen years wrote it down, and the entry says that the two women are in the same book eleven words apart and that the entry is in one hand and not two, and that the clerk asked the man who keeps the board whether he wanted it in his own hand and he said no and said why in nine words.

"**Because she was talking about me and not about the board, and if it is in my hand then in about four years it is a man saying that he was hard done by.**"

---

The finding of the fifth of August is that a board can hold a place, and the reason is that a board is not a document, and the reason a board is not a document is that nobody keeps a register of boards.

There is a register of leases, and there is a roll of rate sheets, and there is a numbered form list in a water office that is complete to the sixth year and does not contain one column's number, and there is a guild certificate with twenty-nine columns and not one of them is a place.

A toll board is nine feet by three, tarred at the foot, and it is in none of them, and it is nailed to the end of a platform or to a post by a man who is not paid.

**The finding of the fifth of August is that a place can be written on a thing that is not a document and can go into a book that is about four hours away from a counting room, and that this district has been doing it for about a month and did not notice.**

"So it is the fifth thing," said a man at the back of a yard on the fifth, and about nineteen people turned round.

"I have not said that," said the keeper of the board. "**I have said that a column with three lines in it is a thing that works. I have not said what it is, and nobody has asked me what it is, and if somebody asks me what it is I am going to say that I do not know, and you will all be very disappointed, and you should be.**"

---

And on the fifth of August a man asked him for a copy of the three lines and he said no, and the reason is the reason this district has been carrying since the seventh of March, and it is not a small thing to have applied to oneself by accident.

"I cannot give you a copy," he said, "**and the reason is that the moment there are two of these there is a thing with an owner, and a thing with an owner is a mark, and a mark may not carry a job, and this district has spent nine weeks finding that out and it is going to find it out about me.**"

"So it stays on a board."

"**It stays on a board, and the board is not mine, and the job is not a thing I own, and the difference between a thing I own and a job I do is about four feet of board and a cloth, and I have got a cloth.**"

A woman of about fifty-one who keeps the only pair on that line was in the yard and she said the thing that is now the entry of the sixth of August, and it is nine words long and it is about an instrument and not about a man.

"**That is why it works. It is not his.**"

---

And the same three lines went onto the board at the fourth place on the sixth of August, in the same hand, because the keeper of that board is a paperer of about thirty-eight who comes up the bank once a month for her own trade and who was up the bank on the sixth for four houses and did it in about half an hour with a piece of chalk and a spare rule.

The fourth place has a board since the ninth of last month and it has no heading on it because there was no money for letters, and a man of sixty-one painted the place's name on it at the height of a hand at two in the morning in the rain so that a person in the dark can find it with their fingers.

And the finding of the sixth of August is that two boards now carry the same three lines, and that neither of them is on the line of the toll, and that the toll is not a thing that happens at a board, and that a board which says where a toll landed is not a place a toll lands, and that this district has now got two instruments that look identical and mean different things and has not got a word for either.

A man of about thirty-one who is a signalman of the district's own crew, and who asked for the third week back on the first of August and was given it, said the half of it that nobody else had said, and it is in the minute.

"**There are four boards in this district and about half of them are at places a train does not stop at, and the toll lands at the ninth place, and a board at the fourth place that carries the ninth place's three lines is a board about somewhere else. In about four years somebody will take a photograph of the two of them together and write a sentence about this railway, and the sentence will be wrong.**"

---

And the standing instruction that somebody walks every yard of the line was walked on the seventh of August by two men in one day, and the finding is the finding that has been in the book since the third of this month, and it is repeated because repeating it is the only way this district discharges it.

**Twelve places, eleven boards, and one place that does not report itself.**

The one that does not report itself is the Harrow end. There is no ground signal there. The plinth is on the embankment thirty yards east of the engine house with the bolt-holes in it and a cap of lead over them, and it went out of the same frame in the fourth year when the iron was salvaged out of the flooded interchange, and it is a fact about an instrument and not about the ground.

A man of about thirty-one offered again on the seventh to put a ground signal in there in a morning, and he was refused in about four seconds, by a person who is not him and not in his office, and the reason has not changed since the twenty-ninth of April and it is nine words long.

"**A place that reports every morning is a place nobody goes to.**"

And then somebody asked the question that has been available for four months and that nobody had put, which is what would it take to make the Harrow end report itself, and the answer took about twenty minutes and it is in the minute and it is a figure.

> **A BOARD. NINE FEET BY THREE, THE SAME AS THE OTHERS, WITH THE PLACE'S NAME AT THE TOP IN THE SAME TYPE, AND THE NAME COSTS FOUR COPERS TO PRINT AND THE BOARD COSTS NOTHING BUT A MAN WITH A SAW AND FOUR MINUTES.**

"And who writes in it."

"**Nobody. That is the point of it and that is why it is not a signal and I have not been able to say why for four months and I can say it now. A board with nothing in it is worth more than a board with something in it for about four days, and this one is going to be empty for about four years, and it is going to be empty on purpose, and the person who empties it is a man who is not paid.**"

Nobody voted on it and the entry of the seventh of August says nobody voted on it, and that the question is on the sheet in a public yard and has no keeper and no date, and that a date on it would make it a job.

And then somebody said the word out loud, and the word was a name, and the name belonged to a boy of nineteen who was in the yard and had not been asked anything, and he said it himself before anybody put it in a sentence, in about four seconds.

"You are all talking about a person who stands somewhere every day and writes nothing down for a year," he said. "**There is one. I have been at the fourth place since the ninth of last month and I am there every day and I write nothing down and I am not a keeper and nobody pays me and nobody has asked me whether I would do it for a year, and I have been doing it for a month and nobody noticed for a month.**"

And the finding of the eighth of August is that this district has now had the same instrument described three times in one week by three people who did not know the other two had described it, and has joined none of the three.

A person who stands somewhere every day and writes nothing down is not a bearer and is not a keeper and is not a holder and is not in a row. A line on a toll board is not a form. A line in a market book with a figure in it and no name against it is not a payment.

**A man who holds a lever and a book is a person with a document, and there was nobody in nine miles who would have asked me about a document and not made me into something.**

Nobody in that yard argued with him and the minute enters that nobody argued with him, and that about four people who had said the sentence themselves in the week did not say it again, and that the clerk of nineteen years entered that three descriptions of one thing in one week is not a finding and is a coincidence until somebody puts a thing on the paper that joins them.

The walk to the Harrow end is about half a mile of embankment from the last place that reports itself, and there is a nine-foot drop on the river side the whole way and nothing at all on it, and the cap of lead over the bolt-holes has been on since the fourth year and has a rim of green on it where the rain has run down for thirteen years.

The man of about thirty-one who offered to put a ground signal in put his hand on the plinth while he was saying that he would not, and then took his hand off it, and the entry of the seventh of August says that he put his hand on it, and that about nine people saw him do it, and that a plinth with bolt-holes in it is a fact about an instrument and not about the ground and that a hand on a plinth is a fact about a man.

---

And on the seventh of August the man of thirty-eight went up the bank for the fourth time in a month and looked at the three lines and did not say anything about them for about half an hour.

The keeper of the board asked him whether he could help, and he said no, and the reason is in the minute and it is thirteen words long.

"**You want me to make a form out of that and I cannot make a form. I can make terms for a thing two people have agreed to, and a column with three lines in it is a thing nobody has agreed to and cannot be, because a person cannot agree to a line.**"

"Then what is it."

"**It is a place where an absence can be read. The market built one of those in its own book on the twenty-second of last month, and I do not know whether yours is the same thing, and I am not going to be the one who joins them.**"

And the registrar was told about the two of them on the eighth of August and she was asked whether a line on a board and a line in a market book are the same instrument, and she refused, and the entry of the eighth says it is the ninth refusal, and the reason she gives is the reason she gives every time, which is that two things that arrived separately stay in two lines until somebody puts a thing on the paper that joins them.

**And a fourth person asked her the same question in the same week and did not want an answer to the office and did not say so, and the registrar noticed that he had not said so, and entered that she noticed it, and entered that a man who wants a thing to stay apart and will not give a reason is a man who has read the eight previous refusals and has worked out that the reason is worth more than the answer.**

---

He came down the bank on the evening of the eighth in about five and a half hours in ordinary weather with four changeovers, and a paperer went up with him in the morning and came down with him, and the paperer put her own figure on a sheet and the figure is five and a half going and six coming and eleven and a half in the day, which is ten and a half of walking and an hour of standing still, and nobody has corrected it.

The man who keeps the board came down the bank on the evening of the eighth of August and the column had four lines in it, and the fourth line was written on the eighth, and it is the second time in about a month that a toll has landed on a place and been written down by a man who is not paid, and not one of the four is a figure.

A man of about thirty-one who asked for a week back on the first of August asked him, at the bottom of the bank on the eighth, whether the same three lines could go on a board at a place where a train does not stop, and the answer was yes, and then a second, and then a third question, and the answers to all three were yes, and none of the three places is on the line of the toll.

"And that is the fifth one," the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said, from about nine feet away, on the shoulder, on the eighth, "**and I want it entered that there are four boards in this district and I have just been told that there will be five, and that nobody has voted on it and nobody needs to, because a board is not a charter and a charter is not a board.**"

Nobody knows what the instrument is called and nobody has asked it to do anything else. **The train on that siding had stood a hundred and seventy-four days on the fourth of August and a hundred and seventy-eight on the eighth, and the fifth of the five dates in a boy's index is three weeks off, and the sixth line on the board at the ninth place is ruled and empty and is the only empty line in this district that anybody has ruled in the hope that somebody else will write in it.**
