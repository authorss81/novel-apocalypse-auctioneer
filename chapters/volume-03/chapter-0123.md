# Chapter 123: Four Things She Would Not Sign

---

The party got over the raised plank road on the twenty-sixth of September with two carts and about ten people and a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households, and the plank nearest the far end is limewood and about nineteen years old, and nobody said anything about it, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody did and did not say why.

They came down onto the flat at about the middle of the afternoon and the first thing anybody saw was the pan with the frame over it and nobody in it, and it was dry, and there was salt on it, and the salt was old and had gone grey and had a crust on it about the thickness of a thumb, and the bed of brine under the frame was cracked loam and had a smell that four people in the party described and only two of them described the same way.

The channel that had fed it was a hole. The masonry that had keyed the frame into the pan was standing about four inches proud of the silt and the sill was gone, and there was a cut in the silt about two feet wide and nine inches deep where the water had been going, and a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households got down into it and put his hand in it and came out with grey silt to the wrist and said one word and then said the same word again in a different tone.

---

The first thing anybody said on the far bank was said by a woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years and who was standing outside a room over a shop with four sheets of paper in her hand, and she said it to the man of thirty-four who mends fencing and not to the party, and it is in the minute in her own words, and the clerk entered that it was thirty-one words and that the woman of forty-four did not know it was being counted.

"**Four things have happened to four people on this flat since the eighth of March and I have written them down and I am not going to sign them, and I would like a room and about nineteen people in it on the twenty-eighth, and I would like the man who came in the cart to be in it and not at the front of it.**"

The man of thirty-four who mends fencing asked her one question and it was about the four sheets and not about the four things, and the minute says the clerk of nineteen years noticed that and entered it and did not say it out loud at the time.

"**Are these signed.**"

"**No. And they are not going to be, and if you ask me again I am going to say the same four words and you are going to write them down and we are both going to be doing the same thing twice in a day.**"

---

What she handed over was four sheets, and none of them had a heading on it, and there were no lines ruled on any of them, and the clerk of nineteen years read all four in nine minutes in a room with the door open, and the minute says the clerk read them and not the man of thirty-four who mends fencing, and says that the clerk asked to read them and was not asked twice.

> **ONE. A MAN OF FIFTY-TWO ASKED ME IN THE WEEK HE CAME OUT OF THE FOURTH CHANNEL WHETHER A PERSON CAN BE ASKED ABOUT A THING THEY CANNOT REMEMBER, AND I SAID I DID NOT KNOW, AND HE HAS NOT ASKED ANYBODY SINCE, AND I HAVE NOT ASKED HIM.**
>
> **TWO. A WOMAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT IN THE SECOND CHANNEL WAS ASKED BY TWO PEOPLE IN ONE WEEK WHAT HER ELEVENTH YEAR WAS LIKE, AND GAVE TWO ANSWERS, AND BOTH ANSWERS ARE IN THIS DISTRICT'S OWN MINUTE, AND SHE HAS ASKED FOR THE SECOND ONE TO BE TAKEN OUT AND HAS BEEN TOLD THAT A MINUTE IS A MINUTE.**
>
> **THREE. A MAN OF TWENTY-SIX SIGNED A SHEET WITH A MARK HE HAS USED SINCE HE WAS NINE AND WAS TOLD TWICE THAT HIS MARK IS HIS YES, AND NO FORM HE HAS BEEN SHOWN HAS A PLACE TO PUT A MARK THAT IS A YES.**
>
> **FOUR. A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO TAKES IN WASHING AT THE SECOND CHANNEL HAS A CHILD THAT IS NINE AND ASKED HER EVERY MORNING WHAT SHE HAD DONE THE DAY BEFORE, AND IN THE FOURTH MONTH OF THIS YEAR SHE COULD NOT ANSWER FOR ELEVEN DAYS RUNNING, AND THE CHILD STOPPED ASKING AND ASKED SOMEBODY ELSE, AND SHE DOES NOT KNOW WHICH OF THOSE TWO THINGS THAT IS.**

Nobody in that room said anything for nine seconds, and then a man of thirty-eight asked her the only question he asked her in nine days, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**Why will you not sign them.**"

"**Because a signature is a person agreeing to a thing, and I am not agreeing to anything, and because one of the four is a man who has asked me one question and I answered it wrong, and asking him to sign my sentence about it would be asking him twice.**"

---

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF SEPTEMBER A WOMAN OF FORTY-FOUR WHO HAS DONE IT ELEVEN YEARS PUT FOUR THINGS ON FOUR SHEETS WITH NO HEADING ON ANY OF THEM, AND THE FOUR THINGS ARE NOT A DOCUMENT, ARE NOT A CLAIM, AND ARE NOT A LIST OF PRICES, AND SHE WOULD NOT SIGN THEM, AND THE REASON IS THIRTY-ONE WORDS LONG AND IS THAT A SIGNATURE IS A PERSON AGREEING TO A THING.**

---

She was asked, afterwards, when she had written them, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote the answer in her own words because the woman asked her to and said that a woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years does not say things to a man of thirty-eight and will say them to a clerk of nineteen years and the two of them are about the same age.

"**The ninth of September. I sat down after the second channel and I wrote the first one and I could not write the second one, and the third one I knew on the fourteenth, and the fourth one I have been carrying since the fourth month and I could not put it down until the twenty-fourth.**"

"Why the ninth."

"**Because the man of fifty-two came out in the fourth month and asked me a question in the week of it and I have not been able to put the answer down in five months and he has not asked anybody since, and I wrote the first sheet and looked at it and the first sheet was about a man who cannot remember a thing and cannot be asked about it, and I have been in eleven frames and I know exactly what it is to want to be asked and not to be able to be.**"

---

Then the six things were put to the four people who had signed in September, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote out the six and they are not on the wall and they belong to a clerk, and the six are that you have been told what it is, that you have been told what it costs, that you have been told who stands in it, that you have been told what happens on the last day, that you have been told what happens if the channel stops, and that you have been told what a year is.

The man of twenty-six answered first, and he answered with his hand and then with his mouth, and the clerk entered that he was asked whether he wanted to sign and he said yes in four seconds.

"**I will sign with this and I have signed with this since I was about nine, and last time the man of forty-four told me that a mark cannot be counted and that it is a defect in the sheet and not in me, and I have thought about that for a fortnight and I am still going to use the mark, and if the sheet needs a name off me then the sheet cannot have me.**"

The woman of sixty-one who had signed from a market two days' walk away answered second, and she said the same eleven words she said in September, and the clerk entered that the eleven words were the same eleven words.

"**I understood it in four minutes and I did not take it back, and I have thought about it since and I have not changed, and I am sixty-one years old in this basin and a woman who changes her mind twice is a woman who was not careful the first time.**"

The third said yes in nine words and the clerk wrote the nine down and read them back, and the third is a woman of thirty-eight who has been in the second channel four years and who is the only one of the four who asked what the release was before she was asked the toll.

Then the fourth said no, and he was a man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten, and he was not on the list of the five things because he had been asked and had not been chosen, and the clerk entered that he was in the room and that he had not been asked and that not asked was written against his name, and that he then asked to be asked.

---

"**I am not going to sign a season on a sheet that has no place to say whether I was in the room, and I am not going to be the reason a form gets a column added to it in a hurry in a fortnight, and I have asked to be asked properly and I have been asked properly and I am saying no, and I am not going to explain it twice.**"

Nobody argued with him and the clerk of nineteen years entered the refusal in four seconds and entered that the refusal was the first one anybody had made to a season, and that the season's sheet has three signatures on it and the form asks whether four people consented, and that **the man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households said the other half in nine words and the minute has it.**

"**We have been told there is no column for it. We have been told that by the man who made the form. And we have now found out what a column that is not there costs, and it cost a season, and it will cost a season every time until the column is there.**"

And the man of twenty-six, who had said yes four seconds before the man who digs loam said no, was asked afterwards whether the two answers were about the same thing, and he said that he did not know and that the man digs loam and that he has been in a bed of brine for a year and a half and that a thing a person has not been asked about is a thing a person cannot be asked about, and the clerk entered that and entered that the man of twenty-six is a person bound to a channel and is not a bearer and may not be entered as one.

---

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF SEPTEMBER THE FOUR OF SEPTEMBER BECAME THREE, AND THE FINDING IS THAT A FORM WITH NO COLUMN FOR WHETHER A PERSON WAS IN THE ROOM CANNOT BE MADE TO WORK BY ASKING HARDER, AND THAT A PERSON MAY ASK TO BE ASKED AND BE ASKED AND STILL SAY NO, AND THAT A REFUSAL ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH IS A REFUSAL AND NOT A MARK AND NOT A QUARREL WITH THE FORM.**

---

The council met on the twenty-eighth of September in a room over a shop with nineteen people in it, and it was not called a council by anybody at the time, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing asked it what it was called and nobody answered him and the entry says nobody answered him and that a clerk of nineteen years entered the not-answering and did not ask a second time.

The room is a room over a shop and it has a door that closes and a bench along one wall and a table that four people in the room are not sitting at, and outside the door there is a board with the days on it in a hand that cannot be read, and the days are the twenty-eighth of September and the twenty-sixth of October and the twenty-fourth of November, and the board was written on by a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel and who is not a clerk of anything and who was not asked to write it and did not offer.

It has no officer and no clerk and no seal and no stamp and no name on the door, and it has three rules and the three were said out loud by four people and entered in the minute in their own words.

The first rule is that the four things are read out and are not answered.

"**I did not write them to be answered. I wrote them because a thing that is only in four heads stops being true in about four months, and I have watched it stop being true twice, and I am not writing it again.**"

The second rule is that anybody may read them out and nobody may add to them.

"**A fifth thing is not a fifth thing. It is a new page, and a new page is a different thing and it has to be started by whoever starts it and not written in the middle of mine.**"

The third rule is that it has no clerk, and the woman of forty-four was asked once whether she would be its clerk and said no in four words.

"**I am not that. I am the woman who wrote four things and I am going to be asked about them for the rest of my life and that is enough paper for one person.**"

Nobody asked her again, and not asked is in the count, and the count is a column and not a bucket, and the clerk of nineteen years said that out loud and the minute says she said it and that a woman of about fifty who is the water at the ninth place agreed with her in four words and that the minute entered the agreement and not the four words, because the count is hers and not the clerk's.

---

**AND ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF SEPTEMBER A ROOM IN WHICH FOUR THINGS ARE READ OUT AND NOT ANSWERED MET FOR THE FIRST TIME, AND IT HAS NO OFFICER AND NO CLERK AND NO NAME ON THE DOOR, AND ITS ONLY INSTRUMENT IS A BOARD WITH THE DAYS ON IT IN A HAND THAT CANNOT BE READ, AT THE DOOR, AND A ROOM WITH A LIST OF DAYS ON IT IS NOT A DOCUMENT AND IS NOT AN INSTRUMENT AND IS NOT A WAY OF SAYING THAT A TOLL LANDS ON A PLACE, AND NONE OF THE FOUR THINGS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE AND NONE OF THE SIX IT HAS BUILT AND NOT NAMED AND NONE OF THE ONE DOCUMENT NOBODY OWNS WAS ADDED TO ANYTHING.**

---

And at about half past three a man of fifty-two who came out of the fourth channel in the fourth month of this year after four years came into the room and sat down at the back, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that he was the man in the first of the four things, and that nobody in the room said his name, and that he sat at the back for forty minutes and did not ask anybody anything.

He was the man who had asked the woman of forty-four, in the week he came out, whether a person can be asked about a thing they cannot remember, and she had said she did not know, and the minute of that week is in the party's book and he had read it, and the clerk entered that he had read it in Alder Reach on the nineteenth of September and that he had come east on a cart with a party of nine to be in a room on a flat and not to see a channel.

The man of thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in nine seconds, whether he had come to ask the woman of forty-four the same question again, and he said no in four seconds and gave the reason in nineteen words, and the reason is in the minute.

"**I have had five months to think of a better question than the one I asked her and I have not got one, and I came four days to sit at the back of a room, and I would like it entered that I came to sit and not to ask.**"

And a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had come to sit and not to ask, and entered that not asked is in the count, and entered that the count is a column and not a bucket and that this is a different count from the one about the woman of forty-five and that the two counts are kept apart and that nobody has ever asked her why she keeps them apart.

And then, at half past four, a man of about twenty-six who has been in a bed of brine for a year and a half stood up in the middle of that room and said that he wanted to say the thing about himself and not be asked, and about four people said no at once and he said it anyway and the clerk entered that four people had said no and he had said it anyway, and that is the way it is entered.

"**I am the loom. That is what a man out here says when he has been in one long enough, and I said it in a cart to a man from the west bank on the fifth of September and a clerk wrote it down, and I have thought about it for twenty-three days and I want it said in this room and not in a cart.**"

"Why here."

"**Because there is a woman in this room who has written down four things that happened to four people and one of the four is a man who cannot remember a thing, and I would like to know which of us is the fourth, and I am not going to be the one to tell her, and I am not going to be the one to ask her.**"

Nobody in that room told her, and the minute says nobody told her and says that the woman of forty-four who wrote the four things looked at the man of twenty-six for nine seconds and then looked at the floor, and that the clerk entered that and entered that she did not know what it meant and was not going to ask and that not asked is in the count.

---

The last thing of the twenty-eighth was said by the man of thirty-eight in nine minutes and he said it to the man of thirty-four who mends fencing and not to the room, and the room heard it, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is forty-nine words long.

"**I have been in this basin nine weeks learning that a person is the seventh part of a lot, and I have been wrong about what that is for. It is not for me. There is no sentence I can say in a room that makes a person agree, and the six things I cannot do, and the one thing I would have to do to do the sixth one is write a name in a line, and I have found out in nine weeks that a name in a line is a thing you carry to a door.**"

"And the four things."

"**The four things are the first instrument this district has built that I cannot carry to a door, and it is four sheets of paper with no heading on them and a woman who will not sign them, and I have looked at it for nine minutes and I have understood about one quarter of it, and I would like that entered under my own name and not above it.**"
---

In the evening the party camped on the rise above the second pan and the clerk of nineteen years wrote the day's minute by lantern light on the back of a leaf of the field book because the other forty-one pages had a channel on them, and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel sat on the cart tail and watched her do it and asked her one question about the fourth of the four things, and the clerk entered that the question was asked and that the answer was given.

"**Is the child going to start again.**"

"**I do not know. She says the child stopped asking me and asked somebody else, and she does not know which of those two things that is, and she wrote it down on the twenty-fourth of September and she has not been asked about it since, and I have been in this room for two days and I have not asked her, and I am telling you that I have not asked her because the four things are read out and not answered and I am in the room.**"

"You are a clerk."

"**I am a clerk and I have been in a room for two days and there is a rule in the room and the rule is not mine and I am keeping it, and a woman of fifty-eight who carries water in Alder Reach told me in the last week of September that a count is a column and not a bucket, and I have been carrying that sentence with me for three days and it is the only thing I have said out loud on that flat that I have not read off a page.**"

**AND THE FINDING OF THE TWENTY-SIXTH, THE TWENTY-SEVENTH AND THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF SEPTEMBER IS THAT A PERSON WHO IS THE SEVENTH PART OF A LOT CANNOT BE ASKED BY A MAN WHO CAN WRITE TERMS, AND CAN BE ASKED BY A ROOM THAT MEETS ON A LIST OF DAYS, AND THAT THE ONLY THING THE MAN WITH THE FIVE POWERS CAN DO ABOUT IT IS LOOK AT IT FOR NINE MINUTES AND UNDERSTAND A QUARTER OF IT AND ASK FOR HIS OWN UNDERSTANDING TO BE ENTERED UNDER HIS OWN NAME.**
