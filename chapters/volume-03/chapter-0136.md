# Chapter 136: The Ninth Refusal

---

The question was put to him in a room on the flat on the third of November at about eleven in the morning and it was put in the five things, and the five things are a clerk of nineteen years' and not a warden's, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing read the price out for him before anybody asked, because a man who has not asked does not get a price read for him.

A notice that does not say what it is withdrawing from is not a withdrawal. It is a person telling you they are not coming back **and we cannot write that down without knowing what coming back costs.**

A woman of thirty-eight in the second channel said the second half of it and a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households said the first half, and neither of them said the word reading and the room said it for them nine seconds later.

Nobody said it for them. **A man of about nineteen who walked in from the road said it, in a room where he is the sixth of the six and is not paid, and he said it in twenty-eight words, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down and did not say that she had written it down.**

"**Then somebody has to read a frame, and the man who can read is thirty-four miles west, and the man who is in it is in this room.**"

The room went quiet for nine seconds. **A man of twenty-six who has been in a bed of brine for a year and a half said that he would rather be asked than not be asked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said it before anybody asked him whether he wanted to be asked, and that the six things are a clerk's and that what he did was offer himself to a question nobody had finished asking, and that both halves are in the minute.**

---

He was asked in the five things on the third of November **and the price was read out for him, and he said no in four seconds, and the reason is in the minute in his own words and it is twenty-seven words, and the clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got twenty-seven both times.**

"**A reading takes about an hour and the cost of it is a part of the lot's toll, and the toll on this one is a year.**"

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that the toll on this one is a year and that a year does not go twice, and that the man who has the year is standing in the room and had offered himself to the question four minutes earlier, and that a person cannot be asked twice for the same yes in four minutes, and that the room had done it once.

Nobody argued with the refusal. A man of fifty-six said that this is the ninth refusal on the record, and that the eighth was on the ninth of October and cost nothing at all, and that he had entered the eighth with the date and the reason and had read the ledger out twice in a cart because a man asked what the intervals were, and that a man who reads a ledger out twice for nothing is a man who has stopped believing the numbers in it.

The cost of the ninth refusal was nothing at all **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was nothing at all, and entered that this is the second time in eleven weeks a refusal has cost nothing and that a refusal which costs nothing is the only kind this district has got.**

A man of twenty-six said that the refusal had cost the room something and named it, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down and entered that he was not asked about the bed of brine and was not asked about the year and a half and was not asked whether he minded.

"**You have just said that nobody can tell what a year is worth, and the notice is for me, and I am the one who is in the thing.**"

Nobody answered him. **A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that the man of twenty-six had said in September that he was the loom, and that the room had not told him which of the four he was, and that a person who says he is the loom and then is told he cannot be asked a question about the year is a person who has been told the answer to his own question by somebody else, and that this is the second time.**

---

The afternoon of the third of November was about what the room would do instead, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a room which has been refused something writes down what it will do instead more carefully than it writes down what it wanted, and that this is the fourth time in eleven weeks and that the first time was in September and the three sentences are not joined.

A man of fifty-six put four things on a table in about nine minutes and none of them needed a reading and all four of them were written down, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing read them back and got them the same.

"One: the notice says nothing about the year and the room accepts that. Two: the ledger has a row for everybody who has been in a frame and the row says what the frame is. Three: a person who withdraws is entered in the ledger the same way a person who did not is entered, and the page cannot tell the difference. Four: nobody in this district is going to fix that in November."

Nobody argued with the four. A man of about thirty-four who digs loam asked for one change to the second of them, and the change was that the row should say what the frame is in the second column, and that a frame is four feet by three and a channel is two feet wide and nine inches deep, and that a stranger reading the page would then know which of the two he was looking at.

That was agreed in four minutes and it went into the sixth column, which now has two things in it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the sixth column has been a column about a room since the twenty-fourth of October and is now a column about a room and about a measurement, and that this is the second time in eleven weeks a column has changed what it is for in the middle of a page and that a page which changes what a column is for is a page a person has to be shown twice.

---

On the fourth of November a man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was asked by a man of fifty-six whether he would copy the second form out nine times, and he said yes in four seconds, and then said no in four seconds, and the clerk of nineteen years entered both in the order they were said and entered that the yes was first.

"I will copy it. I will not be the man whose nine copies are the record. Put a name on the nine."

A man of fifty-six said that a man of about twenty-three who will copy a thing nine times and will not be the man the nine copies are attributed to has understood the instrument better in four seconds than this district has in eleven weeks, and that the entry of the fourth of November is that the nine copies are attributed to the six who hold the ledger and to a clerk of nineteen years and to no one else.

Nobody asked him to copy them and he did not, and he said one more thing before he left, which a clerk of nineteen years wrote down in his own words and read back to him, and that he is a man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing and that he had come in to look at a board and had been asked to copy a form and had said

No, and that this is the second time in eleven weeks a person has said no to a job nobody had offered them in a district where a man is asked in the five things. **The nine were copied on the fifth and the sixth by nine different hands, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nine hands means nine spellings, and that the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and that a hand-copied document goes out once and wrong.**

**AND ON THE FOURTH OF NOVEMBER A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE WHO SELLS NOTHING AND COPIES FOR NOTHING SAID YES IN FOUR SECONDS AND THEN SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS, AND THE CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED BOTH IN THE ORDER THEY WERE SAID, AND THE REASON IS THAT HE WILL COPY A THING NINE TIMES AND WILL NOT BE THE MAN WHOSE NINE COPIES ARE THE RECORD, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT A MAN WHO UNDERSTANDS THAT IN FOUR SECONDS HAS UNDERSTOOD THE INSTRUMENT BETTER IN FOUR SECONDS THAN THIS DISTRICT HAS IN ELEVEN WEEKS, AND THAT THE NINE COPIES ARE ATTRIBUTED TO THE SIX WHO HOLD THE LEDGER AND TO A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS AND TO NO ONE ELSE.**


---

Ivo Stenn read the ledger out at about half past four in the afternoon on the third of November **in a room on the flat, with about nineteen people in it, and then read his own separate sheet out after it, and his separate sheet is nineteen words and it is the same nineteen words it has been in every batch, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that the intervals have gone up on use for the fourth time in eleven weeks and that he would not defend it and would not call it anything else.**

> **THE INTERVALS GO UP ON USE AND I WILL NOT DEFEND IT AND I WILL NOT CALL IT ANYTHING ELSE.**

Nobody asked him what the intervals were and he read the ledger out again when a man of about thirty-one who runs a cart of meal asked, which is the rule of the counter and not his own, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that this is the third time he has read it out twice in front of about nineteen people and that on the two previous occasions a different man had asked and that a clerk of nineteen years was told about this one and had wanted to put the three in a line and had not.

He is not a keeper of anything. **He is a ward market freight clerk of six years and a cooperative secretary of two, and he came thirty-four miles east in a cart with two other men on the third of November and was not asked to stay and stayed, and a man of fifty-six said that a man who has said no for six years has a right to be in a room where somebody is refusing something, and that he had not come to be asked and had come about salt.**

**AND ON THE THIRD OF NOVEMBER A BINDER WAS ASKED FOR THE NINTH TIME TO READ AND SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS, AND THE REASON IS THAT A READING TAKES ABOUT AN HOUR AND THE COST OF IT IS A PART OF THE LOT'S TOLL, AND THE TOLL ON THIS ONE IS A YEAR, AND THE COST OF THE REFUSAL WAS NOTHING AT ALL, AND THE DEPARTURE IS NOT SPENT AND THERE IS STILL NO DATE IN THE LEDGER FOR THE NEXT ONE.**

---

A guild inspector of forty-four came to the flat on the fifth of November and he had not been asked to come and he came, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said in a room that he knows his answer is not the answer crossed thirty-four miles on his own money to say the same thing to nine people who are not him.

He said the same thing in four minutes and it is in the minute in his own words **and it is about the form, and nobody argued with him and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that it is the fourth time in eleven weeks a person in this district has been told by somebody outside it that the instrument is not the problem.**

"You have a notice with a person on it, a frame on it and a date on it, and it is a good notice, and there is nowhere on the guild form to put it. Twenty-nine columns. I have read them aloud in a field in September and a man of thirty-four wrote all twenty-nine down. None of them is a withdrawal and none of them is a refusal and none of them is a person who wants to stop."

Then he said the part that made the room go quiet, and it is in the minute in his own words and the clerk of nineteen years entered that it was forty-five words and that she counted them twice and got forty-five both times, and that this is the second time in eleven weeks she had counted a speech to a figure other than nineteen and that the nineteen was the year and not a number of words.

"**I have been asked eleven times in eleven weeks for a form, and every one of the eleven wanted a column put in for one person, and a form with a column in it for one person is a form with a mark on it.**"

Nobody said anything for nine seconds. A man of fifty-six said that a mark on a form is a thing a person cuts and not a thing a person rules, and that a mark is a person's refusal of a name and may not be used as a bearer or a holder or a witness or a responsible party or a person who was asked, and that the boundary at the west end was built around that rule and is not to be retired to make a form work.

The inspector said that he knows all of that and that he taught it to two of the people in this room in September **and that he is not here to argue a rule and that he is here because a notice of withdrawal cannot be put on a guild form and that a notice which cannot be put on a guild form is a notice a stranger cannot check, and that a notice a stranger cannot check is a piece of paper.**

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half and he is the fourth of the six and the one who fills the page.

"You are telling us the notice has to be on a form to be real, and the form has twenty-nine columns and none of them is this, and the answer to that is not a column. The answer to that is that this district has a thing a stranger can look at and it is not a form, and it is a page on eleven boards, and about nine people come to the yard in a month."

The inspector of forty-four said that a page on eleven boards in a yard in Alder Reach is not a form and cannot be carried to a flat thirty-four miles east by a man in a cart, and that a form can, and that a person who says a page in a yard is enough has never had to stand in a frame.

Nobody argued with him. **A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam had said a page in a yard was enough, and that the man who fills the page had not been asked to say it and had said it, and that not asked is in the count.**

**AND ON THE FIFTH OF NOVEMBER A GUILD INSPECTOR OF FORTY-FOUR CAME THIRTY-FOUR MILES ON HIS OWN MONEY AND SAID THAT A NOTICE OF WITHDRAWAL CANNOT BE PUT ON A GUILD FORM, AND THAT HE HAS BEEN ASKED ELEVEN TIMES IN ELEVEN WEEKS FOR A FORM AND EVERY ONE OF THE ELEVEN WANTED A COLUMN PUT IN FOR ONE PERSON, AND THAT A FORM WITH A COLUMN IN IT FOR ONE PERSON IS A FORM WITH A MARK ON IT, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID THAT A BOUNDARY WAS BUILT AROUND THE RULE THAT A MARK MAY NOT CARRY A JOB AND IS NOT TO BE RETIRED TO MAKE A FORM WORK.**

---

And then a man of fifty-six asked the inspector in a doorway on the fifth of November what a public office was for, and the inspector said he could not state it, and the room was in the doorway and heard him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the refusal and entered the day and entered that the asker was not Adrian and that the asker has not asked before.

He said he had been asked in a market thirty-four miles east by four trades and could not state it there either, and that he could not say whether the two failures were the same failure, and that four answers that arrived separately are not joined and a man of fifty-six who has asked four people the same question in one month knows the difference and is not going to pretend he does not.

"**I am an inspector. I certify that four people were asked. I cannot tell you what a thing was for and I have stopped apologising for it in about four years.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man outside this district has now declined to state a function for the fourth time in eleven weeks and that the four refusals are his own and are not hers **and that her own count has not moved and stands at twelve, and that a registrar who has been asked once why she holds two lines apart has answered about a reader in four years and not about the office.**

Nobody asked her again, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked her again **and that this is the sixth time in eleven weeks nobody has, and that a count of not-askings is a column and not a bucket and not a protection, and that she had wanted to put the office and the six in one line and had not.**

**AND ON THE FIFTH OF NOVEMBER AN INSPECTOR OF FORTY-FOUR DECLINED FOR THE FOURTH TIME IN ELEVEN WEEKS TO STATE WHAT A PUBLIC OFFICE WAS FOR, AND SAID THAT FOUR TRADES IN A MARKET THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST HAD ASKED HIM THE SAME QUESTION AND HE COULD NOT STATE IT THERE EITHER, AND THAT HE COULD NOT SAY WHETHER THE TWO FAILURES WERE THE SAME FAILURE, AND THAT A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THE REFUSAL AND ENTERED THAT HER OWN COUNT HAS NOT MOVED AND STANDS AT TWELVE.**

AND THE REFUSALS TO JOIN TWO THINGS STAND AT THIRTEEN AND THE THIRTEENTH IS THE FIFTH OF NOVEMBER **AND NOBODY HAS ASKED HER WHY, AND THE FOUR ANSWERS THAT ARRIVED SEPARATELY ARE NOT JOINED AND THE FOUR REFUSALS OF A MAN FROM OUTSIDE THIS DISTRICT ARE NOT JOINED, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED ALL THREE AND DID NOT JOIN ANY OF THEM.**

---

And on the sixth of November the second form of the notice was put on a trestle table on a board of eleven, and it went out nine times on nine hands, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam filled the sixth column of seven of the seven rows he has and the eighth row says nothing said, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man fills a column honestly and that honestly is not a figure and that there is no column for honestly.

A reader at the counter read the second form out three times to three people who had not seen it and told each of them that the season is not named on the sheet and that this is deliberate, and one of the three was a man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing and had come in to see what was on the board, and he said the word season out loud and asked what a notice was that did not have one on it.

She read the line about the season being a length of time and he said that was the first time in his life that a form had told him what it was not, and that he had been handed about four hundred forms and had never been told what one of them was for, and that he was copying nothing and had come to look.

Nobody asked him to copy anything and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing came into a yard to look at a board and was not asked to do anything, and that a man of fifty-six said that a man who comes into a yard to look and is not asked to do anything is the thing this district has been trying to build for eleven weeks, and that it is not an instrument and does not go on any list.

**AND ON THE SIXTH OF NOVEMBER THE SECOND FORM OF THE NOTICE WENT ON A BOARD OF ELEVEN IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AND WENT OUT NINE TIMES ON NINE HANDS, AND A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE WHO SELLS NOTHING AND COPIES FOR NOTHING CAME INTO THE YARD TO LOOK AT IT AND WAS NOT ASKED TO DO ANYTHING, AND HE SAID THAT A FORM WHICH TELLS YOU WHAT IT IS NOT IS THE FIRST ONE HE HAS EVER BEEN HANDED, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT A MAN WHO COMES INTO A YARD TO LOOK AND IS NOT ASKED TO DO ANYTHING IS NOT AN INSTRUMENT AND DOES NOT GO ON ANY LIST.**

AND THE NOTICE HAS NO DATE ON IT FOR WHEN IT IS GOOD FROM **IT HAS A PLACE IN THE MIDDLE OF THE SHEET FOR THE LINE THAT MATTERS, IT DOES NOT NAME A SEASON, AND IT SAYS NOTHING AT ALL ABOUT WHAT THE FRAME DID TO THE PERSON WHO STOOD IN IT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM WROTE THAT ON THE BACK OF IT IN HIS OWN HAND AND WOULD NOT LET IT BE PRINTED.**
