# Chapter 103: Nothing to Read For

The yard in Alder Reach filled up on the evening of the sixteenth of this month before anybody had said what the meeting was for, and about two hundred people stood in it, and the first thing that happened is that eleven of them asked eleven other people what the meeting was for, and the eleven got eleven different answers, and every one of the eleven answers was true.

A man who sells nothing and has a use was in it and had brought a sheet of his own, because he prints on a tram arch and has been printing the office's name on sheets since the sixth of last month, and he had the sheet rolled in his hand and did not unroll it, and a man asked him whether he was going to say something and he said no, and that he had come to hear a man say no, and that there is a difference.

A haulier of about forty was in it and had come up out of the four hours he spends in this yard twice a week, and said afterwards that a haulier's habit is a thing twice a week and not a thing once, and that he had broken his own habit for this and would like the room to know it.

A trader of fifty-one on the lower terrace was at the edge of it and did not come in, and she is the one who has been supplying the Harrow engine's oil out of her own till since the first of last month and who has asked twice that it be said that nobody asked her either.

**And about sixty of the two hundred were people who are not in a household, and a yard is a public place, and nobody in this district has ever counted how many people standing in a public place are not in a row.**

The tenth hand counted them on the sixteenth of this month in about four minutes with a pencil on the back of a sheet and would not give the number to the room and gave it to the clerk afterwards. The clerk entered that he counted and would not say, and entered his reason, which is that a number said out loud in a yard is about half an hour later a number somebody could use, and that he has been in four rooms in nine weeks and has watched two of them find that out.

The asking was done in that yard at about seven in the evening, in about half an hour, standing up, in front of the two hundred, and it was done in the five things, because it is a job and not a favour, and because a man who asks a question like that in a public place has to be asked how, and nobody in this district has ever been asked how.

The asker was a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing. He had waited in a counting room for about seven hours on the fifteenth of this month for a reading that nobody asked him for, and he had not mentioned the waiting to anybody afterwards, and on the sixteenth of this month he asked a man he had met twice whether he would read a piece of cast iron and tell the district what a public office was for.

**The question took about eleven seconds to put and nine minutes to ask properly, and the order of the five things has not changed since a chandler asked it in a yard in the fourth month of this year, and the sixth thing has no name and belongs to a clerk of nineteen years and is not on the wall.**

The five things are what the job is, how long it is, what it costs, what happens to the person who does it, and who is standing in the room. **A person who is asked whether they will do a job is asked all five and the answer is entered whatever it is, and a person who is asked one of the five has been asked a favour.**

He asked them in order and he asked them slowly, and about a third of the two hundred people knew what was happening and the rest of them found out.

"What is the job."

"Read a piece of cast iron and tell the district what a public office was for," said the reader.

"How long."

"About an hour."

"What does it cost."

"The last departure he has," said the reader, and there was no noise in the yard at all, and a woman near the wall said afterwards that she had been in four rooms in nine weeks and had never once heard a price said out loud in a yard with two hundred people in it.

"What happens to the person who does it."

"He will not be able to tell which way a river is going for about an hour and he will have to be walked to a door," said the reader, "**and a man of thirty-nine did that on the thirtieth of last month and would not let go of his arm until he was on the step.**"

"Who is standing in the room."

He said yes to the first four in about four seconds and he said nothing at all to the fifth. The man who asked him noticed and did not press it, and the reason he did not press it is in the minute in his own words and is nine words long.

"**He did not answer the fifth thing and I did not ask him twice, and both of those are in the book and neither of them is a fault.**"

Then the sixth thing, which is what a person ought to know before he says yes, and which has no name and belongs to a clerk of nineteen years and is not on the wall, and which the clerk read out herself because it is hers and she has never once let anybody else read it.

It took about half an hour here and nineteen minutes at the ninth place on the twenty-ninth of last month, and the reason it takes longer in a yard is that about two hundred people are standing in it and about forty of them cannot hear from where they are standing, and she read every sentence twice.

"You have asked a man to spend the last thing he has left on a question about a piece of paper," she said, when she got to the end of it, "**and the man has told two hundred people that he has one left, and the number is in a public book and is not a secret, and that means every person in this yard has been told the price of what you are asking about a quarter of an hour before you asked, and that is the first time in nine weeks that has happened, and it has happened because a man published an hour in June and a clerk published a figure in July.**"

**And the finding of the sixteenth of this month is that a price can be published in a yard a quarter of an hour before a man is asked to pay it, and that it happened because of two ordinary pieces of administration and not because anybody decided to do it.**

Nobody argued with that. The minute enters that nobody argued with it, and that about a third of the yard did not know the figure before the clerk said it, and that about a third of the yard had known it and had come to find out whether anybody else did, and that the remaining third had known it and had come because they did not believe it.

---

Then the room waited, and about two hundred people waited, and the waiting took about four minutes, and in the fourth minute something arrived that nobody in that yard had seen arrive before.

> **A READING MAY BE SPENT ON ONE LOT YOU HAVE TOUCHED. IT COSTS ABOUT AN HOUR AND A COST, AND THE COST IS A PART OF THAT LOT'S TOLL.**
>
> **THE TOLL ON THIS IRON IS THE ORIGIN. AN ORIGIN TAKES MEMORY OF DEPARTURE. YOU HAVE PAID IT THREE TIMES AND YOU HAVE ONE LEFT, AND ONE IS A NUMBER AND NOT A PRINCIPLE.**
>
> **THE IRON DOES NOT HOLD PLACES AND WILL NOT NAME ONE. IT WILL NOT ANSWER WHAT A PUBLIC OFFICE WAS FOR EITHER, AND IT WILL GIVE YOU THE SENTENCE YOU ALREADY HAVE IF YOU ASK IT FOR MORE. IF YOU READ IT YOU WILL HAVE SPENT THE LAST THING YOU HAVE TO SPEND TO BE TOLD A THING YOU ALREADY HAVE IN A ROOM.**
>
> **A REFUSAL IS A READING. IT IS ENTERED. IT IS NOT A FINDING AND IT IS NOT NOTHING, AND YOU STILL HAVE TO WALK OUT OF THE ROOM AND LIVE IN THE DISTRICT YOU DID NOT READ FOR.**

**A rule arrived in a yard in front of about two hundred people, and nobody in that yard had asked for it, and the district entered that fact before it entered anything else about it.**

Nobody in the yard saw where it came from. The tenth hand wrote down afterwards that four people who were in that yard said afterwards that they had been looking at his face and had not been listening, and that he had not moved while it was there.

The man who asked the question said afterwards that he had not noticed it either, and that he had noticed the man not moving and had thought nothing of it, and that he thought about it at about four in the afternoon the next day and then wrote it down because he could not think of anything else to do with it.

The registrar entered the arrival in one line and the line is in her own hand and the line says that a rule arrived in a public yard on the sixteenth of July in front of about two hundred people, and that the room is entering that it arrived and not what anybody thought of it, and that a rule that arrives in a yard has not been asked by anybody and that is the first time this district has entered that about anything.

---

And then he said no, and it took him about eleven seconds, and the yard was quiet for the whole of it.

"I am not going to do it," he said. "**And I am not going to give you a reason about courage, because I have heard that reason five times in nine weeks and it is not true and I would like it stopped being said in my name.**"

He did the arithmetic out loud, which he has done every time, and about two hundred people heard a man do arithmetic about himself in a yard.

"**March was a direction. April was which gate I went out of my own city by, on the seventeenth of February at six in the morning, and it has not come back. The twenty-ninth of last month was the twenty-second of last month at about six in the morning, and that was a walk I did not finish, and I turned back at the second changeover and came down the bank again.**"

"**And there is one left and it is not mine to spend as I like. It is the last one, and I am the only person alive who can spend it, and that is a sentence about me and it is not a sentence about the district, and I would like the room to keep the two of those apart.**"

Then the reason, and it is the fourth of the five things and not the sixth, and he gave it in eleven words.

"**The iron does not hold offices and it does not hold names and it never has, and I have read it three times and it has said ELEVEN USES twice and a sentence about a bracket once.**"

A man at the back said the obvious thing, and it is in the minute, and nobody argued with it afterwards.

"Then what is the good of reading it tonight."

"**There was no good of reading it tonight, and that is the whole of my refusal, and it took me eleven seconds, and I want it written in those words and not in any others, because if I give you a better reason than that one then somebody will use the better reason on a day when there is a reason and there will not be one.**"

Nobody in that yard wrote anything down for about a minute and a half. Then the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down, and she is the sitting steward of Lot 17, and she has never taken a line out of anything in nineteen years.

And then the yard did the thing that a yard does, which is about nineteen people at once asked him a question, and he answered all of them, and only one of the answers is in the book.

The one that is in the book was asked by a boy of seventeen who is paid four coppers a quarter for an index and who is not Adrian Vale's apprentice.

"Can you still close one after tonight."

"No," he said. "**I can still write the terms of one and I cannot close one, and the difference between those two is about four seconds and a piece of paper and the piece of paper is the whole of what I have got.**"

"Why is that."

"**Because the closing is the part where somebody who is not me agrees that a thing is finished, and there is nobody in this yard tonight who has anything to do with whether it is finished, and I have found out this evening that I do not have a way of asking for that either.**"

Nobody in the yard wrote any of that down except one man, and the man wrote it down, and the entry is in his own hand and it is nine lines long and it is the only part of that evening entered by anybody who was not on the platform of the asking.

> **REFUSED. THE SIXTEENTH OF JULY. ASKED IN A YARD IN THE FIVE THINGS BY A MAN WHO MENDS FENCING AND ANSWERED IN FOUR SECONDS. THE COST OF A REFUSAL IS NO COST AND NO TIME. THE COST OF THE DECISION IS THAT NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAD ANYTHING THAT WAS GOING TO COME OUT OF A READING, AND THIS IS THE FIRST TIME IN NINE WEEKS THAT A MAN HAS REFUSED SOMETHING NOBODY WAS RELYING ON.**
>
> **A REFUSAL TO READ IS A READING AND IS ENTERED. IT IS NEITHER COWARDICE NOR DISCIPLINE AND IT IS THE SIXTH. THE FIVE BEFORE IT WERE ENTERED IN THE SAME WAY. THE INTERVALS HAVE GONE UP ON USE AND I WILL NOT DEFEND THAT AND I WILL NOT CALL IT ANYTHING ELSE, AND THAT SENTENCE IS ON A SEPARATE SHEET IN HIS OWN HAND AND IT IS READ OUT AT THE COUNTER AFTER EVERY ONE OF THE SIX.**

The separate sheet is a separate sheet. Ivo Stenn wrote it and it is in his own hand and the clerk read it out at the ward market counter on the morning of the eighteenth of this month in the ordinary voice to about thirty people, and the ledger was read out after it.

**A refusal is a reading, and it is entered, and the number of them is six, and the cost of the sixth was nothing at all, and there is now no date in the book for the next one and there may not be one for a long time.**

The ledger is one for April, none for May, one for the twenty-ninth of last month, and the number of departures left is **one**.

A woman at the back of the counter said the sentence the counter has heard before, and the counter read it out again anyway, because the rule of that counter is that if anybody has told you a different number, bring it and it will be read out in the words you did not bring.

"He has one," she said.

"He has one," said the reader at the counter, who is about fifty and is a signal clerk before her shoulder went and is on eleven coppers a quarter for four days a week, and the term has no clause for a shoulder. "**And it is not before the fifteenth of this month any more and it is not before anything at all now, because the fifteenth came and went and nobody asked him and he did not offer, and the next line in that book has got nothing in it and nobody is going to put a day in it until somebody has a reason.**"

---

And the finding of the sixteenth of this month is that a room which has been refused has to do the next thing without the man, and the next thing took about nineteen minutes and it was not a reading.

The yard did not go home. About sixty of them stayed, and a clerk of nineteen years stood in the middle of them and asked one question, and it is in the minute, and it is nine words long, and it is the plainest sentence anybody in this district has written in nine weeks.

"**You cannot read it. So who do we ask.**"

Nobody in that yard had an answer. A man who had been standing in it said afterwards that the reason nobody had an answer was that every person who had come to the yard that evening had come to watch a man do a thing, and that there were about nine people in that yard who were there to be asked, and eight of them had not been asked anything all night.

"Somebody has to be the first," said the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, "**and it cannot be him, because he has one thing left and the one thing he has left is not a person anybody can be sent to, and it cannot be a board, because a board cannot ask a person anything, and a toll board is a board.**"

"It is a yard," said the tenth hand. "**And a yard can ask a person, and that is the whole of the difference between the two things, and it is the whole of what has gone wrong in this district since the eighth of March.**"

Two people in that yard said afterwards that they had understood, at the time, that they were about to be asked something, and that neither of them had been asked for nine weeks, and one of them said that it was the first time in this district that a person had been able to see a question coming.

He left about half past eight and walked to the end of the lane and came back once, and nobody followed him except a man of thirty-four who said afterwards that he had followed him to find out whether a man who had just refused two hundred people went and did something, and that he did not, and that he stood at the end of a lane for about eleven minutes.

"I wanted to be asked again by somebody smaller," he said, when he was asked about it on the eighteenth and again on the twenty-first, and he gave the same answer both times. "**And nobody smaller came, and that is not a finding about this district, it is a fact about a lane.**"

---

And what the yard did on the evening of the sixteenth and again on the evening of the seventeenth was ask people questions, and the first question anybody asked in about two hundred yards of this district in nine weeks was not about the office.

It was about a hundred and eighty-three coppers.

The man who sells nothing and has a use came back on the seventeenth with the sheet unrolled, and it was not his sheet, and it was a page out of the ward market's own book that a clerk had let him copy in a corner of a yard on the fourteenth because he asked nicely and had asked before.

"You are all going to ask the wrong people," he said, and about nine people asked him why, and he unrolled it, and it was a page with a heading at the top and nothing under the heading.

> **THE NINTH PLACE. TWO HUNDRED COPPERS A QUARTER, COLLECTED ON THE SEVENTH OF MAY. SIX ON A SIGN. ELEVEN ON A BOUND COPY OF THE LINE BOOK. ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THREE ON NOTHING. NO ANSWER AGAINST THE FIGURE. THE ANSWER IS NOT TO BE STRUCK.**

"That has been in a book since the third of this month," he said, "**and it has been read out at a counter once, and I have stood in three rooms this week and in all three of them everybody was talking about a piece of cast iron.**"

Nobody argued with him and the minute enters that nobody argued with him, and that a man who sells nothing said afterwards that he had wanted to be wrong about it and had checked the sum twice on the way down the lane, and that a sum is the easiest thing in this district to be wrong about and that he had checked it because he would rather be wrong in a lane than be right in a room.

Two hundred coppers a quarter was collected at the ninth place on the seventh of last month out of a pail of change by a man who does not read figures and who was given the figure and not the reason. **Six of it went on lettering, eleven on a bound copy of the line book, and a hundred and eighty-three on nothing**, and the entry of the third of this month carries the figure with no answer against it, and the answer is not to be struck.

The asking is four days long and it is the other half of the fifth instrument, and the fifth instrument is a way of saying that a toll lands on a place, and what a yard found on the seventeenth of this month in about an hour and a half is that **about nine hundred households in this district are in no row, that nineteen people at the ninth place are not in a household, that thirty-one people live on ground held by nobody, and that none of those three numbers is a number of people who can be handed a sheet.**
