# Chapter 141: The Salt Came West

---

A cart came west over the raised plank road on the morning of the twenty-second of November with about nine hundred and forty pounds of salt in it, and the man driving it got down off the seat before anybody spoke to him and said that the figure was his own weight and not the cart's and that he had checked it twice at the sluice end of the loams and would not swear to it a third time, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had said all of that before he was asked for any of it and that nobody asked him.**

**AND THE CROP SURVIVED, AND THE CHANNEL THE CROP WAS GOING TO SURVIVE ON DID NOT RUN, AND BOTH FACTS CAME WEST IN ONE CART, AND THE ENTRY OF THE TWENTY-SECOND OF NOVEMBER SAYS THEY ARE NOT JOINED AND NOBODY HAS ASKED WHY THEY CAME IN ONE CART.**

The man driving the cart is about forty-one and has done the thirty-four miles eleven times this year, and a man of fifty-six said that the eleven is a count of journeys and is not a count of salt and is not a count of anything anybody can be paid on, and that the four extra miles over the loams are a rise of about ninety feet that a loaded cart does not enjoy, and a clerk of nineteen years asked him to say it once and he said it once.

A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale at the sluice end of the loams and has stood at it nine years was in the yard of Lot 17 on the twenty-second of November because the salt had to be weighed somewhere that was not the Verge, **and she was not asked to come and was not paid and did not say she wanted to be and was not asked whether she was right about any of the three.**

She weighed it in four hours and got nine hundred and forty pounds both times and did not say it was right, and a man of fifty-six asked her what a pan is, on the ground that the room had been using the word for eleven weeks without asking anybody, and she said it in a sentence and then said it again in a shorter one, and the clerk wrote down both and neither of them was the same length as the other.

"**A pan is a place the salt comes out of and it holds about as much as the man standing beside it can lift out in two goes, and the number of them does not say how much salt is in any of them.**"

Nobody argued with her and a man of fifty-six asked what the standing count is, **and she gave it and said it was the standing count in September and had not been recounted since, and that a count of pans is a count of pans and is not a count of salt, and that there is no rate in this basin that turns one into the other and she has looked for one for nine years standing at a scale.**

And on the twenty-second of November the standing count of pans on the Verge was given again and was the count of September, being ninety pans, sixty-one of them standing in river water, six frames, and all six of the frames have sills since the twenty-first of September. The other twenty-three are on the high side and have been out of the water since the flood, and fourteen of the twenty-three have not been counted since the flood either, and nobody counts them, and the entry says that is a fact about who counts and is not a mystery and is not a failure and is not to be asked about a second time.

A man of fifty-six put the two figures on a board and did not add them, and was asked why by a man of thirty-four who mends fencing, and he said it in fifteen words **and a clerk of nineteen years did not count them and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got fifteen.**

"**A count of pans and a count of pounds are two counts and not one.**"

---

The third channel did not run, and the salt that came west came out of the sixty-one and not out of the third channel, and the nine households that took the two thousand coppers on the twenty-fourth of September are nine households thirty-four miles east who are in the fourth month of not having what they paid for and have not been asked about it since the twenty-fifth of September.

A man of fifty-six read the figure of two thousand coppers out in a yard on the twenty-second of November and then read the four hundred coppers out after it, and then read the difference of one thousand six hundred out after that, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered all three and entered that she had put them in one line on purpose and that this was the second time in eleven weeks, and that the three are not added to the twenty-one years and are not added to each other.**

Nobody asked her why she had put them in one line. A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace said that the four hundred coppers is not in the market's own book and is not in the ledger, and that the two thousand coppers is in nine households' mouths and in nobody's book, and that a figure that is in nine mouths is a figure a district can lose in a winter.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF NOVEMBER THE FIGURE OF TWO THOUSAND COPPERS WAS READ OUT IN A YARD AND THE FIGURE OF FOUR HUNDRED COPPERS WAS READ OUT AFTER IT AND THE DIFFERENCE OF ONE THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED COPPERS WAS READ OUT AFTER THAT, AND NONE OF THE THREE IS ADDED TO THE OTHER AND NONE OF THE THREE IS ADDED TO THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS.**

The ninth household is still the only one of the nine that has understood the finding about a private loom since the twenty-fifth of September, **and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said in a yard on the twenty-second that it is also still the only one of the nine that has never been asked a second time, because it did not understand, and that a household which is only asked once is a household the district is not managing.**

He said the district should ask it. A man of fifty-six said the same and then said the other half, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had been asked in six things whether she was going to make the room ask the same question twice and had said that a clerk does not make a room do anything and that a clerk who starts asking on behalf of a room is a clerk who is running the room.

It was not argued with and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the ninth household is nine miles from the sluice **and that it has heard about the cart from a neighbour like everybody else, and that if it comes here it will come here on its own, and that the district has no business making a person arrive so that it can ask them something.**

The clerk entered that, and she entered that the count of not-askings about the ninth household is a column of its own and is not the count about the woman of forty-five and is not added to it, and that the count about the woman of forty-five stands at four with one standing refusal and did not move, and that the woman of forty-five was not asked on the twenty-second of November and her number did not appear.

---

The board outside the room has three days on it in a hand that cannot be read, and the three days are the twenty-eighth of September, the twenty-sixth of October and the twenty-fourth of November, and the twenty-fourth of November is two days away, **and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said on the twenty-second that a room whose only instrument runs out on a date is a room that can be ended by a clerk, and that this is the first time in eleven weeks that a room's own instrument could end the room.**

A man of fifty-six asked what happens on the twenty-fifth if nobody writes anything, and the man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that a room with a date on it and no date after it is a room that has stopped saying when, and that this district had a railway held by a date and not by a need in the middle of the year and knows what that is and it is written down in a book a stranger can take cold.

It was not argued with and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that she wrote the three days and was not asked to write them and did not offer, **and that nobody has asked her why she wrote them in a hand that cannot be read, and that she is not going to say, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had said she would not say and that nobody was going to ask her again.**

And on the twenty-second of November a board outside the room had two days left on it, being the twenty-third and the twenty-fourth, and nobody had written a fourth day, and the fourth day was written in December in a hand that could be read, and there are thirty days between the twenty-fourth of November and the twenty-fourth of December, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the thirty days are a gap and not a finding and that nobody has entered what the room did with them.

---

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was asked on the twenty-second of November, in the six things, to be the clerk of the ledger, and he said no in four seconds, **and it is the second time he has been asked and the answer was in the same four words as the first and in the same order.**

The man of fifty-six asked him for the reason, on the ground that a man who says no twice owes the room the second reason, and he gave it and it is in the minute in his own words and it is fifty-two words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got fifty-two both times and did not say so.

"**I keep a board that says what a toll lands on a place and it has no name on it. If I am the clerk of a book that says what a toll lands on a person then I am the clerk of the other one and I will not do it.**"

Nobody took it up. 

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF NOVEMBER A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING WAS ASKED TO BE THE CLERK OF THE LEDGER AND SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS FOR THE SECOND TIME IN NINE WEEKS, AND SAID THAT HE KEEPS A BOARD THAT SAYS WHAT A TOLL LANDS ON A PLACE AND THAT IT HAS NO NAME ON IT AND THAT HE WILL NOT ALSO BE THE CLERK OF A BOOK THAT SAYS WHAT A TOLL LANDS ON A PERSON, AND THE TWO ARE NOT JOINED.**

---

A woman of about forty-seven who sells salt out of a barrow at the ninth place was in the yard on the twenty-second and was not on any board and was not asked to be, and she asked one question, and it was the only question she asked all morning, and it went into the minute in her own words and it is twenty-seven words.

"**Which channel did it come out of, and is the one that did not run the same one, and if it is then who has the other.**"

A man of fifty-six answered the first half in seventeen words and did not answer the second half,

"**Out of the sixty-one. The sixty-one is the old cut and the third is the new one.**"

And a clerk of nineteen years entered that the second half was not answered and that it was not refused, and that a question which is not answered is not a question that has been put into a column, and that there is no column for it and none was made.

The second channel ran. The third channel did not. A man of fifty-six said both figures out loud and did not join them, and the woman of about forty-seven who sells salt out of a barrow at the ninth place said that she did not know what joining them would be and had not asked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

**And on the twenty-second of November a woman of about forty-seven who sells salt out of a barrow at the ninth place and is not on any board asked one question and got seventeen words back and the other half of it was not answered, and it was not refused, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a question which is not answered is not a question that has been put into a column and that there is no column for it and none was made.**

---

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in the yard on the twenty-second and had come to find out whether there would be salt at the houses she carries to, and she carries to about ninety houses, **and she was not asked whether she wanted the job of finding out and was not asked whether she was right about the number ninety and has never been.**

She said the salt is worth nothing to ninety houses in the fourth month and that what ninety houses want in the fourth month is not salt, and that the second channel ran, and that a person who has been at the second channel all year is a person who has been wet since the first of February.

Nobody asked her why that mattered and she said it anyway, **and a man of fifty-six said that a thing which is worth nothing in the fourth month and everything in the eighth is a thing nobody in this district can price, and that a page of a hundred and eighty-three coppers sits beside it, and that the two are in two books and are not added and that there is no rate.**

The hundred and eighty-three coppers is a page in the ward market's own book with a heading and no name against it. It was not read out on the twenty-second of November and nobody refused, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not read out and that the answer is not to be struck and that a figure is not made wrong by being in a book by itself.

---

A man of fifty-six said two things in one sentence on the twenty-second of November and a clerk of nineteen years did not join them, **and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud that the clerk had been asked to join them and had not and that the count of that has not moved and stands at thirteen, and that her own count has not moved and stands at twelve, and that neither count moved today because a person who does not join a thing while she is writing it is not refusing it.**

She is writing it. The refusal is a thing said out loud to a room and this is a thing written down on one line, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two are different instruments and that she is not going to make them the same instrument because it would be tidier, and that the office's name is in five places and in no register and that she has not been asked about the office and is not going to be.

Nobody asked her, **and that is the seventh time in eleven weeks nobody has, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing entered it and a man of fifty-six said that the number is the only number in this district that has gone up by not doing anything, and that it is the only one he would defend in a room, and that a registrar who is asked why eleven times and answers once has worked out what the question is for, and that nobody in this district has worked it out in eleven weeks.**

---

The train on the siding had stood two hundred and eighty-four days on the twenty-second of November, and a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and beside the figures there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date, and nobody said so and nobody was asked what the rule under the sixth date is for.

The man of about nineteen who walked in from the road asked the boy about the rule on the twenty-second, in four minutes, **and the boy said it was a rule, and the man of nineteen entered that he had been told the same thing on the third of November and on the fifteenth and now on the twenty-second, and that three is a count and that he had stopped asking after the third one on his own and nobody told him to.**

the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale at the sluice end of the loams said that a rule under the sixth of six is the only kind of rule anybody can look at from both sides, and that she has a scale with a mark under the ninth stone and has never once been asked why the ninth, and that the ninth is where her mother's house was and that she has never told the room and told it twice in one morning and was not asked either time.

She was not asked about the ninth stone. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and the count of protected things is five and the boy of seventeen is in neither and neither moved.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF NOVEMBER THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR DAYS, WHICH IS SEVENTEEN DAYS LEFT IN FEBRUARY AFTER THE ELEVENTH, THEN ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR DAYS TO THE THIRTY-FIRST OF AUGUST, WHICH IS TWO HUNDRED AND ONE, THEN THIRTY FOR SEPTEMBER, THEN THIRTY-ONE FOR OCTOBER, THEN TWENTY-TWO FOR NOVEMBER, AND EVERY FIGURE IS BUILT FROM THE ELEVENTH OF FEBRUARY AND NONE IS CARRIED FORWARD OUT OF ANY BOOK.**

---

A man of fifty-six read out on the twenty-second of November, in a lane, the six askings that carry a minute-figure, being fifty-one, thirty-nine, forty-four, thirty-one, twenty-six and forty-four, which is two hundred and thirty-five minutes in all, and he read them and then said that no seventh asking was made in November and that the two people who hold the boundary at the west end were not asked again in November either.

The term runs from the twenty-ninth of September to the thirty-first of March and it is in the market's own book and it has not moved, **and the two dates on one line are the thirty-first of March and the name of the man it is against, and a man of fifty-six said that this district has two dates left and both of them are in March, and that a room which has run out of dates is in the same position as a room which never had any, and that this room is not in that position and has one day.**

Nobody took it up. 

---

The last entry of the twenty-second of November is nine lines long and it is in the clerk of nineteen years' own hand **and it was read out twice at the ward market counter by a reader who is about fifty, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and a man of fifty-six asked her whether she was going to read it three times and she said no and gave the reason in six words and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got six.**

"**A cart is not a finding.**"

Nobody said anything about that for about nine seconds and a man of fifty-six said that it is the best sentence anybody has said in a lane in three weeks, and that a finding is a thing a room agrees to carry and a cart is a thing that arrives, and that this district has been treating arrivals as findings since the thirteenth of August and that this is the fourth time it has been wrong about one.

And a stranger who has been in that yard four times in ten weeks took a fourth copy of the ledger off the board of eleven without being asked, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in this district has ever asked a stranger what he intends to use a copy for, and that there is no column for it, and that the not-asking is in the count and the count is where it was. THE ENTRY OF THE TWENTY-SECOND OF NOVEMBER HAS NO FINDING IN IT.**
