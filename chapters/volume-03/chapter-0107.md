# Chapter 107: Nothing Is Running

The month turned on the first of this month and nobody in Alder Reach put a line in a book about it, for the second time in a year, and the reason is the same reason it was not written down at the first of last month, which is that the printed timetable prints days of the month and not days of the week and gives the reason on its face.

The line book at the east end has nine hundred and six movements in it and one more, and the one more is the twenty-ninth of last month, and there is nothing in it for the twenty-ninth of this month because nothing moved, and there is nothing in it for the thirtieth or the thirty-first or the first of this month either.

The first thing found in a public book in Alder Reach is a new month, and a clerk of nineteen years entered it on the second of this month in one line.

**The line says that the thirtieth of July, the thirty-first of July and the first, second and third of August contain no movements, and that this is the first week since the eighth of March in which that was true, and that a clerk of nineteen years wrote it and would like it read out at a counter on the morning of the fourth.**

The train on that siding had stood a hundred and sixty-eight days on the twenty-ninth of last month, a hundred and seventy on the thirty-first, and a hundred and seventy-three on the third of this month, and the boy of seventeen wrote all three figures in his index and struck none of the old ones.

---

The four boards stood in the rain for three days and nothing was written on any of them, and the finding of the second of this month is about why, and it is a finding a man of about fifty who keeps the fourth one has been asking for since the eighth of last month and has now got.

He is a signalman of a bridge administration and he is not a bearer and he is not paid, and he has said since the eighth of last month that keeping a board is not holding a pair, and he has wiped the bottom line of his own board when it is wet about four times a month and has never once been asked to explain it to anybody.

"I have been wiping that column since the twenty-second of last month," he said, in a yard, on the second, to about nineteen people, "**and for about a month the reason I wiped it was that there was a number in it and I did not agree with the number. And since the twenty-ninth of last month the reason I have wiped it is that there is a sentence in it and I do not agree with the sentence, and neither of those reasons is the reason the board was put up for.**"

"And what is the reason the board was put up for."

"**Nobody has ever told me and I have stopped expecting to be told, and the thing I have found out this week is that a column that is empty because nobody can state it and a column that is empty because there is nothing to state are the same four feet of board, and I have been keeping the difference alive in my own head for a month and it turns out not to be a thing you can keep.**"

The other three boards were checked on the second of August by three people who were not him, and the finding is in the minute and it is nine words long.

The three of them are wiped and the fourth one is wiped, and all four have been wiped since the thirtieth, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went and looked at all four of them on the second of August and took him about an hour and a half and wrote down that he had looked.

The keeper of the ninth place's board is a paperer of about thirty-eight who comes up that bank once a month for her own trade and who has a hook and a sacking sheet. The keeper of the board at the east end is a clerk of the ward market who is not the market and who keeps the line book in the hours the counter is shut. The keeper of the third board is a signalman of about thirty-one of the district's own crew, and he is the one who holds the third of the three weeks, and his week ran out at midnight on the twenty-eighth of last month, and he asked for the board back on the first of August and was given it.

---

And then on the afternoon of the second of this month the district had the argument it had been avoiding since the twenty-ninth of June, and the argument took about two hours and it was in a public yard and about forty people were in it, and there were two positions and neither of them had standing, and that is the whole of the finding.

The first position is that the iron comes out of the bracket.

The second position is that it stays.

The man who argued for it coming out said the sentence that this district has already written down once, and he said it without knowing he had said it before, and about four people in the yard knew, and one of them told him afterwards.

"**A security which is spent on the thing it secures is not a security any more.** It has been in that bracket since the twenty-ninth of last month and there is no charter and there is no term and there is no week and there is no bearer, and a piece of cast iron in a bracket with nobody holding anything is not an instrument. **It is a paperweight.**"

The other side was argued by a woman of about fifty-one who keeps the only pair on that line, and she argued it in eleven words and then sat down, and the eleven words are in the minute.

"**There is one bracket on this line that it fits and it is in a frame two miles west and it took six hours and a quarter in the wet to bring it down here in June. Take it out and it goes back up, and then it comes down again, and every one of those four journeys is a day somebody does not get paid for.**"

"And it is a paperweight."

"**It is a paperweight and it is the only paperweight in this district that can throw a set of points, and the finding of the twenty-seventh of last month was that the bracket was empty for seven weeks because nobody could name a night, and we named a night and it was good, and now the term has gone and there is no night to name.**"

Nobody in that yard could say who was right, and the reason nobody could say is the finding of the second of this month and it is one sentence long.

**There is no one in Alder Reach who is allowed to decide this, and the entry of the second of August says that a thing that nobody is allowed to decide is decided by a date, and that the date is the twenty-ninth of August, and that the twenty-ninth of August is now the next day this district owns.**

It is on a sheet of paper and it went up on the same day, and it cost four coppers, and the man who printed it was not thanked and did not ask to be.

> **ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF AUGUST SOMEBODY WILL HAVE TO DECIDE WHETHER THE IRON COMES OUT OF THE BRACKET. NOBODY IN ALDER REACH IS ALLOWED TO DECIDE IT. THE IRON IS A PAPERWEIGHT UNTIL SOMEBODY WITH STANDING SAYS IT IS NOT, AND THERE IS NOBODY WITH STANDING, AND THE DATE IS THE ONLY THING ON THIS SHEET THAT ANYBODY CAN ARGUUE WITH.**

The man of thirty-eight was in that yard and was asked, in front of about forty people, whether he could decide it, and he said no, and the reason is in the minute and it is nineteen words long.

"**I can write the terms of a thing and I can close a transfer and I can post something somebody else can go and look at. I am not one of the four bearers, I am not a keeper of a board and I am not on the committee, and the line on page one saying I am not one of the four bearers is still true of me four months after it was written.**"

"And if somebody asked you to advise."

"**Then I would advise and I would write it down as advice and not as a decision, and about four years from now a stranger would read the advice and the decision would be missing, and that is the correct condition for advice to be in.**"

And a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, who is not a bearer and has never been a bearer, said the half of it that nobody else had said, and it is in the minute.

"**You have spent nine weeks learning how to ask a person whether they will do a job, and about four of you have been in this yard for a hundred and twenty days, and there is nobody in this yard who is allowed to ask anybody to take the iron out of that bracket, and that is not a failure of the asking. That is the asking working.**"

---

And on the third of this month the first consequence of a railway that is not running arrived on foot, and it arrived in the shape of a man of about forty-five who drives salt.

He came eleven miles and he came on the second and he stood in a yard for about two hours before anybody spoke to him, because nobody in that yard had known he was there, and the finding of the third of this month is that a person from outside this district standing in a public place for two hours is invisible, and that this district has spent nine weeks learning to see the people inside its own boundary.

He is a hauler of salt and the pans on the Verge have been standing in river water since the flood year came up, and two of them have gone to salt on their own, and salt is the one thing this basin cannot do without for eleven months of the year, and the district eats what it can reach.

He showed them the salt. He had about nine pounds of it in a sack on a cart and he put a handful on the boards of Lot 17 in a public yard, in daylight, in front of about nineteen people, and he said what it was before anybody asked him, and nobody had to ask.

"**That is off a pan that nobody has touched since the water came up, and it came out of the ground in about nine weeks, and there are two more of them doing it and a third one that has been doing it since before I came west.**"

"And what happens when the third one goes."

"**Then the Verge has four pans out of about ninety and no way to get at them, and this district has a hundred and eighty coppers a quarter of printed matter and a road that runs four times a month, and I have been trying to work out for two years which of those two things is going to run out first and it is not going to be the road.**"

Nobody argued with him and a clerk of nineteen years wrote it down in his own words and read it out in the ordinary voice, and the reading-back took about half an hour because he was asked three times to repeat a word, and the word was *pans*, and the reason he was asked three times is that he has been saying it for two years and this is the first time anybody has written it down.

**Nobody from the Verge has ever been up that bank and nobody from this district has ever been east of the about four thousand and one households, and the man said so himself before he was asked, in about nine seconds, and then he asked his question and the question is nine words long.**

"**Can the water at your place be counted.**"

"Counted how."

"**Not how much. How often. Whether anybody has ever written down when it was high and who was standing there.**"

Nobody in that yard could answer it and the man who drives salt said he had expected that, and then he said the thing that is the reason this volume is going to be about looms and not about trains.

"**We have got water we cannot put anywhere and salt we cannot get, and the two of them are on the same river eleven miles apart, and I have walked here twice this year to ask a question that four people in a yard cannot answer, and the fourth time I come I will not walk, I will take two men and a cart.**"

He said it in the ordinary voice and about nineteen people wrote it down and one of them is a clerk of nineteen years, and the entry of the third of August says that a threat is not a demand and that this was entered as a fact about a journey and not as an intention, and that the market has four thousand and one households and cannot move a cart to a salt pan and has never been asked to.

And then the man of thirty-eight asked him one question, which he had not been asked all afternoon, and which he answered in about four seconds.

"What would a count of it be worth to you."

"Nothing," he said. "**I have two pans of my own on the Verge and no water in them since the flood and I have been buying salt at the ward market for two years at about nine coppers a pound, and I have come here twice to ask a question, and if you gave me the count I would not know what to do with it. I would want to know whether the water is coming or going, and a count of when a person was standing there does not tell me that.**"

"Then what would tell you that."

"**Somebody who is standing there every day, writing down nothing, for a year.**"

Nobody in that yard wrote that sentence down for about four minutes and then a clerk of nineteen years wrote it down, and the finding of the third of August is that a thing this district does not have and has never had a name for is **a person who is standing somewhere every day and is not paid to and is not a bearer and is not a keeper and is not in a row, and the fourth place has had one since the ninth of last month, and he is nineteen, and he says he is nobody's keeper, and nobody has ever asked him whether he would do it for a year.**

He is the boy at the fourth place. He is in the crowd and he did not say anything and two people noticed that he was in the crowd and wrote it down.

---

And the rest of the first week was nothing happening, and the nothing was written down, which is the first time this district has written down a week for what it did not contain.

On the first of August about nineteen people went up the bank and stood on thirty feet of board in the dark and lit the lamp themselves and did not move a train. On the second, the board at the ninth place was wiped and three tolls were still in its column. On the third, the stall was measured for the fourth time.

**And the boy of seventeen asked, on the third of August, to be asked to write the week down properly in a room, and he had asked for that on the tenth of this month and had not been asked, and the tenth hand asked him on the third of August, in a counting room over a chandler's shop, in front of about nineteen people, and the boy wrote it down in about four minutes in his own hand and read it out.**

> **THE THIRTIETH OF JULY, THE THIRTY-FIRST OF JULY, AND THE FIRST, SECOND AND THIRD OF AUGUST. NO MOVEMENTS. NOTHING RAN. FOUR BOARDS WIPED AND ONE OF THEM WIPED BY A MAN WHO HAS KEPT IT SINCE THE EIGHTH OF JUNE. A STALL MEASURED FOUR TIMES AND NOT MOVED. A MAN CAME ELEVEN MILES WITH NINE POUNDS OF SALT AND STOOD IN A YARD FOR TWO HOURS AND WAS NOT SPOKEN TO.**

"**And the sixth thing is a piece of cast iron in a bracket that nobody is allowed to take out, and I have written that in and I would like somebody to say whether a week is a thing that goes in a column.**"

Nobody said. The minute says nobody said, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that a week is a period and a column is for things that go the same way, and that five of the six things in that column go the same way and the sixth one does not, and that the boy is seventeen and has been right four times in nine weeks and wrong about nothing anybody can find.

---

And the man who posted the security did not do anything on the first of August, and that is the first time in nine weeks that he has had a day with nothing in it, and about four people noticed and one of them wrote it down.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing found him at the end of a lane at about eleven in the morning on the first and asked him what he was doing, and he said he was walking to the end of a lane, and the man asked him why, and he gave an answer that took about four seconds and is in the minute.

"**Because on the thirtieth of last month I had a day a week and a term of labour and a term of stock and three bound copies of a book on three shelves, and on the first of this month I have none of it, and I did not find out that I needed one of those four things until about eleven o'clock this morning, which is a thing I could have found out on the twenty-ninth of June by asking somebody a question and did not.**"

"And what did you find out."

"**That I have been a job for a month and not a person in a district, and that the difference did not show up until the job stopped, and that this is the fifth time in nine weeks that something in this district has only shown up when it stopped, and the first one was a train.**"

Nobody said anything else about it and the entry of the first of August says that, and that a man who is paid nothing for a day has been found out by the day stopping and not by the day being hard, and that the district has no instrument for finding that out before it happens and has never claimed to.

---

And the fourth of August was a day off in this district, and the fourth day of a month is the day the keeper of a board starts, and nobody had told him that, and it is in a book from April that he has read and has never told anybody he has read.

He was asked about it on the third, in a yard, by a man who had looked at the three other boards and had come back to him, and he said he knew, and that he had known since April, and that the reason he had never said it out loud is that a keeper who says out loud that he is going to start on a day is a keeper who has put himself in a position, and a man who is not paid and is not a bearer and is not anybody's keeper has about one position left and it is the one where he does what he says he will do on the day he said it.

He said that in about forty seconds and then he asked the man who had asked him a question that was not the one about the boards, and it is in the minute and it is eleven words long, and the answer to it is in the next chapter.

---

And what else the district did in the first week of August it did very badly and in public, and the badness is in the book.

The line was walked on the fourth of this month by two men in one day and the standing instruction is discharged and not satisfied, because a thing that reports itself and a thing that is walked are two different instruments and this district has got the second one and has still not got the first. **Twelve places, eleven boards, and one place that does not report itself, which is the Harrow end, where the plinth is on the embankment thirty yards east of the engine house with the bolt-holes in it and a cap of lead over them.**

The stall that did not walk west on the twenty-ninth of last month was measured again on the third of this month by the boy of seventeen with a tape, and it is where the train stood, and it is thirty-one feet long, and the platform is thirty feet long, and nobody knows why and it has been measured four times.

The span at St. Orra is still shut, its instrument certificate is still suspended by two people who are not Selik Marne, the middle plank of the old footbridge is still in the service cut, and there are seventeen people on the far bank with a stamped book and no way home, and nobody opened it in August either, and the entry of the third of this month says nobody knows whether that is luck or a system and that in about four years somebody will be able to tell.

**And the finding of the third of August is that a railway held by a date and not by a need stops on the day the date says, and that on the day it stopped nobody died, and that this district does not know yet whether that is a good thing or a piece of luck, and that not knowing is now the only fact about the line that is in the book.**
