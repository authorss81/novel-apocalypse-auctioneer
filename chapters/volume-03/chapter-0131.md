# Chapter 131: The Columns

---

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten was at the pit beside the second channel at about six in the morning on the twenty-second of October with a spade that had been ground down on one side, and nobody had asked him to be there and he said so to about four people before anybody asked him anything.

The reason is in the minute in his own words and the clerk of nineteen years was not in the room. **She was told about it afterwards by a man of about thirty-one who runs a cart of meal and wrote it down, and asked him to say it a second time, and he said it the second time the same.**

"**I said no to a sheet with no place to say whether I was in the room. I am not saying no to a book. A book has a place to say it in.**"

Then he said the other half and it went into the party's minute and not into the market's own book, and the clerk entered that she had put the two in one line and had not joined them and that this is the ninth time she has done it in eleven weeks.

"**A book that four people who counted can go and look at is a book. A book one man keeps is a man's book, and I have been a man's book.**"

Nobody argued with either of them. **A man of about thirty-one who runs a cart of meal out of a lane in Alder Reach said that a man refused a sheet in September who came back in October with a better reason had done the one thing this district has not managed to do in eleven weeks, which is to come back, and the clerk entered that and did not join it to anything.**

He was asked, while he was standing in the pit with the spade, why he does nine days of it in ten. **He said it in twenty-one words and the clerk of nineteen years did not write them down because she was not in the room, and a man of about thirty-one who runs a cart of meal was in the room and wrote them down afterwards in his own hand out of his own memory and not out of a page, which the minute says is the fourth time anybody has done it in eleven weeks.**

"**On the tenth day the pit is under two feet of water and I have no boots that keep it out.**"

A mason of about fifty came to the pit at about ten that morning for loam and did not find any, the second time in about a month, and the man who digs loam told him so and did not apologise for it, and the mason said the same thing he said in September in fewer words and walked on.

> **I HAVE BEEN TO THIS PIT TWICE IN A MONTH AND THERE IS NO LOAM IN IT EITHER TIME. I AM NOT SAYING A CHANNEL HAS TAKEN IT. I AM SAYING THERE IS NO LOAM.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the mason came on the second of October and on the twenty-second of October, and that the two occasions are not joined. **One of them is about a pit and one of them is about a bed of loam laid by a man who is dead, and a clerk who put the two in one line would be making a finding out of a coincidence, and that is the whole of her trade.**

A woman of about twenty-seven who keeps a kitchen garden behind the second channel came for a barrow-load at about four in the afternoon and was told to come back on the tenth, and said she knew, and stood at the edge of the pit for about nine minutes looking at the water in the bottom of it.

She stood at the edge of the pit for about nine minutes and then said the thing that about four people in that room wrote down at home. **It is in the minute in her own words and the clerk of nineteen years checked the count of it twice because it was the only line in the minute that was not about a book.**

"**My mother had a garden behind this channel and she had loam every spring of her life and there has been none here since the water came, and nobody in this flat has ever written down the year a place stopped being able to grow a thing.**"

A man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households said that a year in which a place stopped growing something was a year and not a figure of years and that the two were not the same instrument, and the clerk entered that he had put the two in one line and had not joined them, and that this is the twelfth time.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF OCTOBER A MAN WHO HAD BEEN ASKED ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF SEPTEMBER AND HAD SAID NO CAME BACK TO A ROOM AND SAID THAT HE WAS NOT SAYING NO TO A BOOK, AND A MASON CAME FOR LOAM FOR THE SECOND TIME IN A MONTH AND DID NOT FIND ANY AND SAID THAT HE WAS NOT SAYING A CHANNEL HAD TAKEN IT, AND THE TWO OCCASIONS ARE NOT JOINED, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SEVEN WHO KEEPS A KITCHEN GARDEN BEHIND THE SECOND CHANNEL ASKED FOR A YEAR TO BE WRITTEN DOWN FOR A PLACE THAT STOPPED GROWING SOMETHING.**

---

On the twenty-third of October a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households put it to about nineteen people in a room with two benches that face each other and a table that four people in the room were not sitting at, and he did it in eighteen words and the clerk wrote the eighteen down and read them back and he said they were right.

"**A sheet with twenty-one years on it is not a ledger, and a ledger is a different thing.**"

Then he said what a ledger is and the clerk counted it and got twenty-three, and he asked her to stop counting, and she stopped, and the minute says that he was the first person in ten weeks who had asked a clerk of nineteen years to stop doing the thing she is good at.

"**What a loom took, from whom, in what year, in a column anybody can go and look at. Not the money. The year.**"

And a woman of thirty-eight who has been in the second channel four years said the thing that stopped the room for nine seconds. **It is forty-seven words long and the clerk checked it twice, and they are the only words she has ever said in a room that are about herself and are not about the water.**

"**You are describing a year of mine and you have not asked me whether you may put it in a book, and if you build the book without asking me then the book is the sheet with no column on it and it is a better-looking one.**"

Nobody said no to that and about four people said yes, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a room had heard a reason and had not voted, and that not voting is also a thing a room does, and that she had wanted to write the not-voting down as a finding and had not, and that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the not-voting was the finding.

"Nobody here is going to build a book about me in nine days because a man of fifty-six was in a room and said the word ledger, and nine days is not long enough to have asked me properly in."

---

**On the twenty-fourth of October the man who can be found was asked to design the ledger, in a room on the flat, in front of about nineteen people, and he said no in four seconds, and the reason is nineteen words, and he asked for it to be entered in the same line as the no and not in a second one.**

"**A book the man who can be found builds is a book about him and not about the year.**"

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing read that back to him in the room and got nothing wrong, and then asked the room one question, and the minute says the room took four minutes over it and that a clerk of nineteen years wanted to write the answers down as findings and did not, because they were answers and not findings.

"What is the difference."

And a man of thirty-eight, who is not the man who can be found and who is the only other person in that room who has ever priced a loom, said the difference, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is forty-three words.

"**A man who makes a book and a man named in a book cannot be the same man. That is the whole of the rule and it took the four of September to find it out and it did not take a Binder.**"

Nobody had an answer to that, and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that it was the second time in a month that a person in this room had said something that could not be improved by arguing with, and the minute says that the man of thirty-eight said the same thing about her in September.

He had come east on the twenty-second with a cart and it was two days and a bit, and a man of fifty-six said that four people had crossed the raised plank road in October and that none of the four had been asked whether the road was still holding, and that a man of about forty who put in nine miles of it in the third month of this year has not been asked that either and is not going to be asked.

---

The columns were argued on the twenty-third and written out on the twenty-fourth, and there are six of them, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote the six in her own hand and would not have anybody else write them, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road offered and was thanked and not thanked in the same breath.

> **THE COLUMN HEADINGS ARE SIX AND THEY ARE IN THIS ORDER: WHO STOOD IN IT. WHICH FRAME. WHICH YEAR OF THEIRS. WHEN IT HAPPENED. WHAT THEY SAID. WHETHER THEY WERE IN THE ROOM.**

The first five were not argued about for more than about nineteen minutes altogether, and a man of fifty-six said that four of them were obvious, and that the trouble with an obvious column is that it costs a season to find out it was not there.

**The sixth was argued for about two hours, lost twice, and saved by the man who had said no in September.** A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man who saved it was the same man who had refused a sheet over it, and that this is the first time in eleven weeks that one person had refused a thing and then made the thing, and that she did not join the two, and that she wanted to and that not doing a thing is also a thing she does.

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the sixth column was a column about a room and not about a year, and that a column about a room was the one column in a book of years that a person could check by asking somebody, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that was the reason he had walked in from a pit.

"The **guild form has twenty-nine columns and not one of them is that**, and it cost us a season, and I am not going to be the reason a form gets a column added in a hurry."

And a man of fifty-six said that the sixth column was the only one of the six the four people who counted could not fill out of memory, and that a column the four who counted cannot fill out of memory is a column a stranger can go and look at, and that this was the September argument with the words turned round and he was not going to have it a second time.

And a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that if the sixth column could be filled out of memory then it was not a column, it was a second copy of the fifth, and that this district already had four hundred sheets on it with a train on them that does not run.

---

The guild form was in the room on the twenty-fourth and it was carried thirty-four miles east and back by a man of about thirty-eight with a cart, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing read the twenty-nine column headings out loud in four minutes while a clerk of nineteen years wrote them down in her own hand.

Two of the six were on it. A clerk of nineteen years counted the twenty-nine twice and got twenty-nine both times, and then said out loud that she was not going to do twenty-nine less six. **That would be subtraction and this is counting, and the two are not the same thing, and she had been told off about it once already in a month.**

The twenty-three is the number the clerk entered. **She entered the word *counted* and not the word *left over*, and a man of fifty-six said that was the most important entry anybody had made about a form in eleven weeks and that it was about a word and not about a form.**

The two that matched were which frame and when it happened, and neither of them is about a person, and the clerk entered that the two columns a person would look for first are the two the form already has, and that a form with twenty-nine columns is a form that has been asked two things about a thing and never once about the person in it.

Nobody argued with that and a man of fifty-six said that a district which has spent eleven weeks trying to get a year into a document has been handed, by the one instrument in this basin that anybody trusts, twenty-three columns with nothing in them about a year, and that the guild does not think a year is a thing a person owns.

"That is not the guild saying a year is not real. That is the guild saying a year has no column, and a thing with no column is not a thing anybody can be given back."

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF OCTOBER A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING READ TWENTY-NINE COLUMN HEADINGS OFF A GUILD FORM IN FOUR MINUTES, AND TWO OF THE SIX HEADINGS OF THE LEDGER WERE ON IT, AND TWENTY-THREE OF THE TWENTY-NINE HAD NOTHING IN THEM ABOUT A YEAR, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THE WORD COUNTED AND NOT THE WORD LEFT OVER.**

---

**And then a man of twenty-six who has been in a bed of brine for a year and a half asked for one thing and was refused twice in four minutes, and the standing reason did not move.** The two refusals are both in the minute and neither of them is more than four words long.

He wanted his own row to carry the year and a half.

He said that a ledger with a column for a year and no column for a year and a half was a ledger with a door in it, and that the year and a half was the only number in the whole of the count belonging to a person still in a bed, and that it was on a line of its own in a minute and had not been added to anything and would go on not being added to anything, and that a book which leaves it out is a book saying a man of twenty-six has never stood in a frame.

The woman of thirty-eight in the second channel refused him the first time and in four words, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that the four words were the same four words the woman of forty-four has used about four things, and a man of fifty-six entered that they were not the same four words, and that the clerk had put them in one line and had not joined them, and that this is the tenth time.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF OCTOBER SIX COLUMN HEADINGS WERE WRITTEN IN A CLERK'S HAND, AND THE SIXTH OF THEM WAS SAVED BY A MAN WHO HAD REFUSED A SHEET OVER IT IN SEPTEMBER, AND A MAN OF TWENTY-SIX ASKED TWICE FOR HIS OWN ROW TO CARRY A YEAR AND A HALF AND WAS REFUSED TWICE IN FOUR MINUTES, AND THE FINDING HE SAID OUT LOUD IS THAT A LEDGER WITH A COLUMN FOR A YEAR AND NO COLUMN FOR A YEAR AND A HALF IS A LEDGER WITH A DOOR IN IT.**

The man of twenty-six said that a man who cannot remember a year cannot tell whether the year in a book is his, and that the finding of the twenty-fourth of October was therefore going to be a row that says a figure and cannot be checked by the man it is about, and that this district has been building books its own subjects cannot check for eleven weeks and has now put one in a column with a heading on it.

Nobody answered him. About four people wrote the sentence down at home, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that she had wanted to put the sentence in the column for things that are not findings and had not, and gave the reason in twenty-six words, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing had said the same twenty-six words in a yard in September.

"**It is a finding and it is about a man, and a column for things that are not findings is for things a person got wrong.**"

---

Six people were asked on the twenty-fourth to hold the ledger and six said yes, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote the six in the order they said it and entered that the order had been in the room since the second of October and had not been written down before, and that this is the first time in eleven weeks that an order has existed in a room before anybody wrote it down.

The first is the woman of thirty-eight in the second channel. The second is the man of twenty-six in a bed of brine. The third is the woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel. **The fourth is the man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten, and he is the one who will have to fill it.**

The fifth is the man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households. The sixth is a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road and said he could count and had been asked to and had said yes, and who said yes in four seconds to holding it, which is the fastest anybody has answered anything on this flat since the fourth of April.

He is the sixth of the six and the clerk entered that he is a different man of about nineteen. **The boy of nineteen at the fourth place who filled two lines on a board is not him, and the two of them are in one line and are not joined, and that is the eleventh time.**

He is not paid and was not asked whether he would like to be, and he asked for one thing to be entered before he took the hours, which a man of fifty-six did on the second of October and which is now the second time anybody in this district has asked for a thing to be entered before it happened.

"It says in the book that I was not asked to hold it. I would like that in it, because in nine years somebody will read the book and find the youngest man in it was not asked and will think it was an accident."

Nobody argued with that, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and the count of protected things is five, and that neither moved, and that a man of nineteen who has asked to be entered as not asked is in neither of them and is not to be put in either.

**And a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said the last thing of the twenty-fourth in a yard in four minutes, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote all of it down and underlined nothing.** About nineteen people were in that yard and one of them had come over the road on the eleventh with two ledgers and had gone on the fourteenth and had not been met by anybody.

"**The sixth column is the only one worth having and it is the one we will not be able to fill. I would rather have a book with a column I cannot fill in than a book with five columns I can, and if those two ever get put in front of me again I know which one I am going to take.**"

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF OCTOBER SIX PEOPLE WERE ASKED TO HOLD A LEDGER AND SIX SAID YES, AND THE FOURTH OF THE SIX IS A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM AND DOES NINE DAYS OF IT IN TEN AND HE IS THE ONE WHO WILL HAVE TO FILL IT, AND HE IS NOT A KEEPER AND IS NOT PAID, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID IN A YARD THAT THE COLUMN HE WOULD GIVE UP FOR IS THE ONLY ONE OF THE SIX THAT A STRANGER CAN GO AND CHECK BY ASKING SOMEBODY, AND THAT IT IS ALSO THE ONLY ONE OF THE SIX HE WILL NOT BE ABLE TO FILL.**
