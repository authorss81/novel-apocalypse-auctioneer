# Chapter 132: Before the First of April

---

The six of them worked out on the evening of the twenty-fourth of October that they did not know how many rows the ledger had, and the clerk of nineteen years entered the not-knowing as the first line of the minute of the twenty-fourth and underlined it, which is the second time in eleven weeks she has underlined anything.

**Four figures have been counted on this flat twice and none of them is a count of people who have stood in a frame**, and a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households said the four out loud and they are ninety pans, sixty-one of them standing in river water, six frames with a sill in each, and about eighty people, and that the six frames are not six rows and have never been six rows and that a frame is worked by more than one person in a season and that nobody has ever counted who.

A woman of thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that she had been in a frame in the fourth year and in the seventh year and in the fourth month of this year, and that she was one person and three rows, and that the district had been treating a frame as a person since the fourteenth of August.

Nobody could produce a number, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road said that he could count them if somebody would tell him what to count. **He was asked what a row is and could not answer, and the clerk entered that he had said *I do not know*, and that this is the first time he had said it, and that he was twenty feet from the answer when he said it.**

---

The twenty-fifth of October was spent arguing about a word, and the argument was had by a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace and who has sold forms in this district for nineteen years and did not come east to do it.

**He had the clause copied out in a hand of his own and he would not fill in the last line of it, and the clerk of nineteen years asked him why and he said it in twenty-nine words and the minute has it in his own words.**

"**A form is a thing people use and not a thing people read, and I have sold nine forms this year and been asked to explain six of them.**"

The two words he would not have were release and consent, and he said that a man who has stood in a frame for four seasons is the last person in this basin who is going to have either word explained to him well, and that the person who is going to be handed this is a haulier of about forty who does not read clauses and has said so out loud twice.

Nobody defended the two words and a man of fifty-six said that a clause a person cannot define is a clause that will be read four times and understood none, and that the district had spent eleven weeks learning that a sheet with no column on it cannot be worked by asking harder, and that a word is a column a person reads without knowing they are reading one.

A clerk of nineteen years wanted to enter the word problem in the column for things that are not findings and did not. **She entered instead that it is not a finding, and a man of fifty-six said that a thing a person cannot define in a room is the definition of a thing this district does not have, and that it is not one of the five and not one of the six and not one of the three documents nobody owns, and that he would not have it entered in either, and it was not.**

The man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that the fix was not a definition, and that anybody who opens a form is going to look for a date before they look for a word, and that a clause with two dates in it gets read and a clause with a word in it gets carried.

"That is nineteen years of me telling people what a piece of paper wants off them. They do not read it. They look for the day, and then they decide, and then they tell me they have decided."


---

The twenty-fifth of October was a day of not doing it, and that is in the minute, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing asked whether a day of not doing it was a day lost and a woman of thirty-eight in the second channel said that a book built in a day is a book with a man's handwriting in it and that she had seen four hundred of those.

**She was asked on the twenty-fifth whether she would put her four years in the book, and she said yes, and she gave one condition and would not give it to be written down by anybody, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it in her own hand and read it back and the woman of thirty-eight said it was right.**

The condition is that the sixth column stays.

"You have all decided in three days that a book of years is a good thing and you decided it in a room with no woman in a frame in it, and the woman in the frame has said nothing, and the column that says whether a person was in the room is the only one that asks her, and I am not putting four years in a book that can be checked by anybody except me."

**Nobody argued with that and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was the second time in eleven weeks a person had set a condition on an instrument in a room before the instrument existed, and that the first time was a man of fifty-six about four hours, and that she had put the two in one line and had not joined them, and that this is the thirteenth time.**

And on the twenty-fifth a woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures put a pail down in the lane outside and asked, in a doorway, of nobody in particular, what the book was going to be for, and nobody answered her, and she said that she had not expected to be answered and that she wanted it entered that she had asked, and a man of about thirty-one who runs a cart of meal entered that she had asked and that it was in the count and that the count is a column and not a bucket.

---

And a man of about thirty-four who digs loam and a man of twenty-six who has been in a bed of brine for a year and a half had an argument on the twenty-fifth afternoon that lasted about nine minutes and that a clerk of nineteen years wrote down in full because she said it was the first one in eleven weeks where two people were arguing about the same word from opposite ends of it.

A man of thirty-four said that a man who cannot remember a year cannot check the figure of it in a book, and that this district had been treating that as the same thing as a man who cannot answer, and that it was not the same thing and had not been the same thing since the second of October.

A man of twenty-six said that he could answer anything anybody asked him and had answered a hundred and forty-one questions from a woman with two ledgers in three days, and that what he could not do was look at a figure and say whether it was his.

"Then what is the difference in a form."

"**A no costs a person nothing to give. A yes costs them a year. So a person who cannot remember a year can still be asked, and can still say no, and must never be asked twice for the same yes.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of twenty-six had said the word *must* in a room that has no power to make anybody do anything **and that this is the second time in eleven weeks a person in this district has said *must* about a thing nobody here can enforce, and that the first was a man of thirty-four about a form, and that she had put the two in one line and had not joined them.**


---

On the twenty-sixth of October the room met on the day written on the board in the hand that cannot be read **and the man who can be found was thirty-four miles west and did not come, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing entered that he was not asked to come and would not have come and that the two of those are different and that the second one is a finding.**

About nineteen people were in it. Four of them stand in frames, or have, in this season or a season before it. There is no officer, no clerk, no seal, no stamp and no name on the door, and the four things were read out at the start by a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road and who was not asked to read them out and offered, and the second time in a month a person who was not asked has offered to do something a room was going to ask for.

Then a woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years said the thing she said on the nineteenth of October, and this time she said it as a question with a shape on it, and about four people in the room had been waiting eleven days for her to say it in that form.

She was not asked and she is not an officer of anything and not asked is in the count and the count did not move **and the clerk of nineteen years entered that she had said it twice in eleven days in two forms and that the second form had a date on it and the first one had not.**

"Nobody in this room has said out loud what a worker does on the last day of a season. I have been the last one out of a frame eleven times and I have not once been asked by anybody, and I would like it said in a room and written down, and it is not for tonight, it is for the thirty-first of March."

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten asked her what a worker does on the last day of a season, and she answered him in the minute in her own words, and it is sixty-six words and it is the finding of the twenty-sixth of October before anybody has voted on anything.

"**A worker who says no on the last day of a season is a worker who has already paid. The year went out of me in the third week and the no comes on the last day, and the no is about the next one and I have not got the thing back and nobody can give it to me because there is nothing to give.**"

---

Nobody in the room disagreed with her and about four people wrote it down at home, and a clerk of nineteen years wanted to put it in the column for things that are not findings and did not, and did not give the reason this time, and entered that she had not given it and had wanted to.

A man of fifty-six then put a clause on the table and it is in the minute in his own words and the clerk counted it and got eighty-two and read it back and he said it was right **and the clause is a right and not an instrument and it is the first thing anybody in this district has ever written down that a person may use against a thing that has already happened to them.**

> **A PERSON WHO HAS STOOD IN A FRAME IN THIS SEASON MAY SAY, ON ANY DAY BEFORE THE FIRST OF APRIL, THAT THEY WILL NOT STAND IN THAT FRAME AGAIN. THE NOTICE IS GOOD FROM THE DAY IT IS GIVEN AND NOT THE DAY BEFORE. IT IS NOT A RELEASE AND IT IS NOT A CONSENT AND IT DOES NOT GIVE BACK A YEAR. NOBODY MAY ASK THE PERSON WHY. THE NOTICE IS GOOD WHETHER OR NOT THE FRAME IS RUN AGAIN.**

The room argued it for about two hours and it changed four times, and a clerk of nineteen years kept the four versions. **A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that four versions of a clause is the whole of what this district has learned to do with a document in eleven weeks, and that none of the four was a printing error.**

A woman of thirty-eight in the second channel said the third change and the reason, and she is the reason.

"You have all written *before the first of April* as if it were an early date. It is the last date. A person told in the last week of March has got one week, and a week is not enough to leave a job you have done since you were nine."

A man of fifty-six said the arithmetic out loud and it is thirty-four words and it is in the minute: a hundred and fifty-one days from the first of November to the first of April, and twenty-one weeks and four days, and twenty-one weeks in a house where somebody is going to ask why.

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was asked what the days are and she gave the other half in thirty-four words **and the clerk of nineteen years wrote them down in her own hand and did not join them to the arithmetic.**

"**It is the time it takes the wrong person to find out. That is all a notice is. It is five months for somebody to come and ask me why I am not wanted.**"

Nobody said she was wrong, and a man of fifty-six entered that the clause did not carry a date for giving the notice. **A right with no date for using it is a right a person will use in the last week, and the person it is written for is the person it will be used by, and that is the finding and not a criticism of anybody.**

---

Then a man of twenty-six who has been in a bed of brine for a year and a half was asked what the notice has to say so that he can give it. **He said it in the minute in his own words, and it is twenty-five words, and the clerk of nineteen years did not count it and said so, and he said count it.**

"**Do not put the year on it. I cannot read a description of my own year and I would not know if it was right.**"

The clause does not say what the year was.

A clerk of nineteen years said that out loud and a man of fifty-six said that the four of September had spent four hours making a figure and had not been able to put it on a form in a way the person it belonged to could check, and that the first form anybody had made for a year said nothing about the year on purpose, and that a man who cannot remember a year can still say that he will not do it again.

AND ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF OCTOBER A ROOM WITH NO OFFICER AND NO CLERK AND NO NAME ON ITS DOOR PUT A RIGHT TO WITHDRAW ON A TABLE AND CHANGED IT FOUR TIMES **AND A WOMAN WHO HAS DONE IT ELEVEN YEARS SAID THAT A WORKER WHO SAYS NO ON THE LAST DAY OF A SEASON IS A WORKER WHO HAS ALREADY PAID, AND THE YEAR WENT OUT OF HER IN THE THIRD WEEK AND THE NO COMES ON THE LAST DAY.**

AND THE CLAUSE SAYS A PERSON MAY SAY BEFORE THE FIRST OF APRIL THAT THEY WILL NOT STAND IN THAT FRAME AGAIN **AND IT IS GOOD FROM THE DAY IT IS GIVEN, AND IT IS NOT A RELEASE AND IT IS NOT A CONSENT AND IT DOES NOT GIVE BACK A YEAR, AND IT DOES NOT SAY WHAT THE YEAR WAS, AND A MAN OF TWENTY-SIX ASKED FOR THAT.**

---

A woman of thirty-one in the third channel since the first of April, and this is her second, came into the room on the twenty-sixth at about four in the afternoon and had not been in the room in the morning, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had come and not asked and that nobody had sent for her.

**She was asked in the six things whether her two years could go in the book, and she said yes, and then she asked for a thing nobody in the room had asked for in eleven weeks, and the asking took nine seconds.**

"**Put my name on it. Not a trade and not a doorway. My name. I have been a woman in the third channel for two years and I have been on four sheets in eleven weeks and on every one of the four I was a trade and a number.**"

Nobody argued with her. **A man of fifty-six said that this is the first time in eleven weeks a person has asked to be named in a book instead of described in one, and that the asking is a better instrument than the answer, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered both halves of that and did not join them.**

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing asked her the question a clerk is supposed to ask, which is what happens to the name. **She said it in forty-one words and the clerk counted them twice and got forty-one both times.**

"**It stays on me. I am not giving it to anybody and I am not taking it off, and if somebody reads that book in nine years and finds my name in a column that is me and not the book.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that on the eleventh of October an agent of a guarantee house had sat in a room with two ledgers and said she would rather have a blank column than a name a person cannot take out, and that a woman of thirty-one had on the twenty-sixth of October asked for her name to go in, and that the two of them were in a minute in two rooms on the same flat and were not joined, and that this is the fifteenth time.

And the clerk entered that she wanted to join them more than she has wanted to join anything in eleven weeks, and that she did not, and that not doing a thing is also a thing she does.

The woman of thirty-one was not asked about the twenty-one years **was not asked about the third channel, and was not asked whether she wanted the notice, and a clerk of nineteen years entered all three not-askings and the count did not move.**


---

A person in the room on the twenty-sixth asked to add a fifth thing to the four and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said no in seventeen words, and the seventeen words have been said in this room before and were said in September and are the third of the council's three rules.

"**A fifth thing is a new page and it has to be started by whoever starts it.**"

The person who asked said that a thing that has to be started by whoever starts it is a rule that only stops the first person, and the man of thirty-four said yes, and the minute says he said yes, and that he then said that a room that can be stopped by a rule about its first meeting is not a room, and that a council of a thing and not a room is a thing this district has not got and is not going to get in November.


---

And then a man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said the thing that made the room go quiet for nine seconds, and it is in the minute in his own words, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that four people in the room stopped writing while he said it.

"**A person who says no on the thirty-first of March has not refused a season. A person who says no on the thirty-first of March has been told what a season is by the person who is asking. This clause is a door and a door only opens one way and I want somebody to write down which way it opens.**"

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that the door opened outwards, and that the direction of a door that gives a year back is the one thing this district has never been able to find in anything, including a loom, and that he was not going to argue the direction in a room and would rather it was in the minute in four words, and the four words went in.

And a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the clause did not say a word about the twenty-one years, and that the twenty-one years were against a name until the thirty-first of March, and that the two dates were the same date, and that a person in this district now has a day on which the name comes off and a day on which the year is renewed, and that the two are the same day and that is the whole problem and that nobody had noticed until the twenty-sixth of October.

Nobody asked her why and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had said it in a room and not in a kitchen and that the difference had been entered on the seventh of October about a different sentence **and that the two are in one line and are not joined, and that this is the fourteenth time.**

A man of fifty-six said that the room had two dates and no instrument to move anything, and that this is the third time in eleven weeks a room has ended with two dates on it and nothing to do about either, and that he was not going to say the word instrument in that sentence and had said it anyway and would like it struck.

It was not struck and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not struck and why.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF OCTOBER A CLAUSE WENT ON A TABLE IN A ROOM THAT HAS NO OFFICER, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM ASKED WHICH WAY THE DOOR IN IT OPENS, AND THE MINUTE SAYS OUTWARDS, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO TAKES IN WASHING AT THE SECOND CHANNEL SAID THAT THE DAY THE NAME COMES OFF AND THE DAY THE YEAR IS RENEWED ARE THE SAME DAY, AND NOBODY ASKED HER WHY, AND NOT ASKED IS IN THE COUNT AND THE COUNT DID NOT MOVE.**
