# Chapter 137: The Cell That Is Empty

---

The ledger was open on the board of eleven on the morning of the seventh of November and about nineteen people were in that yard and a man of twenty-six who has been in a bed of brine for a year and a half was standing in it, and he had come west in a cart on the sixth and had paid his own way and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not reimbursed and that nobody offered to reimburse him and that he did not ask.

He is the first person who has ever come to this yard because of something written about him on a page **and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that out loud in that yard, and the clerk entered it, and the man of twenty-six said that it was the first thing anybody had said to him in a year and a half that was not a question about the water.**

He cannot read. A man of about nineteen who walked in from the road read his row to him out loud, all six columns, and the man of twenty-six listened to it and said that the fifth column was right and that the fourth column was right and that the second column was right, and then he asked about the third.

The third column is headed WHICH YEAR OF THEIRS and his cell in it has been empty since the twenty-seventh of October.

"It is empty because you asked twice for a year and a half to go in it and you were refused twice," said a clerk of nineteen years, and she said it in nine seconds and read the two refusals back to him from her own hand, and the man of twenty-six said that he remembered being refused and that he did not remember the date.

**Nobody had noticed the empty cell for eleven days.** A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said it out loud in that yard and the clerk of nineteen years entered it, and a man of fifty-six said that the page had been carried thirty-four miles east and back, that nine copies had gone out on nine hands, that a stranger had read the sixth column standing up on the twenty-ninth of October, and that not one of the eleven copies had anything in it saying that a cell was empty on purpose.

**AND ON THE SEVENTH OF NOVEMBER A MAN OF TWENTY-SIX STOOD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AND HAD HIS ROW READ TO HIM AND THE THIRD CELL WAS EMPTY AND HAD BEEN EMPTY FOR ELEVEN DAYS, AND THE CELL IS EMPTY BECAUSE HE ASKED TWICE FOR A YEAR AND A HALF TO GO IN IT AND WAS REFUSED TWICE IN FOUR MINUTES ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF OCTOBER, AND NOBODY HAD NOTICED, AND THE PAGE DOES NOT SAY WHY.**

---

A stranger who had been in that yard four times in ten weeks and had taken a copy of the ledger cold on the twenty-ninth of October asked, in that yard, at about eleven in the morning, what an empty cell meant, and the question was asked in a yard and not in a room and about nine people heard it.

The man of thirty-four who mends fencing said the true answer first, which is that it means nobody knows, and then a clerk of nineteen years said the answer that was on the page, which is that it means the person was asked twice and refused twice, and that the two of those are different answers and that the yard had just heard both of them.

Then the man who can be found did a thing he has not done in eleven weeks.

He took the pen out of the clerk's hand, which is not his hand and not his pen and not his book, and he put the point of it on the margin beside the empty cell as if he were going to write a word in the margin, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said *do not* and he did not, and the yard heard the two words and about nine people turned round.

And then a rule arrived there at about eleven in the morning on the seventh of November, and nobody standing there had asked for it, and the man of thirty-eight did not ask for it and is the only person on this line who could have got one by asking.

> **A LEDGER IS A RECORD OF WHAT A THING DID. IT IS NOT A REMEDY AND IT IS NOT A PRICE AND IT IS NOT A CLAIM.**
>
> **A YEAR ENTERED IN A LEDGER IS ENTERED ONCE. WHICH COLUMN IT IS IN DOES NOT DECIDE WHEN IT WENT.**
>
> **A RIGHT TO WITHDRAW IS A DOOR IN ONE DIRECTION AND IT DOES NOT REACH BACKWARDS.**
>
> **A PERSON WHO HAS NOT BEEN ASKED HAS NOT WITHDRAWN. YOU MAY NOT ENTER A PERSON AS HAVING WITHDRAWN, THERE IS NO COLUMN FOR IT, AND YOU MAY NOT MAKE ONE IN FRONT OF A STRANGER.**
>
> **THE MAN YOU NAMED IS NOT A ROW. HE IS THE PLACE A PERSON LOOKS WHEN THEY WANT TO ASK WHO OWNS THE YEARS.**

A clerk of nineteen years asked what an address is and the rule did not say, and a man of thirty-eight said it in forty words and the minute has it in his own words, and the man of twenty-six was standing nine feet away when he said it and was not asked whether he agreed.

"**An address is a name on a figure so that a person asking who owes it is answered. It is not a person who has the thing. He has not got a year either and he does not want one.**"

---

At about half past eleven the man of twenty-six asked for his row to be struck out of the page altogether, and he asked for it in a yard and not in a room, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had been asked a question about the page by a person the page is about and that this is the first time in eleven weeks.

He said the reason in fifty words and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got fifty **and the clerk of nineteen years did not count them at all and said so and he said count them anyway.**

"**A hole in a row is a place a stranger will put something. My year went in the third of April and nobody wrote it because a form cannot hold it, and if I leave a hole there then in four years a man with a pen will help me.**"

The room said no to it in four minutes and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing gave the reason and it is in the minute in his own words and it is fifty-seven words, and the man of twenty-six asked for it to be read back twice and it was read back twice the same.

"**A struck row says a thing was entered here and was taken back out of it. A row with a hole in it says a thing was never entered. The page has one of those two and you have asked me for the wrong one, and I would have said so in September and I did not.**"

The man of twenty-six stood in that yard for nine seconds and then said that it was the first time in a year and a half that anybody in this basin had told him the difference between two things he could not see, and that he had asked two people in September what a year was and had been given the water both times, and that a person who cannot see a difference cannot argue about it and that this district had spent eleven weeks arguing about it.

Nobody told him what a year was on the seventh of November and a clerk of nineteen years entered twice that she had been in that yard the whole time and had been asked nothing and had answered nothing, and that not asked is in the count and that the count did not move.

**AND ON THE SEVENTH OF NOVEMBER A MAN OF TWENTY-SIX ASKED FOR HIS ROW TO BE STRUCK OUT OF THE PAGE AND WAS REFUSED IN ABOUT FOUR MINUTES, AND THE REASON IS THAT A STRUCK ROW SAYS A THING WAS ENTERED HERE AND WAS TAKEN BACK OUT OF IT, AND A ROW WITH A HOLE IN IT SAYS A THING WAS NEVER ENTERED, AND THE PAGE HAS ONE OF THOSE TWO AND HE HAD ASKED FOR THE WRONG ONE, AND HE SAID THAT IT WAS THE FIRST TIME IN A YEAR AND A HALF THAT ANYBODY HAD TOLD HIM THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN TWO THINGS HE COULD NOT SEE.**


---

And then a man of thirty-four who mends fencing asked him, in that yard, in front of about nineteen people, to post a security on the notice, and the asking was made properly and he was asked in the five things and he could have said no and he did not say no for about four seconds.

The reason a security was wanted is that a notice which is not on a guild form can be taken away by whoever holds the paper, and a security is the only thing he has that stops a person taking a piece of paper away, and a man of fifty-six said that a security is a thing a stranger can go and look at and that is the whole of what a security has ever been in this district.

He could have posted it. It is the fifth of his five powers and there is no rule against it and nobody in that yard had standing to stop him **and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing stopped him anyway, and the words he used were Adrian's own words from the ninth of October and the clerk of nineteen years entered that they were the same nine words in the same order and that he had heard them said to him and had not argued then either.**

"**A power is a thing you can do to a document and not to a person, and the day somebody asks me to do one of mine to a person is the day I hand the five of them in and go and stand in a frame myself.**"

Nobody argued with it and the yard was quiet for about nineteen seconds and a man of fifty-six said that a rule a man said out loud against his own advantage in front of nineteen people is the only kind of rule this district has ever had that anybody believed, and that the rule was not a rule, it was a sentence, and that a sentence can be said again and a rule has to be written down, and that this one is not written down anywhere and he would not have it written down.

The man of twenty-six said the other half in that yard and he said it about nine feet from the man who can be found, and it is thirty-eight words, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down on the board of eleven with the pen she had not given away, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that was the first time in eleven weeks a clerk had written on a public board and that he was not going to ask her to rub it out.

"**You will not post it because you cannot pay for a year, and I am not able to tell you what one costs, and we are both stood in this yard saying the same thing from opposite ends.**"

**AND ON THE SEVENTH OF NOVEMBER THE MAN WHO CAN BE FOUND WAS ASKED TO POST A SECURITY ON A WITHDRAWAL NOTICE AND COULD HAVE AND DID NOT, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING STOPPED HIM WITH THE NINE WORDS HE SAID ON THE NINTH OF OCTOBER, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT A SENTENCE A MAN SAYS OUT LOUD AGAINST HIS OWN ADVANTAGE IN FRONT OF NINETEEN PEOPLE IS THE ONLY KIND OF RULE THIS DISTRICT HAS EVER HAD THAT ANYBODY BELIEVED, AND THAT IT IS NOT WRITTEN DOWN AND HE WOULD NOT HAVE IT WRITTEN DOWN.**

---

On the eighth of November a copy of the ledger was compared with a copy and the two did not agree, and the comparison took about nine minutes and was done by a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace, and he did it there without being asked because he had a copy in his coat.

The difference is one word in the sixth column of one row **and the word is NOTHING SAID on the copy in his coat and NOTHING HAPPENED on the copy in that yard, and the copy in his coat is the older one by two days, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both and entered that she had written NOTHING HAPPENED on the sixth of November after a room had broken up and that she had not entered the change.**

A man of fifty-six said that the rule is that a document does not become wrong by being copied and that a document becomes wrong by being corrected, and that the whole of the difference is that a clerk of nineteen years corrected a page in a yard on the sixth of November and did not rule a line through the old word.

Nobody argued with that and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road said the other half, and he is the sixth of the six and is nineteen and is not paid, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-five words.

"**There are nine copies and there is no way to know which one is the one, and if a person is going to check a cell he has to know whose hand it is in.**"

A man of fifty-six said that this district built a book on eleven boards on the understanding that a person has to come and stand in front of it, and that the book works, and that the ninth copy of the ninth copy is a problem the book has never had because the book does not go anywhere.

And a clerk of nineteen years entered that this is the second time in eleven weeks that two copies of a thing this district has made have not said the same thing **and that the first was a notice of withdrawal that named a season that never started, and that the two are not joined, and that this is the nineteenth time.**

Nobody joined them and a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said the thing he has said about forms all month in ten words, and that the ten words are the only opinion he has ever had about paper and that he is not going to change it in November.

"**A copy is the document. The original is a room.**"


---

On the ninth of November the ledger was read out at the ward market counter three times to three people, and a reader who is about fifty and was a signal clerk before her shoulder went read the sixth column out first every time, before the third column, and a clerk of nineteen years asked her about that and she said it in twenty-one words and did not soften it.

"**A person who cannot read a figure can read that, and if they read that first they will believe the figures.**"

Nobody argued with her and a man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was in the queue and heard it and said that it is the first time in about four hundred forms that anybody had read out the part that was true before the part that was a figure, and that he had not known that the two could be in that order.

A man of fifty-six said that a counter is the only place in this district where a person arrives without having been asked, and that about nine strangers came to a yard in nine weeks and one came back twice, and that the counter is where a woman of forty-five came once and was not asked a second time, and that the count of not-askings about her is four with one standing refusal and it did not move on the ninth of November because nobody in this room has seen her.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she had wanted to write the woman's number in the entry for the ninth of November and had not **and that a count of not-askings is a column and not a bucket and not a protection, and that a woman who has said no four times does not get a fifth line because a clerk had a blank.**

And a man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing asked, at the counter, in front of about nine people, whether the empty cell in the third column was going to be filled in in April, and the reader read the cell out twice and the clerk of nineteen years said no, and a man of fifty-six said that April is five months away and that the district has a rule that a man is asked a question in the five things and that nobody is going to be asked about a cell in April by anybody who is not the man in it.

Nobody was asked about it and the man of twenty-six was thirty-four miles east by then and had not been asked and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had not been asked, and that not asked is in the count, and that the count is a column and not a bucket, and that she has written that sentence so many times in eleven weeks that it is the only sentence in this book she can write without looking at the page.

**AND ON THE NINETH OF NOVEMBER THE LEDGER WAS READ OUT AT THE WARD MARKET COUNTER THREE TIMES AND THE SIXTH COLUMN WAS READ FIRST EVERY TIME, AND A READER SAID THAT A PERSON WHO CANNOT READ A FIGURE CAN READ THAT, AND THAT IF THEY READ THAT FIRST THEY WILL BELIEVE THE FIGURES, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT THE COUNT OF NOT-ASKINGS ABOUT A WOMAN OF FORTY-FIVE STANDS AT FOUR WITH ONE STANDING REFUSAL AND DID NOT MOVE.**


---

The finding of the seventh of November is that a ledger cell can be empty on purpose and the page cannot say so **and that nine copies of a page went out on nine hands and none of the nine carried the reason, and that a man of twenty-six stood in a yard on the seventh of November and heard his own row read out and the third cell of it was blank and had been blank for eleven days, and he said that the finding was fine with him and that he had not known it was fine until somebody else said it in a yard.**

Nobody argued with him about being fine and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she wanted to write *he is fine with it* on the page and did not, and gave the reason in twenty-two words, which is the sentence a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said in a yard in September and has now said three times in eleven weeks, and which is not the sentence in front of her and has never been.

"**It is what he said and it is not what he meant and I cannot tell the difference and neither can he.**"

A man of about nineteen who walked in from the road said the other half and he is the sixth of the six and the youngest man holding anything in this district, and he is not paid, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-three words.

"**A page that says a man is fine with a blank cell is a page about the man. A page that says why the cell is blank is a page about the clerk.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that, and entered that she had wanted to argue with a man of nineteen and had not, and that a man who has been in a room for eleven weeks and has not said a thing until now is not a man who is being careful, and that a man of about thirty-four who digs loam is the fourth of the six and fills the page and was thirty-four miles east and is not here and was not asked.

**AND ON THE SEVENTH OF NOVEMBER A MAN OF ABOUT NINETEEN WHO WALKS IN FROM THE ROAD SAID THAT A PAGE WHICH SAYS A MAN IS FINE WITH A BLANK CELL IS A PAGE ABOUT THE MAN AND A PAGE WHICH SAYS WHY THE CELL IS BLANK IS A PAGE ABOUT THE CLERK, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT SHE HAD WANTED TO ARGUE WITH A MAN OF NINETEEN AND HAD NOT, AND THAT THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM IS THE FOURTH OF THE SIX AND FILLS THE PAGE AND WAS THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST AND WAS NOT ASKED.**

AND THE THIRD CELL OF THE ROW OF A MAN OF TWENTY-SIX IS STILL EMPTY **AND IT IS GOING TO STAY EMPTY, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED ON THE SEVENTH OF NOVEMBER THAT AN EMPTY CELL ON A LEDGER IS NOT A FINDING AND IS NOT A FAILURE AND IS NOT A MYSTERY, AND THAT IT IS A YEAR AND A HALF WHICH DOES NOT GO TWICE AND WHICH HAS ALREADY BEEN ENTERED ON A LINE OF ITS OWN IN A PARTY'S MINUTE AND WAS NOT ADDED TO ANYTHING.**

AND THERE IS STILL NO COLUMN FOR A REFUSAL AND THERE IS STILL NO COLUMN FOR WHAT SOMEBODY DID NOT LET HAPPEN **AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM RULED NEITHER, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT HE DID NOT AND THAT SHE IS NOT GOING TO ASK HIM WHY.**
