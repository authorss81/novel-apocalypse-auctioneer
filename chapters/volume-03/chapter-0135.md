# Chapter 135: A Frame Is Oak

---

The second form of the notice was written on the first of November at about ten in the morning by a man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten, and it took him about four hours, and he would not let anybody help him with it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he is the fourth of the six who hold the ledger and that he is not a clerk of anything and was not asked to be one.

The top line of the second form does not name a season. It names a person **a frame, and a date, and the three of them are on one line and the line is under the middle of the sheet, and a man of fifty-six said that putting it in the middle is the part he argued for and lost.**

He wanted it at the top. A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said the top is for the season because the season is what a stranger looks for, and that a stranger who has been handed a sheet with a season at the top of it has already decided what the sheet is before he reads the rest, and that the first form failed on the top line and that a second form with the same line at the top of it is a form that has learned nothing.

So it went in the middle and a man of fifty-six said that a document with the important part in the middle is a document that a person has to stay for, and that this district is very good at keeping people and has never once had to keep anybody, and that the two are not the same and are not joined.

> **A PERSON WHO HAS STOOD IN A FRAME IN THIS SEASON MAY GIVE NOTICE ON ANY DAY BEFORE THE FIRST OF APRIL THAT THEY WILL NOT STAND IN THAT FRAME AGAIN. THE NOTICE IS GOOD FROM THE DAY IT IS GIVEN. IT IS NOT A RELEASE AND IT IS NOT A CONSENT AND IT DOES NOT GIVE BACK A YEAR. NOBODY MAY ASK THE PERSON WHY.**
> >
> > **PERSON: ______________**
> >
> > **FRAME: ______________**
> >
> > **DATE GIVEN: ______________**
> >
> > **THE SEASON IS NOT NAMED ON THIS SHEET. THE SEASON IS A LENGTH OF TIME AND THE NOTICE IS ABOUT A FRAME.**

Nobody read the last line aloud in a room where about nineteen people were sitting until a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper read it aloud himself and said that it was the first line of a form in eleven weeks that he had not known was there when he was handed the form, and that he had handed forms to people for nineteen years and had never once written a line about what a form was not.

---

**The second form does not fix the failure of the first one and the room knew that on the first of November and said so.** A clerk of nineteen years entered that the room knew it before the form was finished, and that this is the first time in eleven weeks that a room has known an instrument was not going to work while it was being made, and that the count of instruments that did not work is not one of the five and is not one of the six.

A man of fifty-six put the second problem on the table at about eleven o'clock and it took about nine minutes and it is the reason the second form has a hole in it **and the hole has not been filled and is named.**

""**You have taken a season off a notice and put a frame on it, and a frame is oak, and a frame is the one thing on a loom that a person can walk out of in nine seconds. A frame is not what takes the year. The channel takes the year. And nobody in this district can tell me the difference on a piece of paper, because on a piece of paper a frame and a channel are both a box on a plan.**""

A woman of thirty-eight who has been in the second channel four years said the other half and she is a person who has been in a frame and came out of one, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that she was asked and answered and that she was not asked about the second channel and was not asked about the four seasons.

"**The frame is oak and the frame is the last thing that happens to you. A person gets out of an oak frame and the frame is still there in the morning.**"

Nobody argued with that. **A man of about thirty-four who digs loam wrote it on the back of the second form in his own hand and would not let it be printed, and a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper said that a thing written on the back of a form is the part of a form nobody copies, and that the part of a form nobody copies is the part that gets lost, and that he had four copies of one page in his stall and three of them said something different.**

**AND ON THE FIRST OF NOVEMBER A SECOND FORM OF THE NOTICE WAS WRITTEN WITH A PERSON, A FRAME AND A DATE ON ONE LINE IN THE MIDDLE OF THE SHEET AND NO SEASON ON IT AT ALL, AND THE FINDING OF THE MORNING IS THAT A FRAME IS OAK AND A FRAME IS THE LAST THING THAT HAPPENS TO A PERSON, AND THAT THE THING THAT TAKES THE YEAR IS NOT THE FRAME, AND THAT A NOTICE AGAINST A FRAME IS A DOOR IN THE RIGHT DIRECTION INTO A THING THAT IS NOT THE FRAME.**

---

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures told a man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten what happens in a house when a person is handed a notice in November, and she told it as a thing that happened to a house she knows, and not as a thing she thinks might happen, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it and that the woman was not asked about the water and was not asked about the ninety houses and was not asked to be right.

She said it in four minutes and it is in the minute in her own words and it is a long sentence and a clerk of nineteen years wrote it all down and did not shorten it.

"**The Vanns, at the third house up from the sluice, had a man in a frame in the fifth year and he was told in the second month of the year that the frame was wanted for somebody else, and his wife asked him every day for about nine weeks what he had done wrong, and on the fortieth day he told her, and she told his mother, and his mother told the woman next door, and by the first of April the whole row knew before he did.**"

Nobody said anything for nine seconds and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that the notice is supposed to be the thing that stops that **and that the notice is handed to a person and the person's wife is the person who has to live with it, and that the form has one line on it and the line has one name on it.**

The woman of fifty-eight said the other half and it is twelve words and the clerk of nineteen years wrote them down and did not ask whether she had said them before.

"**A notice is a thing you hand a person in a room.**"

Nobody asked her what a notice should be instead and a man of fifty-six said that the room had been about to find out and that it had just been told, and that a thing a room is told twelve words about by a person who carries water and is not paid is worth more to this instrument than anything anybody else has said in it, and that the room would not have got there and that the entry says so.

**AND ON THE FIRST OF NOVEMBER A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT WHO CARRIES WATER TOLD THE ROOM WHAT HAPPENED AT THE THIRD HOUSE UP FROM THE SLUICE IN THE FIFTH YEAR, WHEN A MAN IN A FRAME WAS TOLD IN THE SECOND MONTH THAT THE FRAME WAS WANTED FOR SOMEBODY ELSE, AND THAT HIS WIFE ASKED HIM EVERY DAY FOR ABOUT NINE WEEKS WHAT HE HAD DONE WRONG, AND THAT BY THE FIRST OF APRIL THE WHOLE ROW KNEW BEFORE HE DID, AND THAT THIS IS THE FIRST TIME A PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT HAS DESCRIBED WHAT A NOTICE DOES TO A HOUSE INSTEAD OF WHAT A NOTICE DOES TO A PERSON.**


---

A woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years was asked on the first of November, in the six things, what happens to a person on the thirty-first of March, and she had said on the nineteenth of October that nobody had said it out loud and had called it a thing for the thirty-first of March and not for tonight, and the clerk of nineteen years asked whether it was a thing for tonight and she said it was.

She said the first thing in forty-one words. **The clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got forty-one both times, and did not say so, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said afterwards that a clerk who counts and does not say so is doing the thing the six of them are about to spend four hours doing, which is checking a number without making a fuss about it.**

"**On the thirty-first of March you are still in it. The last day of a season is a day you are standing in the thing, and that is why the no is the wrong day and I said so in October.**"

Then she said the second thing, and it is in the minute in her own words and it is fifty words. **About four people wrote it down at home and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had been asked in the six things and had answered, and that she was not asked about the eleven years and was not asked about the sixth year, and that not asked is in the count and that the count did not move.**

"**I have been the last one out eleven times and eleven times the answer was somebody else's plan, and a plan is not a column and a person is not a plan, and the eleventh time I said nothing and nobody has ever noticed that there was a first time.**"

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that a plan is not a column and a fortnight is not a figure and that both of those had been entered in the last two weeks by a man of fifty-six, and that a woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years had arrived at the same place from the other end of it and by a road nobody in this district has built, and that the two are not joined.

Nobody joined them and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had wanted to join them and had not and that this is the seventeenth time, and a man of fifty-six said that the count of the times a clerk of nineteen years had wanted to join two things and had not had gone past twenty and that he had started it, and that a man who starts a count and gets to twenty and cannot stop is the reason this district has a column called NOT A FINDING.

**And on the first of November a woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years said that on the thirty-first of March a person is still standing in the thing, and that is why the no is the wrong day.** She said that she has been the last one out eleven times and eleven times the answer was somebody else's plan, and a person is not a plan, and she was asked in the six things and was not asked about the eleven years and was not asked about the sixth year.

---

**The train on the siding had stood two hundred and sixty-two days on the thirty-first of October and two hundred and sixty-three on the first of November.** A man of seventeen wrote both figures in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and beside them there are now six dates on one line and a rule drawn under the sixth of them and no seventh date.

Nobody said so. A man of about nineteen who walked in from the road and is the sixth of the six asked the boy what the rule under the sixth date was for, and the boy said it was a rule, and the man of about nineteen entered that the boy said it was a rule and did not say what

For, and that not asked is in the count. The boy of seventeen is not a keeper of the index, is not paid for it, and was not asked whether he would keep it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered all three and that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and the count of protected things is five and he is in neither and neither moved.

A man of fifty-six did the arithmetic out loud in a lane on the first of November in four minutes and it is in the minute in his own words, and the derivation is from the eleventh of February and not from any other figure in any book.

"Seventeen days left in February after the eleventh, then one hundred and eighty-four days to the thirty-first of August, which is two hundred and one. Thirty more in September makes two hundred and thirty-one. Thirty-one more in October makes two hundred and sixty-two. One more for today makes two hundred and sixty-three."

A clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of the working and that nobody has asked him to give the working twice and that this is the first time in eleven weeks a figure has been put in a minute with the sum under it, and that the sum is the district's own and was made on a flat thirty-four miles east by a man who does not live in this district and came four times in October.

**AND ON THE FIRST OF NOVEMBER THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-THREE DAYS, DERIVED FROM THE ELEVENTH OF FEBRUARY, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX PUT THE SUM UNDER THE FIGURE IN A MINUTE FOR THE FIRST TIME IN ELEVEN WEEKS, AND BESIDE THE BOY OF SEVENTEEN'S INDEX THERE ARE SIX DATES ON ONE LINE AND A RULE UNDER THE SIXTH AND NO SEVENTH DATE.**

---

On the second of November about nine people walked to the second channel and looked at a frame, and a woman of thirty-eight who has been in the second channel four years stood in it without being asked and then got out of it, and it took her about four seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked her to get out and that she got out because a man of about thirty-four who digs loam had said *is it the same four seconds as the other end* and that he had said it standing at the lip.

The frame is four feet by three. The channel under it is two feet wide and nine inches deep. A man of fifty-six who does count for a living measured both and got the same two numbers twice and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing wrote them down and the clerk of nineteen years wrote them down and the two figures are in the minute and one of them is in feet and one of them is in inches and they are not added.

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said the thing that named the hole in the second form **and she said it standing in the mud, and it is thirty-two words and the clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got thirty-two both times.**

"**The frame is where the person stands and the channel is where the year comes out of. A notice about the frame is a notice about the part the person can leave.**"

A man of fifty-six asked what a notice about the channel would look like and a woman of thirty-eight said that nobody knows, and that a channel is a hole with a lip and the lip is what holds it and the lip is not on any of the five boards and was not on any of the five boards in October either, and that she said that in a field on the second of October and that a man of thirty-four wrote it down beside a plinth and did not join the two, and that she is not asking anybody to join them now.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the second form is therefore pointed at the frame and the year comes out of the channel, and that a district with one instrument and one instrument pointed at the wrong one has a worse position than a district with no instrument at all, because a district with no instrument knows it has nothing.

"That is the finding. We have got a door. It is in the right direction. It is in a wall."

Nobody argued with that and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a frame is a frame and a channel is a channel and that the notice is against the frame and the year is taken by the channel, and that the two are not joined, and that a man of thirty-four who digs loam filled seven of the eleven rows on the ledger and has the sixth column open in front of him and has not filled it in one of the seven.

**AND ON THE SECOND OF NOVEMBER A FRAME WAS MEASURED AND FOUND TO BE FOUR FEET BY THREE AND THE CHANNEL UNDER IT TO BE TWO FEET WIDE AND NINE INCHES DEEP, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO TAKES IN WASHING AT THE SECOND CHANNEL SAID THAT THE FRAME IS WHERE THE PERSON STANDS AND THE CHANNEL IS WHERE THE YEAR COMES OUT OF, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM SAID THAT THE DISTRICT HAS ONE INSTRUMENT AND IT IS POINTED AT THE WRONG ONE, AND THAT A DISTRICT WITH NO INSTRUMENT AT ALL KNOWS IT HAS NOTHING.**


---

On the second of November the second form was read out at the ward market counter and at the ninth place and at the fourth place **and it was read out three times and not twice, and a reader at the counter said that the rule of the counter is twice and that she had done it three times and had been asked by nobody to stop and was not going to explain why she had done it three.**

A man of fifty-six said that the third reading was for the page that is not there, and that a district reading a form three times is a district expecting to be asked about it, and that the district is not allowed to expect anything and has been told so about four times in eleven weeks.

A stranger who had been in that yard on the twenty-ninth and had taken a copy cold was in the room on the second of November and asked one question, and it was the only question he asked, and it went into the minute in his own words and it is twenty-six words.

"**If I hold this and I am a stranger and the year is on a page, what does the sheet let me do with the year.**"

Nobody answered him for about nineteen seconds and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said the answer in seven words and the stranger said the man was right and then said a second thing, and the second thing is in the minute and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it is the first time a stranger has agreed with anybody in a room in eleven weeks.

"**It lets you hold it. Nothing else.**"

"**That is the first thing anybody has said in a room in two months that I have not already had a copy of.**"

Nobody said anything about that.

**A clerk of nineteen years entered that a stranger had said that he had not already had a copy of something said in a room, and that there is no column in any book in this basin for what people outside a district have copies of, and that she had wanted to put the four hundred printed sheets and the copy in one line and had not, and that this is the eighteenth time.**

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that the four hundred printed sheets have been out since the sixth of September and that nobody has ever had to print another one, and that a document nobody has ever had to print a second time is either the most useful thing this district has produced or the reason it is behind, and that he does not know which and would rather somebody else said so in about four years.

---
**AND ON THE SECOND OF NOVEMBER A STRANGER WHO HAD TAKEN A COPY OF THE LEDGER COLD ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF OCTOBER ASKED WHAT THE SHEET LET HIM DO WITH THE YEAR, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ANSWERED IT IN NINE WORDS, AND THE ANSWER WAS THAT IT LETS HIM DO NOTHING WITH IT, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT THE FINDING IS THAT A DOCUMENT OF YEARS IN THIS DISTRICT GIVES A PERSON NOTHING TO DO WITH A YEAR.**

AND THE SECOND FORM IS STILL NOT ONE OF THE FIVE THINGS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE AND IS NOT ONE OF THE SIX IT HAS BUILT AND NOT NAMED AND IS NOT ONE OF THE THREE DOCUMENTS NOBODY OWNS **AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ENTERED THAT ON THE SECOND OF NOVEMBER AND WOULD NOT HAVE IT ENTERED IN EITHER LIST.**
