# Chapter 128: What She Was Offering

---

She came over the raised plank road on the eleventh of October with two ledgers under her arm and a man carrying a bag behind her, and the water was not on the planks, and the man carrying the bag was asked to leave the road at the ninth mile and did, and the reason he was asked to leave the road is in the minute and the reason is that the road is a raised plank road and not a place where two people walk abreast, and that this is a fact about the road.

She was let into the room where the four things are read out because a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel was at the door and was asked whether she wanted the room cleared, and she said no, and the minute says she said no in four seconds and says that the asker did not explain who the woman was before she said no, and that the asker entered that afterwards and entered that it was his fault and not hers.

She gave her name and it is in the minute in her own words and it is **Hester Vail**, and the firm took nine more words and the nine are in the minute, and the clerk of nineteen years asked whether to enter the name and she said no in four seconds and gave the reason in nine words.

"**Enter an agent of a guarantee house. Do not enter the name. A name in a room becomes a thing a person can be held to and I am not holding anybody to anything today.**"

And the clerk entered an agent of a guarantee house, and **Hester Vail is not entered in any register in the west bank, and is not on the board of eleven, and is not in the market's own book, and about nineteen people heard it and four of them wrote it down at home and the clerk entered that four of them wrote it down and did not enter what they wrote.**

---

She did not ask for the four things to be read out and she did not ask to be introduced to anybody, and the minute says she was asked three questions in the five things and answered all three in nine seconds, and the clerk entered that she answered faster than anybody in that room had answered anything since the fourth of April.

"What is the job."

"**Sit in this room and be asked things and answer them.**"

"How long is it."

"**Two days, and I have not asked anybody to extend it and I have not asked anybody for anything at all, and I would like that entered first and before I say the other thing.**"

"What does it cost."

"**Nothing to you. That is not a courtesy and I am going to spend four minutes taking it off the table so that nobody in this room has to wonder about it later.**"

For the two days before the eleventh of October she was on the far bank, four miles off this flat and a mile short of the head of the water, at a house that is not in this district and is not on any paper this district holds, and nobody on this flat asked her anything and nobody spoke to her about what she had come for.

The clerk of nineteen years entered that on the ninth and the tenth of October, which were the two days before she came over the raised plank road, there were about nine people on this flat who had heard that a guarantee house had a person coming to it, and had not gone and looked to see whether the person had come.

She entered that a woman of fifty-eight who carries water had looked and had said nine words about it, and that the nine words were that the water was up that day and that she had more work and that a person who is going to be handed the figure of twenty-one years ought to be somebody who has carried water.

Nobody in that room repeated the nine words afterwards and the minute says nobody did, and says that the clerk of nineteen years wrote them down and that the woman of fifty-eight who carries water did not read figures and had not given her sentence to anybody to be written and had given it anyway.

The four minutes she took it off the table in are in the minute and they are the first four minutes of the first day, and they are nine lines long, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that she had not been asked for them and had given them and that a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households read them and said that he had been doing this for nineteen years and had never once had somebody take a price off the table before the price went on it.

Then she put the first ledger on the table and opened it, and the clerk of nineteen years counted the lines in it in four minutes, and the count is nineteen rows and four columns, and the first column is a person, the second is a term, the third is a number of years, and the fourth is a release, and **three of the nineteen rows have a name in the first column and sixteen do not.**

"**It is a lot. First column the person, second the term, third the years, fourth the release, and the first column is a bearer column and sixteen of my nineteen rows have nothing in it.**"

"And the three."

"**The three have names in them and I am not going to read them out and I am going to tell you why, which is that I have been asked this three times in eleven years and the answer I gave the third time was that I would rather have a blank column than a name a person cannot take out. Make of that what you like. I have been called honest and I have been called careful and I have never once been called both.**"

And a woman of about fifty-eight who carries water asked her, on the second day, a question about the wage, and the question is in the minute in the woman's own words, and the clerk entered that it was nineteen words and that the woman of fifty-eight who carries water had counted them herself on her fingers under the table, and the answer is in the minute in the agent's own words and it ran to thirty-one.

"**What does the clerk do all day.**"

"**She writes down what a year takes off a person and she is paid nine coppers a day and she has a day off and she can say no to the work and if she says no to the work the work is not done and everybody knows it, and that is the whole of it and I have had it for nine years.**"

And a woman of fifty-eight who carries water said that she had nine people on her round and that if one of them said no to the work then the water came late that week and everybody knew that too, and the agent said yes in four seconds, and the minute says that the two of them agreed about a thing in four seconds and that it is the only thing in three days that two women agreed about without a clerk writing it down at the time.

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF OCTOBER AN AGENT OF A GUARANTEE HOUSE CAME FOUR DAYS OVER A RAISED PLANK ROAD WITH TWO LEDGERS AND DID NOT HIDE WHAT SHE WAS OFFERING, AND THE OFFERING WAS THAT A GUARANTEE HOUSE WOULD CARRY THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS AND A YEAR AND A HALF AND THE TWO PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT KNOWN AND NOT ASKED, AND THE PRICE WAS NOT COPPERS, AND THE PRICE WAS A STANDING RIGHT IN THE ROTATION, FIRST REFUSAL ON THE PERSON WHO STANDS IN THE FRAME, AND SHE SAID ALL OF IT IN NINE MINUTES AND SHE SAID IT THE SAME WAY TWICE.**

---

And then she said the part that is why this is in the book, and the clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong, and it is in the minute in the agent's own words and it is the first time in ten weeks that a person in this basin has said the word *future* in a room and meant a person.

"**I am not going to put that in smaller words, because you would work out what the small words meant and then we would both be having this conversation in a fortnight instead of now. I am offering to take twenty-one years off your book. I am offering to pay for the sheet that says what a year is. I am offering to put a clerk in this room with a wage and a day off, which nobody in this district has ever had, and I will pay that clerk myself and not ask you for it. And in exchange I want three things and I have written them down and I will read them out and you may write on them.**"

She read three things off a sheet and the sheet has a firm on it and the sheet is in the room and the minute says the sheet was in the room and that a stranger could have taken it, and the clerk entered that nobody stopped him and that he did not, and that he was a stranger with a coat and no bag and that he had been in the room for nine minutes.

> **ONE. A COPY OF EVERY LEDGER THIS DISTRICT KEEPT OF WHAT A YEAR TAKES OFF A PERSON, AND A COPY IS KEPT AT MY COUNTING HOUSE AND IS GIVEN BACK WHEN THE SEASON CLOSES OR WHEN I ASK FOR IT, AND I WILL ASK FOR IT.**
>
> **TWO. FIRST REFUSAL ON THE PERSON WHO STANDS IN THE FRAME IN ANY SEASON, FOR ANY CHANNEL IN THIS DISTRICT, AND FIRST REFUSAL MEANS MY HOUSE NAMES THE PERSON BEFORE THE ROOM DOES AND NOBODY MAY ASK WHY.**
>
> **THREE. THE CROWN ON THE FIGURE STAYS IN THIS BASIN AND GOES TO NOBODY ELSE, AND I WILL PUT THAT IN WRITING AND HAVE IT WITNESSED, AND I WILL NOT CHARGE FOR IT.**

Nobody in that room said anything for nineteen seconds, and a woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years said four sentences, and she is not an officer of that room and is not asked to be and did not speak for it, and the clerk entered that before she spoke it was entered that she was not speaking for it.

"**Yours is the same instrument with a house behind it. Ours is four things on four sheets with no heading on them and I will not sign them, and the difference between those two things is that I have said no in public and you have said yes nineteen times in a book and you have called it a ledger.**"

"That is not a no."

"**It is not a no and I am not going to make it one in front of nineteen people who have never seen a guarantee house, and I am going to say the other three sentences and then I am going to sit down.**"

**THE ROOM ASKED HER WHETHER THE FIRST REFUSAL COULD BE TAKEN OUT OF THE THREE THINGS, AND SHE SAID THAT IT COULD, AND THAT SHE WOULD PUT IT IN WRITING WITHOUT BEING ASKED TWICE, AND THAT SHE HAD PUT IT IN HER OWN LIST BEFORE SHE CAME AND THAT THE LIST IS FOUR LINES LONG AND THREE OF THE FOUR ARE THE SAME KIND OF THING, AND THAT A PERSON WHO HAS BEEN ASKED NOTHING CANNOT ANSWER ANY OF THE FOUR.**

---

**AND THE ROOM DID NOT SAY YES AND DID NOT SAY NO, AND THE ENTRY OF THE ELEVENTH OF OCTOBER SAYS THAT A ROOM OF ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE, FOUR OF WHOM STAND IN FRAMES, WAS ASKED WHETHER IT WOULD GIVE A GUARANTEE HOUSE A COPY OF EVERY LEDGER IT KEEPS AND A FIRST REFUSAL ON THE PERSON WHO STANDS IN THE FRAME, AND THAT IT WAS ASKED BY THE PERSON WHO OFFERED AND NOT BY ANYBODY ELSE, AND THAT NOTHING WAS DECIDED, AND THAT A DECISION WAS NOT REQUIRED ON THE DAY.**

And a man of about thirty-one who runs a cart of meal out of a lane in Alder Reach had come over the road with a party on the second of October and was still on the flat on the eleventh, and he asked her one question on the second day, and the question is in the minute in his own words, and the agent answered it three words early, which the clerk entered.

"**What is it worth to you.**"

"**Twenty-one years and a year and a half and two I cannot name, and I did not get them for nothing and I am not going to tell you what I gave for them, and there are nine people on this flat who have given a year each without a price and I have made them into a book, and that is the whole of what I am.**"

"And the third thing on the sheet."

"**The third thing on the sheet is free and I have offered it free on purpose, because the first two cost you something and the third costs me the argument, and a person who reads a list of three things and counts them is going to count three and not one, and I wanted one of the three to be free so that you would not count.**"

A man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households said that he had counted three, and the agent said that he had, and that he was welcome to, and that a man who counts is a man she can work with and that is not an insult.

Then she said the other thing, and she said it at four in the afternoon with her hands flat on the closed ledger, and the clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it and read it back and got nothing wrong, and it is the reason the ledger is not on any document in this district.

"**I will tell you where the sixteen came from and I am not going to tell you the name of the place, and I want both halves of that sentence in your book in that order.**"

"It is not a courtesy."

"**It is not a courtesy and I want that entered too, and the reason is that a name in a room becomes a thing a person can be held to, and I have held one, and I am not doing it twice in one week.**"

A man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households was asked, after she had said no about the place, whether the sixteen rows were written by sixteen different people or by her, and she said that the sixteen were written by four clerks over eleven years and that the four clerks had never met her and that she had never met any of the four, and that a ledger like that is not a document anybody can check and that the whole of her trade is that it does not need checking, and that she had said that last part out loud on purpose.

Nobody in that room said anything about it and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who has just told you that her instrument cannot be checked has put that in a room on purpose and that this is the fourth time in ten weeks that somebody has said the thing about their own instrument out loud in a room of about nineteen people, and that the other three were a woman of forty-four who wrote four things, a man of about forty-four who has a channel, and a registrar who said that her instrument is a book that works if a person comes to the yard.

And she said that she had heard all three of those, and that she came here for exactly that reason, and that nobody in Alder Reach had told her about any of the three and that she had asked about them in four rooms on her way in and that the four rooms had all told her.

Nobody in that room said anything about the four rooms and the clerk of nineteen years entered that a stranger had been in four rooms in this district asking about a book, a channel and four sheets of paper, and that she did not know which rooms, and that the agent would not say, and that the clerk had not asked, and that not asked is in the count.

Nobody in that room asked her again, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing asked her once, on the way to the door, and she said no in four seconds, and the minute says that no is the fourth no she has given in two days and that three of the four were about her own name and one was about a place and that she did not change the order of them.

And a clerk of nineteen years entered that the heading of the page is in the agent's own hand and that the heading is in the room, and that nineteen people saw a heading and did not read it, and that the agent offered to read it and did not read it and said that she would put her hand over it if anybody asked, and that about four people in the room had been about to ask and had not, and that a woman of fifty-eight who carries water had said that a hand over a page is a mark, and a mark is a person's refusal of a name, and a mark is not a person.

And the finding of the eleventh and the twelfth of October is also **that a guarantee house has no word for what it takes, and uses three wrong words for it and has known for eleven years, and said it out loud twice in two days to about nineteen people and nobody wrote either one down, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote them down after the room had broken up, which a clerk of nineteen years is not supposed to do, and entered that she had done it.**

**AND THE FINDING OF THE ELEVENTH OF OCTOBER IS THAT A GUARANTEE HOUSE OFFERED THIS DISTRICT SOMETHING IT HAS NOT BEEN OFFERED BEFORE, WHICH IS A CLERK WITH A WAGE AND A DAY OFF, AND THE PRICE WAS NOT THE CLERK, AND THE FINDING IS ALSO THAT A PERSON WHO HAS BEEN OFFERED SOMETHING HONEST BY SOMEONE WHO SAYS IT IS HONEST HAS BEEN OFFERED A THING SHE CANNOT REFUSE WITHOUT LOOKING UNGRACEFUL, AND THAT THIS DISTRICT HAS BEEN UNGRACEFUL FOUR TIMES IN TEN WEEKS AND HAS NOT ONCE NOTICED.**

And a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel was not in the room on the second day and has not been asked why, and not asked is in the count, and the count is a column and not a bucket.

And a clerk of nineteen years asked one question of her own on the second day, and the minute says it was the only question in three days that was not about a figure, and the question is in the minute in the clerk's own hand.

"**What is the word for the thing you take.**"

"**There is not one in my house. We call it the first column and we call the year a charge and we call the person the holder, and none of those three words is the thing and I have known that for eleven years and I have said it out loud twice and both times to a man who was about to be difficult with me.**"

"You have not said it out loud to us."

"**I have said it out loud to you twice. On the first day and on the second day. Nobody in this room has written either of them down, and I have not asked anybody to, and that is the first thing I have wanted in eleven years and have not got.**"

And the clerk of nineteen years wrote both of them down after she had gone, at nine in the evening on the fourteenth, and the minute says she wrote them from memory and that a clerk of nineteen years is not supposed to write things down after a room has broken up and that she had done it and that she had entered that she had done it.

And on the twelfth, the thirteenth and the fourteenth of October the agent sat in the room about nine hours a day and answered every question that was put to her, and the room did not get through them faster than that, and the clerk of nineteen years counted the questions at the end of the third day and the count is one hundred and forty-one, and the minute says that a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households asked the last one and it was a question about a woman's ninth year and the agent answered it in nine words and the room wrote the nine words down.

Nobody in this district has ever asked a stranger anything about a woman's ninth year, and the clerk entered that, and entered that the woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years and who has no ninth year heard the nine words and did not say that she had no ninth year, and the minute says she did not say it and that a man of thirty-four who mends fencing noticed that she did not say it and did not ask her about it.

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel was asked on the thirteenth whether she wanted the agent to carry the ledger away, and she said that she did not know what the agent was carrying, and the agent said that she was carrying nothing at all and that the ledger was still on the table, and the woman said that was the first thing anybody had said in ten weeks that was about a table.

**AND THE THING THAT WAS LEFT BEHIND ON THE FOURTEENTH OF OCTOBER WAS A SHEET WITH A FIRM ON IT AND THREE THINGS WRITTEN ON IT IN A HAND THAT WAS NOT A CLERK'S, AND THE SHEET WENT WEST ON A CART ON THE FIFTEENTH OF OCTOBER, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT READ IT ON THE SEVENTEENTH AT THE FOURTH PLACE AND IS WRITING FOUR WORDS UNDER IT THAT ARE NOT IN ANY BOOK, AND HE HAS SAID THEM ONCE, TO HIMSELF, IN A ROOM OVER A SHOP, AND THEY WERE: WE ALREADY HAVE IT.**
