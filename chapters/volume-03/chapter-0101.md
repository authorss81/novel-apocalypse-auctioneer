# Chapter 101: Where Forms Come From

A licensed valuer of twelve years laid a guild certificate face up on a counting-room table on the eighth of this month and counted its columns out loud with a thumbnail, and got to twenty-nine, and then put his hand flat on the paper and said that he had been asked for a thirtieth and that he could not get one out of anybody he knew.

He is not a slow man. He has certified a lot CLEAN four times in this district and each time he has written on the back of it, in his own hand, that a clean lot and an unfilled standing line are both true. **Twenty-nine columns and not one of them is a place, and he said that in ninety seconds in May and did not know he was doing it, and on the eighth of this month he was asked whether a thirtieth can be got and had to answer a man standing up.**

"There is no thirty," he said. "**There is no thirty anywhere in this basin and there has never been one, and the reason is not a rule and nobody in this room is going to be able to make it a rule by wanting it. The reason is that nobody who makes forms has ever needed one.**"

The counting room is over a chandler's shop and it has two windows and one door and about thirty people in it, and the rain that had stopped on the fourth came back on the sixth and had not come back again, and the boards in the room have been in since the eighth of last month and a man of sixty-one painted a place's name on one of them at the height of a hand at two in the morning so that a person who is frightened in the dark can find it with their fingers.

---

The asker was the man who keeps the fourth toll board at the ninth place, and he is thirty-nine and he is a signalman of a bridge administration and he is not a bearer and he is not paid, and he came down the bank for the third time in a month and he came for a form and not for a railway.

He wrote the request out before he left and read it out twice on the shoulder to make sure it said what he meant, and the minute records that, and the minute also records that a form with three columns in it has never once been written out by a person who did not already know what the three columns were for.

What he wanted was one column. **A column for a thing that has a day and a place and an amount, in that order, all three at once, for a toll that lands on a place.**

"I have got three of the four in two months," he said. "**I have got a toll that has a name. I have got a toll that has a date and no place. I have got a toll that has a place and no name. And the thing that would hold all three in one line is the fifth thing this district does not have, and the fifth thing is a form, and I have now been told by two people in two rooms that a form is a piece of paper with columns on it.**"

He put his own request on the table face up and it was four lines long, and one of the lines had been crossed out and rewritten, and the rewrite was longer than the line it replaced.

> **I AM THE KEEPER OF THE BOARD AT THE NINTH PLACE. THE BOARD HAS A COLUMN RULED IN PENCIL AT ITS TWO ENDS AND EMPTY BETWEEN THEM SINCE THE TWENTY-SECOND OF JUNE AND IT HAS THREE THINGS IN IT AND NONE OF THEM IS A FIGURE. I WANT A FORM WITH THREE COLUMNS IN IT. A DAY. A PLACE. AN AMOUNT. I WANT TO KNOW WHO SIGNS IT. I WANT TO KNOW WHAT IT COSTS. I WANT IT BEFORE THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JULY OR I WANT IT REFUSED IN WRITING SO THAT I CAN PUT THE REFUSAL ON THE BOARD.**

Nobody in the room argued with the request. The clerk of nineteen years read it out in the ordinary voice and about thirty people were in the room, and the reader at the ward market counter was sent for and came and read it out a second time, because the rule of that counter is that a document goes out twice.

**The finding of the eighth of this month is that this district has a fifth instrument it does not have, and that the fifth instrument is a form, and that a form is not made in a counting room.**

---

A form is made by an office, and that is the whole of it, and it took the room about eleven minutes, and it is not a discovery about anything at all.

"There are five offices this district deals with and not one of them is in this building," said the man who keeps the board. "**The river office. The water office. The guild. The bridge office. And the one that keeps the roll, and I have been in it four times in nine weeks and I have never once seen anybody making a piece of paper.**"

The roll office makes the rate sheet, and the rate sheet is the only form anybody in this district has ever been handed that fits a person, and it has been in use for nine years, and it has got exactly one thing in it that is not a count.

A signature.

"A signature is a person," said the tenth hand, who had been standing at the back of the room for about twenty minutes and had not said a word until then. "**A rate sheet gets a signature because a rate sheet is a thing a person agrees to pay. A column with a place in it is a thing a person agrees to be charged. And the difference between those two is a signature, and the difference between a signature and a form is that a form can be printed and a signature cannot.**"

"That is right and it does not help," said the man who keeps the board. "**A form is printed. A form is printed by a house that takes a commission. A house that takes a commission is a firm. And a firm cannot be asked, and that is the third time somebody in this room has said that sentence in nine weeks and it is the correct one and I have written it on my own board.**"

"You have not written that sentence on your board," said the tenth hand. "**You have written a sentence about where forms come from and it is the twenty-second one and I have read it, and it is nearly the same sentence and it is not the same sentence, and I have been in nine rooms in nine weeks where a nearly-the-same sentence did half the work of a same sentence.**"

In the next half hour somebody fetched the rate sheet and put it on the table, and the room counted its columns out loud the way the valuer had counted his own, and it took about two minutes and got to nine.

Nine is the whole of it. Fifty coppers a quarter over four thousand and one households, in five columns, and a heading over the four columns that are amounts, and a date at the foot with a place on it, and a line at the bottom for a person to sign. There is no column for a household and there is no column for a person and there is no column for a place. **The rate sheet collects money from four thousand and one households and it cannot say where any of them are, and it has been in use for nine years, and the reason it can collect is that a rate is asked for and a rate that is not asked for is a rate that has not been agreed to.**

"A rate is a form that somebody agreed to," said the man who keeps the board, when he had finished counting. "**That is the only form in this district that has ever worked and it worked because four thousand and one people were asked, and that is not a thing about paper. That is a thing about four thousand and one people being asked.**"

"And the toll board is a form that nobody agreed to," said the tenth hand, "**which is why it has three things in it and no number, and I would like the room to notice that those are the same two facts about forms that we had an hour ago and that we have not moved.**"

The room did notice it. Nobody wrote it down for about half an hour, and then the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down, and the entry says the delay was nine minutes and that the delay is the market's and not the clerk's.

The reader at the ward market counter was still in the room, and she is about fifty and she is a signal clerk before her shoulder went and she is on eleven coppers a quarter for four days a week, and the term has no clause for a shoulder. She had been standing at the back with her arms folded and she asked one question, and it was the only question anybody asked in two hours that was about the work and not about the paper.

"**You have all been talking about the fifth thing this district does not have. I would like to know how many things the people in this room think this district does not have, and I would like it counted out loud, and I would like the number written on the wall under the heading that says NOT ANSWERED, and I would like somebody who is not me to write it.**"

Nobody argued and it was counted out loud in about four minutes and it is four.

**A way of saying that a toll lands on a place. A column that is a place. An office. A station with nobody at it.** Four things, and the fourth of them has a person attached to it and a boy of nineteen who will be there and has not been asked and says he is nobody's keeper.

And the fifth thing the keeper of the board came down the bank for is not on that wall, and the clerk said so, and the reader said she did not know it was a fifth thing and did not want to be told twice in one morning.

**And the count on the wall is four, and the count of what the people in the room were talking about is five, and the finding of the tenth of this month is that the two counts are not joined and are not to be joined, and that a wall is the only instrument in this district that somebody can go and read without anybody having to stand in a room.**

---

The man at the rail was asked, because his office can be asked, and he was asked on the ninth of this month on a published day with about nineteen people at the rail and the request in his hand before anybody asked him whether he would read it.

He is Selik Marne and he is nine years in the post and he is argued with and never defeated and never converted and not wrong.

"I have answered a question like this in a counting room before," he said. "**I am going to answer it again, and it is going to take about four minutes, and none of it is going to help you, and I would like that in the minute before I start so that nobody in this room wastes the four minutes hoping.**"

Nobody wrote it down, and the minute says nobody wrote it down, and the finding of the ninth is that a man who has said in advance that he will not help is the only person in this district who has ever said so about himself before anybody asked him.

Then he said the thing. It was four sentences and it was correct, and it is in the minute in his own words because the reader wrote it down and he did not stop her.

"**My office can be asked and my office cannot make a form either. A bridge office makes a certificate about a span, and the certificate has got a column for a load and a column for a date and a column for the two people who signed it, and it has not got a column for a place, because a bridge is a place and a bridge office does not have to say so.**" He turned the page over without reading it. "**I have read four of the five forms this district runs. Every one of them was written by somebody who needed the paper for themselves and not for you. The reason the fifth thing has not been made is not that nobody here knows how to make it. It is that the man who would make it has never been charged for making it.**"

A woman at the back of the group said the second half of it before he got there, and he looked at her, and she did not stop.

"That is the sentence you said about your own man," she said. "**You said an office that has to ask is an office that can be refused, and now you have said an office that would have had to be charged for it is an office that has not been asked, and those two are one sentence and you have been making it for nine weeks and nobody in this district has written it down once.**"

"It is one sentence," he said. "**I would like it written down, and I would like it written down by a person who is not me, and I would like the entry to say that I said it and not that she did, because in about four years somebody will take that page cold and will want to know who found it.**"

Then he said the sentence he says on published days, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people who did not come to hear it and are getting used to it.

"That is not enough. **And I am going to keep saying it for as long as I hold this post, and nobody in this district believes I will stop and I am not going to stop, and I have said it for nine weeks and the sentence has got longer rather than shorter and the length is the only thing about it that has changed.**"

Nobody argued with him and the minute enters that nobody argued with him, and that about half the people at that rail had come to hear the sentence and had arrived early for it, and that two people who had not come to hear it stayed afterwards for about four minutes, and that the reader entered both of those separately because one of them is a habit and the other one is not.

**The finding of the ninth of this month is that four of the five offices this district deals with can be written to and one of them can be asked, and that the one that can be asked is the only one that has ever answered, and that the reason it has answered four times in nine weeks is that a man in it wanted to be asked about something that was not his.**

He was walked back down the rail afterwards by nobody in particular, which is a thing that happens to him about twice a quarter and which he has never once entered in a book, and on this occasion a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide walked with him for about forty yards and then stopped and said one sentence that went into a book on the tenth and not on the ninth.

"**You have been the only person in this district who says that he cannot help, and it has taken you nine weeks to work out that saying it out loud is the only thing you have that nobody else has, and I am saying so that it is in a book and not in your own head.**"

---

And the reader was asked to read on the tenth of this month in a counting room at about eleven in the morning in the ordinary voice, and he was not asked whether he would read.

He is thirty-eight. He can write a bounded charter, split a use among bearers, close a transfer, make a lot portable or renewable under a stated term, and post a security, and he has posted one and it is a day a week and three bound copies of a book and it ends on the twenty-ninth of this month.

**He cannot maintain a public lot, coordinate more than one bearer, arrange a dispute hearing, or make the consequences of an object visible to a whole settlement, and he said all of that in about eleven seconds and then he said the part that was the answer, and the part that was the answer was not in the four powers at all.**

"I can write the terms of a thing," he said. "**I can put a day on it and a place on it and an amount on it and two people who have agreed to it, and I can close it, and I can post something against it that somebody else can go and look at. A form is not any of those. A form is a blank. A form is a thing made before anybody has agreed to anything, and I have no power in this district over a thing made before anybody has agreed to anything, and there is nothing I can do about that this morning and there is nothing I could do about it in a yard either.**"

"You could write one anyway," said somebody at the back of the room.

"**I could, and I would be the only man in this district who had ever printed a form, and in about four years there would be a form in a drawer with my name at the foot of it and no office behind it, and every one of the nine weeks we have just had would come back up that drawer.**"

Nobody argued with him about it. The minute records that nobody argued with him about it, and records that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that this was the fourth time in nine weeks that somebody had been laughed at for a correct thing, and that nobody in the room laughed, and that he had expected somebody to.

---

The finding of the tenth of this month is four lines long and it is in the public lot book in two hands, and the first hand is the valuer's and the second is the registrar's, and the registrar's is the second half and the reason she gives is the reason she gives every time.

> **A FORM IS MADE BY AN OFFICE. AN OFFICE IS A THING THAT CAN BE ASKED. THIS DISTRICT DEALS WITH FIVE OFFICES AND CAN REACH FIVE REGISTERS AND THE OFFICE BEING LOOKED FOR IS IN NONE OF THEM. EVERY FORM THIS DISTRICT HAS RUN IN NINE YEARS WAS PRINTED BY SOMEBODY WHO NEEDED IT FOR THEMSELVES.**
>
> **THIS IS A FACT ABOUT FOUR PIECES OF PAPER. IT IS NOT AN ANSWER TO ANYTHING. THE REGISTRAR ASKED THAT IT BE ENTERED IN THE COLUMN FOR THINGS THAT ARE NOT FINDINGS AND IT IS ENTERED THERE AND IT IS THE THIRD LINE IN THAT COLUMN SINCE THE FIFTH OF JULY.**

**The four things this district does not have all came out of the same four rooms, and the fifth thing would have to come out of a fifth one, and there is no fifth room in this basin that has ever printed anything at all.** That is the sentence the man who keeps the board wrote on the bottom line of his own board in chalk on the evening of the tenth, and it is the twenty-second sentence on that board, and the rule of the board is that anybody standing in front of it may write one sentence and will not be asked to explain it.

Chalk does not last through a wet week. The keeper wipes the bottom line when it is wet and has said since the eighth of last month that he does not know what the argument for the column is, and that is now the second time in a month that he has said it, and both times in public.

"I said on the second of this month that I did not know what the argument was," he said. "**And there are three things in the column, and now there is a sentence about where forms come from, and I would like it recorded that the sentence is more use to me than the three things are, and that I have not been paid for either of them and that the not being paid is not a finding, it is just a fact about me.**"

---

What the tenth of this month also carried, in the hours the counter was shut, was a boy's hand in an index and a figure in it that has not moved in five weeks and will not move in twenty-one days unless somebody makes a train go.

Orren Pike is seventeen and is paid four coppers a quarter and is not Adrian Vale's apprentice and has said so in a yard in front of more than one person. He keeps an index of a line that nobody built and that he built.

The train on that siding came in on its own wheels on the eleventh of February and has not moved since, and it is nine vehicles, and on the eighth of this month it had stood a hundred and forty-seven days. On the tenth of this month it had stood a hundred and forty-nine, and he wrote the new figure and did not strike the old one, and both of them are in the index because the index is a record of what a line did and not a record of what anybody believed about it.

Beside the figure there are five dates on one line, and the fifth of them is the twenty-ninth of this month, and **not one of the five has anybody's name against it.** He has been asked twice whether that is an oversight and has said twice that it is not, and on the tenth of this month a clerk asked him a third time and he gave a third answer, and it is the longest thing he has said in nine weeks.

"They are the same five things and they do not go the same way," he said. "**One of them is a term running out. One of them is a week turning over. One of them is a day a week that a man wrote in chalk on a board and nobody is paid for. One of them is the day a man says he will come twice a month. And the fifth of them is the day the iron has to be out of that bracket or the bracket is a thing with a paperweight in it.**" He put a hand flat on the page. "**Put them in a column and a stranger can read one of them and think it is the other four. A column is for things that go the same way and these five do not.**"

"And on the twenty-ninth of this month," said the clerk, "what happens to the iron."

"Nothing," said the boy. "**Nobody has asked it to go anywhere and nobody has to take it out, and that is the first interesting thing that has happened on this line in five weeks and it is not written down anywhere except in here, and I would like somebody to ask me to write it down properly and I would like to be asked in a room.**"

Nobody asked him in a room. **The finding of the tenth is also that a boy of seventeen has said in writing that he will only do a thing properly if he is asked in a room, and that four adults heard it, and that a request to be asked in a room is a condition with no end on it and is not a term, and that the count of conditions with no end on them is four and is a different kind of thing from the count of five and is never added to it.**

And on the evening of the tenth a woman of about sixty with a stick was at the back of the counting room and had not been asked anything and had not expected to be, and she asked one question about the four lines on the page, and it is in the minute in her words and it is the second time in nine weeks she has corrected a document by saying what she needed in it.

"**You have written down that there is no thirty anywhere in this basin. You have not written down that nobody asked the five offices whether there is a thirty anywhere in this basin, and those are two different things and one of them is a finding and one of them is a guess, and the guess is the one in the book.**"

The valuer said that she was right, and the entry was altered in four minutes with the alteration signed and dated, and **the finding of the tenth of this month is that the first line anybody in this district has entered about a missing form was wrong in its first hour and was put right by a woman who is on no roll, and that this is now the fourth time in nine weeks that a person nobody asked has been the one who made a document say what it meant.**

And a stranger took a sheet cold off a board of eleven in the yard of Lot 17 on the tenth of this month, in about two minutes, and did not ask anybody anything, and the room let him, and the minute says the room let him, and nobody has ever stopped one of them, and the reason nobody has ever stopped one of them is that a book on a board is the only thing this district has ever built that a hostile stranger can use without asking.
