# Chapter 113: The Last Day This District Owns

A sheet went up on eleven walls and on three toll boards and on the board of eleven in the yard of Lot 17 on the morning of the twenty-fifth of August, and it cost twelve coppers, and the man who printed it is the man who sells nothing and has a use, and he charged twelve and not four and gave the reason in nine seconds when he was asked.

"**Because it has two things on it and I have never printed two things on one sheet in this district before, and one of them is a thing ending and the other one is a thing nobody is allowed to end.**"

The two things are on it in the same type as everything else, and there is a heading over the whole of it, and the heading is four words long and the second word is a day.

> **THE TWENTY-NINTH OF AUGUST**

> **ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF AUGUST THE TERM OF THE BOUNDARY AT THE WEST END ENDS, AND IT IS ASKED FROM TWO ROOMS, AND NEITHER ROOM HAS AGREED TO END ANYTHING. ON THE SAME DAY SOMEBODY WILL HAVE TO DECIDE WHETHER THE IRON COMES OUT OF THE BRACKET. NOBODY IN ALDER REACH IS ALLOWED TO DECIDE IT. THE DATE IS THE ONLY THING ON THIS SHEET THAT ANYBODY CAN ARGUUE WITH.**

The last line is on the sheet because a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said on the second of this month that the date is the only thing on a sheet anybody can argue with, and the man who prints it said he had that line in his own type already and had not known that anybody had noticed it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the district has now spent three twelves on paper in two months, and that the three are three and are not a rate, and the reason she gives is that a month is a month and a rate is not a rate until somebody who is owed it says so.

**THE DISTRICT HAS THREE TWELVES IN TWO MONTHS AND A FOUR COPPERS FOUR TIMES OVER, AND NONE OF THE FIVE FIGURES IS A COLUMN, AND A CHART THAT ADDED THEM WOULD BE A FINDING AND WOULD BE WRONG.**

A sign-writer of sixty-one painted the four words of the heading at the height of a hand on two of the boards because there was no money for letters, and the registrar entered that a notice with a heading nobody can see is a notice for four days, and that the man who keeps the fourth board at the ninth place has a cloth and wipes the bottom line when it is wet, and that both of those facts are four days old again.

---

And on the afternoon of the twenty-fifth a boy of seventeen sat in a counting room over a chandler's shop and put a rule under a line of five dates, and the line is the line in his index, and not one of the five has anybody's name against it, and he has been asked three times in nine weeks whether that is an oversight and has said three times that it is not.

The five are the twenty-ninth of April asked, the twenty-fifth of May, the twenty-ninth of May, the twenty-ninth of June, and the twenty-ninth of this month. He ruled the line in four seconds and then it took him half an hour to say what the rule was for.

"**This is the last line in this book that has a day at the end of it. On the twenty-ninth this month the fifth one comes, and after that there is nothing to put under a rule, and in four years a stranger will take this index cold and see five dates and a rule, and he will think five things were planned, and they were not planned, they were the only five days anybody in this district ever had.**"

"And what happens on the thirtieth."

"**Nothing happens on the thirtieth, and that is the first sentence about a day anybody has written in nine weeks that I have not had to argue anybody out of.**"

Nobody in that room argued with him, and a clerk of nineteen years asked him whether he wanted the entry in his own hand and he said yes, and the reason he gave is thirty-five words long and is the third time in nine weeks he has given a reason in that shape.

"**Because it is about the line and the line is mine, and the man who mends fencing was right that a column is for things that go the same way and these five do not.**"

The entry of the twenty-fifth of August says that a boy of seventeen has written the end of a line of five dates under a rule in his own hand, and that nobody voted on it, and that the finding is that **a district that runs out of dates is in the same position as a district that never had any, and it takes about nine weeks to tell those two apart, and nobody was looking.**

---

The argument about who was allowed to decide what happened at the bracket on the twenty-ninth of August took two hours and it was in the yard of Lot 17 in front of forty people on the twenty-sixth, and there were three positions and none of them had standing, and that is the whole of the finding.

The first position is that the iron comes out.

The second is that it stays.

The third was put by a man of thirty-one who is a signalman of the district's own crew, and it had not been put before, and it is the reason the twenty-sixth of August is the shape it is.

"**There is no committee. There is no charter. There is no week with anybody in it. I have asked four people in two days whether there is a committee and three of them said no and the fourth said there used to be, and what there used to be was a charter that ended at midnight on the twenty-eighth of last month, and the charter is the only piece of paper in this district that ever said who was allowed to decide anything, and it said it for a month and it is gone.**"

Nobody in that yard had an answer to it, and nineteen people looked at a sheet on a wall that said a thing nobody is allowed to decide is decided by a date, and that sheet is four coppers and went up on the second of this month.

A woman of fifty-one who keeps the only pair on that line was in the yard and she said the thing that is now the entry of the twenty-sixth of August, and it is twenty-four words long, and she said it in the doorway of a shed and did not come in.

"**A thing nobody is allowed to decide is decided by a date, and a date decides it, and that is all it ever did.**"

And a man of thirty-four who mends fencing, who is not a bearer and has never been a bearer, said the other half, and it is in the minute.

"**You have spent nine weeks learning how to ask a person whether they will do a job, and four of you have been in this yard for a hundred and twenty days, and there is nobody in this yard who is allowed to ask anybody to take the iron out of that bracket, and that is not a failure of the asking. That is the asking working.**"

Nobody voted on that either, and the minute says nobody voted on that either, and says that a district which has spent nine weeks learning to ask a person has arrived at a day on which it has nobody to ask, and that this is the first time the two things have been in the same sentence.

**THE FINDING OF THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF AUGUST IS THAT THE ONLY DOCUMENT IN THIS DISTRICT WHICH EVER SAID WHO WAS ALLOWED TO DECIDE ANYTHING ENDED AT MIDNIGHT ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF LAST MONTH, AND THAT NOBODY HAS REPLACED IT, AND THAT THE TWENTY-NINTH OF AUGUST IS IN THREE DAYS.**

---

And then nobody went east, and that is the other half of the twenty-sixth, and the reason is in the minute in the man who drives salt's own words and it is seventy-three words long.

"**I have been ready since the third of this month. I am not going thirty miles east in the four days before the day this district has been waiting nine weeks for, because if the river is on the planks on the second of September I lose a season of salt, and I would rather lose a season of salt than have a party of nine miss the one day they came for.**"

Nobody argued with him and the minute says nobody argued with him, and says that the party of nine had been told to be ready on the first of September since the twenty-third and had been moved by nobody.

The twenty-ninth was then composed, and it was composed in two rooms for the boundary and in nineteen people for the bracket, and the two of them were not joined, and the clerk who wrote both down entered that she had been told to write them separately and did not know why and did not ask.

The boundary asking is asked from a counting room over a chandler's shop and from a yard on the tide, and the woman of forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide has lent the yard again and is not thanked and insists that it be entered as not a security, and that clause is now on six documents in nine weeks.

The asker at the counting room is a woman of sixty with a stick who is on no roll and has been in four rooms in nine weeks and has corrected a document by saying what she needed in it five times, and she was asked on the twenty-seventh whether she would do it and she said yes in four seconds and asked for the asking not to be put in her name.

The asker at the yard on the tide is a woman of fifty-one who keeps the only pair, and she is not the reader and is not a keeper of a board and is not on a committee, and the minute says she read the asking out in the ordinary voice and read it twice because the rule of a counter is that a document goes out twice, and that there is no counter on the tide.

**AND THE TWENTY-NINTH OF AUGUST WAS COMPOSED BY FOUR PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT ADRIAN VALE, AND IT IS THE SECOND TIME IN NINE WEEKS THAT A DAY HAS BEEN COMPOSED BY SOMEBODY ELSE, AND THE FIRST TIME WAS THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JULY, AND NEITHER OF THE TWO COMPOSITIONS WAS VOTED ON BY ANYBODY.**


---

The asking itself was four lines long and it took twenty-six minutes to compose, and nine of the minutes were the sixth question, and the clerk of nineteen years has said that the twenty-six is the shortest this district has run and that the reason is that the room did not have to find out anything.

> **THE TERM OF THE BOUNDARY AT THE WEST END ENDS ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF AUGUST. IT IS ASKED TWICE, ONCE FROM A COUNTING ROOM AND ONCE FROM A YARD ON THE TIDE. THE QUESTION IS THE SAME IN BOTH ROOMS. WHAT IS THE JOB, HOW LONG IS IT, WHAT DOES IT COST, WHAT HAPPENS TO THE PERSON WHO DOES IT, AND WHO IS STANDING IN THE ROOM.**

And the sixth question, which has no name and belongs to the clerk of nineteen years and is not on the wall, was read out to about nineteen people in the counting room on the afternoon of the twenty-eighth and it took nine minutes, and what it added on that afternoon is entered separately because it is not part of the asking.

"**One more thing before you say it again, and it is not mine. If you say yes, then the next person who comes up this bank in five weeks is going to stand in a room with two people who have already said yes, and one of them is going to be tired of saying yes, and nobody will be able to tell which one. So a yes is not a thing you say to a room. A yes is a thing you say to the day, and only on the day, and if you cannot say it on the day then the answer is not asked and not asked is not no.**"

Nobody argued with her and a woman of fifty who is the water at the ninth place, and who is fifty and has stood in her own doorway for eleven years, was in that room and said one sentence afterwards in four seconds.

"**I have been asked that question six times and I have given the same two sentences six times and I would like it entered that on the sixth time I noticed I was not listening to the first half of it, and that this is a fact about me and not about the question.**"

The clerk entered that and entered it in the minute and not in the book, because the minute is for the district and the book is for a stranger, and the reason she gives is thirty-four words long and it is about the difference between the two.

"**A stranger in four years wants to know what this district was sure of. He does not want to know what a woman of fifty was doing with her attention on the sixth time.**"

---

And on the evening of the twenty-seventh, in a lane, a man of thirty-four who mends fencing asked a man of thirty-eight whether he would decide the bracket on the twenty-ninth, and he said no before the question had finished, and the reason is in the minute in his own words and is seventy words long.

"**I am not one of the four bearers, I am not a keeper of a board and I am not on the committee, and the line on page one saying I am not one of the four bearers is still true of me in the ninth week, and I would like that line read out on the twenty-ninth in a room so that a stranger knows it was not forgotten.**"

The man who mends fencing read the line out. Nobody argued with him and the minute says nobody argued with him, and says that a district which has found a man who can do a thing and cannot be the man who does it has found the same thing four times in nine weeks and has entered it four times.

"Can you advise."

"**Then I would advise and I would write it down as advice and not as a decision, and four years from now a stranger would read the advice and the decision would be missing, and that is the correct condition for advice to be in.**"

"You have said that before."

"**I have said it before and I am saying it again and I would like the entry to say that I have said it twice and that a man who says the same true thing twice is not a man with two ideas.**"

And a man of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in that lane and had a tin under a bed he does not have, and he said the thing that he came to say in nine seconds and the minute records that it was the fourth time in nine weeks he had said a thing nobody had asked him for.

"**I have got a hundred and eighty-three coppers under a bed that nobody is owed, and on the sixteenth of this month a man at a counter agreed to read the number out loud once in every quarter until it moves, and the day it is on the twenty-ninth is not the twenty-ninth of September. And I have watched four people in nine weeks agree to stand in a room on a day, and I have watched a man be given a day with a number on it, and I want to say one thing about the twenty-ninth and then I am going home.**"

Nobody asked him what it was.

"**It is that all four of them asked, and nobody has asked the iron, and the iron does not care, and that is going to be the finding on the twenty-ninth and I would like it written down on the twenty-eighth so that it is not a surprise to anybody.**"

And the clerk of nineteen years asked him whether he wanted that written as a finding, and he said no, and gave the reason in forty words, and the reason is the reason he gave on the ninth of this month when he asked for his own sentence about an office to be struck.

"**I have been wrong about money three times and I do not know what a bracket is, and a thing I do not know about is not a finding and it is in the column for things that are not findings and not in the book.**"

And the registrar entered it in the column for things that are not findings, which has six things in it in nine weeks and has seven after the twenty-eighth of August, and she did not read it out and did not strike it, and the reason she gives is that she is not going to read a thing out in a room and then have a day turn out to be a way of doing it.

---

A stranger took a copy of the notice off the board of eleven in the yard of Lot 17 on the afternoon of the twenty-seventh, in a minute, and did not ask anybody anything, and nobody stopped him, and the minute says nobody stopped him and says that the notice has been taken cold four times in nine weeks by four different people and that three of the four came back.

He came back on the twenty-eighth and asked one question at the counter, and it is seven words long.

"Who is allowed to take it out."

"**Nobody. That is the sentence at the bottom and it is on the sheet in the same type as everything else, and a stranger who has the sheet does not have a different answer from a person who has been in the yard.**"

"And who decided that."

"**A date did, on the second of this month, and the date is in ten days, and I am not the date and neither are you and neither is the man whose name is not on anything.**"

Nobody at that counter said anything else about it and the entry of the twenty-eighth of August says that a stranger has asked this district the same question twice in nine weeks, that he asked it about a piece of paper the first time and a piece of iron the second, and that neither question has been answered in a room, and that the sheet he is holding is the reason a stranger can ask it.


And on the twenty-eighth of August, at about four in the afternoon, the man of thirty-eight went up the bank for the fifth time in a month and looked at the column on the fourth board and did not say anything about it for half an hour, and the keeper of the board did not ask him, and the minute says the silence was half an hour and that nobody broke it.

"**You have written another man's question in your column and it is still there and it is the twenty-seventh sentence and it has been there for a fortnight.**"

"**It is in the column because a column is where I put things nobody has answered, and I did not put it in the column, I put it on the bottom line, and there is a difference, and the difference is four inches.**"

He pointed at it. The question is in chalk on the bottom line of a nine-foot board at the end of a platform, at the height of a man's knee, and it is the only sentence on that board that a person has to stoop to read, and he said that in four seconds and the reason is in the minute.

"**A thing you can read standing up is a notice. A thing you have to stoop for is somebody's question, and a notice is a thing this district publishes about itself and a question is a thing this district cannot answer, and the two of them are not the same instrument and there is no register of boards.**"

"And you have got five of them now."

"**I have got one of them. There are five and I keep one and the other four are kept by four people who are not me, and one of the four is kept by a clerk in the hours the counter is shut and one of the four is kept by a man who is about thirty-one and one of the four is a paperer who comes up the bank once a month for her own trade, and a man of sixty-one painted the name of the last one on at two in the morning because there was no money for letters.**"

Nobody argued and the entry of the twenty-eighth of August says that five toll boards now exist in this district, that one of the five is at the place the toll landed at and four of the five are somewhere else, that none of the five is on a toll, and that the district has a finding about that which is nine weeks old and has been extended three times by people who were not in the room when it was made.

**THE FIFTH INSTRUMENT IS STILL UNNAMED AND STILL UNOWNED, AND A THING THAT LANDS ON A PLACE IS A PLACE, A DAY, AND EITHER AN AMOUNT OR THE FACT THAT THE PLACE CANNOT SAY ONE, AND THE FIFTH LINE IN THAT COLUMN IS RULED AND EMPTY, AND THE KEEPER SAYS THE FIFTH LINE IS GOING TO STAY EMPTY AND HE IS NOT THE FIFTH DAY AND HE HAS SAID SO IN PUBLIC TWICE.**

And then the keeper of the board said the other half of it, and it is eighty-four words long and it is about a boy and not about a board, and the boy was not in the room and has not been asked it.

"**There is a boy at the fourth place who has been standing in the same spot every day since the ninth of last month writing nothing down, and I found out about that in a yard a fortnight ago from three people who had not spoken to each other, and nobody has asked him for a year and nobody has paid him, and I am not going to be the fourth person in nine weeks to describe that instrument and join none of them.**"

The clerk of nineteen years wrote that down and did not join it, and the entry says that a fourth description of one thing in one week is not a finding, and that the fourth of the four is four hundred yards from a river and has not been asked anything, and that the three descriptions and the fourth are in four places and are not in one book and are not to be.

The train on the siding had stood a hundred and ninety-five days on the twenty-fifth of August and a hundred and ninety-six on the twenty-sixth and a hundred and ninety-eight on the twenty-eighth, and the boy of seventeen wrote all three and struck none of the old ones, and the fifth of the five dates in his index is the day after the one this district is going to.
