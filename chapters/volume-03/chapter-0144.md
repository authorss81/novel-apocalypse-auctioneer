# Chapter 144: The First of December

---

A man of fifty-six read the interval out loud in a lane on the thirtieth of November at about eleven in the morning and a clerk of nineteen years wrote it down and put the sum under it for the sixth time in four weeks, **and the figure is a hundred and twenty-one days and seventeen weeks and two days between the thirtieth of November and the thirty-first of March, and the thirtieth of November is the last day anybody can hand in a notice and is not a day anybody will.**

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in that lane and heard it and said that the same interval is three months and two days, and a man of fifty-six entered that she said three months and two days and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that too and that the two are in one lane and are not joined and that the four months she said on the twenty-eighth of November is in a different minute.

It was not argued with and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a district has three figures for one interval now, a hundred and twenty-three days, a hundred and twenty-one days and a hundred and fifty-one days from the first of November, **and that all three are correct, and that two of them stop at the thirty-first of March and the third goes on to the first of April, and that a person who has been handed a figure for the wrong interval has been handed a figure and not the interval, and that nobody in this district has ever been handed the right one and been told which it was.**

And on the thirtieth of november A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THE INTERVAL BETWEEN THE THIRTIETH OF NOVEMBER AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH AS A HUNDRED AND TWENTY-ONE DAYS AND SEVENTEEN WEEKS AND TWO DAYS, AND A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT WHO CARRIES WATER AND DOES NOT READ FIGURES CALLED IT THREE MONTHS AND TWO DAYS, AND THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-TWO WEEKS IS IN A MINUTE OF THE THIRTIETH OF OCTOBER AND IS NOT JOINED TO EITHER.

---

The month turned on the first of December and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it had turned in a book and did not enter a finding about it, **and entered that the new month is December, that last month is November and that the month before last is October, and that the board outside the room carried the twenty-fourth of December on it in a hand that anybody could read and that the room has twenty-three days.**

A man of fifty-six read out what the month of November had held, in a yard, and it took about nine minutes, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is a list and not a finding, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it and entered that a list of a month is not a finding and that the entry of the twenty-second of November has no finding in it and that the entry of the twenty-fourth of November has none either and that the count of entries with no finding in them since the eighth of September is four.

Nobody took it up and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the month held a board with three days on it and then four, a third form of a notice, a fourth day written in a hand anybody can read, a cart of salt, and a stranger's fifth copy of a page, **and that four of those five are about instruments and one of them is about a stranger and that the two are two things and that he is not going to join them in a yard.**

A man of fifty-six said that the finding of the month is the fifth line that was refused, and that a room refusing to put a fifth line on a form is a room that has decided what a form is for, and that in eleven weeks this is the first time a room has refused to add something to a document and that the five lines it refused were refused in about four minutes and that the count of the four minutes is not a column and is not in one.

**AND THE MONTH TURNED ON THE FIRST OF DECEMBER AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THE NEW MONTH AS DECEMBER AND LAST MONTH AS NOVEMBER AND THE MONTH BEFORE LAST AS OCTOBER, AND THE BOARD OUTSIDE THE ROOM CARRIED THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF DECEMBER ON IT IN A HAND ANYBODY COULD READ, AND THE ROOM HAS TWENTY-THREE DAYS.**

---

A man of fifty-six asked on the first of December, in the five things, whether the man the figure of twenty-one years is against would read the third channel so that the charter could say on its face what the toll of it is, and the answer took him four seconds and it was no.

The reason is in the minute in his own words and it is forty-seven words, **and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got forty-seven both times and did not say so and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got forty-seven and said it out loud and then said it again.**

"**A woman of thirty-one told me in September what the toll of it is and a man of fifty-two told me in the fourth month of this year and neither of them was a reading. I would be paying a year to be told by a thing.**"

Nobody took that up and a man of fifty-six said that this is the first refusal in nine that was about his own convenience and that the ninth was about somebody else's cost, and that the two are not the same instrument and neither of them is cowardice, and that the count of refusals to read is nine and does not move today because a man who says no and gives a reason that costs him nothing is not the same as a man who says no nine times.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the count does not move and that no tenth entry was made, **and that this is the first time in eleven weeks that a refusal was given in a room and not entered, and that the reason is on the face of the entry and that the entry says a man gave a reason and a clerk did not write it down twice.**

Nobody asked her why and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

And on the first of december HE WAS ASKED, IN THE FIVE THINGS, TO READ THE THIRD CHANNEL SO THAT THE CHARTER COULD SAY ON ITS FACE WHAT THE TOLL OF IT IS, AND HE SAID NO IN FOUR SECONDS, AND THE REASON IS THAT HE KNOWS WHAT THE TOLL OF IT IS AND WAS TOLD IT IN SEPTEMBER BY A WOMAN OF THIRTY-ONE AND IN THE FOURTH MONTH OF THIS YEAR BY A MAN OF FIFTY-TWO AND NEITHER OF THEM WAS A READING, AND THE COUNT OF REFUSALS TO READ IS NINE AND DID NOT MOVE.

---

A man of fifty-six counted the days out loud in that room on the first of December and there are twenty-three of them between the first and the twenty-fourth, **and a woman of thirty-eight who has been in the second channel four years said that twenty-three days is not a season and that a season is what she is standing in, and that nobody is going to close a charter in twenty-three days that is about a season of about five months.**

Nobody argued with her and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that the charter is not about the season and is about the next one, and that the next one starts on the first of April and has not been certified and that a charter that says what a person may do in a season that has not been certified is a charter about a season and not about twenty-three days.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two are two things and that the woman of thirty-eight was asked in the five things whether she will be in the rotation and said that she will be asked in December and not on the first of December, **and that she was not asked about the four seasons and was not asked about the eleventh year and was not asked about the four hundred coppers and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.**

The six of them were named again in that room and the order has not changed since the twenty-seventh of October, and the man of fifty-six entered that the order of six people is a thing a clerk can copy and that the order of six people in a year is a thing a clerk cannot, and that nobody in the room has asked what the order will be in April and that he is not going to ask.

**AND ON THE FIRST OF DECEMBER A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX COUNTED THE DAYS OUT LOUD AND THERE ARE TWENTY-THREE OF THEM BETWEEN THE FIRST AND THE TWENTY-FOURTH, AND A WOMAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT WHO HAS BEEN IN THE SECOND CHANNEL FOUR YEARS SAID THAT TWENTY-THREE DAYS IS NOT A SEASON, AND THAT A SEASON IS WHAT SHE IS STANDING IN, AND THAT NOBODY IS GOING TO CLOSE A CHARTER IN TWENTY-THREE DAYS THAT IS ABOUT A SEASON OF ABOUT FIVE MONTHS.**

---

Selik Marne was not asked on the first of December and was in the yard at about eleven in the morning and said the two words he says on published days, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he said them and was not asked about them, **and that the span at the hut he works at is still shut and has not been opened in eleven weeks and that the seventeen people on the far bank are still on the far bank and that the two facts are in one line and are not joined.**

He was not on a bank asking and nobody put him on one, and a man of fifty-six said that out loud in a yard, and Selik Marne said that it is not enough and that he has said so on published days and does not stop, and that a man who is not on an asking can still be in a yard, and that a clerk entered the second half of that and the clerk entered that she had wanted to leave it out and did not.

a clerk of nineteen years entered that the offer of a guarantee house is still neither accepted nor refused and that nothing about it was said on the first of December, **and that the six of the six are a man who takes a price off the table in four minutes, a first refusal on the person who stands in the frame, sixteen bearer columns with no name in them and a city she would not name, and that none of the four is in any book in the west bank and that the two are two things.**

**AND ON THE FIRST OF DECEMBER SELIK MARNE WAS NOT ASKED AND WAS IN A YARD AND SAID THE TWO WORDS HE SAYS ON PUBLISHED DAYS AND WAS NOT ASKED ABOUT THEM, AND THE SPAN IS STILL SHUT AND THE SEVENTEEN PEOPLE ON THE FAR BANK ARE STILL ON THE FAR BANK, AND HE IS NOT ON A BANK ASKING AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT SHE HAD WANTED TO LEAVE A SENTENCE OUT AND DID NOT.**

---

A man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was in that yard on the first of December and asked a question and it is the only question he asked, and it went into the minute in his own words and it is thirty-one words, and a clerk of nineteen years counted it once and got thirty-one and did not count it again because a man of about nineteen told her not to on the first of November and she has not done it since.

"**A board that works, is that a form, because a form is a thing people use and not a thing people read, and two people have used one in eight days.**"

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper answered it in twenty-three words, **and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got twenty-three both times, and a man of fifty-six said that a figure of twenty is a figure of a length and is not a figure of anything else, and that the count of nineteen belongs to a sheet in a different room and the two are two things.**

"**A form is a thing with a place to write on it. A board is a thing with a place to stand at.**"

It was not argued with and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a board is the only instrument in this district with no heading on it and that the four hundred printed sheets have the office's name on them and that a board has nothing, and that a thing with nothing on it that four people use is not a form and is not the fifth instrument this district does not have, and that the count of the five did not move today and is five.

**AND ON THE FIRST OF DECEMBER A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-THREE WHO SELLS NOTHING AND COPIES FOR NOTHING ASKED WHETHER A BOARD THAT WORKS IS A FORM, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WHO DEALS IN SECOND-HAND PAPER ANSWERED THAT A FORM IS A THING WITH A PLACE ON IT TO WRITE AND A BOARD IS A THING WITH A PLACE TO STAND, AND THE COUNT OF INSTRUMENTS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE IS FIVE AND DID NOT MOVE.**

---

A woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years was in that yard on the first of December and was not asked for anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that she is not an officer of anything and was not asked to be one and has not signed the four things and is not going to in December.**

She said one thing to nobody in particular and a man of fifty-six heard it and entered it and it is twenty-seven words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-seven and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had wanted to put the count in the entry and had not and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was.

"**The first of April is not a day I am looking forward to. It is a day about four hundred miles of water is looking forward to.**"

It was not argued with and a man of fifty-six said that the sentence is the only one anybody has said in eleven weeks about the first of April that was not about the district, **and that a person who has done it eleven times is a person the district has asked nothing for nine weeks and that the entry says so and that the entry is the only place it is written down.**

Nobody asked her about it, and nobody asked the clerk who entered it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked her and that nobody asked her about the fact that she had entered it, and that this is the ninth time in eleven weeks nobody has, and that the count of a clerk of nineteen years being asked nothing is not the count of the woman of forty-five and is not the count of the registrar's refusals and is not in any column and is not going to be.

---

Ivo Stenn was in that room and read the ledger out, and the ledger is one for April, none for May, one for the twenty-ninth of June, one for the sixth of September, one for the ninth of October and one for the third of November, **and beside it on a board a boy of seventeen has six dates on one line and a rule drawn under the sixth and no seventh date, and a man of about nineteen asked him in November what the rule was for twice and was told it was a rule.**

Then he read his own separate sheet out after it, and it is nineteen words, and it is the same nineteen words it has been in every batch.

> **THE INTERVALS GO UP ON USE AND I WILL NOT DEFEND IT AND I WILL NOT CALL IT ANYTHING ELSE.**

Nobody asked him what the intervals were. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the intervals have gone up on use for the fifth time in eleven weeks and that he will not defend it and will not call it anything else, and that the sheet as printed contains twenty words and has contained twenty words since the second batch of this volume and that it has been called nineteen words in every book in this district since the day he wrote it, and that a clerk of nineteen years has not been asked to correct the count and has not corrected it and is not going to.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the two are two things and that a sheet and a count of a sheet are two instruments, **and that a man who writes a sentence and a book that calls it a different length are both telling the truth about a thing nobody in this district can measure, and that the departure is unspent and there is still no date in the ledger.**

**AND ON THE FIRST OF DECEMBER IVO STENN READ OUT SIX DATES AND THEN READ OUT HIS OWN SEPARATE SHEET, WHICH IS NINETEEN WORDS AND IS TWENTY AS PRINTED, AND THE INTERVALS HAVE GONE UP ON USE FOR THE FIFTH TIME IN ELEVEN WEEKS, AND THE DEPARTURE IS UNSPENT AND THERE IS STILL NO DATE IN THE LEDGER FOR THE NEXT READING.**

---

A woman of about thirty-one who has been in the third channel since the first of April wrote on a board on the first of December, in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, that she asked for her own name to go in the ledger on the second of October and it went in and nobody has asked her since whether she is standing in the third channel, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read it over her shoulder and did not stop her and told her afterwards what a board is.

It worked, and that is the second time in eight days a thing written on a board has worked, **and a man of fifty-six said that the first time was a man losing four days on a road and this time is a woman who is in a channel, and that the two are two things and that a board is the only instrument in this district that a person can use without asking.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a line which was already full was written on again, and that the count of lines is six and did not move, and that the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty and was not written on and that nobody was asked about it, and that the two uses of a board in eight days are in two minutes and that there is no column for a rate of use and that a column is a thing a person is paid for.

Nobody took it up and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the instrument is unnamed and unowned and has five boards and six lines full and a seventh ruled and empty, **and that the two uses in eight days are the reason the count has been made four times in three weeks and has given four numbers, and that none of the four numbers is a finding and that the woman who made the last one is not paid.**

And on the first of december A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO HAS BEEN IN THE THIRD CHANNEL SINCE THE FIRST OF APRIL WROTE ON A BOARD THAT SHE ASKED FOR HER OWN NAME TO GO IN THE LEDGER ON THE SECOND OF OCTOBER AND THAT NOBODY HAS ASKED HER SINCE WHETHER SHE IS STANDING IN THE THIRD CHANNEL, AND IT WORKED, AND THE COUNT OF LINES IS SIX AND DID NOT MOVE AND THE SEVENTH LINE AT THE NINTH PLACE IS RULED AND EMPTY.

---

The train on the siding had stood two hundred and ninety-two days on the thirtieth of November and two hundred and ninety-three on the first of December, **and a man of seventeen wrote both figures in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and the sum was put under the figure in a minute for the seventh time in four weeks and every figure is built from the eleventh of February.**

The room let it stand and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said again that a man who does a sum in front of strangers is not checking a number and is teaching it, and that this district has been teaching numbers in lanes for eleven weeks and calling it counting, and that a person who has been taught a figure by a lane is a person who will say it out loud in four years in a room they have not been in.

Nobody asked him about the four years. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he had said the four years out loud and had not been asked why and that the not-asking is entered and the count is where it was, **and that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and the count of protected things is five and the boy of seventeen is in neither and neither moved.**

And on the thirtieth of november THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY-TWO DAYS AND ON THE FIRST OF DECEMBER TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY-THREE, AND SEVENTEEN DAYS REMAIN IN FEBRUARY AFTER THE ELEVENTH, THEN ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FOUR TO THE THIRTY-FIRST OF AUGUST, WHICH IS TWO HUNDRED AND ONE, THEN THIRTY FOR SEPTEMBER, THEN THIRTY-ONE FOR OCTOBER, THEN THIRTY FOR NOVEMBER, THEN ONE FOR THE FIRST OF DECEMBER.

AND THE ROOM HAS TWENTY-THREE DAYS **AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX ASKED IN A YARD ON THE FIRST OF DECEMBER WHAT THE MONTH OF NOVEMBER HELD AND READ OUT A LIST AND NOT A FINDING, AND THE LIST IS ON ELEVEN LINES OF A MINUTE AND THE ENTRY OF THE FIRST OF DECEMBER IS ELEVEN LINES LONG AND HAS A LIST IN IT AND NO FINDING.**

AND THE COUNT OF REFUSALS TO READ IS NINE AND THE COUNT OF PROTECTED THINGS IS FIVE AND THE COUNT OF CONDITIONS WITH NO END ON IT IS FOUR AND THE COUNT OF NOT-ASKINGS ABOUT THE WOMAN OF FORTY-FIVE IS FOUR WITH ONE STANDING REFUSAL AND NONE OF THE FOUR COUNTS MOVED IN THE MONTH OF NOVEMBER AND NONE HAS MOVED SINCE THE FIRST OF DECEMBER.
