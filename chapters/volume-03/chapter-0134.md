# Chapter 134: A Notice Withdraws a Season

---

The first withdrawal notice in this district was filled in on the thirtieth of October at about eleven in the morning by a man of twenty-six who has been in a bed of brine for a year and a half. **It took him about nine minutes and he did it himself, because a form is a thing people use and not a thing people read, and he had been told that by a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper.**

**It was filled in at a table in a room with two benches that face each other, with a clerk of nineteen years, a man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten, and the man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households, and about nine people who were not asked to leave and did not.**
---

The form itself was settled on the twenty-eighth and it is a single sheet. **It is in the minute in the hand of a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper, who wrote it out twice and would not sign it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who writes a form and will not sign it is a man who has been in this district eleven weeks.**

> **A PERSON WHO HAS STOOD IN A FRAME IN THIS SEASON MAY GIVE NOTICE ON ANY DAY BEFORE THE FIRST OF APRIL THAT THEY WILL NOT STAND IN THAT FRAME AGAIN.**
> >
> > **SEASON WITHDRAWN FROM: ______________**
> >
> > **FRAME: ______________**
> >
> > **DATE GIVEN: ______________**
> >
> > **THE NOTICE IS GOOD FROM THE DAY IT IS GIVEN. IT IS NOT A RELEASE AND IT IS NOT A CONSENT AND IT DOES NOT GIVE BACK A YEAR. NOBODY MAY ASK THE PERSON WHY.**

The line at the top is the whole of the argument and it took about four hours on the twenty-eighth and about nine minutes on the thirtieth **and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that four hours is one day and a half in this district and that nobody has ever been charged for the difference.**

A man of fifty-six said that the line could be left blank and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said no in thirty words, and the thirty words went in, and the reason came after in thirty.

"**A blank at the top of a form is not a space. It is a place for the next person to write, and the next person is always a stranger.**"



**The line at the top of the notice names the season.** It says the season of the first of October to the thirty-first of March, because that is the term the third channel was written for on the eighth of September and the term nobody has ever asked to change. A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that a notice with no season on it is a notice with nothing to withdraw from, and that the draft had gone through four versions on the twenty-sixth and had come out with a season on it.

**AND ON THE THIRTIETH OF OCTOBER THE FIRST WITHDRAWAL NOTICE IN THIS DISTRICT WAS FILLED IN BY A MAN OF TWENTY-SIX, AND IT NAMES A SEASON OF THE FIRST OF OCTOBER TO THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH, AND THE SEASON DID NOT START ON THE FIRST OF OCTOBER, AND NO CERTIFICATE HAS BEEN ISSUED FOR IT, AND THE ONLY SEASON HE HAS STOOD IN A FRAME FOR IS ONE OF TWO DAYS IN SEPTEMBER UNDER A CONSENT THAT WAS CERTIFIED TWICE AND IS IN A DRAWER THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST.**

---

A woman of thirty-eight who has been in the second channel four years was asked on the thirtieth whether she wanted one of these notices, and she said she would take one on the first of April and not before, and the clerk of nineteen years asked why and she gave the reason in forty-four words and the clerk counted them and got forty-four both times.

"**Give me that in the first week of April and I will hand it in the same morning. Give it to me now and I carry it twenty-two weeks and somebody in my house asks me about it every day of the twenty-two weeks.**"

A man of fifty-six entered that this is the second time in two days that a person in a frame has said the notice is twenty-two weeks too early, and that he had said a hundred and fifty-one days on the twenty-sixth and that the woman of thirty-eight had said twenty-two weeks on the thirtieth, and that the two figures are in two minutes and are not joined, and that the clause on the twenty-sixth does not carry a date for giving the notice, and that two people who are the people it is written for have now said so separately and neither of them was asked by the other.


---

The trouble began four minutes after the man of twenty-six had written his name, and it began because he asked a question nobody in the room had an answer to. **The question is in the minute in his own words and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down in her own hand and read it back and he said it was right.**

"**What am I withdrawing from.**"

Nobody answered for nine seconds and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nine seconds is the fourth-longest silence in the party's minute in eleven weeks and that the three longer ones are all the same man not finishing a sentence, and that this one is not a man not finishing a sentence and that she entered the two in one line and did not join them.

A man of fifty-six said that he was withdrawing from the season and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that he was withdrawing from the frame. A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a frame is a thing a person stands in and a season is a length of time, and that the notice has one line at the top of it and the line says a length of time, and that the two are not the same and the notice has only room for one of them.

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing then asked him **properly, in the six things, whether the notice as it stood was what he wanted, and he said yes, and the six things were done in the six things and were a proper asking and not a formality, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the asking was proper and that she had checked.**

Then he said the second half, and this is the whole of the failure, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirty-six words and the clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got thirty-six both times.

"**I said yes to the top line and nobody read me the bottom one and there is no season. I have been in that frame two days in September and a certificate is in a drawer.**"

Nobody argued with him and the room went quiet. A man of fifty-six said out loud that the season of the first of October to the thirty-first of March had not started, and that a bed of brine in the third channel had been full since the first of October and had nobody in it, and that a woman of thirty-eight had come to work and was not in it, and that the loss had been entered as two days and as a fortnight.

"**Then I am not withdrawing from anything and the sheet is a lie about me and somebody is going to read it and think I was told about a season.**"

A clerk of nineteen years asked him what he wanted done with the sheet and he said that he did not know and that he would like to go and stand in a bed of brine for a year and a half and not have a season put on it, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that sentence and that it is the second time in eleven weeks a person in this district has said a thing aloud that nobody could answer and that neither of the two was answered. She entered that she had wanted to write under it that the man asked for the sheet back and that he did not ask for the sheet

Back, and that a clerk of nineteen years is not supposed to keep a sentence a person did not ask her to keep, and that she has now got three of them and that three is the number at which a clerk begins to be an instrument.

---

Then a man of fifty-two who came out of the fourth channel in the fourth month of this year after four years was asked, in the six things, whether he wanted a notice, and he said no in six words, and the six words are in the minute and the room did not argue with them and about four people found them harder than the ones they expected.

"**I am not in a frame.**"

He gave the reason afterwards in forty words. A clerk of nineteen years wrote them down and entered that he was asked and answered, and that he was not asked about the fourth channel and was not asked about the four years and was not asked about the fourth month of this year.

"**A notice is a thing a person in a frame is handed. I have been out since the fourth month and nobody has asked me what I am going to do, and now they have, and the answer is no.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it and entered that this is the first time in eleven weeks that a person has been asked whether they want a right and has said no, and that a person who says no to a right has not withdrawn and has not refused a season, and that there is no column for either of those on the page and that this is a third kind of thing.

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that not asked is a column and a person who has been asked and answered is not not asked, and that the two are not the same, and that the difference is a person who was asked and answered, and that this district has had a column for that since the second of October and it is the sixth one, and that the sixth one is the only one nobody can fill.


---

The notice was read out at the ward market counter on the afternoon of the thirtieth, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and a reader who is about fifty and was a signal clerk before her shoulder went read it out twice and got it the same both times, and about nine people were at the counter and one of them was a man of thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper and who had not been asked to come.

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing out loud at about half past three in the afternoon **and it went into the counter's minute and it is thirty-six words and the reader wrote them down herself, which she has not done in nine years.**

"**A stranger reads a notice with a man's name and a date on it and reads a man who was told about a season. The guild form has twenty-nine columns and one of them is consent.**"

A clerk of nineteen years who was at the counter asked whether a notice was a consent. **A man of thirty-four said no and then said the other half in nine seconds, and both halves are in the minute.**

"**A notice is a notice. A notice with a person's name and a date on it and the word season in between them is a shape a consent fits inside, and a guild form does not ask what shape a thing is.**"

The reader at the counter said that she had read out four hundred forms in nine years and had never once been asked what a form was for. **The difference between a consent and a notice is on the form somewhere and not in the reader, and a person who cannot tell the difference in a room is not going to be told the difference by a piece of paper.**

**AND ON THE THIRTIETH OF OCTOBER A NOTICE OF WITHDRAWAL WAS READ OUT AT THE WARD MARKET COUNTER AND READ OUT TWICE, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID THAT A STRANGER READS A NOTICE WITH A MAN'S NAME AND A DATE ON IT AND READS A MAN WHO WAS TOLD ABOUT A SEASON, AND THAT THE GUILD FORM HAS TWENTY-NINE COLUMNS AND ONE OF THEM IS CONSENT, AND THAT A NOTICE WITH A NAME AND A DATE AND THE WORD SEASON IN BETWEEN IS A SHAPE A CONSENT FITS INSIDE.**

---

The rest of the afternoon of the thirtieth was the room trying three other top lines, and all three were written on the sheet and all three were struck by the man of twenty-six himself, and a clerk of nineteen years kept the four versions and entered that a form in this district now has four versions of its own top line and that three of them were written by a man who cannot remember a year and knows that he cannot.

The first of the other three said THE FRAME OF THE THIRD CHANNEL. The second said THE BED OF BRINE and the third said WHATEVER FRAME THE PERSON IS IN WHEN THE NOTICE IS GIVEN, and the man of twenty-six struck that one himself in about four seconds, and the reason he gave is in the minute in his own words and is twenty-seven words.

"**That is a notice a stranger can use against me in four years. I have seen a bed of brine and I will not see it again.**"

**AND ON THE THIRTIETH OF OCTOBER THREE OTHER TOP LINES WERE WRITTEN ON A WITHDRAWAL NOTICE AND ALL THREE WERE STRUCK BY THE MAN THE NOTICE WAS FOR, AND THE THIRD SAID WHATEVER FRAME THE PERSON IS IN WHEN THE NOTICE IS GIVEN, AND HE STRUCK IT IN FOUR SECONDS AND SAID THAT A NOTICE A STRANGER CAN USE AGAINST HIM IN FOUR YEARS IS NOT A NOTICE, IT IS A THING HE HAS NOT SEEN.**


---

The notice was withdrawn on the thirty-first of October and nobody had taken a copy of it, and that was the whole of why it could be. **A clerk of nineteen years said so out loud in a room and entered that she had held it, and that a clerk of nineteen years is not supposed to hold a document, and that she had done it, and that not doing a thing is also a thing she does and that doing a thing she is not supposed to do is also a thing she does.**

The entry of the thirty-first carries a date and a name and a reason and it is the second time in eleven weeks that this district has written all four down about a thing that stopped, and the first time was a sheet nailed to a stake at the ninth mile, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that the two are not joined.

"This one is not a loam sill and that one is not a form with no column, and I would like it entered that the first one was about a channel and this one is about a person and a notice, and that the only thing they have in common is that somebody wrote down the day and was not asked to and did not stop."

AND THE FINDING OF THE THIRTY-FIRST OF OCTOBER IS THAT THE FIRST USE IN THIS DISTRICT OF A RIGHT TO WITHDRAW WITHDREW A MAN OF TWENTY-SIX FROM A SEASON THAT NEVER STARTED **AND THAT THE YEAR AND A HALF THAT WENT OUT OF HIM IS ON A LINE OF ITS OWN IN A PARTY'S MINUTE AND IS NOT IN THE LEDGER, AND THAT THE NOTICE DOES NOT TOUCH IT, AND THAT THE NOTICE IS GOOD WORK AND IT IS WORTH NOTHING.**

---

The entry went into the ward market's own book at about ten in the morning on the thirty-first of October and it is eleven lines long and it is the second-shortest entry the book has carried since the eighth of March, and it was read out twice at the counter by a reader who is about fifty and who asked for it to be noticed that it is in the book.

> **THE THIRTY-FIRST OF OCTOBER. A NOTICE OF WITHDRAWAL WAS FILLED IN ON THE THIRTIETH BY A MAN OF TWENTY-SIX WHO HAS BEEN IN A BED OF BRINE FOR A YEAR AND A HALF. THE NOTICE NAMED A SEASON OF THE FIRST OF OCTOBER TO THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH. THAT SEASON DID NOT START. NO CERTIFICATE WAS ISSUED FOR IT. THE NOTICE WAS WITHDRAWN THE SAME DAY IT WAS READ OUT. THE YEAR AND A HALF IS ON A LINE OF ITS OWN IN A PARTY'S MINUTE AND IS NOT IN THE LEDGER AND IS NOT ADDED TO ANYTHING. THE MAN IS NOT NAMED IN THIS ENTRY.**

A man of fifty-six wanted his name in the last line and the clerk of nineteen years said no in four words. **The four words are in the counter's minute and the reason came after in twenty words, and she read it back and nobody in the room said she was wrong.**

"**A man who withdrew from a season that never started is a sentence a stranger can repeat for nine years.**"

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that the same reason was given in about September about a man of about forty-four who has the second cut and would not be in a book, and that the two are about two different men and that the clerk had put them in one line and had not joined them, and that this is the sixteenth time.

Nobody argued with that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the entry has a date and a person and a reason and no name, and that the first thing this district ever wrote down with all four on it was a sheet nailed to a stake at the ninth mile on the twenty-first of September, and that a date, a place, a person and a reason had cost a nail and a stake and had taken four hours to make, and that this one took a clerk of nineteen years four minutes and was written in a room and not nailed to anything.

AND THE ENTRY OF THE THIRTY-FIRST OF OCTOBER IS ELEVEN LINES LONG AND IT CARRIES A DATE AND A PERSON AND A REASON AND NO NAME **AND THE CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS SAID THAT A MAN WHO WITHDREW FROM A SEASON THAT NEVER STARTED IS A SENTENCE A STRANGER CAN REPEAT FOR NINE YEARS, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID THAT THE SAME REASON WAS GIVEN IN SEPTEMBER ABOUT A DIFFERENT MAN AND THAT THE TWO ARE NOT JOINED.**


---

A man of fifty-six said the arithmetic of the whole thing out loud in a room in four minutes. **It is in the minute in his own words and the clerk of nineteen years wrote all of it down and did not join any of the four.**

"The ledger has eleven rows and one of the eleven says a man was not asked and one of the eleven is not a year. The notice is one and it is not a row. So a man can stand in a frame, can be asked, can be written down, can be given a right, can use the right, and the page will not show that he used it, and the man who filled the page is the man who can be found, and the page will say the figure and not the refusal."

Nobody argued and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing entered that a withdrawal notice is not one of the five things this district does not have and is not one of the six it has built and not named, and is not one of the three documents nobody owns, and that he had wanted to put it in one of the two lists and had not, and that a thing that belongs to neither list is not promoted into either one by being said out loud in a room.

And a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said the other half and he is the fourth of the six and he is the one who will have to fill the page, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that the man who will have to fill it said the thing and that she had wanted to put the two in one line and had not.

"**You cannot put a refusal in a column headed what a thing did. A ledger is what happened. A right is what somebody did not let happen, and there is no column on this page for what somebody did not let happen, and I am the one who has to rule it or not rule it.**"

Nobody ruled it. **A clerk of nineteen years entered that a column was not ruled on the thirty-first of October, and that a man who fills a page for years has to rule a column himself, and that he did not, and that she has entered that he did not and that she is not going to ask him why.**

**AND ON THE THIRTY-FIRST OF OCTOBER A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM AND DOES NINE DAYS OF IT IN TEN SAID THAT A LEDGER IS WHAT HAPPENED AND A RIGHT IS WHAT SOMEBODY DID NOT LET HAPPEN, AND THAT THERE IS NO COLUMN ON THE PAGE FOR WHAT SOMEBODY DID NOT LET HAPPEN, AND HE IS THE MAN WHO WILL HAVE TO RULE IT AND HE DID NOT RULE IT, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT HE DID NOT AND THAT SHE IS NOT GOING TO ASK HIM WHY.**

AND THE NOTICE IS NOT ONE OF THE FIVE THINGS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE AND IS NOT ONE OF THE SIX IT HAS BUILT AND NOT NAMED AND IS NOT ONE OF THE THREE DOCUMENTS NOBODY OWNS **AND IT BELONGS TO NEITHER LIST AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID THAT A THING THAT BELONGS TO NEITHER LIST IS NOT PROMOTED INTO EITHER ONE BY BEING SAID OUT LOUD IN A ROOM.**
