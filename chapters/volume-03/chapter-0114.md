# Chapter 114: The Twenty-Ninth of August

The iron was in the bracket at nine in the morning on the twenty-ninth of August and it was still in the bracket at noon and at about four in the afternoon, and the train on that siding had stood a hundred and ninety-nine days, and nineteen people stood at the end of a thirty-foot platform in front of it for most of the day because there was nothing else to do on the day this district had been waiting nine weeks for.

The iron is the length of a forearm and it weighs four pounds and one ounce and **the ninth place is the only frame on the line that it fits,** and the bracket is four feet off the ground at the end of the platform, and the platform is thirty feet long and there is a lamp on a post cut out of a green pipe and a rail along the back edge and a hole in the ground that is not a mark.

Nobody threw the points. Nobody put a hand on a lever. A mechanic of forty-three brought **a bar about ten feet long** to the end of the platform at about eleven in the morning and stood with it for two hours and did not put it in, and four people asked him why at intervals and he gave the same answer each time and it is eighteen words long.

"**Nobody in this yard can say no to me, so it is not a thing I can do.**"

---

**THE FINDING OF THE TWENTY-NINTH OF AUGUST IS THAT A THING NOBODY IS ALLOWED TO DECIDE IS NOT DECIDED BY A DATE, AND THAT THE SHEET WHICH SAID OTHERWISE WENT UP ON THE SECOND OF AUGUST FOR FOUR COPPERS AND WAS IN EVERY WAY CORRECT, AND THAT NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT KNEW IT WAS CORRECT UNTIL THE DAY IT WAS WRONG.**

The man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that at half past four in the afternoon in nine seconds, and it is in the minute in his own words, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down and asked him whether he was saying that a sheet a stranger is holding in four places is wrong.

"**I am saying the sheet is right and the district is wrong, and the difference between those two is the whole of the last day, and in four years a stranger will read that sheet and see a rule, and the rule worked on the day and the rule did not do anything, and both of those are in one paragraph on a piece of paper four coppers long.**"

"Which of them is the finding."

"**Neither. The finding is that this district has had a rule for nine weeks that says a date will decide things, and the rule has never been wrong, because the only two times a date has arrived in nine weeks the date has not had to decide anything, and a rule that has never been tested is not a rule and this one is now tested and it is a sentence and not a rule.**"

And then a woman of fifty-one who keeps the only pair on that line, who is fifty-one and has held the only pair on that line for eleven years, said the half of it that nobody had said, and she said it from the back of the platform and did not come any nearer than about nine feet.

"**A date is not a decision. A date is the last day somebody can do something, and on the twenty-ninth of July four things ended and nobody stood up, and on the twenty-ninth of August nothing ended and nobody stood up, and those are the same day twice and I have been on this line for eleven years and it is the first time I have seen a date arrive and go and leave the thing exactly as it was.**"

Nobody in that yard argued with her and the minute says nobody argued with her, and says that a man of thirty-eight who has been in four rooms in nine weeks asked her afterwards whether she thought the boundary would end, and that she said no, and that the two of them are not the same thing and she was asked why and gave a reason.

"**Because the boundary is two people and a hole and a term, and a term is somebody's to hold, and the iron is nobody's. I am one of the two people and I have said yes five times and I am going to be asked again in a room this afternoon, and the iron is not mine and I will be asked it again in five weeks and the answer will be the same.**"

The mechanic of forty-three put the bar down on the ballast at about ten past four, and it is in the minute that he put it down and not that he was stopped, and nineteen people have said since that this is the distinction this district has been building for nine weeks and that it came out of a man's hands.

---


---

The mechanic of forty-three said one more thing on the platform on the twenty-ninth and the clerk of nineteen years entered it, and it is about nine hours long in his own words and it is the only entry of that day that anybody asked to be shortened.

"**I have worked on frames for nineteen years and I have put a bar to about forty of them and I have never once done it because somebody told me to, and today I stood on a platform for two hours with a bar and a decision I am not allowed to make, and I want to say the thing I have been not saying all day, and then I am going to take the bar home.**"

"What is the thing."

"**That I could have done it. I want it in the book that I could have done it and did not, because in four years the book will say that on the twenty-ninth of August a group of people stood on a platform and did not do anything, and that is a story about cowardice, and it was not cowardice, it was that every one of us was waiting for a person to arrive who had the right to tell us to, and that person does not exist, and I would like the sentence to say he does not exist rather than that we were waiting.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything and nineteen people said something afterwards and the minute says that about nineteen people said something afterwards and that the nine were not the same nine.

And a man of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures stood at the front of the platform for most of the day and said one thing at about eleven in the morning, and the minute puts it in his own words and it is forty-five words long.

"**A thing nobody asked is not a thing nobody can do, and I have been on this bank since about six in the morning and nobody has asked me to do anything, and I would like it entered that I was here and not asked.**"

And that was entered, and not asked is entered as not asked and not as a refusal, and the count of not-askings about a woman of forty-five did not move and is a different column and is not to be added to this one, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that she had wanted to put it in the same column and had been told by the tenth hand that a column is a column and not a bucket.

A stranger was on the platform twice, at about ten in the morning and again at about three in the afternoon, and he did not ask anybody anything either time and stood about nine feet off and watched, and four people noticed him and nobody spoke to him, and the minute says nobody spoke to him and that this is the second time in nine weeks that a stranger has stood in a public place and been left alone and that both times he came back and asked one question afterwards.

He came back at half past four and asked the man who keeps the fourth toll board a question that is eight words long, and the board keeper wrote it down and did not answer it and wrote it on the bottom line in chalk, and it is the twenty-ninth sentence on that board and the second one that is somebody else's question.

"**WHICH OF THESE THINGS COST SOMEBODY A YEAR.**"

Nobody in that lane answered him and nineteen people were on the platform and a man who carries water and does not read figures was about nine feet away and did not say anything, and the entry of the twenty-ninth of August says that the question was asked thirty miles from the only place in this district where anybody could have answered it, and that nobody in Alder Reach knew at the time that it had been asked.

And at about two miles west, at the twelfth place, a man of sixty-one stood in a frame with a lever in it and did nothing for an hour, and he had come at his own cost, and the cost is two days a month in somebody else minding a pumping engine, and he had been coming twice a month for nineteen years and the twenty-ninth of August was one of the two.

He had a book in his coat with a rubber band on it and the band had perished and the book had to be held shut with a thumb, and in it, on nine hundred lines, **four words appear and nothing else,** and the twenty-ninth of August is the second of the nine hundred that has a date on it that is not the day it was written on.

FRAME TURNED. NOTHING SENT.

He came down the two miles afterwards to the ninth place in the rain, at about six in the evening, and nineteen people were still on the platform, and he was not asked anything and he stood at the back for an hour and a half and then asked one question, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is thirteen words long.

"**Is the man going to try it again on the twenty-ninth of September.**"

Nobody answered him and the minute says nobody answered him, and says that the man of thirty-eight said the only thing anybody said in eleven seconds and that it was not an answer to the question.

"**There is no date on anything in this district after the twenty-ninth of August except two, and both of them are somebody else's, and I am one of the people who has to be in a room on one of them, and the other one is a date I put on a thing myself and anybody in a room can take off me in a sentence.**"

"Then nobody is going to try it again."

"**I cannot say that and neither can you, and the reason neither of us can say it is that a thing nobody is allowed to decide is not decided by a date, it is only not decided today, and that sentence is new and it is nine minutes old and it is going to be in a book in four hours.**"

---

The boundary at the west end was asked on the twenty-ninth of August, from two rooms, in twenty-six minutes of which nine were the sixth question, and the asking was composed by four people and none of them is Adrian Vale, and the answers were entered in two books about nine hours apart by two people who were not in the same room.

The woman of the column was asked from a counting room over a chandler's shop and she answered where she lives, in her own doorway, in the same two sentences and in the same order she has used since the twenty-ninth of March, and a chandler of thirty-four who keeps a counter of oil on Ash Row was in the room and heard it.

The keeper of the only pair was asked from a yard on the tide and she gave the condition, then the word, then the yes, in that order, and the condition was the same condition she has given five times, and the yard is lent by a woman of forty-four who keeps a Road House and is not thanked and insists it be entered as not a security.

**THE TERM OF THE BOUNDARY AT THE WEST END RUNS FROM THE TWENTY-NINTH OF AUGUST TO THE TWENTY-NINTH OF SEPTEMBER, AND IT IS TWO NAMED PEOPLE AND A STATED TERM AND A PUBLISHED ENTRY AND A HOLE IN THE GROUND THAT IS NOT A MARK, AND NOBODY VOTED ON IT.**

The register that goes with it is short and it is in the public lot book in the registrar's own hand and it is **the second time in nine weeks that a day has been composed by somebody else and put in by somebody else and voted on by nobody.**

> **THE TWENTY-NINTH OF AUGUST. TWO THINGS HAPPENED ON IT AND THEY ARE NOT JOINED. THE BOUNDARY WAS ASKED FROM TWO ROOMS AND BOTH ANSWERED AND THE TERM RUNS TO THE TWENTY-NINTH OF SEPTEMBER. THE IRON WAS NOT DECIDED BY ANYBODY. THE ONLY DOCUMENT THAT SAID WHO COULD DECIDE IT WAS A CHARTER AND IT ENDED AT MIDNIGHT ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF JULY.**

The registrar was asked on the evening of the twenty-ninth whether the two things should be joined, and she did not refuse, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that as a first and it is the first time in nine weeks that she has been asked to join two things and has not said no.

"**They arrived separately and I am keeping them in two lines, and I would like the entry to say that I was asked and that I did not refuse, and that the reason I did not refuse is that a rule of nine weeks has just been found to be wrong in public and I am not going to join anything to a day that has just been proved to be a sentence and not a rule.**"

Nobody argued and the minute says nobody argued, and says that a man who mends fencing said afterwards that she had refused eleven times in nine weeks and had now been asked once and had said no to nothing, and that the two of those are the same decision.

---


---

And on the morning of the twenty-ninth, before any of it, a man of thirty-four who mends fencing walked the whole line in one day, because the standing instruction is discharged and not satisfied and repeating it is the only way this district discharges it, and he wrote down that he had walked it.

Twelve places, eleven boards, and one place that does not report itself. He got to the Harrow end at half past two in the afternoon and put his hand on the plinth and took it off again, and the cap of lead has been on it since the fourth year and has a rim of green on it where the rain has run down for thirteen years, and nothing about that is a mystery and nothing about it is joined to anything.

**AND A PLACE THAT REPORTS EVERY MORNING IS A PLACE NOBODY GOES TO, AND THAT HAS BEEN THE REASON SINCE THE TWENTY-NINTH OF APRIL, AND IT WAS REFUSED AGAIN IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS IN THE FOURTH MONTH OF THIS YEAR, AND THE FINDING OF THE TWENTY-NINTH OF AUGUST IS THAT A REFUSAL THAT HAS BEEN GIVEN FOUR TIMES IS STILL A REFUSAL AND NOT A RULE AND NOT A FINDING AND NOT A THING THE DISTRICT CAN BUILD.**

At the fourth place he stopped for half an hour and the boy of nineteen was there and had been there since about six in the morning and the two of them did not talk about the bracket, and the man who mends fencing said afterwards that the boy had asked him one question and that he had not been able to answer it and that he had not written it down because it was not a finding.

"What was it."

"**He asked whether the twenty-ninth was a day that things got decided on, and I said I did not know, and he said that was the right answer, and then he said that he had been at that place every day since the ninth of last month and that nobody had ever asked him what a day was for, and that he had not told anybody, and that he was telling me because I had walked the line and nobody else had.**"

"And you did not write it down."

"**I did not write it down because he did not ask me to, and because he is nineteen and he is not a keeper and he is not a bearer and he is nobody's and he has said so in a yard in front of nineteen people, and a man who mends fencing is not a clerk and is not a form.**"

The entry of the twenty-ninth of August says that a man of thirty-four walked twelve places and eleven boards and one place that does not report itself, and that he was told something at the fourth place by a person who is nineteen and who told nobody, and that he entered that he was told and not what he was told, and that this is the first time in nine weeks that a person has refused to have a thing written down after somebody had asked him to say it out loud in the open.

---

And then the notice came down, on the morning of the thirty-first, off eleven walls and three toll boards and the board of eleven in the yard of Lot 17, and it came down because the man who prints sheets said in a yard that a notice about a day is a notice for four days and this one was thirteen, and a clerk of nineteen years took them down herself in an hour and put them in a folder with about nine other sheets that were begun and not finished.

The folder has no name on it and she wrote not a finding on the top of it in her own hand in the fourth month of this year, and she said in August that she would like somebody to ask her why a folder needs a name and that she does not know the answer, and nobody has asked her and the minute says nobody asked her.

**THE FINDING OF THE THIRTY-FIRST OF AUGUST IS THAT A SHEET ABOUT A DAY COMES DOWN AFTER ABOUT FOUR DAYS AND A SHEET ABOUT A DISTANCE AND NINE PEOPLE STAYS UP UNTIL SOMEBODY UNKNOWS IT, AND THIS DISTRICT HAS GOT ONE OF EACH AND THE ONE ABOUT A DAY IS IN A FOLDER WITH NO NAME ON IT.**

And on the morning of the thirtieth of August, a boy of seventeen wrote two hundred days in his index and did not write a sixth date, and the train had stood two hundred days, and a hundred and ninety-nine was the day before and is a figure nobody had said out loud in nine weeks, and the boy said the round one out loud in a yard in the ordinary voice and nineteen people wrote it down.

The standing order that somebody walks every yard of the line is discharged and not satisfied, and it is a fact about an instrument and not about the ground, and the Harrow end cannot report itself, and there is a plinth on the embankment thirty yards east of the engine house with the bolt-holes in it and a cap of lead over them, and a ground signal was refused again in four seconds in the fourth month of this year for a reason that is nine words long and has not changed.

The line book at the east end has **nine hundred and six movements in it and one more,** and the one more is the twenty-ninth of June, and there is nothing in it for the twenty-ninth of this month because nothing moved, and the boy of seventeen measured the stall for the fifth time on the thirtieth and it has not moved and he wrote down that he measured it and did not write down why.

**AND AFTER THE TWENTY-NINTH OF AUGUST THIS DISTRICT HAD TWO DATES AND NOT ONE OF THEM IS ABOUT A PIECE OF IRON, AND THE FINDING OF THE THIRTIETH OF AUGUST IS THAT A DISTRICT WHICH HAS RUN OUT OF DAYS IS NOT THE SAME AS A DISTRICT THAT HAS RUN OUT OF THINGS, AND THE SECOND ONE IS FURTHER OFF THAN THE FIRST ONE LOOKS.**

---

And then on the evening of the thirty-first of August a woman of fifty who is the water at the ninth place was asked, before the cart goes, whether she would go thirty miles east, and she was asked in the five things in a counting room over a chandler's shop in front of nineteen people, and she said no, and the reason took half an hour and it is in the minute in her own words.

"**I have not been asked this in five months and I have said yes five times to a smaller thing, and I want the entry to say that I noticed the difference and that I have not been entered in a count of not-askings for a sixth time, and that I would like the count left where it is.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said the count was at four with one standing refusal and that it was a column and not a bucket, and the woman of fifty said yes to that, and the entry of the thirty-first of August says that the count did not move and that the woman of forty-five was not asked for anything on the thirty-first of August and is in the count as not asked.

The party of nine became eight, and a man of thirty-one who is a signalman of the district's own crew and one other man who is not on the sheet at all went on the second cart, and the finding of the thirty-first of August is that **a document about people said nine and eight went, and ten went, and neither of the two that were added was on it, and the sheet is nailed to a board in a yard and a stranger took a copy of it cold on the twenty-seventh, and the man who prints it has been asked for a correction and has declined one.**

"And why."

"**Because the first one I declined was a correction about a railway and this one is a correction about people, and I have spent nine weeks learning that a document does not owe anybody a correction, and a man who prints a day and then unprints it will go on to printing a name and unprinting that.**"

Nobody argued with him and the entry says that a document which has become wrong for a reason nobody caused is a document nobody owns, that this has now happened twice in this district and not once, and that the two are not the same document and are not joined, and that the registrar entered both of them and struck neither and read the sentence out twice at the counter on the thirty-first because a document goes out twice.
