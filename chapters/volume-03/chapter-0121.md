# Chapter 121: The First Channel Fails

---

The cart came up the raised plank road on the twenty-first of September with a sheet nailed to a stake across the tail-board, and the water was off the planks, and about nineteen people were standing at the ninth mile because a cart with a sheet on it does not come up that road for nothing.

The plank nearest the far end is limewood and about nineteen years old, and nobody said a word about it, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing looked at it for the length of a breath and then looked at the sheet on the stake and said nothing about either, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that he said nothing about both and did not ask him why because she had asked him about a plank once in September and had been told that a clerk does not ask a man about a plank.

The sheet had a date on it, and the date was the twenty-first of September, and it had been written on the far bank of a river that came up nine feet in the flood year and has not gone down, and it was written by a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households and who does not count for a living. He wrote it standing up.

He wrote two things on it, and **the second of them is the reason the sheet is nailed to a stake and not carried in a hand.**

---

> **THE FIRST CHANNEL FAILED. THE DAY IT FAILED IS NOT KNOWN AND IS ENTERED AS NOT KNOWN. THE SALT OFF THE PAN WITH THE FRAME OVER IT AND NOBODY IN IT STOPPED ON OR ABOUT THE TWELFTH OF SEPTEMBER AND WAS FOUND ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OF SEPTEMBER BY A MAN WHO COUNTS PANS AND WHO SAID HE WOULD NOT SAY A NUMBER TWICE. THE REASON IS ON THE BACK OF THIS SHEET IN THE WORDS OF A MASON OF ABOUT FIFTY WHO CAME FOR LOAM AND DID NOT FIND ANY.**
>
> **I SAID ON THE SEVENTH OF SEPTEMBER THAT THE PAN WITH NOBODY ON IT WOULD BE THE FIRST OF THE SEVEN TO FAIL AND THAT NOBODY WOULD KNOW IT HAD FAILED. I HAVE BEEN FOURTEEN DAYS RIGHT AND I WOULD LIKE THE ENTRY TO SAY THAT I SAID IT ON THE SEVENTH AND NOT TODAY. THE OTHER THING IS THAT I HAVE BEEN RIGHT ABOUT A THING THAT COST A YEAR OF NOBODY'S MEMORY BECAUSE THERE WAS NOBODY IN IT TO COST.**

Nobody at the ninth mile could read it, and the man of thirty-four who mends fencing read it out in the ordinary voice, twice, because the rule of a counter is that a document goes out twice, and the second time he read it slower and stopped at the second sheet and asked the cart driver whether the man of fifty-six had said that out loud or whether the cart driver had heard him, and the cart driver said he had not been on the flat, he had been on the road, and the man of thirty-four said the sentence out loud again anyway.

The back of the sheet was a mason of about fifty, and he had come out for loam and did not find any, and he walked to the cut because a mason cannot help it, and what he said there is in the party's minute in his own words, and the clerk counted it twice and got nineteen both times.

"**The sill is a foot of loam laid by a man who is dead, and your flood year put nine feet of river over the whole flat in one season, and a foot of loam does not come back.**"

Then a man of fifty-six who does count for a living was asked whether the cut had held all flood year, and he said that a cut with no sill in it is a hole and not a channel, and that the six that are left have sills in them because somebody laid them, and that a man who lays a sill in a river flat lays a foot of loam because that is what he has got and because there was nothing else on that bank to lay.

---

Nobody in that lane argued, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it and then entered the other half of it in the same line, because the rule of a clerk is that a clerk enters both halves in one line and does not join them.

The other half is that a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households said the words *cannot report itself* about a salt pan on the seventh of September in a cart, and that a man of thirty-one who walks every yard of a railway line put his hand on a plinth thirty yards east of an engine house in the same week.

The entry says the two are in one line and are not joined, and says that the clerk has put them in one line and not joined them, and says that this is the fourth time she has done that in nine weeks.

The entry also says **that she has never once been asked by anybody why she does it, and that she does it because the other half of a sentence is somebody else's job and she has never been given it.**

The man of thirty-four who mends fencing read that last part out and read it twice, and about four people heard the second reading and one of them said that a clerk who has a rule and has not been asked about it is a thing this district has four of, and nobody said which four.

---

The train on the siding had stood two hundred and twenty-two days on the twenty-first of September, and nobody said so, and a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and beside it there are five dates on one line and a rule drawn under the fifth of them in his own hand and no sixth date.

Nobody argued about that either, and the clerk entered that the figure of two hundred and twenty-two days is a count from the eleventh of February and not a count from anything else, and that the fifth date has been ruled under since the twenty-fifth of August and has not been written above, and that the boy was asked on the nineteenth of September what the fifth date was for and gave the same nine words he has given three times and that they are in the minute in his own hand and that the clerk entered that he was asked and not that she understood him.

---

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OF SEPTEMBER A CHANNEL FAILED AND IT WAS WRITTEN DOWN WITH A DATE ON IT ON A SHEET NAILED TO A STAKE AT THE NINTH MILE, AND THE FINDING IS THAT A CHANNEL IS A CHANNEL AND A RIVER IS A RIVER, AND THE REASON WAS A THING SOMEBODY COULD STATE, AND THE DAY IT FAILED COULD NOT BE STATED AND IS ENTERED AS NOT KNOWN AND IS NOT ENTERED AS A MYSTERY.**

---

The party of nine went east on the twenty-second of September and ten went, and it was composed in a yard in nineteen minutes because a yard can ask a person and a board cannot and a form cannot, and **nine were asked in the five things and four were not asked, and not asked was written against the four in the clerk's own hand and was not the fifth thing because the fifth thing is what a person is asked after the four have said yes.**

Of the nine, one was a chandler of about thirty-four who keeps a counter of oil on Ash Row and a stall in front of it, and one was a woman of fifty-one who keeps a stall on the lower terrace and who supplies the Harrow engine's oil out of her own till, and the clerk entered both descriptions in the same line so that no stranger reading the entry cold could take one of them for the other.

A woman of fifty-one who keeps a stall on the lower terrace said no to the five things and gave the reason in four words, and the clerk entered that she was not asked again and that not asked is in the count.

"**My stall is open.**"

And the chandler of about thirty-four who keeps a counter of oil on Ash Row said yes in the first question and then asked the clerk in the second question whether the yard had a lamp in it, and the clerk said no, and he said he would bring one, and the clerk entered that he had offered a lamp and that he had not been asked and that the lamp was a lantern and the lantern went in the second cart and four people had watched him carry it out.

The salt man did not go, and gave the reason in nine words, and it is in the minute.

"**My two pans are under four feet of river and have been since the flood year, and I have not got a cart free before the twelfth of October.**"

And the man of thirty-eight said the thing he had come to say about his own figure, and he said it standing at the tail-board with the sheet on the stake behind him, and it is in the minute in his own words, and the clerk wrote the number of it in the margin because the clerk counts.

"**I am holding a hundred and eighty-three coppers that nobody is owed until the thirtieth of this month, and I am not going to set nine days of a cart on looking at numbers that are not mine.**"

Nobody argued with that, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that the finding of the twenty-second of September is that **the party of nine is going east for the first time without the man who has spent nine weeks pricing everything in front of him, and that this is a thing that has happened to him and not a thing he did.**

The two carts were loaded between two in the afternoon and five, and the loading took three hours, and the clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it because a party that goes thirty-four miles is only as good as what is in the carts.

The first cart took the field book, the nineteen lines, a folded sheet with a date on it, the party's own minute from the twenty-first to the twenty-fourth of August, and a sealed fold with two guild certificates in it that had been certified on the fifteenth and the seventeenth of September and could not be struck. The second cart took two lanterns, a coil of rope, about forty pounds of meal, a tent that had been repaired twice, and a churn.

The churn is in the minute because a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households asked who had put it there and was told that a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide on the tide had put it there herself on the evening of the twenty-first without being asked, and that she had said nine words about it, and the nine words are in the minute and the clerk entered them under the churn and not above the list.

Nobody in the yard argued about the churn and about four people said that a thing a person brings without being asked is not the same as a thing a party asks for, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that it was the same thing, and was asked how he knew, and gave a reason that is eleven words long.

"**Because nobody in this yard can say no to me, and I did not ask her.**"

And the clerk of nineteen years entered that sentence and entered that it is the ninth time in ten weeks that the same reason has been given in this district by three different people, and that the three have never met.

---

And on the twenty-second, at the fourth place, a boy of nineteen who is paid nothing said three words about the sheet on the stake, and nobody had asked him, and the minute says he said it and that four people heard it.

"**That is the second time a thing has been written down with a date on it where a thing stopped working, and the first one was a stall, and nobody wrote down what the stall was for either.**"

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing wrote the three words down and underlined them, and the clerk of nineteen years did not know what they were and asked, and he told her, and she wrote them out.

**THE DATE ON IT.**

---

Then the minute of the twenty-second of September was read out in the yard it was composed in, in four minutes, and it is nineteen lines long, and it is the shortest entry this book has carried since the eighth of March that is about people going somewhere.

> **THE TWENTY-SECOND OF SEPTEMBER. THIRTEEN ASKED IN FIVE THINGS AND NINE CHOSEN AND TEN WENT AND NO SHEET WAS PRINTED ABOUT IT. FOUR OF THE THIRTEEN WERE NOT ASKED AND NOT ASKED IS WRITTEN AGAINST ALL FOUR. THE FIGURE OF ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THREE COPPERS IS HELD BY A MAN WHO IS NOT ON THE CART AND ANYBODY IN THAT ROOM MAY HAVE ENDED IT IN A SENTENCE SINCE THE FOURTEENTH OF AUGUST AND NONE OF THEM HAS. THE FIRST CHANNEL HAS FAILED AND THE REASON IS ON A SHEET AT THE NINTH MILE WITH A DATE ON IT AND THAT SHEET WAS WRITTEN BY HAND AND COST A NAIL AND A STAKE AND NOTHING ELSE.**

And a man who does not read figures read that out at the ward market counter the same evening and it took four minutes, and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and it went out twice, and forty people were at the counter and a stranger took a copy of it cold off the board of eleven and did not stop anybody.

The stranger read the fourth line twice and then asked the man at the counter a question in four words, and the man at the counter said that it was a question and not a figure, and that the man who reads figures was thirty-four miles east and would be back on the twenty-ninth.

The stranger said that he would be in the district on the twenty-ninth and that he would be glad of the same sheet again, and the sheet went out twice, and the man at the counter entered that a stranger had asked for a sheet in advance and that this had never happened in nine weeks, and that the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice and not that it is kept.

---

**AND THE FINDING OF THE TWENTY-FIRST AND THE TWENTY-SECOND OF SEPTEMBER IS THAT A REASON IS THE ONE COLUMN THIS DISTRICT HAS NEVER MANAGED TO BUILD, AND THAT IT MANAGED ONE ON THE FIRST OCCASION A THING STOPPED, AND THAT A MASON OF ABOUT FIFTY PUT IT IN NINETEEN WORDS IN A FIELD WITHOUT BEING ASKED FOR IT, AND THAT A PERSON WHO CAN COUNT AND A PERSON WHO CAN READ THE COUNT ARE NOT THE SAME PERSON, AND THAT THIS DISTRICT HAS BEEN PAYING FOR THE FIRST FOR NINE WEEKS AND HAS NEVER PAID FOR THE SECOND.**

---

Then a man of about forty-three who is a mechanic and who brought a ten-foot bar to a bracket on the twenty-ninth of August and put it down at ten past four without anybody stopping him was in the yard, and he was not on the party and was not asked to be, and he stood at the back for nineteen minutes and then said the only thing he said.

"**A man who lays a sill is dead and a man who counts pans is alive and neither of them writes anything down, and the two of them were on the same flat for nine years, and one of them knew and the other one counted.**"

"And who should have written it."

"**Not me, and I have thought about it for four minutes standing here, and I have got to the same place twice, which is that the reason a channel failed belongs to whoever digs them and this district has never once asked a digger anything, and a party of nine is not a digger.**"

Then the man of thirty-eight said one sentence and it is in the minute in his own words and it is nineteen words long, and the mechanic entered that he had not been asked to be in the room and was in it.

"**Then go and ask nine diggers and not nine readers, and I will go with you, and that is the only job I have this month.**"

And the mechanic said no in four seconds, and gave the reason in nine words, and the reason is in the minute.

"**I mend engines and not channels and I would be asking about water and not about a reason.**"

And a clerk of nineteen years entered that the man of thirty-eight had offered to go and ask nine diggers on the twenty-second of September, and that a man of about forty-three who is a mechanic had refused, and that the refusal is the first refusal in nine weeks that was not entered as a refusal to read, and that it was entered as a refusal and not as anything else, and that the column for things that are not findings did not get it.

And in the evening, in the yard, at about six, nineteen people read out what the two days were, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing read it out and not the clerk, and the clerk of nineteen years said afterwards that she had offered and that he had said no in four seconds and that she had let him.

"**You have read out four sheets this week and three of them were other people's and one of them was yours.**"

"It is my minute."

"**It is my minute and the day was the twenty-first and the twenty-first is not a page anybody can hold, and if a man of thirty-four who mends fencing reads the twenty-first out then a stranger will hear the twenty-first in his voice and not in a clerk's, and the twenty-first is the only day this year a stranger is going to want.**"

And about nine people stayed after that, and a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households was asked whether the salt man would be able to come east at all before the twelfth of October.

He said he did not know, and that the man had not been asked and had answered about a cart and not about himself, and that this was the same man who had walked eleven miles twice in two years and had been right both times.

He said that nobody had ever once asked him whether he wanted to be the man who drives salt into Alder Reach, and that this district might find out on the twelfth of October.

And a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide came into the yard at half past four and asked for the sheet and was given it, and she read it herself because she can read, and she read it twice, and she said two things about it and the second of them took nine seconds.

"**I have a churn in the second cart that I put there this morning and I did not ask anybody, and I would like the minute to say that a woman brought a thing to a party she is not on, and that the party took it, and that neither of those is a service and that I am not owed anything for either.**"

"You are not on the party."

"**No, and I have not been asked to be and I am not going to ask, and the woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years on the far side of that water and me are two people with the same number and the clerk has written both of us down twice with the trade in and that is the whole of the trouble with numbers in this district.**"

Then the man of thirty-eight asked her a question about the churn and asked it badly, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that he asked it badly and that the woman of forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide answered it anyway, and that this is the third time in ten weeks that a person has answered a badly asked question and asked for the entry to say so.

"**What do you want it for.**"

"**I asked her why she was bringing a churn to a party she is not on, and the answer is that she has eleven pounds of salt in a cellar and nine people in a yard and a week in front of her, and she is not being generous and she is not making a point, and I asked her as though she were doing one of those two things and she told me what she was doing.**"

"**You are not doing either of those two things.**"

"**No. I am doing the third thing, which is that a party that goes east is going to come back, and a woman with a churn and no way of getting it to them is a woman who has decided to be the person who does it, and that is not a virtue and it is a chore and I have four of them and one of them is mine.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything about the four chores, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing entered that she had said four and that he had asked her what the four were, and that she had answered in nineteen words and the nineteen words are in the minute.

"**Water, fuel, the salt that is not in my cellar, and a person to hold a door who is not paid. And two of those four I do not need this week, and one of them is yours, and I am not going to argue about it in a yard.**"

And then a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a stall of chalk and oil on a cart came into the yard and asked for the sheet to be read out a third time, and the man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that the rule is twice, and she said that the rule is twice and that she had not been in the room for the first one.

Nobody argued and it was read out a third time, and about four people who had heard it twice said nothing at all the third time, and the clerk entered that, and entered that a woman who keeps a stall of chalk and oil on a cart had asked for a document a third time because she had not been in the room for the first two, and that this district has four things it does not have and one of them is a place where a person has to be to hear a thing, and that a cart stall is a place where a person has to be to sell anything, and that the two have never once been in the same room.

She said one thing before she left and the clerk entered it in her own words and it is nineteen words long.

"**I sell chalk and oil and a person cannot buy a thing that is stopped by a board until they know the board is there.**"

And a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that he would put the third reading in the minute and did, and the clerk entered that he said he would and did, and that this is the second time in ten weeks a man of thirty-four who mends fencing has said he would put something in a minute and has done it, and that the first was the nineteen lines on the twentieth of September.

---

**AND THE FINDING OF THE TWENTY-SECOND OF SEPTEMBER IS THAT A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS WROTE A LINE AT THE BOTTOM OF A MINUTE THAT SAID WHAT A ROOM WOULD SAY AND THEN STRUCK IT, BECAUSE A MINUTE ENTERS WHAT A ROOM HAS SAID AND NOT WHAT A ROOM WILL SAY, AND THE STRIKE IS IN THE MINUTE, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING READ IT OUT AND ASKED HER WHY SHE HAD STRUCK IT AND SHE GAVE THE REASON IN NINE WORDS, AND THE NINE WORDS WERE THAT SHE HAS STRUCK FOUR THINGS IN TEN WEEKS AND WROTE DOWN ALL FOUR.**

**AND THE FINDING OF THE TWENTY-FIRST OF SEPTEMBER IS THAT A DISTRICT OF FOUR THOUSAND AND ONE HOUSEHOLDS RECEIVED THE NEWS OF A FAILED CHANNEL ON A SHEET NAILED TO A STAKE, AND THAT THE SHEET CARRIED FOUR THINGS — A DATE, A PLACE, A PERSON, AND A REASON — AND THAT THIS DISTRICT HAS SPENT NINE WEEKS BEING ABLE TO WRITE DOWN THE FIRST THREE AND HAS JUST MANAGED THE FOURTH ON THE FIRST OCCASION IT MATTERED, AND THAT A STRANGER ASKED FOR THE SHEET IN ADVANCE AND THAT HAS NEVER HAPPENED BEFORE.**
