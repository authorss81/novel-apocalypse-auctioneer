# Chapter 150: What a Stage Is Not

---

The board outside the room carried the twenty-fourth of December in a hand anybody could read and the twenty-third of December was the day before it, **and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot 17 at about ten in the morning and eleven of them had come to see whether a fifth day was written and the eleven did not say so and were not asked and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had counted nine pairs of eyes again and that the difference of two is not a finding.**

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that the room had a charter closed on it and a day on a board and that a room with both of those is a room that can say when it next meets, and that nobody has written a fifth day and that nobody is going to, and that a clerk of nineteen years said the same thing and was not asked to and that not asked is in the count and the count did not move.

Nobody argued and a woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that the fourth day was written by a man who counts for a living in a hand anybody could read and that she wrote the first three in a hand that could not be read and that the two are not joined, **and that she has not been asked why she wrote them in a hand that cannot be read and she is not going to say and that this is the tenth time in eleven weeks nobody has asked her.**

Nobody asked her. And a man of fifty-six said that the tenth is the number and that a count of not-askings about a woman who takes in washing is a column of its own and is not the count about the woman of forty-five and is not added to it,** and that the count about the woman of forty-five stands at four with one standing refusal and did not move on the twenty-third of December and her number did not appear.**

---

The last of the four uses of a board in December was on the twenty-first of December and it was a woman of about forty-seven who sells salt out of a barrow at the ninth place, and she wrote on the back of the second toll board, on a line that was already full, that a person who is not in a household has nowhere to put a load of salt, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the count of lines is six and did not move and that the seventh line at the ninth place is ruled and empty and was not written on and that nobody was asked about it.

It worked. That is the fourth time in twenty-eight days that a thing written on a board has worked, and a man of fifty-six said that the four are four different people and that a man losing four days, a woman in a channel, a man whose salt is on nobody's sheet and a woman who is not in a household are four and not one, and that the four are not joined and that the instrument is still unnamed and still unowned.

A woman of fifty who is the water at the ninth place made the count of uses on the twenty-first of December and gave a number, and a man of fifty-six made the same count an hour later and gave a different one, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered both and entered that she was not going to decide and that the count of uses in twenty-eight days has now been made six times and has given six numbers and that the six is not a rate and that a column is a thing a person is paid for and that she is not paid.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OF DECEMBER A WOMAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SEVEN WHO SELLS SALT OUT OF A BARROW AT THE NINTH PLACE WROTE ON A BOARD THAT A PERSON WHO IS NOT IN A HOUSEHOLD HAS NOWHERE TO PUT A LOAD OF SALT, AND IT WORKED, AND IT IS THE FOURTH TIME IN TWENTY-EIGHT DAYS THAT A THING WRITTEN ON A BOARD HAS WORKED, AND THE COUNT OF LINES IS SIX AND DID NOT MOVE AND THE SEVENTH LINE AT THE NINTH PLACE IS RULED AND EMPTY.**

---

A man of fifty-six put the question in a lane on the twenty-second of December and it was the last question anybody put in that lane in this year, and a clerk of nineteen years answered it and did not answer it, and the refusals to join two things stand at thirteen and her own count stands at twelve and neither moved on the twenty-second of December and the question is in a lane and not in a room.

Nobody argued and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that the two counts have not moved since the fifth of November and that a room which has stopped counting its own refusals has either stopped refusing or has started refusing in places it does not write down, **and that this district has not stopped refusing and that the not-writing-down is the thing nobody has looked at in eleven weeks.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that and that she was not asked about it and that not asked is in the count,** and that the office's name is in five places and in no register and that it was not answered in December and that it is not going to be answered by anybody standing in a lane in the last week of a year.**

And the answer to what a public office is for **was not answered in December, AND THE NAME IS IN FIVE PLACES AND IN NO REGISTER, AND ELEVEN TRADES COULD NOT STATE WHAT IT DID, AND FOUR ANSWERS WERE GIVEN IN FOUR ROOMS AND WERE NOT JOINED, AND FOUR GAPS IN FOUR BOOKS WERE NOT JOINED, AND NOBODY HAS ASKED THE REGISTRAR WHY SHE HOLDS TWO LINES APART.**

---

The hundred and eighty-three coppers was read out at the ward market counter on the twenty-second of December for the first time in eleven weeks and it is a page in the ward market's own book with a heading and no name against it, and a reader who is about fifty read it out and read the sixth column out first, as she does.

Nobody refused. A man of fifty-six said in the room that the figure is not to be struck and that it has not been struck, and that a page with a heading and no name against it is the only page in this book that has been in it since the middle of the year and that nobody has ever said who is owed it and that the answer is not to be struck either.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure of a hundred and eighty-three coppers and the figure of twenty-one years are in two books and are not added and that there is no rate, **and that a year is not a price and a price is not a year, and that the page was read out on the twenty-second of December and nobody refused, and that this is the first time it has been read out and that the not-reading is not to be entered as a refusal.**

Nobody argued and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten said that he could not read the page and that he had not asked for it to be read out,** and that a man who cannot read a figure and has not asked for it is a man in the count of not-askings and that the count did not move and that a clerk entered that he had not asked and that is the eleventh time in eleven weeks.**

Nobody asked him and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him, **and that the count of things this district does not have is five and the count of instruments it has built and not named is six and the count of documents nobody owns is three and that the fourth was not made in December and is not to be made.**

And the figure of one hundred **and eighty-three coppers was read out AT THE WARD MARKET COUNTER ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF DECEMBER FOR THE FIRST TIME IN ELEVEN WEEKS, AND IT IS A PAGE WITH A HEADING AND NO NAME AGAINST IT, AND NOBODY REFUSED, AND THE ANSWER IS NOT TO BE STRUCK, AND IT IS NOT ADDED TO THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS AND THERE IS NO RATE.**

---

A man of fifty-six read the six askings out in a lane on the twenty-first of December and they are fifty-one, thirty-nine, forty-four, thirty-one, twenty-six and forty-four, which is two hundred and thirty-five minutes in all, **and he read them and then said that no seventh asking was made in December and that the two people who hold the boundary at the west end were not asked again in December, and that the term runs from the twenty-ninth of September to the thirty-first of March and has not moved.**

Nobody argued and a clerk of nineteen years entered the six figures and the total, and entered that no seventh asking was made in December, and that the two people who hold the boundary were not asked, and that not asked is in the count, and that the count of not-askings about the two people who hold the boundary is a column of its own and is not the count about the woman of forty-five and is not added to it.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a district which has asked two people the same question for a month and has not asked them since the end of September has a term that ends in March and nobody holding it in the middle, **and that the two of them have not been asked whether they want it and that a woman of fifty who is the water at the ninth place proposed the shape in July and that neither of them has been asked and that this is the twelfth time in eleven weeks nobody has.**

Nobody asked him and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had said the twelfth time and that the count of it is twelve and is in no column, and that a district which has stopped asking two people about a boundary is not the same as a district which has been told no twice,** and that the two are not joined and that the difference has not been worked out in eleven weeks.**

And on the twenty-first of december A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX READ OUT THE SIX ASKINGS THAT CARRY A MINUTE-FIGURE, WHICH ARE FIFTY-ONE, THIRTY-NINE, FORTY-FOUR, THIRTY-ONE, TWENTY-SIX AND FORTY-FOUR, WHICH IS TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIVE MINUTES, AND NO SEVENTH ASKING WAS MADE IN DECEMBER AND THE TWO PEOPLE WHO HOLD THE BOUNDARY WERE NOT ASKED AGAIN.

---

A woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was in that yard on the twenty-third of December and was not asked for anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked and that not asked is in the count and the count did not move, and that she carries water to about ninety houses and that the figure of ninety was said by her and has not been checked and was not asked to be checked.

She said one thing and it went into the minute in her own words and it is twenty-one words, **and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got twenty-one and a clerk of nineteen years did not count them and said so and he said count them anyway and she did and got twenty-one both times.**

"**You have a page and I have a bucket and neither of them is going to be in the fourth sheet.**"

Nobody argued with it and a man of fifty-six entered it in the minute and did not join it to the hundred and eighty-three coppers, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had wanted to join the two and had not and that this is the twenty-eighth time,** and that the page has a heading and no name against it and that a bucket has no heading and no name and that the two are not the same instrument.**

Nobody argued and the woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said the other half, **and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the fourth sheet says HOUSEHOLDS and that a woman who carries water to ninety houses and is not in one of them is in the fourth sheet in the same way a person is in a room who is not spoken for, and that the two are not joined.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-THIRD OF DECEMBER A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT WHO CARRIES WATER AND DOES NOT READ FIGURES SAID THAT A ROOM CAN HAVE A PAGE AND SHE CAN HAVE A BUCKET AND NEITHER OF THEM IS GOING TO BE IN THE FOURTH SHEET, AND THE FOURTH SHEET SAYS HOUSEHOLDS, AND THE TWO ARE NOT JOINED AND THE COUNT OF NOT-ASKINGS ABOUT HER IS THE ELEVENTH TIME IN ELEVEN WEEKS NOBODY HAS.**

---

A slate went past a kitchen in Alder Reach for the fifth time in the middle of December and neither of them wrote on it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that five is a count and that a count of five is not a finding, and that the four sentences in charcoal are under a fifth line in chalk and that the chalk line has been there since the fourth of April, and that she was not told what was in the sentences and did not ask.

Nobody asked her about the sentences. A man of fifty-six said that a slate that has gone past a kitchen five times is a public document, and that the four hundred printed sheets of an office's name have a person's attention and a slate has none,** and that the two are not joined and that a slate is not a copy and a copy is not a document and the original is a room.**


---

The train on the siding had stood three hundred and fifteen days on the twenty-third of December and a man of seventeen wrote the figure in an index in a hand that is not a clerk's hand, and the sum was put under the figure in a minute for the last time in this year, and the sum is from the eleventh of February and not carried forward out of any book.

"**Seventeen days left in February after the eleventh, then one hundred and eighty-four to the thirty-first of August, which is two hundred and one. Thirty for September, thirty-one for October, thirty for November, thirty-one for December.**"

Two hundred and one and thirty is two hundred and thirty-one, and thirty-one is two hundred and sixty-two, and thirty is two hundred and ninety-two, and thirty-one is three hundred and twenty-three on the thirty-first of December, and there are eight days of that month left and the eighth is three hundred and fifteen.

Nobody argued and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam said again that a man who does a sum in front of strangers is not checking a number and is teaching it, **and that this district has been teaching numbers in lanes for eleven weeks and calling it counting, and that he said in the middle of December that he would stop saying it in December and start again in January, and that it is the twenty-third and he has stopped.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that he had stopped and that nobody was asked and that not asked is in the count, and that beside the boy of seventeen's index there are six dates on one line and a rule under the sixth and no seventh date, and that the count of readings is one for April, none for May, one for the twenty-ninth of June, one for the sixth of September, one for the ninth of October and one for the third of November, and that the refusals to read are nine.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-THIRD OF DECEMBER THE TRAIN ON THE SIDING HAD STOOD THREE HUNDRED AND FIFTEEN DAYS, AND THE COUNT OF REFUSALS TO READ IS NINE, AND THE DEPARTURE IS UNSPENT, AND THERE IS STILL NO DATE IN THE LEDGER FOR THE NEXT READING, AND IVO STENN'S SEPARATE SHEET IS NINETEEN WORDS AND IS TWENTY AS PRINTED AND HE WILL NOT DEFEND THE INTERVALS AND WILL NOT CALL IT ANYTHING ELSE.**

---

The last entry of the twenty-third of December is eleven lines and it is in the clerk of nineteen years' own hand and it was read out twice at the counter and the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice.

The ninth line of it says what a stage is not, and she wrote it in the ninth line and not the first because a clerk of nineteen years said in the middle of September that people read the top of a page.

> **A STAGE IS NOT A MOMENT. A STAGE IS A LENGTH OF TIME WITH A PERSON STANDING IN IT AND A DATE ON IT AND SOMEBODY ELSE'S PLAN AT THE END OF IT, AND A CHARTER IS A STAGE, AND A SEASON IS A STAGE, AND THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH IS NOT A MOMENT AND THE FIRST OF APRIL IS NOT A MOMENT, AND A DOCUMENT THAT SAYS A STAGE IS OVER HAS SAID THAT A LENGTH OF TIME IS OVER AND HAS NOT SAID THAT ANYBODY IS OUT OF IT.**

Nobody argued and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a clerk wrote the stage is not a moment in the margin of a book in the middle of the year and underlined it twice, and that this is the same sentence with the underlining taken off and put in the ninth line of a minute at the end of a year,** and that the two are not joined and that the underlining is in a book a stranger can take cold.**

A man of fifty-six said that the charter is a stage and that the season is a stage and that the word steward is a stage, and that the three all end on the thirty-first of March, and that on the thirty-first of March the name comes off the figure and the address ends and the season renews, and that the three are on one line and are not joined, and that the first of April is a day this district has not reached and is not a day this district is going to be told about.

And the last entry of the twenty-third **of December is eleven lines AND ITS NINTH LINE SAYS THAT A STAGE IS NOT A MOMENT AND THAT A STAGE IS A LENGTH OF TIME WITH A PERSON STANDING IN IT AND A DATE ON IT AND SOMEBODY ELSE'S PLAN AT THE END OF IT.**

---

Then the yard.

The ledger was on a trestle table in the yard of Lot 17 on the afternoon of the twenty-third of December with about nine people standing in front of it, and the page was open at a place where the third column is a column of years and the figures in it are not coppers, and the left-hand column is headed WHO STOOD IN IT and every person on that page at that place is a person who is not in a household, and a man of fifty-six said that out loud and that nobody argued and that he said it because it is the left-hand column and a person reads the left-hand column first.

A man of sixty-one with a frame, who keeps the frame at a place two miles west and comes two miles twice a month, had come in that afternoon and had not been asked to, and he had written on the head of the page, at the height of a hand, the name of a place, **and the name he wrote is the name the documents say, which is the ninth place, and he wrote it in a hand that shakes and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he wrote it and that nobody asked him to and that he has written four words on about nine hundred lines in nineteen years and that this is the fifth thing he has written and the largest of them.**

Nobody asked him what it was for and a man of fifty-six said out loud that nobody is going to ask him, and that a place's name at the height of a hand is for a person who cannot see it and is standing in the dark,** and that the district has been putting names at the height of a hand since the middle of the year and that a man of sixty-one has done it twice and that the two are not joined.**

And nobody in that yard knows what the instrument is called. A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the sentence about it out loud on the twenty-third of December and it is eighteen words and a clerk of nineteen years counted them twice and got eighteen both times, and the eighteen words are the last eighteen words anybody in this district has said about a thing that has five boards and no name.

"**We have got a page and a door and a name and none of them is called anything.**"

The ledger is nine copies on eleven boards and a stranger had already taken one cold that afternoon, at about eleven in the morning, without being asked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in this district has ever asked a stranger what he intends to use a copy for, and that there is no column for it, and that the not-asking is in the count and the count did not move.

**AND THE LEDGER STANDS ON A TABLE IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN WITH ELEVEN ROWS FILLED AND TWENTY RULED AND EMPTY AND A LINE UNDER THE ELEVENTH ROW WITH NOTHING UNDER IT, AND A RIGHT TO WITHDRAW BEFORE RENEWAL IS IN A THIRD FORM WITH A HOLE IN IT THAT THE FORM SAYS OUT LOUD, AND A CHARTER IS CLOSED ON FOUR SHEETS BY NINETEEN PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT ONE MAN, AND THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS IN A BOOK AGAINST ONE NAME UNTIL THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH, AND A YEAR IS THE REASON ON THE SIXTH SHEET OF A FOOD AGREEMENT AND A YEAR HAS NO NUMBER ON IT, AND THE FIFTH INSTRUMENT IS IN USE IN FOUR PLACES THIS MONTH AND HAS NO NAME AND NO OWNER, AND THE COUNT OF PROTECTED THINGS IS FIVE AND THE COUNT OF CONDITIONS WITH NO END ON IT IS FOUR AND THE COUNT OF NOT-ASKINGS ABOUT THE WOMAN OF FORTY-FIVE IS FOUR WITH ONE STANDING REFUSAL AND NONE OF THE THREE COUNTS MOVED.**
