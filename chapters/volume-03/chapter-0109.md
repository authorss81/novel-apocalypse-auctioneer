# Chapter 109: A Sentence Somebody Said

A sentence was said out loud in a counting room in Alder Reach on the ninth of this month at about four in the afternoon by a man who does not read figures, and it took nine seconds, and it is the closest anybody in this district has come to an answer to a question that has been open since the eighth of last month, and it is not an answer, and it is in a column for things that are not findings, and the person who said it asked for it to be struck and was refused.

The man does not read figures and has carried water at the ninth place for about nineteen years and is fifty-eight and is one of the four households on ground held by nobody, and he is the man who had a tin under a bed with a hundred and eighty-three coppers in it that nobody was owed.

He said it because he had been in the room for about an hour and had not said anything, and then somebody said the four answers again, the four answers four people in four rooms gave on the sixth of last month.

A place a person could refuse. A place a name could be charged to. A place a cost could be put in front of before it was paid. And a sign-writer of sixty-one who did not know.

**All four of those are about carrying something somebody would not carry themselves, and the registrar's reason for not joining them is that they arrived separately, and the finding of the ninth of this month is that somebody has finally joined them and the joining is wrong and the person who did it did it in a yard in the rain in June without being asked.**

"I have been in this room an hour," he said, "**and I have been thinking about the second one, about a name being charged to. And I have been thinking about the third one, about a cost being put in front of a person before it was paid. And I have been thinking about the four of them, and they are not four offices. They are one thing said four times by four people who did not know about each other.**"

Nobody argued for about four seconds.

"And that one thing is an office, and I know that it is an office because I have been alive for fifty-eight years and I know what an office is, **and it is the thing that takes the weight. That is all an office is. It is a thing that takes the weight for a person who cannot carry it, and it does not make the weight lighter, and it does not have to, and that is the whole of what it is for.**"

---

And then the room did the thing it does, which is about nine people at once asked a man who does not read figures a question, and he answered all of them, and one of the answers is in the book and the rest of them are not.

The one that is in the book was asked by a woman of about sixty with a stick, who is on no roll and is in no row and who has been in four rooms in nine weeks, and she asked him why he had waited an hour.

"Because I have been wrong about a rate sheet three times," he said, "**and two of them were in front of people, and the third one was the two hundred coppers, and I have been in this room an hour working out whether I am about to be wrong about an office in front of about thirty people, and I have worked out that I am, and I have said it anyway, because the alternative is saying nothing and letting four of you go on for another month.**"

"And how do you know you are wrong."

"**I do not know. I have worked out that a man who has been wrong three times about money has no business being the man who says what an office is, and I have said it anyway, and both of those are true, and I would like them in the same line.**"

Nobody argued and the entry of the ninth of this month says that the man who said the sentence has been wrong about money three times in eleven weeks and has never once been wrong about a quantity, and that the district entered that distinction on the nineteenth of last month and has not used it since, and that it is the reason he was the right person to say the wrong thing.

Three other people in that room agreed with him and the minute says three and not four, and the three are a licensed valuer of twelve years, a clerk of the ward market who is not the market, and a man of about thirty-one who is a signalman of the district's own crew, and each of the three gave a different reason and all three reasons are the same sentence.

"That is a toll board," said the licensed valuer, in about four seconds.

"That is a market," said the clerk of the ward market.

"That is a railway," said the man of about thirty-one, and he said it last and he said it quietly, and the minute says quietly, and that he said it after the other two and not before them.

And the tenth hand said the thing that is the reason the sentence is not in the book as a finding, and he said it in about nine seconds and it is the eleventh time in nine weeks he has been the man who stops a room, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**Three things in this district take a weight and none of them is an office, and a toll board takes a sentence, and a market takes coppers, and a railway takes a departure out of a hundred and forty-two, and not one of them has ever carried anything for a person who could not carry it themselves, and that is the difference between the four answers and what we have actually built.**"

---

The registrar was asked to enter it as a finding and she refused, and it is the tenth time, and the reason she gives is the reason she gives every time, which is that two things that arrived separately stay in two lines until somebody puts a thing on the paper that joins them.

"**And I will say the other half of it, which I have not said ten times. The four answers did not arrive separately. They arrived in four rooms, on the same day, about two hours apart, from four people who were each doing a different job. That is not separate. That is the opposite of separate. And nobody has ever put that in a book, and I am putting it in one now, and it is the first thing I have entered in nine weeks that nobody asked me to enter.**"

Nobody in that room argued with her and the minute enters that nobody argued with her, and that a clerk of nineteen years asked her whether she was refusing to enter a true thing or a useful thing, and that she gave an answer that is in the minute and is nineteen words long and is the best sentence anybody said in that room on the ninth of this month.

"**I do not know if it is true. It came from a man who carries water and who is right about most things and who has never in his life had to be right about an office. I know that it is useful, and useful is the thing I have been refusing for nine weeks, and a registrar who enters the useful thing is a clerk with a better pen.**"

---

And then the man who said it asked for it to be struck, and he asked in about four seconds, and the request is in the minute and it is the second time in nine weeks that a person has asked for a sentence of their own to be taken out of a public book.

"Take it out," he said. "**I said a thing in a room and I have been in four rooms in nine weeks and I know what a room does, and if that sentence is in a book in four years a stranger is going to read it and think that this district found out what the office was, and we did not find out anything, and I would rather it was not there.**"

And the registrar refused, and the refusal is in the minute and the reason she gives is not the reason she gives every time, and the minute says that.

"**A sentence somebody said out loud is not a finding and it is not nothing. It goes in the column for things that are not findings, and that column is the only place in this book where a person can be wrong out loud and stay wrong, and if I strike this then the only thing left in four years is four gaps in four books and no sentence at all, and in about four years somebody will put the four gaps side by side and that is a system and there is no sentence anywhere on the page to stop him.**"

"**And the answer is not to be struck. Not hers and not yours and not mine.**"

---

And the man of thirty-eight was told about the sentence on the tenth of this month and he did not argue with it and he did not agree with it, and what he said is in the minute in his own words and it is the second of two things he said in that week about limits.

"I said on the twenty-first of last month that I cannot close a transfer to nobody," he said, "**and that is a fact about me and it is in a book, and a man has now stood in a room and said that the reason I cannot do it is that an office existed before the district did, and that is a history and I have been cited as a document for it.**"

"Are you saying it is wrong."

"**I am saying that I do not know whether it is wrong. I am saying that a thing that a man can do is not evidence about a thing that a man cannot do, and that this district has been eaten alive for nine weeks by figures that are evidence about the present being read as evidence about the past, and that I am the figure this week.**"

"Then what do you want done."

"**I want the sentence in the column for things that are not findings with my sentence under it, saying that it is about the present and not about the past, and that a limit of mine is not a history of anybody's.**"

It was done, in nine minutes, in two hands, and the two hands are the registrar's and the tenth hand's, and the entry is under the sentence and not above it, and the registrar was asked whether it should go above and said no and gave a reason in nine words.

Nobody in that room knew what the column for things that are not findings was for, and the clerk of nineteen years was asked on the tenth of this month and answered in about two hours, and the answer is the sixth thing anybody has entered under that heading and it is the longest.

**A THING THAT IS NOT A FINDING AND NOT NOTHING GOES IN THIS COLUMN, AND THE COLUMN HAS HAD SIX THINGS IN IT IN NINE WEEKS. A SENTENCE A PERSON SAID OUT LOUD AND DID NOT WANT. A NUMBER A BOY OF SEVENTEEN COUNTED AND WILL NOT SAY. A QUESTION ASKED BY A STRANGER AT A DOOR. A CORRECTION NOBODY OWED. A SHEET A PERSON TOOK COLD. AND A SENTENCE A MAN WHO CARRIES WATER SAID IN A ROOM AND WOULD LIKE TAKEN OUT.**

"And the reason it exists," she said, "**is that a book of findings only tells a stranger in four years what this district was sure of, and a book of findings and a column of things that were not sure of tells him what this district was prepared to be wrong about, and the second of those is the more useful of the two and it is the one nobody builds.**"

"And you build it."

"**I have entered six things in it in nine weeks and it is the only column in this book that I have ever wanted and it is the one nobody has ever voted to have.**"


"**People read the top of a page.**"

---

And then a fourth person agreed, about eleven minutes later, and then the room did the thing that this district does about seven times a fortnight, which is to start writing the answer.

A clerk of nineteen years took a sheet and put a heading on it, and the heading was four words long, and it was the first time in nine weeks that a sheet in this district had been headed with a sentence about a public office rather than with a date, and she wrote the first line under it and stopped.

She stopped because of what the man of about thirty-one said, and what he said is four words long and it is in the minute.

"**That is a title.**"

"It is a heading."

"**It is a title and a title says the answer is already known and a title is what you print at the top of a board in a yard. I have read a heading like that on three boards in this district and every one of them was wrong, and the wrong one is on a toll board at the east end with a man in it who is not paid.**"

Nobody argued with him and the sheet was not thrown away and was not kept, and the entry of the tenth of this month says that it lies in a folder in the ward market counting room with about nine other sheets that were begun and not finished, and that the folder has no name on it, and that a clerk of nineteen years wrote *not a finding* on the top of the folder in her own hand, and that she would like somebody to ask her why a folder needs a name and she does not know the answer.

And then the seventh search was run, and it was run on the eleventh and the twelfth of this month, and it is the first one anybody has run that was not a search at all.

Four people went into four rooms again and this time they were not looking for anything, and the instruction was one line long and it is in the clerk of nineteen years's hand and it is the whole of the seventh search.

> **GO BACK INTO THE SAME FOUR ROOMS AND FIND THE FIRST THING IN EACH ONE THAT SOMEBODY WROTE DOWN ON PURPOSE ABOUT A PERSON WHO COULD NOT CARRY SOMETHING THEMSELVES.**

They found one in each room, and all four are in books, and all four are about eleven to nineteen years old, and not one of them mentions the office, and the finding of the twelfth of this month is four documents that do the work of an office in four rooms and have never been joined and are not to be joined.

The first is in the roll office and it is nine years old and it is a page of a rate book with a heading on it that a clerk wrote and then stopped writing, and under the heading there are nineteen lines and every one of the nineteen is a person paying a rate for a ground that four thousand and one households are not on, and the column at the foot of the page for the amount is in coppers and the column for the name is in a hand nobody alive uses.

> **PERSON NOT IN A HOUSEHOLD. RATE PAID DIRECTLY. NINETEEN LINES. THE COLUMN AT THE FOOT IS SET AND IT IS THE ONLY FIGURE ON THE PAGE THAT IS IN FIGURES.**

The second is in the water office and it is eleven years old and it is a sheet headed for a person who could not reach a standpipe, and there are six names on it and five of the six are struck and the strike on each of the five is a different hand, and the reason for the strike is written on the sheet in one word each, and the word is *moved*, and nobody in the water office knows what a person moved from.

The third is in the guild room and it is nineteen years old and it is a certificate of a clean lot with a standing line on it that was never filled in, and the standing line is the twenty-eighth of twenty-nine and it has a date under it and a signature over it and no name, and it has been in a drawer for nineteen years behind about four hundred other papers, and the licensed valuer of twelve years found it in about four minutes because he has taken that drawer out before.

The fourth is in the bridge office and it is fourteen years old and it is a standing instruction of one page with a span on it and a clause at the foot about a person who is to be moved off a bank before a flood, and the clause says who moves them and it does not say who says so, and about nine people have read that page in fourteen years and nobody has ever asked about the missing half.

**And the seventh search is the first one in nine weeks that found something, and the something is four documents, and the four documents are all about a person who could not carry a thing, and not one of the four says what a public office was, and the district has now searched seven times and has four gaps, seven gaps, four answers and four documents, and none of them is an answer, and the question in the public lot book dated the fifth of July is still a question, and the entry of the twelfth says that a question gets harder by the number of things that turn out to be near it and not by the number of people who have been asked about it.**

---

And on the evening of the thirteenth of this month a cart came into Alder Reach with two men on it and about nine tons of salt in it, and the man who drives salt got off it, and he had said on the third of this month that the fourth time he came he would bring two men and a cart, and the fourth time was thirteen days later and he had not said what the fifth time was.

He did not come to the counting room. He went to a toll board at the end of a platform where the fourth day of August had put four lines in a column, and he stood in front of it for about four minutes, and then he asked the man who keeps the board a question, and the question is nine words long, and it is in the minute, and the man who keeps the board does not know the answer and wrote it down anyway.

The cart stood in a lane for about an hour and the two men got down and looked at the boards of eleven in the yard of Lot 17 for about twenty minutes, and one of them said afterwards that he has carried salt for about thirty years and has never in his life been in a yard with a book on a wall, and that he stood in front of it for a while because it was the only thing in Alder Reach that had his name nowhere on it.

**And the finding of the thirteenth of this month is that about ten tons of salt came into this district on a cart and stopped, and that the district has four thousand and one households and a rate of fifty coppers a quarter and no instrument that can say where a ton of salt has to go, and that the market has a printed timetable and four toll boards and a question in a public book and no column anywhere for a thing that has arrived.**

"**Who do I write this on, when it is a place.**"

The man who drives salt said it in the ordinary voice and then said the other half of it, and the other half is in the minute and it is nineteen words long, and it is the first thing anybody has said in this district that puts the office question and the toll question in the same mouth.

"**You have got a column on that board that takes a place and a day and an amount, and you have got four lines in it, and I have got about nine tons of salt and no column on any board in this district that takes a ton.**"

"And what do you want."

"**I want to know who signs for it. That is all. I have carried salt for about thirty years and I have never once been asked who signs for a ton, and it has never once occurred to me that that was a question anybody could answer, and it occurred to me about four minutes ago in front of your board.**"

Nobody in that lane answered him, and about nineteen people were on the platform by about six in the evening.

The entry of the thirteenth of August says that a man who has carried salt for thirty years asked the first question anybody in this district has asked about where a thing goes when it arrives, and that the question is not about a rate and is not about a place and is not about an office, and that three people in that lane said afterwards that it is about all three of them, and that the registrar was not present and has not been told yet.

**And the man who keeps the board wrote the question down anyway, in the column, under the four lines, in chalk, on the evening of the thirteenth, and it is the twenty-seventh sentence on that board and the first one that is somebody else's question, and he was asked afterwards whether he had any standing to put another person's question on a board and he said no and said why in nine words.**

"**I have no standing and I have got a piece of chalk and four feet of board and nobody has told me to stop.**"
