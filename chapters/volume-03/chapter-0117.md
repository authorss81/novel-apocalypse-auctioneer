# Chapter 117: Not a Lot You Can Carry

He wrote the terms on the eighth of September in a room over a shop on the second channel in forty minutes, and he wrote them on the back of a page of a ward market minute book because there was no other paper, and the clerk of nineteen years copied them out afterwards in nineteen minutes and the copy is the one that exists, and both of them are in a folder with about nine other sheets that were begun and not finished.

A lot is a thing and a use and a bearer and a toll and a term, and the terms he wrote have all five, and there is one purchaser and one price and one security and one signature at the foot, and the finding of the eighth of September is that **he has written the most complete set of terms this district has ever seen for a thing that cannot be a lot, and that every one of the five is correct.**

> **ONE LOOM. THE THIRD CHANNEL. THE FRAME, THE CHANNEL, AND THE BED OF BRINE. THE PRICE IS TWO THOUSAND COPPERS AND IT IS PAID IN ONE SUM AT THE SIGNING. THE BEARER IS ADRIAN VALE, OF LOT 17, ALDER REACH. THE TOLL IS THE TOLL OF THE LOAM AND IT IS TAKEN AT THE LOAM AND NOT ASKED FOR BY ANYBODY. THE TERM IS ONE SEASON AND THE SEASON IS FROM THE FIRST OF OCTOBER TO THE THIRTY-FIRST OF MARCH. THE RELEASE IS AT THE END OF THE TERM. THE REMEDY IS AN APPEAL TO A WARD MARKET COUNTER.**

The security is on the second page and it is a security and not a payment, and the clause about it was written by the registrar's office in the sixth year of this year for a different thing and was copied out in four seconds and it says that it is inspectable by any person at any time without asking anybody's permission.

> **THE SECURITY IS THE STANDING SALVAGE AND THE SIGNED LEASE AT LOT 17 AND A TWO-SEASON NAMED REPAIR-CREW TERM AND FORTY COPPERS OF CHALK AND OIL. THE SECURITY IS A THING SOMEBODY ELSE CAN GO AND LOOK AT AND IT IS NOT HIS RECOGNITION AND IT IS NOT HIS MARK. IF THE USE IS BROKEN THE SECURITY IS FORFEIT TO THE APPEAL AND IT IS NOT RETURNABLE BY ANYBODY INCLUDING THE MAN WHO POSTED IT.**

Nobody voted on it and nobody argued about it and the minute says nobody argued about it, and says that a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households read both pages in four minutes and then asked one question, and the question is nine words long, and it is the only question anybody asked the document.

"**Who is going to stand in it.**"

And the man of thirty-eight did not answer that on the eighth of September. He answered it on the ninth, in nine seconds, in front of nineteen people, and the answer is in the minute in his own words and it is nineteen words long, and it is the fourth limit he has given in nine weeks and it is different from the other three.

"**I cannot be the bearer, and the reason is not modesty. The toll on this lot falls on the bearer, and the toll is a year of a worker's memory, and my own toll in this business is memory of departure, and I would stand in that channel for a season and come out of it not knowing which way the river goes, and I have one of those left and it is mine, and I am not going to spend it on a bed of brine.**"

"And the price."

"**The price is a figure I did in eleven seconds off the price of salt and I would like it entered that the price is a figure I did in eleven seconds, and that a man who has been an auctioneer for nineteen years does not stop being one because he is thirty-four miles from his own counter.**"

The clerk of nineteen years entered that and entered that the price is not a valuation of a loom and is a valuation of salt with the word loom put on it, and that a licensed valuer of twelve years had sent a letter east on the fourth of September which said in nine words that he could not certify a lot that has no column in it and that he would not travel to say so, and that the letter is in the folder.

**AND ON THE NINTH OF SEPTEMBER A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT ESTABLISHED IN PUBLIC THAT A LOT NEEDS A BEARER AND THAT HE COULD NOT BE ONE, AND THE FINDING OF THE NINTH IS THAT A LOT WITH NO BEARER IS NOT A LOT, AND A THING THAT IS NOT A LOT CANNOT BE SOLD, AND HE HAD WRITTEN THE TERMS BEFORE HE ESTABLISHED THAT.**

Nobody in that room said anything about it for a minute and a half, and then a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said the thing that is now the entry of the ninth of September, and the clerk of nineteen years read it back and corrected one word and it is the seventh time in nine weeks that a person has corrected a document by saying what they needed in it.

"You have got a document that is correct and that cannot be used, and the reason it cannot be used is not in the document."

"**Then where is it.**"

"**It is in the fifth line from the top and it is the only line about a person and it says a name, and a name is not a person, and I have been in nine rooms in nine weeks and six of them were about a name with nothing behind it and I am not going to be the seventh.**"

---

And then a man of thirty-one who is a signalman of the west bank's own crew said no to a thing on the tenth of September and the reason is nine words long and it is the first thing in nine weeks he has refused and he did not have to.

He was going to walk somebody back over the raised plank road on the tenth of September and he said no in four seconds, in front of nineteen people, and the minute says he said it before he was asked, and that he had been asked in the five things on the sixth of August and had said yes in four seconds, and that an answer given in four seconds is an answer and a person may take it back in four seconds and be asked again in four seconds.

"**I will not go back over that road and be the man who walks somebody.**"

"Will you be asked properly."

"**Ask me properly and I will say yes again. That is not a refusal, that is an answer with a date on it, and if I had refused on the sixth of August in four seconds and been asked properly on the seventh I would have said yes, and the reason I am saying no on the tenth and saying yes in four seconds when I am asked again is that I did not know on the sixth of August what I knew on the tenth, and that is what being asked properly means and it is the only thing anybody has ever used that phrase for.**"

He was asked again on the tenth, in the five things, in nine minutes, and he said yes in four seconds, and the minute says he said it in the same four words he has used for a week.

"**I will walk one person.**"

Nobody argued and the entry of the tenth of September says that a man of thirty-one said no in four seconds and then said yes in four seconds on the same day and that both of those are entered and that neither of them cancels the other, and that the man who mends fencing said afterwards that a person who can be asked twice and answer twice differently is the only kind of person this district has ever produced, and that it is the reason a charter can be closed at all.

A man of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was not on either bank for that and has not been asked it and the count of not-askings did not move.

---

The two pages were then read out on the eleventh of September to about nineteen people from nine households on the second channel, and the reading-back was done by a woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years and who is not a clerk and is not paid and has never been asked to do anything in her life, and the clerk of nineteen years asked her whether she would read it back and she said yes in four seconds.

She got through the first page in nine minutes and stopped at the second line of the second page, and the minute says she stopped there and that about four people were waiting and that she was not sorry and that the man of thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that the four minutes of silence were the best four minutes of the week and that he would have given a month for them.

"I cannot read that."

"You read the first page."

"**I read the first page because it is about a frame and a bed and I have stood in both of them. That line is about a person and I do not know what a security is and I have never claimed to and I am not going to pretend that I have read a word of it.**"

The clerk read the second line herself and read it back to the room, and the woman of forty-four listened to it and then said the thing that is now the entry of the eleventh of September, and it is nineteen words long, and it is the sentence the whole of the next three days is made of.

"**You have written what a person gets if this goes wrong, and you have not written what a person gets if it goes right, and the second one is the one that happens.**"

Nobody in that room argued with her and four people who had come thirty-four miles to find out about a document were in the room, and the guild inspector of forty-four stood with his folder under his arm and did not say anything, and the minute says he did not say anything for a minute and a half and then said one sentence and it is nine words long.

"**I would have written both. That is the difference between us.**"

**AND THE FINDING OF THE ELEVENTH OF SEPTEMBER IS THAT A DOCUMENT CAN BE CORRECT IN EVERY LINE AND STILL BE MISSING THE ONLY LINE THAT MATTERS, AND THAT THE MISSING LINE WAS POINTED AT BY A PERSON WHO STANDS IN A CHANNEL AND WHO HAS DONE IT ELEVEN TIMES.**

Then a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households asked the question that nine households on the second channel had all been asked on the west bank in nine weeks, and he asked it in four minutes and it is in the minute in his own words and it is nineteen words long.

"**You have written one purchaser and one price and one security and one signature, and that is what a sale is, and I have been selling salt into a market for two years and I would like somebody on your bank to tell me the sentence that says a thing and a person are not sold together.**"

Nobody in the room could tell him and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that a licensed valuer of twelve years had had it in a document in the fourth month of this year and that it was a certificate of a clean lot with twenty-nine columns and not one of them is a place, and that the whole of what was learned in that room in eleven weeks was that a lot is clean and cannot be sold to a village.

And he said the other half, and the minute has it, and it is the sentence that the eighteenth of September turns on.

"**And the sentence that is missing out here is the same sentence with a person in it. A lot is clean and cannot be sold to a village. A loom is not a lot and cannot be sold to a man. The first one has been in a book since the fourth month. The second one has been in a field since the eleventh of September and it has not got a certificate and nobody is going to be able to make one, and a man who has a form with twenty-nine columns on it has told us so in nine seconds.**"

And then the guild inspector of forty-four did the thing he came to do, and nobody had asked him to and nineteen people were in the room and he said afterwards that he had been waiting two days for a room with about nineteen people in it and had not wanted to make one.

He asked for the six things that hold a lot in this district, and he asked for them in the ordinary voice, and four people on the west bank could say three of them and nobody could say all six, and he wrote them out on the back of a page in four minutes and the six are the thing, the use, the bearer, the toll, the term and the release, and the seventh is the remedy and there are seven and not six, and he said so.

"**A lot has a thing and a use and a bearer and a toll and a term and a release and a remedy, and every one of the seven is a thing you can point at on a piece of paper. And this district has spent nine weeks building six instruments it cannot name and a rate sheet with four thousand and one households on it, and I have come thirty-four miles to be told that a man has written all seven, and the man is right about six of them.**"

"Which is the seventh."

"**There is no seventh. I said there were seven because that is how many there are. And the man has six of them and I do not yet know which one he has got wrong, and I have been in a field for two days and I have a bag and a folder and I would like to find out, and if nobody will help me I will go home on the fourth cart and certify a shed.**"


---

And on the eleventh of September, in the evening, the man of thirty-eight was asked by a man of thirty-four who mends fencing, in four minutes, in front of nineteen people, whether he was going to go on with it, and the asking was in four things because the man who mends fencing had worked out in nine weeks that a thing with four things in it can be asked standing up and a thing with five has to be in a room, and this one was in a room.

"What is the job."

"**Sell a loom.**"

"How long is it."

"**About an hour, and then somebody says no.**"

"What does it cost."

"**A year, and not mine, and the minute says the cost of a refusal is that nobody had anything that was going to come out of it, and that was written on the sixteenth of July about a piece of cast iron and it is the same sentence and I would like it read out.**"

The man who mends fencing read it out, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that the same sentence had now been used twice and that the second use cost nothing and the first use cost nothing, and that a man who uses one true sentence twice in nine weeks is not a man with two ideas.

"Who is standing in the room."

He described it and he took nine seconds over it, and he named the woman of forty-four who had read back the first page and had stopped at the second line, and the man of twenty-six who had said he was the loom, and the man of fifty-two who had said the year is not the seller's, and the guild inspector of forty-four with his folder, and the man of thirty-one who had said no in four seconds and yes in four seconds, and the salt man, and the tenth hand, and the paperer, and himself.

"And what did you leave out."

"**The man of fifty-eight who carries water, because he is not on this bank and he is not on the other one and I did not ask him and that is not asked and I am not going to say his name in a room when he is not in it, because a name in a room where the person is not in the room is a thing you carry to a door.**"

The clerk of nineteen years entered that and entered that it is the fourth time in nine weeks a person has declined to put a name in a public record on the ground that it can be carried to a door, and that the reason is now on a wall in two places and in a book in two hands, and that the count of people who have said that sentence is four and the four of them are not a body and are not to be counted as one.

Then a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households said the thing about the ninth of September, and he said it to the man of thirty-eight and not to the room, and the clerk of nineteen years heard it and entered it and was asked by him afterwards whether she was going to put it in and she said yes and he said he knew.

"**You have written a sale and the sale has no worker in it. Not one line. You have written a price and a security and a term and a release and a remedy, and nineteen people out of nine households on this bank have stood in that frame and been asked whether they would sign it, and you have not asked one of them, and I am not saying that to be hard on you. I am saying that you wrote the seventh thing and it is the only one that cannot be written, and I would like it entered that I said it and that you did not argue.**"

"I am not going to argue," said the man of thirty-eight. "**I am going to be told no by the only person whose no counts, and she is going to be standing in a frame when she says it, and I have known that since the fourth of September and I wrote the terms anyway, and a clerk of nineteen years has entered that I knew, and the entry is under the terms and not above them, because people read the top of a page.**"

The clerk of nineteen years entered the terms of a sale on the back of a ward market minute book, and underneath them, on the same sheet, in a second hand, she entered four lines about a limit of his, and the four lines are in the party of ten's own minute and in four books on the west bank, and the registrar read them on the fifteenth of September and put her own hand under all of them and gave the reason she gives every time.

**AND THE FINDING OF THE ELEVENTH OF SEPTEMBER IS THAT A MAN WROTE THE CORRECT TERMS FOR A THING AND KNEW BEFORE HE WROTE THEM THAT THE SEVENTH OF THE SEVEN COULD NOT BE WRITTEN DOWN, AND THAT A CLERK ENTERED IT UNDER HIM AND NOT ABOVE HIM, AND THAT A PERSON IS NOT A LINE AND A LINE IS NOT A PERSON AND THE DISTRICT HAS SPENT NINE WEEKS LEARNING THAT AND THE FIRST DOCUMENT THAT ASSUMED IT WAS WRITTEN BY A MAN WHO HAS SAID IT OUT LOUD FOUR TIMES.**

And on the tenth of September a man of forty-four arrived at the second channel with a bag and a certificate folder, and he is a guild inspector of sixteen years and he came thirty-four miles because a licensed valuer of twelve years had written to him on the fourth, and the letter said nine words and the nine words were that a lot had been offered for sale and had no column in it.

He has certified about four hundred lots in sixteen years and he has never been asked to certify a person and he said so in nine seconds when the man of thirty-eight asked him what he could do about a loom.

"**I can certify that a thing exists. I can certify that it is where somebody says it is, on a day, with a weather, and I can put a number on it in a column, and I have four hundred of those and not one of them has ever been about whether a person will lose a year.**"

"And you have a form."

"**I have a form with twenty-nine columns on it and it has been the same form since the second year, and not one of the twenty-nine is a place, and I have been saying that in this basin for six years and nineteen people have heard me and one of them wrote it on a wall.**"

Then he said the other half of it, and it is the reason the last three days of September are the shape they are, and it took him half an hour and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**I have a column for a signature. I have a column for a date. I have a column for a place, except that I do not, and I have four columns for two people, which is two columns too many, and I have never in sixteen years needed a fifth because two people have always been the whole of it. And I have not got a column for whether a person was in the room.**"

"And you have never needed one."

"**I have never needed one because a signature means the person was in the room. That is what a signature has always meant, and I have certified four hundred lots on it, and it has been true every time, and a man from a district that has four thousand and one households and a rate sheet has come thirty-four miles and told me about a thing and I would like to know what he is proposing to do with a form that does not have that column.**"

**AND THE FINDING OF THE TENTH OF SEPTEMBER IS THAT A FORM WITH TWENTY-NINE COLUMNS AND FOUR COLUMNS FOR TWO PEOPLE HAS NEVER NEEDED A COLUMN FOR WHETHER A PERSON WAS IN THE ROOM, AND THAT THIS IS NOT A FAULT IN THE FORM, AND THAT IT IS THE ONLY THING THIS DISTRICT HAS FOUND IN NINE WEEKS THAT IS NOT A FAULT IN ANYBODY.**

And the man of thirty-four who mends fencing wrote the last line of that day in his own book and read it out to nobody, and the clerk of nineteen years saw him do it and did not ask him for it and entered that she had not asked, and the entry says that not asking is a thing two people have done in nine weeks and that she does not have a column for it either.
