# Chapter 133: Four Hours on Eleven Boards

---

The filling began at about six in the morning on the twenty-seventh of October on a trestle table in a room with two benches and a table that four people in the room were not sitting at. **The six of them were there and a clerk of nineteen years and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing, and a man of about thirty-eight who had come east with a cart and had not been asked to stay and stayed.**

The page is ruled to thirty-one rows and by four o'clock in the afternoon eleven of them were filled and twenty were ruled and empty **and the clerk of nineteen years entered the twenty and did not call them a failure and did not call them a mystery.**

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that a page ruled to thirty-one was a page that expected to be full. **A sheet gets full, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had said in September that two more years go on a sheet when the season starts and had now been proven right by a piece of paper, and that she did not want the credit for it.**

Nobody argued about the twenty and about four people said out loud that they had expected a number and there was not one. **A man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households said that a number of rows is a number of people, and that the district had never counted its people in frames, and that the twenty ruled rows are the honest part of the page, and that he would rather have twenty honest rows than one made-up figure.**

---

**The sixth column was filled five times in the four hours and it is the only column anybody in that room argued about at the end rather than at the start**, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the sixth column had been the subject of about two hours of argument on the twenty-fourth of October and about nine minutes of argument on the twenty-seventh, and that the nine minutes were about a person and not about a column.

Five people were in the room on the second of October and all five are in the sixth column, and the clerk entered that the sixth column says YES five times and that a column of five identical words is a column and not a finding.

> **THE THIRD ROW. A WOMAN OF THIRTY-EIGHT IN THE SECOND CHANNEL. FOUR SEASONS. THE FIFTH OF SEPTEMBER, THE FIFTH OF MARCH, THE FIFTH OF MARCH, THE FIFTH OF MARCH. WHAT SHE SAID: I AM NOT SAYING IT WAS A GOOD YEAR. WHETHER SHE WAS IN THE ROOM: YES.**

Nobody in the room read that row out to her. **She was not asked about it and she has not seen the page, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not sent a copy, that nobody has offered to send her one, and that the first copy of the ledger went to a stranger in a yard and not to a woman in a channel.**

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that sending her a copy was the first thing anybody should have done and that it was the second thing nobody in this district has learned to do. **The clerk entered that the first thing is to ask and the second thing is to send and that the district has neither, and that this is a claim about the district and not about her.**

---

The fifth column was the one that took the longest and it is the column headed WHAT THEY SAID, and four of the six gave a sentence and two did not, and a clerk of nineteen years wrote the four in their words and not hers and would not let anybody read one of them back to the person it belonged to.

The two who did not give a sentence are in the fifth column as NOTHING SAID **and the clerk of nineteen years entered that NOTHING SAID IS NOT NOTHING HAPPENED, and that she had wanted to put the difference in a column of its own and had not, and that a column for a distinction nobody can check is a column for a clerk's own comfort.**

A man of fifty-six asked her the question and she answered it in thirty-seven words, and a man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper was not in the room and has not been asked about it.

"**A man who is in a frame cannot remember the year and he is not a fool and he is not being difficult. He has nothing to describe and I have a column that wants a description.**"

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that a district which had spent eleven weeks learning that a year is taken out of a person had now spent four hours discovering that a person cannot give an account of the year that was taken, and that those are the same finding from two ends, and that the second one is the worse of the two because the first one had a figure in it.

Nobody argued with that and about four people wrote it down at home, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a sentence had been said in a room and that about four people had carried it out of the room in their heads, and that this is the first time in eleven weeks a sentence has left this flat, and that she did not know what a sentence leaving a flat was and had not asked.

The fourth row is the one that is not a year **and the man it belongs to filled it himself and would not let anybody else near it, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that he came to the table at about the second hour and wrote it and that nobody asked what it said and that he read it out himself in his own words and that nobody counted them.**

> **THE SEVENTH ROW. A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM AND DOES NINE DAYS OF IT IN TEN. WHICH YEAR OF THEIRS: NINE DAYS IN TEN, AND THE TENTH IS UNDER TWO FEET OF WATER AND I HAVE NO BOOTS THAT KEEP IT OUT.**

He said that he is the fourth of the six and that he is going to have to fill this page for as long as there is a page, and that a man who fills a page for years has to be able to write something on it that is true, and that nine days in ten is what he has and that a year is not what he has, and that a clerk of nineteen years had asked him which of the two he wanted written and he had said write both and let the row be ugly.

Nobody called it ugly. **A man of fifty-six said that a ledger with a column headed WHICH YEAR OF THEIRS and a row in it that is not a year is the first document in this basin that has admitted on its face that it does not know, and that this is the opposite of a form with no column on it, and that the two are not joined and are not to be.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF OCTOBER A PAGE WAS RULED TO THIRTY-ONE ROWS AND ELEVEN WERE FILLED IN FOUR HOURS BY SIX PEOPLE AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS, AND THE SEVENTH ROW SAYS NINE DAYS IN TEN AND IS NOT A YEAR, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT IT IS THE FIRST DOCUMENT IN THIS BASIN THAT HAS ADMITTED ON ITS FACE THAT IT DOES NOT KNOW.**

---

And the eleventh row was ruled and empty at four o'clock in the afternoon and it was the only one of the eleven that anybody in the room argued about for longer than nine minutes **and what is in it is a word in the last column and nothing else in any of the other five.**

A woman of thirty-eight in the second channel knows the man of about forty-four who has the second cut, and she has told the room he exists, and she would not enter a figure for him, and when a clerk of nineteen years asked her in the six things she gave the reason in nineteen words and the clerk counted them and got nineteen.

> **I KNOW HOW MANY SEASONS HE HAS DONE AND I AM NOT WRITING IT DOWN BECAUSE HE HAS NOT BEEN ASKED AND A STRANGER READS FIVE COLUMNS AND THINKS A MAN AND HE IS A MAN AND HE IS NOT A MAN WHO HAS BEEN ASKED.**

> **THE ELEVENTH ROW. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WHO HAS THE SECOND CUT. WHICH YEAR OF THEIRS: —. WHEN IT HAPPENED: —. WHAT THEY SAID: —. WHETHER THEY WERE IN THE ROOM: NOT ASKED.**

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing read that row out twice in the room and got it the same both times. **Then he said that it is the only row on the page a stranger who cannot read a figure can read from end to end, and that a row with four empty columns and a word in the sixth is worth more than a row with five figures in it, and that he had been wrong about that in September and had stopped being wrong about it in October.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the row is not a claim and is not a remedy and is not a finding that a man is owed nothing, and that a person entered as not asked is not a person entered as owed nothing, and that the two are different and that the difference is the sixth column.

Nobody asked the man of about forty-four whether he wanted his row there. **A clerk of nineteen years entered that the row was entered on the twenty-seventh of October by a person who was not him about a person who was not in the room, and that not asked is in the count, and that the count is a column and not a bucket, and that the count did not move.**

---

The third column was the one a man of fifty-six tried to total at about half past three in the afternoon, and the clerk of nineteen years would not let him, and the minute says the room went quiet for nine seconds and that four people later said it was the quietest nine seconds of the four hours.

He wanted the sum of the third column entered under the eleven rows.

She refused in seventeen words and then gave a reason in nineteen **and the reason is the standing reason and it did not move, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that he had heard it in September and had not heard a better one since.**

"**A column of years is not a column of money and a year does not go twice.**"

A man of fifty-six said that the twenty-one years in the market's own book is a figure and is against his name until the thirty-first of March, and that the third column of this page is a set of years, and that if the two were the same figure then one of them was wrong, and that the clerk entered that the twenty-one was made in a room by four people on the second of October and that this page was made by six people in four hours and that the two are in two books and there is no rate.

The clerk of nineteen years said that she had wanted to write the word *floor* under the twenty-one for about nine minutes on the second of October and had not, and that the man of fifty-six had said then that it was a floor and not the total, and that a clerk who has been told a thing by a person in a room may put that person's sentence in her own book, and that putting a word of her own under a figure in his is a different thing and is not the same.

The man of fifty-six said that was fair and that he wanted it entered that he had been refused and had not argued, and the clerk entered that, and entered that a man who is refused in a room and does not argue is not a man who agrees, and that the two are not joined.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF OCTOBER THE THIRD COLUMN OF A LEDGER OF YEARS WAS NOT TOTALLED, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX ASKED FOR IT TO BE TOTALLED AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS REFUSED IN FOUR WORDS AND THEN IN NINETEEN, AND THE REASON WAS THAT A COLUMN OF YEARS IS NOT A COLUMN OF MONEY AND A YEAR DOES NOT GO TWICE, AND THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS IN A MARKET'S OWN BOOK AGAINST ONE NAME AND IS NOT THE SUM OF THIS PAGE, AND THE TWO ARE IN TWO BOOKS AND THERE IS NO RATE.**

---

On the twenty-eighth of October the man who prints was asked in his own doorway for a sheet about the ledger, and he is Halloway Reed and he was not on the cart and was not asked to be, and he had the six column headings in a clerk's hand and looked at them for nine seconds and gave the price of two things and refused to give a third.

A sheet about one thing is four coppers and a sheet about two things is twelve coppers **and a ledger with six columns and a heading on it is neither, and he said so and would not invent a price, and the minute says this is the sixth time in eleven weeks he has declined to make a thing and that he has printed five sheets in two months.**

"**I will not put a number on a thing I have never made. You go and hand-copy it and you will do it badly, and the bad copy is the copy the stranger takes, and I am telling you that now and not after.**"

Nobody argued with him. **A man of fifty-six said that a district that has a man who will not invent a price is a district that has one of the five things it needs, and Halloway Reed said that he is not one of the five things and is a man on a tram arch, and the minute says he said it in a doorway and not in a room, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered both halves and did not join them.**

A man of about thirty-eight who deals in second-hand paper on a lane off the lower terrace said that hand-copying a page of eleven rows would take four people about two hours and would be done by people who are tired at the end of the fourth hour, and that he had four copies of one page in his stall and three of them said something different from each other, and that the three had been copied by three different men in three different months.

"And nobody has ever asked a person whether the copy they are holding is the one that was made. The copy is the document. The original has been in a room on a flat since the second of October."

Nobody argued with that either. **A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said the finding of the twenty-eighth of October in twenty-nine words, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down and read it back and it was right.**

"**A copy with a man's name on it is the same instrument until somebody compares the two, and comparing the two is a job nobody in this district has.**"

AND ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF OCTOBER THE MAN WHO PRINTS DECLINED TO PRICE A SHEET ABOUT SIX THINGS **AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WHO DEALS IN SECOND-HAND PAPER SAID THAT THE HAND COPY IS THE COPY THE STRANGER TAKES, AND THAT HE HAS FOUR COPIES OF ONE PAGE IN HIS STALL AND THREE OF THEM DISAGREE WITH EACH OTHER, AND THAT NOBODY IN THIS DISTRICT HAS EVER ASKED A PERSON WHETHER THE COPY THEY ARE HOLDING IS THE ONE THAT WAS MADE.**

---

A seventh line was ruled on the board at the ninth place on the twenty-eighth of October and it is empty, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing ruled it himself and would not say why and was not asked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered twice that she had not asked him and that the reason she had not asked was that the man who ruled a line on a board in this district is not a person who has come to be asked.

**There are still five boards.** The number of instruments this district has built and not named is still six, and the number of things it does not have is still five, and neither of those two numbers moved on the twenty-eighth of October, **because a board is a place a line of chalk goes and not an instrument, and a line that is empty is a line and not an instrument and not a way of saying that a toll lands on a place.**

A woman of fifty who is the water at the ninth place said in a street and not in a room that a board had been used to stop somebody five times since the eighteenth of October, and that she had said four before that and had been asked by nobody to keep a count, and that she was not going to be asked to be right.

A man of thirty-eight said that the count of the four in October was a count in eleven days and that this one is a count in about a month, and that the two are not the same number and are not the same instrument, and that there is no column in this district for a rate of use, and that a column is a thing a person is paid for.

Nobody paid her and she said so and the clerk entered it, and the entry says that a woman of fifty who is the water at the ninth place is not paid, was not asked to be right, and is not going to be asked, and that this is the sixth time the same three things have been entered about her in eleven weeks.


---

On the twenty-ninth of October the page went to the yard of Lot 17 and was copied out by nine hands over two days and one copy went on the board of eleven **and a stranger took it cold at about eleven in the morning with a coat and no bag and read the sixth column standing up.**

A reader at the counter, who is about fifty and was a signal clerk before her shoulder went and is on eleven coppers a quarter for four days a week, read a row out twice to a man she had not seen before. **The rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and the man asked her for the sixth column and she read that out twice as well and did not comment on it.**

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing was in that yard and asked the stranger what he had understood. **The stranger said he had understood that somebody had been in a room on the second of October, and the man of thirty-four said that was exactly the sentence the column was for, and that it was the first time in eleven weeks that a person who came here cold had read one sentence of an instrument and got it right without being told what it meant.**

The stranger said one more thing and it went into the minute and it is not about a place and is not about a name and is not a figure.

"**I have a copy of this. I have taken four copies of things in this yard in two months and I have never once been asked to give one back, and I do not know what a copy of this is for and I am not going to ask.**"

Nobody answered him. **A clerk of nineteen years entered that a stranger had said out loud that he did not know what a document was for, and that this is the fourth time in eleven weeks a stranger has said a thing like it, and that the four are not joined, and that she had wanted to put the four in one line and had not.**

**AND ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF OCTOBER THE LEDGER WENT ON THE BOARD OF ELEVEN IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AND A STRANGER TOOK A COPY COLD AND READ THE SIXTH COLUMN, AND HE SAID HE HAD UNDERSTOOD THAT SOMEBODY HAD BEEN IN A ROOM ON THE SECOND OF OCTOBER, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID THAT WAS THE FIRST TIME IN ELEVEN WEEKS THAT A PERSON WHO CAME TO THIS YARD COLD HAD READ ONE SENTENCE OF AN INSTRUMENT AND GOT IT RIGHT WITHOUT BEING TOLD, AND THE FINDING OF THE PAGE IS THAT THE SIXTH COLUMN HAS NOW BEEN READ BY SOMEBODY WHO WAS NOT ASKED TO READ IT.**

AND THE PAGE IS RULED TO THIRTY-ONE ROWS AND ELEVEN ARE FILLED AND TWENTY ARE RULED AND EMPTY **AND ONE OF THE ELEVEN SAYS A MAN WAS NOT ASKED, AND ONE OF THE ELEVEN IS NOT A YEAR, AND THE TOTAL OF THE ELEVEN IS NOT ENTERED ANYWHERE BECAUSE A COLUMN OF YEARS IS NOT A COLUMN OF MONEY AND A YEAR DOES NOT GO TWICE.**
