# Chapter 110: A Term and Not a Quarter

A man of about thirty-one asked on the fourteenth of this month, at a counter, in the ordinary voice, whether the hundred and eighty-three coppers could be struck out as unpayable, and about nine people were at that counter and the reader read the page out twice and then read the question back, and the room said no, and the reason took about four minutes and it is in the minute in the ward market's own words and it is eleven words long.

"**There is no column for unpayable and I am not inventing one because a man has had an idea in a lane.**"

"And if it is never paid."

"**Then it stays at a hundred and eighty-three for four years and a stranger can read it in four years and ask about it, and that is what a figure in a public book is for, and I have said so for nine years and nobody has ever asked me why a figure is in a book at all.**"

**And the page was read out whole, and it takes about four minutes, and the reading is in the ordinary voice at a counter in front of about forty people, and this is the third time in four weeks that the page has been read out and it is the first time it has been read out by a man who is going to read it out again.**

Two hundred coppers a quarter was collected at the ninth place on the seventh of last month out of a pail of change by a man who does not read figures and who was given the figure and not the reason. Six of it went on lettering. Eleven went on a bound copy of the line book. **A hundred and eighty-three on nothing, and the four coppers for a bushel of white paint are on a line of their own and are not out of the two hundred, and nobody has found out whose they are.**

Then somebody said the other thing, and it was said by a clerk of nineteen years who is the sitting steward of Lot 17, and it took her about eleven minutes to say and it is the whole of what the counter did on the fourteenth of August.

"A figure nobody is chasing is a figure nobody is chasing. It has been in the market's own book for twenty-three days and it has been read out twice and nobody has been named against it and nobody has said that they will look at it. **The entry says the answer is not to be struck and the entry does not say that anybody is going to go and look for the answer, and those are two different sentences and only one of them is true.**"

---

So a man was asked on the fourteenth of this month, in about nine minutes, in five things, standing up, at a counter in front of about thirty people, whether he would hold the figure.

He is a Binder. **He may write a bounded charter, split a use among bearers, close a transfer, make a lot portable or renewable under a stated term, and post a security. He may not maintain a public lot, coordinate more than one bearer, arrange a dispute hearing, or make the consequences of an object visible to a whole settlement, and this district has no public lot, four bearers and no hearing.**

He said yes in about four seconds and then said what it was, and it took about half an hour.

The asking was in the five things and the five things are what the job is, how long it is, what it costs, what happens to the person who does it, and who is standing in the room, and a clerk of nineteen years read the sixth one out afterwards and it is hers and it has no name and it is not on the wall, and it took about nineteen minutes, and this is the third time in nine weeks she has read it out in a room where the person being asked had already said yes.

"What is the job."

"Read a number out loud at a counter on a day."

"How long is it."

"To the thirtieth of September and then somebody decides."

"What does it cost."

"**The last departure he has,**" said the reader at the counter, from about nine feet away, and the room did what it did on the sixteenth of this month and went quiet.

"What happens to the person who does it."

"**He reads a number and about forty people hear a number and about nine of them will go home and do something about it and about thirty-one will go home and forget it, and I do not know which nine and I have not asked and I am not going to.**"

"Who is standing in the room."

He said yes to the first four in about four seconds and he said nothing at all to the fifth, for the third time in nine weeks, and nobody asked him twice, and the minute enters all three of those, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that a man who does not answer the fifth thing three times running has worked out that the fifth thing is the room and has decided not to describe the room to it.

**And the minute is in his own words and it is the first entry in nine weeks written by him about himself that is not a limit.**

"**I cannot make the payment. I cannot close a transfer to a person I cannot name and I said so in a room on the twenty-first of last month and about nine people wrote it down. What I can do is say the number out loud at a counter on a day, and if it has not moved by the day after, say it again, and keep saying it until it moves or until somebody tells me to stop.**"

"And that is a job."

"**That is a responsibility. A job can be refused and this one can be taken away from me in a sentence by anybody in this room, and the difference is the whole of what I have got and I would like it written in those words.**"

He was asked what it cost and he said the figure, and the figure is one departure and the last one, and about thirty people in that room had been told the figure in a public yard on the sixteenth of this month and had come to see whether anybody would put a number on the other side of it.

He was asked what happened to the person who did it, and he said it in nine words, and the nine words are the reason this is a responsibility and not a post.

"**Nobody is sent to look for anything. I am not going to go up a bank and knock on a door. I am going to stand at a counter and read a number, and a man who reads a number is not a man anybody can be sent to a place.**"

---

And the term of it is the thirtieth of September, and it is a term and not a quarter, and the difference was put in the minute by the clerk of nineteen years and not by him.

> **MR VALE WILL READ OUT THE FIGURE OF ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THREE COPPERS AT THE WARD MARKET COUNTER ONCE IN EVERY QUARTER UNTIL IT MOVES. THE TERM IS TO THE THIRTIETH OF SEPTEMBER AND THE TERM IS A TERM AND NOT A QUARTER, BECAUSE A QUARTER COMES ROUND AGAIN BY ITSELF AND A TERM DOES NOT. ANYBODY IN THE ROOM MAY END IT IN A SENTENCE AND NONE OF THEM NEEDS HIS PERMISSION. THE ANSWER IS NOT TO BE STRUCK AND THE FIGURE IS NOT TO BE STRUCK WHETHER OR NOT.**

Nobody voted on it and the minute says nobody voted on it, and that four people asked whether it should have gone to the market, and the clerk said no, and the reason she gave is in the minute and it is nineteen words long.

And the man who carries water asked one question in the middle of it, and it is the only question he asked in about two hours, and he asked it from the back of the room, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down and did not answer it, and the entry of the fourteenth of August says she did not answer it and that she was asked afterwards why and gave a reason in nine words.

"**Because he asked it in a room with forty people in it and he has been in a room with forty people in it twice this month and both times he has said a thing that was in the wrong mouth, and he is fifty-eight and he has not got a practice at it, and I am not going to hand him a sentence in front of them.**"

"What was the question."

"**He asked whether the man who read the number out would still be here in October. And I have written the question down and I have not written the answer, and in about four years a stranger will read the question and not the answer and will decide that the man walked away, and that is the correct condition for it to be in.**"

And the finding of the fourteenth of August is that a person in a public book is now something that happens to a man about four times a year, and that a man who has been in this district for nineteen years has taken three of them in a fortnight, and that the district has no form for the fourth kind of thing, which is a man who carries something of his own every day and nobody in this district has been shown where to put it down.

"**The market funds what is written. He is not asking for money. He is asking for a date, and a date is not a rate, and I have never seen a market pay for a date and I do not want to start.**"

---

And the reading ledger was read out at the ward market counter on the afternoon of the fourteenth of this month after the figure, and it is one for April, none for May, one for the twenty-ninth of last month, and the number of departures left is one, and the refusals are six.

Ivo Stenn's separate sheet was read out after the ledger, in his own hand, in the ordinary voice, and it is nineteen words long and it has been read out after every one of the six.

"The intervals go up on use and I will not defend it and I will not call it anything else."

A woman at the back of the counter asked him what intervals, and he read the ledger out again, and she said that she had understood the first time and had wanted to hear it twice, and he said that was the rule of the counter and not his own, and she said she knew it was the rule of the counter and had wanted to hear it twice anyway, and about nineteen people at that counter said afterwards that it was the first time in nine weeks that anybody had asked for a number to be read twice for no reason at all, and that the counter read it twice, and that the rule and the reason were kept apart on the page.

**And then a man of about thirty-one said the thing that about four people in that room had been thinking since the fourteenth of July and that nobody had said, and it took him about nine seconds, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is nineteen words long.**

"**He has agreed to read a number he cannot move, at a counter, four times, at a cost of one departure, and nobody has asked him what happens on the first of October and neither have I.**"

Nobody in that room answered it and the minute enters that nobody answered it, and that the man who said it said afterwards that he had not asked it in order to be answered and that he had asked it because four people were going to walk out of that counter with the same wrong picture and one of them was himself.

And on the morning of the fifteenth of this month at about a quarter to seven, a slate went past in a kitchen under the Ward Nine tram line, and neither of them said a word, and about nine minutes afterwards a reader at the ward market counter wrote down that seven people have now seen that kitchen and that she is still not going to ask, **because the answer would be in a room and not in a book.**

And not asked is in the count, and the count of not-askings about a woman of forty-five stands at four with one standing refusal, and she was not asked for anything on the fifteenth of August and nobody has written down that she was on no roll in nine years.

Nobody in that kitchen said anything about a figure or a rate or a quarter, and the entry of the fifteenth of August says that, and that a clerk of nineteen years was not in that kitchen and does not know what was said in it and has not asked and will not.

---

And the twenty-ninth of this month is the date this batch ran on and it is gone, and the count of what came back and what did not is four figures and the four figures are in the public lot book on the thirty-first of July in the tenth hand's hand, and the four are a count and not a total, because a household is a count and a person is a count and the two are not added anywhere in this district's books.

One: the security ended at midnight and nobody stood on the platform the next morning.

Two: the three weeks ran out and one of them ran out with nobody in it, and one of the three has offered to take the following week and be asked properly, and being asked properly has still not happened.

Three: the boundary was asked a fifth time from two rooms and both of them said yes in the same two sentences and in the same order, and the room has not agreed to end anything.

Four: the man who comes twice a month came, and stood in a frame for about an hour, and did not put his hand on the lever, and the entry in his own book is the same four words he has written about nine hundred times.

And the fifth figure is not a count of anything, and the four figures are not the whole of what ended, and the finding of the thirty-first of July is that four things ended on one day and one of them was a man, and a man does not end: he either comes or he does not.

And the twenty-ninth of July is the first day in the history of this instrument on which nothing was decided by anybody and everything ended anyway, and about eleven people wrote that down in four rooms in nine hours, and the four copies are not joined.

**And a man does not end. He either comes or he does not, and the entry of the thirty-first of July says that this district spent four months building an instrument whose loudest feature was a man who could stop it, and that on the twenty-ninth of July the instrument did not need him, and that nobody has yet worked out whether that is a triumph or a hole in the design.**

The one departure in a hundred and forty-two is still a hundred and forty out of a hundred and forty-two, and the keeper of the book of departures is fifty-one and has been eleven feet above that flood line for a year, and she was asked in August whether the number had moved and said no and said why in about four seconds.

"**It moves when a train moves. There has not been a train since the twenty-ninth of last month, and if you want the fraction to get smaller then somebody has to run one, and I am not going to be the one to suggest it in a room with the number on the wall.**"

---

And on the afternoon of the sixteenth of this month at about half past four the figure was read out at the ward market counter for the first time by the man who had asked to hold it, and the reader at the counter is about fifty and is a signal clerk before her shoulder went and is on eleven coppers a quarter for four days a week.

About forty people stood there and it took about nine minutes and the number was the same number.

Then a stranger asked him a question at the door on his way out, and the stranger had taken a sheet cold off a board of eleven in the yard of Lot 17 on about the eleventh of this month and had come back with it, and the question is four words long.

"What are you worth."

And he answered it in about eleven seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years was standing there and wrote it down, and the minute says she wrote it down and that she underlined one line of it, and that nobody has ever asked her which line and she has never said.

"**I do not know, and I am not going to tell you, and if I told you a number you would believe it, and I have spent nine weeks finding out that a number a person believes is the most expensive thing this district has ever handed out.**"

"And if I asked you what you do."

"**I read a number out loud on a day and I am holding it until the thirtieth of September, and on the first of October somebody else will do it or nobody will, and either of those is fine and I would like it written down that I said fine.**"

Nobody in that lane argued and the entry of the sixteenth of August says that nobody argued, and that the stranger took his sheet and went away, and that about four people at that counter said afterwards that the answer to the first question was worth about four coppers to print, and that the answer to the second one was the whole of the term, and that the man who said it heard both of those and wrote neither down.

---

**And the eighth of this month is where the fifth instrument is, and it is still unnamed and it is still unowned, and it has four lines in a column on a board at the end of a platform and the same four lines in chalk on a board two miles west at a place where a train does not stop, and a fifth place has been promised, and the man who keeps the first of the two boards is not paid and does not own the column and has said that a thing nobody owns is the only kind of thing that works.**

The train on that siding had stood a hundred and eighty-four days on the fourteenth of this month and a hundred and eighty-six on the sixteenth, and the five dates in a boy's index have no names against them, and the fifth of them was the twenty-ninth of last month and it is three weeks behind, and the boy of seventeen has written a week down properly in a room once and has asked to be asked again.

And the question in the public lot book is dated the fifth of July and it is still a question, and it is now harder than it was on the fifth of July by four gaps in four books and seven copies of the same gap and four answers and four documents about a person who could not carry a thing and one sentence in a column for things that are not findings, said out loud by a man who carries water, who asked for it to be taken out, and which was not taken out.

The seven searches are in the book in order and they are four lines each and there is no room in any of them for an answer.

One was in May and it was for the name and about two hours and four people, and a fifth person found nothing.

Two was in the middle of May and it was for the name again and a man of about fifty-six off the lower terrace, and it failed.

Three was at the end of May and it was for the name and a clerk of the ward market, and it failed.

Four was on the fourth of this month and it was for the function and eleven trades, and no two of them wrote the same sentence.

Five was on the fourteenth of last month and it was for the work, and it found four gaps in four books in four rooms.

Six was on the eleventh of this month and it looked for the first thing written on purpose about a person who could not carry something, and it found four documents and none of them mentions the office.

The seventh is not a search and it is on the ninth of this month.

**And the seventh was not a search. It was a sentence, said out loud by a man who carries water at the ninth place, in a room, in about four seconds, and it is the closest anybody in this district has come, and it is in a column for things that are not findings, and the man asked for it to be struck and was refused, and the refusal is the eleventh the registrar has given and the reason she gives is the reason she gives every time except once.**

And nobody is to answer it in a guess, and a guess entered in a public book is a thing a stranger can take cold in four years, and this district has had enough of those, and the entry that says so is the ninth page this district has written for the purpose of refusing to know something, and it is the one page in nine weeks that nobody has argued about.****
