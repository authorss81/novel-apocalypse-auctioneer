# Chapter 106: The Twenty-Ninth of July

The iron was in the bracket at nine in the morning on the twenty-ninth of this month and it was still in the bracket at midnight, and nobody threw the points, and nobody put a hand on a lever, and the train on that siding had stood a hundred and sixty-eight days, and nine of those days had been in a charter and the hundred and sixty-eighth was the first of the ones after it.

The term ran out at midnight on the twenty-eighth. Nobody extended it and nobody shortened it and nobody voted on it, and the four people who can revoke the whole of a charter in a sentence did not use it, and the finding of the twenty-ninth of this month is that **an instrument can end without anybody standing up.**

---

Nobody had told the keeper of the fourth toll board what to do with the column, and he did it himself, and it took about four minutes, and it is the twenty-sixth sentence on that board and it is in chalk.

**THE COLUMN HAS THREE THINGS IN IT AND NOT ONE OF THEM IS A FIGURE AND THEY WENT IN FOUR WEEKS AGO AND THEY ARE STILL THERE, AND ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JULY A TERM ENDED AND NOBODY PUT A FOURTH THING IN IT, AND I WOULD LIKE IT NOTICED THAT A BOARD DOES NOT GO BLANK BECAUSE THE ARGUMENT IS OVER.**

The sign-writer of sixty-one was in Alder Reach on the twenty-eighth and would not come up the bank, and he said why in about four seconds at a rail, and the reason is that a man who comes twice a month does not come twice a month and four days in one, and that is his arrangement and it is nineteen years old.

A bridge administrator of nine years was at the same rail on the twenty-eighth, on a published day, with about nineteen people, and he said the sentence he says on published days and added one sentence after it, and the added sentence is the first thing he has ever added after it and it is in the minute.

"That is not enough. **And I am not going up the bank on the twenty-ninth, and the reason is that I have not been asked, and the reason I have not been asked is that I would be the sixth person on a bank that has four people on it, and I have been the man who makes this place unsafe for nine years by being in it with a schedule.**"

"**And I would like it entered that I said no to a thing nobody offered me, because in about four years somebody will read that page and see a man who was not there, and there is going to be a question about why, and I would like the why to be in my own hand.**"

The minute says that nobody at that rail said anything at all, and that about four people understood it, and that the man who asked the boundary a fifth month on the twenty-ninth was not him and was never going to be him.

---

The woman of the column was asked from a counting room over a chandler's shop on the twenty-ninth of this month at about ten in the morning, and the asking was read out in the ordinary voice by a man of about thirty-eight who buys old paper off four houses once a month, and the answer came back the same day and it is in the woman's own hand on a sheet that had been up the bank and down it again in one day.

> I DO NOT LIKE IT, AND I SAY YES.

The keeper of the only pair was asked from a yard on the tide on the twenty-ninth of this month at about ten in the morning, and the answer was given in the same order she has given it in since the twenty-ninth of March: the condition, then the word, then the yes.

Both answers were entered on the twenty-ninth of this month in two books nine hours apart by two people who were not in the same room, and the two entries are not joined to each other, and the registrar was asked to keep them so and agreed and gave the reason she gives every time, and **that reason is now eight refusals and one of them is about two answers to the same question in the same week.**

The term of the boundary at the west end now runs from the twenty-ninth of this month to the twenty-ninth of August. It is on the notice, on three toll boards, on the ninth place's own board's bottom line, and in a public book, and it is two named people and a stated term and a published entry and a hole in the ground that is not a mark.

**And a boundary held by two people who are each asked from a different room is a boundary either room can end, and the counting room has not agreed to end anything, and the yard on the tide has not agreed to end anything, and the finding of the twenty-ninth of this month is that an instrument which either room can end is an instrument that neither room has to.**

Nobody voted on that either.

**And the finding of the twenty-ninth of this month is that an instrument which either room can end is an instrument that neither room has to, and that the two rooms entered it without voting on it in the same way they entered the asking without voting on it, and that a thing entered without a vote is the only kind of thing this district has never had to defend.**

The minute of the twenty-ninth is four lines long and it is in the public lot book in the tenth hand's hand and it is the shortest entry this book has carried since the eighth of March.

> **THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JULY. THE TERM ENDED AT MIDNIGHT. THE SECURITY ENDED AT MIDNIGHT. THE USE DID NOT RUN. THE IRON IS IN THE BRACKET. THE MAN WHO ENDS THE USE CAME TWICE A MONTH AND DID NOT HAVE TO.**
>
> **THE FOUR PEOPLE WHO CAN REVOKE THE WHOLE OF THIS CHARTER IN A SENTENCE WERE NOT ASKED WHETHER THEY WISHED TO AND NONE OF THEM WAS ASKED, AND NOT ASKED IS ENTERED AS NOT ASKED AND NOT AS A REFUSAL, AND THE RECORD SAYS THAT A PERSON WHO HAS NOT BEEN ASKED HAS NOT REFUSED AND CANNOT BE COUNTED EITHER WAY.**

The tenth hand asked for one thing to be added to it before it went in, and the addition is in his own hand and it is on the same line.

"**Put that the rotation turned on the second of this month for the first time under a closed charter and that it turned because a week ran out and nobody had to be given a day to make it turn, and put it in the entry about the twenty-ninth, because in four years a stranger will read the twenty-ninth and want to know whether the thing turned and the answer is that the turning was the fifteenth of last month and the ending was this.**"

---

And the security ended at midnight on the twenty-eighth of this month, and it is a day a week at the ninth place from the first hour of the morning until the hour a train is due to move, and three bound copies of a line book at eleven coppers each, thirty-three coppers, out of a man's own hand, and it was inspectable by any person at any time without asking anybody's permission, and on the twenty-ninth of this month at about eleven in the morning a man did not stand on the platform.

He had told nobody he was not going to. **A man of about fifty who keeps the board asked him why, standing at the bottom of thirty feet of board, and he said the reason in nine words and the nine words are in the minute.**

"**It ended at midnight and I would have been a habit.**"

"And you have kept a day a week for about a month."

"**I have kept it for a month and it was the last day of a term and there was no train due to move, and if I had stood on that platform at eleven o'clock this morning then in about four years a man would write that the platform had a man on it, and he would be right, and the man on it would have been a man keeping a habit for a train that was not going, and I would rather not be the first man in this district who is a habit.**"

Nobody argued with him and the minute enters that nobody argued with him and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that a security which is spent on the thing it secures is not a security any more, and that a security which ends on the same day as the thing it secures was never a security at all, and that the entry of the twenty-ninth is the first one in nine weeks that is about a security that stopped.

And the thirty-three coppers of the three bound copies of the line book are on three shelves nine hours apart, and the copies are still on the shelves, and nobody has taken them back, and a clerk of nineteen years asked on the thirtieth whether they should be, and was told no by about nine people in four minutes, and the finding is that a security is a thing somebody else can go and look at, and that a thing somebody else can go and look at does not need the man who posted it to agree.

---

And at about four in the afternoon on the twenty-ninth of this month, at the twelfth place two miles west, a man of sixty-one stood in a frame with a lever in it and did nothing for about an hour.

He had come at his own cost and the cost is two days a month in somebody else minding a pumping engine, and he had been coming twice a month for nineteen years and the twenty-ninth was one of the two. He had a book in his coat with a rubber band on it and the band had perished and the book had to be held shut with a thumb, and in it, on about nine hundred lines, four words appear and nothing else.

Nobody threw the points. The nine vehicles stood where they had stood for a hundred and sixty-eight days and the frame was at the west end of a loop that had not been used and the water was four feet below the rail and falling.

He did not put his hand on the lever. The minute of the twenty-ninth says that he did not, and it says that he wrote the entry himself afterwards in his own book, and the entry is four words and a date, and the four words are the same four words he has written nine hundred times, and it is the only one of the nine hundred that has a date on it that is not the day it was written on.

FRAME TURNED. NOTHING SENT.

"I have never written that one," he said afterwards, at about six in the evening, to about nineteen people who had come two miles to ask him about it. "**I have got nine hundred lines and I have never once written that the frame did not turn, and I did not write it tonight either, and the reason I did not is that the frame did not turn because nobody asked it to, and a frame that nobody asked to do a thing has not done nothing.**"

Somebody in that group asked him what the standing was for, and he gave the ninth thing anybody has asked him for and it is in the minute.

"**The standing is for the day it is needed. There has not been one.**"

---

And they were in the same room for about half an hour and they had never met, and the man of sixty-one did not come two miles to meet anybody and had come two miles because he comes twice a month, and the man of thirty-eight had not come and was there because a room asked him whether he wanted to be there.

He had said in a counting room on the eighteenth of last month that he would have said no if he had been asked, and he was asked on the twenty-ninth of this month, and the asking was in five things in about half an hour in a front room, and he was asked what the job was and how long it is and what it costs and what happens to the person who does it and who is standing in the room, and on the fourth of the five he answered.

"It costs nothing," he said, "**because the reading is not being done. The cost is in March and it was a direction and in April it was a gate and on the twenty-ninth of last month it was a walk I did not finish, and there is one of them left and I am not spending it on this.**"

The asking went to the fifth thing and he said nothing at all, and the man who asked did not ask him twice, and the minute enters both of those, and the reason the man did not ask him twice is in the minute in his own words and it is nine words long.

"**He has said no six times since March and he has never once said no to a person.**"

---

And then the man of sixty-one asked him a question, and it was the first question anybody had asked Elias Rooke in a fortnight and it was not one the room knew about, and it is in the minute in Rooke's own words and it is eleven words long.

"**On the eighteenth of last month you said you would have said no. Would you.**"

And Adrian said no, and the room waited, and the room got about four seconds, and then he said the answer, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is not in any book anywhere else.

"**I would have said no because I have said no six times and I did not like saying it and I wanted it to stop being something I had to say, and a man of sixty-one who has come two miles twice a month for nineteen years is not a man I would have said no to, and I said it before I had met him, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

"Did you meet me."

"No."

"So you said no to a man you had not met."

"**I said no to a job I had been asked about by nobody, and the man in the job was not in it, and I have been wrong about that for about six weeks and a person corrected me about it in about nine seconds, and I would like that on the page in the place where I am wrong and not at the bottom where I am right.**"

Nobody in that room said anything about it being a moment. A clerk wrote it down and underlined one line of it and nobody has ever asked her which line and she has never said.

The room did not go home then either. About nine people stayed, and a man of about fifty-one who keeps the only pair was one of them, and she had come down the bank two days before for the asking and had come down again, and she was asked by the tenth hand whether she would go back up, and she said she would stay the night, and the reason she gave is in the minute and it is about a bed and a stove and is nine words long.

"**There is a stove in that hut and there is about nineteen people on this bank and a night in the rain is worse than a walk.**"

And the man of thirty-one, who is a signalman of the district's own crew and who holds the third of the three weeks, was not on the bank at all on the twenty-ninth of this month, and his week ran out at midnight on the twenty-eighth with nobody in it, and the entry of the twenty-ninth says that about four people in two rooms thought about that on the twenty-ninth and that one of them has already said he will take the following week and be asked properly, and that being asked properly has still not happened, and that not asked is entered as not asked.

Then the man of sixty-one said the thing that is the reason for the whole of the afternoon, and it took him about eleven seconds, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is nine words long.

"**You were not wrong. You just had not been asked.**"

And the room entered that as the whole of it, and the registrar entered that it is the eighth refusal and that the reason she gives is that a man who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the record says **not asked**, and that it said not asked on the twenty-ninth of this month about a question nobody put.

---

The night came and about nineteen people walked down that bank, and the lamp at the ninth place was lit by whoever was standing on the platform, and the day was the day, and the day was written in chalk, and a chalk line is not a mark, and the man who lights lamps in this district has a standing about a day of the week that nobody in this district can check against anybody else and he did not raise it.

The fourth place got nothing, and about four people had walked two miles west in the rain to find that out, and the finding of the twenty-ninth is that a station with nobody at it can be a thing that does not happen, and that the sheet at that place still says in its own type that a train stops there once a month in the dark, and that the twenty-ninth of this month was the month and there was no train and nobody has printed a correction, because a correction is owed by a road and the road was not open and there is no road to owe it.

"There is nothing to correct," said the man who sells nothing and has a use, on the thirtieth, in a yard. "**A correction is owed by a road. There was no road, so there is no correction, and the sheet is now wrong in about four hundred places and there is nobody who owes anybody anything, and in about four years somebody will take that sheet cold and think that this railway runs in the dark once a month.**"

"Can you print one."

"I can print one and I will not print one," he said, "**because a correction that says nothing ran is a correction about a railway and not about a promise, and this district has spent nine weeks learning the difference and I am not going to be the one who teaches it the other way in August.**"

Nobody argued with him and the entry says nobody argued with him, and that the sheet is still on the wall at the fourth place, and the finding of the thirtieth of this month is that a document which has become wrong for a reason nobody caused is a document that nobody owns.

**And the finding is also that the thing just made fits in neither of the two lists this district keeps, and that this is the first time in nine weeks that has happened. There are four things the district does not have, and they are on a wall in a counting room under a heading that says NOT ANSWERED, and this is not a fifth of them. And there are six instruments the district has built and has not named, and a rehearsal and a platform and a rail and a correction and a question and a security, and this is not a seventh of them either.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that in about four minutes and asked for it to be checked twice, and it was checked twice, and the registrar said the two lists are not the same list and the number five belongs to the first of them and not the second, which is the finding of the fourth of this month, and which a stranger can now find on two pages of a public book if they want it.

The iron was in the bracket. The nine vehicles were on the siding. The line book at the east end has nine hundred and six movements in it and one more, and the one more is the twenty-ninth of last month, and there is nothing in it for the twenty-ninth of this month because nothing moved.

**And the term of a charter ran out on the twenty-ninth of July and a security stopped on the same day and a boundary was asked a fifth time from two rooms and a man stood in a frame for an hour and did nothing, and none of those four things was voted on by anybody, and the finding of the twenty-ninth of this month is that this district has four instruments that end on a day and not one of them needed a room to end it, and that a district which spent nine weeks learning how to ask a person has just discovered that it does not know how to say no, and has not yet worked out which of those two is the more dangerous.**

The twenty-ninth of this month is over. The next one is the first of August, and it is the first day in about four months on which there is no charter, no security and no use, and the brass is in the bracket, and the board is in Alder Reach with three things in a column and no fourth, and nobody in this district has yet said the word *waiting* out loud in a book.
