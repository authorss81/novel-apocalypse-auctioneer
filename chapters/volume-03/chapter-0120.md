# Chapter 120: The Thing That Fails


---

And before any of that, on the morning of the nineteenth of September, the party of ten read out what they had, in a room over a shop on the second channel, and it took an hour and a half, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing read it out and not the clerk, and the clerk of nineteen years said afterwards that she had offered and that he had said no in four seconds and that she had let him.

"You have written it four times since the fourth and I have read it out four times and I am not going to be the fifth person to say those words in a room."

"It is your minute."

"**It is my minute and the party of ten put me in it on the second of September because a clerk of nineteen years is the only person on that list who can read a figure out, and I have been wrong about that inside a week, and I am not saying so in a book with nineteen people in it, and I am saying it here, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing has written it down and the clerk of nineteen years did not.**"

Then the reading-back was done by the woman of forty-four who has done it eleven years, and she read both the nineteen lines and the two certificates, and she got through them in nine minutes and stopped once, and the minute says she stopped once and that about four people were waiting and that this time nobody said the four minutes were good.

"I cannot read that column."

"Which column."

"**The one with the four names in it. I can read a name. I cannot read what a name is worth and there is a figure under the four names and I have stood in a channel for eleven years and I know what a year is worth and I am not going to read a figure next to it in a room and pretend the two are the same size.**"

The guild inspector of forty-four said in four seconds that she was right and that the figure was his own, and that he had put it there because a form asks for a figure, and that a form which asks for a figure beside a name is a form that will eventually be read by somebody who does not know what either of them is.

"Then take it out."

"**I cannot take it out. There is a figure in the column and the column is the column. I could certify a second time without the figure and it would be valid and it would be the third piece of paper in a drawer about the same sheet, and I have decided that I would rather have one bad piece of paper in the world than three good ones in a drawer, and I would like that in the minute in my own words.**"

And it was, and the clerk of nineteen years read it back and got nothing wrong, and **the minute says the nineteenth of September is the first day in nine weeks that a person read a document back and the clerk was not the one reading it, and the clerk got nothing wrong.**

---

And on the nineteenth of September the reading ledger was read out on the far bank in the ordinary voice by the man who keeps it, which is Ivo Stenn, and it is one for April, none for May, one for the twenty-ninth of June, and one for the sixth of September, and the number of departures left is one, and there is no date in the ledger for the next one.

The sixth of September is the seventh refusal and it is in the ledger as a line with nothing against it, and the cost of a refusal is no cost and no time. About nine people from households on this bank were in the room and four of them did not know what a reading was, and one of them asked, and was answered in four seconds by a man who has been a clerk of a market for six years.

**He said that a reading is a thing a man does to a piece of metal, and that on this bank there is no piece of metal that anybody can read, and that this is a fact about the bank and not a want, and that a district which has spent nine weeks learning to ask a person is going to have to learn to ask a river as well, and that nobody has worked out how.**

Then Stenn read out his own sheet, which is nineteen words long, and which has been read out after every one of the seven refusals, and which he has refused to defend since the seventeenth of March. Four people on this bank had never heard it before the nineteenth of September and two of them wrote it down. A man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households asked him what the intervals were, and he read the ledger out again, and said that was the rule of the counter and not his own, and the man said he knew it was the rule of the counter and had wanted to hear it twice.

Nobody in that room asked him anything else and four people who had come thirty-four miles heard the intervals go up for the first time and the clerk of nineteen years wrote them down in her own hand and entered that she had heard a number she had been reading for nine weeks and had never once been told what it was about.

**AND ON THE NINETEENTH OF SEPTEMBER THE INTERVALS WENT UP AGAIN, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED WHY, AND IVO STENN GAVE THE REASON IN NINETEEN WORDS, AND THE FINDING IS THAT THE INTERVALS HAVE GONE UP ON USE AND HE WILL NOT DEFEND THAT AND WILL NOT CALL IT ANYTHING ELSE, AND IT IS THE FIRST TIME IN NINE WEEKS THAT THE INTERVALS WENT UP ON A REFUSAL THAT WAS ABOUT SOMEBODY ELSE'S COST.**

---



The registrar said on the twentieth of September, in a counting room over a chandler's shop, in front of nineteen people, that her own instrument had failed, and it took her nine minutes, and nobody in that room argued with her, and the minute says nobody argued with her and says that a man of thirty-four who mends fencing had written down before she started that he was going to write down everything she said, and had not written anything.

**THE FINDING OF THE TWENTIETH OF SEPTEMBER IS THAT THE ONLY INSTRUMENT THIS DISTRICT HAS BUILT THAT ANYBODY CAN CARRY TO A DOOR IS A BOOK ON ELEVEN BOARDS, AND THAT IT WORKS BY BEING READ BY SOMEBODY WHO HAS COME TO THE YARD, AND THAT THE ONLY THING IN NINE WEEKS THAT NOBODY COULD CARRY IT AGAINST WAS A PIECE OF PAPER IN A DRAWER THIRTY-FOUR MILES AWAY.**

She put it in three parts and the three parts are in the minute in her own hand and the clerk of nineteen years read them back and did not correct a word, and that is the first time in nine weeks that the clerk has read the registrar back without correcting anything.

"The first part is that I cannot strike the certificate, and I am going to say it in the plainest words I have, and I have said eleven things in this district that were plainer and I have never once said them in a room with nineteen people in it, and I said that on the sixteenth and I am not going to soften it now that I have had four days to think about it. I am a person with a set of powers. None of them is a strike of another body's instrument, and I have said so in a room thirty-four miles from here by a man who cannot do it either, and two people who cannot do a thing are not a thing that can be done."

"The second part."

"The second part is worse and it took four days. **My instrument is a book.** It is nine copies on eleven boards in a yard and nineteen people can read it and a stranger can take a sheet off it cold in a minute, and I have spent nine weeks treating that as the strongest thing in this district, and it is the strongest thing in this district and it is a thing that only works if a person comes to the yard. A mark on the iron does not hold places. A form with no column is a form that cannot be refused. And a book that a stranger can read cannot make a thing untrue in a room he has never been in, and I have known that since I was twenty-six, and I have never once had to say it out loud, because in nine years in this basin nothing has ever been a threat to anybody. A certificate is the first thing anybody has put in a drawer in this district in my time and it is thirty-four miles away and it is about two pans of salt."

A man of thirty-eight was at the back of that room, and had come out of the far country in the night after the last thing he did there and had walked in from the ninth mile, and had not been asked to be in the room and was in it. The minute says he was at the back, and that nobody asked him to speak, and that he did not speak until about half way through the second part.

"The third part."

"The third part is that I have spent nine weeks being the only person in this district who can say no, and the reason a registrar is a thing at all is that she can stop a thing, and **on the sixteenth of September I found out that being the only person who can say no is not the same as being able to stop a thing.** I have been the first half of that for nine weeks and did not know there was a second half, and I want it entered that I did not know, and that nobody in this room is to go about telling people I knew and said nothing, because I did not know and I would have said it."

And then the man of thirty-eight said the only thing he said in that room, and it is thirty words long, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down and underlined one line of it and nobody has asked her which line and she has never said.

"**You have not failed, and a person who is told their instrument failed in a room with nineteen people in it has been told a thing that is not true.**"

"You have a counter-book about that."

"**I do. I entered your nine minutes under the column for things that are not findings, and I entered them there because you are not wrong out loud, you are right, and the column is for people who are wrong, and I have been asked to keep them apart for nine weeks and I am going to say this once and then I am going to stop saying it.**"

Nobody in that room argued and the registrar said yes, and the minute says she said yes in four seconds, and that **a woman of fifty-one who keeps a stall on the lower terrace, who supplies the Harrow engine's oil out of her own till, said nothing at all and has asked twice that it be said that nobody asked her either, and that nobody asked her on the twentieth of September either,** and says that she then said something else in four seconds that the clerk entered and that about four people in the room heard and that the clerk has entered under it and not above it because people read the top of a page.

And nobody asked her why she keeps two lines apart, and the minute says nobody asked her why, and that this is the third time in nine weeks that nobody has, and that the twelfth refusal of the sixteenth of September was a refusal of her own instrument and not of a question about the office, and that the count of not-askings about the woman of fifty-one is five with two standing refusals, and that a man of thirty-four who mends fencing has said in public that a registrar who is asked why eleven times and answers once is a registrar who has worked out what the question is for, and that this is the first time in nine weeks that anybody has said so in a room she was standing in.

---

The nineteen lines went into the public lot book on the twentieth of September in the same hand as everything else, and the entry says what they are and are not, and **the entry is the thing that has been true for nine weeks and is now true about something else.**

> **THE NINETEEN LINES OF THE TWENTIETH OF SEPTEMBER ARE ENTERED AND THEY ARE NOT A FINDING. THIS DISTRICT HAS FOUR THINGS IT DOES NOT HAVE: A WAY OF SAYING THAT A TOLL LANDS ON A PLACE, A COLUMN THAT IS A PLACE, AN OFFICE, AND A STATION WITH NOBODY AT IT. IT HAS SIX INSTRUMENTS IT HAS BUILT AND NOT NAMED: A REHEARSAL, A PLATFORM, A RAIL, A CORRECTION, A QUESTION, AND A SECURITY. AND IT HAS ONE DOCUMENT THAT HAS BECOME WRONG FOR A REASON NOBODY CAUSED AND IT BELONGS TO NEITHER LIST. THE NINETEEN LINES ARE NONE OF THE FOUR AND NONE OF THE SIX AND NONE OF THE ONE, AND THEY ARE NOT TO BE ADDED TO ANY OF THEM.**

A man who does not read figures read that out at the ward market counter on the afternoon of the twentieth and it took four minutes, and **the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and it went out twice,** and forty people were at the counter and a stranger took a copy of the certificate cold off the board of eleven at about the same time as he had taken a copy of the notice about the twenty-ninth of August.

**AND A STRANGER TOOK TWO SHEETS COLD ON THE SAME AFTERNOON, ONE OF WHICH SAID A CONSENT AND ONE OF WHICH SAID NOBODY COULD STRIKE IT, AND HE TOOK THEM IN THE SAME MINUTE AND DID NOT KNOW THEY WERE ABOUT THE SAME THING, AND THE FINDING OF THE TWENTIETH OF SEPTEMBER IS THAT THIS DISTRICT'S STRONGEST INSTRUMENT AND A DRAWER THIRTY-FOUR MILES AWAY ARE THE SAME INSTRUMENT POINTING IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS.**

A paperer of thirty-eight read both of them out in a yard on the evening of the twentieth, in the ordinary voice, and read the first one twice and the second one once, and the clerk of nineteen years asked her why and she said the first one is a thing somebody might rely on and the second one is a thing somebody might rely on wrongly, and the first one goes out twice and the second one goes out once because about four people in this district are going to hear it and nineteen are going to have to live with it.

A man who sells nothing and has a use was asked on the twentieth whether a sheet about a consent and a sheet about a strike were the same kind of document. He said yes in four seconds and then said the other half, and it is in the minute in his own words, and it is fifty-five words long.

"**One of them is about a person and one of them is about nobody being able to do a thing, and both of them are in my own type and both of them cost four coppers, and a stranger will not be able to tell which of the two is the one that stops you.**"

---

And on the twentieth of September, in a yard in Alder Reach, about nineteen people read out the four things the district does not have, and the six instruments it has built and not named, and the one document that has become wrong for a reason nobody caused. It took nineteen minutes. **It is the first time in nine weeks that the two lists have been read out in the same room by the same voice**, and a stranger took a copy of the page cold in the middle of it and did not stop anybody and did not ask anybody anything.

The man who sells nothing and has a use was asked whether the four and the six and the one could be printed, and he said the four could and the six could and the one could not, and gave the reason in nine seconds.

"**The four are things this district wants and the six are things it built. A person will pay four coppers for a list of what a place wants. Nobody has ever paid four coppers for a list of what a place made, and if they did I would print it and charge them again.**"

"And the one."

"**The one is a sheet that says a train comes once a month in the dark to a place two miles west of a platform, and it went up on a wall in the fourth month of this year, and if I print the fact that it is wrong then I am the fourth man to make a document out of it and three of them have been wrong, and a document that everybody has touched is not a document nobody owns any more, it is a document everybody has an opinion about.**"

And on the twenty-first of September the party of ten came back to the west bank and the finding of the twenty-first is that a party that has been east of the about four thousand and one households for twenty days comes home with nineteen lines, a certificate, a second certificate, four descriptions of a job, a number of pans, and no salt at all.

The man who drives salt came back on the first cart with nothing on it and nineteen people were at the ninth mile of the raised plank road at about four in the afternoon to see two empty carts come over, and the haulier of forty was there, and he had said in the third week of August that the water is on the planks or it is not.

"You went thirty-four miles to find out who signs for a ton."

"I went thirty-four miles to find out whether the water was coming or going, and I have been asking the wrong question for two years, and the answer was a woman of thirty-one and a certificate that two men in this district cannot get at, and I am carrying nothing home and I would do it again in four days and that is the second time in a year I have said a thing like that and I would like it entered that I said it."

Nobody argued with him and a clerk of nineteen years entered the sentence, and entered that the man who drives salt had brought nine tons of salt into this district on the thirteenth of August, and that this district had bought a hundred and ten pounds of it at nine coppers a pound for nine hundred and ninety coppers on a line of its own, and that the rest had gone back east on the twenty-second of August in a cart, and that he had come back twenty days later with nothing and had said he would do it again.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OF SEPTEMBER A DISTRICT OF FOUR THOUSAND AND ONE HOUSEHOLDS HAD SENT NINE PEOPLE THIRTY-FOUR MILES EAST AND TEN WENT AND GOTTEN BACK A CERTIFICATE NOBODY COULD USE, AND THE FINDING IS THAT THE PARTY CAME HOME WITH MORE THINGS THAN WENT AND NOT ONE OF THEM WAS A TON, AND THAT A DISTRICT WHICH CANNOT SIGN FOR A TON HAS SENT NINE PEOPLE TO FIND OUT WHY AND HAS COME BACK WITH A FIGURE IN A DRAWER INSTEAD.**


And then on the twenty-first of September a sheet went up on eleven walls and on three toll boards, and it cost four coppers, and it is the fourth sheet this district has printed in two months and it is the only one of the four that is about one thing, and the thing is a day, and the day is nine days off.

> **ON THE THIRTIETH OF SEPTEMBER A TERM ENDS. A FIGURE OF ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THREE COPPERS IS TO BE READ OUT AT THE WARD MARKET COUNTER ONCE IN EVERY QUARTER UNTIL IT MOVES. THE MAN WHO AGREED TO READ IT WILL NOT BE IN THIS DISTRICT ON THAT DAY. ANYBODY IN THAT ROOM MAY HAVE ENDED IT IN A SENTENCE AT ANY TIME SINCE THE FOURTEENTH OF AUGUST AND NONE OF THEM HAS.**

The man who printed it was not thanked and did not ask to be, and he charged four coppers and said in nine seconds that a sheet about one thing is four coppers and a sheet about two things is twelve, and that he has never printed one about a person and would not, and that this district has now printed four sheets in two months and three of them are about things and one of them is about a day, and that he could not tell which of those four has been worth the money and would not be asked.

The finding of the twenty-first of September is that a district with four thousand and one households, five toll boards, six instruments it cannot name and a rate sheet nine years old **printed a sheet on the twenty-first of September that says somebody else reads the number, and that a sheet with a number in it and no name on it costs four coppers, and that this district has been saying for nine weeks that a figure in a public book can be carried to a door, and has not once said that a figure with no name on it can.**


Then the boy of seventeen was asked at the ninth mile about the train, and he said what he has said three times, and he said it in twenty-seven words, and nobody argued, and a clerk wrote it down.

"**There has not been a train since the twenty-ninth of June and the sheet at the fourth place says there is one a month in the dark.**"

Nobody argued with him about it and the clerk wrote it down, and the minute says that a sheet which has become wrong for a reason nobody caused is a document nobody owns, and that it is now in three places. The three are a printed sheet at a place a train does not stop at, a notice about a party of nine going thirty-four miles east, and a line in a public book with four names on it and a figure under the four names, and the three are not the same document, and they are not joined, and they are not to be.

And a woman of fifty-eight who carries water and does not read figures was at the ninth mile and had not been asked anything and said one thing, and **the minute says he said it and not she,** and says he said it nine seconds after she had said nothing at all, and that a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that **a man who speaks for a person who has not spoken is the same instrument as a mark on a sheet and is not a person,** and the clerk entered it and did not join it to anything.

And the last thing of the twenty-first of September was said in a counting room over a chandler's shop at about six in the evening to about nineteen people, and it is the last entry of the month and it is nineteen lines long, and it is in the tenth hand's hand, and **it is the shortest entry this book has carried since the eighth of March and it is the only one that has no finding in it at all.**

> **THE TWENTY-FIRST OF SEPTEMBER. NINE PEOPLE CAME BACK. ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF SEPTEMBER THE TERM OF THE BOUNDARY AT THE WEST END ENDS AND IT HAS TO BE ASKED AGAIN FROM TWO ROOMS. ON THE THIRTIETH OF SEPTEMBER THE FIGURE OF ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-THREE COPPERS IS READ OUT AT THIS COUNTER BY A MAN WHO IS NOT HERE, AND NOBODY KNOWS WHO READS IT, AND ANYBODY IN THAT ROOM MAY HAVE ENDED IT IN A SENTENCE AT ANY TIME SINCE THE FOURTEENTH OF AUGUST AND NONE OF THEM HAS.**

And the party of ten was read out afterwards in nineteen minutes and ten people read it, and one of the nine was the man who sells nothing and has a use and one of the nine was a chandler of thirty-four who keeps a counter of oil on Ash Row, and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and is a different kind of thing from the count of five and was not added to it, and the protected category is closed at five and nothing was added to it, and the count of not-askings about a woman of forty-five stands at four with one standing refusal and did not move.
