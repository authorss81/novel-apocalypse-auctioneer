# Chapter 112: A Yard Can Ask

The party that was going to go thirty miles east was composed in a yard in Alder Reach on the twenty-first, twenty-second and twenty-third of August, and it was composed in a yard because a yard can ask a person and a board cannot and a form cannot, and that is the whole of what this district has learned in nine weeks and it has not learned it anywhere else.

Nine people were asked and nine people were asked properly, in the five things, standing up, in daylight, in front of nineteen at a time, and the asking took two days and the finding of the twenty-third of August is that **the asking is the only instrument this district owns that can be carried into a room it has not been in.**

The five things are what the job is, how long it is, what it costs, what happens to the person who does it, and who is standing in the room. The order has not changed since a chandler asked it in a yard in the fourth month of this year, and the sixth thing has no name and belongs to a clerk of nineteen years and is not on the wall, and she read it out herself on the second evening in nineteen minutes.

**THE NINE ARE A MAN WHO DRIVES SALT, ONE OF HIS TWO MEN, THE READER, THE TENTH HAND, A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS, THE FREIGHT CLERK OF THE WARD MARKET, A PAPERER OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT, A SIGNALMAN OF THIRTY-NINE WHO KEEPS A BOARD AND IS NOT PAID, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING AND IS NOT A BEARER AND HAS NEVER BEEN A BEARER.**

Four people were not asked, and the entry of the twenty-third says four, and it says why each of the four was not asked, and one of the four reasons is a man of thirty-four writing a name into a list.

A woman of fifty-one who keeps the only pair on the line at the ninth place was asked in the lane on the twentieth and said no in the order she has used since the twenty-ninth of March, which is the condition, then the word, then the answer, and the condition was that somebody is on that platform at night whether she is on it or not.

"A woman of fifty who is the water at that place was written into the list of people who had been asked on the twenty-first," said the clerk of nineteen years, "**and she had not been asked, and I found it at half past four in the afternoon, and it was corrected in four minutes and it is entered as not asked, and not asked is not a no and she has not said no and she has not said yes and I am not going to ask her today.**"

"And will you ask her before the cart goes."

"**I will ask her before the cart goes and if she is not there when the cart goes then it is not asked, and I would rather go thirty miles east with eight and not nine than put a line in a book saying a woman was asked.**"

The boy of nineteen at the fourth place was not asked either, and the reason is that he is nineteen and nobody thought of him, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that thinking of him is the whole of what nine weeks has not done, and that it is the same thing as not having a column for a ton.

---

A bridge administrator of nine years was at a rail in Alder Reach on the twenty-second, on a published day, with about nineteen people, and he was not asked to go, and he said so in his own hand before anybody said anything.

"That is not enough. **And I am not going thirty miles east, and the reason is that I have not been asked, and the reason I have not been asked is that I would be the first person anybody from this district has seen on that bank in nine years and I have been the man who makes this place unsafe for nine years by being in it with a schedule.**"

He added one sentence after it, and it is the second time in nine weeks he has added one, and the first one was on the twenty-eighth of July, and the reader entered both in the same column and said nothing about them being the same man.

"**And I would like it entered that I said no to a thing nobody offered me, because in four years somebody will read that page and see a man who was not there, and there is going to be a question about why, and I would like the why to be in my own hand.**"

Nobody at that rail said anything. His span is still shut to all carriage, its instrument certificate is still suspended by two people who are not him, the middle plank of the old footbridge is in the service cut, and seventeen people are on the far bank with a stamped book and no way home, and he said the sentence about the certificate being correct in four seconds and did not say anything else about it.

A woman of forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide walked with him about forty yards afterwards and then stopped, and the reader wrote down what she said and the reader is not the tenth hand this time and did not know what the tenth hand would have said about it.

"**He has now said no to two things nobody offered him, in a fortnight, in his own handwriting, and both of them were about not being the first person seen.**"

---

The man who could write the terms of the party was not asked to write them, and he said so in four seconds when he was asked, and the reason he gave is in the minute in his own words and is eighty-three words long.

"**I can write the terms of a thing two people have agreed to. Nine people in a cart is not a thing two people have agreed to, it is a thing nine people have each agreed to separately, and those are nine agreements and not one, and I would be writing a document about nineteen people in a form I made up last night, and I have said in public that a form is a thing made before anybody has agreed to anything.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said that was the correct answer and that the terms of the party were composed by her and by the tenth hand in a counting room over a chandler's shop on the twenty-third in an hour, and that a chandler of thirty-four who keeps a counter of oil on Ash Row was in the room and heard it and said nothing, and the minute says he said nothing on purpose and that he has said he would say nothing on purpose about a form.

> **NINE PEOPLE ARE GOING THIRTY MILES EAST ON THE FIRST OF SEPTEMBER. FOUR DAYS OUT. ABOUT TEN MILES A DAY AND NO MORE, BECAUSE THE GROUND BETWEEN THE RIVER AND THE LOAMS HAS NOT CARRIED A WHEEL SINCE THE FLOOD YEAR. TWO CARTS. THE NINE ARE ASKED IN THE FIVE THINGS AND THE ASKING IS IN A YARD AND THE YARD IS THE ONLY INSTRUMENT IN THIS DISTRICT THAT CAN BE CARRIED INTO A ROOM IT HAS NOT BEEN IN.**
>
> **THE NINE ARE NOT A CHARTER. A CHARTER HAS A LOT AND A TOLL AND A TERM AND A BEARER AND AT LEAST TWO WITNESSES WHO ARE NOT TRAVELLING ON IT, AND THIS HAS NONE OF THOSE AND IS NOT ANY OF THOSE, AND NOBODY IS TO ENTER IT AS ANYTHING BUT A PARTY.**

The twenty-third of August also produced the answer to the question about the person who carries nothing, and it is in the minute in the freight clerk's own words and he wrote it himself.

"**Who is standing in the room. I have been six years a clerk of a market and I have asked a person about a job in nine rooms in nine weeks, and every time I have read that fifth thing it has come out as the people who are listening, and this is the first time I have noticed that a person going thirty miles east is asking whether the people on the bank will still be here when he comes back.**"

Nobody argued with him and four people in that yard understood the whole of that on the twenty-third of August and nobody has said so since.

---

The man of thirty-nine was asked on the twenty-second, in the five things, in nine minutes, in front of nineteen people, and he is a signalman of a bridge administration and he is not a bearer and he is not paid and he keeps the fourth toll board at the ninth place, and it is the first time in nine weeks that anybody has asked him anything about himself.

"What is the job."

"**Stand in a lane thirty miles east and look at a thing that makes salt.**"

"How long is it."

"**Four days there and four days back and however long they make me, and nobody has said which and I am not going to be the one who decides it for them.**"

"What does it cost."

"**Nothing.**"

About nine people in that yard said something at once and the asker waited them out, and the minute records that he waited them out and that it took eleven seconds.

"I have got eleven coppers a quarter for the bench and the lamp and the chalk and the oil," he said, "**and I have had it since the seventeenth of last month, and it was never for me and the market gave it to a board, and the board is a thing and I am not a board. What this costs me is four days of a trade I am paid for on the days I am here, and nobody is paying those four days, and I have been the only man at that end since the eighth of June and nobody has asked me what happens to it while I am gone.**"

Nobody argued with that and the asker asked the next one.

"What happens to the person who does it."

"**I will come back and I will not be the man that left, because there will be a fifth line in that column in ten days and it will be in my hand and nobody will be able to read it but me, and that is not a cost, that is the opposite of a cost, and I have been trying for two months to work out how to tell somebody that and I have not managed it in nine weeks.**"

"Who is standing in the room."

He answered that one, and he took nineteen seconds over it, and he is the first of the nine to have answered the fifth thing, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that fact on the page and did not underline it.

"**A woman of forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide and lent me a yard once, and a man who sells nothing and has a use, and a man who mends fencing, and a man who carries water and is fifty-eight, and a chandler of thirty-four, and nine more. And the man who is asking me is a man of thirty-four and he is not a bearer, and he is going to be the man who comes back and writes down what we all said, and I would like that written down as an answer and not as a description.**"

Then the sixth thing, which has no name and belongs to the clerk of nineteen years and is not on the wall, and she read it out on the second evening in nineteen minutes and read every sentence twice.

"**And on the second evening two of the nine asked to be asked again afterwards, and they were asked again on the twenty-third in nine minutes each, and the answer of one of them changed and the answer of the other did not, and both of the answers are in the minute, and the one that changed is in the book and the one that did not is in the minute, and the difference is that a book is for other people and a minute is for us.**"

---

The man who sells nothing and has a use printed a sheet on the twenty-third, at the tram arch, and it cost twelve coppers and it is the third twelve coppers this district has spent on paper in two months and the registrar entered that it is a third and is not to be merged with the other two, and the reason she gives is that a month is a month and a rate is not a rate.

> **NINE PEOPLE ARE GOING THIRTY MILES EAST ON THE FIRST OF SEPTEMBER AND BACK WHEN THEY COME BACK. NOBODY KNOWS WHEN THEY COME BACK. THIS DISTRICT HAS NO COLUMN FOR A DAY THAT IS NOT YET, AND THE SHEET IS PRINTED IN THE SAME TYPE AS EVERYTHING ELSE.**

He charged twelve for it and not four, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing asked him why in nine seconds and he gave the answer in the ordinary voice and it is sixteen words long.

"**Because it has a date on it and a distance on it and mine cost four.**"

Nobody argued with him and the minute says nobody argued, and says that a man who has been wrong about what a place costs for four months was right about that in nine seconds and that the district entered the fact that he was right and did not enjoy it.

**AND A SHEET WITH A DATE ON IT IS A DIFFERENT ANIMAL FROM A SHEET WITHOUT ONE, AND THIS DISTRICT HAS SPENT TWELVE COPPERS LEARNING THAT ON THE TWENTY-THIRD OF AUGUST, AND THE FIRST FOUR SHEETS COST FOUR COPPERS EACH AND NONE OF THEM HAD A DATE ON IT.**

The ward market did not pay for it. The clerk of nineteen years was asked and said the same thing she says, in nine words, and she said it in the same order, and the man who prints did not ask twice.

"**The market funds what is written. This is not written. It is printed, and printed is a man selling something.**"

---

Then the river, which came up nine feet in the flood year and has not gone down, and the four days, and the thing that made the party nine instead of eleven.

A haulier of forty was in the yard on the twenty-third and he had made the eleven-mile walk up that bank in five and a quarter hours four times in two months with four changeovers, and he said what the eastern bank would cost in four minutes and the minute is four lines long.

"**Thirty miles and a river in the middle of it. The river is nine feet above where it was and it is not a ford and it has not been a ford since the flood year, and there is a raised plank road at the ninth mile that a loaded cart can go over in a day if the water is off the planks, and if the water is on the planks then nobody is going and that is the whole of the risk.**"

"And if the water is on the planks on the second of September."

"**Then the party stands on this bank for a day and comes home, and nobody has been hurt, and the finding of that day is that a thing nobody is allowed to decide is decided by a river, and this district has had about nine weeks of that and could do with a rest.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything about the rest, and the minute says nobody did, and says that a man of thirty-one who is a signalman of the district's own crew said afterwards that the man who mends fencing has been right about the shape of things four times in nine weeks and has been wrong about one of them, and did not say which.

The answer that changed on the twenty-third was a clerk of nineteen years, who said on the second evening that she would go and said on the third morning that she would not go until somebody had stayed, and somebody stayed, and the finding is that **a person asked twice is not a person asked once and not a person asked three times.**

The paperer of thirty-eight is the one whose answer changed back, and she gave the reason in four minutes on the twenty-third in the ordinary voice and it is in the minute, and it is about a sheet and not about herself.

"**I said yes on the second evening because I wanted to go and I have wanted to go since a man in a lane on the thirteenth of August stood in front of a column that takes a place. I said no on the third morning because the thing I have wanted to go and see is a thing somebody is standing in, and if I go and see it I come back and I am the only person in this district who has seen it, and then in four years a stranger will ask me what it was and I will be the only one who can answer, and that is a job I have not been asked to do.**"

"And will you still be able to go."

"**I will go on the second cart. There is a second cart and there is a man of thirty-one on it and he is not going to be able to read a thing to a stranger either, and that is why he is on it, and I asked for that this morning and it was granted in four seconds.**"

The man of thirty-one who is a signalman of the district's own crew is not on the nine, and the man of thirty-four who mends fencing is on the nine, and neither of them said anything about it, and the minute says that neither of them said anything about it and that this was asked afterwards and that they gave the same answer, which is that a paperer of thirty-eight knows what she is worth in a room.

And a boy of seventeen who is paid four coppers a quarter for an index was asked on the twenty-third, in a counting room, in front of nineteen people, to write the party's terms down properly in a book, and he said yes in four seconds, and it is the second time in nine weeks he has been asked in a room and it is the first time he has said yes.

**HE WROTE IT DOWN IN ABOUT FOUR MINUTES IN HIS OWN HAND AND READ IT OUT, AND IT IS THE ONLY DOCUMENT IN THIS DISTRICT THAT SAYS WHAT A PARTY IS, AND IT SAYS THAT A PARTY IS NINE PEOPLE WHO HAVE EACH SAID YES SEPARATELY AND THAT NINE OF THOSE IS NINE AND NOT ONE.**

Nobody argued with it and the clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy of seventeen has now been asked in a room twice and has said yes twice, and that he had asked to be asked and had not been asked for four months, and that a request to be asked in a room is a condition with no end on it and is not a term, and that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and is a different kind of thing from the count of five and is never added to it.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF AUGUST THE PARTY WAS NINE AND THE TOLL WAS NIL AND THE TERMS SAID SO, AND THE FINDING OF THE TWENTY-FOURTH IS THAT A PARTY OF NINE PEOPLE GOING SOMEWHERE TOGETHER IS NOT AN INSTRUMENT AND IS NOT A JOB AND IS NOT A CHARTER, AND THAT IT IS THE FIRST THING IN NINE WEEKS THAT WENT OUT OF THIS DISTRICT WITH NO COLUMN ANYWHERE FOR IT.**

The train on the siding had stood a hundred and ninety days on the twentieth of August and a hundred and ninety-two on the twenty-second and a hundred and ninety-four on the twenty-four, and the fifth of the five dates in a boy's index is the twenty-ninth of this month and the boy of seventeen wrote the twenty-fourth down on the twenty-fourth and did not write a sixth date, and a clerk asked him about that on the twenty-fourth and he said that there was not a sixth date because there was not a sixth thing, and the minute says he said it in nine words and that nobody argued.

Two miles west, at the fourth place, a sheet is still nailed to a wall that says in its own printed type that a train stops there once a month in the dark, and there has not been one since the twenty-ninth of June.

A paperer of thirty-eight read that sheet aloud on the twenty-second to nine people who were going thirty miles east and did not comment on it. The minute says she read it aloud because the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and that nobody in the room said a word about it, and that this district has now printed four hundred words of notices and has never printed a correction for anything.

**A DOCUMENT WHICH HAS BECOME WRONG FOR A REASON NOBODY CAUSED IS A DOCUMENT NOBODY OWNS, AND IT FITS IN NEITHER OF THE TWO LISTS THIS DISTRICT KEEPS, AND THE LIST IT IS NOT ON IS THE LIST OF THE SIX INSTRUMENTS THIS DISTRICT HAS BUILT AND NOT NAMED, AND A BOY OF SEVENTEEN WROTE THAT DOWN ON THE TWENTY-SECOND AND ADDED NO FINDING UNDER IT.**

And the reading ledger was read out at the ward market counter on the twenty-third in the ordinary voice, as it is read out, and it is one for April, none for May, one for the twenty-ninth of June, and the number of departures left is one, and there is no date in it for the next one.

Ivo Stenn was going thirty miles east and read it out himself before he read out his own separate sheet, which says in nineteen words that the intervals go up on use and that he will not defend that and will not call it anything else, and which is read out at the counter after every refusal, and there has not been a refusal since the sixteenth of July.
