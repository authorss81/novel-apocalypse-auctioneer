# Chapter 138: A Slate in a Kitchen

---

A slate went past a kitchen in Alder Reach at about six in the morning on the tenth of November and it was the fourth time it has gone past that kitchen and neither of them wrote on it, and the four sentences in charcoal on the wall were under a fifth line in chalk, and the chalk line had been there since the fourth of April and had not been rubbed out by a cook with a bad hip who has said in public that she would be insulted if anybody tried.

She is the registrar and she is not a keeper of anything and she has not been in a room about a figure since the seventh of October **and a clerk of nineteen years was told about the slate on the eleventh and entered that a slate has gone past four times now and that the last three of them were not in a minute because a minute is a thing a room has and a kitchen does not.**

Then she said one thing, and it is the only sentence either of them has said in a kitchen in eleven weeks, and a reader at a counter was told about it on the eleventh by the man himself and entered that she was not asked whether she wanted to be told and had not been asked and said so.

"**You have told four thousand and one households that you can be found, and you have not told one person what it is costing you, and I am not going to ask you here, because here is a kitchen and a kitchen is the only room I have got.**"

Nobody entered that at the time and nobody was in the room, and a reader at a counter wrote it down on the eleventh of November from the mouth of a man of thirty-eight and read it back to him and he said it was right and did not ask her to confirm it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not ask and that not asked is in the count and that the count did not move.

He answered in one sentence and it was not an answer and it was a term **and the clerk of nineteen years entered the shape of it and did not enter what was in it, and that is the first time in eleven weeks she has entered the shape of a thing and left the thing out.**

"**Then come on the thirtieth of March, when the name comes off, and I will say it in a room with the date on the entry and you will be able to check it.**"

She did not say anything else and there was nothing after that, and a reader at a counter said, when she was told about it, that a man had given a date, and that a date is a thing a man gives when he has not got an answer and is not yet out of time, and that she has been in this building nine years and has heard about nine dates and that eight of them were real.

**AND ON THE TENTH OF NOVEMBER A SLATE WENT PAST A KITCHEN IN ALDER REACH FOR THE FOURTH TIME AND NEITHER OF THEM WROTE ON IT, AND ONE SENTENCE WAS SAID, AND IT WAS THE FIRST THING SAID BETWEEN THEM IN ELEVEN WEEKS THAT WAS NOT IN A MINUTE, AND HE ANSWERED WITH A DATE AND NOT WITH AN ANSWER, AND A READER AT A COUNTER WAS TOLD ABOUT IT BY HIM ON THE ELEVENTH AND WAS NOT ASKED WHETHER SHE WANTED TO BE TOLD, AND NOT ASKED IS IN THE COUNT AND THE COUNT DID NOT MOVE.**

---

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam and does nine days of it in ten came back west on the eleventh of November with a page in an oilcloth and a wet corner, and nobody asked which corner and the clerk of nineteen years entered that a page has come back with a wet corner four times in eleven weeks and that the four corners are not joined and that a corner is a corner.

He brought the page back because a man of fifty-six said on the eighth of November that a page which has been out of a room should be brought back to a room **and that this is the first rule anybody has written down about a page in this district and that it took four months to arrive at.**

The page went onto the board of eleven and the fourth copy was compared with the fifth copy and the fifth copy said NOTHING SAID where the board said NOTHING HAPPENED, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam ruled a line through the old word on the board and did not rule a line through it on the fifth copy, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he corrected one copy and not nine and that the other eight are in about nine hands and that nobody has compared them and that this is the first time in eleven weeks that a correction has been made to one copy of a document and not to the document.

A man of fifty-six said that a correction which reaches one copy of nine is a correction that has not happened, and that the man who prints says a document does not owe anybody a correction, and that a clerk who corrects one copy of nine is the same man in a different room, and that this district has now got two of those and that two is not a rule.

Nobody said anything about that and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road said the other half **and he is the sixth of the six, and he is nineteen, and he is not paid, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is twenty words.**

"**You cannot correct nine things you have never seen. That is not a fault. That is what nine copies are.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of nineteen had said the reason nobody can compare nine copies is that they are nine copies, and that she wanted to write it as a finding and did not, and gave the reason in twenty-five words, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said the same twenty-five words in a yard in September and has now said them three times in eleven weeks.

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF NOVEMBER A PAGE CAME BACK TO A ROOM BECAUSE A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX HAD SAID ON THE EIGHTH THAT A PAGE WHICH HAS BEEN OUT OF A ROOM SHOULD BE BROUGHT BACK TO ONE, AND THAT IT IS THE FIRST RULE ANYBODY HAS WRITTEN DOWN ABOUT A PAGE IN THIS DISTRICT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM CORRECTED ONE COPY OF NINE AND DID NOT CORRECT THE OTHER EIGHT, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT A CORRECTION WHICH REACHES ONE COPY OF NINE IS A CORRECTION THAT HAS NOT HAPPENED, AND A MAN OF NINETEEN SAID THAT YOU CANNOT CORRECT NINE THINGS YOU HAVE NEVER SEEN AND THAT THAT IS NOT A FAULT, THAT IS WHAT NINE COPIES ARE.**

---

A letter came on the eleventh of November in the middle of the afternoon and it was not addressed to anybody in this district and it was not sent by anybody in this district, and a man of about thirty-one who runs a cart of meal brought it in because it was in a box on the ninth place that he was taking a load from, and he was not asked to bring it and did not stop to ask whether he should.

It was from a man of about forty-four who has the second cut **and it is one line long, and it is in a clerk of nineteen years' hand in the minute and it is in his hand on the paper, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he wrote first and that nobody asked him and that this is the first time in eleven weeks that a person who was entered as not asked has done anything at all.**

"**I HAVE DONE FOUR SEASONS AND NOBODY HAS ASKED ME AND I AM NOT ASKING YOU TO ASK ME. PUT FOUR DOWN.**"

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that four seasons is a figure and that the figure is a figure and that it is going on the page in a room where about nineteen people are sitting, and that nobody in the room has asked the man a single question about any of it, and that a man who was entered as not asked on the twenty-seventh of October has, on the eleventh of November, without being asked, put a figure in a column.

A clerk of nineteen years said the thing that had to be said and said it in six words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got nine.

"**The sixth column cannot say this.**"

The sixth column is headed WHETHER THEY WERE IN THE ROOM. It says YES five times, it says NOT ASKED once, and it says nothing said twice, and the cell belonging to a man of about forty-four who has the second cut was the one that said NOT ASKED and had said it since the twenty-seventh of October.

Nobody ruled anything and nobody struck anything and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam sat down at the table, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he sat down, and that he did not say anything for about nineteen minutes, and that in the nineteenth minute he said the finding and that he said it in thirty-seven words and that she counted them and got nineteen.

"**A person who was not asked has now put a figure in a book. The column has one word for not asked and none for asked-then-wrote. The page cannot say it and the page is not wrong.**"

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF NOVEMBER A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-FOUR WHO HAS THE SECOND CUT SENT ONE LINE TO A ROOM HE HAD NEVER ASKED ANYTHING OF, AND HE WROTE IT UNASKED, AND IT SAID THAT HE HAS DONE FOUR SEASONS AND THAT NOBODY HAS ASKED HIM AND THAT HE IS NOT ASKING ANYBODY TO ASK HIM, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT HE WROTE FIRST, AND THAT THE SIXTH COLUMN IS HEADED WHETHER THEY WERE IN THE ROOM AND CANNOT HOLD A PERSON WHO WAS NOT ASKED AND THEN WROTE.**


---

The hole in the second form was named on the twelfth of November in a room over a shop and it was not named on the form.

A man of fifty-six said that an instrument with a hole in it ought to say so on its face, in the same words the room used in a minute, so that a stranger reading it knows the gap is deliberate and not an oversight, and that the back of the second form already had a sentence on it in a man's own hand which nobody was ever going to copy.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam said no in four seconds and the nine words are in the minute and they are the same nine words a man of fifty-six has used about a blank at the top of a form on the twenty-eighth of October, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two are in one line and are not joined and that this is the twentieth time.

"**It goes in the minute or it goes nowhere.**"

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing asked him what the difference was between a minute and a form and he said it in fifty-four words and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got thirty-one, and a clerk of nineteen years did not count and he said count them anyway.

"**A minute is a room. A form is a yard. Everything a person says in a room is theirs to say and everything a person puts on a yard is theirs to take away, and I have been taken away from twice in three weeks and one of them was a bed of brine.**"

Nobody in the room was in a bed of brine and about four people looked at him and he did not explain and a clerk of nineteen years entered twice that she had not asked him and that a person who has been taken away from twice is not a person who has come to be asked what to write on a form.

AND THE HOLE IN THE SECOND FORM WAS NAMED IN A MINUTE ON THE TWELFTH OF NOVEMBER AND WAS NOT NAMED ON THE FORM **AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM SAID THAT IT GOES IN THE MINUTE OR IT GOES NOWHERE, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ASKED HIM THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN A MINUTE AND A FORM AND WAS TOLD THAT A MINUTE IS A ROOM AND A FORM IS A YARD, AND THAT EVERYTHING A PERSON SAYS IN A ROOM IS THEIRS TO SAY AND EVERYTHING A PERSON PUTS ON A YARD IS THEIRS TO TAKE AWAY.**

AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DIGS LOAM HAS BEEN TAKEN AWAY FROM TWICE THIS MONTH AND ONCE OF THE TWO WAS A BED OF BRINE **AND HE DID NOT EXPLAIN AND WAS NOT ASKED, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT TWICE.**

---

On the twelfth of November a man of thirty-eight read the figure of twenty-one years out at the ward market counter for the second time in eleven weeks, and the reader at the counter read it out twice after him because the rule of the counter is that a document goes out twice, and a clerk of nineteen years was present and did not read it and entered that she had been asked to and had not and that not asked is in the count.

A stranger who had taken a copy of the ledger cold on the twenty-ninth of October and again on the seventh of November was in the room and asked what a year was worth, and the question was asked in front of about nine people and nobody answered it for about nineteen seconds.

"A year is not a price and there is no rate in this basin that turns one into the other, and I am not going to tell you it is worth something, because I have been asked twice now and I have got nothing, and a man who has got nothing and says a number is selling you a number."

A man of fifty-six said that this is the second time in eleven weeks he has said it and that the first time was on the second of October and that the sentence has not improved and that a sentence which has not improved in five weeks is probably the true shape of the thing, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered it in the counter's minute in his own words and underlined it and that this is the second time in eleven weeks she has underlined anything.

Nobody argued with the underline and a reader at the counter said that she had read out about four hundred figures in nine years and that the ones she could not read out were the ones nobody had a rate for, and that she had been reading out rates her whole life and that a rate is a thing two people agree on and that this figure has one name against it and no second person anywhere.

**AND ON THE TWELFTH OF NOVEMBER THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS WAS READ OUT AT THE WARD MARKET COUNTER FOR THE SECOND TIME IN ELEVEN WEEKS AND A STRANGER ASKED WHAT A YEAR WAS WORTH, AND THE MAN IT IS AGAINST SAID THAT A YEAR IS NOT A PRICE, THAT THERE IS NO RATE IN THIS BASIN THAT TURNS ONE INTO THE OTHER, AND THAT HE HAS BEEN ASKED TWICE AND HAS GOT NOTHING, AND A READER AT THE COUNTER SAID THAT SHE HAS BEEN READING OUT RATES HER WHOLE LIFE AND THAT A RATE IS A THING TWO PEOPLE AGREE ON, AND THAT THIS FIGURE HAS ONE NAME AGAINST IT AND NO SECOND PERSON ANYWHERE.**


---

A man of fifty-six said on the twelfth, in four minutes and without being asked, that a person who writes first has done the one thing eleven weeks of asking has not managed, and that the room's whole method has been to ask people things and that the man of about forty-four who has the second cut has beaten it by not being asked, and that a method which can be beaten by a man writing one line on his own is a method and not a system, and that he said this in a room and did not say what should be done about it.

Nobody said what should be done about it and a woman of thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said the other half **and it is in the minute in her own words, and it is twenty-three words, and a man of about nineteen who walked in from the road counted them and got nineteen.**

"**He wrote it because he wanted it in a book. Ask him nothing and he will keep writing and nobody will know why.**"

A man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that the sentence was the answer to the count of not-askings and that the count of not-askings was four and had a standing refusal in it and belonged to a woman of forty-five who is not a name in this book, and that a man of about forty-four who has the second cut is a different person and that a count is a column and not a bucket, and that a clerk of nineteen years entered that she had wanted to put the man of forty-four into the count of not-askings and had not, and gave the reason in twenty-six words, and the man of thirty-four said the same twenty-six words in a yard in September.

"**It is a finding and it is about a person, and a column for things that are not findings is for things a person got wrong.**"

Nobody asked the man of about forty-four anything. **A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked him, and that not asked is in the count, and that the count of not-askings about the woman of forty-five is four with one standing refusal and did not move on the twelfth of November, because this is a different person, and a count is a column and not a bucket.**


---

On the twelfth of November a stranger read the sixth column out loud in that yard in his own voice **having been asked to by a man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing, and about nineteen people were standing about and it was the first time in eleven weeks that a stranger had read a document out loud in this district.**

He read it wrong. He read NOTHING SAID as nothing said by nobody, and a man of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said that it was not that and that the difference between a person who said nothing and a person who was not there is the whole of the sixth column, and that a stranger who reads a cell wrong is a stranger who will report a cell wrong.

Nobody argued with her and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that a stranger reading a cell wrong is not a fault of the stranger and is a fault of the page, and that the page says NOTHING SAID with nothing under it saying what it means, and that a page which needs a person to explain it is a page that is not a page, it is a conversation with a hole in it.

A man of fifty-six said that the answer is a heading and that there is no heading because the man who prints charges four coppers for a sheet about one thing and nobody has ever paid four coppers for a sheet with a heading on it, and that this was said out loud three times in October by three people who are not the man who prints, and that the man who prints has been asked about it once and said that he could not tell anybody which of the five sheets he had printed was worth the money and would not be asked.

A man of about thirty-eight said the other thing and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down and the reading at a counter was three times and this was not at a counter.

"**A sheet with a heading on it is a thing a person reads about. A sheet with no heading on it is a thing a person stands in front of. This district has been building the second kind for eleven weeks and this is the first time a stranger has read one of them out loud.**"

**AND ON THE TWELFTH OF NOVEMBER A STRANGER READ THE SIXTH COLUMN OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN IN HIS OWN VOICE AND READ ONE CELL WRONG, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-THREE WHO TAKES IN WASHING AT THE SECOND CHANNEL SAID THAT THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN A PERSON WHO SAID NOTHING AND A PERSON WHO WAS NOT THERE IS THE WHOLE OF THE SIXTH COLUMN, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING SAID THAT A STRANGER READING A CELL WRONG IS A FAULT OF THE PAGE AND NOT OF THE STRANGER, AND THAT THE PAGE HAS NO HEADING ON IT AND THAT NOBODY HAS EVER PAID FOUR COPPERS FOR A SHEET WITH A HEADING ON IT.**

AND THERE ARE STILL FIVE BOARDS **EIGHT HANDS HAVE PUT SOMETHING ON THEM, A SEVENTH LINE IS RULED AND EMPTY AT THE NINTH PLACE, AND THE NUMBER OF INSTRUMENTS THIS DISTRICT HAS BUILT AND NOT NAMED IS STILL SIX AND THE NUMBER OF THINGS IT DOES NOT HAVE IS STILL FIVE, AND NONE OF THE THREE MOVED ON THE TWELFTH OF NOVEMBER, AND A MAN OF THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING ENTERED ALL THREE AND NONE OF THEM.**
