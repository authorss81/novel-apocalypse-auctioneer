# Chapter 118: A Price and a Body

He asked her on the twelfth of September in nine minutes, in the five things, standing up, in daylight, in front of nineteen people, of whom nine were from the west bank and ten were from households on this side, and the room is a shed with an open side that looks at the third channel about two hundred yards off, and she came out of the channel into it twenty minutes before it began, and she did not sit down at any point in the nine minutes.

Ressa Karrow is thirty-one and she is in her second year and she has been in the third channel since the first of April, and the asking was made in a room with a roof on it because a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said that a thing that is going to be refused should be refused somewhere with a floor, and a clerk of nineteen years corrected him and the minute says the clerk said it and not the man.

"What is the job."

"**Buy a loom.**"

"How long is it."

"**A season. The first of October to the thirty-first of March.**"

"What does it cost."

"**Two thousand coppers, and a security, and the season is the season and not a year.**"

"What happens to the person who does it."

"**He stands in a channel for a season and the channel takes a year of his memory, and the channel does not care that he is the purchaser, and I have told him that I cannot be the bearer and he has written that down.**"

"Who is standing in the room."

He described it and she listened and then she said the first of the things she said, and the clerk of nineteen years counted them and the minute says she said four sentences over eleven minutes and that he did not interrupt her once.

"**You have got two thousand coppers and a security and a term and a release and a remedy, and there is not one of them about me.**"

"About what."

"**About the one who has to be in the frame, and the one who will not know what the eleventh of June was, and the one who stops the wind when it is wrong, which on this channel is me and which I get wrong about twice a year and have got right every time since the second of May, and that is the whole of what a year of me is worth to anybody.**"

Then he asked her the question that the five things do not have a place for, and he asked it in four seconds and the minute says he asked it in four seconds and that it was the worst four seconds of a day that about nineteen people have said was a good day.

"How much."

"**That is not a question anybody has ever asked me and I have been asked nine thousand things, and you asked it in four seconds and I have decided to answer it, and I want it in the minute that I answered it because it was badly asked and not because it was a good question.**"

"Take it."

"**Take what.**"

"**Whatever you think the loom is worth. I will pay it. Say a number and I will pay it and the security goes with it and the two thousand is a figure I did in eleven seconds and I have said so in a room and I will do it again.**"

---

And she thought about it for four minutes, and the minute says four minutes and that nobody in that room said anything in them, and then she gave the sentence that is now the entry of the twelfth of September and it is the reason this district did not buy a loom and is the reason it will not buy one in five weeks either.

"**A price is a number a person can hand over. I am not a number and I am not something a man hands over, and if you buy that frame you will own a thing that needs me in it, and in four months you will be standing in a yard asking me why it has stopped working, and I will have to tell you that I cannot remember last year.**"

Nobody in that room said anything and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down in full and read it back to her and read it back twice, and the woman corrected one word and the word was *remember* and she wanted *know*, and the clerk entered that this is the seventh time in nine weeks a person has corrected a document by saying what they needed in it.

"Read it again."

The clerk read it again. Then the woman of thirty-one said the second sentence, and it is eleven words long.

"**The price is not the thing. The body is the thing.**"

A man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households said afterwards, in four minutes, that the first sentence was forty-nine words long and that he had not heard a sentence of that length in two years of selling salt into a market on the other bank, and that the second sentence was the whole of the answer and the first one was the reason the second one was believable.

**AND ON THE TWELFTH OF SEPTEMBER A MAN OFFERED A PRICE FOUR TIMES, IN FOUR MINUTES, AND A WOMAN REFUSED FOUR TIMES IN THE SAME FOUR SENTENCES, AND THE FINDING IS THAT AN ITEM'S PRICE CANNOT SEPARATE THE PERSON WHOSE BODY MAKES IT WORK, AND THAT THE PRICE WAS NEVER THE THING AND HE KNEW IT WAS NOT THE THING ON THE EIGHTH OF SEPTEMBER.**

And then he did the thing that about nineteen people in that room had not expected, and the minute is four lines long and it is the fourth limit he has given in nine weeks and the clerk of nineteen years entered it under the other three and not above them.

"I cannot close this," he said. "**Not because she said no. Because a transfer needs a person to transfer from and a person to transfer to, and the frame is held by nine households that have a secretary and not by a person, and the bed is a hole, and the year is in neither of them, and I cannot write a person's name in a line and call the line a thing I have closed, and binding cannot create consent and it cannot create a person.**"

"And you wrote the terms."

"**I wrote the terms because a man who cannot close a thing is allowed to write what it would take, and because I wanted to see the whole of it on one page, and because about nineteen people crossed a river on the first of September to find out and I am the only one of them who can put a thing on paper, and I would rather hand you a page that says no than a page that says a number.**"

Nobody in that room argued with that and the guild inspector of forty-four, who came thirty-four miles on the tenth and who had been in the room for an hour, said the one sentence that is in the minute and it is thirty words long.

"**I have certified four hundred lots and I have never been in a room where the man offering had already said the answer out loud before the price was named.**"

---


---

And on the twelfth, in the evening, in half an hour, he asked her again, and he asked her again properly, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that the second asking was in six things and was not one of the five, because the five things ask a person whether they will do a job and the six things ask a person whether they still want to after they have been told what it is, and the six have no name and are not on the wall and belong to a clerk of nineteen years.

She answered the six in nine minutes and she did not use the same four sentences and the minute says she did not use the same four sentences, and that a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that this was the moment he understood that she had meant it, and that the four sentences on the twelfth were the reason and the nine minutes on the twelfth evening were the person.

"You have been told that the price is two thousand and that it could be four."

"**And I have been told that the man who set it says it is a figure he did in eleven seconds, and that is worth more to me than four thousand, and I want it entered that I said that, because a woman who is bought twice is a woman who was only bought once.**"

"You understand that if you said yes the channel would be run by you for somebody on the other bank of a river and not by the nine households on this one."

"**I understand it. And I will tell you the thing nobody has asked me for, which is that I do not mind the salt going into a cart. I have watched nine carts go west in two years and I have never once minded. What I will not do is be a bed with a man's name on it.**"

"And if the man on the west bank is not the man who stands in a yard in four months."

"**Then somebody will be, and that is the whole of the sentence, and I do not know one of their faces.**"

Nobody in that room argued and the man of thirty-eight said one sentence at the end and it is thirteen words long and it is in the minute in his own words and it is the whole of what he did on the far bank of a river in nine days.

"**Then I have nothing to sell and I knew it on the fourth.**"

And a man of fifty-six who is a secretary of a cooperative of nine households was asked afterwards by the clerk of nineteen years what a man with four powers was doing writing a sale of a loom, and he gave a reason that is forty-nine words long and it is the last thing he said on the subject and he has not said it again.

"**He is an auctioneer and this is the only thing anybody has put in front of him in nine weeks that he could not price, and I do not think that is a failure and I think the district is going to have to learn to live with it.**"

---

On the thirteenth of September the man who drives salt came into the room where the terms were lying on a table and he asked to see them, and a clerk of nineteen years handed them to him, and he read both pages in eleven minutes standing up, and he is a man who does not read figures and the clerk read the second page aloud to him in the ordinary voice because the rule of a counter is that a document goes out twice.

He said nothing about the terms and he said one thing about the price, and it is in the minute in his own words and it is twelve words long.

"**Nine coppers a pound is what I have paid for two years.**"

"That is what the price was worked out from."

"**I know it was. I have known it since the eighth of September and I did not say anything on the ninth or the tenth or the eleventh, and I have been not saying something on purpose for four days because I did not want to be the man who had asked a man from a market to come and read my pans.**"

"And now you have said it."

"**Now a woman has said four sentences in a field and the price is the wrong thing and I can say it without it being about me.**"

The entry of the thirteenth of September says that a man of forty-five who drives salt, who came eleven miles twice in two years and then nine tons on a cart, said twelve words about a price in a room and that nobody argued with him, and that the twelve words were the price this district has been paying at its own counter for two years, and that a district with four thousand and one households has been buying its salt at nine coppers a pound from about four pounds a week and has never once asked what a ton of it costs anybody.

**AND THE FINDING OF THE THIRTEENTH OF SEPTEMBER IS THAT THE PRICE OF A LOOM WAS WORKED OUT OFF THE PRICE OF SALT, AND THE PRICE OF SALT WAS LEARNED FROM A HAULIER, AND THE HAULIER HAD BEEN SILENT ON PURPOSE FOR FOUR DAYS SO THAT A STRANGER COULD DO THE DIFFICULT PART, AND NONE OF THE THREE OF THEM KNEW THAT WAS WHAT WAS HAPPENING.**


---

And on the fourteenth of September a man of thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of thirty-eight what he was going to do with the two pages, and he was asked in four minutes in front of nineteen people and the man answered in four seconds and then said the other four sentences anyway, and the minute is the whole of it and it is in his own words.

"They are not a charter. They are not a security. They are not a term. They are a page with a correct set of terms on it for a thing that is not a lot, and I am not going to fold it up and put it in my coat."

"Where then."

"**In the folder, with the other sheets that were begun and not finished, and the folder has not a finding on it in my own hand, and the clerk of nineteen years put not a finding on the folder in the fourth month of this year and would like somebody to ask her why a folder needs a name, and nobody has asked her and I am not going to ask her tonight because she is tired and because it is a question about a folder.**"

"And the woman of thirty-one."

"**She is not a bearer and she is not in it and she is not a line in it and I have not written her name on either page, and a man of fifty-six told me on the eleventh that I had written a sale with no worker in it, and he is right, and I am going to have to go back to a district that has four thousand and one households and explain that the only thing anybody in this district cannot do is the one thing I am for.**"

A woman of fifty-one who keeps the only pair on the west bank had said a sentence on the third of July in a field and four people were in the room, and nobody connected the two out loud and the clerk of nineteen years entered the two in one line and did not join them and entered the fact that she had not joined them.

Then the man who drives salt said the last thing of the fourteenth of September and it is in the minute in his own words and it is forty-five words long, and he said it to the man of thirty-eight and not to the room, and the room heard it.

"**You came thirty-four miles to find out who signs for a ton, and you have found out that a woman of thirty-one is the signature, and I want to know what the district you come from is going to do on the first of October.**"

And a clerk of nineteen years said that the thirtieth of September was the last day of a responsibility held by a man of thirty-eight, and that it was a term and not a quarter, and that it was a number and not a person, and that a term is not a quarter because a quarter comes round again by itself and a term does not.

"And who ends it."

"**Anybody in the room, in a sentence, and none of them needs his permission. It was agreed on the fourteenth of August at a counter in front of thirty people and the figure has been read out once and it is a hundred and eighty-three coppers that nobody is owed, and it goes on a page in the market's own book with no name against it, and the answer is not to be struck.**"

The man of thirty-eight said nothing about that and the minute says he said nothing about it, and says that a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that a man who has said nothing about a figure for four days is a man who has worked out that the figure and the person are not going to be in the same place on the same day, and that this district has spent nine weeks finding that out one subject at a time and this is the first time two of them have been in the same sentence.

**AND THE FOURTEENTH OF SEPTEMBER ENDED WITH A PAGE IN A FOLDER WITH NO NAME ON IT, A MAN THIRTY-FOUR MILES FROM HOME WHO HAD SPENT NINE DAYS FINDING OUT THAT HE COULD NOT DO THE ONE THING HE IS, AND A PARTY OF TEN WHO WERE THIRTY-FOUR MILES FROM A COUNTER AND WENT BACK TO THIRTY-FOUR ON THE FIRST OF OCTOBER, AND NONE OF THE THREE THINGS WAS A FINDING AND ALL THREE WERE ENTERED.**

And on the fourteenth of September a man of thirty-one who is a signalman of the west bank's own crew walked one person to the second cart across about two hundred yards of bank and did not let go of her arm until she was on the step, and the minute says he did it in four minutes and that she did not ask him to and that he did not offer and that neither of them said anything about the thirtieth of last month, and that a man of thirty-nine had walked a man in the rain on that day and had written down that the man said the word which four times and could not finish any of the sentences.

And on the thirteenth of September the man who drives salt asked for the third channel to be run for two days to fill his two pans, which are under four feet of river and have been since the flood year, and the asking was made in a room and four people signed, and the sheet went to the guild inspector on the fourteenth of September and he certified it on the fifteenth.

Four signed. A woman of sixty-one who holds the ground the third loom stands on signed on the thirteenth and had been at a market two days' walk west since the eleventh, and she came back on the fourteenth and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing told her what she had signed, in nine minutes, in her own doorway.

"You signed a thing you were not in a room for."

"**Then it is a thing I signed and not a thing I was asked for, and those are two things and I have been in this basin for sixty-one years and I know which one of them is worse and it is the second one.**"

"And will you take it back."

"**No. And I want the entry to say that I understood it in four minutes and I did not take it back, and that a woman who has understood a thing and does not take it back is in a different position from a woman who was never asked, and that the only instrument for telling those two apart in this basin is a column for whether a person was in the room, and the sheet does not have one, and the man who made the sheet has told us so himself in nine seconds.**"

The man of thirty-four who mends fencing did not take the sheet back and he did not take the signature back and he entered the whole of it in the party's minute on the fourteenth, and the entry is nineteen lines long and it is the longest entry anybody on either bank has made in nine weeks.

**AND ON THE FOURTEENTH OF SEPTEMBER A SHEET WENT TO A MAN WITH A FORM OF TWENTY-NINE COLUMNS, AND THE SHEET HAS FOUR SIGNATURES ON IT AND FOUR OF THE FOUR ARE PERSONS, AND ONE OF THE FOUR WAS NOT IN THE ROOM, AND THE FORM HAS NO COLUMN FOR THAT, AND HE CERTIFIED IT ON THE FIFTEENTH IN ABOUT NINE MINUTES, AND NOBODY ON EITHER BANK HAD ASKED HIM NOT TO.**

And a man of twenty-six who has been in a bed of brine for a year and a half signed the sheet with a mark, and his mark is his own and it is how he signs and it has been how he signs since he was about nine, and the clerk of nineteen years entered that and then entered the rule, and the rule has been in this district since the seventh of March and it is thirty-eight words long.

A mark is a person's refusal of a name. A mark is not a person. **And a mark may not be used as a bearer, a holder, a witness, a responsible party, or a person who is asked.**

**AND THE FINDING OF THE FOURTEENTH OF SEPTEMBER IS THAT A MAN OF TWENTY-SIX WHO HAS BEEN IN A BED OF BRINE FOR A YEAR AND A HALF SIGNED A SHEET WITH A MARK HE HAS USED SINCE HE WAS NINE, AND THAT HE WAS ASKED WHETHER HE WANTED TO SIGN IT AND HE SAID YES IN FOUR SECONDS, AND THAT A PERSON WHO HAS SAID YES IS A PERSON WHO HAS SAID YES, AND THAT THE FINDING IS THAT A MARK ON A SHEET IS A MAN'S WAY OF SAYING YES AND IS NOT A MAN, AND THAT THE TWO OF THOSE ARE THE SAME THING DONE BY TWO PEOPLE WHO WERE BOTH ASKED.**

And the clerk of nineteen years said the other half, and the minute has it, and it is the reason the fifteenth of September is the shape it is.

"**The man of twenty-six was asked and he said yes and his yes is worth as much as anybody's. And the mark cannot be counted, and he knows it cannot be counted, and he signed with it anyway because it is what he has, and if the sheet needs a name off him he cannot give one and that is not a defect in the man, it is a defect in the sheet, and a defect in the sheet is not a thing this district can fix on the far side of a river with no press and four coppers.**"
