# Chapter 111: Nobody Signs for a Ton

A cart stood in a lane in Alder Reach from the evening of the thirteenth of August to the morning of the twenty-second, and on the morning of the seventeenth a man who does not read figures counted the sacks in it and got nine tons, and the count stayed in the lane and did not go into a book, and the cart was not on a rate sheet and was not on a toll board and was not in a yard.

He is fifty-eight and he has carried water at the ninth place for nineteen years and he has a tin under a bed with a hundred and eighty-three coppers in it that nobody is owed, and he counted the sacks because nobody had offered to, and he said afterwards that he counted them twice and got nine both times and that a ton is a weight and a weight is not a number a man can see.

The two men who came with the cart slept in a stable and the man who drives salt **slept in the cart for four nights** and washed in a trough at the end of the lane, and nine people in that lane were asked over four days what should be done with nine tons of salt and every one of the nine gave the same answer, which was that nothing should be done to it.

**THE NINE TONS ARE ON A CART AND THE CART IS IN A LANE AND THE LANE IS NOT A PLACE, AND A THING THAT HAS ARRIVED IN THIS DISTRICT HAS NOW BEEN IN IT FOR NINE DAYS AND NOTHING HAS HAPPENED TO IT AND NOTHING WAS EXPECTED TO HAPPEN.**

The cart's own paper said nine tons. A man who has been in this district for nine weeks weighed it by hand against a cart standard that is **a shade out of true** and said nearer ten, and a man who does not read figures said that nearer ten is not a ton and a ton is a ton, and the two figures have been in a book side by side since the eighteenth and **neither has been struck, and a district with two figures for a thing that has arrived has two figures and not a correction.**

Nobody in this district owns a scale. A licensed valuer of twelve years said in a yard on the eighteenth that he could not certify a weight he had not put on a certified beam, and that **a certificate of a weight nobody has weighed is a certificate of an opinion,** and that the opinion would be his and he did not sell opinions.

"Then what is nine tons."

"**It is what the paper says, and the paper was written thirty miles east by a man who was not lying to you, and a document is a better witness than a beam that is a shade out of true.**"

Nobody argued with the valuer and the minute says nobody argued with him, and says that about four people in that yard had understood before he spoke that two figures for one thing that has arrived is two figures and not a correction, and that this district has no instrument for a correction nobody owes.

---

The ward market bought a hundred and ten pounds of it on the nineteenth, at nine coppers a pound, and the sum is nine hundred and ninety coppers, and it went into the market's own book on a line of its own with a date on it and the name of the man who sold it in the seller's own hand.

> **SALT, ONE HUNDRED AND TEN POUNDS, AT NINE COPPERS A POUND. NINE HUNDRED AND NINETY COPPERS. BOUGHT OF A MAN WHO DRIVES SALT, ON THE NINETEENTH OF AUGUST, IN A LANE. THE FIGURE IS ON A LINE OF ITS OWN AND IS NOT THE NINE HUNDRED AND SIX OF A QUARTER AND IS NOT THE EIGHT HUNDRED AND NINETY-FOUR THAT IS CARRIED.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered the sum in four minutes and read the entry back, and the reading-back was done by the man who does not read figures, which is **the sixth time in nine weeks that the two of them have done it for each other,** and neither of them has suggested that it should stop.

Nine coppers a pound is the price he has been paying at this counter for two years, and the clerk asked him whether the market should pay less for nine tons than for a hundred and ten pounds, and he said no, and said why in nine seconds.

"**Because I have been paying nine coppers a pound for two years for four pounds a week, and there are four thousand and one households in this district and nobody in it has ever bought nine tons at once, and the price has not gone up because I bought more, and the price is not mine to go up or down.**"

Nobody argued with him and the minute says nobody argued with him, and says that a man who has carried salt for thirty years said, in the lane, in four seconds, that the market had bought a hundred and ten pounds and that he had brought nine tons.

"And the rest."

"**On the cart, and the cart goes back east on the twenty-second, and it is not me that is taking it back, it is that nobody here has signed for it.**"

---

Nobody in Alder Reach has ever signed for a ton, and **a ton is not a small thing to have not done once in nine years.**

The question was asked on the thirteenth of August in nine words in front of a toll board, and the man who keeps the board wrote it in the column in chalk without standing to do it, and it is the twenty-seventh sentence on that board and **the first one that is somebody else's question.**

He has **no standing to have written it** and he said so and gave his reason in nine words, and the reason is on the board's own bottom line, where anybody standing in front of it may write one sentence and will not be asked to explain it.

**WHO DO I WRITE THIS ON, WHEN IT IS A PLACE.**

A freight clerk of six years who is also the secretary of a cooperative, and who has spent those six years saying no, said on the eighteenth that the question had been copied into his cooperative's own minute without being asked, by a man who has been in six rooms in nine weeks.

"And what is the answer."

"**There is no answer and I have written the question in a minute of this cooperative in my own hand and I have not answered it, and a minute with a question in it is a thing anybody can read in four years and nobody can carry to a door.**"

Three people in that lane on the thirteenth had said afterwards that the question is about a rate and a place and an office at once. On the eighteenth a fourth person said it, and the fourth person was a paperer of thirty-eight who comes up that bank once a month for four houses, and she said the other half of it before she had said the first.

"**It is about a rate and a place, and it is about an office, and those are three things, and this district has spent nine weeks arguing about whether one of the three is the same as one of the other two, and nobody has asked the man who wrote the question which of the three he meant.**"

The man who drives salt was asked on the eighteenth and he said he did not know, and he said it in nine seconds, and the minute says he said it in nine seconds and that he had said it twice before in the lane without being asked.

"**I have been asking it since four minutes after I got off that cart, and I have not got an answer and I have not been told an answer is coming, and if somebody asks me which of the three I mean I will say all three, because a man who carries salt for thirty years does not know the difference between a rate and an office and has never had to.**"

Nobody in that lane said whether that was a good answer, and the minute says nobody did, and says that **a man who says he does not know which of three things he means is easier to go back to in four years than a man who has chosen,** and says that a man who has been in nine rooms in nine weeks wrote afterwards that a man who cannot tell three things apart and says so is more use in a minute than a man who can.

---

**THE REGISTRAR WAS TOLD ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF AUGUST AND SHE ENTERED IT AS A QUESTION AND SHE DID NOT ANSWER IT, AND THE ENTRY SAYS THAT A QUESTION THAT HAS NOT BEEN ANSWERED IS NOT A REFUSAL.**

She was not in the lane and she was not asked to be. She was told by a clerk of nineteen years in four minutes in a counting room over a chandler's shop, and a chandler of thirty-four who keeps a counter of oil on Ash Row was in the room and heard all of it, and the room has two windows and one door and the door is open in August.

She read the question out twice, which is the rule of the counter and not her own, and then she wrote it into the public lot book in nine copies on eleven boards in the yard of Lot 17, and the entry has a date on it and a heading and nothing under the heading.

> **WHO SIGNS FOR A TON THAT COMES TO A PLACE. ASKED BY A MAN WHO DRIVES SALT IN A LANE ON THE THIRTEENTH OF AUGUST AND ENTERED ON THE EIGHTEENTH OF AUGUST. NOT ANSWERED.**

The clerk asked her whether that was a refusal and she said it was not, and the reason she gave is in the minute and it is thirty-seven words long.

"**A refusal is a thing somebody said no to. A question is a thing nobody has answered yet, and this district has eleven refusals in it and no questions, and that is the worse of the two.**"

And then she said the other half, and it is in the minute in her own hand, and the clerk of nineteen years asked afterwards whether it was part of the answer and it was not.

"**Three people in a lane said that this is about a rate and a place and an office at once, and I have been told the three, and I have put the three in a line in that order, and I have not put anything between them, and I would like the entry to say that I have not put anything between them.**"

Nobody asked her why. The clerk of nineteen years wrote that down and did not ask and has said since that she nearly asked twice and did not, and the minute of the eighteenth says that nobody asked the registrar why she keeps two lines apart, and that it is the second time in nine weeks that nobody has.

---

The fifth board went up at the east end on the twentieth of August with the same four lines in it in chalk, and a fifth line ruled and empty underneath them, and a keeper who is a clerk of the ward market who is not the market and **who keeps the line book in the hours the counter is shut.**

It was promised on the eighth and it went up on the twentieth, and the reason it took twelve days is in the minute, and it is that **the man who promised it had to be asked four times and the fourth time he said yes in one word and did not say why.**

**THE FIFTH BOARD IS AT THE EAST END AND IT IS NOT ON THE LINE OF THE TOLL, AND FIVE BOARDS EXIST AND ONE OF THEM IS WHERE THE TOLL LANDED AND FOUR OF THEM ARE SOMEWHERE ELSE, AND ONE OF THE FOUR IS A PLACE WHERE A CART STOPPED.**

The man who drives salt had asked for it on the seventeenth. He had not asked for a board, because he did not know that a board could be had; he had asked the keeper of the fourth board for a fifth board that would take a ton, and the keeper had understood him, and the answer was no, and the reason is in the minute in the keeper's own words and is thirty-five words long, and **the board at the east end has now got a reason and the board at the ninth place has not, and neither of them has been asked for one.**

"**A board says where a toll landed. A ton is not a toll and a ton does not land anywhere, and a board that takes a ton is a rate, and I keep a board.**"

The man who drives salt said he had expected that, and he said the other half of it in four seconds, and the other half is in the minute.

"**I have been asking about a rate since four minutes after I got off that cart. I am asking about a place, because a place is the only thing that can be put on a thing and be found again. And I have been asking about an office for two years, and I did not know that was what I was asking until a man who mends fencing wrote it on a wall in July, and I have read that wall about nine times since.**"

Nobody argued with him, and nineteen people were in the lane, and a clerk entered that **four people in nine weeks have now said that this question is about three things, and that the four of them have all said it in a different order,** and that a clerk asked the man to put the three in an order and he said no.

Two miles west, at the fourth place, a board went up with the same four lines in chalk on the same day, and a boy of nineteen was standing under it and was not asked anything by anybody in that lane or in this one, and the entry of the twentieth says that he was not asked whether he would go thirty miles east and that **not asked is entered as not asked and is not a refusal and is not a yes.**

He has been at that place every day since the ninth of last month and he writes nothing down and nobody pays him and nobody has asked him. He is **not a keeper and not a bearer and not in a row,** and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing said in the lane that the boy is the only person in this district who is standing where the answer would be and is not being paid to be anywhere, and that this district has spent nine weeks building six instruments it cannot name and has had a seventh one standing at a place a train does not stop at since the ninth of last month.

Nobody wrote that down for four minutes and then a clerk of nineteen years wrote it down, and the entry says that three descriptions of one thing in one week is not a finding and is a coincidence until somebody puts a thing on the paper that joins them, and **that the fourth of those descriptions is four days old and unpaid and has not been asked by anybody.**

---

And then the salt man said the thing that the rest of the month is made of, and he said it on the twentieth of August in a lane at about four in the afternoon, and it took eleven seconds, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**I cannot answer it here and you cannot answer it here, and the reason is not that nobody in this district is clever enough. The reason is that the thing I am asking about is thirty miles east of this lane and not one of you has ever been to it.**"

Nobody in the lane had an answer to that and nobody claimed one.

And a clerk of nineteen years wrote the next line in her own hand and it is the twenty-eighth sentence on that board, and it is the first one in nine weeks that is about a distance: **THE ONLY PLACE THE QUESTION CAN BE ANSWERED IS THIRTY MILES EAST, AND NINE PEOPLE WROTE THAT DOWN, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WROTE IT TWICE BECAUSE HE WOULD NOT TRUST A THING HE HAD HEARD ONCE.**

The district has four thousand and one households and a rate of fifty coppers a quarter and a printed timetable and four toll boards and a question in a public book and no column anywhere for a thing that has arrived, and on the twentieth of August it found out that the reason is not the column.

It is that thirty miles east there is a thing that makes the salt, and the making of it takes something out of a person, and the taking is not asked for by anybody, and the person does not find out that it has been taken until afterwards and cannot say what it was.

He did not say all of that in the lane. He said the first half of it, and a man of thirty-four who mends fencing wrote the second half down in a yard on the twenty-first and read it out, and the salt man said it was right and had not said it, and the minute records both of those and records that nobody argued with either.

A boy of seventeen who is paid four coppers a quarter for an index and who is not Adrian Vale's apprentice asked one question at the end of it, and it is in the minute in the boy's own words and it is nineteen words long, and it is the fourth time in nine weeks he has asked a question in public and been right about it.

"**What happens to the salt if the water on the pans goes down and nobody can put it back.**"

Nobody in the lane knew, and the man who drives salt said he had been walking eleven miles twice a year for two years asking that question in a different form, and that nobody in this district had ever asked it in that form at all, and that a boy of seventeen had asked it in nine words in a lane full of salt.

And then a woman of fifty who is the water at the ninth place said the thing that about four people in that lane had been thinking since the thirteenth, and she is fifty and she has stood in her own doorway for eleven years, and she said it from the step and she did not put the pail down.

"**Nine tons of salt is not a shortage in this district. Nine tons of salt is a district that has stopped being able to say where a thing goes, and it has been that since about the eighth of March, and the salt is only the first thing that has come up the lane and stood still.**"

---

And on the evening of the twentieth a woman of fifty-one who keeps the only pair on that line came down the bank for the second time in a month and stood at the end of a platform in the lane and looked at nine tons of salt for eleven minutes and **said nothing at all about it,** and then said one sentence, and the sentence is in the minute.

"**Nine tons of salt is not a thing this district can hold and a pair is not a thing that holds it either, and I have come down here to say that I am the only pair at that place and I am not going thirty miles east and leave it.**"

A man of thirty-eight was in the lane and asked her whether she would be asked properly, in the five things, and she said yes before he finished the sentence, and then she said the rest of it **in the order she has used since the twenty-ninth of March, which is the condition, then the word, then the answer,** and the condition was that somebody else is at the ninth place on the nights she is not there, and the answer was yes.

**AND THE FIFTH BOARD WENT UP AT THE EAST END WITH THE SAME FOUR LINES IN IT AND A FIFTH LINE RULED AND EMPTY, AND A COLUMN WITH NOTHING IN IT IS WORTH MORE THAN A COLUMN WITH A PRICE IN IT FOR ABOUT FOUR DAYS, AND THE KEEPER AT THAT END HAS SAID SINCE THE TWENTIETH OF AUGUST THAT HE WILL NOT BE THE FIFTH DAY.**

The train on the siding had stood a hundred and eighty-seven days on the seventeenth of August and a hundred and ninety on the twentieth, and the five dates in a boy's index have no names against them, and the fifth of the five is the twenty-ninth of this month, and a boy of seventeen wrote both figures in his own hand on the twentieth and struck none of the old ones, and did not say what he thought the fifth date was, because he has said three times that it is not an oversight and there was no fourth time in a lane full of salt.
