# Chapter 105: Two Rooms and a Yard

A sheet of paper went up on eleven walls and on three toll boards on the morning of the twenty-third of this month, and it is the first notice this district has ever printed about a day on which four things come to an end, and the four things are written on it in the same type as everything else, and the notice has a cost of twelve coppers and it is not the market's twelve.

A correction of one sheet naming three places cost twelve coppers on the third of this month, three places at four. **This notice names four things and it is not a correction and it is not a road that owes it, and the man who printed it charged four coppers and would not be thanked and said he would not have been.** The registrar entered that the district now has two fours in one month and that the four on this notice is not the four on that sheet, and the reason she gives is the reason she gives every time.

> **ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF JULY, FOUR THINGS END. THE SECURITY POSTED BY MR VALE, WHICH IS ONE DAY A WEEK AND THREE BOUND COPIES OF A LINE BOOK. THE THREE WEEKS OF THE CHARTER, WHICH TURN BY THEMSELVES. THE TERM OF THE BOUNDARY AT THE WEST END, WHICH IS HELD BY TWO NAMED PEOPLE. AND THE ARRANGEMENT BY WHICH A MAN COMES TWICE A MONTH. NOBODY MAY EXTEND ANY OF THEM. THE ROOM THAT ASKED FOR THE FOURTH ONE HAS NOT AGREED TO END ANYTHING.**

The last sentence is on the notice because the woman of the column asked for it to be on the notice, and she asked for it in about four seconds, and the reason is in the minute and it is nineteen words long.

"**I would rather be ended by a room than by a flood, and if that is going on a board then the room has to be on the board too, next to me, in the same size.**"

The notice is one sheet and it went up on eleven walls and on three toll boards and on the board of eleven in the yard of Lot 17, and the sign-writer of sixty-one painted the four words at the head of it at the height of a hand on two of the boards because there was no money for letters, and the registrar entered that a notice with a heading nobody can see is a notice for about four days, and that the man who keeps the fourth board has a cloth and wipes the bottom line when it is wet.

A stranger took one of the eleven sheets cold on the afternoon of the twenty-third of this month, in about a minute, in a lane, and did not ask anybody anything, and nobody stopped him, and the minute says nobody stopped him.

"**He will have read four things ending on one day and no reason in the whole of it,**" said the man who sells nothing and has a use, on the twenty-fourth, in a public yard, to about nineteen people. "**A notice that says what ends and not why it ends is half a notice, and a half notice is worse than none, because a man with none will ask and a man with half will not.**"

Nobody argued with him and nobody printed the why, and the entry of the twenty-fourth says that the why is on a page in the public lot book and on a board in four places and is nine weeks long, and that a man who wanted it on a sheet was told it would cost four coppers a place and the market does not have four coppers, and that he said he would find the four coppers himself, and that he has been wrong about what it costs for four months and is not going to be argued out of it in a yard.

---

The fourth asking was composed on the twenty-fourth and the twenty-fifth of this month in two rooms, and this is the first asking in four months in which neither of the two women it is for was asked in a room.

She proposed the shape on the twenty-ninth of last month, on a shoulder, in the rain, in one sentence, and the room entered it without voting on it because there was nothing on it to vote about.

"**Ask me next month from the counting room and I will come down, and ask the keeper from the yard on the tide, and neither of them has to walk.**"

Nobody had worked out what she meant for nineteen days. On the twenty-third of this month a clerk of nineteen years worked it out in about eleven minutes, in front of about nineteen people, and the working-out is in the minute and it is four lines long.

> **EACH OF THEM IS ASKED FROM A ROOM AND EACH OF THEM ANSWERS WHERE SHE LIVES. THE COUNTING ROOM HOLDS ONE SHEET AND THE YARD ON THE TIDE HOLDS THE OTHER, AND NEITHER HOLDER GOES UP THE BANK, AND THE TWO ANSWERS COME DOWN ON THE SAME DAY IN THE SAME ROPE.**

"A person answered where she lives," said the clerk, "**is a person who was not brought to the answer. And the three askings before this one were all done in a room, and in all three of them a room had to be built at the top of a bank for the occasion, and in all three of them a person stood in a building that had been put there for her.**"

---

The counting room is over a chandler's shop and it has two windows and one door, and the man who asked there is a ward market freight clerk of six years who is also the secretary of a cooperative and who has spent those six years saying no.

He did not go up the bank. He sat down on the twenty-fourth of this month at about ten in the morning, with about nineteen people in the room, and he wrote the asking on a sheet in his own hand, and he read it out to the room, and the room corrected it in eleven places, and the corrections are all in the minute and nine of the eleven were made by a man who does not read figures.

**The asking was composed in thirty-one minutes, and nine of the thirty-one were the sixth question, and the sixth question has no name and belongs to a clerk of nineteen years and it is not on the wall, and this is the second time in nine weeks it has been used in a room where nobody was going to be asked anything.**

The nine minutes were the clerk's and not the freight clerk's, and she read the six questions off the back of a sheet in her own hand and read the sixth one twice, and the freight clerk wrote down what she read and corrected her twice, and the second correction was that a question which is not going to be asked a person is not a question.

"Then what is it," he said.

"**It is a question a person is going to be asked by somebody else in four days, and I am writing it now because the man who is going to ask it does not know it yet, and the woman who is going to answer it is four and a half hours up a bank, and neither of those is a reason to write it later.**"

He read the sheet back to her in the ordinary voice, and she corrected four words in it, and the four words are the four that matter and the minute lists them.

"I have spent six years writing things that other people agreed to," he said, halfway through, "**and I have never once written down a question for this district, and I want it entered that I did not know it was a different thing to do and that it took me the first nine minutes to stop sounding like a rate.**"

The sheet went up the bank on the twenty-fifth at about seven in the morning with a paperer of about thirty-eight, who goes up that bank for her own trade once a month and who has been going up it on the twenty-ninth for nine years and who is not going up it for this.

The asking cost the counting room a morning and it cost a man who does not read figures a morning, and it cost the ninth place nothing at all, and that is the first asking in four months in which the ninth place has cost nothing at all.

A man of about fifty who keeps the fourth toll board wrote that on the bottom line of his own board on the evening of the twenty-fifth, in chalk, and it is the twenty-fifth sentence on that board, and he said afterwards that he had been keeping the line for about a month and a week and that this was the first line on it that was about him.

The answer came back the same day and it is in the woman's own hand and it is two sentences long, and they are the same two sentences she has said in the same order every month since the twenty-ninth of March.

> I DO NOT LIKE IT, AND I SAY YES.

Nobody struck the first half. The minute enters that nobody struck it, and that the clerk of nineteen years read it out in the counting room on the twenty-sixth in the ordinary voice, and that about nine people were in the room, and that a man who does not read figures said afterwards that the first sentence is the one he would not have believed if he had not heard it out loud.

And the finding of the twenty-sixth of this month is that a person who is asked a question from a room answers it in the same two sentences she has used for five months, in the same order, and that this district has been reading those two sentences as a refusal and a concession and has never once asked her whether the order means anything.

The tenth hand asked her on the twenty-seventh of this month, in a doorway, in about four minutes, and she gave the answer in nine words and it is in the minute and it is not about the boundary.

"**I say the bad one first so that the person writing it down cannot forget it.**"

"And would you say it if the term were longer."

"**I would say it if the term were longer and I would say it if it were shorter, and the reason is not the length. The reason is that the man writing it down is about to write something down, and I want the sentence that stops him to be on the page above the sentence that lets him.**"

Nobody in the doorway argued and the clerk of nineteen years entered that the two sentences are in one order and the order is hers and not the room's, and that this is the fifth month running, and that a rotation which turns in the same order for five months is not a rotation and is a habit, and that the district entered that finding in March and has not acted on it.

"And I am not going to act on it," she said, when she was told. "**A habit that stops a flood is worth nine hours of a paperer a year and I have been the water here for eleven years and I know what I am looking at.**"


The answer came down the bank on a sheet with the reading-back on the back of it, and the reading-back is the fourth asking's and it is nineteen words long and it is in the freight clerk's hand and not in hers, and the twenty-four hours between the answer and the reading-back are entered as a delay and the delay is the counting room's.

---

The yard on the tide is lent, and it is lent by a woman of about forty-four who keeps a Road House on the tide, and she has lent it three times, and each time she has insisted that it be entered as not a security, and it is, and the reason she gives is that in about four years somebody will write that the Tidefall Road House stood behind a public charter, and it lent a yard.

**The man who asked in that yard is a sign-writer of sixty-one. He wrote the heading on the first toll board in this district on the sixth of last month in letters three inches high and it took nine minutes, and he painted a place's name on a board at the height of a hand at two in the morning in the rain so that a person who is frightened in the dark can find it with their fingers, and he has written the phrase that is at the top of three boards and on five hundred printed sheets twice in his own hand in thirty-one years of lettering jobs and has no office and no customer and nobody who gave him the words.**

He said afterwards, at the yard, in about eleven minutes, why he said yes to a job, and it is in the minute and it is the fifth thing he gave in that order and it is the longest.

"**I am the only man in this district that nobody has ever asked to read a document out loud. I write things on boards for a living and I have spent thirty-one years putting other people's words at the top of things and being asked for the letters, and on the twenty-seventh of last month a man asked me to write a place's name at the height of a hand and I did it for nothing and it was the first work I have done in about four years that was not a heading.**"

She lent it a fourth time on the twenty-fourth of this month and entered the same condition and added one word, and the word is *free*, and she said it out loud twice, and about nine people wrote it down, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that a word on a page is a thing a stranger can take cold, and she said she was sure of it and had been saying it since May and that nobody had ever put it in a sentence until he did.

The yard is cobbles and it is about a hundred feet by sixty and there is a tide wall at the back of it with a mooring ring in it, and the asking took nineteen minutes there, and the keeper of the only pair came down for it.

She is fifty-one and has kept the hut at the ninth place for eleven years and holds the only pair on that line. **She came down the bank for the first time in eleven years, and the entry says so, and the entry says the reason she gave is that the question had a day on it and that a question with a day on it is a question a person can answer from four hours away.**

She said her answer in that order and it has been her order since the twenty-ninth of March: the condition first, then the word, then the yes.

Then the reading-back. It is the paperer's trade to read a stranger's sentence back and the tenth hand's to read the answer, and the two of them did it in that order in the yard on the tide on the twenty-sixth of this month, and the answer was in her hand and not in his, and the man who keeps the only pair read it back to the woman who keeps the only pair and she corrected one word in it.

"Nobody has ever corrected a reading-back in four months," said the paperer, afterwards, in a counting room, to about nineteen people, "**and she corrected a word about a drainage cut, and she was right, and I had written it from her own book and I had got it wrong, and that is the first time in nine weeks that a person has corrected a document by saying what they needed in it and it was the sixth time overall.**"

---

**And the finding of the twenty-seventh of this month is that a rotation is not free. It is only ever free to the person who is asked.**

The three askings before this one put the whole cost on the bank. The first cost a signalman a day he was not paid for. The second cost a clerk of the ward market a day and a night. The third cost a paperer of about thirty-eight nothing at all, because she was going up that bank on the twenty-ninth for four houses and had been going up it for nine years, and a thing which turns at no cost is not a rotation.

**This one cost the counting room a morning and a man who does not read figures a morning and a yard on the tide a morning and a woman of fifty-one four and a half hours down a bank and four and a half hours back up it, and it cost the ninth place nothing, and nobody in this district has ever run a rotation that gets more expensive as it gets better.**

Nobody argued about that and the minute enters that nobody argued about it, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that nobody in this district knows which arrangement lasts longer, and that the district has had a free one and an expensive one and has had each of them for a month, and that in four years somebody will be able to say and not one person in that room is going to be able to say today.

---

And the other half of the twenty-seventh is the thing the yard on the tide was lent for, and it is a sheet of three lines and a man of sixty-one with a book of nine hundred four-word lines in his coat.

He has not been asked whether he will be there on the twenty-ninth. **The entry of the twenty-seventh says he will be, because he comes twice a month and the twenty-ninth is one of the two, and it says that a man who comes twice a month for nineteen years is not a substitute and is not a keeper and is the only person in this district that the railway cannot be sent to anybody else instead of.**

He is not a bearer and he holds no week and his only standing is the ending, and the whole of his job is nine words on the back of a mechanic's invoice, and nobody extended the term and nobody shortened it.

The three who hold the weeks were each told separately, and each of them was asked the same question, and the three answers are in three hands and none of them is the same.

**The keeper of the only pair is first and she said the week runs out and she does not know what she will do with the following week and would like that written down. The man of sixty-eight at the pumping engine eleven miles west is second, and he said that a week is a place a person stands and not a place a person is, and that his week costs him two days a month in somebody else minding his engine, and that he did that arithmetic himself in the middle of last month and put the two days in the minute in his own hand. The signalman of the district's own crew of about thirty-one is third, and he said he would not be here on the twenty-ninth of this month because he had a standing arrangement, and the standing arrangement is that he is two and a half miles up a bank reading somebody else's question back, and the arrangement was made in the middle of last month and is not a job and pays nothing.**

"**So there is a week with nobody in it on the twenty-ninth of this month,**" said the man who sells nothing, in a yard on the twenty-seventh, "**and that is the first time in nine weeks that this district has had a week with nobody in it, and I would like somebody to say out loud whether that is a problem with the rotation or a fact about the people in it.**"

Nobody said it out loud. The minute enters that nobody said it out loud and that about four people had clearly thought of it, and that one of the three who holds weeks has already told the other two that he will take the week after this one if they want it and be asked properly, and that being asked properly has not yet happened and is entered as not asked.

And the question that was put to him at a rail on the twenty-seventh of this month, by a man who mends fencing, in about four minutes, and it is the first question anybody has asked Elias Rooke since the fifteenth of last month, and it is in the minute in the asker's words and it is nine words long.
"**What happens on the twenty-ninth if nobody needs you.**"

And he answered in about eleven seconds and it is in the minute in his own words and it is the seventh thing he has said about himself in nine weeks and it is the shortest of the seven.

"**Then the term has ended it. That is what a term is for.**"

And on the morning of the twenty-seventh a copy of the notice went up the bank in a hand that is not the sign-writer's, because the sign-writer of sixty-one was in Alder Reach and would not come up, and the woman of the column read it standing in her own doorway with a pail in her hand and put the pail down, which is the fourth time in ten weeks that anybody in this district has entered her putting a pail down as an act.

The notice was pinned to the board at the ninth place at the height of a hand, and the pin is a nail, and the nail came off a gate on the bank, and the finding is that a nail is not a mark and a nail does not carry a job and a nail is free, and the whole of the finding took about four minutes and nobody was paid for any of it.

**And the four askings are now four, and the minutes are fifty-one and thirty-nine and forty-four and thirty-one, and the fourth asking is the first one in this district that cost the person who was asked nothing at all, and the district has entered that it does not know whether that is the cheapest one or the most expensive one. And the other thing the twenty-seventh settled is the standing of the two rooms, and it is the standing that matters, and it is not the boundary. A counting room can end a boundary by not asking again and a yard on the tide can end a boundary by not lending itself, and neither of those is ending anything: both of them are stopping, and a boundary which either room can stop is a boundary that will be stopped by a flood inside four years unless somebody goes and stands in one of the two rooms every month, and nobody has standing to go and stand in either of them.**

And the man of sixty-one came twice a month for nineteen years and nobody gave that away and nobody took it away, and on the twenty-seventh a man who mends fencing asked him what his standing was for and he said it was for the day it is needed, and there has not been one, and a man who cannot be replaced has not been worked out, and that is entered as not known and not as a delay, and reading it is not the same as doing anything about it and she would like both of those on the page.

The fifth of the five dates in a boy's index is in two days and he has still not been asked to write anything down properly in a room.

And the other half of the twenty-seventh of this month is the security, and the security ends at midnight on the twenty-eighth, and nobody told the man who keeps the board until the twenty-sixth, and he was told by a boy of seventeen in about four minutes at the bottom of thirty feet of platform.

"It ends at midnight," the boy said, "**and I have got the date off a board of eleven in the yard of Lot 17 and I have read it out to a man who keeps a board and I am seventeen and I am not anybody's keeper, and I would like it entered that I read it out and that you did not ask me to.**"

"**Nobody has to ask you to read a date off a board. A date on a board is a thing anybody can go and read, and that is the whole of what a date on a board is for, and it took you four minutes to say it and it will take about four years for this district to be as good at it as you.**"

And the finding of the twenty-seventh of this month is that a boy of seventeen read a date off a public board to a man who keeps a board, and that the man did not send him away, and that the man is not a bearer and the boy is not a keeper and neither of them has any standing in the four things that end, and that the reason the boy was up there at all is that he came up on the seventeenth for his own reasons and did not tell anybody what they were.

Nobody asked him what they were and the minute says nobody asked him and that a clerk of nineteen years wrote *not asked* in the margin and underlined it, and that he has underlined two hundred and six other things in nine weeks and that this is the first one she has underlined twice for no reason she can give.


Nobody in that yard wrote it down for about two minutes, and then the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down, and the entry says that the man who asked the question said afterwards that he had asked it because he did not want the man to have a job on a day when there was nothing to stop, and that the man who was asked had not been told what the question was going to be and had been told on the day, which is the standing condition, and that two people who know in advance will agree with you.

He had been asked in turn whether that was a kindness, and he had said it was not, and the reason it was not is in the minute in his own words. **A man who is paid to turn up and has nothing to turn up has been kept in a place for nineteen years for a reason nobody has ever asked him about, and this district is not going to be the reason.**
