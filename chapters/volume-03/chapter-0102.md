# Chapter 102: A Gap Is Not an Office

Four people went into four rooms on the eleventh of this month and the only instruction they had was one sentence, and the sentence was written out by a clerk of nineteen years and it is two lines long and it is the whole of the sixth search.

**Do not look for the name. Do not look for what it did. Look for what it would have had to write down.**

The four were a ward market freight clerk of six years who is also the secretary of a cooperative and who has spent those six years saying no, a licensed valuer of twelve years with a guild certificate in his coat, a clerk of the ward market who is not the market and who keeps the line book at the east end in the hours the counter is shut, and the man of thirty-nine who keeps the fourth toll board at the ninth place, who is a signalman of a bridge administration, who is not a bearer and who is not paid.

None of them is the reader. **The minute says in the registrar's own hand that none of the four is Adrian Vale, and gives the reason, and the reason is that a search for what a thing would have written down is a search of four books, and a man who can see inside an object is the wrong man to send into a room with a book in it.**

---

The roll office was first and it took about an hour, and the rate book on the desk in the room with the counter is nine years old and is copied twice a year and has four thousand and one households in it in a column, and it has one other column, and the other column has a heading and has never been written in.

The heading is nineteen characters long and it is ruled in the same hand as the rest of the book and it is about nine hundred households in this district that are in no row and are in that column nowhere.

> **PERSON NOT IN A HOUSEHOLD. RULED IN THE NINTH YEAR. NEVER WRITTEN IN. THE COLUMN IS ELEVEN CHARACTERS WIDE AND THE RULING IS THE DOING OF THE WORK AND THE WRITING IS THE PART NOBODY HAS.**

"Nobody writes in it," said the clerk of the ward market. "**Not because there is nobody to write. Because a person who is not in a household has got no page.**"

That is the first gap, and it is in the ninth year of a book that gets its columns ruled by somebody who is paid to rule them, and the ninth year is the year four drafts of a standing order with no expiry were written in this basin and not one of them was read by anybody who wrote the one before it.

Then the water office, which is on the second floor of a room with a door on it and which keeps a metered column at mile one and three quarters and which copies its book twice a quarter. The meter has a number and a date and a place and no person, and that is not a gap, because a meter belongs to an office.

"It is a gap anyway," said the licensed valuer. "**The meter is charged and nobody is told. The office takes the water and puts it against a number, and the number goes down the hall into a ledger, and the ledger has a column for the amount and a column for the quarter and not one column for the place, and the place is mile one and three quarters and it is standing on the same page and it is not in a column.**"

Then the guild room, which is a counter and a press and a smell of hot paper, where a certificate is filed by a person and a thing and a day. **Twenty-nine columns and not one of them is a place, and the valuer said it out loud in the guild room in front of a clerk who had never noticed it, and the clerk went and fetched a second certificate to look at, and the second one has the same twenty-nine.**

That is now the third time anybody in this district has confirmed it and the first two times were not a clerk.

Then the bridge office, on a published day, at a rail, with about nineteen people present because a bridge office is the one office in this district that can be asked, and its standing instructions run to about four pages and have a span and a date and a signature at the foot of every page and not one place.

> **A SPAN. A DATE. A LOAD. TWO SIGNATURES. THE STANDING INSTRUCTION IS GOOD AND IT HAS BEEN FOLLOWED FOR NINE YEARS AND IT CANNOT BE CHECKED AGAINST WHERE THE SPAN IS, BECAUSE THE SPAN IS WRITTEN ONLY ON THE PLAN AND THE PLAN IS NOT IN THIS ROOM.**

**And then the four of them were in four rooms, and the fourth room was the one that can be asked, and the one that can be asked gave them the true thing, and the true thing is that the four of them have the same shape and the same shape means nothing.**

---

The four of them compared notes on the afternoon of the thirteenth in the counting room over a chandler's shop, and the comparison took about two hours and it produced one arithmetic sum and one argument, and the argument is the reason the finding of the fourteenth is in two lines and not one.

The sum is that there are four of them, and the argument is about the fifth. The board keeper said the fifth was obvious, and he said it in about eleven seconds, and he was not being clever and he was being a man who has kept an empty column for a month.

"There are five offices this district deals with," he said, "**and we have been in four of them, and the fifth one is the roll of public offices, and if a thing like that was ever going to be on paper it would be on that roll, and I have been in that room four times in nine weeks and I have never once been asked for anything in it, and I would like somebody to send me back there tomorrow.**"

Nobody sent him back there. The clerk of the ward market said no, and the reason she gave is in the minute and it is four words, and the four words are *that is the fifth search*.

"That was the first search," she said, "**and the first search was run in May by five people for about two hours and it failed, and the reason it failed is that the roll does not have the office in it, and going back to it would be going back to the place where we already know it is not, and a man who keeps a column does not get sent to look at a place that has already been looked at.**"

"And if it is on the roll now," said the board keeper. "**It was not on the roll in May and there are five more months of paper in that room and about nine hundred households that came in since the flood year.**"

Then Ivo Stenn, who is six years in that office and who is the secretary of a cooperative and who has spent six years saying no, did the thing he is at his best at, which is to say a number out loud and make the room wait for it.

"It is not five months of paper," he said. "**It is a book that is ruled on a day and a column that is ruled when the book is ruled, and a column ruled in the ninth year for a thing that had not been written in eleven years is a column somebody ruled on purpose in the ninth year, and that is not the same as five months of additions.**"

"**And the twenty-nine columns on that certificate are twenty-nine columns in the ninth year too, and the first two of them are the man's name and his trade, and a man wrote that down in the ninth year on purpose and had never had to think about a place because he was not putting a place on it.**"

"So the shape is old," said the clerk of the ward market.

"**The shape is at least nine years old and I cannot tell you that because I have not seen the book in the ninth year and I have told you four minutes ago that the ruling is on purpose and I have not got a document for the ruling being on purpose, and I would like the minute to say both of those sentences and not only the first.**"

Nobody argued. The clerk of nineteen years wrote both sentences down and the entry is in the public lot book in the freight clerk's own words, and it is the first entry in nine weeks in which a man has corrected his own finding inside the finding.

**And the finding of the fourteenth of this month is that the four of them are the same shape, and the finding of the fourteenth is also that the same shape is the shape of a column that would take a place, and those two findings are in two lines and are not joined, and the reason they are not joined is that a column that would take a place is a thing four offices might all want and is not a thing four offices would all have built.**

A man in the room asked what the difference was between a thing four offices wanted and a thing four offices built, and it took about eleven minutes, and the answer came from the licensed valuer of twelve years and it is in the minute in his own words and it is nine words long.

"**Nobody builds a column for a thing they do not have.**"

---

She said so out loud on the afternoon of the fourteenth, in a counting room, with about forty people in it, and it is the seventh time.

The registrar is the registrar and not a vote and not independent, and she has refused six times to join two things, and on the seventh she gave a different reason, and she noticed that she had given a different reason, and she said that out loud as well, and the minute has both of those in it in her own hand.

"**I have held these apart six times and I am holding them apart a seventh, and I am going to say why I have said the same sentence six times before I say the new one, and the reason is that I have begun to be asked about it, and that has not happened in nine weeks and I am not going to do it in a hurry.**

"**The sentence has not changed because the paper has not changed. Two things that arrived separately stay in two lines until somebody puts a thing on the paper that joins them, and nobody has put a thing on the paper, and on the day somebody does I will join them in about four seconds and it will be the fastest thing I have ever done in this room.**"

Then the new reason, and it is not the old one, and the room noticed that too.

"**And the second reason is one I have not given before and I am giving it now, because if I do not somebody else will. A shape is not an answer. Four books with the same hole in them is a shape, and a shape in four rooms is still a shape, and in about four years a stranger will take four pages cold and put them side by side because it is the interesting thing to do, and he will see a system, and there is no system, and the only thing standing between that stranger and a system is a line in a book that says the four of them arrived separately.**"

She put her hand flat on the page, which is a thing she has done twice in nine weeks.

"**The second reason is the page. That is all it is. It is a page, and it is a wall, and a column is the only thing I have ever been able to offer anybody, and a column is not a protection and I have entered that once and I am entering it again.**"

---

And then a woman of about sixty with a stick asked her why, and the asking is in the minute, and the room had about forty people in it and none of them had asked and one of them did.

She is on no roll and she is in no row and she has been in four rooms in nine weeks, and on the seventh of this month a clerk wrote down that six people have now seen a kitchen under the Ward Nine tram line and that she is still not going to ask, because the answer would be in a room and not in a book.

She said she was asking because she had been in four rooms in nine weeks and had never once been told why a thing was being kept apart, and had always assumed it was a rule, and had come to the conclusion in about the ninth week that there was no rule and that everybody was being careful on purpose, and that this was worse.

That is a good sentence and it is in the minute in her own words.

"You have said the same thing seven times," she said. "**I would like to know what you are protecting. Not what the office was. What you are protecting.**"

And the registrar answered, and it took about four seconds, and the entry is in her own hand, and it is in the column for things that are not findings and not under the heading that says NOT ANSWERED.

"**I am protecting the person who reads it. In four years. I am protecting a person I will never meet, who will take a page cold and put four pages side by side because it is the interesting thing to do, and I would like him to find on the page that a woman in a room said the four of them arrived separately, and I would like him to have to decide whether to believe her.**"

"**And in about four years he will not believe me, because I am a person and the page is a page, and he will be right, and the rightness of that is the whole of what I have got.**"

Nobody in the room argued with it and the minute enters that nobody argued with it, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said afterwards that it was the fourth time in nine weeks that a person had said the true thing in a room and had then been asked what it was for.

---

The other half of the fourteenth is arithmetic, and it is four words long, and it is the reason this search was run and the reason it failed.

The water office keeps its book twice a quarter and copies it. The roll office keeps its rate book and copies it twice a year. The bridge office copies its standing instructions. The guild files everything and files nothing twice.

**A gap gets copied along with the book that has it in it, and the finding of the fourteenth is that a thing nobody in this basin wanted has now been duplicated by three offices' own routines, and that the number of the same gap in this district has gone from four rooms to about seven places, and that nobody has to do anything at all for it to keep going.**

The man who keeps the board was told this in a room and did not appear to enjoy it, and he said so, and what he said is in the minute.

"I came down that bank to get a piece of paper with three columns in it and I have come away with a count of seven," he said, "**and I want it on the page that the seven is the wrong way round. A month ago I had one column and I knew exactly what it was for, and now I have seven of the same hole in seven books and I still do not know what it is for, and the difference between those two men is that one of us has a piece of paper in his hand and the other one has seven.**"

That is in the public lot book in four hands and none of the four is hers, and the tenth hand wrote the last line of it in his own hand and it is one sentence long.

"**This district has found a way to spread a thing it cannot explain without anybody deciding to spread it, and the instrument for spreading it is a clerk who copies a book twice a quarter, and that clerk is owed eleven coppers and is not paid.**"

---

And then the fifteenth of this month came and went, and what happened on it was that nothing happened on it, and that is the first time in nine weeks that a day this district published in advance did not produce the thing it was published for.

The hour opened in the morning, because the limit in the book is a date and not a clock, and the page anybody can read says in its own words that the next permitted hour is not before the fifteenth of this month. He was in the counting room from about nine and had said in advance that he would be there, and about twenty people came, and nobody asked him anything at all.

They waited. It is worth saying what twenty people did in a counting room for about seven hours, because it is the only ordinary thing that has happened in one in nine weeks. Two of them swept the boards because the boards take chalk dust and the clerk had asked for it. A man of about fifty sat on the windowsill and cleaned a lamp that he had brought. Four people who had come together argued about a bushel of white paint for about half an hour and settled it themselves. The man who keeps the fourth toll board did not come, because he was up the bank and could not come.

The boy of seventeen came, because he is paid four coppers a quarter for an index and an index is a thing you carry, and he spent the afternoon doing arithmetic on the back of a sheet and would not say out loud what it was for, and at about three in the afternoon he said out loud what it was for.

"**Seven places in a district of about four thousand and one households is not a pattern. It is seven. A pattern needs enough of them to be a pattern and nobody in this room knows how many is enough, and I am not going to be the one who writes a number at the bottom of a page and makes everybody else live under it.**"

"And you have written the number anyway," said the man who waits.

"**I have written seven, because seven is what I counted, and I have written under it that I do not know what seven is, and those two lines have to travel together or one of them is a lie.**"

And the count of not-askings did not move that day. A woman of forty-five was not asked for a fifth month on the fifteenth of this month and was not asked for anything else, and the entry says she was not asked and that not asked is in the count, and that the count stands at four not-askings and one standing refusal, and that the registrar entered on the seventh of this month that a count is a column and not a protection and has entered it again today because the room was full of people and the wall is where it goes.

At about two in the afternoon a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked somebody whether the reader was going to read the iron today, and the somebody said the reader was forty feet away with a slate, and the man said that he would wait, because a man who has published the hour of a reading is entitled to wait for it.

**The finding of the fifteenth of this month is that a man who has published the hour of a reading is entitled to wait for it, and that nobody had told him that, and that he worked it out, and that he waited from about nine in the morning until about four in the afternoon, and that the hour went by and he was not asked and he did not offer.**

Nobody in that room wrote it down for about two hours. The clerk of nineteen years wrote it down, and the delay is entered, and it is the fourth delay this district has published, and the reason she gives is that a day nobody has to pay anything for is a day the district does not think of as a day yet, and that this is the same failure as the one about a rotation that turns at no cost, and that the district has now failed in the same direction twice in a fortnight.

He said one thing about it, at about four in the afternoon, to the man who had waited, and the man wrote it down himself in his own hand, and it is the fifth sentence anybody has entered under a heading that says NOT ANSWERED and it is a question nobody has to answer.

"**You did not ask me and I did not offer, and neither of those is a thing I did, and I would like the entry to say that the reason is that there was nothing to read for, and not that there was something to read for and I was tired.**"

---

The sixteenth of this month is where a room asked him a different question, and the question is not about what the office was, and it is the next chapter.
