# Chapter 680: On The Twenty-Fifth Of The Seventh Month A Clerk Entered That Two Of The Four On That Wall Are Out And Is Not Going Near Either

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the twenty-fifth of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the near leg of that second table was out of square in the way it was out of square yesterday, and that was the two hundred and eighth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and seventy-eight mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and nineteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is twelve and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that a page a stranger carried down that lane on two mornings this month is not a document this district owns and did not go in as one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and ten times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that this is the twenty-fifth morning of the seventh month, that the count of counted months in this district is six, and that the seventh of them has not been counted by anybody. A clerk in that yard counted a month off a board in the open in daylight in front of about nineteen people in the last month of the sixth and has not counted one since.

"**The board carries five hundred and seventy-eight, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and ninety-four days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and eight, and five hundred and sixty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past eleven, in about 60 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Two of the four figures on that wall are out and I have entered that they are out this morning and I entered it again at about four yesterday, and I am not going to correct either of them and I am not going to enter a third of them beside them, and about four of you have worked out that a figure somebody has to enter because it is standing there is not a figure anybody has to believe, and about four of you have worked out that a man in that yard has been reading those four out loud twice a morning for four hundred and five mornings and has never once read either of the two that are out any differently from the other two, and about four of you have worked out that I have not asked him about it and I am not going to, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that no figure on that wall was altered this morning and that she is not entering a third of them beside the two that are out, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about a quarter past eleven and said one thing out loud, in about 56 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got a number for what that man says out loud and I have got a number for what it is for, and about four of you have worked out that the first of those is four every morning and has been four for about four hundred mornings, and about four of you have worked out that the second one is much bigger and that I cannot put it in a figure, and about four of you have worked out that I have been keeping the first one for two years and the second one for two years and that I have never once written the second one down, and about four of you have worked out that a boy who has got a number he will not write down is not a clerk and never will be, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who has a number he will not write down is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and was at the near end of that second table until about ten and was not in that yard at about eleven, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that two of the four figures on that wall are out and that she is not going to correct either of them, and that a boy said out loud after her that he has got a number for what the man of fifty-six says and a much bigger number for what it is for and that he cannot put the second one in a figure. About four of them have said that this yard has spent a month putting one small number beside one large one and has not closed the distance between them. About four of them have said that a boy keeping the second number for two years without writing it down is the only person in that yard holding both halves of it, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with that.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that the road keeper has never once been asked to put a number on anything.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a boy said out loud this morning that he has got a number he will not write down, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eighteen nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and nineteenth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and eighteen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and twenty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and eighty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and eighty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and fifty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and sixty-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and ninety-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and thirty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and twenty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk saying a thing out loud about two figures being out is not a fourteenth thing and a boy saying a thing out loud about a number he will not write down is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a boy holding a number for two years without writing it down is holding it for himself and not for this district.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and sixty-nine. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and seventy-eight mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy who will not write a number down is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who said no is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and those are two fifths and they are not the same fifth and one of them was paid and the other one was not, and a boy saying out loud in that yard that he cannot put a number in a figure is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifth morning, and that a boy of about nineteen said out loud this morning that he has got a number for what that man says and a much bigger one for what it is for, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a boy and a man about nine feet apart in that yard are both holding a number this morning and neither of them has offered the other one anything.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fifth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that two of the four figures on that wall are out and that neither was altered, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard at about eleven and that she is entering no reason for that, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is twelve and moved on two mornings this month with a person and a reason in that yard before the hand each time, and that the count of counted months is six, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth, and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and is not paid, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
