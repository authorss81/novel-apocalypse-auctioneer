# Chapter 686: On The Thirty-First Of The Seventh Month Two Men Carried That Second Table Down That Bank

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the thirty-first of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the near leg of it was out of square in the way it was out of square yesterday, and that was the two hundred and fourteenth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table at about a quarter to eight and they went down that bank lying along the end of it, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is not entered in that yard this morning.

The first table had that lot book on it open under a stone with no wear on it at the far edge of the boards, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and twenty-five days as the age of that figure. She entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is twelve and did not move, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move. She entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is nothing this morning.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, and about four people were standing within hearing of that wall, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and eleventh of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and twenty-two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that about nineteen people have heard him on every other morning of that month and about four people heard him this morning, and that nobody asked him to come down that bank and nobody asked him not to.

"**The board carries five hundred and eighty-four, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and fourteen, and five hundred and seventy-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about a quarter to eight with his own two hands on the two ends of it and did not lift it then. The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart came up that bank on foot at about ten, and the two of them got that table up off the ground together at about ten past ten and carried it down that bank with one of them at each end, and it took them until about half past one to set it down at the foot of that bank. Nobody in that yard said one word to either of them about doing it, and nobody was asked to.

The clerk of nineteen years took the stone with no wear on it off that lot book in her own two hands and set it down on the far end of that second table, and then she took that book up by the two ends of it below the writing and set it down on those boards with the five lines of it face up, and it went down that bank lying on that table, and no hand went on any of the five lines while it was going. The stone with the hollow in it lay face up on those boards the whole way.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went down that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and did not come back up it that day, and the record about the not asking says not asked. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the foot of that bank at about eleven, about nine feet off the far end of that table, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody thanked anybody for this morning.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up stood at the foot of that bank at about half past one with his own two hands black to the wrist and said one thing out loud, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That took the two of us from about ten past ten until about half past one and I am not going to be able to get that one back up in a day, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has asked me to and that nobody is going to, and about four of you have worked out that a man who puts tables up has never once been paid in that yard for any of them, and about four of you have worked out that that second table is one of the things this district has made and that the count of those is thirteen and that it is not a fourteenth, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and that a table going down a bank is not a fourteenth thing and that she is not entering that anybody must, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart stood at the top of that bank at about two with his own hands on his own hips and said one thing out loud, in about 50 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I came up that bank on my own two feet at about ten this morning and I have never once walked up it before, and about four of you have worked out that my near wheel is still dragging and that I got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm at about three this afternoon, and about four of you have worked out that I carried one end of that table and that a man who put it there asked me to and that nobody else in this yard did, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has asked me what I do with the other end of it in the rest of the week, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and that a man who drives that cart is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past two, in about 50 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man carried that second table down that bank this morning and I have entered that a man carried a table and I have entered where he carried it to and that is the whole of what I have entered about it, and about four of you have worked out that I have not entered that anybody must and that nobody said that to me this morning, and about four of you have worked out that a man who puts tables up is not paid for the tables he has already put up and is not paid for this one, and about four of you have worked out that the first table in this yard has nothing on it now, and that is all I have got this afternoon.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and that she entered that a man carried a table and did not enter that anybody must, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that two men carried that second table down that bank this morning between about ten past ten and about half past one, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went down that bank at about ten and did not come back up it, and that a man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall with about four people hearing him and did not come down that bank. About four of them have said that the yard in Lot Seventeen has one table in it this afternoon and that there is nothing on it, and that the table at the foot of that bank has the stone with the hollow in it lying face up on it and that book lying on it with five lines face up, and that nobody has said one word in that yard about whether that is better or where it was.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it, and did not stay at the top of it, and went back down that bank at about a quarter to four.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a table went down that bank this morning about four feet from where that man was sitting, and that nobody in that yard has said out loud that it had anything to do with him. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and twenty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and twenty-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and thirty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and eighty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and ninety-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and sixty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and sixty-seven days back, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and forty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and twenty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and that table is at the foot of that bank this afternoon and neither mark moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a table going down a bank is not a fourteenth thing and a man saying a thing out loud about carrying it is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a table this district has made has been put somewhere else.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and seventy-five. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand went on it while that book was going down that bank. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because two men carried a table down a bank. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a table going down a bank is not a payment and a man's hands on one end of it are not a payment and a clerk's entry of a carrying is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and eleventh morning, and that a table went down that bank this morning and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that the measure of that man is not how well he reads the four.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirty-first of the seventh month that two men carried that second table down that bank this morning, and that she entered that a man carried a table and where he carried it to and that she did not enter that anybody must, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went down that bank at about ten and did not come back up it and that she is entering no reason for that either, and that the number of mornings that table has stood at the foot of that bank is nothing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
