# Chapter 676: On The Twenty-First Of The Seventh Month A Clerk Said Out Loud That A Counter Is A Place And Is Not On The List Of Four

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the twenty-first of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the ground at the north end of that yard had gone soft overnight where the water stands against the fence, and that was the two hundred and fourth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and seventy-four mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and fifteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eleven and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven this morning and that she is entering no reason for that either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and first of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that he was in that yard at about eleven yesterday while a man was asking out loud who is going to answer a stranger, and that he read his four through the whole of it and has never once said so himself.

"**The board carries five hundred and seventy-four, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and ninety days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and four, and five hundred and sixty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past eleven, in about 57 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Those three pages up that bank are the only three things in this district a stranger can walk up to and read with nobody standing next to him, and about four of you have worked out that I have read the first two lines of all three of them and that I have not read to the end of any of them this month, and about four of you have worked out that a clerk reading a stranger's page where it lies is not this district owning it, and about four of you have worked out that a counter is a place and that this district has four places in it where those three lines can be read, and that a counter is a place and is not one of the four, and that the count did not move this morning, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a shop counter is a place and is not on the list of four and did not go on it, and that a page a stranger carried down a lane is not a fifth document, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about a quarter past eleven and said one thing out loud, in about 49 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About nineteen people in this yard heard a question asked out loud in this yard yesterday morning and about four of you have worked out that I counted the quiet after it and not the question, and about four of you have worked out that a question is a thing a person says and a quiet is a thing a yard has got, and about four of you have worked out that a clerk in this yard has entered both of them in the same book, and about four of you have worked out that I have not asked that clerk to enter anything of mine and that is the first thing I have not asked anybody for since I got here, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who counts a quiet and not a question is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight and was at the near end of that second table until about ten and was not in that yard at about eleven, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and about four of them have said since that he was at that end of that table at about eleven yesterday an hour and a half after he was not at it, and that nobody in that yard has asked him why and he has not said.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that those three pages are the only three things a stranger can walk up to and read with nobody standing next to him, and that a boy said out loud after her that he counted the quiet yesterday and not the question. About four of them have said that a page a stranger carried down a lane is not a fifth document and a counter is not a fifth place. About four of them have said that the woman who keeps a scale up that bank has not been asked one thing about those three pages by anybody in this yard this month. About four of them have said that she is not in the column either and she has not been asked to be.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a clerk has said out loud in that yard that a stranger can read three pages with nobody standing next to him, and that a road keeper goes past that counter twice a day and has never once stopped at it. About four of them have said that is a thing about a clerk and not about a road.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and set the shafts down at the top of that bank and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that a counter is a place and is not on the list of four, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and fourteen nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fifteenth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and fourteen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and twenty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and seventy-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and eighty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and fifty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and fifty-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and ninety-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and thirty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and nineteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk saying a thing out loud about three pages is not a fourteenth thing and a boy saying a thing out loud about a quiet is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that reading a page is a thing a person does and three pages on a counter are still three pages on a counter.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and sixty-five. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and seventy-four mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a clerk read the first two lines of three pages a stranger had brought. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a clerk who read two lines is not in it and a boy who counted a quiet is not in it and the woman who keeps a scale is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk reading a stranger's page where it lies is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a clerk read the first two lines of a stranger's page this morning and the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up did not read one line of it. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and first morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two. About four of them have said that a man who has said four figures out loud four hundred and one mornings in the ordinary voice has still never been asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-first of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard at about eleven this morning and that she is entering no reason for that either, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven and that nobody has asked him why, and that the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and a shop counter is a place and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
