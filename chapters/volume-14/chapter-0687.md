# Chapter 687: On The First Of The Eighth Month A Clerk Said Out Loud That A Count Of Nothing Is Not A Count Of Nobody And Entered One

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the first of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and the first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen has had nothing on it since about ten past ten yesterday morning, and that was the two hundred and fifteenth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard. The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it, and that lot book was lying on it face up with five lines on it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and twenty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is twelve and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is one this morning and that it was nothing yesterday morning and that a count of nothing is not a count of nobody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twelfth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and twenty-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that he said the four this morning at about a quarter to eight at the top of that bank with about nineteen people within hearing of it and that the table they have been standing round for about a month is at the foot of it, and that nobody has said one word to him about that.

"**The board carries five hundred and eighty-five, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and one days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and fifteen, and five hundred and seventy-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past eleven, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That table has stood at the foot of that bank one morning and I entered nothing yesterday morning and one this morning and a count of nothing is not a count of nobody, and about four of you have worked out that I have been at this book nineteen years and that this is the first number I have entered in it that is a count of mornings a thing has been somewhere and not a count of days, and nobody has asked me whether that is the right way to count it and I am not going to stop and ask, and the first table in this yard has nothing on it and I am the only person in this district who writes figures down and I have got nothing to write this morning at the near end of it, and there is not another word of it in me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a count of nothing is not a count of nobody, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is one, and that she entered it this morning at about a quarter to eight and that the thing she said out loud was not entered before she said it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no, and about four of them have said since that the two of them have been at that table about nine feet apart for the second morning running and that neither of them has said one word to the other one.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was in that yard at about ten with nothing in his own hands and said one thing out loud, in about 40 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have not put a card under that leg and I am not going to, and about four of you have worked out that I have been at that leg about four times a week for a month and that it is going over further down there than it did up here, and nobody has asked me to go and look at it and I have not gone, and I put that table up and I have not put the first one up and nobody in this yard has asked me about either of them, and that is all of it this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that a count of nothing is not a count of nobody, and that a man who puts tables up said out loud after her that he is not going to put a card under that leg. About four of them have said that the yard in that bank has one table in it this morning and that the first table in it has nothing on it, and that about nineteen people were in that yard by ten in the ordinary way. About four of them have said that a man who put that table up is standing in that yard this morning with nothing in his own hands and that nobody in it has asked him what he is going to do today. About four of them have said that a clerk has entered a number of mornings for the first time in nineteen years and that a man who puts tables up has said out loud that he is not going to do a thing he has been doing for a month, and that neither of those two things has been asked about by anybody.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that the yard in that bank has had one table in it since yesterday morning and that the road keeper has gone over the top of that bank twice a day through all of it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that a count of nothing is not a count of nobody, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and twenty-five nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and twenty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and twenty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and thirty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and ninety days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and ninety-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and sixty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and sixty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and forty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and thirty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk entering a count of mornings for the first time in nineteen years is not a fourteenth thing and a man saying a thing out loud about a card he is not going to put under a leg is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a thing a clerk finds out how to count is not a thing the clerk has made.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and seventy-six. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the two pages on the end of that second table are at the foot of that bank and are not on any table in that yard, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a clerk said out loud that a count of nothing is not a count of nobody. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a clerk is not in it and a man who puts tables up is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk entering the first morning a table has stood at the foot of that bank is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank this evening to read them and did not look at the fourth one. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twelfth morning, and that a clerk said out loud this morning that a count of nothing is not a count of nobody, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that the table he has not been asked about is at the foot of that bank and the wall he reads every morning is at the top of it, and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what to do with that distance.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the first of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is one and that it moved this morning from nothing, and that a count of nothing is not a count of nobody, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is twelve and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
