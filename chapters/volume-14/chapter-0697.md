# Chapter 697: On The Eleventh Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Cuts Reeds Stood At The Foot Of That Bank And Was Not Given One Word

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the eleventh of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and about half of those leaves had gone over the west wall of that yard in the night, and that was the two hundred and twenty-fifth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard. The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it, and that lot book was lying on it face up with five lines on it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and thirty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is eleven this morning and that it moved this morning from ten, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-seven was standing at the near end of that second table at about nine with his own hands behind his own back and he stayed there until about ten and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man who cuts reeds came down that bank at about nine this morning and stood at a table with a book open on it and that nobody in that yard said one word to him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was at the foot of that bank at about nine and entered no reason for it and entered that she did not ask him one thing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-second of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and forty-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time.

"**The board carries five hundred and ninety-five, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and eleven days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and twenty-five, and five hundred and eighty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 63 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and sixty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man was at the foot of that bank this morning and I entered that he was there and I did not ask him one thing and I am not going to, and about four of you have worked out that he said a thing out loud in this yard once before and that he said then that he had not got a word for what a man does when a man says two things out loud on one morning, and about four of you have worked out that he has not been given one this morning either and that nobody in this yard has worked out a way to give him one, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank is eleven and that I entered it before he came down, and about four of you have worked out that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who cuts reeds is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that nobody has been appointed to that column, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his own two hands in his own pockets and said one thing out loud, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been at that table four mornings this week and I am not going to come down that bank on the mornings when I have got nothing to do at that end of it, and about four of you have worked out that that is about four mornings a week and that nobody has asked me to come or to stay, and about four of you have worked out that I came down that bank this morning to say that and that I said it standing up, and about four of you have worked out that a man who puts tables up can say a thing out loud in a yard and still not be in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that she entered no request and no refusal beside it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man stood at the near end of that table at about nine with his own hands behind his own back and that there were three of them at that table this morning and that none of the three spoke.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who cuts reeds came down that bank at about nine this morning and stood at the foot of it and was not given one word. About four of them have said that a clerk said out loud in that yard that she did not ask him one thing and is not going to, and that he said a thing out loud in that yard once before and has not been given one this morning either. About four of them have said that a man who puts tables up said out loud this morning that he is not going to come down that bank on the mornings when he has nothing to do at that end of it, and that about four mornings a week is a figure nobody in that yard has entered.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it, and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man was at the foot of that bank at about nine and a clerk entered that he was there and entered no reason, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and thirty-five nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and thirty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and thirty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and forty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and seventy-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and seventy-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and fourteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and fifty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and forty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man coming down that bank to stand at a table and being given nothing is not a fourteenth thing and a man saying a thing out loud about how often he will come down a bank is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man standing somewhere is not a thing anybody else has made.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and eighty-six. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the two pages on the end of that second table are at the foot of that bank and are not on any table in that yard, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man stood at that table and was given nothing. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a man who cuts reeds is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man standing at a table and being given nothing is not a payment and a man saying how often he will come down a bank is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the ninth evening and did not look at the fourth one. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-second morning, and that a man stood at the foot of that bank at about nine and was not given one word, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and twenty-two mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the eleventh of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is eleven, and that a man who cuts reeds was at the foot of that bank at about nine and was not given one thing and was not asked one thing, and that a man who puts tables up said out loud in that yard that he is not going to come down that bank on the mornings when he has nothing to do at that end of it, and that she entered that and entered no request, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
