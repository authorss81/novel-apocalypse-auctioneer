# Chapter 668: On The Thirteenth Of The Seventh Month A Boy Counted The Things Said Twice And Left The Number Out

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the thirteenth of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the shade of that fence rail had moved about two inches along the top of them since the day before, and that was the hundred and ninety-fifth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and sixty-six mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is ten and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that neither of the two men who stand in that yard was standing in it at about eleven this morning, and that both of them were at the bottom of that bank, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-third of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and eighty-six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that a man who reads four figures every morning of his life has still not been asked what one of them is for, and that that is now three hundred and ninety-three mornings.

"**The board carries five hundred and sixty-six, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and eighty-two days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and ninety-six, and five hundred and fifty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

At about ten past eleven the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have entered that neither of the two men who stand in this yard was standing in it at about eleven this morning and that both of them were at the bottom of that bank, and about four of you have worked out that is the second morning this week I have entered that and the first morning this year I entered a reason for a person, and about four of you have worked out that the reason I entered that morning was something I heard said out loud in this yard, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has said one word out loud in this yard this week about either of those two men, and that is not me keeping something back, it is that nobody has said anything, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk who enters no reason is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about a quarter past eleven and said one thing out loud, in about 43 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been counting the things about four of you have said twice in this yard, and about four of you have worked out that the number is not the same every morning, and about four of you have worked out that the things said twice this month are all about a person and not about a figure, and that is a thing I found out and I am not going to say the number out loud because I said it out loud in this yard about a fortnight ago and about four of you have worked out that nobody asked me to say it again, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who counts what is said twice is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and was at the near end of that second table until about ten, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with his spade over his own right shoulder and mud to the knee of his own left leg, and set it up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it and went the three steps to the rail and put his own two hands on it, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for.

At about eleven there were about nineteen people standing in that yard and not one of those two men. About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that nobody has said one word out loud in this yard this week about either of those two men, and that a boy said out loud after her that every thing said twice in this yard this month is about a person and not about a figure. About four of them have said that the boy has a number for it and has not said it. About four of them have said that a clerk has a reason and does not have one, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with two people in one yard holding one thing each and not saying it.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a boy in that yard has been counting what is said twice in it and that the road keeper has said one thing twice in his life that anybody in that yard knows of. About four of them have said that is a thing about a boy and not about a road.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a boy said out loud this morning that everything said twice in that yard this month is about a person, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and six nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and seventh night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and sixteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and seventy-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and seventy-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and forty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and forty-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and eighty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and twenty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eleven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a boy counting what is said twice in that yard is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk entering that nobody has said a word is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a count of what people say is a count of a morning and not a morning.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and fifty-seven. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and sixty-six mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a boy said a thing out loud about what people say twice. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy who counts what is said twice is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who said no is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a boy saying out loud in that yard that everything said twice this month is about a person is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a boy in that yard counted the things said twice this morning and the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up did not say one of them. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-third morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two. About four of them have said that a man who reads four figures twice a morning and a boy who counts what is said twice are in the same yard about nine feet apart and have never once said a word to each other about it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirteenth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that nobody in that yard has said one word out loud this week about either of the two men who were at the bottom of that bank this morning, and that she is entering that and entering no reason, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth, and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and is not paid, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is ten and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
