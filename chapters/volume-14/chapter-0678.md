# Chapter 678: On The Twenty-Third Of The Seventh Month A Clerk Said Out Loud That A Figure Somebody Has To Enter Is Not A Figure Anybody Is Paid By

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the twenty-third of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and a stranger went down that lane at about half past nine with a page in his own right hand and did not stop at that yard, and that was the two hundred and sixth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and seventy-six mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and seventeen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eleven and did not move at about a quarter to eight, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and third of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that a stranger went down that lane at about half past nine this morning and the man of fifty-six was reading the four off that wall at the same moment about two hundred feet below him, and that neither of them looked up and that has happened now twice this month.

"**The board carries five hundred and seventy-six, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and ninety-two days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and six, and five hundred and sixty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past eleven, in about 62 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a figure on a page in this district and it names a man and it does not say what he is to be used for, and I have written a figure about a day, and I have written a figure about a night, and about four of you have worked out that a figure somebody has to enter because they have to enter one is not a figure anybody is paid by, and about four of you have worked out that I have been entering figures in that book for nineteen years and not one of them has ever been handed to a person, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that a book full of figures is not that thing and never was, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a book full of figures is not a payment and did not pay the fifth of the five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about a quarter past eleven and said one thing out loud, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man went down that lane at about half past nine this morning with a page in his own right hand and he did not stop at this yard and he went on down past the bottom of that bank, and about four of you have worked out that I watched him go and that I said it out loud in this yard before anybody asked me, and about four of you have worked out that I do not know what is on that page and that I have got no way of finding out, and about four of you have worked out that that number is twelve now and that I have not moved it and about four of you have worked out that it is not mine to move, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is twelve. She entered that it moved this morning and that a person and the reason were in that yard before her hand moved, and entered that a boy who watches a lane and names what he saw is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight and was at the near end of that second table until about ten and was not in that yard at about eleven, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a boy said out loud this morning that a man went down that lane with a page in his own right hand and went on down past that bank, and that a clerk said out loud before him that a figure somebody has to enter is not a figure anybody is paid by. About four of them have said that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and that a boy in that yard has just said out loud that he watched a man go down a lane and cannot find out what is on his page. About four of them have said that the two of those are not the same thing and one of them is not paid. About four of them have said that the man the page sent is at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own two hands on that rail and has not been asked one thing by anybody in that yard since he said no.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour an hour after the stranger went down it and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a road keeper and a stranger went down the same lane about three hours apart this morning and neither of them said one word to the other. About four of them have said that is a thing about a lane and not about a road.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that not one figure in that book has ever been handed to a person, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and sixteen nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and seventeenth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and sixteen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and twenty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and eighty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and eighty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and fifty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and fifty-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and ninety-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and thirty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and twenty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a figure is not a fourteenth thing and a boy saying a thing out loud about a lane is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a figure somebody has to enter is a figure and not a wage, and that a day is not a day of work and is not a day of pay.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and sixty-seven. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and seventy-six mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning, and a clerk in that yard said out loud this morning that a book full of figures is not the thing this district does not have. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a clerk is not in it and a boy is not in it and a stranger with a page is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk saying out loud in that yard that a book full of figures is not that thing and never was is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man went down that lane this morning with a page in his hand and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and third morning, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is twelve and moved this morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two. About four of them have said that a man goes down that lane about nine times in two months and a man of fifty-six reads four figures off a wall two hundred mornings and neither of them has ever been asked to do the other thing.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-third of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that not one figure in that book has been handed to a person, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is twelve and moved this morning on the word of a boy who watched the lane, and that a page folded in a stranger's own right hand is not a document this district owns, and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and is not paid, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
