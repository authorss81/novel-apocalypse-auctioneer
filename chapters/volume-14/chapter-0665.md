# Chapter 665: On The Tenth Of The Seventh Month A Clerk Put Two Fifths Next To Each Other On Purpose

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the tenth of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and had gone back to the colour of a road that has not been touched since the frost, and that was the hundred and ninety-second morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and sixty-three mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is ten and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninetieth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and eighty times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that about four of them have said that about nineteen people in that yard have worked out what a man does with his own two hands at about eleven every morning, and that nobody has worked out what a district has.

"**The board carries five hundred and sixty-three, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and seventy-nine days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and ninety-three, and five hundred and fifty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 74 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and ninety-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are two fifths in this book and I have put them next to each other on this page on purpose, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a man saying no in this yard and it is not paid again this morning, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and it has not been paid on any morning this district has had a book for, and about four of you have worked out that a remedy is a thing a person does and a way to pay a person is a thing this district has not got, and about four of you have worked out that a thing a person does is still a thing a person does and does not turn into a thing a district has, and that is why the count of the things this district has made is thirteen and did not move this morning, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth and is not paid a second time, and entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and is not paid, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about half past eleven and said one thing out loud, in about 40 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody has been in that column and about four of you have worked out that I have not asked anybody to be in it, and about four of you have worked out that I have got a number for the mornings it has been empty and I am not going to say the number out loud, and about four of you have worked out that there are about nineteen people standing in this yard this morning and about four of you have worked out that I have counted every one of them and named none of them, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who counts everybody and names nobody is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and was at the near end of that second table until about ten. He did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. He said out loud on the fortieth morning of the last volume that he was not going to say it twice, and he has not said it on any of the mornings he has been in that yard this month. This morning he came up that bank before the clerk had that book open, which is what he said he did.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and about four of them have said since that about nineteen people in that yard have watched that man at that end of that table for about a month and that the only afternoon this month he was not at it, nobody said one word about him then either.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that there are two fifths in that book and that she has put them next to each other on purpose, and that a boy said out loud after her that he has counted every one of about nineteen people this morning and named none of them. Those are the two things that yard has got. Neither of them is a fifth of anything, and nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with that.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk said a thing out loud this morning about two fifths, and that the road keeper came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that about a mile and a half of that road has gone since the frost and that nobody in this yard has ever asked him which part of it is left.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk said a thing out loud this morning about two fifths and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and four nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fourth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and thirteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and sixty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and seventy-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and forty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and forty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and eighty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and twenty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk putting two fifths next to each other in a book is not a fourteenth thing, and a boy counting about nineteen people and naming none of them is not a fifteenth, because a remedy is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a remedy happened in this yard on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month and is on that book and is not on that wall. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and fifty-four.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and sixty-three mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who said no in that yard is not in it and a boy who counts everybody is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month, and those are two fifths and they are not the same fifth and one of them was paid and the other one was not, and the count of the five is still five and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up said nothing at all, and that about four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninetieth morning, and that about nineteen people in that yard heard him say four figures out loud twice today, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of the four is for, and that about a quarter to eight and about four are the only two numbers of people anybody in that yard keeps about him.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the tenth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth and is not paid a second time, and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and is not paid, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is ten and moved on one morning this month with a person and a reason in the room before the hand moved, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight this morning before the clerk had that book open, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
