# Chapter 675: On The Twentieth Of The Seventh Month A Man Asked Out Loud Who Is Going To Answer A Stranger Next Week And Nobody Said A Word For Fourteen Seconds

There was a thin grey mist standing in the bottom of that ditch at about seven on the twentieth of the seventh month and it had come up the bank to about the middle of the fence rail by about nine, and it was gone off the boards of that second table by about ten, and that was the two hundred and third morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and seventy-three mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and fourteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eleven and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that a stranger came down that lane at about half past eight yesterday with a page folded in his own right hand and that he is not a resident of this district, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundredth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that this morning is the first morning in four hundred that anybody in that yard has written the word four hundred in a place where he could see it, and that he read it out loud in the ordinary voice without stopping.

"**The board carries five hundred and seventy-three, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and eighty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and three, and five hundred and sixty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 57 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have entered that the mist was in the bottom of that ditch at about seven this morning and that it was off those boards by about ten, and about four of you have worked out that a mist is a thing the night did and is not entered as a figure, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth line of that book says go down a lane and ask a man who digs loam and that there is a man who has been at the bottom of that bank every morning of this week, and about four of you have worked out that I have not entered that the two of them were in the mist together because I was not at the bottom of that bank and I did not see it, and that is the whole of what I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk does not enter a thing she did not see, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about eleven that yard held about nineteen people in the open in daylight, and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of it with a flat book under his own left arm, and he said one thing out loud, in about 3 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Who is going to answer a stranger next week?**"

Nobody in that yard said a word. The mist had gone, the rail was dry, and the man of fifty-six went on reading the four off that wall in the ordinary voice without looking up, and the fourteen went by with the sound of about nineteen people not saying anything in it. The boy of about nineteen counted that silence on his own two fingers and got fourteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Fourteen. Nobody gave me one back, and I counted that quiet twice by hand.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the thing was a question, and entered that nobody in that yard answered it for fourteen seconds, and entered that the boy counted the fourteen and said the fourteen out loud, and entered that a man who keeps a tally is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that a column has not been ruled this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and was at the near end of that second table until about ten, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for, and he was not in that yard at about eleven. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was not in that yard at about eleven either, and came up that bank at about half past eleven with his own coat over his own left arm, and set his spade up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it and put his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard at about eleven this morning and asked out loud who is going to answer a stranger next week, and that nobody in that yard said a word for fourteen seconds, and that a boy counted the fourteen and said the fourteen out loud in a low voice. About four of them have said that the man of fifty-six went on reading his four off that wall through the whole of it and did not look up once. About four of them have said that about nineteen people have now heard that question said out loud in that yard and that nobody has ever heard anybody in that yard answer it, and that the fifth line of that book has been standing on that book working exactly as it was written for a month and has never once been answered by a person in this district either.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a man asked out loud in that yard this morning who is going to answer a stranger and that the road keeper has been going up that lane twice a day for more than three hundred mornings and has never once been asked to answer a stranger either.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man asked out loud in that yard this morning who is going to answer a stranger next week, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and thirteen nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fourteenth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and thirteen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and twenty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and seventy-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and eighty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and fifty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and fifty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and ninety-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and thirty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and eighteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man asking a question in that yard is not a fourteenth thing and a boy counting a silence is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a question asked out loud is a thing a person does and not a thing this district has got, and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is not a question.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and sixty-four. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and seventy-three mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. It does not say where to go and it does not say when and it does not say what for, and this morning a person in that yard said out loud who is going to answer a stranger next week, and that is the first thing anybody in this district has said out loud about that line that is not a number and is not a date. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man asked a question in that yard and nobody answered it. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a boy who counted fourteen is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a question asked out loud in that yard at about eleven is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about three things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up said nothing at all. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundredth morning, and that he read the four off that wall twice today and was in that yard the whole time a man was asking a question in it. About four of them have said that a man who has read four figures off that wall four hundred mornings and was never asked one thing about them was standing about nine feet from a question about a stranger and did not look up. About four of them have said that is not a thing anybody in that yard has worked out what to do with.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twentieth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud in that yard at about eleven and that she entered the thing he said, and that nobody in that yard answered it for fourteen seconds, and that the boy counted those fourteen and said that number out loud in a low voice, and that she has entered the question and the fourteen and no answer, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eleven and did not move, and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and is not paid, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
