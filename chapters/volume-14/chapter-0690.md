# Chapter 690: On The Fourth Of The Eighth Month A Man Put The Near Leg Of That Table Square At The Foot Of That Bank And Nobody Thanked Him For It

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the fourth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and the water standing at the north end of that yard had gone down about another inch since the day before, and that was the two hundred and eighteenth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard. The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it, and that lot book was lying on it face up with five lines on it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and twenty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is thirteen and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is four this morning and that it moved this morning from three, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifteenth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and thirty times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that a man went down that bank at about eight this morning and worked at the near leg of that second table with his own hands until about eleven, and that the man of fifty-six read the four at about a quarter to eight and did not go down and was not asked to.

"**The board carries five hundred and eighty-eight, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and four days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and eighteen, and five hundred and seventy-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up went down that bank at about eight with his own two hands and his own knee and put the near leg of that second table square, and said one thing out loud, in about 51 seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said on the first morning of the eighth month that I was not going to put a card under that leg and I have not, and about four of you have worked out that I have been at that ground about three hours this morning with my own hands on it and that I have got that leg square and that it is square, and a man is not thanked in this district for a thing he does to a table and I am not going to say that he is, and this yard has one table in it and that this one is four mornings old where it is standing and that both of those numbers are mine and not yours, and I have got no more of it than that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past eleven, in about 62 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank is four and I have entered it four mornings running and I entered nothing on the morning it went down, and about four of you have worked out that a count of nothing is not a count of nobody and that a count of four is not a count of anybody either, and the first table in this yard has had nothing on it for four mornings and I sit at the near end of it every morning with that book and nothing to enter at that end of it, and I have entered no reason for that and nobody has asked me for one, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid and I entered that this morning before I said anything out loud, and it is all I have got and it is not much.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and is not paid, and that a man putting a table leg square at the foot of a bank is not a payment and did not pay it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no, and nobody in that yard thanked the man who put that leg square.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man went down that bank at about eight this morning and worked at the near leg of that second table with his own hands until about eleven, and that a clerk said out loud after him that a count of four is not a count of anybody either. About four of them have said that the yard in that bank has had one table in it for four mornings and that the table at the foot of that bank has been standing there four mornings, and that neither of those two numbers was standing anywhere in this district before the thirty-first of the seventh month. About four of them have said that a man said out loud this morning that a man is not thanked in this district for a thing he does to a table, and that nobody in that yard has worked out a way to say that to his face. About four of them have said that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight this morning with about nineteen people within hearing of it and did not go down that bank to look at what was happening at the foot of it.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a man was at the foot of that bank at about eight this morning and that a road keeper was not, and that neither of those two facts is entered as a reason for anything.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man worked at a table leg about nine feet off that man this morning from about eight until about eleven, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and twenty-eight nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and twenty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and twenty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and thirty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and ninety-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and ninety-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and sixty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and seventy-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and forty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and thirty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man putting a table leg square is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a count of four is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a leg put square is a leg and not a figure and that a table this district has made standing somewhere else is a table and not a thing this district has done this morning.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and seventy-nine. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the two pages on the end of that second table are at the foot of that bank and are not on any table in that yard, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man put a table leg square at the foot of a bank. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man putting a leg square is not a payment and a clerk entering four is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up has been going down that bank for four evenings and did not look at the fourth one. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fifteenth morning, and that a man went down that bank at about eight this morning and worked at a table leg with his own hands until about eleven, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and fifteen mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is, and that nobody in that yard is going to be asked about it this afternoon.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fourth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is four, and that a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said out loud in that yard that a man is not thanked in this district for a thing he does to a table, and that she entered that he said it and entered no thank you of her own, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is thirteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
