# Chapter 670: On The Fifteenth Of The Seventh Month A Man Put A Number On A Lane And Nobody Asked Him Which Man It Was

There was a hard white rime on the boards of that second table by about seven on the fifteenth of the seventh month and it had gone off them by about nine, and that was the hundred and ninety-seventh morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and sixty-eight mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is ten and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that neither of the two men who stand in that yard was standing in it at about eleven this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-fifth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and ninety times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that a man came down that lane this morning with a page in his own right hand and the man of fifty-six was reading four figures off that wall at the same moment and neither of them looked up.

"**The board carries five hundred and sixty-eight, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and eighty-four days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and ninety-eight, and five hundred and fifty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the top of that bank at about half past eight with his own left hand flat on the end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 57 seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man came down that lane at about half past eight this morning with a page folded in his own right hand and he did not stop at that yard and he went on down past the bottom of that bank, and I am saying it out loud in that yard before anybody asks me for it, and about four of you have worked out that I do not know what is on that page and I have not looked at it and I was not going to, and about four of you have worked out that I cannot tell that clerk which man that was and that nobody in that yard is going to ask me, and about four of you have worked out that a man who puts tables up has spent this morning watching that lane instead of a table leg, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eleven, and entered that it moved this morning and that a person and the reason were in that yard before her hand moved, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past eleven, in about 56 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That number is eleven this morning and it was ten yesterday and a man told me so in that yard at about half past eight before I had the book open for the second entry, and about four of you have worked out that a page folded in a man's own right hand is not a document this district owns, and that the count of the documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and about four of you have worked out that I have entered the number because a person said it in that yard and not because I saw the man, and about four of you have worked out that a clerk who enters a thing on the word of a person is not a person who answers a stranger, and that is the whole of what I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk who enters a count on the word of a person is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight and was at the near end of that second table until about ten, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with mud to the thigh of his own right leg, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for.

At about eleven that yard held about nineteen people and not one of those two men. About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man said out loud at about half past eight this morning that a stranger went down that lane with a page in his own right hand and did not stop at that yard, and that a clerk entered the number of them after he had said it. About four of them have said that the fifth line of that book says go down a lane and ask a man who digs loam and that nobody has ever put those two facts in the same sentence in that yard. About four of them have said that the man the page sent is at the bottom of that bank this morning and that nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with that.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a man went down that lane this morning who has not been asked about the eleven miles either and never will be. About four of them have said that is the first time in this district's history that a person in that yard has put a number on somebody who was not in that yard.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said out loud this morning that a stranger went down that lane past that yard, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and eight nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and ninth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and eighteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and seventy-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and seventy-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and forty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and fifty-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and eighty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and twenty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and thirteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man putting a number on a lane is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk entering it is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a stranger walking past a yard is not a thing this district made and a yard is not a thing a stranger has been told about.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and fifty-nine. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and sixty-eight mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man put a number on a lane. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a stranger with a page is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk entering the number of a stranger on the word of a man who puts tables up is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man went down that lane this morning with a page in his hand and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-fifth morning, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eleven and moved this morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fifteenth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eleven and moved this morning at about half past eight on the word of a man who puts tables up, and that a page folded in a stranger's own right hand is not a document this district owns, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam were both at the bottom of that bank this morning and that she is entering no reason for either, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
