# Chapter 654: On The Thirtieth Of The Sixth Month, The Last Morning Of This Month Before Anybody Had Counted It, The Man Who Digs Loam Said Out Loud That He Has Not Once Put That Spade Down

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the thirtieth of the sixth month, and the boards were dry and hard and had gone back to the colour of a road that has not been touched since the frost, and that was the hundred and eighty-first morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and fifty-two mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and ninety-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is nine and did not move, and entered that nobody in that yard had counted a month off that board this month, and entered that the count of counted months is five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-ninth of those mornings. About four of them have said that nobody in that yard has counted a month off that board since the ninth of the sixth month in the month before and that a clerk of nineteen years has put a finger on that board in the open three times in about a year, and that about four of them have said that the man of fifty-six read his four at about a quarter to eight this morning and was not asked one thing about any of it.

"**The board carries five hundred and fifty-two, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and sixty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and eighty-two, and five hundred and forty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about a quarter to seven with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and his own boots not dry, and stood at the far end of that second table, and said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about 51 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been at the far end of that table at about eleven every morning this month with my own spade against the leg of it, and about four of you have worked out that I have not once put that spade down while I am standing there, and about four of you have worked out that I have worked out that if I put it down I would have to go and pick it up again, and about four of you have worked out that there is nothing in this yard I could do with the time in between, and that is not a complaint and I am not standing here saying it as one, and that is all I have got this morning and I am going back down that bank after this.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is still paid nothing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Three pages are lying on a counter up that bank this morning and there are four places in this district where those three lines can be read and a counter is not one of them, and about four of you have worked out that I have not put that counter on the list and I am not going to, and about four of you have worked out that the woman who keeps that scale has those three pages on her own counter and I have not asked her where they came from and she is not in this book, and about four of you have worked out that a shop is open every morning of this year and that is a fact and not an answer to anything, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with his own right hand flat on the near end of that second table for about ten minutes, and about four of them have said since that he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for and that about four of them have said he has not said it since the morning he said he was not going to say it twice.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man said one thing out loud this morning about a spade and a clerk said one thing out loud an hour later about a counter, and about four of them have said that about nineteen people in that yard have watched a man stand at the far end of a table for about a month and have not seen him put anything down once, and that about four of them have said that neither of the two things said out loud this morning had anything to do with each other and that nobody in that yard has worked out why both of them happened on the same morning.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud this morning about a spade and that the road keeper came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that this is the last morning of this month that anybody in that yard can name without counting it off that board first.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud this morning about a spade and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in two hundred and ninety-three nights and has slept on two hundred and ninety-two of them. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and ninety-third night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and ninety-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and fifty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and sixty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and thirty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and thirty-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and seventy-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and eleven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and ninety-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man standing at a table with a spade is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a counter is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a shop counter is a place and is not on the list of places and the count is kept in a different book. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and forty-three.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and fifty-two mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this month and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and a man saying a thing out loud about a spade is not a remedy and did not advance it this morning. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who said a thing about a spade is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud in that yard that he is going back down that bank after this is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said a thing out loud this morning about a spade and that about four of them have worked out that the last mark on that board for this month was cut at dawn and that nobody in that yard has counted the marks from one end of that month to the other, and that about four of them have said that is a different thing from a clerk counting a month off a board in the open.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-ninth morning, and that a man said out loud this morning that he has not once put his own spade down while he was standing at that table, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirtieth of the sixth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that nobody in that yard has asked the man it names one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is nine and did not move, and that the count of counted months is five and did not move, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail.
