# Chapter 653: On The Twenty-Ninth Of The Sixth Month A Man Who Mends Fencing Said One Thing About A Rail He Put In Himself

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the twenty-ninth of the sixth month and the boards were wet at the far end of it from the night before, which was the hundred and eightieth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and fifty-one mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and ninety-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is nine and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is five and did not move, and entered that the wet on those boards is a thing the night did and is not entered as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-eighth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and fifty-six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that about four of them have said that two of the four have been out for longer than anybody in that yard has been standing at that wall.

"**The board carries five hundred and fifty-one, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and sixty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and eighty-one, and five hundred and forty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and stood at the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 53 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put that rail in myself about four weeks ago and I have not been back to it since and I am not going to, and about four of you have worked out that a man puts his own two hands on that rail every morning at about eleven and does not lean on it, and about four of you have worked out that I have never asked him why he does not lean on it and I have not and I am not going to, and about four of you have worked out that I am telling you that he does not lean on it because it is the only work of mine that anybody in this yard has ever used and I did not build it to be used, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a rail in that fence is a thing a person did and not a thing this district has, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 50 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Two of the four figures on that wall are out and I am not going to say how far either of them is out and I am not going to say it in this book either, and about four of you have worked out that two people have already said the size of one of them out loud in this yard and about four of you have worked out that I know which two people those were and I am not going to say it, and about four of you have worked out that a figure on a wall that is out is still the figure a man reads out loud three hundred and seventy-eight mornings running, and that is not my decision to make and I have not made it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the size of the error in either of the two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about eleven with mud to the knee of his own right leg and set his spade up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it, and went the three steps to the rail in the fence and put his own two hands on it. A man said out loud this morning that he has not been back to that rail in about four weeks. About a quarter of an hour afterwards the man who puts his own two hands on that rail every morning came up that bank and put his own two hands on it. Nobody in that yard has ever seen those two men say one word to each other, and nobody in that yard has ever asked either of them about the other.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man said one thing out loud this morning about a rail and a clerk said one thing out loud an hour and a half later about two figures that are out, and about four of them have said that a rail is a thing a person did and two figures that are out is a thing a wall has got, and about four of them have said that the count of the things this district has made did not move this morning on account of either of them, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with a man who tells you what work of his anybody is using.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud this morning about a rail and that the road keeper came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that about a mile and a half of that road has gone since the frost and that nobody in this yard has ever asked him which part of it is left.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud this morning about a rail and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in two hundred and ninety-two nights and has slept on two hundred and ninety-one of them. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and ninety-second night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and ninety-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and fifty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and sixty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and thirty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and thirty-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and seventy days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and ten marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and ninety-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a rail in a fence is not a fourteenth thing and a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a rail is a piece of wood a man cut and drove into that ground with his own hands and that this district has not got a book for that. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and forty-two.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and fifty-one mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this month and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and a man saying a thing out loud about a rail he put in himself is not a remedy and did not advance it this morning. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who said what his yard is for and is not saying it again is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud in that yard that a rail he put in is being used is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the man who said a thing out loud this morning about a rail went down that bank before the buckets went down it and came up again before the woman of about fifty-eight came past, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has asked him where he goes when he goes down that bank and that he has not said.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-eighth morning, and that a man said out loud this morning that a rail he put in himself is being used by a man who does not lean on it, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-ninth of the sixth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that nobody in that yard has asked the man it names one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is nine and did not move, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail.
