# Chapter 669: On The Fourteenth Of The Seventh Month A Clerk Entered A Depth And Refused To Make It A Figure

There was a hard white rime on the boards of that second table by about seven on the fourteenth of the seventh month and the whole of that bank was white along the top of it, and that was the hundred and ninety-sixth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and sixty-seven mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is ten and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the rime on those boards is a thing the night did and is not entered as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-fourth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and eighty-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that he said his four this morning with the rime still on the boards of the table in front of him and did not look down once, and that a man who has read four figures three hundred and ninety-four mornings running is not a man who looks at boards.

"**The board carries five hundred and sixty-seven, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and eighty-three days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and ninety-seven, and five hundred and fifty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

At about ten past eleven the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about 65 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is water standing in the bottom of that ditch at the bottom of that bank this morning and I have entered that it is there and I have not entered a figure for the depth of it, and about four of you have worked out that a clerk can put a number on a thing the length of her own thumb in about the time it takes to look at it, and about four of you have worked out that if I enter that number this morning then about a week from now somebody will ask me what it was on the day, and about four of you have worked out that I do not want to be a person somebody asks that, and about four of you have worked out that the man who digs loam looks at that water every morning of his life and has never once put a number on it for anybody, and that is the whole of what I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the depth of the water at the bottom of that bank is not entered as a figure this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and was at the near end of that second table until about ten, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with his own boots black to the ankle and set his spade up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for.

At about eleven that yard held about nineteen people and the two men who stand in it were not in it. About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that there is water standing in the bottom of that ditch and that she has not put a number on it. About four of them have said that a man who digs loam looks at that water every morning and has never put a number on it for anybody either. About four of them have said that two people in this yard are not putting numbers on the same thing this morning and that nobody in this yard has asked either of them to.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that the eleven miles has about four feet of water in the bottom of it in August and that a clerk has just said out loud in that yard that she will not put a number on about four inches of water at the bottom of a bank. About four of them have said that is a thing about a clerk and not about a road.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that she will not put a number on the water at the bottom of that bank, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and seven nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eighth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and seventeen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and seventy-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and seventy-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and forty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and fifty days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and eighty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and twenty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and twelve marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk declining to enter a depth is not a fourteenth thing, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a figure somebody refuses to write down is not a figure this district has got, it is a figure this district has decided not to have, and those are not the same thing and one of them is not a fourteenth.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and fifty-eight. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and sixty-seven mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a clerk declined to put a number on a depth. About one thing was said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a clerk is not in it and the man who said no is not in it and a man who mends fencing is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk saying out loud in that yard that she will not put a number on four inches of water is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about one thing was said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up said nothing at all. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-fourth morning, and that about one thing was said out loud in that yard today and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two. About four of them have said that the yard can go a whole morning on one thing said out loud and still have about nineteen people in it at about six.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fourteenth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that there is water standing in the bottom of that ditch this morning and that no figure has been entered for the depth of it, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam were both at the bottom of that bank this morning and that she is entering no reason for either, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is ten and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
