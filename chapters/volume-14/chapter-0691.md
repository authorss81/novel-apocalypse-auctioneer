# Chapter 691: On The Fifth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Mends Fencing Came Up That Bank And Stood At A Table With Nothing On It

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the fifth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and the water that was standing at the north end of that yard has not stood there since the day before yesterday, and that was the two hundred and nineteenth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it, and that lot book was lying on it face up with five lines on it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and thirty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is thirteen and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is five this morning and that it moved this morning from four, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about a quarter to eight a man of about thirty-four came up that bank into that yard with nothing at all in his own hands, and about four people in that yard who were standing about nine feet off the first table have said since that he came up at the same hour a man of fifty-six was reading four figures off the wall at the top of that bank, and that neither of the two of them said one word to the other one. That man stood at the near end of that first table for about the length of a page and then went back down that bank the way he had come up it, and a clerk entered that a man came up that bank into that yard and entered no reason for it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixteenth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and thirty-two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that about four people in that yard heard him this morning before eight and that a man of about thirty-four came up that bank at the same hour and did not stay for the second time, and that nobody has asked him what any one of the four is for.

"**The board carries five hundred and eighty-nine, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and five days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and nineteen, and five hundred and eighty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the far end of that second table at about half past eight with his own two hands on the leg of it and said one thing out loud, in about 45 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and seventeen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That table has stood at the foot of that bank five mornings and I have been at the far leg of it four of those five with my own hands on it, and about four of you have worked out that nobody has asked me to be at that leg and that I am not going to say what I am doing down there, and about four of you have worked out that a man who puts tables up has not been paid in that yard for a table in six months and that I have not asked anybody about it, and about four of you have worked out that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts tables up is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 60 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank is five and I have entered it five mornings running, and about four of you have worked out that a man came up that bank into that yard at about a quarter to eight this morning and that he had not been in that yard since the thirty-first of the seventh month, and about four of you have worked out that I entered that he came up and I entered no reason for it and that nobody asked me for one, and about four of you have worked out that a man coming up a bank is not a man coming down a lane and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is thirteen and did not move this morning, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is five, and that a man came up that bank into that yard and that she entered no reason for it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he had come up that bank once this morning and gone down it again, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the two of them were at that table about nine feet apart for the fifth morning running and that neither of them has said one word to the other one.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man came up that bank into that yard this morning at about a quarter to eight and stood at a table with nothing on it and went back down it again, and that a man who puts tables up said out loud after him that he is not going to say what he is doing down at that leg. About four of them have said that a clerk has entered a count of mornings for a thing at the foot of that bank for the fifth morning running and a man coming up that bank with no reason beside it, and that neither has been asked about by anybody. About four of them have said that a man has been at that yard before eight this morning and that the man of fifty-six was reading the four off that wall at the top of the same bank at the same hour, and that the two of them did not meet.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it, and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man came up that bank and stood at a table with nothing on it this morning and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and twenty-nine nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and thirtieth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and twenty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and thirty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and ninety-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and ninety-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and sixty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and seventy-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and forty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and thirty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man putting a leg right on a table at the foot of a bank is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a man coming up a bank is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a body going up a bank and back down it is a body and not a figure.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and eighty. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the two pages on the end of that second table are at the foot of that bank and are not on any table in that yard, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man came up a bank and went down it again. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who puts tables up is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man coming up a bank and going down it again is not a payment and a clerk entering five mornings is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the fifth evening running and did not look at the fourth one. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and sixteenth morning, and that a man of about thirty-four came up that bank at about a quarter to eight and stood at the near end of a table with nothing on it, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and sixteen mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is, and that nobody in that yard is going to be asked about it this afternoon.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fifth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is five, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank into that yard at about a quarter to eight and stood at the near end of the first table and went back down it, and that she entered that and entered no reason for it, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is thirteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
