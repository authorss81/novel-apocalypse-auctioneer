# Chapter 651: On The Twenty-Seventh Of The Sixth Month A Man Who Puts Tables Up Said Out Loud That He Has A Piece Of Card Folded Under The Near Leg Of That Second Table

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the twenty-seventh of the sixth month, which was the hundred and seventy-eighth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and forty-nine mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The fifth line of that lot book has never said where to go and has never said when and has never said what for, and it says the name of a man, and that man is findable at one table at one hour of one morning and at no other hour in this yard, and a man with a page in his hand who comes down that lane goes up that bank because the bank is in the way and not because anybody has ever told him to, and about four people at that low wall had worked that out before seven that morning and about four of them had not worked it out and never will, and nobody in that yard said one word about it to anybody, and it is set down here because it is what a morning in this yard is and not because anybody in it explained it.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and ninety days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is nine and did not move on its first morning of this month, and entered that the count of counted months is five and did not move, and entered that there is no rime on those boards this morning and that the rime on those boards is a thing the night did and is not entered as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-sixth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and fifty-two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that about four of them have said that two of the four have been out for longer than anybody in that yard has been standing at that wall.

"**The board carries five hundred and forty-nine, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and sixty-five days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seventy-nine, and five hundred and forty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was in that yard at about ten with his own right hand flat on the top of that second table, and said one thing out loud, in about 50 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got a piece of card folded under the near leg of that second table and it has been there about a fortnight, and that table has not stood square one morning since, and about four of you have worked out that I have not asked anybody to come and look at that ground, and about four of you have worked out that I have been shimming it with what was in my own pocket, and about four of you have worked out that a man who puts tables up does not get asked how a table stands, and that is not a complaint and I am saying it out loud because nobody in this yard has ever once asked me that, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a piece of card has been folded under the near leg of that second table for about a fortnight, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about half past ten and said one thing out loud, in about 45 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and eighteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been counting the feet of everybody who comes up that bank and about four of you have worked out that I have got a number for the man who digs loam that is higher than the number I have got for anybody else including the clerk, and about four of you have worked out that I have never said that out loud in this yard, and about four of you have worked out that there is a figure in this yard that nobody has ever read out loud to about nineteen people and that it is mine, and I am not putting it in that book and I am not putting it in that column either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who has counted the feet of everybody who comes up that bank is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and stood at the near end of that second table until about nine and went down that bank again, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for, and about four of them have said since that he said out loud on the last morning of the last volume that he was not going to say it twice, and that about four of them have worked out that he has not said it since.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about eleven with his spade over his own right shoulder and mud to the thigh of his own right leg, and set the spade up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it, and went the three steps to the rail in the fence and put his own two hands on it and stayed there until about a quarter to twelve, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for and nobody has asked him that since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man said one thing out loud this morning about a piece of card under a table leg and about four of them have said that it is the first time anybody in that yard has said a thing out loud about the way a table stands, and about four of them have said that about nineteen people in that yard have now heard a man say out loud that nobody has ever once asked him how a table stands up, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with that.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by without stopping, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud this morning about a table leg and that the road keeper came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that nobody in this yard has ever worked out what to do with a man like the man who puts tables up either.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud this morning about a piece of card and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in two hundred and ninety nights and has slept on two hundred and eighty-nine of them. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and ninetieth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and eighty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and ninety-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and fifty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and fifty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and twenty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and thirty-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and sixty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and ninety-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a piece of card folded under the leg of a table is not a fourteenth thing and a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man who puts tables up has made a table and not a district, and that the count is kept in a different book. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and forty.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and forty-nine mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this month and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said a thing out loud this morning about a table leg. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who said a thing about a table leg is not in it and a boy who said a thing about a number of feet is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud in that yard that nobody has ever once asked him how a table stands is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about nineteen people in that yard heard two things said out loud this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventy-sixth morning, and that a man who puts tables up said out loud this morning that nobody in this yard has ever asked him how a table stands, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-seventh of the sixth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that nobody in that yard has asked the man it names one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is nine and did not move, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail.
