# Chapter 700: On The Fourteenth Of The Eighth Month A Boy Counted That Yard And A Clerk Entered The Count And The Count Of Counted Months Did Not Move

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the fourteenth of the eighth month and the night had left them exactly as dry and as hard as it had found them, and the clerk entered that as a thing the night did and entered no figure for it, and there was nothing on those boards at all, and that was the two hundred and twenty-eighth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. The second table was at the foot of that bank face up with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it and the copy is at the near end of those two, and that lot book was lying open on it with five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about and has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and thirty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fourteen this morning and that it moved this morning from thirteen, and that a count of nothing is not a count of nobody, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that low wall have said since that a clerk of nineteen years was at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight with her own hand on that page and her own eyes on that page and that she did not look up while she wrote the count of that board down.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about four people were standing within hearing of that wall at about a quarter to eight and about nine people were standing within hearing of it at about four, and neither of those two numbers is the number that has been standing at that wall for four hundred mornings, and no reason is entered for either of them. About nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-fifth of those mornings, and that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and fifty times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of them is for.

"**The board carries five hundred and ninety-eight, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and fourteen days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and twenty-eight, and five hundred and eighty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The boy of about nineteen stood at the near end of that first table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted this yard at about ten and I counted it again at about eleven and I got about nineteen both times, and about four of you have worked out that I have counted that yard every morning this month and that it has not moved once and that I have never entered the number anywhere, and about four of you have worked out that there is a table at the foot of that bank with a book open on it and that nobody in that yard has ever sent anybody down that bank to read it, and about four of you have worked out that I have not worked out how you would send anybody anywhere, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who counts a yard and names nobody is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 70 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That table has stood at the foot of that bank fourteen mornings and I have entered fourteen mornings running and I entered nothing on the morning it went down, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of those five lines is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that it is not paid and that the count of the five is still five, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that it is not a fourteenth this morning, and about four of you have worked out that a boy counting that yard twice is not a payment and that a table at the foot of that bank is not a payment, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and is not paid, and that a clerk entering fourteen mornings is not a payment and did not pay it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was in that yard at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that first table. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a boy counted that yard at about ten and at about eleven this morning and got about nineteen both times, and that a clerk said out loud after him that the fifth of those five lines is still one condition with no end on it. About four of them have said that the yard in that bank has one table in it and that there is nothing on it, and that the table at the foot of that bank has the stone with the hollow in it lying face up on it and that book lying open on it with five lines.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it, and that he came up that lane a second time in the afternoon and read them again as he went by.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, and he did not stay at the top of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a boy counted a yard this morning and a clerk entered a count of fourteen at the foot of that bank, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and thirty-eight nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and thirty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and thirty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and forty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and seventy-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and eighty-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and seventeen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it, and nobody has worked out a day on which it will arrive.

Two hundred and fifty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and forty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a boy counting that yard twice is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a condition with no end on it is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a thing a person does this morning is not a thing a district has done this morning.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and eighty-nine. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a shop counter is a place and is not on that list and was not added to it this morning, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and a clerk entered that number at the foot of that bank this morning and has not entered it in that yard, and the copy is at the near end of those two.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and the man who said no is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk entering fourteen mornings and a boy counting about nineteen people twice is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth. Those are two different fifths and they are on this page one above the other and nobody in that yard has ever put them in the same clause out loud.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, read three lines in that lot book standing up about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it, and did not stop, and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up has been going down that bank every evening this month, and that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-fifth morning, and that a boy said out loud this morning that he counted that yard twice and got about nineteen both times, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and twenty-five mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fiftieth morning of the nineteenth year after the long fracture that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is fourteen and that a count of nothing is not a count of nobody, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that it moved once this month and that a person and the reason were in that yard before her hand moved, and that no person was added to this district this morning, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
