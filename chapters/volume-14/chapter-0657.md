# Chapter 657: On The Second Of The Seventh Month A Clerk Of Nineteen Years Said Fifteen Words Out Loud To One Man About Four Feet Away From Her And The Boy Counted Them

There was a hard white rime on the boards of that second table by about seven on the second of the seventh month and the whole of that bank was white along the top of it and had gone the colour of a road that has not been touched since the frost, and that was the hundred and eighty-fourth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and fifty-five mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and ninety-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is nine and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the rime on those boards is a thing the night did and is not entered as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and eighty-second of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and sixty-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time, and that about four of them have said that two of the four have been out for longer than anybody in that yard has been standing at that wall.

"**The board carries five hundred and fifty-five, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and seventy-one days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and eighty-five, and five hundred and forty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter to eight, in about 6 seconds, to a man about four feet away from her, and the boy counted what she said and got fifteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Two pages. Not three, and I counted them twice, and you can go and look.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about 43 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fourteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He has read those four off that wall twice a morning for about a year and a bit, and about four of you have worked out that he does not look at that wall while he is saying them, and about four of you have worked out that I have timed him on about nine mornings and I have got the same time for all nine of them and it is not the same time it takes me to say them, and about four of you have worked out that I have never asked him how he does it and I am not going to, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering how the man of fifty-six does it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about ten with his own coil of wire over his own left shoulder, set it down at the foot of that low wall, and was at the near end of that second table until about eleven, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about eleven with his spade over his own right shoulder and set it up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it, and went the three steps to the rail in the fence and put his own two hands on it, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said fifteen words out loud to one man about four feet away from her this morning and about four of them have said that it was the first thing said out loud in that yard this month before about a quarter to eight, and about four of them have said that a boy said out loud after it that he has timed the man of fifty-six on about nine mornings and got the same time every time, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has asked the man of fifty-six how he does it and that nobody in that yard is going to.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk said a thing out loud this morning to one man about four feet away from her and that the road keeper came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that about a mile and a half of that road has gone since the frost and that nobody in this yard has ever asked him which part of it is left.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a boy said a thing out loud this morning about timing a man, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in two hundred and ninety-six nights and has slept on two hundred and ninety-five of them. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and ninety-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and ninety-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and sixty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and sixty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and thirty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and thirty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and seventy-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and fourteen marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk saying fifteen words out loud to one man is not a fourteenth thing and a boy saying a thing out loud about nine mornings is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a word said in a yard is a thing a person does. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and forty-six.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and fifty-five mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and a clerk saying fifteen words about two pages did not advance it this morning, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a reading of a lot does not move because somebody counted two pages out loud. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a clerk is not in it and a boy is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk saying fifteen words out loud to one man is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a clerk said fifteen words out loud this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and eighty-second morning, and that a boy said out loud this morning that he has timed him on about nine mornings and got the same time every time, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the second of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that nobody in that yard has asked the man it names one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is nine and did not move, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail.
