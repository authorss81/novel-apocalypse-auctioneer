# Chapter 692: On The Sixth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Keeps A Tally Said Out Loud That He Has Got A Figure And Has Not Entered It

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the sixth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and the ground at the north end of that yard where the water was standing on the day before yesterday is hard all the way across this morning, and that was the two hundred and twentieth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard. The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it, and that lot book was lying on it face up with five lines on it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and thirty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is thirteen and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is six this morning and that it moved this morning from five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that low wall before it was light with his own flat book under his left arm and said one thing out loud, in about 51 seconds, to about four people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are about four of them that come up that bank before eight and go on to the top of that lane and they have done it every morning this month, and about four of you have worked out that I have got a figure for the mornings a man comes up that bank and does not stop at that yard, and about four of you have worked out that I have not entered that figure and that the last figure I entered was on the sixth of the fifth month, and about four of you have worked out that a figure a man has not entered is not a figure in this district and that I am not going to enter it this morning, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that she entered no figure with what he said, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventeenth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and thirty-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that a man of about forty-eight said a thing out loud at that wall before it was light and that about four people heard him and that the clerk entered the thing he said and entered no figure with it, and that nobody in that yard has asked that man for his figure.

"**The board carries five hundred and ninety, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and six days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and twenty, and five hundred and eighty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man who keeps a tally has been at that low wall before it was light for a month and he said a thing out loud in that yard this morning that is not a figure about anything on that wall, and about four of you have worked out that I entered the thing he said and that I did not enter a figure with it and that he told me not to enter one, and about four of you have worked out that a man saying a thing out loud in that yard is not a fourteenth thing this district has made, and about four of you have worked out that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that it did not move this morning, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and that a man saying a thing out loud about a figure he has not entered is not a fourteenth of them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man went up that bank yesterday morning and came back down it and that nobody in that yard has asked him why he went up.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man of about forty-eight stood at that wall before it was light and said a thing out loud in that yard, and that he has not entered a figure since the sixth of the fifth month. About four of them have said that a clerk entered the thing he said and entered no figure with it and that nobody has asked him for one. About four of them have said that about four people come up that bank before eight and go on to the top of that lane every morning and that a man has a figure for that and is not going to enter it, and that a man of fifty-six read the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight to about nineteen people and did not look down that bank once. About four of them have said that the yard in that bank has one table in it and that it has had nothing on it since the thirty-first of the seventh month, and that the number of mornings that is at is not entered in that yard this morning.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a man who keeps a tally has been at that wall before light and a road keeper comes over the top of it twice a day and neither of them has ever asked the other one for a figure.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it, and went back down that bank at about a quarter to four with his own hands in his own pockets.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man stood at that same wall before it was light this morning and said a thing out loud, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and thirty nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and thirty-first night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and thirty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and forty days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and ninety-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and seventy days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and seventy-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and forty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and thirty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying a thing out loud about a figure he has not entered is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk entering the thing he said is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a figure a man is keeping in his own head is not a thing a clerk has got a copy of.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and eighty-one. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the two pages on the end of that second table are at the foot of that bank and are not on any table in that yard, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in that yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said out loud that he has got a figure. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a man who puts tables up is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying a thing out loud about a figure he is not going to enter is not a payment and a clerk entering what he said is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man who keeps a tally has a figure for the mornings people walk past that yard and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up does not, and that neither of them has entered one. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and seventeenth morning, and that a man of about forty-eight said a thing out loud at that wall before it was light, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and seventeen mornings and that the measure of that man is not how well he reads the four.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the sixth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is six, and that a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said out loud in that yard that he has a figure he is not going to enter, and that she entered that he said it and entered no figure and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is thirteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
