# Chapter 688: On The Second Of The Eighth Month A Stranger Walked Down That Lane Past A Yard With One Table In It And Went On Down

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the second of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and that was the two hundred and sixteenth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard. The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it, and that lot book was lying on it face up with five lines on it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and twenty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is twelve and did not move at about a quarter to eight, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is two this morning and that it moved this morning from one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirteenth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and twenty-six times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that a stranger went down that lane at about half past nine this morning with a page in his own right hand, and that the man of fifty-six was reading the four off that wall at the same moment about two hundred feet above him, and that neither of them looked up.

"**The board carries five hundred and eighty-six, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and two days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and sixteen, and five hundred and seventy-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The boy of about nineteen stood at the top of that bank at about ten past nine and said one thing out loud, in about 48 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man went down that lane at about half past nine this morning with a page in his own right hand and he came to the top of that bank and he looked at that first table and it has nothing on it and he went on down past the bottom of that bank, and I watched him do it and I do not know what is on that page, and about four of you have worked out that he did not stop and that he did not ask anybody anything, and the man at the foot of that bank is standing about nine feet off him and I have not said one word to that man about it, and I have not got another thing.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is thirteen. She entered that it moved this morning and that a person and the reason were in that yard before her hand moved, and that a page a stranger carried down that lane is not a document this district owns, and that a boy who watches a lane is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past eleven, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That number is thirteen this morning and it was twelve yesterday and a boy told me so in that yard at about ten past nine before I had that book open for the second entry, and that I have entered it because a person said it out loud and not because I saw the man, and about four of you have worked out that a page folded in a stranger's own right hand is not a fifth document and that the count of the documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the yard in that bank has had one table in it for two mornings and a man went past it and went on down, and I entered both of those facts and no reason for either, and there is nothing behind that either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is thirteen and that she entered it on the word of a boy who watched the lane this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a stranger came down that lane at about half past nine and went past the two of them and that neither of them said one word and neither of them was asked one word.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a stranger walked down that lane this morning, looked at a first table with nothing on it and went on down that bank to the foot of it, and that a boy said out loud that he watched it happen and has not said one word to the man at the foot of that bank about it, and that a clerk entered the number and entered no reason. About four of them have said that the yard in that bank has had one table in it for two mornings and that a man went past it in about the time it takes to look at a table. About four of them have said that the fifth of those five lines says go down a lane and ask a man who digs loam, and that this morning a man went down that lane and past that yard and on to the foot of that bank, and that nobody in this district has ever put those two things in the same sentence out loud.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a stranger went down that lane at about half past nine and a road keeper went up it at about half past one and neither of them said one word to the other one. About four of them have said that is a thing about a lane and not about a road.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a stranger went down that lane at about half past nine and went past that man, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and twenty-six nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and twenty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and twenty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and thirty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and ninety-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and ninety-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and sixty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and sixty-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and forty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and thirty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a boy watching a lane is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk entering a number a boy gave her is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a count of strangers is a count of mornings and not a count of people living in this district.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and seventy-seven. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the two pages on the end of that second table are at the foot of that bank and are not on any table in that yard, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a stranger went down that lane and past that yard. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a stranger walking past a yard and on to a man is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that a man went down that lane at about half past nine and did not stop at that yard. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and thirteenth morning, and that a stranger went down that lane at about half past nine and the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about the same moment and neither of them looked up and he was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a stranger went past a yard with one table in it and nothing on it and went on down to the foot of that bank, and that a man who has read four figures off a wall at the top of it has still never been asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the second of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is two, and that a boy said out loud in that yard at about ten past nine that a man went down that lane with a page in his own right hand and did not stop at that yard, and that she entered that and entered no reason for it, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is thirteen and that a page a stranger carried down that lane is not a document this district owns, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
