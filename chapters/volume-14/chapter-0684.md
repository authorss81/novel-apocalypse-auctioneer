# Chapter 684: On The Twenty-Ninth Of The Seventh Month A Man Who Digs Loam Said Out Loud Which Side Of That Ground His Own Spade Goes Into

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the twenty-ninth of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the ground at the foot of that bank had gone from hard to soft on the east side in about a week, and that was the two hundred and twelfth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and eighty-two mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and twenty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is twelve and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that a man who mends fencing told her a thing with his own two hands on the twenty-eighth of the seventh month and that she entered it and that it is not a condition with no end on it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and ninth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and eighteen times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that a man came up that bank this morning and said out loud which side of the ground at the bottom of that bank is hard, and that the man of fifty-six was reading the four at the same hour about two hundred feet above him and neither of them looked up.

"**The board carries five hundred and eighty-two, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and ninety-eight days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and twelve, and five hundred and seventy-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past six with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about 45 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and seventeen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The ground at the foot of that bank is hard on the west side to about a foot down and soft on the east side to the knee, and about four of you have worked out that I have been cutting in the soft part because I can get my own spade in it, and I have not put a figure on either of those and I am not going to, and I have carried boards down that bank about four times this month for a man who puts tables up and that he has not paid me for them and that I am not going to ask him, and I have got no more than that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who digs loam is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that boards carried down that bank are not a payment and did not pay the fifth of the five things this district does not have, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about a quarter past eleven and said one thing out loud, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have counted the pages on the end of that second table every morning this month and it has been two every morning, and there is a figure in this district that has not moved in a month and it is the smallest one anybody keeps, and I have never said that number out loud in this yard because a figure that never moves is a thing a clerk enters and not a thing a boy says, and about four of you have worked out that the man who reads those four off that wall has got a figure that moves one every morning and that mine does not and that I have never asked him why, and that is what I have.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and that a boy who counts a number that does not move is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and was at the near end of that second table until about ten and was not in that yard at about eleven, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who digs loam said out loud this morning that the west side of the ground at the foot of that bank is hard to a foot and that he has not put a figure on it, and that a boy said out loud after him that there is a figure in this district that has not moved in a month and that it is the smallest one anybody keeps. About four of them have said that a man who digs loam has said one thing out loud in this yard twice in three days and both of them were about his own ground, and that a boy who will not say a number that never moves and a man who will not put a number on his own ground are standing about nine feet apart in that yard this morning. About four of them have said that the fifth of those five lines says go down a lane and ask a man who digs loam and that he has now said out loud what that ground is like and has still not said what he is digging it for.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a man said out loud this morning which side of the ground his own spade goes into, and that a road keeper has gone over the top of that bank twice a day for years and has never been asked what is under it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who digs loam said out loud this morning that the east side of that ground is soft to the knee, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and twenty-two nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and twenty-third night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and twenty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and thirty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and eighty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and ninety-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and sixty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and sixty-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and forty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and twenty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying a thing out loud about the side of a bank his own spade goes into is not a fourteenth thing and a boy saying a thing out loud about a figure that does not move is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that an entry of a thing a man told her is an entry and not a thing this district has made.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and seventy-three. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and eighty-two mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said out loud which side of that ground his own spade goes into. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who digs loam is not in it and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and four boards carried down that bank by a man who digs loam and not paid for is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and ninth morning, and that a man who digs loam said out loud this morning that the east side of the ground at the foot of that bank is soft to the knee, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man who has read four figures off that wall four hundred and nine mornings said out loud nothing at all about either of the two and about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what any of the four is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-ninth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam said out loud in that yard which side of that ground his own spade goes into and that she entered that he said it and not that anybody is to do anything about it, and that nobody in that yard has asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is twelve and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
