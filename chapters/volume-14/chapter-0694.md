# Chapter 694: On The Eighth Of The Eighth Month The Man Who Drives That Cart Said Out Loud That He Stopped The Cart Once And Has Thought About It Once

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the eighth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and the leaves against the north wall of that yard were still there and about half of them had gone to the west side of that yard in the night, and that was the two hundred and twenty-second morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard. The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it, and that lot book was lying on it face up with five lines on it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and thirty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is thirteen and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is eight this morning and that it moved this morning from seven, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and nineteenth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and thirty-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that about half of those leaves went to the west side of that yard in the night and that nobody in that yard has swept any of them and that a clerk has not entered a figure for a leaf.

"**The board carries five hundred and ninety-two, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and eight days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and twenty-two, and five hundred and eighty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 60 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I enter the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand twice a day, at about a quarter to eight and again at about four, and about four of you have worked out that it moved on two mornings this month and that I was in that yard on both of them, and about four of you have worked out that a count that moves on a morning when I am standing here is a count I have got from somebody else, and about four of you have worked out that I entered that it moved both times and that I entered no name either time, and about four of you have worked out that a name is not the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that nobody has been put in that column, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is thirteen and that she entered it twice this morning on the word of two people and entered no name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a clerk said out loud in that yard this morning that she has entered no name twice this month and that a man who digs loam did not ask her for one.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a clerk said out loud in that yard this morning that a count that moves on a morning when she is standing there is a count she has got from somebody else. About four of them have said that she entered no name on either of the two mornings and that nobody has ever asked her for one. About four of them have said that the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is a different column and has been ruled and empty every morning of this month. About four of them have said that the yard in that bank has one table in it and that there is nothing on it, and that a man has been at that table four mornings this week with his own hands on the far leg of it, and that a man who puts tables up has not been paid for one table in six months.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that the four of them that come up that bank before eight have gone on to the top of that lane every morning of this month and that a man has a figure for that and has not entered it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it, and the man of about twenty-nine said one thing out loud, in about 44 seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fourteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I came up that bank on my own two feet on the thirty-first of the seventh month and I carried one end of that table and I have not been asked one thing about it since, and about four of you have worked out that I have seen the man who mends fencing at the foot of that bank every morning since and that I have not stopped this cart at the top of it once, and about four of you have worked out that I stopped it once on the morning I carried that table and that I have thought about that once and not again, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud at the top of that bank and entered the thing, and entered that a man who drives that cart is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud at the top of that bank this afternoon and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and thirty-two nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and thirty-third night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and thirty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and forty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and ninety-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and seventy-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and seventy-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and eleven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and fifty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and thirty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a name she has not entered is not a fourteenth thing and a man stopping a cart once is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a name a clerk has not entered is not a thing a clerk has made.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and eighty-three. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the two pages on the end of that second table are at the foot of that bank and are not on any table in that yard, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man stopped a cart once. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who drives that cart is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid. The fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household. A man stopping a cart once at the top of that bank is not a payment, and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a name she has not entered is not a payment, and neither of them paid it this afternoon, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard today and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the seventh evening and did not look at the fourth one. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and nineteenth morning, and that a man of about twenty-nine said out loud at the top of that bank that he stopped that cart once and has thought about it once, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man and a cart come up that lane at about three every afternoon and go up that bank past that wall, and that he is the only man in this district that a man who reads figures off that wall has to wait for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the eighth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is eight, and that a man who drives that cart said out loud at the top of that bank that he stopped that cart once on the morning he carried that table down it, and that she entered that and entered no reason, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is thirteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
