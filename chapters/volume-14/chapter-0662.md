# Chapter 662: On The Seventh Of The Seventh Month The Man Who Digs Loam Was At The Far End Of That Second Table At About Eleven And Was Not At It In The Afternoon And Nobody In That Yard Asked Him Where He Was

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the seventh of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard, and that was the hundred and eighty-ninth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and sixty mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is ten and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and eighty-seventh of those mornings. About four of them have said that he read his four at about a quarter to eight this morning and again at about four and got all four both times, and that about four of them have said that about nineteen people in that yard have watched the far end of that second table all afternoon and that nobody in that yard has said one word about it.

"**The board carries five hundred and sixty, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and seventy-six days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and ninety, and five hundred and fifty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about 44 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and sixteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The man who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven this morning and he was not at that table in the afternoon, and I have entered that and I have entered no reason, and about four of you have worked out that about four people at that wall have looked at that far end between about twelve and about four and about four of you have worked out that none of them went down that bank after him, and about four of you have worked out that I have not entered where he went and nobody asked me and I am not going to be the one who asks.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven and was not at that table in the afternoon, and entered no reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about one and said one thing out loud, in about 23 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The man who digs loam is not at that end of that table this afternoon and I have not counted him, and about four of you have worked out that I count everybody, and about four of you have worked out that I am not counting a man who is not here, and I have got nothing else this afternoon.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who does not count a man who is not there is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with his own coil of wire over his own left shoulder and was at the near end of that second table until about ten, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about eleven with his spade over his own right shoulder, set it up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it, and went the three steps to the rail in the fence and put his own two hands on it until about a quarter to twelve, and then he was not at that table again that day, and nobody in that yard asked him where he was.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that about nineteen people in that yard have this month seen a man at the far end of that second table for about a quarter of an hour every morning, and that about four of them have said that this afternoon the far end of that second table was empty from about a quarter to twelve onward and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard has said one word about it and that about four of them have said that a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not at that table and entered no reason at all.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at about half past one this afternoon and went past a yard with an empty end to a table in it, and that about four of them have said that he was not asked about the eleven miles and was not asked about the man who was not there either.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man was not at the far end of that second table this afternoon and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and one nights and has slept on three hundred of them. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and first night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and ten days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and sixty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and seventy days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and forty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and forty-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and seventy-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and nineteen marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man not being at a table is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entering no reason is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man standing somewhere else is a thing a person does. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and fifty-one.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and sixty mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and a man not being at a table this afternoon is not a remedy and did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a reading of a lot does not move because a man was somewhere else this afternoon. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a clerk is not in it and a boy is not in it and the man who was not at that table is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk saying out loud in that yard that she is not entering a reason is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about nineteen people in that yard spent this afternoon looking at an end of a table with nobody at it, and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it and did not look at that end either.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and eighty-seventh morning, and that a man was not at the far end of that second table this afternoon, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the seventh of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that nobody in that yard has asked the man it names one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is ten and did not move, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail and was not at that table in the afternoon, and that nobody in that yard asked him where he was.
