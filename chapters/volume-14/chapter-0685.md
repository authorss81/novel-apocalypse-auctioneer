# Chapter 685: On The Thirtieth Of The Seventh Month A Man Who Keeps A Tally Stood At That Low Wall At A Quarter To Eight And Found Nobody Standing At It

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the thirtieth of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the ground at the foot of that bank was hard on the west side and soft on the east side in the way it was yesterday, and that was the two hundred and thirteenth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and eighty-three mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and twenty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is twelve and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the near leg of that second table has been out of square for about a month and that she is entering it as a leg and not as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and tenth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and twenty times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that a man who keeps a tally stood at that low wall at about a quarter to eight this morning and that the man of fifty-six was reading the four about nine feet from him at the same hour and neither of them looked up.

"**The board carries five hundred and eighty-three, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and ninety-nine days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and thirteen, and five hundred and seventy-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at that low wall at about a quarter to eight and said one thing out loud, in about 50 seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been coming up that lane before that wall is light and I have counted who is standing at it and this morning I got none, and about four of you have worked out that I have been getting none for about a month, and about four of you have worked out that I have never said that number out loud in this yard and that I am saying it now because there is a clerk here with a book, and about four of you have worked out that a wall with nobody at it is not an empty wall and that it has two tables and a book on one of them and about nineteen people in it by ten, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past eleven, in about 60 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The near leg of that second table has been out of square for about a month and I have entered it every morning of that month as a leg and not as a figure, and about four of you have worked out that a leg going over is not a condition with no end on it and that the count of those is four, and about four of you have worked out that the man who put that table up has been at that leg about four times a week and that he has not asked me for a number for it and that I have not offered him one, and about four of you have worked out that a figure nobody has asked for is not a figure this district has got, it is a figure this district has not been asked for, and that is the whole of what I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk's entry of a leg is not a fifth condition with no end on it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and was at the near end of that second table until about ten and was not in that yard at about eleven, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who keeps a tally said out loud this morning that he got none standing at that wall before it was light, and that a clerk said out loud after him that the near leg of that second table has been out of square for about a month and that she enters it as a leg and not as a figure. About four of them have said that this is the second morning this month a person in that yard has said out loud that nobody has ever asked them for a number. About four of them have said that a wall with nobody standing at it before eight and about nineteen people standing in that yard by ten is the ordinary shape of this yard and that it has been going on about a month. About four of them have said that a man who keeps a tally has asked nobody in that yard what he does with a tally and that a clerk has entered what he said and that neither of those two things is going to be asked about this afternoon.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a man stood at that wall before it was light and counted nobody at it, and that a road keeper has gone past that wall twice a day for more than three hundred mornings and has never stopped at it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who keeps a tally said out loud this morning that he got none standing at that wall before it was light, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and twenty-three nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and twenty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and twenty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and thirty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and eighty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and ninety-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and sixty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and sixty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and forty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and twenty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying a thing out loud about an empty wall is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a leg is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a tally a man keeps in his own head is a thing a man has and not a thing this district has made, and that a leg going over is a leg and not a figure.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and seventy-four. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and eighty-three mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said out loud that he got none standing at that wall. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man who puts tables up is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud in that yard that he counted nobody at that wall is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and tenth morning, and that a man who keeps a tally stood at that low wall before it was light and said out loud that he got none, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that two men have stood at that wall before it was light this month about nine feet apart and neither of them has ever been asked what the other one is doing there.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirtieth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said out loud in that yard that he got none standing at that wall before it was light, and that she entered that he said it and not that a wall is empty, and that the near leg of that second table has been out of square for about a month and that she has entered it as a leg and not as a figure, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is twelve and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
