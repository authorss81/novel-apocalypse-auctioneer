# Chapter 677: On The Twenty-Second Of The Seventh Month A Man Said Out Loud That He Had Been Back To His Own Post Four Times And That It Had Not Moved

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the twenty-second of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the near leg of that second table was out of square in the way it was out of square yesterday, and that was the two hundred and fifth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and seventy-five mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and sixteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eleven and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and second of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that a clerk read two lines off a stranger's page yesterday morning and a man read four figures off that wall this morning, and that the yard has three people in it who read things written down and not one of the three has been asked what any of it is for.

"**The board carries five hundred and seventy-five, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and ninety-one days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and five, and five hundred and sixty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past eleven, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A page in this district points at a man and not at a yard, and I have been at that book nineteen years and I have never once been able to enter where he is, and about four of you have worked out that a name is a thing a clerk can write down and a place is not, and about four of you have worked out that I could enter this yard on that page in about the time it takes to write it, and about four of you have worked out that if I enter a place then the man is not findable at one table at one hour of one morning any more and that he is findable everywhere, and that is not a man, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to do that, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man is not put in a place and that a place is not put in a man, and that a column has not been ruled this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and said one thing out loud, in about 57 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I set a post in my own fence on the far side of the lane in the third month of this year and I have been back to it four times since the water went down, and about four of you have worked out that it has not moved one inch in all that time, and about four of you have worked out that a man who goes back to a post four times is not a man anybody in this yard pays to go and look, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to say out loud what I go down that bank for because there is nothing in that yard that could hold it, and that is the third time I have said that and I am not going to say it a fourth, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who mends fencing is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for. About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man said out loud this morning that he has been back to his own post four times and that it has not moved, and that a clerk said out loud before him that a page in this district points at a man and not at a yard and that she is not going to put a place on it. About four of them have said that a man who has said three times that he is not going to say what he goes down that bank for has now said that to about nineteen people. About four of them have said that the man that page points at is standing nine feet away with his own two hands on that rail, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with that.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a man said out loud this morning that he is not going to say a thing a fourth time. About four of them have said that the road keeper goes up that lane twice a day and has never once said a thing three times.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said out loud this morning that he is not going to say it a fourth time, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and fifteen nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and sixteenth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and fifteen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and twenty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and eighty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and eighty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and fifty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and fifty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and ninety-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and thirty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and twenty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying a thing out loud about a post is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a place is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man looking at his own fence four times is a thing a man does and a yard does not have a fence.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and sixty-six. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and seventy-five mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said out loud that he has been back to his own post four times. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who mends fencing is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a boy is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud in that yard that nobody in it pays him to go and look at a post is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty and came back up it full at about half past six, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said out loud this morning that he is not going to say a thing a fourth time, and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and second morning, and that a man who mends fencing said out loud this morning that he is not going to say a thing a fourth time, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two. About four of them have said that a man in that yard has now said the same refusal three times and a man in that yard has read the same four figures four hundred and two times. About four of them have said that neither of the two has ever been asked to do the other thing.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-second of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that a clerk has said out loud in that yard that she is not going to put a place on a page that points at a man, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud in that yard that he is not going to say a thing a fourth time, and that the count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eleven and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
