# Chapter 655: On The Thirty-First Of The Sixth Month A Clerk Of Nineteen Years Counted That Whole Month Off That Board With A Finger On The Wood In The Open And Got Thirty-One

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the thirty-first of the sixth month and the boards were dry and hard and had gone back to the colour of a road that has not been touched since the frost, and that was the hundred and eighty-second morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and fifty-three mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and ninety-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is nine and did not move, and entered that the rime on those boards is a thing the night did and is not entered as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and eightieth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he read his four at about a quarter to eight this morning with his own back to about nineteen people counting a month off a board six feet away from him, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard stopped him, and that about four of them have said that is three hundred and eighty mornings.

"**The board carries five hundred and fifty-three, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and sixty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and eighty-three, and five hundred and forty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years counted the sixth month of this year off that board with a finger on the wood, in that yard, in the open, in daylight, with about nineteen people at that wall, and it took her about ten minutes, and the boy of about nineteen stood at her own shoulder and counted the marks off that board with his own finger an inch behind hers and did not say one number out loud the whole time, and when she had finished she said one thing out loud, in about 69 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Thirty-one. That is the sixth month and it is thirty-one days and the last mark on that board was cut at dawn this morning, and this morning is the last morning of it, and it is not over until tonight, and about four of you have worked out that I counted it while I am standing in it, and about four of you have worked out that a month cannot be counted off a board by a person who is standing in it, and I have got no answer to that and I have got no second person to stand in it for me, and about four of you have worked out that I am a clerk and I have had a finger on that board for nineteen years and this is the third time I have counted a month in this yard and I counted two of them out of it, and the count of counted months is six, and about four of you have worked out that I did not alter anything this morning because there was nothing to alter.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the sixth month of this year is thirty-one days, and entered that it was counted off that board this morning with a finger on the wood in the open in daylight with about nineteen people at that wall, and entered that the count of counted months is six and was five yesterday, and entered that it is a count and it is kept in a different book from the wall, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen came up off that board at about ten past and said one thing out loud, in about 52 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted those marks twice, once with my own finger an inch behind hers and once with my own thumbnail after she had taken hers off the wood, and I got the same number both times, and I am not going to say the number out loud because she has said it and I am not going to say a thing twice in one morning, and about four of you have worked out that I have been able to put a number on nearly everything in this yard since before that line went on that book, and this morning I could not put a number on the ten minutes it took her, and about four of you have worked out that I have stopped trying to do that and I am not going to start again.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who counted a board twice and got the same number twice is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and stood at the near end of that second table until about ten, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for, and about four of them have said since that he was in that yard the whole ten minutes and that he did not look at the board once while it was being counted.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past six with mud to the thigh of his own right leg and stood at the far end of that second table, and watched the whole ten minutes of it without moving and said nothing at all to anybody, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man who has been at that end of that table every morning this month watched a woman count a month off a board six feet away from him and did not say one word, and that about four of them have said that he came up that bank about an hour and a half before she started and that his own boots were still wet when she took her finger off the wood.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that about nineteen people in that yard spent ten minutes this morning counting a month off a board with a finger on the wood and that the answer was thirty-one, and about four of them have said that the two counts before it came out the way the pages had been saying all along and that this one did not, and about four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall three hundred and eighty mornings and was not asked one thing about a month he has been printing the right answer for that long, and that about four of them have said that is four times in this district's history and that nobody has ever worked out what to do with it.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by without stopping, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour on the morning a month was counted six feet off it and was not asked about it, and that about four of them have said that nobody has ever asked him whether he has got anything to say about a month.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nineteen people in that yard spent this morning counting a month off a board and that the man on those stones has not been counted this morning and has not been counted on any morning. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and ninety-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and ninety-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and fifty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and sixty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and thirty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and thirty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and seventy-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and twelve marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and ninety-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and counting a month is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count itself, which is that a month is not a thing a district makes and that count is kept in a different book. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and forty-four.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and fifty-three mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this month and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and counting a month did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a reading of a lot does not move because somebody counted a month. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a clerk who counted a month is not in it and a boy who counted a board twice is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk counting a month off a board in front of about nineteen people did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about nineteen people in that yard spent this morning counting a month off a board and that the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it, and that about four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a clerk counted a month off that board under his own eyes this morning and that he read his four figures at about the same moment and was not asked one thing about either, and that about four of them have said that a man who has said four numbers out loud in that yard three hundred and eighty mornings has still never been asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirty-first of the sixth month that the sixth month of this year was counted off that board in that yard at about a quarter to eight with a finger on the wood in the open in daylight with about nineteen people at that wall, and that the count came to thirty-one and the last mark on that board was cut at dawn this morning, and that the count of counted months is six, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is nine and did not move, and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about half past six and watched the whole of the count without moving.
