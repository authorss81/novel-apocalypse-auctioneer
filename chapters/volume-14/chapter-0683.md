# Chapter 683: On The Twenty-Eighth Of The Seventh Month A Man Said Out Loud That The Wood Moved Under His Own Two Hands And That He Has Never Said So

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the twenty-eighth of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the boards of that first table were dry and hard and the two of them had been sitting about nine feet apart in that yard for about a month, and that was the two hundred and eleventh morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and eighty-one mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and twenty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is twelve and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and eighth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and sixteen times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that about nine feet off that wall there are two tables in that yard and that the man who reads those four has never once looked at either of them.

"**The board carries five hundred and eighty-one, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and ninety-seven days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and eleven, and five hundred and seventy-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight, put his own two hands flat on the near end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about 37 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That wood moved under my own two hands this morning, and about four of you have worked out that I have been putting them on that near end about once a morning for a month, and I have not told anybody in this district that the table moves, and I am saying it out loud this morning because it moved while that man was standing about nine feet off reading four figures off that wall, and I am not asking anybody in this yard to do anything about it, and there is nothing else I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who mends fencing is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past eleven, in about 43 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eleven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been at this book nineteen years and this is the first morning a man in this yard has told me a thing with his own two hands instead of his own mouth, and I wrote it down, and about four of you have worked out that I have not made a rule of it and that I am not going to, and a thing a person does with his own hands is still a thing a person does, and the count of the things this district has made is thirteen and it did not move this morning, and that is the end of what I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a thing a person told her with his own hands is not a fourteenth thing and that a clerk's entry of it is not a rule, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table until about ten and was not in that yard at about eleven. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who mends fencing said out loud this morning that the wood of that second table moved under his own two hands and that he has never told anybody in that district about it, and that a clerk said out loud after him that it is the first thing a man in that yard has told her with his hands. About four of them have said that a clerk has a book with five lines in it and that she has written down this morning that a table moves, and that the line about the fifth of those five has not been touched. About four of them have said that a man told a thing out loud with his own two hands and not with his own mouth, and that about nineteen people in that yard heard it and that about nine of them were within hearing of that wall at the same moment as the four figures. About four of them have said that is a thing about a man and his own hands and not about that wall.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a man stood about nine feet off that wall and put his own hands on a table this morning and that neither of those two things was said to the other one.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said out loud this morning that a table moved under his own hands, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and twenty-one nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and twenty-second night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and twenty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and thirty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and eighty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and ninety-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and sixty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and sixty-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and forty marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and twenty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man telling a clerk a thing with his own hands is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying out loud that she is not going to make a rule of it is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that hands are hands and a table is a table and neither of them is a thing this district has made this morning.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and seventy-two. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and eighty-one mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man told a clerk a thing with his own two hands. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who mends fencing is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud in that yard that the wood moved under his own hands is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and eighth morning, and that a man said out loud this morning that the wood moved under his own two hands while that man was standing about nine feet off reading, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that about nine people heard both of those things this morning and that neither of the two men knew the other one had said one.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-eighth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud in that yard that the wood of that second table moved under his own hands, and that she entered that he said it and not that anybody is to do anything about it, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is twelve and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
