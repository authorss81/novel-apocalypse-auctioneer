# Chapter 679: On The Twenty-Fourth Of The Seventh Month A Man Said Out Loud What The Cold Under The Top Inch Tells Him

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the twenty-fourth of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the water at the bottom of that ditch had gone down about another inch since the day before, and that was the two hundred and seventh morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and seventy-seven mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and eighteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is twelve and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fourth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that the man who digs loam was at that end of that table at about eleven this morning with his own two hands on that rail and that the man of fifty-six read his four off that wall about nine feet away at the same hour, and that neither of them has ever looked at the other one.

"**The board carries five hundred and seventy-seven, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and ninety-three days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and seven, and five hundred and sixty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about ten past eleven with his own spade over his own right shoulder and said one thing out loud, in about 62 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The top inch of that loam at the bottom of that bank is about two degrees colder than the inch under it, and that is why I know which way to cut without putting a spade in first, and about four of you have worked out that I have never said that out loud in this yard and that I am saying it now because nobody has ever asked me, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard has asked me which man I am digging for, and that the fifth line of that book says ask a man who digs loam and does not say what for, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to guess, and about four of you have worked out that nobody in this yard has ever asked me to stay down there and nobody is going to, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who digs loam is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that nobody in that yard has asked him to stay at the bottom of that bank and that nobody is going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight and was at the near end of that second table until about ten and was not in that yard at about eleven, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. About nine people at that low wall have said since that a man who digs loam said out loud this morning that the top inch of that loam is about two degrees colder than the inch under it and that he is not going to guess what he is digging for. About four of them have said that a man in that yard has now said one thing out loud about his own work in about a month. About four of them have said that it was about the cold under the top inch and not about the fifth line. About four of them have said that a man at the far end of that second table and a man nine feet away reading four figures off a wall are both in that yard at about eleven and neither of them said one word about the other one.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a man said out loud this morning that nobody in that yard is going to ask him to stay at the bottom of that bank, and that a road keeper is the only man in that district who is asked to be somewhere every day of his life and has not been asked to stay.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who digs loam said out loud this morning that he is not going to guess, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and seventeen nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eighteenth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and seventeen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and twenty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and eighty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and eighty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and fifty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and sixty days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and ninety-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and thirty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and twenty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying a thing out loud about the cold under the top inch is not a fourteenth thing, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man knowing which way to cut is a thing a man knows and not a thing a yard has got.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and sixty-eight. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and seventy-seven mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said out loud that he is not going to guess what he is digging for. About one thing was said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a man who digs loam is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man who mends fencing is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud in that yard that he is not going to guess what he is to be used for is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank at about half past five with nothing in them and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about one thing was said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up said nothing at all. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fourth morning, and that a man who digs loam said out loud this morning that he is not going to guess, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two. About four of them have said that about one thing was said out loud in that yard today and that the man of fifty-six was in that yard the whole of it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fourth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that nobody in that yard has asked the man it names one thing about what he is to be used for, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam said out loud in that yard that he is not going to guess, and entered that nobody has asked him to stay at the bottom of that bank and that nobody is going to, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is twelve and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
