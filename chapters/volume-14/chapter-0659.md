# Chapter 659: On The Fourth Of The Seventh Month A Man Who Keeps A Tally Said Out Loud That A Stranger Came Down That Lane With A Page In His Own Hand And Went On Past That Yard, And A Clerk Entered Ten

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the fourth of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard, and that was the hundred and eighty-sixth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and fifty-seven mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and ninety-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that a man came down that lane at about half past eight with a page folded in his own right hand and went on down past that bank, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and eighty-fourth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he read his four at about a quarter to eight this morning and that about nine people at that wall have stood in that yard since before he started and that nobody in that yard has asked him one thing about the man who came down that lane at about half past eight.

"**The board carries five hundred and fifty-seven, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and seventy-three days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and eighty-seven, and five hundred and forty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came up that bank at about nine with the flat book shut under his own left arm and stopped at the east end of that yard, and said one thing out loud, in about 65 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and seventy-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man came down that lane at about half past eight this morning with a page folded in his own right hand and he did not stop at the top of it and he did not come up that bank and he went on down past the bottom of it, and about four of you have worked out that I was at the top of that lane and I saw him go past and I did not stop him, and about four of you have worked out that the reason that man is the tenth is that he came down that lane with a page in his hand and that is the whole of the reason and there is no other one, and about four of you have worked out that a man who walks past a yard with a page in his hand has been sent somewhere and is not a man who is looking, and that is all I have got this morning and he is not from this parish.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the reason out loud with it, which is that a man came down that lane with a page in his own hand, and entered that a stranger is not a resident of this district, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The count of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is ten, and it moved this morning, and the reason is in this yard and the man who gave the reason is a man who keeps a tally and he said it out loud to about nineteen people before I put the number in this book, and about four of you have worked out that I do not move that number to fill a page and I have not moved it this morning for a page, I have moved it because a man came down that lane, and about four of you have worked out that a stranger is not a resident and that there has not been a new person added to this district this morning and the count of that is still nothing.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is ten and moved this morning with a person and a reason in the room before the hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight and was at the near end of that second table until about ten, and about four of them have said since that he came up that bank before the clerk had that book open and that he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for and that about four of them have said he has not said it since the morning he said he was not going to say it twice.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past ten with his spade in his own two hands and not over his own shoulder, set it up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it, and put his own two hands on the rail in the fence and stayed there until about a quarter to twelve, and nobody asked him one thing about the man who came down that lane this morning with a page in his own hand, and nobody has ever asked him one thing about what he is to be used for.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that about nineteen people in that yard have now been told twice about a man who came down that lane this morning, once by a man who keeps a tally and once by a clerk of nineteen years, and about four of them have said that the man who keeps a tally said it first and that the clerk put the number in her book after he had said it and not before, and about four of them have said that a man who walks past a yard with a page in his hand has been sent somewhere and is not a man who is looking and that nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with being the place he was sent to.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man came down that lane this morning with a page in his own hand and that the road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and was not asked about it and was not asked whether he had seen the man either.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud this morning about a stranger and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in two hundred and ninety-eight nights and has slept on two hundred and ninety-seven of them. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and ninety-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and ninety-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and sixty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and sixty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and thirty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and forty days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and seventy-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and sixteen marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man counting strangers down a lane is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk entering ten is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a person going down a lane is a thing a person does. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and forty-eight.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a page a stranger carried down that lane is not a fifth document and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and fifty-seven mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and a stranger walking past that yard with a page in his hand is not a remedy and did not advance it this morning, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a reading of a lot does not move because somebody counted a person. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and no stranger is in it and a man who keeps a tally is not in it and a clerk is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying out loud in that yard that a stranger came down that lane with a page in his hand is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man came down that lane this morning with a page in his own hand and went on down past that bank and that the buckets went down that bank at about half past five and came back up it full and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what to do with a place that people go past on their way somewhere else.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and eighty-fourth morning, and that a man who keeps a tally said out loud this morning that a stranger came down that lane and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the fourth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that nobody in that yard has asked the man it names one thing about what he is to be used for since he said no, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is ten and moved this morning with a person and a reason in the room before the hand moved, and that a man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail.
