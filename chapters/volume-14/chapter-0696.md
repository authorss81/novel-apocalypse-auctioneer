# Chapter 696: On The Tenth Of The Eighth Month A Stranger Stood At The Foot Of That Bank About Four Feet Off A Man And Said Nothing To Him

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the tenth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and the leaves against the west wall of that yard had begun to go over the top of it, and that was the two hundred and twenty-fourth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard. The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it, and that lot book was lying on it face up with five lines on it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and thirty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is thirteen at about a quarter to eight and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is ten this morning and that it moved this morning from nine, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen was at the top of that bank at about ten past nine and said one thing out loud, in about 56 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man went down that lane at about half past nine this morning with a page in his own right hand and he came to the top of that bank and he looked at that first table and it has nothing on it and he went on past that yard, and about four of you have worked out that he went on down to the foot of that bank and stood at the near end of that second table, and about four of you have worked out that I watched him do it and that I do not know what is on that page, and about four of you have worked out that he did not ask anybody anything and that the man who digs loam was nine feet off him and did not say one word, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen. She entered that it moved this morning and that a person and the reason were in that yard before her hand moved, and that a page folded in a stranger's own right hand is not a document this district owns, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-first of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and forty-two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that a man went down that lane at about half past nine with a page in his own right hand and that the man of fifty-six was reading the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight, and that neither of them looked up.

"**The board carries five hundred and ninety-four, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and ten days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and twenty-four, and five hundred and eighty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 69 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That number is fourteen and it was thirteen at about a quarter to eight this morning, and about four of you have worked out that a boy told me so in that yard at about ten past nine before I had that book open for the second entry, and about four of you have worked out that a man who comes to the top of that bank and looks at a table with nothing on it and goes on down is a person and that I have entered the number on his word and not because I saw the man, and about four of you have worked out that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and that I entered that this morning before I said anything out loud, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and that she entered it on the word of a boy who watched that lane and that a stranger is not a resident of this district, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail and said one thing out loud, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That man stood at the near end of that table this morning and he read those five lines and I was nine feet off him at the other end of it, and about four of you have worked out that he did not ask me one thing and that I did not say one word to him, and about four of you have worked out that the fifth of those five lines says go down a lane and ask a man who digs loam and that he went down that lane this morning, and about four of you have worked out that he stood nine feet off me and did not say it, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who digs loam is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that nobody asked that man one thing about what he is to be used for, and that nobody has asked him that since he said no, and entered no name of the stranger because the stranger did not read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for, and nobody asked him one thing about the stranger.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a stranger came down that lane at about half past nine this morning with a page in his own right hand and went past a yard with one table in it and nothing on it and went on down to the foot of that bank.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it, and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a stranger stood nine feet off that man this morning at the other end of a table and read five lines off a book, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and thirty-four nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and thirty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and thirty-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and forty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and ninety-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and seventy-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and seventy-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and thirteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and fifty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and thirty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a stranger reading five lines at the foot of a bank is not a fourteenth thing and a man who digs loam saying a thing out loud about it is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man who came down a lane and went back up one has not made a thing in this district at all.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and eighty-five. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the two pages on the end of that second table are at the foot of that bank and are not on any table in that yard, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a stranger read those five lines and said nothing to anybody. About three things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a stranger is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a stranger standing nine feet off that man and not asking him one thing is not a payment and a clerk entering a number a boy gave her is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man who read those five lines this morning has read them before and that nobody in this district knows when he first read them. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-first morning, and that a stranger went down that lane at about half past nine and stood at the bottom of that bank and the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and twenty-one mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the tenth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is ten, and that a man came down that lane with a page in his own right hand at about half past nine and stood at the near end of that second table and said nothing to the man who digs loam, and that she entered the number of people who have come down that lane on the word of a boy and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and did not move between about ten past ten and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
