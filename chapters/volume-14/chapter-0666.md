# Chapter 666: On The Eleventh Of The Seventh Month Neither Of Two Men Was In That Yard At About Eleven

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the eleventh of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and had gone back to the colour of a road that has not been touched since the frost, and that was the hundred and ninety-third morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and sixty-four mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is ten and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-first of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and eighty-two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that the fourth of the four has been out longer than anybody in that yard has been keeping a book of any kind.

"**The board carries five hundred and sixty-four, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and eighty days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and ninety-four, and five hundred and fifty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

At about ten past eleven the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are two men this morning who were not standing in this yard at about eleven, and I have entered both of them at the bottom of that bank, and I have entered no reason for either, and about four of you have worked out that I could have entered one this morning in about the time it takes to say it out loud, and about four of you have worked out that a reason a clerk puts in this book is a thing she would be saying twice a morning for as long as that yard stands, and about four of you have worked out that I have been at this book nineteen years and I have never once entered a reason I did not hear said out loud in this yard, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam were both at the bottom of that bank this morning, and that she is entering no reason for either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about a quarter past eleven with a sheet of his own flat on the rail in front of him and said one thing out loud, in about 47 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been putting a number on the people in this yard who say a number out loud, and about four of you have worked out that the number is most often nothing, and about four of you have worked out that I have got that on a sheet of my own in two columns, and that this district has four columns of not asking and not one of the four is mine, and about four of you have worked out that I have been keeping that sheet since before that line went on that book, and about four of you have worked out that I have not asked anybody to come and look at it, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a sheet of a boy's own is not a fifth column of not asking, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about eleven that yard held about nineteen people and not one of the two men who stand in it on most mornings. The clerk was at the near end of that first table with her own shoulder against the edge of it. The boy was four feet off the near end of that second table. The man of fifty-six had his own back to the wall and his own eyes on four figures about nine feet off him. About four people at that low wall had worked out that neither of those two men was in that yard at about eleven, and that about four of them had worked out that the two of them had been up that bank before eight and were not standing in it now, and that about four of them had said that nobody in that yard went down that bank to look, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight with nothing at all in his own hands and was at the near end of that second table until about ten and then was not at it, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with his spade over his own right shoulder and mud to the knee of his own left leg, and set the spade up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it and went the three steps to the rail in the fence and put his own two hands on it, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour on a morning when about nineteen people in that yard had watched the same two doors in the same hour and neither of them came through either one. About four of them have said that a clerk entered that and entered no reason and that is the whole of what this district does with a thing it cannot explain.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that she entered no reason for two men who were not in that yard, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and four nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and fifth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and fourteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and sixty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and seventy-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and forty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and forty-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and eighty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and twenty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a clerk entering two men at the bottom of that bank is not a fourteenth thing and a boy keeping a sheet of his own is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that an entry is a piece of a morning and not a morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and fifty-five.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move. A copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and sixty-four mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a clerk entered two men in a book. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a clerk who entered no reason is not in it and a boy with a sheet of his own is not in it and the man who said no is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk entering that two men were at the bottom of that bank is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about nineteen people in that yard stood in it at about eleven this morning with two men not in it, and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-first morning, and that about nineteen people in that yard heard him say four figures out loud twice today. About four of them have said that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what one of the four is for, and that about a quarter to eight and about four are the only two numbers of people anybody in that yard keeps about him.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the eleventh of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam were both at the bottom of that bank this morning and that neither of them was in that yard at about eleven, and that she is entering no reason for either, and that the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth and is not paid a second time, and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household and is not paid, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is ten and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
