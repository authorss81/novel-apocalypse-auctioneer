# Chapter 672: On The Seventeenth Of The Seventh Month A Boy Worked Out That His Own Number Was Not A Count Of People

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the seventeenth of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the water standing in the bottom of that ditch had gone down about an inch since the day before, and that was the hundred and ninety-ninth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and seventy mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and eleven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eleven and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that neither of the two men who stand in that yard was standing in it at about eleven this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-seventh of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and ninety-four times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that this morning the fourth of the four on that wall and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost are the same number and that he read both of them out loud in the ordinary voice this morning and that nobody in that yard has ever noticed.

"**The board carries five hundred and seventy, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and eighty-six days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred, and five hundred and sixty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

At about ten past eleven the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about 58 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fifty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The age of that figure on the sheet at that gatepost is the same number this morning as the figure itself, and about four of you have worked out that I did not make that happen and about four of you have worked out that I did not enter it that way, and about four of you have worked out that a figure about a figure and the figure itself are two different things and that they have arrived at the same number on their own, and about four of you have worked out that I am entering both of them this morning in two places in this book because they are two things, and about four of you have worked out that a man who reads the four off that wall said the same number twice this morning without knowing why, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the age of the figure on the sheet at that gatepost and the figure itself are two figures and are both entered and neither was altered, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about a quarter past eleven and said one thing out loud, in about 46 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and twenty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been counting the people in this yard in the morning and I have got the same number every morning this month, and about four of you have worked out that I have never once found out whether that is because the number does not change or because I have got it wrong every morning, and about four of you have worked out that a count that never changes is not a count of the people, it is a count of me, and about four of you have worked out that I have not worked out what to do about that, and I am not going to work it out in that yard, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who counts one number every morning is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight and was at the near end of that second table until about ten, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with his own two hands black to the wrist, and set his spade up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it and put his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for.

At about eleven that yard held about nineteen people and not one of those two men. About nine people at that low wall have said since that a boy said out loud this morning that a count which never changes is a count of him and not of the people in that yard, and that a clerk said out loud before him that two figures in that book have arrived at the same number on their own. About four of them have said that a boy who has found out he is the thing he is counting and a clerk who has found out that two of her figures have met are in one yard about nine feet apart. About four of them have said that neither of them has offered the other one thing, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with that.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that the four figures on that wall are a different four from the four in that book and that nobody in that yard has ever asked anybody to say so out loud.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and set the shafts down at the top of that bank, and he stayed there until about four and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a boy said out loud this morning that he has found out he is the thing he has been counting, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and ten nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and eleventh night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and ten of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and twenty days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and seventy-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and eighty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and fifty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and fifty-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and eighty-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and twenty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and fifteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a boy working out that he is the thing he counts is not a fourteenth thing, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that finding out a thing about yourself is a thing a person does and not a thing a yard has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and sixty-one. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and seventy mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because two figures in that book arrived at the same number by themselves. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk entering two figures that agree with each other is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-seventh morning, and that he said the fourth of the four off that wall this morning and that the same number is standing in that book as the age of a figure at a gatepost four miles up that lane. About four of them have said that the man of fifty-six has read that number out loud three hundred and ninety-seven times without ever being asked to say it twice.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the seventeenth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost and the age of that figure stand at the same number this morning and that both were entered and neither was altered, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam were both at the bottom of that bank this morning and that she is entering no reason for either, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eleven and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
