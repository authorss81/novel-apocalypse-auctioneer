# Chapter 671: On The Sixteenth Of The Seventh Month A Boy Admitted He Had Two Numbers For One Thing And Said Neither

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the sixteenth of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the rime had not come back on them since the night before last, and that was the hundred and ninety-eighth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and sixty-nine mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and ten days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eleven and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that neither of the two men who stand in that yard was standing in it at about eleven this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-sixth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and ninety-two times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that he said them in the ordinary voice both times this morning and that a man who has said four figures out loud three hundred and ninety-six mornings has never once said one of them slowly.

"**The board carries five hundred and sixty-nine, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and eighty-five days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and ninety-nine, and five hundred and sixty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

At about ten past eleven the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about 61 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and sixty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eleven and it did not move this morning, and about four of you have worked out that the last time it moved a man said so in that yard before I had the book open, and about four of you have worked out that if it moves again this month then the person who says it will be a person who was looking at that lane and not a person who was told about it, and about four of you have worked out that I have entered it both times on a person's word and never once on my own eyes, and about four of you have worked out that is a thing I have decided is good and about four of you have worked out that I have never asked anybody whether it is, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk who enters a count on a person's word is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about a quarter past eleven and said one thing out loud, in about 50 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have two numbers for the people who come up that bank before a quarter to eight, and about four of you have worked out that I have had one of them for a long time and the other one since the rime came back, and about four of you have worked out that I counted both ways this morning and got two numbers that are not the same, and about four of you have worked out that I am not going to say either of them out loud because the first one is right about four mornings a week and the second one is right the rest, and that is a thing I have worked out about myself and not about anybody else, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy with two numbers for one thing is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight and was at the near end of that second table until about ten, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with his own right boot laced across the instep instead of under it, and set his spade up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it and went the three steps to the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for.

At about eleven that yard held about nineteen people and not one of those two men. About nine people at that low wall have said since that a boy said out loud this morning that he has two numbers for the people who come up that bank before a quarter to eight and that he is not going to say either, and that a clerk said out loud before him that she has entered the lane count on a person's word twice now and never once on her own eyes. About four of them have said that a boy and a clerk in one yard are both keeping numbers off other people and neither of them has offered one of them to anybody, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with that.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a boy in that yard has got a number for everybody who comes up that bank in the morning and has never once got a number for the man who keeps that lane. About four of them have said that is a thing about a boy and not about a man.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a boy said out loud this morning that he has two numbers for one thing, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and nine nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and tenth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and nineteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and seventy-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and seventy-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and forty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and fifty-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and eighty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and twenty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and fourteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a boy with two numbers for one thing is not a fourteenth thing, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a boy being right about four mornings a week is a thing about a boy and not a fourth column of not asking.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and sixty. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and sixty-nine mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a boy admitted out loud that he had two numbers for one thing. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy with two numbers is not in it and a clerk is not in it and a man who mends fencing is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a boy saying out loud in that yard that he is not going to say either of his numbers is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five, and were carried back up it heavy, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up said nothing at all. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-sixth morning, and that about two things were said out loud in that yard today, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two. About four of them have said that this yard has got about nineteen people in it at about six and one thing said out loud in it and nobody has ever worked out which of the two is the reason the other is there.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the sixteenth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that a boy of about nineteen said out loud in that yard that he has two numbers for the people who come up that bank before a quarter to eight and that he said neither, and that a clerk does not enter a number a boy has not said, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eleven and did not move, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam were both at the bottom of that bank this morning and that she is entering no reason for either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
