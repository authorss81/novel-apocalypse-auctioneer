# Chapter 689: On The Third Of The Eighth Month A Boy Counted That Yard At Ten And At Eleven And Got About Nineteen Both Times

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the third of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and the ground at the foot of that bank was hard on the west side and soft on the east side in the way it was the day before, and that was the two hundred and seventeenth morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard. The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it, and that lot book was lying on it face up with five lines on it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and twenty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is thirteen and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is three this morning and that it moved this morning from two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fourteenth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and twenty-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that the two men who have been at that table at about eleven every morning for a month were at it again at about eleven this morning, and that neither of them has ever asked the other one one thing.

"**The board carries five hundred and eighty-seven, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and three days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and seventeen, and five hundred and seventy-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that first table at about ten and counted that yard, and counted it again at about eleven, and said one thing out loud, in about 45 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and eighteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted this yard at about ten and I counted it again at about eleven and I got about nineteen both times, and about four of you have worked out that a table went down that bank two mornings ago and that I counted this yard the same number before that and after it, and I have been counting this yard for about a month and it has not moved once, and I have never worked out whether that is because the number does not move or because I count it wrong every morning, and I am not going to work it out in that yard this morning either, and that is the whole of what I counted.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who counts a yard twice and names nobody is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past eleven, in about 57 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That book is on that table at the foot of that bank and the fifth of those five lines is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and I entered it this morning the way I entered it yesterday and the morning before, and about four of you have worked out that a condition with no end on it does not care which end of a bank a book is lying on, and the count of those is four and it did not move this morning, and a woman of about fifty-eight went down that bank last night and read the first three lines of that book standing up and did not read the fourth one, and I have entered that she did not and I am entering no reason for it, and that is the end of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk's entry of what a woman did not read is not a fifth condition with no end on it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that bank at about ten with nothing at all in his own hands and about nine feet off the near end of that second table, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no, and nobody in this district has asked him one thing about what a stranger who walks past that yard is to be used for either.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that a boy counted that yard at about ten and at about eleven this morning and got about nineteen both times for the first time since a table went down that bank, and that a clerk said out loud after him that a condition with no end on it does not care which end of a bank a book is lying on. About four of them have said that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and that the fifth line of that book is one of them and that the count did not move this morning. About four of them have said that a woman of about fifty-eight goes down that bank in the evening now to read three lines and that a clerk has entered that she did not read the fourth one and has entered no reason. About four of them have said that about nineteen people have been coming up that bank to that yard every morning this month and that none of them has ever gone down it except the two men and the woman and the man with the buckets.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a boy counted that yard twice this morning and got the same number and that a road keeper has gone up that lane twice a day for more than three hundred mornings and has never counted it once.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk said out loud this morning that a condition with no end on it does not care which end of a bank a book is lying on, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and twenty-seven nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and twenty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and twenty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and thirty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and ninety-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and ninety-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and sixty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and seventy days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and forty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and thirty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a boy counting a yard twice is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a condition with no end on it is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a book going down a bank is a book going down a bank and is not a thing this district has made this morning.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and seventy-eight. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the two pages on the end of that second table are at the foot of that bank and are not on any table in that yard, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a clerk entered what a woman did not read. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk entering the fact that a woman did not read a fourth line is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up was in that yard this evening about four feet off the fourth one and did not look at it. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and fourteenth morning, and that a boy said out loud this morning that he counted that yard at about ten and at about eleven and got about nineteen both times, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a boy and a man in that yard have been counting the same thing every morning for a month and neither of them has ever asked the other one what he got.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the third of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is three, and that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move and that the fifth line is one of them and is still on that book, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is thirteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
