# Chapter 674: On The Nineteenth Of The Seventh Month A Boy Counted That Yard At Ten And At Eleven And Got The Same Number Twice

There was no rime on the boards of that second table on the nineteenth of the seventh month and the boards were dry and hard and the near leg of that second table was out of square in exactly the way it was out of square yesterday, and that was the two hundred and first morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for a hundred and seventy-two mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is entered as two, and it is not one more than it was the day before.

The first table had that lot book on it under a stone with no wear on it, and the book has five lines on it, and the fifth of those five is in a clerk's hand at the dicta of the man the third line is about, and it has not been touched, and the fourth of those five is still nine words in that same hand, and nobody in that yard put a hand on any of those three this morning. The second table had the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and thirteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is eleven and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that neither of the two men who stand in that yard was standing in it at about eleven this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-ninth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud seven hundred and ninety-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that this is the last morning of the third week of the seventh month and that nobody in that yard has counted the weeks out loud in any of them.

"**The board carries five hundred and seventy-two, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and eighty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and two, and five hundred and sixty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

At about ten past eleven the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about 53 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and forty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have entered that neither of the two men who stand in this yard was standing in it at about eleven for a fourth morning this month, and about four of you have worked out that I have entered that four times and entered no reason four times, and about four of you have worked out that the count of conditions with no end on it is four and that a clerk entering something with no end on it is not a fifth condition, and about four of you have worked out that I have not said a word about it and about four of you have worked out that the reason I have not said a word about it is that there is no reason in that yard to say, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk's entry is not a condition with no end on it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood about four feet off the near end of that second table at about a quarter past eleven and said one thing out loud, in about 54 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted this yard at about ten this morning and I counted it again at about eleven and I got the same number both times, and about four of you have worked out that the two men who are not in that yard at about eleven are not the reason the number is the same, because about four of you have worked out that I counted it at about half past ten yesterday and the day before and it is the same then as well, and about four of you have worked out that I am not saying who is in that yard and I am not saying who is not, and that is the first time I have said a number in that yard that I could put a name to every one of, and that is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy who counts a yard twice and names nobody is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about a quarter to eight and was at the near end of that second table until about ten, and he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven with his own right hand open and his own left hand shut, and set his spade up against the leg of that second table at the far end of it and put his own two hands on the rail, and nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for.

At about eleven that yard held about nineteen people and not one of those two men. About nine people at that low wall have said since that a boy counted that yard at about ten and at about eleven this morning and got the same number both times and said that the two men who are not in it are not the reason, and that a clerk said out loud before him that she has entered that fact about those two men four times this month and entered no reason four times. About four of them have said that a boy has counted a yard and worked out what the count is not, and that a clerk has entered a thing four times and worked out that she has nothing to enter with it. About four of them have said that the two of them are the only people in that yard doing sums, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what to do with that.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a boy in that yard has counted that yard at about ten and at about eleven and got the same number and that a road keeper goes up that lane twice and has never once counted it. About four of them have said that is a thing about a boy and not about a road.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it and stayed there until about four, and he did not turn round once.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a boy said out loud this morning that a count of a yard is not a count of the people in it, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and twelve nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and thirteenth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and twelve of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and twenty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and seventy-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and eighty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and fifty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and fifty-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and ninety-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and thirty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and seventeen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a boy counting a yard twice is not a fourteenth thing, because a thing a person does is not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that counting a yard is a thing a boy does and not a thing a yard has.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and sixty-three. The number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for a hundred and seventy-two mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is still on that book and no hand has gone on it. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a boy counted a yard twice and got the same number. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and a boy who counts twice and names nobody is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man who said no is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a boy saying out loud in that yard that he could put a name to every one he counted and is not going to is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. Empty, the buckets went down that bank at about half past five and were brought back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up past those tables at about ten to six and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up said nothing at all. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninety-ninth morning, and that a boy counted that yard at about ten and at about eleven and got the same number both times, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two. About four of them have said that a boy and a man in that yard both count the same morning every day and have never once compared what they got.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the nineteenth of the seventh month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam were both at the bottom of that bank this morning and that she is entering no reason for either, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is eleven and did not move, and that the number of conditions with no end on it is four and a clerk's entry is not a fifth, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
