# Chapter 699: On The Thirteenth Of The Eighth Month The Man Who Mends Fencing Said One Thing Out Loud About A Wire In His Own Pocket

There was no rime on the boards of that first table on the thirteenth of the eighth month and the boards were dry and hard and there was nothing on them at all, and the boards had gone the same colour as the boards of that second table and there is no figure entered for that, and that was the two hundred and twenty-seventh morning after the count, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone on to the top of that lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard. The second table was at the foot of that bank with the stone lying face up on it, and the hollow in the middle of that stone is one inch deep and there is nothing at either end of it, and two pages were lying along the end of it, and that lot book was lying on it face up with five lines on it, and about nine people at that low wall have said since that they have looked at that hollow about four hundred mornings and that nobody has ever put a thumb in it and that nobody has ever said out loud in that yard what it is for.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at the near end of that first table at about a quarter to eight that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered four hundred and thirty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. Entered that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand since the sixth of the fifth month is fourteen and did not move, and entered that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirteen this morning and that it moved this morning from twelve, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-fourth of those mornings. About four of them have said that he has said the four of them out loud eight hundred and forty-eight times this month, and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him to say any of them a third time. About four of them have said that the two ends of that bank have a man at each of them every morning this month and that a man of fifty-six reads the four off a wall at the top of it and a man who mends fencing stands at the bottom of it, and that the two of them have not once been in the same place at the same hour.

"**The board carries five hundred and ninety-seven, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and thirteen days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and twenty-seven, and five hundred and eighty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about ten with nothing at all in his own two hands and said one thing out loud, in about 59 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That near leg is square and it has been square since a man put it square with his own hands, and I have stood at this end of that table every morning this month and I have not looked at it once, and about four of you have worked out that I have got a length of fencing wire in my own pocket that I have been carrying since the day before that table went down this bank, and about four of you have worked out that there is a gap in the fencing at my own yard about the length of my two hands and that I am not going to mend that table, and about four of you have worked out that a man who mends fencing is not a man who mends tables and that nobody has asked me the difference, and that is all I have got this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who mends fencing is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and entered that nobody in that yard thanked him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten past ten, in about 71 seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eighty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man who mends fencing said a thing out loud in that yard this morning and I entered it, and about four of you have worked out that he has been at the foot of that bank every morning this month and that nobody in this district has asked him one thing about any of it, and about four of you have worked out that the number of mornings that second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirteen and that I entered it at about a quarter to eight before he said anything out loud, and about four of you have worked out that a man saying a thing out loud in that yard is not in the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that I am not in that column either, and about four of you have worked out that a man is not thanked in this district for a thing he does to a table and that he was not thanked this morning, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirteen. She entered that a man who mends fencing said a thing out loud in that yard and that she entered that he said it and entered no thank you of her own. She entered that nobody asked the man who digs loam one thing about what he is to be used for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table at about eleven with his own spade against the leg of it and his own two hands on the rail. Nobody asked him one thing about what he is to be used for, and nobody has asked him that since he said no. About four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said a thing out loud this morning about a length of wire in his own pocket and that the man who digs loam was nine feet off him at the other end of that table and did not say one word.

About nine people at that low wall have said since that the man who mends fencing said one thing out loud in that yard this morning and that it is the first thing he has said in that yard in a month, and that he did not say the sentence about what his yard is for. About four of them have said that what he said was about a length of wire in his own pocket and a gap in a fence at his own yard. About four of them have said that nobody in that yard thanked him and that a clerk entered that he was not thanked. About four of them have said that a man of fifty-six read the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for, and that a man who mends fencing said a thing out loud at the bottom of that bank and was not asked what any of it is for either.

The road keeper came up that lane at about half past one, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at his usual hour and was not asked about it. About four of them have said that a man who mends fencing said a thing out loud in that yard this morning and that a road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and was not asked about any of it.

The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, set the shafts down at the top of it, and went back down that bank at about a quarter to four with his own hands back on the shafts.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said a thing out loud at the foot of that bank about four feet off him this morning, and that the man on those stones has not been given one thing and has not been asked one thing in three hundred and thirty-seven nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his three hundred and thirty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on three hundred and thirty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Three hundred and forty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Three hundred and seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Three hundred and seventy-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is four hundred and eighty days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and sixteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two hundred and fifty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are two hundred and forty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table at the foot of that bank, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man saying a thing out loud about a wire in his own pocket is not a fourteenth thing and a clerk saying a thing out loud about a man not being thanked is not a fifteenth, because a thing a person does is a thing a person does and not a thing a district has, and a clerk entered the reason out loud with the count, which is that a man who has not been thanked has not made a thing anybody in this district can come at.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at five hundred and eighty-eight. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the two pages on the end of that second table are at the foot of that bank and are not on any table in that yard, and three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door propped open, and that shop has been open on every morning of this year and nobody in this yard has asked the woman who keeps a scale one thing this month.

The fifth of those five lines is one condition with no end on it and the count of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move, and that fifth line is still one condition with no end on it and still working exactly as it was written, and it is on that book at the foot of that bank and no hand went on it this morning. The reading of that lot is the fifth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it was paid on the twenty-fifth of the sixth month by a person saying no in that yard, and it is not the sixth, and it is not paid a second time this morning because a man said a thing out loud about a wire. About two things were said out loud in that yard today and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it, and the man who mends fencing is not in it and a clerk is not in it and the man of fifty-six is not in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man saying a thing out loud about a length of wire in his own pocket is not a payment and a clerk entering that a man was not thanked is not a payment and neither of them paid it this morning, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fifth of the five and is not the sixth.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets. The buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full past that second table at the foot of it, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up that bank at about ten to six, went back down it, read three lines in that lot book standing up at the foot of that table and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about two things were said out loud in that yard this morning and that the woman who reads the first three lines of that book standing up went down that bank for the eleventh evening and did not look at the fourth one. About four of them have said that is not a failing.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the four hundred and twenty-fourth morning, and that the man who mends fencing said one thing out loud in that yard this morning, and that the man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and was not asked about either of the two and was not asked what one of them is for. About four of them have said that a man has read four figures off that wall four hundred and twenty-four mornings and that nobody in that yard has ever worked out what the measure of that man is, and that a man at the bottom of that bank said one thing out loud this month about half as often.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four in the afternoon in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirteenth of the eighth month that the fifth line of that lot book has stood on that book working exactly as it was written and has not been touched, and that the number of mornings the second table has stood at the foot of that bank is thirteen, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud in that yard and that she entered that he said it and entered no thank you of her own and entered no name, and that the number of people who have come down that lane with a page in their hand is fourteen and did not move between about a quarter to eight and about four this afternoon, and that the count of counted months is six and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.
