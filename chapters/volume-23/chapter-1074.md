# Chapter 1074: The Three Hundred And Fifty-Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And No Words For That Kind

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and that post has been standing there longer than the sheet has. It is eight hundred and thirteen days old on that sheet.

nine hundred and seventy-two marks have been cut into that board. That train on that siding has stood one thousand two hundred and eighty-eight days without a wagon coming off it. Nothing has been entered at that rail for one thousand and two days. nine hundred and sixty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid was open four hundred and fifty days, was not run, and is not a lot and not open this morning. This yard is six hundred and seventy-seven days behind the figure on the second line of that book, and that rule said out loud in that yard has stood six hundred and eighty-two days. seven hundred and fifty-two days have gone since the first day of the eighth month. That body four hundred miles off is six hundred and ninety-one days past a printing it did not make.

That ninth of the nine printed nights went back eight hundred and fifty-five days. Along the edge of that second table there are six hundred and seventeen marks in chalk, and six hundred and thirty-one marks have been cut off that board.

A clerk in this district has counted seven months and there is nothing here for it to sit on. Those four documents have never come into this district. Four places here will let those three lines be read and a fifth will not. There are four conditions and no end is set on any of them. Fourteen is how many things have been made in this yard. Five things were never got here, and the fifth of those five is unpaid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and the page at the back of that lot book was not a place one could go on until the two hundred and eighth morning, so nothing written on that page before that morning was a line for a reason, and it is counted nowhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

One figure was said out loud at that yard this morning at a scale and it did not come down in anybody's mouth after it, and the page at the back of that lot book is at sixty-three lines and took no line on it. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and eighty-eight mornings and the near rail has stood on the near side of it three hundred and thirty-eight mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and fifty-eight mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and fifty-seventh morning. It is neither the second table nor the near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and thirteenth night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and twelve of the nights of it. A man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall this morning for the seven hundred and ninety-ninth of those mornings and got all four of them. That is the two hundred and fourteenth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. That flat answer was given to that stone twice this morning and it was the two hundred and forty-seventh time running.

That lot book has lain open along those boards since before the light and nothing at all is entered in it. Five lines are ruled across one of its pages and the column on the page beside that has one name in it under a heading which still says whoever. There is a second space on that form and it is empty and nobody in this district has ever asked about it. Thirty pages are behind that lane, each of them in its own carrier's hands, and it brought nothing down this morning. That record has stood two hundred and seventy-three days. It has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about nine people on it.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones for the three hundred and third afternoon running and gave that afternoon its one word. Neither of these has ever been given to anybody in this district and nobody here has ever gone up to look at either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll of ninety-eight has been paid once and there is no paying it twice. Three lines of the first thirty-three on that back page went through this district and nobody here heard them read.


---



She has two forms of words and has had them since she was twenty-six.

One of them is for a morning when nothing has gone out loud in that yard that goes at the back of that book. It is the common of the two. It is ready before the light and it takes about four seconds to say and she has said it on every working morning of fifteen years.

The other one is for a morning when something has. She has used it four times in fifteen years and she could say every word of it.

What she has never had is words for a morning when something has gone out loud and nothing is written.

She did not know until about two this afternoon that such a morning existed.



---



At ten to eight the boy said his count at those boards before her hand had gone near anything.

"Thirty," said Orren Pike. "And there is nothing behind it, and you have not asked me what that means in a week and a half and I have noticed that you have not."

"You have noticed a great many things this week," said the clerk. "I have not asked you because you would tell me and then you would have said it to somebody, and I have not got anybody in this yard that I want you to have said it to."

She shut the book without writing anything in it and he went off up the slope.



---



At about half past twelve the woman who keeps a scale was at her own pan about four feet off the near end of the second table with a bar on it, and she let it want the wind until it stopped wanting it.

The clerk was at the rail about nine feet off with her hands in her sleeves and she watched the woman do what she always does, and she did not ask her anything, and nobody in that yard ever has.



---



At about two the woman said a figure of her own out loud, once, at her own scale, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing.

It went out across that ground and up the face of that bank.

Nothing came back to her out of that yard. Nobody thanked her. Nobody asked her what she weighs things for. Nobody asked her where that figure had come from, and nobody asked her for the figure she had said.

About nineteen people heard it and every one of them was doing something else at the time, and that is the whole of what about nineteen people is.



---



The clerk stood at the rail with her hands in her sleeves and looked down at the open book.

The page at the back of that lot book had a worn place in it about four inches in from the foot, where the paper has gone thin in a band about the width of two fingers. She has never once corrected for it in fifteen years and she did not then. She was not going to write on it this afternoon, and she was not going to write on it this morning either.

She had written a line across it once, on one morning a long way back, on the only other morning she has ever had the second form of words for.

She did not have a third form of words, and she had never met one, and it was about two o'clock in the afternoon of an ordinary day and she was standing nine feet away from a rail with a pen in a holder in her coat.



---



At about two she went along to the near end of that second table and stood about four feet off the woman and did not say anything, and the woman did not say anything, and the man at the end of that second table put his cloth down on his own boards and said a thing about the weather to nobody and neither of the two women took him up.

"You have not asked me what she said," said the man at the end of that second table.

"No."

"You have not asked me what she weighs things for."

"I would not ask you that either," said the clerk.



---



At about four his hand went up on that wall and took all four off it in the order they come, and the palm came down flat on the stone under the lowest one and lay there.

"Seven hundred and ninety-nine mornings, all four of you."

Then the flat answer, and it has never once been the same sentence twice.

"Two hundred and forty-seven mornings," he said out loud to that stone, "and I have had you twice today and got you both times, and a woman said one figure out loud at that yard this afternoon and about nineteen of us heard it and not one of us did anything, and I have been at this wall since before the light and I am not going to stand here this evening and pretend I do not know that."

About nineteen people heard it go out of him, and it was the two hundred and forty-seventh time he has said it there, and there is nobody in this district who has ever asked him a thing about it.



---



At ten past four she read that morning out loud with the pen still in the holder.

"The three hundred and fifty-eightth morning," she said. "One figure was said out loud at a scale in this yard at about two and it did not come down in anybody's mouth after it, and nothing goes on at the back of this book for it, and it goes down in my own hand with nothing in it beside it, and the saying of it came before the writing of it."

That was the first of her two forms of words and it was the wrong one and she said all of it.

Then she wrote the three hundred and fifty-eighth morning under the last line herself, in her own small careful hand, and took no line for it.

About four of the nineteen said the goodnight at about half past four, and the boards came off the cart face down and went up the bank.



---

That page stood at sixty-three lines at the end of that morning and no line went onto it. That lane brought nothing down this morning and it stands at thirty pages. Three of the first thirty-third lines on that back page are not read by anybody here and never have been. That last of the five is five and it is unpaid. Seven months has been entered as a count and it sits on nothing whatever here. Four hundred and eleven is on that sheet at that gatepost and it is on it in the morning.
