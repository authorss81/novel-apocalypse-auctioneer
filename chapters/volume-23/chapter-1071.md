# Chapter 1071: The Three Hundred And Fifty-Fifth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And One Word On The Stones

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and it has not been off that sheet in all that time. It is eight hundred and ten days old.

That board carries nine hundred and sixty-nine marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand two hundred and eighty-five days without a wagon coming off it. At that rail nothing has been entered for nine hundred and ninety-nine days. nine hundred and sixty days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid stood open four hundred and fifty days and was not run, so it is neither a lot nor open this morning. six hundred and seventy-four is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. six hundred and seventy-nine is how long that rule said out loud in that yard has stood. It has been seven hundred and forty-nine days since the first day of the eighth month. That body four hundred miles off is six hundred and eighty-eight days past a printing it did not make.

That ninth of the nine printed nights went back eight hundred and fifty-two days. six hundred and twenty-eight marks have been cut off that board. Along the edge of that second table there are six hundred and fourteen marks in chalk.

A clerk in this district has counted seven months and there is nothing here for that count to sit on. Four documents are wanted here and not one of the four is here. Those three lines can be read at four places in this district and at no fifth of them. There are four conditions in this district and an end has never been put on any one of them. Fourteen things have been made in this basin and there is nothing else in it. Five of them were never got and the fifth of those five is unpaid.

On the one hundred and eighty-second morning a reason was said out loud in that yard, and the page at the back of that lot book had nowhere to put one before the two hundred and eighth morning, so nothing written on that page before that morning was a line for a reason, and it is counted nowhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

Nothing that goes at the back of that book went out loud in that yard this morning, and the page behind that lot book stands at sixty-three lines and took no line. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and eighty-five mornings and the near rail has stood on the near side of it three hundred and thirty-five mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. A man has stood at the foot of that bank three hundred and fifty-five mornings on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood at that end of that ground for the three hundred and fifty-fourth morning, and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on the seven hundred and tenth night of that run, having slept on seven hundred and nine of the nights of it. A man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall this morning for the seven hundred and ninety-sixth of those mornings and got all four of them. That is the two hundred and eleventh morning running with nobody having asked him anything. That flat answer was given to that stone twice this morning and this morning it was the two hundred and forty-fourth time running.

It has lain open along those boards since before the light with nothing entered in it. Five lines are ruled across one of its pages and the column on the next of them carries one name, under a heading that still says whoever. Nobody has ever been asked about the second space on that form and it is empty. Thirty pages are behind that lane in their own carriers' hands and none of them came down it this morning. That record has stood two hundred and seventy days. Public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about nine people standing on it.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones for the three hundredth afternoon running and one word was the whole of it. This district has never given either of these to anybody, and nobody here has ever gone up to see either of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight and is paid and spent. Three lines among the first thirty-three on that back page went through this district and nobody in it heard one of them read.


---



He came up that bank at about eight and there is nowhere at the top of it to sit down, so he stood at the end of it with his hands behind him and looked at eleven feet of boards with the light lying flat along them.

He had been coming up that bank in the morning for seventeen years and he had come up it in the afternoon three hundred times, and the two were not the same walk and he had never told anybody that.



---



At ten to eight the boy was at those boards and he had the pencil out before he opened his mouth, which he has done every morning since the light was still on the wood.

"Thirty," said Orren Pike. "And there is nothing behind it this morning, and I have said nothing behind it every morning this week, and I am going to keep saying it until somebody in this yard tells me there is a reason to stop."

"You will get a long wait," said the man of about thirty-two, going along his own boards with the cloth over his arm.

"Then I will keep saying it."

The boy put the pencil back in his coat and did the spaces along that stack, and got nine, and did them again from the far end, and got nine.



---



About four people were at the top of that bank and they were not talking to each other, which is what about four people at the top of that bank are for on about four mornings in ten. He was not one of them.

Below them the turner stood at that frame with one hand on the casting, and the wheel was at its speed.

"It is at that speed," said one of them.

"It is," said another.

"Since before the light was over the top."

Nobody said the next of it. He stood at the end of that bank and let it not get said. He had come up there for a morning with nothing to do in it, and this was a morning with a great deal to do in it, and he had not expected to be given a third thing.



---



The man of about thirty-two had been along his own boards before eight and had said one thing out loud to nobody in particular, and it was that the space under his near corner had taken two more strips since the day before and had not gone down.

"That is what packing is," said the man of about thirty-four, who was on his own boards at the far end with a cloth and a foot and about four inches of sound pallet lying flat on the boards where he could see it.

"You have not seen it packed."

"I have seen it packed and I have seen it emptied and I am telling you what a space does," said the man of about thirty-four. "I am not asking you anything and you are not telling me anything. It is a good morning for both of us."



---



At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his number up into about four hundred yards of air in the voice he keeps for it, and about four voices took it up after him out of step with one another, one of them half a beat behind the other three, and it went off the top of that bank.

The man of sixty-one was four hundred yards off from the foot of that bank when the light went, and he came down to those stones at about half past three and sat down on them.

Seventeen years and three hundred afternoons, and he had never once come down to them at any other hour.



---



The stones are flat and about the length of a cart board and they lie in a dip with the grass grown close on three sides, and on a wet morning they are the wettest thing within four hundred yards. This morning they were dry.

He had noticed that on about the fourth afternoon and had not said so, and he sat down on them at about half past three with his back to the low wall and his face to the yard, and the light was still on the yard and would be for a while.

The word is not a hard thing to say. It is one word and it has been in his mouth since before he held what he holds, and the whole difficulty of seventeen years has been the hour and not the word.

The hour is when the light leaves them.



---



At about a quarter to four the light came off the yard and went up the face of that bank and reached the dip, and it lay along the stones for about the length of a hand and then it stopped and lay there.

He waited.

At about ten past four it was still there. A stone that has been wet and has dried holds the light a while after the rest of that dip has gone, and he had known that for three hundred afternoons and had never had it happen at that hour.

The man of about sixty-four was four feet off him at the foot of that low wall, doing what he does there, and he did not turn round and he did not say anything, and nobody asked either of them anything, which is how those two have gone at each other for longer than he has been counting.

At about twenty past four the light went off the stones.



---



He put his head forward a little and said it. One word, in the ordinary voice, at the hour it had gone for three hundred afternoons, and the grass in that dip took it and did not give it back, and that was the whole of the three hundredth afternoon.

Then he sat there with his mouth shut and his hands on his knees.



---



The boy came down that slope at about ten past five, which was forty minutes after the light had gone off the stones, and he had the boards behind him and the goodnight going up the bank ahead of him.

"You were up there this morning."

"I was."

"I said thirty at the boards and you were up there and I did not see you say anything."

"I did not say anything."

The boy stood about six feet off and looked at the stones and then at him.

"What is it you say on these?"

"That is the second time you have asked me that," said the man of sixty-one, "and you are the second person who has asked me that, and I am not going to tell you what it is, and I am not going to tell you it is nothing either. Put down whatever you like about that and get on with your boards."

The boy did not say anything for a while.

"I have said out loud this week that I will not do that part again," he said, "and I have not said out loud what I am going to do instead of it."

"Then you have not said out loud what you are going to do instead of it."

The boy went up the slope after the boards.



---



About four people at the top of that bank have said since that they have not said what that word is. Nobody in this district has ever asked any of the four of them for it, and the man who says it on those stones is not one of the four, and not one of the four has ever asked him for it.



---



That page was still at sixty-three lines when the covers came over it, and it did not take a line and did not carry a sixty-fourth. Nothing came down that lane and it stands where it has stood, at thirty pages. Of the first thirty-three lines on that back page, three have gone through this district and nobody here has ever heard one of them read. That fifth of the five is five and unpaid. A clerk in this district has counted seven months and there is nothing here for it. Four hundred and eleven is on that sheet and will be on it in the morning.
