# Chapter 1099: The Three Hundred And Eighty-Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month, And What She Said At Four

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and no part of this basin has used it for anything. That figure is eight hundred and thirty-eight days old.

That board carries nine hundred and ninety-seven marks. That train on that siding has stood one thousand three hundred and thirteen days and no wagon has come off it in that time. Nobody has entered anything at that rail for one thousand and twenty-seven days. Nine hundred and eighty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

That bid was open four hundred and fifty days and it was not run, and it is not a lot and not open this morning. Seven hundred and two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that book this yard stands. Seven hundred and seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Seven hundred and seventy-seven days since the first day of the eighth month. That body four hundred miles off is seven hundred and sixteen days past a printing it did not make.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is eight hundred and eighty days back. Six hundred and fifty-six marks have been cut off that board. Six hundred and forty-two marks in chalk stand along the edge of that second table.

Seven months has been counted in this district and there is nothing here that carries it. Four documents have never once been inside this district. Three lines are readable in four places in this district and there is no fifth place with any of them on it. Four conditions stand in this district and none of them has an end set on it. This basin has made fourteen things and nothing else at all. Five of those fourteen things were never got in this basin and the fifth of that five is unpaid.

A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning, and that page at the back of that lot book could not take a line for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning came, so nothing written on it before that morning was a line for a reason, it is counted nowhere, and no figure has ever been said out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason has been said out loud in it.

Nothing went out loud in that yard this morning for the back of that page and there is still nothing at the back of that page. The first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

That second table has stood at the foot of that bank four hundred and thirteenth mornings and the near rail has stood on the near side of it three hundred and sixty-third mornings, and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. Three hundred and eighty-third mornings is how long a man has stood at that foot of that bank on the word of the man who keeps it. The table of the man of about thirty-two stood there for the three hundred and eighty-second morning and it is not that second table and it is not that near rail. On the seven hundred and thirty-eighth night of that run the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall, having slept on seven hundred and thirty-seven of the nights of it. A man of fifty-six read four figures off that wall this morning for the eight hundred and twenty-fourth of those mornings and got all four of them. That is the two hundred and thirty-ninth morning running with nobody having asked him anything. That was the two hundred and seventy-second time running for the flat answer he gives to that stone.

Nothing has ever been written at the back of that lot book and it lies open along those boards this morning as it does every morning. One page of that book has five lines ruled across it, and the column on the page after that holds a single name under a heading which still says whoever. That form has a second space in it and it stands empty and nobody in this district has ever asked anybody about it. There are thirty pages behind that lane in their own carriers' hands and it brought nothing down it today. That record has stood two hundred and ninety-eight days. About nine people were on that ground at ten to eight and that ground has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

A man of sixty-one sat on those stones for the three hundred and twenty-eighth afternoon running and one word was the whole of that afternoon. It has never been given to anybody in this district, and neither one of them: about eleven feet between that wall and the top of that bank, and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert.

That toll came to ninety-eight, it has been paid, and it is spent. Three of the first thirty-three lines on that back page came through this district and none of the three was ever read out in it.

---

She came in at half past seven because that is her hour, with the book under her arm and the pen in the holder, and she was four hundred mornings and more into the doing of that.

What she wanted off that morning was to reach the end of the reading.

---

That is a thing that has been wanted before and it has not always been got.

She reads the morning out loud before she writes anything on it, every morning she writes at all, and she has two forms of words for it and has had both of them since she was twenty-six. The first of them is for a morning on which nothing at all went out loud. The second is for a morning when something did, and she has used it four times in fifteen years and the place in the middle of it for the thing itself is the same size every time.

What she cannot do, on most mornings, is get to the end of one without stopping somewhere and starting again further along.

---

She read that morning out loud at about four with the pen still in the holder.

"The three hundred and eighty-third morning," she said. "Nothing went out loud in that yard today for the back of this book, and there is nothing at the back of this book for it. A man stood at his own table from half past nine until about four with a cloth in his hand and did not say one word out loud to anybody in that yard. A woman put a bar on her own pan at half past twelve, four feet off the near end of that second table. A man of fifty-six had his hand up on that wall at a quarter to eight and got four figures off it, and had it up again at about four and got the same four off it. A boy was at those boards at ten to eight and said a number and nothing behind it. A load that is two days in one went up that bank off one cart at half past four while the light was still on that ground. The turner stood at the near end of that first table all day."

She got to the end of that, and there was nothing after it for her to go over.

---

Then she stood there and found that the whole of that morning had come out of her in about four minutes, and that about four people on those eleven feet of boards had stood still through all of it with a foot on a board.

She had the pen in her hand while she read it.

She noticed that about halfway through, and then she finished it, and then she thought about it.

---

Fifteen years of that reading and she had never once tried it with her hands empty.

She put the pen in her pocket and read that morning over again, out loud, from the top of it, with nothing in her hand at all.

It took longer. Not much longer, but it went all the way through without one place in it that she had to go back to, and the reason was that she had been holding that pen the way she holds a pen when she is about to write, which is a different grip from any other grip there is.

---

She stood at that rail for about a minute after that with her hands empty and did not do anything at all, which is a thing she has not done on that ground in fifteen years, and then she took the pen back out of her pocket and wrote the three hundred and eighty-third morning under the last line in her own small careful hand.

There is a band in that paper four inches in from the foot of the back page where the page has gone thin, and she has known about it for years and has never once made any allowance for it and she was not going to start now.

Three hundred and eighty-third.

The gap came out in the middle of it where it always comes out, and the ink spread either side of it into the thin of the paper and put the two halves of the figure further apart than a figure ought to be.

She looked at it for a while and then she put the pen back in the holder and did not do anything about it.

---

Nobody in that yard has ever asked her why she reads it out loud, and nobody has ever asked her to say it again, and nobody has ever asked her what a line on that page is for.

About half past four the light left those boards and four of that nineteen said the goodnight and the boards came down off the cart face down and went up the slope.

The boy was at the near end of that stack with the light still on it and said what he says at that hour, and then the boards came down off the cart face down and went up the slope on about nine pairs of shoulders.

She did not go up with the last of them. She stood at that rail with the book shut under her arm and the pen in her pocket and said the last part of that reading out loud again under her breath, by herself, and then she picked the book up and went.

She went up that slope behind the last of them with the book shut under her arm and the pen in her pocket, and at the top of that bank about four people were standing about and none of them said anything to her about any part of it.

---

That page is at sixty-five lines, which is where it was before the light came, and she took no line on it. There was nothing down that lane today and it is at thirty pages behind it. Those three have gone through this district unread in it. The fifth of that five is still five and unpaid. That count of seven months is on nothing at all. That gatepost sheet carries four hundred and eleven and it will carry it in the morning as well.

She read that morning out loud with her hands empty and it went all the way through, and then she wrote the morning under the last line with the gap in the middle of it that is always in the middle of it.
