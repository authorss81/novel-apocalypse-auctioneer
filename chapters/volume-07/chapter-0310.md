# Chapter 310: The Day Before the Last Day of the Month

---

The thirtieth of the seventh month was the hottest day of that month and the boards at the near end of the lane had lifted at four joints by ten, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him, and a clerk of nineteen years was in that yard and said afterwards that this was the tenth morning he had said three figures out loud in it and that he had not noticed the tenth until the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said so.

"**Two hundred and eighteen days on the board and five hundred and thirty-four days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and forty-eight, and two hundred and nine days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is two days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and one days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and twenty-eight days, and the table against that wall stood up for the twenty-fourth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood ninety-eight days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two is one day to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a stay is counted without the day a man arrived on and that the man of about sixty-four came into that gate on the twenty-first of this month and has been in this district nine days this morning, and that nine days is the same nine he gave about his own walking on the twenty-first and that the two are two figures about the same man and are not added together and do not make eighteen.

She entered that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning on the thirtieth of the seventh month, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that no number on the sheet at that gatepost moved on any of the ten days of this block and the figure on it is four hundred and eleven, and that two is a figure about a ladder and is not a figure about anybody and is not a finding.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past ten to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**There is a figure on the sheet at that gatepost and it is four hundred and eleven and it is four hundred and eleven this morning and it was four hundred and eleven on the twenty-first of this month and it is going to be four hundred and eleven tomorrow. There is a table standing against a wall about four feet high eleven miles off this district and that is the twenty-fourth morning it has stood up and nobody is going to put it up, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam put it down there four times this month and cannot put it up and has not told the man it belongs to. And there is a book four hundred yards from this gate in a room with a shut door on it and one person in this district who may write in it and nobody has been asked to write in it and the record about the not asking says not asked. Three things. I have said all three out loud and none of them moved today and I have not been asked to do anything about any of them and I am not going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure somebody says out loud in a yard is a figure, and that a man who has said three things out loud and has not been asked to do anything about any of them has not refused and has not consented and cannot be counted either way, and that a table, a figure and a book are three things and are not added together, and that a man who mends fencing is not a keeper of any of the three and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything.

The man of fifty-six said one thing back to that at about eleven and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that about nine people heard it.

"**I have said every week for five months that a door is not a keeper, and on the twenty-fourth of this month a clerk wrote three things down in one hand and the third of them was a shelter and a way to take the record off, and the third of them is not true this morning and nobody has made it true, and I am not going to stand in this yard on the last morning but one of this month and tell anybody what I think about that. Two days. That is all I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to give an opinion has not withdrawn the opinions he has given and cannot be counted either way, and that two is a figure about a ladder and a man who says a figure out loud is not saying anything about the day it belongs to.

---

About nine people were still in that yard at four and the shade off the east wall had come across to the trestle table and the boards at the near end of the lane had gone down again by themselves, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was at the end of that table with his leg out in front of him under it and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he had been at that end of that table for about six hours on his own two feet and that six hours is a figure about six hours and is not a figure about a man and is not a finding.

At about four the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the end of that trestle table with his hand-cart against the wall inside the gate and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the tent had been rolled since the thirtieth of the sixth month and had not gone up on the twentieth or the twenty-first of this month or on any other day of it, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a tent is not a lean-to and a lean-to is not a shelter and the three are not added together.

At about half past four the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing read the five lines of print out loud in about nine feet of lane outside the office to about four people, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat stood at the foot of her own gate at the third house up that lane from the scale and listened to the whole of it from about where she was, and nobody had asked either of them to do anything.

Then the man of about sixty-four, who had been at the foot of that low wall since about ten and had gone down the lane to the bank at about half past two, came back up into that yard at about five and stood at the end of that trestle table about four feet off it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man came up a lane and stood in a yard and that is not a market and is not a place anybody is made to stand in, and that he is not a bearer of a message in this district and is not a holder of a room four hundred yards away and is not a party of anything, and that nobody asked him anything at all this afternoon and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up the lane at about six with his right arm hanging and stopped at the end of that trestle table and said one thing out loud in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fifty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That arm has not gone above the shoulder on any of the thirty days of this month and I am not going to keep saying it. The twenty-fifth morning was the first morning in a month that table was not carried out and nobody put it up, and I was not out there for it and nobody asked me to be, and I have not told the man it belongs to and I am not going to today either, and I would like both of those entered as two things about me and not as one thing about him. I have got an arm that has not gone above my shoulder since the first of this month and I have got four hundred yards of bank between me and that man and I have not walked them. That is the whole of it and I am not going to make it into a reason for anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who digs loam is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to tell somebody a thing has not told him and cannot be counted either way, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and the eight did not move again at about six in the evening on the thirtieth of the seventh month, and that an arm and four hundred yards of bank are two things about a man and are not two figures about anybody.

She entered what had not moved on the thirtieth of the seventh month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and twenty-first days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood ninety-eight days and nobody has sat in it on any of them, and a man sat down on the end of that table on the sixteenth of this month and not in the chair, and those are two different things. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that out of a ladder and did not ask him, and he is a man of about forty-eight and not thirty-four and has said of himself that he is fifty-one. The rival record has been in force fifty-nine days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered. Three documents this district does not own, and no document came into that yard in the ten days of this block, and the five lines of print on the end of that table are not a fourth of them and a copy is not a fourth and a name that was not said is not a fourth. Five protected things and no sixth, and a name is not a sixth and a courier is not a sixth and a figure of mornings is not a sixth. The instruments this district has built and not named are six, and nothing in the ten days of this block became a seventh of them. Four conditions with no end on it and no fifth proposed, thirteen refusals to join two things and the registrar's own count of twelve, nine refusals to read with the departure unspent and no date in any ledger for the next reading, five boards and six lines full with a seventh ruled and empty at the ninth place, four columns of not-askings and no fifth ruled. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move on any of the ten days, and the three are three figures and are not added together. No line has been put on a board and no count has been struck and no name has been written on anything in this district in the ten days of this block, and the record about each of those says not asked. She entered the number of days without entering what is going to happen in them.

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was thirty days old and the first day of the eighth month was two days off, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the table eleven miles off was still standing against a wall about four feet high with nobody going to put it up in the morning, and the book four hundred yards away still had a shut door on the room it is in and nobody had been asked to write in it.

**AND ON THE THIRTIETH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH, WHICH WAS THE DAY BEFORE THE LAST DAY OF THAT MONTH, THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN, A TABLE ELEVEN MILES OFF WAS STILL STANDING AGAINST A WALL ABOUT FOUR FEET HIGH WITH NOBODY GOING TO PUT IT UP, AND A BOOK FOUR HUNDRED YARDS AWAY STILL HAD A SHUT DOOR ON THE ROOM IT IS IN AND NOBODY HAD BEEN ASKED TO WRITE IN IT, AND A CLERK ENTERED THE NUMBER OF DAYS WITHOUT ENTERING WHAT WAS GOING TO HAPPEN IN THEM.**
