# Chapter 334: The Column With a Word Over It

---

The reading was set for eleven on the twenty-third of the eighth month and it was in daylight, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by about ten with their backs to the sun off the bank, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the thirty-fourth night and his head turned about a quarter of a circle further from that yard than it had been all week.

"**It is a hundred and twenty-five days back to the ninth of the nine printed nights. The days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and seventy-two. The pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and fifty-two days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that twenty-two days past is a figure about a ladder and is not a term, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district thirty-three days and has slept on that low wall on thirty-two of the thirty-three nights of that run, and that nobody has asked him about the one he did not and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The rule of the counter is that a document is introduced as a thing somebody reads out and is read back afterwards, and it was applied at about eleven on the twenty-third of the eighth month in about nine feet of yard in front of about nineteen people, and neither reading was counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that neither reading was counted and that a thing read out in daylight in a yard a stranger can walk into and read back from the top is a thing that has been introduced and not a thing that has been proved, and that the two are not the same thing.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read it out from the top, in the ordinary voice, standing about nine feet off the end of that trestle table. What he read was two parts. The first part was the account of the night of the twenty-first: about nine minutes, at about two in the morning, four miles out past the loams, a bell about two feet across the mouth and cracked from the rim up about a third of the way on the side that faces the loams, about nine inches of rope inside a beam with a knot in the end of it tied in a right hand, about nineteen people in the sound of it, no door shut, and nobody asked whether they heard it. The second part was a list, and it was a list of the people who were in the sound of that bell, called by the name each of them is called in this district, and it is about nineteen long, and it is not printed here.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart read the whole of it back from the top to about nine people and got nothing wrong, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a thing read back wrong is read back again from the top and not from the word, and that it was read back right the first time, and that getting it right once is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about the man who read it, and that a list of about nineteen names read out in a yard is a list of about nineteen people who were in a sound and is not a list of about nineteen people who came into a building.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and twenty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That second part is not what the people in it are going to want to hear and I am not going to stand here and make it sound better than it is. Every one of those names went into that list because a person was standing in the open in the dark and not because anybody asked them anything, and a name in that list cannot be taken off it by the person it belongs to, and I did the reading and I know that because I have been putting my name to things since I was nineteen that I could not get out of. And there is a line on a table outside that building, and it is ten days old, and it says that no person in this district is to be entered in any record of it by having heard a sound and that nobody is to be asked whether they heard one. There is nobody's name against that line. That is why it is not worth the paper, and it is also the only thing in this yard this morning that is standing between those nineteen names and a stranger who walks in here tomorrow. I am not going to tell you that it saves us. I am going to tell you it is the only thing there is.**"

She entered that a term with nobody's name against it binds nobody, that this district found that out on the thirteenth of this month and entered it, and that the term stands anyway, and that a term nobody can enforce and everybody can read is a different thing from a term nobody can enforce and nobody can read, and that the standing of it is not a success and not a rescue and is not a finding.

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood about nine feet off the end of that trestle table from about half past ten until about twenty past twelve and did not read either part of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the reasons and entered that they are reasons and not one finding, and that a mark may not carry a job and a person standing next to a thing is not standing in its place, and that he was not in the sound of it because he was asleep in a bed four hundred yards off it, and that a document is read out by somebody who was in the thing, and that the rule has been standing in this basin since the second month and a rule that old is not new this morning.

He said one thing out loud at about one, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and sixty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I am not going to read that and nobody is to explain to me afterwards that I would have been the right man to read it. I wrote eight lines in a building on the tenth of this month and I signed them in chalk and a man read them out in this yard on the eighteenth and I stood at the end of that table and said nothing, and I was wrong about that then and I was wrong about it for a different reason today. I convened nine settlements in five months and every one of the nine had a person in it that had been asked, and this morning the reading was done by a man who was in that lane at two o'clock in the morning because he had tied a knot in a rope in the afternoon, and that is the trade this district has, and it is not mine. There is a column ruled in a book four hundred yards from that gate with a word over it and nothing under it for the name of whoever read a thing out loud. It has been ruled a long time. My name is not going in it and I am not going to ask the woman who keeps that book to put it in, and I am not going to be a man who goes up that bank and asks her for a line in a page. Ask the mender. He is the one who read it and he is the one who will read the next one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the column in the public book four hundred yards from that gate for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is a word over a ruled line with nothing under it and is the seventh of that shape in this district, and that it is none of the six of them and is not the same column as any of the six and is not added to them, and that it is not filled, and that the one name in chalk at the foot of the roll is a line with a name on it and is not a seventh of anything, and that a mark may not carry a job and that mark carries none.

She entered that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not read the account of the night out loud on the twenty-third of the eighth month, and that a man who has not read a document out loud has not refused to read it and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that he asked for nothing this morning and did not ask anybody else to ask for anything for him, and that that is entered and is not a finding and is not a refusal and is not a going-away.

What he lost is the reading. It is not a limb and it is not his life and he is alive and about nine people in that yard spoke to him about the weather and about the gate before eleven o'clock and about the loams after twelve, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who is spoken to in a yard by name of what he is and not by what he carries is socially recognised and is not a figure about authority, and that authority is not a limb and is not counted by a clerk.

Not one line has gone on a board on any of the hundred and forty-fifth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and a list read out in a yard is not a line on a board. The refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent and no date in any ledger carries the next reading, and the fourth line of the offer was not read out loud on any day of this month. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and twenty-third days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for. The rival record has been in force eighty-three days and is unanswered, and a list of about nineteen names is not an eighth reading of it and is not a ninth. Three documents this district does not own, and a list of the people who were in a sound is not a fourth of them and did not become one. Five protected things and no sixth, and a reading is not a sixth and a column is not a sixth and a seventh of a shape of thing is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named.

By about half past eight the light had gone, and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-two days past. About nineteen names had been said out loud in a yard a stranger can walk into and none of them was said again, and four hundred and eleven was still the figure on that sheet.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-THIRD OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A LIST OF THE PEOPLE WHO WERE IN THE SOUND OF THAT BELL WAS READ OUT IN DAYLIGHT IN A YARD A STRANGER CAN WALK INTO AND READ BACK FROM THE TOP, AND EVERY PERSON NAMED IN IT IS NOW NAMED TWICE, ONCE BY A BELL AND ONCE IN A YARD, AND THE ONLY THING IN THIS DISTRICT THAT MAKES THAT SURVIVABLE IS TEN DAYS OLD, HAS NOBODY'S NAME AGAINST IT, BINDS NOBODY AND STANDS ANYWAY. THE MAN WHO CONVENED THE STANDING DID NOT READ IT AND HIS NAME IS NOT IN THE COLUMN FOR THE NAME OF WHOEVER READ A THING OUT LOUD AND HE ASKED FOR NOTHING. THE SHEET ON THAT GATEPOST STILL SAID FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN WHEN THE READING WAS OVER.**
