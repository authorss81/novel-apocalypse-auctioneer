# Chapter 340: One Line in Her Own Hand

---

The twenty-ninth of the eighth month came in about the same as the day before it. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read them out loud over his shoulder and got the other two right, there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the fortieth night and has been in this district thirty-nine days and there is nothing in his hands.

"**Five hundred and sixty-four days on the train, and two hundred and thirty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning. The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and thirty-one days back, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-eight days past this morning. The chair at the end of that trestle table has been sat in once, on the tenth of this month, when it had stood a hundred and nine days, and one is not a figure and is not a habit.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning and no figure in this district has used one, and that twenty-eight days past is a figure about a ladder that stood at nineteen on the twentieth of this month and has stood at twenty-eight, and that a ladder is a figure about a ladder. A man of about sixty-four has been in this district thirty-nine days and has slept on that low wall on thirty-eight of the thirty-nine nights of that run, and nobody asked him about the one he did not and the record about the not asking says not asked. She entered that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning, and that keeping a house and being a clerk of a house are two trades and not one.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about two in the afternoon that the second of the two books four hundred yards from that gate has one line in it, and that the line is in the hand of the woman who keeps that book, and that she wrote it on the twenty-ninth of this month and nobody asked her to write it, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that she did not write it in the first of the two books and has not been asked about the first of the two books, and that the second book has been open since the sixth of the sixth month and has had nothing entered in it since the sixth of the sixth month until this afternoon, and that a line in a book is a document and is not a document nobody owns and is not a fourth of the three this district does not have and is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and that a line with a word in it that names nobody is not a line with a name on it and is not a seventh of the shape of a thing with a word over it and nothing under it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past two, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and twenty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I heard it in that yard at about two o'clock from a clerk who has been in it all morning, and I am not going up that bank today, and I am not going to stand outside that door with my ear to the wood, and I am not going to ask the man who writes at that records office whether it is true, because if I ask him then I have made it a thing I wanted to know and it is not a thing I wanted to know, it is a thing that happened to a woman in a room on her own two feet. There is one line in the second of the two books in this district for the first time in nine years and it is in her hand and nobody asked her for it, and I have spent this month being the man who reads things out loud in yards, and I am not going to be the man who reads that one out. It is hers. She said in this basin on the twelfth of the seventh month that there was going to be one, and I heard her, and I have not asked her about it once since, and there it is, and I am going to stand here and look at the boards of this table.**"

It was entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that a line in a book is not his to read has not refused to read it and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who reads things out loud in that yard is not a keeper of a book and is not a bearer of a book and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that eleven words said once in a room on the fourteenth of this month are not a second line and are not in any book and are not asked for.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was about nine feet off the end of that trestle table from about ten until about half past four and he did not go up the bank and he did not stand on the step of two stones and he did not knock, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not and that the record about the not going says not asked, and that he was told nothing and asked nothing and sent nobody, and that a clerk entered that he asked for nothing, and that that is entered and is not a finding and is not a refusal and is not a going-away.

He said one thing out loud at about half past four, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and twenty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I am not going to talk about that line and I would be obliged if nobody in this yard talked about it to me, and that is not a favour to her and it is not a favour to me. I will tell you what I have got and what I have not got and then I will stop. I have got a roll with a column over it and nothing in the column, and a pair of terms nailed up outside a building where a reed man says it is a good shed, and a list of about nineteen names said out loud in this yard on the twenty-third of this month, and a name said in a yard on the twenty-sixth that is one of the four hundred and eleven, and one line in the second of those two books that somebody else wrote this afternoon. I have not got a way of pricing any of it. I have not got a removal that did not happen explained. I have not got a ninth charter's line or a third column of that covenant or the ninth place on that page. And I have not got the two past pullings worked out and I am not going to have them worked out by anybody in this yard this evening.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard what he has got and what he has not got has made a list and not a finding, and that two lists are two lists and are not added together, and that a thing this district has not worked out is not a promise about a later day and is not a defect in the ledger, and that a list of six things a man has got is not a figure about those six things and is not a figure about the man.

---

The eight lines of the roll were read out loud in that yard at about half past six by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and were read back from the top by the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart, and the two terms on the table with four legs were read out after them from the top of that as well, and nothing was wrong with any of the three readings, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that none of the three readings was counted, and that a document that has been read out and read back five times in twelve days is a document that has been introduced and not a document that has been proved, and that the fourth line of the offer that has been on a table in this district for six months was still not read out loud on the twenty-ninth of this month and the refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the foot of the east wall of that yard with his hand-cart against it and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a tent rolled on a hand-cart is not a shelter and is not a document and is not a sixth of the five protected things in this district.

The old charter in the book four hundred yards from that gate is not corrected and is not replaced and is not superseded and is not taken out, and its fifth line is still wrong on its face in a book a stranger may walk up to, and the four hundred and eleven is not struck by anything said in that yard in this month and a wrong count is not struck. The ninth charter's line, the third column of the covenant and the ninth place on the page out of the cave are still ruled and still empty and were named in the ledger of this district twice and filled in none of them, and a seventh of a shape of thing is not added to a sixth of a shape of thing. The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has stood at that since the first of the sixth month, which is eighty-nine days this morning, and that is a figure about the figure and is not a day-count for the sheet, and a sheet at a gatepost is a state and not a place.

The ninth of the nine printed nights was the twentieth of the fourth month and is a hundred and thirty-one days back and nobody in this district can say whether that night happened, and a plank does not keep nights, and a clerk entered that a clerk who could have made a finding out of a night in the fourth month and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing, and that it has been named on the page at the end of every day of this month and closed in none of them.

The woman of fifty-eight went up that bank at about half past three with an empty bucket and came down it at about five with a full one and stopped at the foot of it for about nine minutes with nobody there, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked about the line in the second of the two books and that she was not asked about anything else.

The light went at about half past eight and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-eight days past. About nine feet of brick at the near end of a lane behind a bank had a bench and a stool and two buckets of water in it and the plank door standing open on the hasp, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put his left hand flat on the outside wall of it for about nine minutes and the wall was warm, and he took his hand off and put it in his coat and the wall stayed warm.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH THE SECOND OF THE TWO BOOKS FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM THAT GATE HAD ONE LINE IN IT IN THE HAND OF THE WOMAN WHO KEEPS THAT BOOK, AND NOBODY ASKED HER TO WRITE IT AND THE RECORD SAYS NOT ASKED, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN, AND THE COUPLE DID NOT SETTLE AND DID NOT SPEAK ABOUT IT. THAT FIGURE IS EIGHTY-NINE DAYS OLD THIS MORNING AND IS A FIGURE ABOUT THE FIGURE AND NOT A DAY-COUNT FOR THE SHEET.**
