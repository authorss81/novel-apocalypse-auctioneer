# Chapter 335: The One He Chose

---

The twenty-fourth of the eighth month came up grey off the low ground and about nineteen people were standing about the edges of the yard of Lot Seventeen with nothing in their hands, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the middle of them out loud over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the thirty-fifth night and the head of him turned about a quarter of a circle further from that yard than it had been all week.

"**Two hundred and forty-three days on the board and five hundred and fifty-nine days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and seventy-three, and two hundred and thirty-four days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-three days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and twenty-six days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and fifty-three days, and the table against that wall stood up for the forty-ninth time this morning, and the chair at the end of that trestle table, sat in once on the tenth of this month after standing empty a hundred and nine days, is a chair and is not a figure and is not a habit.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that twenty-three days past is a figure about a ladder and is not a term, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district thirty-four days and has slept on that low wall on thirty-four of the thirty-five nights of that run, and that he has now slept on that wall thirty-four times and that a man who has slept on a wall thirty-four times has not been asked to be anything and is not a keeper of a wall.

---

He said one thing out loud at about ten in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I am going to say this once in this yard and I am not going to say it in a lane and I am not going to be the man somebody walks up to behind the bank and asks. The man standing at the foot of that wall has a name in his head that he has carried for eighteen years and it is the name of somebody in the four hundred and eleven, and he is not going to say it here, and nobody in this yard is going to make him, and I am not going to stand beside a man and ask him for it either, which I have had a whole day to do and have not done. If anybody in this yard wants to know what a name is worth to a body four hundred miles off that is holding a count of answerers, then you can stand at about ten feet off that wall tonight and you will hear whatever he says to whoever he says it to, and you will get not one word more of it than the next person, and that is the whole of what I have got to give this morning.**"

It was entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he is not going to ask a man for a thing has not refused to ask and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a name is not a figure and a name said out loud is handed to a body that is already holding a count of answerers, and that the four hundred and eleven has not moved on any of the days of this month.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against spent the whole of that day choosing a person. A clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of the day as one line and not as a paragraph, and entered that about four hundred yards of lane and one bank and one gatepost and one room were in it, and that four people were in it and that one of them is a woman of fifty-eight who carries a second bucket to a house in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off, and that she is the only person in this district who has done that and who has never once asked that house a question in nineteen years, and that she said so out loud in the room four hundred yards from that gate on the twenty-ninth of the sixth month and a clerk entered the whole of it, and that the house she carries to is not one of the four hundred and eleven and is not on any sheet in this district and is one of the five different ninths and was not asked this month.

He stood at the bank from about eleven until about one and the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was there with about nine sacks weighed one after another and the blanket of the scale folded over her arm, which she does not do, and he did not go down that lane to her and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not and that a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale is not a figure about a name and is not a refusal of one, and that her three words are rate and even and stock and that none of the three has been said again in this district and that none of the three may be explained by her.

He stood at the foot of that gatepost at about half past two and the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat was reading the two sheets on it, and he said two sentences to her and neither of them was a question, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two sentences that are not questions are not a request and are not a form and are not a consent, and that she looked at him for about eleven seconds and then went back up the lane without answering either of them, and that she has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way.

He went up the bank at about three to the room four hundred yards from that gate and stood on the step of two stones where a man of about sixty-four had laid a sheet of print flat on the ninth of this month, and he did not pick it up and he did not knock, and he came back down the bank at about half past three. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man standing on a step is not holding a sheet and is not holding a door and is not holding anything, and that the record about the not knocking says not asked, and that the sheet of print is still on the top step of two stones and where a sheet is is not an answer to anything.

He said one thing out loud at about half past four, in the ordinary voice, to a woman of fifty-eight at the foot of that bank, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and forty-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been choosing a person all day and I am going to tell you that I chose you, because you are the only one in this district who has carried a second bucket to a house in the ground in the shadow of that building eleven miles off and has never once asked that house a question in nineteen years, and you said that out loud in that room on the twenty-ninth of the sixth month and a clerk wrote the whole of it down. I did not choose the woman who keeps a scale because I have got nothing to put in her hands and she has four houses standing empty and nine sacks a day and I am not going to be the reason a woman of thirty-six years old hears a name. I did not choose the woman who keeps a goat because she asked the right question about that lane on the eighteenth of this month and if I put a name in her hands she will do something with it, and I cannot ask her to do nothing with it. I am not asking you what you think. I am asking you to come to a room with a shut door in it at half past four tomorrow and stand in it while a man says a name once, and then you can go, and I am not going to follow you up the lane.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud to a person that he chose her has not asked her what she thinks and has asked her for one thing, and that a request with a reason in it is not a form and a form is not a consent, and that the record about the not asking what she thinks says not asked, and that a woman of fifty-eight is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a bucket and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that nobody in that yard has asked her what she thinks and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The room is about nine feet by nine at the top of that lane, past the third house from the scale, with a stool by the wall and a window about a foot and a half square and a board nailed across the outside of it. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing agreed to leave it for about twenty minutes on the afternoon of the twenty-fifth and said that he would rather not be in it either, and went down that lane at about half past four and stood at the end of the trestle table about nine feet off the other side of it for about twenty minutes and did not go up the bank.

She said one thing to him at the foot of that bank at about half past four and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted, and that what she said was that in nineteen years nobody in this district has asked her for a thing without asking her what she thought about it first, and that he had come and asked her for a thing and had not asked her what she thought, and that she was not going to say yes and she was not going to say no in a lane, and that she would be at that room at half past four the next day because she carries water down that hill at half past four every day and it is the same road.

The refusal with no reason a clerk of a house has given is eight and it did not move on any day of this month, and a refusal with a reason is not one of the eight, and a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house. The rivals: nine refusals of eleven coppers a week, eight refusals with no reason, and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and the three are three figures and are not added together, and the three refusals given in that yard in the eighth month are the fifth, the sixth and the fourteenth of it and are three refusals on three days of three different things and are not a tenth and not a ninth and not an eighth. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and forty-sixth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and a man choosing a person is not a line on a board. The rival record has been in force eighty-four days and is unanswered, and a man choosing a person is not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and a room with a shut door in it is not a fourth of them. Five protected things and no sixth, and a choice is not a sixth.

The day came down about half past eight and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-three days past, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and a man of about thirty-four was standing about nine feet off the end of that trestle table for about twenty minutes on his own and not because anybody asked him to.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST SPENT THE WHOLE DAY CHOOSING ONE PERSON OUT OF FOUR, AND HE CHOSE THE WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT BECAUSE SHE IS THE ONLY PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT WHO HAS CARRIED A SECOND BUCKET TO A HOUSE IN THE GROUND AND HAS NEVER ASKED THAT HOUSE A QUESTION IN NINETEEN YEARS, AND HE DID NOT ASK HER WHAT SHE THOUGHT, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM ANYTHING AND HE ASKED FOR ONE THING. THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
