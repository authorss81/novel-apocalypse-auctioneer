# Chapter 339: Nothing in His Hands

---

There was nothing in the hands of the man of about sixty-four on the morning of the twenty-eighth of the eighth month. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table, the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and nobody in that yard said one word about the hands.

"**Two hundred and forty-seven days on the board, and there are two hundred and seventy-seven days on it since anybody entered one, which was the twenty-fourth of November. The table against that wall stood up for the fifty-third time this morning, and the chair at the end of that trestle table has been sat in once, on the tenth of this month, when it had stood a hundred and nine days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that twenty-seven days past is a figure about a ladder and is not a term, and that a man of about sixty-four came into that gate on the morning of the twenty-first of the seventh month with nothing in his hands and has been in this district thirty-eight days and has slept on that low wall on thirty-seven of the thirty-eight nights of that run, and that he carried a message from one place to another on his own two feet for eighteen years and said it out loud once in a room on the twenty-fifth of this month and there is nothing in his hands this morning and that is not a figure about him.

---

He filled two buckets at the trough outside that wall at about eleven and carried them up the two goes of the bank to the building with two doors and put them down inside it against the end wall where two buckets have been standing since the eleventh of this month, and about nine feet of brick with a bench and a stool in it took them without a word.

She entered that a man who carries a message from one place to another on his own two feet is not a keeper of it and does not become one, and that a man whose trade is over is not a failure and is not a tragic figure and is not redeemed, and that a man who carries two buckets of water up a bank on the morning after he has said a name out loud in a yard is not a figure about a name and is not a figure about a trade, and that nobody in that yard said anything about it and that that is entered and is not a finding and is not a refusal and is not a redemption.

At about one a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came up that lane with a sack on her shoulder, stopped about four feet off the end of that trestle table, asked him whether it was going to rain in the morning, and went on up the lane without waiting for the answer. He said he did not know. She said that was the answer she expected, and neither of them said anything else, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two sentences about the weather are not a finding and are not a refusal.

He said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and fourteen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have got nothing to carry. I am saying it out loud in this yard because if I do not say it then somebody in here is going to spend the next month being careful at me about it, and I would rather it was me that said it. I carried one thing for eighteen years and I said it in a room three days ago, which was the twenty-fifth, and a woman of fifty-eight said it in this yard on the twenty-sixth, and there is nothing in my hands now and there is not going to be anything in my hands for the rest of what I have got. That is the whole of it. I am not a man who has lost a thing and I am not a man who has been let go of anything. I am a man who has arrived, and I am going to be here, and I am not asking any of you for a job or a wall or a line in a book, and if one of you wants to give me a thing to do then ask me, because I will say yes to about half of it and no to the rest and I will tell you which before I do it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard what he has and has not got has made a list and not a finding, and that a man who is still in this district thirty-eight days after he came into that gate has not been asked to stay and has not been asked to go, and that a stay is counted without the day of arrival and that a man staying is not a post and is not a role and is not a bearer of that wall, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man who keeps a road was at the foot of that low wall all morning and nobody in that yard asked him one question about the eleven miles, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who brought back eleven miles in the seventh month and was in that yard every morning of this month without being asked once about them has not agreed with anything and has not disagreed with anything and cannot be counted either way.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the bank from before eight until about five with about nine sacks weighed one after another, and the four houses that stand empty and belong to her are not going to be offered again before the first day of the season after this one, on her own statement, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody has counted the people who have left those four houses and that the record about the not counting says not asked, and that four empty houses are four figures about four houses and are not a figure about her and are not added to the four hundred and eleven.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood about nine feet off the end of that trestle table from about ten until about four and did not go down to the low wall once, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he did not and that the record about the not going says not asked, and that a man standing nine feet off a man for six hours has not said anything to him and has not said anything about him and is not standing in his place.

He said one thing out loud at about four, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and thirty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**On the fifteenth of this month I worked out in a room that the sound of that bell went over the ground the sheet at that gatepost is about, and that every name in a record of a bell is the name of somebody who did not come to a door, and that saying one of those names out loud hands it to a body four hundred miles off that is already holding a count of answerers. I have had nine days with that and I have not got a price for it and I am not going to pretend that not having one is a kind of knowing. A man of about thirty-four told me in the sixth month that a subtraction is arithmetic and it is not a finding, and that nobody in this basin has ever had a way of pricing a difference, and he was right then. I cannot tell you what that name is worth. I am not going to tell you it is worth nothing, because I do not know that, and I am not going to tell you it is worth a great deal, because I have got nothing to put that on. I am going to leave it as a thing this district has got no way to price, in public, today, so that it is a thing and not a silence.**"

She entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he cannot price a difference has not refused to price it and cannot be counted either way, and that a number made by knocking, a number made by walking and a number made by listening are three numbers and are not added together, and that a name said out loud in a yard is not any of the three and is not a fourth of them and is not added to them, and that a figure this district has no way to price is not a mystery left dangling and is not a promise about a later day, and that a clerk who could have made a finding about the price of it and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing.

---

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat came up that lane at about half past four and read the two sheets on that gatepost and stood at the foot of it for about nine minutes with the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing nine feet off her, and neither of them said one word to the other. A clerk of nineteen years entered that two people standing nine feet off each other at the foot of a gatepost reading a sheet is two figures about two people and is not a figure about the sheet, and that a hand that has not closed since the thirteenth of the sixth month is a figure about a hand, and that the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was offered nothing this afternoon and copied nothing, and that nobody in that lane has asked either of them anything and the record about the not asking says not asked.

There has been no line on a board on any of the hundred and fiftieth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and a man saying that he has nothing to carry is not a line on a board. The refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent and no date in any ledger carries the next reading, and the fourth line of that offer has now been on a table in this district for six months and has not been read out loud in a yard in any of them. The rival record has been in force eighty-eight days and is unanswered, and a man with nothing in his hands is not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and a pair of buckets is not a fourth of them. Five protected things and no sixth, and a wall a man sleeps on is not a sixth and is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named. Four conditions with no end on it and no fifth was proposed on any day of this month. Four columns of not-askings, and a column with the word NOT ASKED over it and nothing under it is not a fifth of them.

The light went off the low ground at about half past eight and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-seven days past. There were two buckets of water against the end wall of a building with two doors and both of them had been filled that morning, and that gatepost still carried the one figure it has carried since the first of the sixth month, which is four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH THE MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-FOUR HAD SAID A NAME OUT LOUD AND HAD NOTHING TO CARRY AND NOBODY SAID ANYTHING ABOUT THAT IN A YARD, AND A MAN WHOSE TRADE IS OVER IS NOT A FAILURE AND IS NOT A TRAGIC FIGURE AND IS NOT REDEEMED, AND A MAN WHO CARRIES A MESSAGE IS NOT A KEEPER OF IT AND DID NOT BECOME ONE BY SAYING IT, AND A FIGURE THIS DISTRICT HAS NO WAY TO PRICE WAS ENTERED IN PUBLIC AS A THING AND NOT AS A SILENCE. THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET ON THAT GATEPOST IS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN, WHICH IS EIGHTY-EIGHT DAYS THE SAME FIGURE AND IS A FIGURE ABOUT THE FIGURE.**
