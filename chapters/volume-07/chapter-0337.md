# Chapter 337: A Name in One Person's Head Is Not Safe

---

It was the twenty-sixth of the eighth month and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the thirty-seventh night and he had heard a name said in a room four hundred yards up that lane yesterday afternoon and he had not slept on that wall and he had not been asked about either.

"**Two hundred and forty-five days on the board and five hundred and sixty-one days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and seventy-five, and two hundred and thirty-six days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-five days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and twenty-eight days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and fifty-five days, and the table against that wall stood up for the fifty-first time this morning, and one sitting in the chair at the end of that trestle table since the tenth of this month, when it had stood a hundred and nine days, and one is not a figure and is not a habit.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that twenty-five days past is a figure about a ladder and is not a term, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district thirty-six days and has slept on that low wall on thirty-six of the thirty-seven nights of that run, and that a name in the head of a man who has said it once is not a figure about a night and is not a figure about a man.

---

She came down the bank at about five with two buckets, the way she does, and filled the second of them at the trough outside the wall of that yard, and before she asked anybody for anything and before anybody had asked her for anything she said it out loud in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

The two words are not on this page.

It was entered that a name was said in that yard and that a clerk may enter that a name was said and not what it was, and that the name is the name of a person who is one of the four hundred and eleven, and that a name said out loud in a yard a stranger can walk into is not a consent the person it belongs to gave, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a name is not a figure, and that a name in one person's head is not a record and is not a column and is not a line on a board, and that every person in that yard heard two words and none of them was written down by anybody and the record about the not writing says not asked.

She said one thing after it, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what she said and got two hundred and thirty-eight, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**I am not going to hold it. That is the whole of what I have come down this bank to say and I have said the other thing and now I am going to say this. I have carried two buckets up that hill for nineteen years and there is one of the eleven I carry to that I have never put one question to her about, in any of those nineteen years, and twice I did ask that woman something, and both times it was about a bucket, and she is the woman in that house and she is not one of the four hundred and eleven and she is not on any sheet in this district and I have never once stood in that yard and used a name. I know what that name is worth. I do not know what it is worth. I know that a name that is in one head is a name that can be got out of that head by anything at all, and I have watched people carry things for eighteen years and I am not going to be the one who hands a thing on for a living and then be surprised when somebody asks me to hand it on again. Ask me whatever you want after this and I will tell you, and I am not asking for anything this evening and I am not going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the ninth of the eleven houses a woman of fifty-eight carries water to is one of the five different ninths in this district and was not asked on the twenty-sixth of this month, and that a sixth was available to a clerk of nineteen years this morning and was not taken, and that a clerk who could have made a sixth and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing, and that a woman of fifty-eight is not a keeper of a house and is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a bucket and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the foot of the east wall of that yard with his hand-cart against it and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and he came about nine feet off her and asked her one question, in the ordinary voice, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted.

She said no in about four seconds and the reason was her own, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that a man who puts tables up is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a table and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that the three refusals given in that yard in the eighth month were given by one woman to three men on three days about three different things, and that this one was given by a different woman to a different man about a different thing and is not a fourth of anything and is not a tenth and is not a ninth and is not an eighth.

"**No. I have lost the use of both hands and I put a table up against a wall about four feet high eleven miles off about fifty mornings running and nobody has put a hand on it but me, and if your two words went on the end of that table then a stranger could walk up eleven miles of road and read them, and I said in this yard in the sixth month that a sheet of print out where a person walks past it is worth more than a sheet in a room, and I am not going to be the man who takes back a thing he said out loud in a yard. You have said it where about nineteen people could hear it and I am not going to say that was a mistake, because it was not a mistake, and you have not asked me anything about it and I am not going to ask you.**"

It was entered that a document in a room four hundred yards from that gate is a document in a room, and that the same document nailed to a table eleven miles off is the same document and is a different set of eyes on it, and that a man who has lost the use of both hands is not a figure about a document and is not a reason to refuse anybody anything, and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was at the end of that trestle table when she said it and he was the only man in that yard who knew what the two words were, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who is the only person in a yard who knows a thing is not a bearer of that yard and has not been asked to be one, and that a man who is the only person in a yard who knows a thing cannot ask one person in that yard to do anything with it and cannot ask anybody else to do anything with it either.

He said one thing out loud at about ten past five, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and seventy-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I heard it. I was thirty feet off her and I heard it and I am not going to say the two words back in this yard to show you that I heard them. I have got nothing to add to it and I have not got a correction and I have not got a reason why she should not have done it, and I have got a bad thing in my own head about what it is going to cost her, and I have had since about half past four yesterday afternoon, and she got here first. I am not going to be the man who goes up that bank tonight and tells a woman she should not have said it, and I am not going to be the man who says it was a good thing to say. It was a thing she decided to do and I was not in the room when she decided it and I have not been asked about that and I am not going to be.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he is not going to tell a person she should not have done a thing has not told her and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not telling says not asked, and that he did not go up the bank that evening and did not ask anybody else to go up it and sent nobody, and that a clerk entered that he asked for nothing, and that that is entered and is not a finding and is not a refusal.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane for the whole of that and did not stand up and did not look round, and about nine people in that yard looked at him and then looked away, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nine is a figure about nine people looking and is not a figure about a man, and that a man who carries a message from one place to another on his own two feet is not a keeper of it and did not become one by saying it, and that a man whose trade is over is not a failure and is not a tragic figure and is not redeemed, and that a clerk may not say that about him and may not say the other thing either.

Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and forty-eighth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and two words in a yard are not a line on a board. The refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move in the month of August, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and the man or the woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house. The rival record has been in force eighty-six days and is unanswered, and a name is not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and a name said in a yard is not a fourth of them. Five protected things and no sixth, and a name is not a sixth and a bucket is not a sixth. The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved, and a name said out loud in a yard is not one name added to the four hundred and eleven and does not make four hundred and twelve, and the four hundred and eleven is a count of the people who answered a door and a name in a yard is a count of nothing at all.

The day came down about half past eight and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-five days past, and two words had been said in that yard and were not written down anywhere, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT SAID A NAME OUT LOUD IN A YARD A STRANGER CAN WALK INTO BECAUSE A NAME IN ONE PERSON'S HEAD IS NOT SAFE, AND SHE SAID IT BEFORE SHE ASKED FOR ANYTHING AND ASKED FOR NOTHING, AND HER OWN LINE IN THIS DISTRICT IS THAT SHE HAS ASKED THAT WOMAN ABOUT A BUCKET TWICE IN NINETEEN YEARS AND BOTH TIMES SHE ASKED HER ABOUT A BUCKET, AND THE NAME IS NOT ON THIS PAGE AND IS NOT IN ANY BOOK. THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
