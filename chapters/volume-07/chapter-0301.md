# Chapter 301: A Man Arriving With Nothing

---

The twenty-first of the seventh month came in hot before seven and there was no wind in that lane at all that morning, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing laid the three figures on the end of the trestle table in front of him and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten.

"**Two hundred and nine days on the board and five hundred and twenty-five days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and thirty-nine, and two hundred days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is eleven days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is ninety-two days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and nineteen days, and the table against that wall stood up for the fifteenth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood eighty-nine days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that eleven is ten days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved on any of the fifty days since the first of the sixth month, **and that fifty is a figure about the figure and is not a figure about the sheet, because the day that sheet went back up on that post is not a day this district has.** She entered that a board that has not been washed since the twenty-fourth of December is a board and not a calendar, and that the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning on the twenty-first of the seventh month, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house.

Then the gate took two goes, the way it always takes two goes, because the ground behind that post is soft, and the second of the two goes was a man coming in off the lane and not going out of it.

He was a man of about sixty-four who carries a message from one place to another on his own two feet, and he came up that lane on the twenty-first of the seventh month at about a quarter past ten in the morning, and he had nothing in his hands. No case and no portfolio and no list and no seal and no paper, and both hands were down at his sides the whole way from the gate to the trestle table, and he was about five foot nine and grey at the temples and thin across the top of the shoulders, and the outside edge of the sole of his right shoe was gone and the heel of his left one had come off in a piece about the size of a coin, and every step he took put a pale print about nine inches long on the dry part of that yard, and he did not look down at one of them.

He came round the end of the trestle table and stopped about four feet off it and stood there. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him the one question anybody in that yard asked him before twelve, which was what he was carrying, and he said he was carrying nothing, and about four people heard him say it, and then he said one thing about his own two hands, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have come nine days on my own two feet and I have got nothing in my hands and I am not going to be given anything this morning, and I would like the nine entered as a figure about my own walking and not as a figure about any ground between here and where I started, and I would like it entered that nobody has asked me where I started from and the record about the not asking says not asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nine is a figure a man gave about his own legs and is not a figure about a road in this district and is not a figure about a distance, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to be given anything has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and that he gave no name to anybody in that yard and nobody asked him for one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A woman about nine feet up the lane asked him then, in the ordinary voice, what he had come for, and he said he had heard a bell, and he did not say what kind of bell or how far off it was, and about four people heard him say that as well, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said one thing about a sound has not been asked a question about it and has not refused one and cannot be counted either way, and that nobody in that yard asked him whether he had heard anything and that nobody was going to, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing about the two sets of hands, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and thirty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A man came out of this gate at about two in the afternoon on the nineteenth of last month with a case in it and a list in the case, and that list is not in this district and nobody in this district has seen it and nobody has counted it, and this morning a man came through the same gate with nothing in his hands at all. I am not drawing anything out of those two things this morning and I am not going to draw anything out of them in this yard. He has not got a case because I have not seen him put one down, and he has not got a list because I have not seen him produce one, and a thing I have not seen is not a thing he has not got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a thing nobody has seen is not a thing a man has not got, and that two men coming through one gate with opposite contents is not a figure about either of them, and that a man who mends fencing is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything.

At about half past eleven, when the shade off the east wall had come round as far as the foot of the trestle table, the man of about sixty-four stood at the gatepost and read the sheet nailed there at the height of a man's shoulder from about four feet off, and did not put a hand out towards it, and came back and stood at the end of the table where about nine people were reading the five lines of print and read those from about four feet off as well, and neither time did he come closer to either of them.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was at the foot of that table at about the same time and said four or five words to nobody in particular about having his voice back that morning after two days of not having any of it, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that nobody asked him what it was about and that the record about the not counting says not asked.

At about half past twelve a woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the bank with about four people being weighed one after another and did not come up the lane, and the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them came down off the low wall and asked the man of about sixty-four, in the ordinary voice and not to anybody in particular, where a man of sixty-four who is walking about a district in the seventh month was going to sleep, and he said he had not worked that out, and the man of about thirty-seven said all right and went back to the wall.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a yard a stranger can walk into is a room with no roof and is a place where a thing gets said out loud, and that it is not a place anybody is put up for the night, and that a man who has not been asked where he is going to sleep has not refused anywhere and cannot be counted either way, and that a tent rolled on a hand-cart against the wall inside that gate is not a lean-to and is not a sixth of the five protected things in this district.

---

The heat in that yard between about one and about three was the worst of it. Somebody put a pot of water on at the gate about one and about six of them drank out of it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud twice in that hour for people coming in off the lane, and both times a man at the back of that yard asked him what the word *place* meant, and both times he said it did not have a meaning he was going to give it, and the third time nobody asked him and the third reading went the same as the first two.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was not in that yard at that hour and nobody said where he had gone. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came through the gate at about half past two on his way out to the far side of the four miles of road he has kept for eleven years, and the man of about sixty-four was still standing at the end of that trestle table with his hands down, and the two of them said four or five words to each other about the heat and nothing else, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of that road and has never been asked to be one.

About four of them went up the lane at about five. The sheet of five lines of print was still lying on the end of the trestle table with one corner turned over by somebody who had been holding it down with a thumb, and the piece of paper with two lines of print on it and the chalk mark of a third one started was where it has been lying since the fifth of this month, and the chair at the end of that table was where it has stood for eighty-nine days.

The man of about sixty-four did not go up the lane with anybody. He went and sat down on the low wall along the lane at about ten to six, on the shaded side, the way a man sits down when he has been on his feet for nine days, and about four people went past him on their way out and none of them asked him anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that about four people went past a man on a wall and asked him nothing, and that four is a figure about four people walking past a man and is not a figure about the man.

She entered the rest of what had not moved on the twenty-first of the seventh month in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph. Three documents this district does not own. Five protected things and no sixth. Four conditions with no end on it and no fifth proposed. Thirteen refusals to join two things and the registrar's own count of twelve. Nine refusals to read, and the departure unspent, and there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and those two are not the same figure and are not added together. Five boards and six lines full, and a seventh ruled and empty at the ninth place. Four columns of not-askings, and she did not rule one. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and twelve days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The rival record has been in force fifty days and has been read out in that yard seven times, and the eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of last month, and nobody has answered it. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the ninety-one days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that figure out of a ladder and did not ask him for it.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was twenty-one days old and the first day of the eighth month was eleven days off, and a man of about sixty-four who carries a message from one place to another on his own two feet was sitting on the low wall along the lane with nothing in his hands.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-FOUR CAME ON FOOT INTO THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN WITH NOTHING IN HIS HANDS AND GAVE NO NAME TO ANYBODY AND WAS NOT ASKED FOR ONE, AND HE SAID HE HAD COME NINE DAYS ON HIS OWN TWO FEET, AND HE READ TWO SHEETS IN THAT YARD FROM ABOUT FOUR FEET OFF AND CAME NEITHER OF THEM CLOSER.**
