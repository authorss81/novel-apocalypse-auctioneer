# Chapter 304: The Earliest Day

---

The twenty-fourth of the seventh month came up white and very hot and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was not in that yard at ten in the morning, and had gone out of that gate at about ten to five and had not been in it since, and about nineteen people were standing about in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**Two hundred and twelve days on the board and five hundred and twenty-eight days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and forty-two, and two hundred and three days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is eight days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is ninety-five days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and twenty-two days, and the table against that wall stood up for the eighteenth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood ninety-two days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that eight is seven days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that **this is the earliest day on which the bell in the oak frame four miles out past the loams may be pulled, and that being the earliest day is not the same as the day, and that nobody has said it is the day.** She entered that the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning on the twenty-fourth of the seventh month, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house.

A man of about nineteen who counts said at about ten that he had seen a man go out through that gate at about ten to five that morning with a coat over his arm and nothing else in his hands, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not asked to describe where the man had gone and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that nobody in that yard knew at about ten in the morning where the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the three things out loud at the end of that trestle table, in the ordinary voice, standing about four feet back from it, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and fifteen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A clerk wrote these three down in one hand on the twenty-third of last month and I am going to say them in the order she wrote them. The first is that a bell that is rung records everybody who hears it for one night. The second is that a bell rung when there is nothing to answer it cannot be rung again for a month. The third is that a community has to provide a shelter and a way to take the record off. That is the whole of them. I have said all three out loud in this yard this morning because this is the earliest day the second of the three allows, and because about nine people in here have been carrying the second of them round since the twenty-second and it is easier to have it said where everybody can hear it. The first of the three is a thing about bells. The second of the three is a thing about a month. The third of the three is a thing about a building, and there is one at the near end of the lane behind the bank, about nine feet of brick, and that is where I am going to stop and let somebody else say the rest of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a bell and is not a keeper of one, and that the three things have now been said out loud in that yard in front of about nineteen people and are in the same hand in a book four hundred yards away as they were before, and that saying a rule out loud does not make it true and does not make anybody a holder of it, and that a bell is not a figure and is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named.

At about one, when the shade off the east wall had come round to the foot of the trestle table, the man of about sixty-four was at the low wall along the lane and the man of fifty-six was four feet from him, and the man of about sixty-four asked him one question, out loud, in the ordinary voice, which was what a month was, and the man of fifty-six said one thing back and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and forty-seven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A month is the length of the second of those three things and nothing else. A bell rung on the twenty-fourth of last month can be pulled again on the twenty-fourth of this month, and not on the twenty-third, and that is a fact about that bell and about a length of time and it is not a thing anybody in this basin has agreed to and it is not a document. And I will tell you the part I have never said out loud to anybody, which is that I have been saying that second thing out loud since the twenty-second and I have not once asked myself whether a month is the same length every time, and I do not know, and a man who stands at a gate with a board ought to be the last man in a district to find that out.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk, and that a man who has said out loud that he does not know something cannot be counted either way and the record about the not asking him says not asked, and that a month is a length of time and not a document and not a term and not a figure about anybody.

The man of fifty-six said the rest of it, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in the same yard, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and sixty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The third of the three is not true this morning and nobody is going to make it true today. There is a building at the near end of the lane behind the bank with two doors in it, about nine feet of brick and no window, a plank door in the end wall with a nail in a hasp that has not moved since the eleventh of last month, a curtain on a nail, a table with four legs against the outside wall, and a ditch behind it about three feet deep with about a foot of standing water in the bottom of it. That is a building. It is not a shelter, because a shelter is a thing a community provides and about nine people came into that building on the night of the twenty-fourth of last month and nobody in this district has asked one other person in this basin whether they would come into it. There was a man at a table with four legs that night who put a piece of paper and a piece of chalk out on it and said out loud that anybody who wanted to be on that paper could write their own name on it and nobody would stop them, and that man has a hand that has not closed since the eleventh of last month, and nobody has asked him to do it again. Nobody has been asked. That is the whole of what the third of the three is short of, and it is not short by a rope.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a building is not a shelter and a shelter is not a building and the two are not added together, and that a table with four legs outside a door is not a counter until somebody asks somebody a question at it, and that a person standing in a doorway is not holding the door, and that **a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way and the record about the not asking says not asked**, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and a man of fifty-six is not a clerk of a house.

---

About nine people walked out to the frame at about two in the afternoon. The yard was nearly empty by then and the heat had got the length of the lane up behind them, and they went out past the loams on the four miles and came to the frame of oak about eleven feet high with its two uprights about four feet apart, and the bell hanging in the middle of it on a headstock of iron about two feet across the mouth with a crack running up from the rim about a third of the way on the side that faces the loams, and the clapper still in it, and about nine inches of rope left hanging inside the beam where nobody standing on the ground can reach it. The second frame was about nine feet back and newer and was holding nothing at all.

Nobody went up. Nobody sent for anything. A man of about forty who had a cart of his own off the lane put both hands on the upright and pushed on it once to see what it was like, and it did not move, and he took his hands off it and stood about nine feet back from it with his hands in his pockets for the rest of the time they were there.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood under the beam for about four minutes looking up at about nine inches of rope he could not reach and did not try to reach, and then he said four or five words out loud to about nine people, and they were not counted.

"**It is the earliest day. I am not saying it is the day.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that about nine people stood under a frame of oak at about half past two in the afternoon on the twenty-fourth of the seventh month and that nobody pulled that bell, and that nobody was asked whether it should be pulled and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a bell does not need a person and a person standing next to a bell is not deciding what it rings and a man standing under a beam for about four minutes has not been put in a place and cannot be counted either way, and that the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road had gone out from that frame at about ten to five that morning and was not in it at about two in the afternoon, and that nobody in this district knows where he was at about two in the afternoon, and that the record about the not knowing says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the top of the ditch behind the building with two doors for about a minute on the way back and looked down into it and did not go down, and about a foot of standing water was in the bottom of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who stood at the top of a ditch and did not go down into it has not refused the ditch.

She entered what had not moved on the twenty-fourth of the seventh month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and fifteenth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood ninety-two days and nobody has sat in it on any of them. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the ninety-four days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that out of a ladder and did not ask him. The rival record has been in force fifty-three days and has been read out in that yard seven times, and the eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of last month, and nobody has answered it. Five protected things and no sixth, and a building is not a sixth and a shelter is not a sixth and a bell is not a sixth. Three documents this district does not own, and no document came into that yard today. Four columns of not-askings, and she did not rule one. The instruments this district has built and not named are six, and about nine people standing under a frame is not a seventh of them. No number on the sheet at that gatepost moved today and four hundred and eleven is four hundred and eleven.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was twenty-four days old and the first day of the eighth month was eight days off, and the earliest day on which that bell might have been pulled was the day it was not pulled on.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH, WHICH WAS THE EARLIEST DAY THAT THE BELL IN THE OAK FRAME FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS MIGHT BE PULLED AGAIN, ABOUT NINE PEOPLE STOOD UNDER IT FOR ABOUT FOUR MINUTES AT HALF PAST TWO IN THE AFTERNOON AND NOBODY PULLED IT, AND THE THIRD OF THE THREE THINGS A BELL HAS TO HAVE BEFORE THIS DISTRICT WILL CALL A SOUNDING SAFE WAS NOT TRUE, AND NOBODY WAS ASKED WHETHER IT SHOULD BE PULLED.**
