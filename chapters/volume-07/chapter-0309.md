# Chapter 309: What He Came For

---

The twenty-ninth of the seventh month was hot from before six and the boards at that end of the lane had begun to lift at the joints by ten, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him and his hands on his knees.

"**Two hundred and seventeen days on the board and five hundred and thirty-three days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and forty-seven, and two hundred and eight days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is three days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and twenty-seven days, and the table against that wall stood up for the twenty-third time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood ninety-seven days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that three is two days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that a hundred days is a round figure and that a round figure is a figure about a calculation and is not a figure about anybody, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that the ninth of the nine printed nights is the twentieth of the fourth month and is a hundred days back this morning and that nobody in this district can say whether that night happened and she did not rule a column about it and is not going to.

At about half past ten the man of about sixty-four stood up off that wall and came and stood at the end of the trestle table, on his own two feet, without being asked by anybody, and he said one thing out loud in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got four hundred and thirty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I came here because I heard a bell. I am going to say the rest of it this morning and I am not going to say all of it, and the part I am not going to say is not going to be said in a yard. I was in the sound of it on the night of the twenty-fourth of last month at about two in the morning. That bell was about four hundred miles from where I was standing and it was a cracked bell in a frame of oak and it held for about nine minutes, and I was in the sound of it for the whole of those nine minutes, and I did not go to a door and I did not go to a building and I stood where I was and heard it. There is a piece of paper in a room four hundred yards from that gate with about nine names on it in about nine hands and that is the list of the people who came in. I am not on it. I am in the record of that night, and that record is not a paper and is not in a book in this district and cannot be taken off, and there is no other one of it I can get to from here. In the record of that night there is a name. That name was taken off a list before the Long Fracture and I have carried it since I was a young man, and I will not say it in a yard, and I am telling you I have got it and not telling you what it is, and I would like both halves of that entered as two things and not as one. Here is the last of it. I am a courier. That is the word and I have not got a better one. A man at the back of this yard used that word about me on the twenty-second of this month and meant a man who takes a parcel for a shop and is paid at the end of a week. He was right about that man and wrong about me, and it is the same word and not the same thing. I have carried this one thing for eighteen years and never written it down, and if anybody here asks me to put it down I am going to say no, and that is a refusal with a reason and the reason is that a name in a book is a name anybody with the book can find.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a name is not a figure, and that a figure a person says out loud in a yard is a figure and a figure nobody says out loud is a figure in a book four hundred yards away, and that a man who has said out loud that he has been carrying something for eighteen years has not been asked to put it down and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and a man who carries a message from one place to another on his own two feet is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that the name was not said in that yard and is not in this entry and is not on any sheet in this district. She entered that nobody in that yard asked the man of about sixty-four whether he had heard anything at all and that nobody was going to be, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a man who has said out loud that he will not say a thing in a yard has not refused to say it anywhere and cannot be counted either way.

---

Nobody in that yard said one word for about nine seconds. A man of about forty with a cart of his own off the lane started to say something and did not finish it and stood about four feet off the end of that trestle table with his mouth shut, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put his hand flat on the board and left it there, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that about nine seconds is a figure about nine seconds and is not a figure about anybody and is not a finding, and that a man who has started a sentence and not finished it has not said the rest of it and the record about the rest of it says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said one thing out loud at about a quarter to eleven to about four people and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that about four people heard it.

"**He is one of the people that bell recorded and he is not one of the people that came through that door, and I have watched that yard a good while now and I have watched people try to put those two things together, and this morning I watched about nine people in this yard not do it, and I am the man who went out and counted the ground it went over, and I am not going to be the one who starts it either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of that road and has never been asked to be one, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to be the one who starts a thing has not refused the thing and cannot be counted either way.

The man of fifty-six said one thing at about twenty past twelve, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and seventy-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**He has stood in this yard and told nine days of walking and a name he is not going to say and a word that was used wrong about him seven days ago, and I am the man in this basin who has said out loud every week for five months that a door is not a keeper and a wall is not an address, and I have not got one word of that which helps me here. I am not going to take this off him and I am not going to be pleased about it in front of nineteen people, and I would like a clerk to write down that what a man said out loud in this yard this morning was the shape of a thing and not the thing, and that the shape is worth exactly what I have always said a shape is worth, which is nothing, and that I have never once said out loud that a shape is worth nothing and I have been wrong every time I have thought it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has said out loud that a thing is worth nothing has not withdrawn a thing he said before and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The rest of that day went slowly and about four people were still in that yard at six. The man of about sixty-four sat back down on the low wall at about half past twelve and stayed on it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud twice that afternoon and the man of about sixty-four did not read them over anybody's shoulder either time and sat about nine feet away with his hands on his knees.

She entered what had not moved on the twenty-ninth of the seventh month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and twentieth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood ninety-seven days and nobody has sat in it on any of them, and a man sat down on the end of that table on the sixteenth of last month and not in the chair. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the ninety-nine days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that out of a ladder and did not ask him, and he is a man of about forty-eight and not thirty-four and has said of himself that he is fifty-one. The rival record has been in force fifty-eight days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of last month, and it is unanswered. Three documents this district does not own, and no document came into that yard today, and a name that was not said is not a document and did not become a fourth of the three. Five protected things and no sixth, and a name is not a sixth and a word is not a sixth and a shape is not a sixth. The instruments this district has built and not named are six, and a courier is not a seventh of them and a courier is not an instrument. Four conditions with no end on it, thirteen refusals to join two things and the registrar's own count of twelve, nine refusals to read with the departure unspent and no date in the ledger, nine refusals of eleven coppers a week and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and neither figure is the other figure and the two are not added together. Four columns of not-askings, and she did not rule one. No number on the sheet at that gatepost moved today and four hundred and eleven is four hundred and eleven and the figure on that sheet is a figure about the people who answered a door.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was twenty-nine days old and the first day of the eighth month was three days off, and a man of about sixty-four was sitting on a low wall with a name in his head and nothing in his hands.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-NINTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-FOUR STOOD UP OFF A WALL IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AND SAID OUT LOUD, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE, THAT HE WAS IN THE SOUND OF THE BELL FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF ON THE NIGHT OF THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF LAST MONTH, THAT HE IS IN THE RECORD OF THAT NIGHT, THAT THERE IS A NAME IN THAT RECORD WHICH WAS TAKEN OFF A LIST BEFORE THE LONG FRACTURE, AND THAT HE WILL NOT SAY IT IN A YARD, AND HE SAID THE WORD FOR WHAT HE IS AND IT IS NOT THE WORD SOMEBODY USED, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT A NAME IS NOT A FIGURE.**
