# Chapter 306: Eleven Miles

---

The twenty-sixth of the seventh month was the fourth hot day running and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came in through that gate at about half past six that morning, said nothing to anybody, and sat down at the foot of the low wall along the lane, and he was still there when the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing laid the three figures on the end of the trestle table, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right.

"**Two hundred and fourteen days on the board and five hundred and thirty days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and forty-four, and two hundred and five days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is six days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is ninety-seven days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and twenty-four days, and the table against that wall stood up for the twentieth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood ninety-four days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that six is five days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man sat on the low wall along the lane before anybody in that yard had said one word to him about anything.

At about half past ten the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road stood up off that wall and came round the end of the trestle table and said one thing out loud in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and forty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I counted it from that frame and not from this gate, because that is where I started from and I walked outwards, and I want that said before the number, because it is the only part of it I am certain about. I did not mark anything and I did not count anybody and I have got no wheel and no cord and no chain and I counted it the only way a man on his own two feet can count a distance, which is by how long it takes him to walk and by what the ground is like, and the ground stopped being ground I know somewhere around the point where I could not be sure any more, and the last place I could be sure is eleven miles from that frame. That is the whole of it. I went past the point and carried on for about a mile and a half to see whether the not-being-sure came back, and it did not, so I have got eleven miles and about a mile and a half, and I am not going to give the mile and a half a name. I have not told the man who asked me what I think it means and I am not going to tell this yard either, and I have not been asked by anybody in this yard what I think it means, and I would like that entered, and the record about the not asking says not asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of that road and has never been asked to be one, and that a distance a man counted on his own two feet is a figure about a man's walking and is not a figure about a road, and that about a mile and a half is a figure about a man's walking and is not added to eleven miles and does not become twelve and a half, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to say what a figure means has not refused to say it and cannot be counted either way.

At about eleven the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked out to the gatepost and stood under the sheet nailed there at the height of a man's shoulder with about nine people behind him and said one thing out loud in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and nineteen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That sheet says a building eleven miles off this district. That is the distance on it, and it is the only distance on it, and it has been the same distance on that post on every morning that a man has read it out in this yard. This morning a man counted a bell at eleven miles. I am going to say the rest of it and I am not going to stop in the middle this time, because two men have now stopped in the middle of a thing in this yard and neither of them said the rest and I am not going to be a third. Eleven miles and eleven miles is two figures. They are the same length. Putting the same length twice on one page is not a sum and it is not a meaning and it is not a pattern anybody has looked for, and I have got no way of pricing the difference between them because this district has never had a way of pricing a difference, and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam said that in this yard on the twentieth of this month and I am repeating him and not agreeing with him. Nobody put one of them there. That is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two figures of the same length are two figures and are not added together, and that a clerk who could have made a finding out of them and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing, and that nobody in that yard has said the word coincidence and that the word is not in this chapter and is not in this district, and that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a figure and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything.

The man of fifty-six said one short thing about that at about half past eleven, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that about nine people heard it.

"**Nobody put one of them there.**"

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came up the lane from the bank at about half past eleven and stopped at the end of that trestle table where about nine people were standing and said one word out loud before anybody asked her for anything, and then went on and said a few more of them, and the first of them was even, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody asked her for it and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

"**Even. I have stood at that scale nine years and weighed about nine sacks a day and I have never weighed a day, and even is a word about a beam, and I have not had either of those two things on my scale and I am not going to say they are even because they are the same length. I am not saying they are not even either. I am telling you the word before somebody else in this yard does and gets it wrong in front of nineteen people.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a scale is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of anything, and that even is a word about a beam and is not a finding, and that a woman who has said out loud that she does not know has not refused to know and cannot be counted either way, and that her four empty houses are not going to be offered again before the first day of the season after this one on her own statement and did not become a fifth of anything today.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was at the end of that trestle table at about half past twelve and said one thing out loud in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fifty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A man walked eleven miles on his own two feet in daylight and brought the number back to a yard, and that is the first thing anybody in this basin has been able to do about that bell in a month. I am not going to say what it means and I have not asked him what it means and I am not going to put it anywhere. There is a book four hundred yards from this gate with one person in this district who may write in it and she told a man on the eighth of this month not to be asked again this month, and the month runs out on the thirty-first of it. I am not going to carry eleven miles up that bank this month. I am going to carry it out of that gate at the end of the day in my own head, where it was this morning before it was anybody's.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who convenes nine settlements is not a representative of a settlement that has not asked to be represented, and that a figure a man carries in his own head is not a document and not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to put a thing anywhere has not refused to put it anywhere and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The rest of that day was the hottest of the four. About nine people stayed in that yard past two and the rest went up the lane, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road sat on the low wall again from about one and did not say anything else that anybody wrote down, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam stood at the end of the trestle table for about nine minutes at about half past two looking at nothing in particular and went up the lane with his right arm hanging.

At about half past three the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing read the five lines of print out loud in about nine feet of lane outside the office to about four people, because a thing that is only read by one man in a yard stops when that man is ill, and he got through all five of them with his ordinary voice and nobody stopped him. The man of about sixty-four was at the end of that trestle table in that yard and did not go up the lane to hear it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two men were about four hundred yards apart and one of them was reading five lines out loud and the other one did not go and hear them, and that four hundred yards is a figure about a lane and not about either of them.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was asked about a third thing by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing at about four and said four or five words, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the tent was still rolled on the back of that hand-cart.

She entered what had not moved on the twenty-sixth of the seventh month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. A refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and seventeenth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood ninety-four days and nobody has sat in it on any of them, and a man sat down on the end of that table on the sixteenth of this month and not in the chair. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the ninety-six days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that out of a ladder and did not ask him. The rival record has been in force fifty-five days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of last month, and it is unanswered. The instruments this district has built and not named are six, and a distance a man counted on his own two feet is not a seventh of them. Three documents this district does not own, five protected things and no sixth, four conditions with no end on it, thirteen refusals to join two things and the registrar's own count of twelve, nine refusals to read with the departure unspent and no date in the ledger, five boards and six lines full, four columns of not-askings and no fifth ruled. **No number on the sheet at that gatepost moved on the twenty-sixth of the seventh month and the figure on it is four hundred and eleven and it was arrived at by counting the people who answered a door, and the eleven miles on that sheet and the eleven miles a man counted on his own two feet are two figures and are not added together.**

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was twenty-six days old and the first day of the eighth month was six days off, and about four hundred and eleven people in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off this district were to be moved on or before a day that had not come, and the number had not moved, and a figure about a bell had been said out loud in a yard that day and had not been added to anything.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SIXTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD SAID OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN THAT THE SOUND OF THE BELL IN THE OAK FRAME FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS ON THE NIGHT OF THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF LAST MONTH WENT ELEVEN MILES, AND THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST ALSO SAYS ELEVEN MILES, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT THE TWO ARE TWO FIGURES AND ARE NOT ADDED TOGETHER, AND A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX WHO KEEPS A SCALE SAID THE WORD EVEN BEFORE ANYBODY ASKED HER FOR IT.**
