# Chapter 324: Two Lines

---

On the thirteenth of the eighth month the light came over the east wall of that yard at about a quarter past eight and there were about nineteen people in it by ten, and about nine feet of brick at the near end of the lane behind a bank with a chair and two buckets of water and a sheet of print in it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the twenty-fourth night.

"**Two hundred and thirty-two days on the board and five hundred and forty-eight days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and sixty-two, and two hundred and twenty-three days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twelve days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and fifteen days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and forty-two days, and the table against that wall stood up for the thirty-eighth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table was sat in once, on the tenth of this month, and had stood a hundred and nine days before that, and one is not a figure and is not a habit.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that twelve days past is a figure and is not a term. A man of about sixty-four has been in this district twenty-three days and has slept on that low wall on twenty-three of the twenty-four nights of that run, and nobody asked him about the one he did not and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

He went into that building at about ten with a second sheet and wrote two lines on it in the order they go, one about what a person may take and one about a sound, and the second of them was the fourth line of the sheet he had read out loud in that yard on the seventh, eighth and ninth of this month, with the word that was printed over the blank one left out of it.

What he wrote, on one sheet, at about ten in the morning in a building a stranger may walk up to, is this.

> **A PERSON MAY TAKE WATER, SHELTER AND MEDICINE IN THIS DISTRICT AND IS NOT ASKED FOR A PUBLIC IDENTITY IN RETURN, AND A PUBLIC IDENTITY IS NOT GIVEN UP BY TAKING ANY OF THE THREE.**
>
> **NO PERSON IN THIS DISTRICT IS TO BE ENTERED IN ANY RECORD OF IT BY HAVING HEARD A SOUND, AND NOBODY IS TO BE ASKED WHETHER THEY HEARD ONE.**

He got both of them down in about twenty minutes and then he could not hold the pen up in his right hand, and wrote the second of the two in his left, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two lines are two lines in two hands and that both hands belong to one man who is the registrar of this district's records office, and that neither of those hands is a clerk's hand and that a clerk of nineteen years did not write either of them and has not been asked to and is not going to.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of the trestle table at about half past eleven and read the two of them out loud in the ordinary voice to about nine people without being handed the sheet, and then asked one question about them in the same voice, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Those are two terms. I have read things out loud in this yard for five months and I know what a term is when I hear one, and neither of those has anybody's name on it. Not the person who gives it, not the person who takes it, not a house, not a road, not a person. A term with nobody's name against it does not bind anybody. I have read it out in daylight in front of about nine people and I want somebody to tell me which one of us it is for, and I am not asking for the name, I am asking for the finger.**"

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood about four feet off the end of that trestle table for a while and then said one thing back, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and sixty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**For nobody. There is no finger. There is one person in this basin who may write in the book four hundred yards from this gate and she has not been asked about any of this and I am not going to ask her, and a district is not a body and cannot be named on a sheet of its own, and I found that out this morning by writing one down and looking at it. And here is the other half of it, and I did not know that until you asked. A term nobody can enforce and everybody can read is not the same thing as a term nobody can enforce and nobody can read. It is a different thing. The first one is a term. The second one is a piece of paper nobody has opened. I am going to put this one where somebody can open it and I am not going to pretend that is the same as a person having agreed to it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a term with nobody's name against it binds nobody, that this district has found that out on the page this morning and has entered it, and that the term stands anyway, and that the standing of it is not a finding and is not a success, and that a term which binds nobody and can be read by everybody is a different thing from a term which binds nobody and can be read by nobody, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two pieces of paper in the book four hundred yards from that gate are not corrected by anything written this morning and are not taken out of it, and that the first of the two says itself that a bell that is rung records everybody who hears it for one night and cannot be taken off afterwards, and that the second of the two is a list of who came into a building and put their own names on a piece of paper, and that a list of who came into a building is not a list of who heard a bell and was never going to be, and that the second of the two lines written in that building this morning is the answer to that difference and that the answer is not in the book and the book was not asked to hold it.

---

He carried the second sheet out at about half past four and put it flat on the top of the table with four legs against the outside wall of that building and weighted the near corner with a stone about the size of a fist, because the boards at the near end of that lane lifted at four joints, and squared it with the edge of that stone, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a sheet of print on a table outside a building is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a table with four legs outside a door is not a counter and is a table with four legs, and that a stone is a stone and is not a seal and is nobody's signature.

The rest of that day went over the two lines. About half past five the light went off that table and came back on it about ten minutes later when a break in the cloud went over, and both times the stone was where he had put it and the near corner of the sheet was still lifting at about four joints in the wind, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a sheet that lifts at four joints is held by a stone and not by a term. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart read the eight lines of the charter out loud in that yard at about half past five for three people off the lane and got nothing wrong, and a clerk entered that a thing read back right is read back again from the top and not from the word, and that getting it right on the eleventh of the eighth month is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about the man who read it. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was not in that yard and nobody said where he had gone. A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody said where he had gone and that a man who has gone has not refused to be asked and cannot be counted either way.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came back up that lane at about six and asked the clerk of nineteen years one question, in the ordinary voice, and to nobody in particular, which was whether the second of those two lines binds him, and she said that she did not know and that she was nineteen years a clerk and not the registrar and that a term is not enforced by a clerk and never has been. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk of a house is not a registrar and that a term which binds nobody is not kept by the person who writes it down, and that the man who drove the cart asked the question himself and that nobody asked him to and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that he is not a bearer of that term and has not been asked to be one.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was at the foot of that low wall at about half past six and said one thing out loud to about four people which was that he is not going to be a man standing in that ditch behind that building at two in the morning on any night this month, and a clerk entered that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to be somewhere has not refused to be there and cannot be counted either way, and that a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road is not a bearer of a ditch and is not a keeper of a building and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything.

The rival record has been in force seventy-three days and is unanswered, and a term about a sound is not an answer to it. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and thirty-fifth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and a term written on a sheet weighted with a stone is not a line on a board. Three documents this district does not own, and a term is a line and a line is not a document and did not become a fourth. Five protected things and no sixth, and a term is not a sixth and a stone is not a sixth and a table with four legs is not a sixth.

The day came down about half past eight and the first day of the eighth month was twelve days past, and two lines of print were lying on a table against the outside wall of a building at the near end of a lane behind a bank with a stone on the corner of them, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE THIRTEENTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH TWO TERMS OF THE PUBLIC MARKET OF THIS DISTRICT WERE WRITTEN IN A ROOM A STRANGER MAY WALK UP TO, ONE OF THEM ABOUT WATER, SHELTER AND MEDICINE AND ONE OF THEM ABOUT A SOUND, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT A TERM WITH NOBODY'S NAME AGAINST IT BINDS NOBODY AND THAT THIS DISTRICT FOUND THAT OUT ON THE PAGE AND ENTERED IT, AND THAT THE TERM STANDS ANYWAY. THE TWO PIECES OF PAPER IN THE BOOK FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM THAT GATE ARE NOT CORRECTED BY ANYTHING WRITTEN THAT MORNING AND NOT TAKEN OUT OF IT. THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
