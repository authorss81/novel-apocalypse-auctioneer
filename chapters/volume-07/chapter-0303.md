# Chapter 303: Nobody Ever Asked Him

---

The twenty-third of the seventh month was grey over the loams by about eight and the sun came through it about eleven, and there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and the man of fifty-six read the board out from the end of the trestle table with the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing holding it flat in front of him and got all three of the figures.

"**Two hundred and eleven days on the board and five hundred and twenty-seven days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and forty-one, and two hundred and two days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is nine days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is ninety-four days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and twenty-one days, and the table against that wall stood up for the seventeenth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood ninety-one days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nine is eight days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a board is a board and a figure on a board is a figure on a board and neither of them is a finding.

At about half past ten, with about nine people standing about four feet off the end of that trestle table and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road twenty feet away with his back to the east wall, the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came round the end of the table and asked him, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of everybody, whether he would go and do a thing, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and twenty-seven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been in this district thirteen days and I have not asked anybody to do anything in any of them, and I am aware that a man who convenes nine settlements is not the man anybody in this basin should be asking, and I am asking you anyway, and I am going to say what I want before anybody in this yard can work out that I have a reason. That bell in the oak frame four miles out past the loams was pulled on the night of the twenty-fourth of last month at about two in the morning for about nine minutes. There is a piece of paper in a room four hundred yards from this gate with about nine names on it in about nine hands and that paper is the list of the people who came into a building. I want to know how far that bell went. I want you to go out from that frame on your own two feet in daylight and walk outward until you cannot be sure any more, and I want you to come back and tell me the number and nothing else. I am not asking you what you think it means and you are not to tell me. I am not asking you to mark anything and I am not asking you to count anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who convenes nine settlements is not a representative of a settlement that has not asked to be represented, and that a man who has asked a question in a yard has not obtained a consent by asking it, and that nobody in that yard had been asked whether they wanted to hear the question, and that the record about the nineteen people not being asked says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came off the wall and said one thing back to that, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and fifty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have kept four miles of that road eleven years and I have not been asked to do a thing on it in all eleven of them, and I have not been asked to do a thing in this yard at all, and the reason for both of those is the same one, which is that nobody asked me. I have thought about that on the way here this morning and about nine times and I have got the same answer every time, and it is not that I would have said no. I have walked that road every day of a week for eleven years and there has never been a question in it. So yes. I will go out from that frame in the morning at first light and walk outward until I cannot be sure any more, and I will not mark anything and I will not count anybody and I will not tell you what I think it means. And I am going to say the rest of it now because if I do not say it today somebody in this yard will say it for me on some day I did not name. I am sixty-four in the road, not in the years, and four miles of it is a long day for a man of my age, and if it takes me two days to do it then it takes two days, and if I cannot get up out of something out there I will sit there until I can.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of that road and has never been asked to be one, and that a man who has said out loud what his own legs may or may not do has not refused a thing and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who is sixty-four in the road and not in the years has said a figure about himself and the figure is not a figure about the road.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the foot of that trestle table through all of it with his hands behind his back and said one thing about it afterwards, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and thirty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have read two sheets out loud in this yard in front of about nineteen people this month and both of them were printed four hundred miles off and neither of them had my name on them. This morning a man asked another man to do a thing and it was the first thing said out loud in this yard in a month that was not on a sheet. I am not going to tell anybody what it is worth. I am going to tell them that a man who reads things out loud for a living has just watched somebody do the thing he does, in daylight, for nothing, in front of nineteen people, and that I noticed it, and that noticing it is not a finding and is not a figure about anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that noticing a thing is not a finding has not made a finding and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who convenes nine settlements asked a man a question in a yard this morning and did not ask a yard a question, and that the record about the not asking a yard says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the bank with her scale under the lean-to through the middle of the morning and about four people were weighed, and she came up the lane behind the last of them at about one and stopped about nine feet off the end of that trestle table and listened to the two men for about a minute and went back down without saying anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who has listened and said nothing has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking her says not asked.

The man of about sixty-four was on the low wall along the lane where he had been the day before, with his heels off the ground and the soles of both shoes showing, and he listened to all of it without turning round, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud at about half past one for about four people, and the man of about sixty-four read them over about nine feet of his shoulder at the same time and came no nearer.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road went up the lane at about four to get his coat and came back down at about half past four with it over his arm, and there was nothing else in his hands at all, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man going out on foot for two days with a coat over his arm and nothing else in his hands is not a figure about anybody and is not the same shape of thing as a man going out with a case, and that the two are not added together and the first of them is not in this canon and the second one went out of that gate on the nineteenth of last month.

About half of that yard was gone by six. The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the end of the trestle table with his tent still rolled on the back of the cart and said four or five words about a man going out on foot with a coat over his arm, and they were not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that they were not counted and that about four people heard them.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing came up the lane at about six with his copy of nothing under his arm and stood at the foot of the trestle table about four feet off the man of about sixty-four for as long as a reading takes, and neither of them said one word to the other, and the man of about twenty-three read the five lines of print out loud to nobody and got through all five of them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two men stood about four feet apart at the foot of a table for the length of one reading and said nothing, and that two men and a reading and about four feet are three things about an afternoon and are not three figures about anybody and are not a finding.

She entered what had not moved on the twenty-third of the seventh month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. The eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning on the twenty-third of the seventh month, and a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and fourteenth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood ninety-one days and nobody has sat in it on any of them. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the ninety-three days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that out of a ladder and did not ask him, and he is a man of about forty-eight and not thirty-four and has said of himself that he is fifty-one. The rival record has been in force fifty-two days and has been read out in that yard seven times, and the eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of last month, and it is unanswered. Three documents this district does not own, five protected things, four conditions with no end on it, thirteen refusals to join two things and the registrar's own count of twelve, nine refusals to read with the departure unspent and no date in the ledger, five boards and six lines full, four columns of not-askings and no fifth ruled. Four hundred and eleven is on the sheet at that gatepost and no number on that sheet moved today.

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was twenty-three days old and the first day of the eighth month was nine days off, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was in that yard with his coat over his arm and was going to be out of it at first light.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-THIRD OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD WAS ASKED, IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN, IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE, TO GO OUT FROM THE BELL IN THE OAK FRAME FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS AND WALK OUTWARD ON HIS OWN TWO FEET UNTIL HE COULD NOT BE SURE ANY MORE, AND HE SAID THE REASON NOBODY HAD EVER ASKED HIM WAS THAT NOBODY HAD ASKED HIM, AND HE SAID HE WOULD GO OUT AT FIRST LIGHT.**
