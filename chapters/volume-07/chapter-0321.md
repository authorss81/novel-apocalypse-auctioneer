# Chapter 321: The Charter Is Drawn

---

The tenth of the eighth month came in about the same and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the twenty-first night.

"**Two hundred and twenty-nine days on the board and five hundred and forty-five days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and fifty-nine, and two hundred and twenty days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is nine days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and twelve days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and thirty-nine days, and the table against that wall stood up for the thirty-fifth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood a hundred and nine days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nine days past is nine days past and is not a term in a ladder, and that the ladder off the first day of the eighth month has no terms in front of it this morning and is not going to get any, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man of about sixty-four came into that gate on the morning of the twenty-first of the seventh month and has been in this district twenty days, a stay being counted without the day he arrived on, and that he has slept on that low wall on twenty of the twenty-one nights of that run and has not slept on it on one of them, and that nobody asked him about the one he did not sleep on it and the record about the not asking says not asked. She entered that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning on the tenth of the eighth month, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that nine days past is not an answer and is not a finding.

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said at about half past ten that he was going to write a charter for the building with two doors, and he said it to about nineteen people in a yard they could all walk into, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a thing said out loud in a yard is a thing said out loud in a yard and that a word in a man's mouth is not a document and that no charter of any kind was in that yard until about ten past eleven this morning and that one was carried into a building at the near end of a lane behind a bank.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him one question about it at about a quarter past ten, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**If a person writes their own name in there, that is them being in something. If they do not, they are not. And you have got a column over that says nobody is to be put under it, and so where is the person who is in it and not in it at the same time. I have read things out loud in this yard for five months and I have never once read a thing that put a person on both sides of a line, and I am asking you that in daylight in front of about nine people and not in a lane.**"

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood about four feet off the end of that trestle table and did not answer him for about eleven seconds, and then he said that he did not know, and that was the answer, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he does not know has not refused to say and cannot be counted either way, and that a question asked in a yard is a question a person can refuse, and that this one was asked in a yard.

---

He went up the lane behind the bank at about ten past eleven with a sheet of paper, a straight edge, a stub of chalk and the chair from the end of that trestle table, which he carried by its back with one hand because his two hands were needed for the other three things and there was only the one of him, and a clerk entered that a chair is not one of the five protected things in this district and that a chair being carried into a building is a chair being carried and is not a figure about anybody.

The building is about nine feet of brick with no window in it and a plank door in the end wall with a nail in a hasp, and a curtain on a nail inside, and a ditch behind it about three feet deep with about a foot of standing water in it. There is no floor in it that a man has laid and no stove and no bed and no water butt, and there is a bench against the end wall and a stool, and there is a clerk of nineteen years in it with a book under her arm, and she had been in there since about a quarter past ten and had not been asked to be in there by anybody.

He put the chair against the end wall and sat down in it at about a quarter past eleven, and the first thing he did in that room in five hours was the thing nobody had done in that chair in a hundred and nine days, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man sitting in a chair is not a finding and is not a habit, and that a chair is a chair and is not a document and is not a threshold and is not a shelter, and that nobody asked him whether he wanted to sit in it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the old charter in the book four hundred yards from that gate is not corrected by anything written this morning, is not replaced by it, is not superseded by it and is not taken out by it, and that the fifth line of it is still wrong on its face in a book a stranger may walk up to, and that a wrong count is not struck by writing a correct one beside it, and that the four hundred and eleven is still four hundred and eleven at about a quarter to twelve this morning and the record about the not striking says not asked.

He ruled the first line of that column at about half past eleven with the straight edge and a stub of chalk and it came out straight, and he said one thing at about two in the afternoon to the one person in that room, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and thirty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have got a word over a line and nothing under the line and I have been carrying that word around this yard for three days like a man carrying a hot thing, and the word is NOT ASKED, and I did not find it out this morning, I found it out on the fourth of the eighth month when a woman asked how many people were standing in that yard, and I have been wrong about this document since the moment I started it. I want it to be a thing that stops a removal, and it is going to stop one, and stopping one is not the same as working, and I have not got anybody to ask whether it works and I am not going to go and find one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a room that he has been wrong about a document since the moment he started it has not withdrawn the document and cannot be counted either way, and that a document built for persons who have not been asked is not a document that has been tested against anybody, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

What he wrote, in one hand, between about half past eleven and about four in the afternoon, is this, and it is a single sheet and it is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own.

> **THIS IS THE ROLL OF THE BUILDING WITH TWO DOORS AT THE NEAR END OF THE LANE BEHIND THE BANK.**
>
> **A PERSON GOES IN THIS ROLL BY WRITING THEIR OWN NAME ON A LINE OF THEIR OWN, IN THIS ROOM, IN DAYLIGHT. NOBODY MAY WRITE A NAME FOR ANOTHER PERSON AND NOBODY MAY ASK A PERSON TO WRITE.**
>
> **NOBODY IS ASKED TO GO IN THIS ROLL. NOBODY IS REFUSED FOR NOT GOING IN IT. A PERSON WHO HAS NOT BEEN ASKED HAS NOT REFUSED AND CANNOT BE COUNTED EITHER WAY, AND THE RECORD ABOUT THE NOT ASKING SAYS NOT ASKED.**
>
> **NOT ASKED**
>
> **______________________________**
>
> **THE LINES UNDER THAT WORD ARE RULED AND THEY ARE LEFT AS THEY ARE. NOBODY IS TO BE PUT UNDER THAT WORD AND NOBODY IS TO BE ASKED WHETHER THEY WOULD LIKE TO BE.**
>
> **THIS ROLL GIVES NO FIGURE OF ANYBODY. THE FIGURE ON A SHEET AT A GATEPOST IS NOT ENTERED IN IT, IS NOT TO BE ENTERED TWICE, AND IS NOT TO BE ASKED FOR A SECOND TIME.**
>
> **THIS ROLL IS IN A ROOM A STRANGER MAY WALK UP TO AND READ. IT SAYS NOTHING ABOUT HOW A PERSON LEAVES IT, BECAUSE A PERSON LEAVES IT WHEN THEY GO.**

He got the eight lines down between about half past eleven and about two and then he had to stop for a while, and about half an hour after two he could not hold the pen upright in his right hand at all and ruled the second line, the one under that word, with the straight edge braced against his forearm and got it about a sixteenth of an inch out of true by the time it reached the edge of the sheet, and he looked at it and left it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a line ruled by a man with a forearm against a straight edge is a line and is not a name and is not a figure, and that a man who has ruled a line about a sixteenth of an inch out of true and left it has not spoiled a document, and that a document is not a person and does not have a hand.

At about half past six he carried the chair out of that building by its back with his left arm and got it down the two goes of the bank and along that lane and across that gate, and it took him about eleven minutes, where a man of about thirty-four walks it in about four, and he set it down at the end of that trestle table about four inches from where it stood this morning and sat down in it for about nine minutes. He put the sheet of print flat on the bench against the end wall before he lifted the chair, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nine minutes is a figure about nine minutes and is not a figure about anybody, and that eleven minutes is a figure about eleven minutes and is not a figure about a leg, and that the sheet of print is on the bench in that building and a stranger may walk up to the plank door of it and read it off the bench.

What had not moved on the tenth of the eighth month went in at the end of the day in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and thirty-second days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and a line ruled with a straight edge on a sheet in a building is not a line on a board. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and eleventh days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for. The rival record has been in force seventy days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered, and a charter is not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and a charter a stranger may walk up to and read is not a fourth of them and is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named. Five protected things and no sixth, and a charter is not a sixth and a ruled line is not a sixth and a chair is not a sixth. Four columns of not-askings and she did not rule one this morning and is not going to, and a column with a word over it and nothing under it on a sheet of this district's own is not a fifth column of not-askings and is not the same figure as the four.

The day came down about half past eight and the first day of the eighth month was nine days past, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and a sheet of print was on a bench in a building with two doors at the near end of a lane behind a bank, and nobody had been asked to go in it.

**AND ON THE TENTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A CHARTER WAS DRAWN IN A ROOM AND IT REGISTERS ONLY THE PEOPLE WHO CHOOSE TO ENTER AND IT CARRIES A COLUMN WITH A WORD OVER IT AND NOTHING UNDER IT AND NOBODY IS TO BE ENTERED IN IT, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT THE OLD CHARTER FOUR HUNDRED YARDS AWAY IS NOT CORRECTED BY IT AND IS NOT REPLACED BY IT AND IS NOT SUPERSEDED BY IT AND IS NOT TAKEN OUT BY IT, AND THAT A WRONG COUNT IS NOT STRUCK, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
