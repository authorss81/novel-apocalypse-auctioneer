# Chapter 332: The Night of the Twenty-First

---

Nothing came of the day until about nine in the evening, and the twenty-first of the eighth month was clear and about hard and the yard of Lot Seventeen had about nineteen people in it by ten, and the man of fifty-six had the board over his shoulder and read the middle figure out loud and got the other two right, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard and had come back from the loams the evening before with his left hand in a cloth and nobody asked him about his hands and the record about the not asking says not asked.

"**Five hundred and fifty-six days on the train. Nobody has entered a day on that board since the twenty-fourth of November and there are two hundred and seventy of those, and two hundred and thirty-one days separate the second of January and this morning. One sitting in the chair at the end of that trestle table since the tenth of this month, when it had stood a hundred and nine days, and one is not a figure and is not a habit.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a day is not a night and the twenty-first is not a date that anybody may read a night off, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district thirty-one days and has slept on that low wall on thirty-one of the thirty-one nights of that run, and that there has not been one of them he did not and nobody asked him about that either and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The building with two doors at the near end of the lane behind the bank had its plank door open on the hasp and the curtain on the nail hanging clear of it, and the two sheets of print lay on the table with four legs with the stone about the size of a fist on the near corner of them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a sheet of print on a table is a sheet of print on a table and that a door that is open is a door and not a watch and not a keeper and not a stand-in for anybody.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man who keeps a road one question at about four in the afternoon, in the ordinary voice, in about nine feet of that lane, and the question was whether he would go up that lane in the small hours and stand outside that building in the dark for about a quarter of an hour, and no sound had been said out loud to anybody in that yard and the question did not name one, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and ninety-two and it went in the minute in his own words.

The man who keeps a road said no in about four seconds, and the reason was his own, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that a man who keeps a road is not a clerk of a house, and that the eight did not move this afternoon and the man or the woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house.

"**No, and the reason is that I have already been out once. I went out to that bell in daylight on the twenty-sixth and the twenty-seventh of the seventh month over ground I had never walked and it took me two days and it brought back eleven miles, and every one of those eleven miles is a mile I could not have told you about without putting my hands on the ground in front of me. I know the edge of that sound and I know what my own boots do on it, and I am not going to stand in a lane in the dark at half past one in the morning and wait for a thing I have already been out to find out, and I am not a person anybody in this district should want holding a lantern in the dark tonight. You are asking me because you have got a building with a door in it and a rope with a knot in it and no man for either, and that is the whole of the asking and you do not have to pretend it is something else.**"

She entered that a man who has said out loud in a lane that he is not the person anybody should want has not refused to be wanted and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of that road and has never been asked to be one, and that eleven miles is a figure about a bell and about a man and is not a figure about a lane.

The man who digs loam came up that lane at about five and stood at the end of the table with four legs with his right arm hanging against his side, and that arm has not gone above the level of his own shoulder since the first of the seventh month and it is the same this evening as it was on the first of this month. He said one thing out loud at about half past five in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and forty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have got about two hundred yards of lane here and a building at the end of it with no window in it and about three feet of ditch behind it with a foot of standing water in the bottom, and I have said in that yard that I have been in nine things in this basin that I did not agree to and I am not going to be in a tenth one tonight because a rope has a knot in it. Nobody has asked me to stand in that lane tonight. I want that entered, because in nine weeks somebody will write that people stood in that lane and it will be in the same hand and it will read as though they had been asked. There are about nine people going up that lane at about nine o'clock and I am going with them as far as the ditch and I am going home, and if that bell goes at two then I will hear it from a bank four hundred yards off and that is the whole of what I have undertaken and nobody asked me for it, and I have not got a hook or a line or a lamp and I have not asked anybody in this district for one of those things this month, and I would like that written down next to the other thing that was written down, because the two of them are the same size.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that nobody asked him to be somewhere has not refused to be there and cannot be counted either way, and that he has not been asked what he is going to do about it and has said so in that yard, and that an arm that has not gone above the level of a shoulder since the first of the seventh month is a figure about an arm and is not a figure about a night.

---
The man of fifty-six said one thing out loud at about five in the afternoon, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted, and that what he said was that he was not afraid of the night and that he was afraid of the morning, and that he had said it once before in the fifteenth year and had not said it since.

About nine people went up that lane at about nine o'clock and stood about the ends of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nine is a figure about nine people and is not the figure on the sheet at that gatepost and does not make any other figure, and that a yard in which a thing is said out loud is a yard and a lane in which nine people stand in the dark is a lane, and a place where somebody is asked a question about what they want is neither of them.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against went up the bank at about half past eight, did not go into that building, went back down across that yard and out through that gate, and was not in this district again until about two in the morning, and a clerk entered that he did not go in and that nobody asked him to and that he did not say he would and that nobody in that lane has asked him.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went out past the loams at about one in the morning with his left hand in a cloth, and got up the frame of oak the way he got up it yesterday afternoon, and put the loop of the knot over the inside of his right wrist and let the rest of the rope hang, and stood there with his weight on the frame and nothing in his left hand but the cloth.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person standing on a frame is not deciding what the frame rings and that a bell does not need a person and that nobody in that lane has asked whether it should be pulled, and that it has not been pulled between the night of the twenty-fourth of the sixth month and this one, and that the last time before this it went was on the twenty-fourth of the sixth.

At about two in the morning it went.

It came out of a bell about two feet across the mouth and cracked from the rim up about a third of the way on the side that faces the loams. The frame of oak moved about an inch and came back, and the nine inches of rope went from hanging to hard in less time than it takes to look at a thing, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had his right forearm through the knot in it and his weight on the frame and his left hand with the cloth in it flat against his chest. He did not pull it. After the first half minute he was not doing anything with it at all.

Four miles off, the about nine people who had gone up that lane at nine o'clock and had not gone home found out together what nine minutes of that is. One of them said a word out loud. Nobody has said since what the word was, and nobody in that lane asked the man who said it about it.

The man who keeps a road was at the foot of the low wall and was on his feet inside the first half minute. He had said in that lane at four in the afternoon that he was not going to stand in the dark and find out where a sound of that bell stops, and there he was standing in the dark, and at about the second minute he said out loud, to nobody, that the edge of it had gone past the end of that lane and up the bank and had stopped out past a gate that is not there. He said it again at about the sixth minute and it had stopped in a different place the second time, and he did not go up the bank to look for either of the two and nobody asked him to.

The man who digs loam was on a bank four hundred yards off with his arms down at his sides, and when it reached him he sat down on the turf where he had been standing, and he was still sitting on it when it stopped and he got up off it on his own and said nothing about it to anybody.

The man of fifty-six was in a field, and he had been in a field both of the other times he had heard that bell, and a field is the worst place in this district to be standing at two in the morning. He went down into it on his forearms and stayed down.

In about four hundred yards of yard there were about nineteen people who had been standing in it since about two in the morning. The man of about sixty-four was at the end of that yard with his back to the wall. At about the fourth minute he put his right hand flat on the left side of his head and did not take it off again that night, and about nine people in that yard were near enough to see him do it and not one of them said a word about it, and the record about the not saying says not asked.

The other people in the sound of it were the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them, at the foot of the east wall of that yard with his hand-cart against it and the tent still rolled on the back of it; the man of about thirty-eight who cuts reeds; the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart; the man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing; the man of about forty with a cart of his own; the man of about forty-five who digs; a clerk of nineteen years; the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat; the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale; the woman of fifty-eight; and a child of about eight who was not asked anything by anybody in that yard.

Nobody in that sound was asked whether they heard it, and no hand went up and no name was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the second of the two terms on that table says that no person in this district is to be entered in any record of it by having heard a sound and that nobody is to be asked whether they heard one, and that that term binds nobody and stands anyway, and that the answer to the third question this yard was going to ask is not required and is not taken, and that a yard which asks nothing about a sound is not a place where a person is asked a question about what they want.

The sound did not stop. The sound went on for about five minutes and then it went on past five minutes, and about nineteen people were in it in about two hundred yards of lane and in about four hundred yards of yard, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that about nineteen people are in the sound of it and that every one of them is in the record of the second night and cannot be taken off it, and that nobody asked them.

Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and forty-third days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and nine minutes of a bell out past the loams is not a line on a board. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and twenty-second days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for. The rival record has been in force eighty-one days, read out in that yard seven times, its eighth reading refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month and unanswered, and a bell is not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and a sound that came back out of a bell is not a fourth of them. Five protected things and no sixth, and a bell is not a sixth and a rope is not a sixth and a hand on the side of a head is not a sixth. Four conditions with no end on it and no fifth was proposed on any day of this month. Four columns of not-askings, and a column with the word NOT ASKED over it and nothing under it is not a fifth of them. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week, eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and the three are three figures and are not added together, and none of the three moved on any day of this month. The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved, and the figure has stood at four hundred and eleven since the first of the sixth month, which is eighty-one days this morning, and that is a figure about the figure and is not a day-count for the sheet.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIRST OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A BELL FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS WAS PULLED A SECOND TIME SINCE THE NIGHT OF THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH, AT ABOUT TWO IN THE MORNING, FOR ABOUT NINE MINUTES, AND ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE WERE IN THE SOUND OF IT AND NOBODY ASKED WHETHER THEY HEARD IT, AND NOBODY DECIDED THAT IT SHOULD BE PULLED, AND THE REASON FOR THE KNOT IN THE ROPE IS IN THE BINDING AND NOT IN A MAN. FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN IS WHAT THE SHEET ON THAT GATEPOST SAID THIS MORNING AND IT SAID THE SAME YESTERDAY.**
