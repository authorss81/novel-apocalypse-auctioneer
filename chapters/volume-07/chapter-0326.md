# Chapter 326: Two Figures of the Same Length

---

The fifteenth of the eighth month came in about the same, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the twenty-sixth night and was in that yard about nine feet off the end of the trestle table for about an hour this morning and about nine feet off it for the rest of the day.

"**Two hundred and thirty-four days on the board and five hundred and fifty days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and sixty-four, and two hundred and twenty-five days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is fourteen days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and seventeen days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and forty-four days, and the table against that wall stood up for the fortieth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table was sat in once, on the tenth of this month, and had stood a hundred and nine days before that, and one is not a figure and is not a habit.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that fourteen days past is fourteen days past and is not a term in a ladder, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district twenty-five days and has slept on that low wall on twenty-five of the twenty-six nights of that run, and that nobody asked him about the one he did not sleep on it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

He went into the building with two doors at about eleven with the sheet of five lines of print that came off a stake in that gatepost on the seventh of the eighth month in his right hand, and put it on the bench against the end wall, and then went back up the lane and down again with a stool, and set the stool down and did not sit on it.

What he wanted to do, and what he did not do, is the whole of the fifteenth of the eighth month and a clerk of nineteen years entered it as one line and not as a paragraph. A man of about thirty-four who keeps a road counted the edge of a sound out from a bell four miles out past the loams on the twenty-sixth and twenty-seventh of the seventh month and brought back eleven miles, and the sheet nailed to a gatepost in that yard says the people are in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off this district, and the two figures are the same length, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against had both of them on the bench in front of him at about half past eleven with a clerk of nineteen years standing in the room, and he did not put them together, and he did not write one of them next to the other, and he stood there for about forty minutes and then put the five lines of print back inside his coat and said out loud that he was not going to do it.

He had the whole of it in his head by about one and it was the only thing in it. Two numbers the same length, one of them walked out on two days by a man who had never walked that ground and who had not been asked to and who had paid for it in his legs, and the other one printed by a body four hundred miles off that has never been in this district and that this district cannot see. If they are the same length then the ground the sheet is about is the ground that sound went over, and if the sound went over that ground then everybody standing on it heard it, and everybody standing on it is not the four hundred and eleven, because the four hundred and eleven is the people who answered a door. Put those two things in one line and you have a list that is bigger than four hundred and eleven and every name in it is the name of somebody who did not come to a door, and a man who says that name out loud in a yard is handing it to a body that is already holding a count of answerers, and a name said out loud is a consent the person it belongs to did not give. He had all of that by one o'clock. He also had, by about ten past one, the other half of it, and the other half was that he did not know one single thing about the night of the twenty-fourth of the sixth month except that it had happened, and that the only man in this district who was in the sound of it was standing about nine feet off the end of that trestle table at this moment with his hands on his knees, and that the man had not been asked one question about that night by anybody in this district, and that he was not going to be the first one.

The man of fifty-six said one thing about it at about two, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and sixteen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been in this yard five months saying that a door is not a keeper and a wall is not an address and I have never in my life put two numbers together that somebody else had walked out, because a man of about thirty-four walked eleven miles on two days for nothing and I am not going to be the man who takes the two of them and makes a morning out of it. Two figures of the same length are two figures. Nobody put one of them there. That is the whole of what I have got and I have not got an argument and I am not going to have one today.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he is not going to put two figures together has not made a finding and cannot be counted either way, and that a clerk who could have made a finding out of two figures and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing, and that that is entered against the clerk and not against the man of fifty-six.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing at about four, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people at the end of that trestle table, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and ninety-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**There is a list that is bigger than four hundred and eleven and I cannot recommend it and I cannot refuse it and I am not the one holding it, and I have spent five hours this morning finding that out and I am going to say the finding out loud so that it is on a page somewhere and not only in me. A number made by knocking is a number about the people who answered a door. A number made by walking is a number about a length. A number made by listening would be a third number, and I have got no way of taking one of them off another, and neither has anybody in this district, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam said in a yard in the sixth month that a subtraction is arithmetic and not a finding and he was right then and he is right now. I am not going to make it. I am not going to stop anybody making it. I am going to write down that I had it in my hand at half past eleven this morning and put it down.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he is not going to make a finding has not made one and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to stop anybody else making one has not refused anybody's finding and cannot be counted either way, and that a number made by listening is a third number and that the three are three numbers and are not added together, and that a bell is a voice and a voice reaches everybody within the sound of it and a list of who heard it is a list of who existed on a night, and that neither of those is a figure this district can price, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The rest of that afternoon went the way the afternoons of this month have gone. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the eight lines of the roll out loud in that yard at about half past five and got through all of them. The man of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the bank with about four people being weighed one after another and did not come up the lane. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she did not come up the lane, and that a woman who has not come up a lane has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and that she is not a keeper of that lane and is not a keeper of a scale and is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything.

The man of about sixty-four was asked nothing by anybody in that yard on the fifteenth of the eighth month. A clerk of nineteen years entered that, and that a man who carries a message from one place to another on his own two feet is not a keeper of it and does not become one by standing in a yard for a day, and that he has been in this district twenty-five days and has not been asked one question about the night of the twenty-fourth of the sixth month by anybody in it, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood about nine feet off him for about forty minutes at about one o'clock and did not ask him anything, and that nine feet is a figure about nine feet and is not a figure about either of them.

What had not moved on the fifteenth of the eighth month went in at the end of the day in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and thirty-seventh days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and two figures of the same length are not a line on a board. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week, eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and the three are three figures and are not added together, and none of the three is a tenth or a ninth or an eighth of the thing that was written this month, and the two refusals in that yard this month are not a figure about any of them. The rival record has been in force seventy-five days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered, and two figures of the same length are not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and a finding that was not made is not a document and did not become a fourth. Five protected things and no sixth, and a number made by listening is not a sixth and a stool is not a sixth and a room is not a sixth.

The day came down about half past eight and the first day of the eighth month was fourteen days past, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the number on the five lines of print in that coat was still the same number it has been since the seventh of the eighth month.

**AND ON THE FIFTEENTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD HAD WALKED THE EDGE OF A SOUND OUT AND BROUGHT BACK ELEVEN MILES AND A SHEET AT A GATEPOST SAYS ELEVEN MILES, AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST HAD BOTH OF THEM ON A BENCH IN A ROOM AND DID NOT PUT THEM TOGETHER, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT A CLERK WHO COULD HAVE MADE A FINDING OUT OF TWO FIGURES AND DID NOT IS NOT A CLERK WHO HAS FOUND NOTHING, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
