# Chapter 320: Nothing

---

The ninth of the eighth month came in about the same and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the twentieth night.

"**Two hundred and twenty-eight days on the board and five hundred and forty-four days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and fifty-eight, and two hundred and nineteen days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is eight days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and eleven days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and thirty-eight days, and the table against that wall stood up for the thirty-fourth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood a hundred and eight days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that eight days past is eight days past and is not a term in a ladder, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man of about sixty-four came into that gate on the morning of the twenty-first of the seventh month and has been in this district nineteen days, a stay being counted without the day he arrived on, and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning on the ninth of the eighth month, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that eight days past is not an answer and is not a finding.

---

He took the sheet of five lines of print off the end of that trestle table at about half past ten, in front of about nineteen people, without asking anybody for it and without saying anything about it, and put it inside his coat against his chest on the side away from the arm that had carried a table eleven miles twice, and went out of that gate and up the lane, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing watched him go and said nothing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who carries a message from one place to another on his own two feet has taken a sheet of print off a table in a yard and that nobody asked him to and nobody asked him not to and the record about the not asking says not asked.

It took him about nine minutes to get up the two goes of that bank, which is about two hundred yards of soft ground at the top of it, and he stopped at the top of the first go and stood there for about a minute and a half with his hand on his knee and then went on, and stopped at the top of the second one and stood there for about a minute, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that about nine minutes is a figure about about nine minutes and is not a figure about a bank and is not a figure about two hundred yards of soft ground and is not a price of anything, and that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walks that bank in about four minutes and is a man of about thirty-four and is not a figure about the man of about sixty-four, and that the two figures are not added together.

There is a step of two stones outside the door of that room and a door with a handle and a fanlight over it that lights nothing, and he stood on that step with the sheet under his arm for about four minutes and did not put a hand on the handle and did not knock, and then he crouched down and put the sheet of five lines of print flat on the top step, squared it with the edge of a boot, and stood up and looked at it from about two feet off for a while, and then went back down the bank, and it took him about eleven minutes, and he came back up that lane at about twenty past twelve and sat down at the foot of the low wall along it with his coat folded beside him and his hands on his knees, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about where he had been.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a sheet of print laid on the top step of two stones outside a door with a shut door on it is not in a book, and that a stranger may walk up to that step and read it, and that nobody in this district has asked whether anybody will, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a man who carries a message from one place to another on his own two feet is not a keeper of that sheet and does not become one and has not been asked to be one, and that he did not knock and the record about the not knocking says not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said one thing about the bank at about one, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and sixty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have watched that man be in this district for nineteen days now and be somewhere I could not see him for part of most of them, and nobody in this yard has asked him one question about any of it, and I have got the strongest reason of anybody here to ask him one and I am not going to. I keep four miles of road. I have not asked him which way he went and I am not going to, and a road is not a thing anybody is on just because they are on it, and he is not a man on my road. He came down a lane this morning and went up a bank and stood on a step and came back down and he did it in nine minutes and it took me four, and neither of those numbers is about him and both of them are about legs, and a leg is not a figure.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of that road and has never been asked to be one, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to ask a question has not refused to be asked and cannot be counted either way, and that a leg is not a figure and two legs are two legs and not a figure and are not a price of anything.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing at about two, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A sheet of print that came off a stake in that gatepost yesterday morning and has lain on the end of that table since about ten yesterday went up a bank this morning on a man's own two feet and is lying on a step of two stones four hundred yards from here, and a stranger can walk up to that step and read all five of the lines off it, and that is the only thing that has been done to it since it came off that stake, and it was not done by the man who read it out and it was not done by me. I have read it out three times and all three of them were yesterday, and I have not been asked what it is for and I am not going to be, and the man who carried it has not said one word about where it came from and has not said one word about who left it on the stake. I am not going to follow him up that bank. That is a road and I have not got a leg for it today and I have not been asked to go.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to follow a person has not refused to follow him and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a document and is not a bearer of anything, and that a leg is not a figure, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The rest of that day went the way the days of this month have gone. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart read the five lines of print out loud in about nine feet of lane outside the room four hundred yards away to about four people at about half past four, from a step, with the sheet in his hand, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat walked down to the foot of the step and listened to the whole of it and asked him one question about the third line and he answered it and she went back up the lane. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who drives a cart is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a document, and that a woman who keeps a goat asked one question about a line of print on a step and the question was about the line and not about the man, and that a question asked in a lane about a line of print is not a question asked in a yard about what a person wants and the two are not the same thing, and that nobody asked the man of about sixty-four whether he had heard either of them read.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the bank with about four people being weighed one after another and did not come up the lane. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was at the end of that trestle table at about six and did not pick anything up. The tent was still rolled on the back of that hand-cart against the wall inside that gate, and the chair at the end of the trestle table was still empty, and a clerk entered that a tent rolled on a hand-cart and a chair nobody has sat in are the same this evening as they were on the thirty-first of the seventh month, and that neither of them is a figure about a man.

What had not moved on the ninth of the eighth month went in at the end of the day in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and thirty-first days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood a hundred and eight days and nobody has sat in it on any of them, and a man sat down on the end of that table on the sixteenth of the seventh month and not in the chair, and those are two different things. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and tenth days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for. The rival record has been in force sixty-nine days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered. Three documents this district does not own, and a sheet of print on a step of two stones is not a fourth of them and is not a document anybody has asked for. Four columns of not-askings and she did not rule one this morning and is not going to. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week, eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and the three are three figures and are not added together, and a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book refused two things in this month and neither of the two is a tenth or a ninth or an eighth of anything.

The day came down about half past eight and the eighth month was nine days old, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the table eleven miles off was still standing against a wall about four feet high with nobody going to put it up, and the book four hundred yards away still had a shut door on the room it is in and nobody had been asked to write in it, and a sheet of five lines of print was lying on the top step of two stones outside that door where a stranger could walk up and read it, and nobody had asked whether anybody would.

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN, THE TABLE ELEVEN MILES OFF WAS STILL STANDING AGAINST A WALL ABOUT FOUR FEET HIGH, THE BOOK FOUR HUNDRED YARDS AWAY STILL HAD A SHUT DOOR ON THE ROOM IT IS IN AND NOBODY HAD BEEN ASKED TO WRITE IN IT, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT A DAY PASSING IS NOT AN ANSWER AND THAT THIS DISTRICT STILL DOES NOT KNOW WHETHER IT HAS BEEN STOPPED OR IS LATE, AND THAT A DISTRICT WHICH HAS LOOKED AT THE SAME THREE THINGS FOR TEN DAYS AND CHANGED NONE OF THEM HAS FOUND OUT WHAT A DAY IS WORTH, AND THAT IS NOT NOTHING.**
