# Chapter 313: What a Day Like That Does to a Yard

---

The second of the eighth month came in about the same and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the chair at the end of that table was where it has stood for a hundred and one days, and the board outside the room four hundred yards from that gate was not washed on the twenty-fourth of December and was not washed this morning either.

"**Two hundred and twenty-one days on the board and five hundred and thirty-seven days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and fifty-one, and two hundred and twelve days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is one day past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and four days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and thirty-one days, and the table against that wall stood up for the twenty-seventh time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood a hundred and one days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that one day past is one day past and is not a term in a ladder, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a day on which a thing is past is a day and not a person and cannot be counted either way, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district twelve days, a stay being counted without the day he arrived on.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the five lines of print down flat on the end of that trestle table at about half past ten, squared them with the edge of his right hand, and read nothing out loud, and about nine people stood in that yard and waited for him to start, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who reads things out loud in yards read nothing out loud on the second of the eighth month, and that a man not reading a sheet aloud in a yard on one morning is not a refusal to read and did not become a tenth of anything.

"**I read those five lines out loud on the twelfth of last month and I have read them to about nineteen people in this yard about four times since and a man of about twenty-three read them in a lane outside the room four hundred yards from this gate on the thirtieth of the seventh month, and yesterday morning the day on a post came and went and nothing came with it, and this morning I have got nothing to read that I have not read.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that he has got nothing to read has not refused to read and cannot be counted either way, and that a document that is read out in a yard over and over stops being a thing anybody is listening to after a while, and that a man who has said that has not counted the while and did not count it this morning, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

Nobody wrote on the board outside the room all day. It stood about four feet off the end of the trestle table on its own two legs with a nail in the top of it and the twenty-fourth of December still on it under the dust, and there is a piece of chalk tied to one of those legs on a string about a foot long, and about nine people looked at that board during the day and none of them put a hand on the chalk, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a piece of chalk on a string is not a document and is not a fourth of the three this district does not own, and that a board nobody has written on is a board and not a calendar and that a man looking at one is not a man writing on one.

At about eleven the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up the lane and stopped at the end of that trestle table and put his right hand out flat, palm up, under the piece of paper with two lines of print on it and the chalk mark of a third one started, and got his fingers to the near edge of it, and could not get it up off the wood, and tried it a second time with the same hand and could not get it up off the wood, and took it off with the other hand at the bottom corner and dragged it toward himself about nine inches and let go of it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that an arm that has not gone above the level of that shoulder since the first of the seventh month has not gone above the level of that shoulder on the second of the eighth month either, and that a hand will do a thing an arm cannot do, and that nobody in that yard said one word about it and the record about the not saying says not asked.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was at the foot of that table from about half past eleven and asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing for a piece of paper and a stub of chalk, and got both, and copied the five lines of print onto the piece of paper between about half past eleven and about two, and got four of the five of them on clean and the fifth one stopped about two thirds of the way down and would not go any further, and put the chalk down on the end of that trestle table at about two, and said one thing out loud to nobody, and it was not counted. A clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted, and that a hand which has not closed since the thirteenth of the sixth month did not close on the second of the eighth month, and that a copy is not a document and did not become a fourth of the three, and that a man who copies for nothing is not a clerk of a house and is not paid and is not a bearer of anything.

"**I have been able to hold a chalk for about two and a half hours today and I got four lines of that and a bit, and the bit is the fifth line and I am not going to go over it again, because a copy that is not the thing is a copy and a copy of a fifth line I have only got two thirds of is a copy of nothing. I am not going to be the man who reads it out wrong. Somebody else reads it and I will hand it to whoever wants it.**"

The chair at the end of the trestle table was moved about four inches by somebody at about two and put back about four inches by the same somebody at about half past two, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a chair that has stood a hundred and one days without anybody sitting in it can be moved four inches and put back four inches in one day and that the day it was moved is not the day anybody sat in it, and that a man sitting down on the end of that table has not sat in the chair.

---

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was against the east wall with his hand-cart and the tent still rolled on the back of it when he said one thing out loud at about two, in the ordinary voice, to about four people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and sixty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That table has stood against that wall for twenty-seven mornings. I know what every one of those twenty-seven is because a man counts them out loud in a yard every morning and I have never once been asked to check one of them and I am not going to start today. It went up on the seventh of this month at about eight in the morning with one person standing at it, and I cannot set it up on my own, and I am not going to take it down, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam carried boards out to it four times and has not told me one word about it and I am not going to ask him this afternoon. Twenty-seven is a figure about a table. I am a figure about a man who cannot use two hands and that is a different figure and the two of them are not added together and neither one of them is me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about thirty-seven is not a keeper of a table and has never been asked to be one, and that a man who says out loud that he cannot set a thing up on his own has not refused to do anything and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said one thing at about half past three to about nine people and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fifty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Yesterday the before came off that day and today the day is a day past, and the number on that post has not moved and has not been going to move, and the only thing that has changed in this district since the first of the sixth month is that a sheet has stopped having a before in front of it. That is not a finding about anybody and it is not a figure about a person and I have no way to set it beside the four hundred and eleven and I am not going to try. I said yesterday that not knowing is a finding, and a clerk wrote it down, and I have thought about that word all morning and I am not going to take it back. I just want it entered that the finding is about us and not about them, because there is nobody on that sheet to be a finding about.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has said out loud that a finding is about a district and not about a body has not withdrawn anything he said yesterday and cannot be counted either way.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was at the foot of the low wall along the lane for about two hours in the middle of the day and then went back up that lane on his own two feet, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a leg which has eleven miles and a ditch about three feet deep in it from two days on foot in the last third of the seventh month is a leg and not a figure, and that nobody in that yard asked him how it was and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The rest of the afternoon went the way the afternoon of the first of this month went. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing sat down on the end of that trestle table at about three for about half an hour with his legs under it and did not read anything out loud while he was sitting on it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man sitting down on the end of that table on the second of the eighth month has not sat in the chair, and that the chair has stood a hundred and one days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.

What had not moved on the second of the eighth month went in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and twenty-fourth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and nobody put a line on one this morning. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and third days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that out of a ladder and did not ask him for it, and he has not been in that yard since the twenty-first of the seventh month and has not been asked for it. The rival record has been in force sixty-two days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered, and the second of the eighth month did not answer it. Five protected things and no sixth, and a copy is not a sixth and a piece of chalk is not a sixth and a table standing for twenty-seven mornings is not a sixth. Four conditions with no end on it and no fifth proposed. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and neither figure is the other figure and the two are not added together. Three documents this district does not own, and the piece of paper with four lines and a bit on it is not a fourth of them and a man who did not read out this morning is not a fourth of them.

The day came down about half past eight and the eighth month was two days old and the first day of the eighth month was one day past, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the board outside the room was still unwashed, and the chair at the end of that trestle table was still empty.

**AND ON THE SECOND OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING READ NOTHING OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN FOR THE FIRST TIME IN A MONTH, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN SAID OUT LOUD THAT TWENTY-SEVEN MORNINGS IS A FIGURE ABOUT A TABLE AND NOT ABOUT A MAN WHO CANNOT SET IT UP ON HIS OWN, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID THAT THE ONLY THING THAT HAD CHANGED IN THIS DISTRICT SINCE THE FIRST OF THE SIXTH MONTH WAS THAT A SHEET HAS STOPPED HAVING A BEFORE IN FRONT OF IT, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
