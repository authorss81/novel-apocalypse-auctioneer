# Chapter 333: Eleven Minutes After

---

The twenty-second of the eighth month came in about two hundred yards of lane with about four minutes of that sound still in it, and about nine people were standing in the lane and about four hundred yards of yard, and nobody had opened a door and nobody had shut one, and the plank door of the building with two doors was standing open on the hasp exactly as it had been standing open at nine o'clock the night before.

"**Two hundred and forty-one days on the board, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-one days past this morning. The table against that wall stood up for the forty-seventh time this morning. A man sat in the chair at the end of that trestle table on the tenth of this month, and it had stood a hundred and nine days before that, and one is neither a figure nor a habit.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a sound of about nine minutes is a figure about nine minutes and is not a figure about anybody, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district thirty-two days and has slept on that low wall on thirty-one of the thirty-two nights of that run, and nobody asked him about the one he did not and the record about the not asking says not asked. She entered that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning, and that somebody who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the eight.

---

The last four minutes of it were not the same sound as the first five and about nine people in that yard said so out loud in the ordinary voice within about ninety seconds of each other, not to one another, and then stopped saying it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that what came back came back as sound and is not a paper and is not in a book and is not in either of the two books four hundred yards from that gate, and that a record of a room is not a record of a night, and that a list of who came into a building is not a list of who heard a bell, and that a sound which about nine people in a yard all call the same is not the same to anybody who was not in it, and that the eight lines on the bench against the end wall of that building say the same thing on their own face and have said it since the tenth of this month.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came down off the frame of oak at about ten past two and had a mark on the inside of his right forearm where a knot had been for about five minutes, and he walked the four miles back in the dark with the cloth in his left hand, and got into the yard of Lot Seventeen at about half past three. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a mark on a forearm from a knot in a rope is a mark on a forearm and is not a figure about a night, and that a man who held a rope is not a keeper of the rope and has not been asked to be one.

The sound stopped at about nine minutes past two. Eleven minutes after it stopped the man of about sixty-four said one thing out loud in that yard in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and nineteen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have lost the ear on the left side and I have not got it back and I am not going to be the man who lets somebody else work it out on some later morning. I said on the twenty-ninth of the seventh month in that yard that I would rather everybody knew a thing about my body on a day I named than have it come out of a conversation on some later afternoon, and tonight is the day I named and this is the thing. It went at about the fourth minute of it and every minute after that has been on the other side of it and I was standing in the middle of nine minutes of the loudest noise I have stood in since I was thirty-one and I am telling about nineteen people now, at eleven minutes after, because I told them I would and because a man who carries a message from one place to another on his own two feet is going to have to hear a great deal more of these than I am going to want to hear, and that is not a grief and I am not going to stand in a yard and make it one. I am telling you and I am going to sit down.**"

She entered that a man who says out loud in a yard what has been taken off his body has not made a finding and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that an ear is a figure about an ear and is not a figure about a man, and that a man who carries a message is not a keeper of it and does not become one and is not redeemed and is not a failure, and that the standing of a man in a yard at about twenty minutes past two in the morning is a standing and is not a post.

He sat down on the low wall along the lane, and that is where he had slept thirty-one of the thirty-two nights of his run, and a clerk entered that a man sitting down on a wall is not a figure about a wall.

The man of fifty-six came out of a field at about a quarter past two and was in the last of it and was in that yard by half past two, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six heard that bell twice in the fifteenth year and was in a field both times and went and looked at it and was told nothing either time, and that he is in a field on this side of it as well and went and looked at it as well and has been told nothing again, and that the two of those are the same shape and not a third shape, and that a shape noticed a second time is not a finding and a third time is a habit, and that a man who said a sentence in the fifteenth year and has said it again in the eighteenth has not made a finding out of either.

He said one thing out loud at about three in the morning, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and forty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I said this in the fifteenth year, out loud, in a field, and I said it again at five this afternoon in that yard, and I am saying it a third time tonight out loud in the same yard, and I am not going to pretend to anybody that saying it three times makes it a finding. I am not afraid of the night. I have been afraid of the morning for three years and tonight I have found out what I am actually afraid of and it is not the morning either. I am afraid of the day after the morning, when everybody will have had a night to think about and there will be a thing on a table in that yard with a list on it. And I will say the other half of it out loud because I said the other half out loud in the fifteenth year and a clerk wrote it down then, and a man of fifty-six who cannot write down what he is afraid of has no business being careful in one direction only, and that is what I am, and I have said so about myself about a dozen times in four months. I am not a clerk. Do not put this in a column and do not put my name over it. I said it in a yard and it is a thing a man said in a yard, and that is the whole of what it is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a sentence said in the fifteenth year and again in the eighteenth is a shape noticed a second time and is not a finding, and that the second time it was said this afternoon it was not a finding either, and that the fourth line of the offer that has been on a table in this district for six months was not read out loud tonight and is not going to be read out loud in this district until somebody says out loud why not and is told why not, and that the refusals to read are nine.

---

He came out of the gate at about ten past four in the morning into a yard with the sun about a hand's breadth off the top of the far wall and about nineteen people standing in it who had been standing in it since about two in the morning, and he asked nobody what had happened, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has not asked is not a man who has been told, and that nobody in that yard at ten past four in the morning was asked whether he had heard anything.

She entered that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against went out of that gate at about eight in the evening the night before and was not in this district until about two this morning and slept from about two until about four, and that his right hand has not closed properly since the tenth of this month and has not slept since the night of the fourteenth and that neither of those is a reason and that neither of them is a finding, and that a sound which goes eleven miles did not wake a man four hundred yards off it, and that the eleven miles is a figure about a bell and the four hundred yards is a figure about a man and the two are not added together and that nobody in this district has a way of drawing a line, and that a clerk who could have made a finding out of a sound and a distance and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing.

He stood at the end of that trestle table for about nine minutes before he could look at anything on it, because the yard was too bright after the dark and he had to stand with his back to it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up the bank at about five with the cloth in his left hand and said nothing at all to him and went past him into the yard and stood about nine feet off the end of the table the other way, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nine feet is a figure about nine feet and is not about either of them.

The man who keeps a road was at the foot of the low wall all morning and nobody in that yard asked him one question about eleven miles, and that is a figure about a month and not about a man, and he said nothing about the eight days he was on the road in the seventh month to anybody in this district and has not been asked.

Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and forty-fourth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and a sound of about nine minutes is not a line on a board. A child of about eight was in the sound of it and was not asked anything by anybody in that yard, and a clerk entered that a child is not a figure about the second night and is not a refusal of anything and that nobody in that yard said one word to him about the four minutes of it that were left. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and twenty-second days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for. The rival record has been in force eighty-two days and is unanswered, and a sound is not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and what came back on that bell is not a fourth of them and did not become one. Five protected things and no sixth, and an ear is not a sixth and a knot is not a sixth. The third column of a covenant in a book a stranger may walk up to is still ruled and still empty, and a ninth charter's line and the ninth place on a page out of a cave are still ruled and still empty, and a night is not a third column and does not fill any of the three. The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved.

It was about half past eight before the light was off the far wall, and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-one days past. About nineteen people were in that yard who had been in that yard at two in the morning, and the sheet on that gatepost still said four hundred and eleven.

**AND IN THE NIGHT OF THE TWENTY-FIRST OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A BELL FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS WENT FOR ABOUT NINE MINUTES AND ON THE MORNING OF THE TWENTY-SECOND A MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-FOUR LOST AN EAR IN IT AND SAID SO OUT LOUD IN A YARD ELEVEN MINUTES AFTERWARDS, AND A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX SAID IN A YARD A SENTENCE HE SAID IN THE FIFTEENTH YEAR, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT A SHAPE NOTICED A SECOND TIME IS NOT A FINDING. THE RECORD OF THE SECOND NIGHT CAME BACK AS SOUND AND IS NOT A PAPER AND IS NOT IN A BOOK. FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN IS WHAT THE SHEET ON THAT GATEPOST SAID THIS MORNING.**
