# Chapter 308: Four Days

---

The twenty-eighth of the seventh month came in white and got the heat up early and stayed on it, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of fifty-six read the board out from the end of the trestle table with the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing holding it flat in front of him and got all three of the figures, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was at the gate trying to get back up the two goes of the bank into the lane.

"**Two hundred and sixteen days on the board and five hundred and thirty-two days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and forty-six, and two hundred and seven days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is four days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is ninety-nine days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and twenty-six days, and the table against that wall stood up for the twenty-second time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood ninety-six days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that four is three days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man who went out of that gate at about ten to five on the morning of the twenty-fourth and came back into it at about half past six in the morning of the twenty-sixth was out of this district for two days and a night, and that those two days and a night are not the four days from the twenty-fourth to this morning, and that the two are not added to the four.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the whole of the ladder out loud at about half past ten in the ordinary voice to about nine people, standing about four feet back from the end of the trestle table, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and twenty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Four days. I am going to say all of it out loud, once, and then I am not going to say it again this month, and I would like a clerk to write down that I said it once. The ladder off the first day of the eighth month, counted off one morning in this yard at a time, is eleven, ten, nine, eight, seven, six, five, four, three, two, and the first of those ten was counted off the twenty-first of this month and the last of them is the count off the thirty-first of this month, and the day after that is the first of the eighth month and it does not have a figure in front of it at all. Eleven was this morning seven days ago. Ten was the twenty-second. Nine was the twenty-third, which is the day that man was asked to go out. Eight was the twenty-fourth, which is the day that bell could have been pulled and the day that man went out. Seven was the twenty-fifth, which is the day that man went down into a ditch. Six was the twenty-sixth, which is the day he gave the figure. Five was yesterday. And this morning is four. That is the whole of it and it has taken four minutes and about nine people have heard all of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a ladder is a figure about a ladder and is not about anybody, and that a day is not a person and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road to say how he felt about any of the ten of those mornings says not asked, and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about half past ten in the morning on the twenty-eighth of the seventh month.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road got back up the two goes of the bank at about a quarter past eleven, on the second attempt, and came and sat down at the end of that trestle table and put his leg out in front of him under the table, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who cannot get up a bank the first time has not refused the bank, and that about nine people in that yard watched him do it and that nine is a figure about nine people.

Then he said one thing about it, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and ninety-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I went up that bank twice in one week and I have told this yard on the eighteenth of this month that I cannot tell a habit from a figure and that I looked for a test for six days and did not find one, and I want to add the other half of it this morning before somebody adds it for me on some later day. I have got eleven miles in my legs from two days on foot and a ditch about three feet deep in it, and I did not fall in that ditch and I got out of it. My leg is not a figure. A leg is not a number and it is not on a board and it cannot be put beside another leg and added to it, and if a man four hundred miles off could count legs the way he counts doors then he would have a number about this district by now and he has not got one, and that is the only good news anybody in this yard has had in a month. I am not going to walk out there again. Nobody asked me to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a leg is not a figure and is not a price of anything and is not a figure about anybody, and that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of that road and has never been asked to be one, and that a man who has said out loud that his body is what a thing cost has not consented to the thing and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The woman of fifty-eight was at the bank with two buckets at about half past twelve and stayed about forty minutes and said one thing to nobody in particular while she was filling the second of them, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted, that two buckets were not counted, that nobody counted them, and that the record about the not counting says not asked.

"**Four days, and I am not going to stand at this bank and count anything down to anything. I have carried water to eleven houses for nineteen years and I have not counted them once in this district and I am not going to start on the fourth day. There is a house in the shadow of a building eleven miles off that is the ninth of the eleven, and I said in a yard on the sixth of this month that I was not going to say one word about her again, and I said it on the eighteenth, and that was twice, and I am not going to make it three. Four is a figure. That is all it is this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman of fifty-eight who carries water to eleven houses is not a keeper of anything, and that no figure about her eleven houses was produced on the twenty-eighth of the seventh month, and that a house in the ground is not one of the four hundred and eleven on the sheet at that gatepost and is not on any sheet in this district, and that the ninth of the eleven is one of the five different ninths in this district and that the five are not added together, and that a figure a woman of fifty-eight has twice refused to put on a bank is not a figure about the bank and is not a figure about the day.

The table against the wall about four feet high eleven miles off this district stood up for the twenty-second time this morning and nobody is going to put it up, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam was not in that yard and had not stood at the end of that trestle table since the twenty-sixth of this month, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who said out loud on the twentieth of this month that he was not going to tell the man it belonged to has not told him, and that the record about the not telling says not asked.

About four people were in that yard at about four and about nine had gone up the lane, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud once at about half past four for a woman who had come down the lane on hearing that there was a man in that yard who walked roads, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road stayed where he was and listened to it with his leg still out in front of him under the table, and the man of about sixty-four stood at the foot of the low wall about nine feet from that woman and did not come any nearer to her than that.

The woman said thank you and went back up the lane and nobody asked her what she had come down for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman came down a lane and was read to and went back up it and that is not a refusal to read and not a consent to read, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing about that at about five and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and twenty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A woman came down that lane this afternoon to be read to and she is gone again, and I have read the same five lines to about nineteen people in this yard on and off since the twelfth of this month and I have not once asked any of them what they thought, and I asked this one nothing and I am not going to. I would like it entered that a man who reads things out loud for a living in a yard has now told this yard twice in ten days that he is not going to ask, and that I do not know what a reader is for and I have not got a better version of that either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a document and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he does not know what a thing is for has not refused the thing and cannot be counted either way, and that a shape of that kind noticed a second time in ten days is not a finding, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

She entered what had not moved on the twenty-eighth of the seventh month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and nineteenth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood ninety-six days and nobody has sat in it on any of them. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the ninety-eight days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that out of a ladder and did not ask him, and he is a man of about forty-eight and not thirty-four and has said of himself that he is fifty-one. The rival record has been in force fifty-seven days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of last month, and it is unanswered. Three documents this district does not own and no document came into that yard today. Five protected things and no sixth, and a table against a wall eleven miles off is not a sixth. The instruments this district has built and not named are six, and a ladder of ten figures is not a seventh of them. Four conditions with no end on it, thirteen refusals to join two things and the registrar's own count of twelve, nine refusals to read with the departure unspent and no date in the ledger, five boards and six lines full, four columns of not-askings and no fifth ruled. No number on the sheet at that gatepost moved today and four hundred and eleven is four hundred and eleven, and the eleven miles on that sheet and the eleven miles a man counted on the twenty-sixth are two figures and are not added together.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was twenty-eight days old and the first day of the eighth month was four days off, and nothing that had been counted down to it had moved a day nearer it than it was.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH THE WHOLE OF THE LADDER OFF THE FIRST DAY OF THE EIGHTH MONTH WAS READ OUT LOUD ONCE IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AND WAS ELEVEN, TEN, NINE, EIGHT, SEVEN, SIX, FIVE AND FOUR, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD COULD NOT GET BACK UP THE TWO GOES OF THE BANK INTO THE LANE ON THE FIRST ATTEMPT AND GOT UP ON THE SECOND, AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT A LEG IS NOT A FIGURE.**
