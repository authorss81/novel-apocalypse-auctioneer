# Chapter 314: Sixty-Three Days

---

The third of the eighth month came in about the same and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was not in that yard at ten and had not been in it since about eight, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard knew where he was and that the record about the not knowing says not asked.

"**Two hundred and twenty-two days on the board and five hundred and thirty-eight days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and fifty-two, and two hundred and thirteen days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is two days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and five days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and thirty-two days, and the table against that wall stood up for the twenty-eighth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood a hundred and two days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two days past is two days past and is not a term in a ladder, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district thirteen days, a stay being counted without the day he arrived on, and that a day a man is not in this district is not a day he has been here.

The man of fifty-six said one thing at about half past ten, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and sixty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That rival record took effect on the first of the sixth month and it has been in force sixty-three days. I am the man who reads the board out in this yard and I have read that figure out loud four times in three weeks and I have not read the record out loud once in two months, and the reason is that it was read out seven times and the eighth time a man refused it out loud with a reason, and a refused reading is not a reading, and I am not going to be the man who makes it an eighth by asking for one. Sixty-three days is a figure about a record. It is not a reading of the record and it is not an answer to the record and it does not move one, and a figure about how long something has been true is not a thing anybody can refuse, and I would like both of those written down in the same hand.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure of days about a record is not a reading of that record, and that the record has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month and a refused reading is not a reading, and that the record is in force sixty-three days and is unanswered, and that this day did not answer it and did not refuse it and is not a tenth refusal of anything.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing about that at about eleven, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A thing that has been refused once is a thing that somebody is going to try again eventually, and the way it gets tried is by somebody saying the words *read it out* in a yard in a voice that has decided in advance. Nobody has said those two words in this yard since the twenty-fourth of the sixth month and I have been listening for them every morning since, and I would like it entered that I have been listening, and that listening for a thing is not the same as asking for it and I have not asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that he has been listening for two words has not asked for them and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a document and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

He came back up the lane at about half past two and came into that yard through a gate that takes two goes and did not stop at the trestle table and went and sat down on the low wall along the lane at about four minutes past three, and about nine people were in that yard and none of them asked him anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard asked that man one question between eight in the morning and four minutes past three in the afternoon, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a man who has been somewhere and has come back has not refused to say where he was and cannot be counted either way.

At about four a clerk entered where he had been, out of what a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat said at the foot of her own gate at the third house up that lane from the scale, which was that a man of about sixty-four had gone down that lane past her about eight in the morning and had been at the near end of the lane behind the bank about one.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a building with two doors at the near end of that lane is about nine feet of brick and no window, and that it has a plank door in the end wall with a nail in a hasp that has not moved since the eleventh of the sixth month, and a curtain on a nail, and a table with four legs against the outside wall, and a ditch behind it about three feet deep with about a foot of standing water in it, and that a man stood at that table for about twenty minutes and did not put a hand on the hasp and did not lift the curtain, and that nobody asked him one question at that table, and that a table with four legs outside a door is not a counter until somebody asks somebody a question at it, and that nobody asked anybody anything at it, and that a building is not a shelter and a shelter is not a building and neither of them is a fourth of the three documents this district does not own.

There is about a foot of standing water in the bottom of that ditch and it has been in the bottom of it all month, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about sixty-four stood at the top of that ditch for about nine minutes at about half past twelve on the third of the eighth month and did not go down into it, and that a man standing at the top of a ditch is not a man in a ditch, and that nobody has asked him why he stood there and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that about nine minutes is a figure about about nine minutes and is not a figure about a man and is not a price of anything.

---

The rest of that afternoon went quietly and about four people were still in that yard at six. The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was asked by the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat whether he had eaten, and said he had, and had not, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said two answers to one question has not been asked a second question and cannot be counted either way, and that a woman who keeps a goat is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a goat that is not there.

The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was in that yard from about ten and went up the lane at about half past two and read the five lines of print out loud in about nine feet of lane outside the room four hundred yards from that gate to about four people, with his ordinary voice and with the piece of paper with four lines and a bit of a fifth on it in his other hand, and nobody stopped him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man whose hand has not closed since the thirteenth of the sixth month read all five lines out loud on the third of the eighth month and got through all of them, and that a voice is not a copy and is not a document and did not become a fourth of the three.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that yard at about six and put his left hand flat on the end of the trestle table and left it there for about a minute and a half and picked up the piece of paper with two lines of print and the chalk mark of a third one started, and put it back where it was, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who picks a piece of paper up off a table in a yard and puts it down again has not read it and has not been asked to and cannot be counted either way, and that his right arm has not gone above the level of that shoulder on the third of the eighth month either.

What had not moved on the third of the eighth month went in at the end of the day in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and twenty-fifth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and the man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing put four lines and a bit of a fifth on a piece of paper on the second of this month and did not put a line on a board, because a piece of paper is not a board. Nine refusals to read, and the fourth line of that offer was not read out loud on the third of the eighth month, and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and fourth days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for. Four columns of not-askings and she did not rule one this month and is not going to. The instruments this district has built and not named are six, and a man standing at a table with four legs outside a door is not a seventh of them, and twenty minutes is not a seventh of them, and a listening is not a seventh of them.

The day came down about half past eight and the eighth month was three days old and the rival record had been in force sixty-three days, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE THIRD OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX READ OUT LOUD THAT THE RIVAL RECORD HAD BEEN IN FORCE SIXTY-THREE DAYS AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT A FIGURE OF DAYS ABOUT A RECORD IS NOT A READING OF THAT RECORD AND IS NOT AN ANSWER TO IT AND DID NOT BECOME AN EIGHTH, AND A MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-FOUR WENT DOWN THE LANE AT EIGHT IN THE MORNING AND CAME BACK UP IT AT FOUR MINUTES PAST THREE IN THE AFTERNOON AND WAS ASKED NOTHING BY ANYBODY.**
