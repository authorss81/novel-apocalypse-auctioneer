# Chapter 312: The Day

---

The first of the eighth month of the eighteenth year after the Long Fracture came up over the loams white and hot and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the eleventh night.

"**Two hundred and twenty days on the board and five hundred and thirty-six days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and fifty, and two hundred and eleven days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is this morning and does not have a figure in front of it at all, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and three days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and thirty days, and the table against that wall stood up for the twenty-sixth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood a hundred days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the ladder that stood at one yesterday morning has no term in it this morning, and that the day after a one is a day and not a figure, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning and will not be used in it, and that a man of about sixty-four came into that gate on the twenty-first of the seventh month and has been in this district eleven days, a stay being counted without the day he arrived on, and that eleven days is a figure about a man on a low wall and is not a figure about what is on a gatepost.

Nobody was refused this morning, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody was refused because nothing was asked of anybody, and that a refusal is a thing a person does to a thing that asks it something, and that a day on which nothing arrives is not a day on which anybody has said no.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked out to the gatepost at about half past ten and stood under the sheet nailed there at the height of a man's shoulder with about nine people behind him and read out the line on it about the day, and the line says that the people in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off are to be moved on or before the first day of the eighth month, and he said the words *on or before* out loud twice in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and six, and it went in the minute in his own words.
"**On or before. That is what is on that post and it is the only day in it and the only two words in the whole of it that are not a building and a number. It is not on the day. It is on or before it. Yesterday was the last day of it being before. This morning it is the day, and a sheet that says on or before a day is not complied with by that day arriving and it is not un-complied with by it either, and I have been in this yard a month saying that a door is not a keeper and I am going to say one more thing about a word and then I am going to stop. A word that says on or before is a word that has left a place for a person to be late in. There is no place in that sheet for anybody to be late in and it is a sheet about four hundred and eleven people and it has one number and one day and no place and no person and now it has not got a before in front of the day either, because the before is this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure somebody says out loud in a yard is a figure, and that the two words *on or before* are on a sheet that names no place and no person and that a sheet which says on or before is not complied with by a day passing and is not un-complied with by a day passing, and that this district does not know whether it has been stopped or is late, and **that not knowing is a finding and is entered as one.**

The rest of that morning went the way the morning of the twenty-second of the seventh month went, and a clerk entered that two mornings that go the same way are two mornings and are not one morning, and that the twenty-second of the seventh month is the day a man of about forty with a cart of his own used a word wrongly about a man of about sixty-four, and that a word used wrongly on a day is not a different day from any other day and has not made one.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale had the blanket out at the bank from before eight and had weighed about four sacks by the time the light came properly, and a man of about forty-five who digs and fills in holes for anybody who asks him was in that yard from about nine until about eleven and dug a hole about a foot deep and a foot across beside the gatepost and filled it in again before the light got on it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a hole is not a stake and that a man who digs and fills in holes in a yard is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own and did not become one.

---

The gate was the thing that day. Three or four people stood at it in ones and twos over about five hours and looked up the eleven mile road and up the lane, and about nineteen people were in that yard at one and about nine at three and about four at six, and a clerk of nineteen years counted nobody and entered that a count of people standing at a gate is a count of people standing at a gate and is not a count of anybody who is on a road.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood about four feet off the end of that trestle table from about ten until about one and then went and stood at the gate for about forty minutes and then came back to the table, and said one thing out loud at about half past two to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fifty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have convened nine settlements and I can put nine sheets in nine rooms, and I have done it in a morning, and this morning I have stood at a gate for forty minutes doing nothing at all and I have not been able to do the other thing this morning, and the two things are not the same size and I am not going to stand here and pretend they are. Nobody in this yard was refused today. Nobody was asked anything today either, and a day on which nobody asks anybody anything is not a day on which anybody has said no, and there is a difference between those two things and it is the whole difference between a day that passes and an answer. I have got nothing to add to that and I am not going out this afternoon to go looking for something to add.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who convenes nine settlements is not a representative of a settlement that has not asked to be represented, and that a man who has said out loud that a day on which nobody asks anybody anything is not a day on which anybody has said no has not refused to ask and cannot be counted either way, and that a gate that takes two goes took two goes in both directions this morning for about nine people and that a gate is not a door and is not a keeper of a day.

At about half past four a man of about forty with a cart of his own came up the lane as far as the gate and stopped and looked up the eleven mile road for about a minute and a half and then said one thing to nobody in particular, and it was not counted, and a clerk entered that it was not counted, and that what he said was that there was nothing on the road, and that nothing on a road is a figure about a road and is not a figure about the people behind whoever is not on it.

The woman of fifty-eight came down to the bank at about five with two buckets, the way she does, and filled the second of them and said one thing to nobody while she was doing it, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted, that two buckets were not counted, that nobody counted them, and that the record about the not counting says not asked.

"**I have carried water to eleven houses for nineteen years and I came down this bank this evening the same as I came down it yesterday, and I have not asked any of the eleven whether they were expecting anything and I am not going to this evening. A person can be got out of a house in half an hour if they want to be got out, and that is a thing a sheet cannot do twice, and I am not going to stand here this evening and be one of the reasons nobody came.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman of fifty-eight who carries water to eleven houses is not a keeper of anything, and that no figure about her eleven houses was produced on the first of the eighth month, and that a house in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off is not one of the four hundred and eleven and is not on any sheet in this district, and that the ninth of the eleven is one of the five different ninths in this district and that the five are not added together.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was at the end of that table from about ten until about six with his leg out in front of him under it and did not stand up once, and a clerk entered that a man who sits at the end of a table for eight hours on a day when nothing comes is not a figure about that day and has not been a keeper of that table and has not been asked to be.

---

She entered what had not moved on the first of the eighth month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and twenty-third days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood a hundred days and nobody has sat in it on any of them, and a man sat down on the end of that table on the sixteenth of the seventh month and not in the chair, and those are two different things. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and second days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that out of a ladder and did not ask him for it, and he is a man of about forty-eight and not thirty-four and has said of himself that he is fifty-one. The rival record has been in force sixty-one days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered, and nobody in this district has answered it on any of the days of this month and this day did not. Three documents this district does not own, and the five lines of print on the end of that table are not a fourth of them, and a day on which nothing comes is not a fourth of them and did not become a document.

The day came down about half past eight and the eighth month was one day old, and nothing had come up the eleven mile road or down the lane all day, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was four hundred and eleven at ten in the morning and four hundred and eleven at half past eight in the evening, and a clerk entered the number of days and entered no reason at all and entered nothing whatever about what is going to happen in them.

**AND ON THE FIRST OF THE EIGHTH MONTH, WHICH IS THE DAY THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST NAMES, NOTHING CAME, AND NOBODY WAS REFUSED BECAUSE NOTHING WAS ASKED OF ANYBODY, AND THE FIGURE ON THAT SHEET WAS FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN AT TEN IN THE MORNING AND FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN AT HALF PAST EIGHT IN THE EVENING, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT A DAY PASSING IS NOT AN ANSWER AND THAT THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT KNOW WHETHER IT HAS BEEN STOPPED OR IS LATE, AND THAT NOT KNOWING IS A FINDING.**
