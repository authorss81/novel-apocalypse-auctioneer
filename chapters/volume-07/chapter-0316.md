# Chapter 316: No

---

The fifth of the eighth month came in about the same and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the sixteenth night.

"**Two hundred and twenty-four days on the board and five hundred and forty days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and fifty-four, and two hundred and fifteen days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is four days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and seven days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and thirty-four days, and the table against that wall stood up for the thirtieth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood a hundred and four days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that four days past is four days past and is not a term in a ladder, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district fifteen days, a stay being counted without the day he arrived on, and that a table standing against a wall for a thirtieth morning is a figure nobody in that yard has asked for, and that a figure nobody has asked for is not a finding.

---

She came up that lane at about eleven on her own two feet and nobody went with her and nobody met her at the bottom of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she came on her own line and not on anybody's, and that nobody in that yard knew she was on the road and nobody sent anybody to see.

She was a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book, and she had walked it for eleven years, and there was a cut about the length of two fingers on the inside of her left shin that had been there long enough to be the same colour as the rest of her, and she came up that lane with her weight on the outside of that boot and did not go up the two goes of the bank in front of that yard, and went round by the ditch at the end of it, and came in through a gap in the wall about four feet wide about nine yards along. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book is not the woman of about twenty-nine who keeps the room with nothing over the door, and is not the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, and is not the woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat, and is not the woman of fifty-eight, and that a trade is the thing that tells them apart and that none of them carries the identification on its own.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against asked her one question at about half past eleven, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, standing about nine feet off the end of that trestle table, and the question was whether she would join this yard to a charter, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a word in a man's mouth is not a document, and that no charter of any kind was in that yard and none was read out of it and none was passed round it and none was described to anybody, and that a charter that registers only those who choose to enter is not in this district and has not been in it and nobody in this yard has seen one.

She said no in about four seconds, and the reason was her own, and a man of about nineteen counted what she said and got a hundred and forty-six, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**No. I keep a road for people who are not in any book and I have walked it for eleven years, and nobody in this yard has asked me one question about it in all that time, and nobody in this yard has asked one of the people on it anything in all that time either, and that is not a thing I am saying here to make a point of, it is just what the yard does, and I am not going to say yes to a thing on the strength of a yard that has not asked anybody anything. I am not speaking for anybody. I am one of them and I have not been asked, and a person who has not been asked has not agreed to anything and neither has anybody on a road I walk. That is the whole of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that a woman who keeps a route is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a road and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that this refusal is not a tenth refusal of eleven coppers a week and is not a ninth refusal with no reason and is not an eighth about the ninth holding, and that the nine and the eight and the seven are three figures and are not added together, and that a man who asks somebody a question and is refused in about four seconds with a reason of her own has not been refused by anybody but that person and cannot be counted any other way, and that the last time anybody in this district is on the page about her is the ninth of the seventh month, which is twenty-seven days back this morning, and that nobody in this yard said one word about her on any of the days in between and the record about the not saying says not asked.

---

She stood at the end of that trestle table for about forty minutes after the no and did not say anything else at anybody in that yard, and the yard got about nine minutes of quiet out of it and then started up again, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that about nine minutes is a figure about about nine minutes and is not a figure about a refusal and is not a figure about a person, and that a woman standing at the end of a table after she has said no is not waiting to be asked again and has not been asked again.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them came round the end of that trestle table at about ten past twelve to move the five lines of print and the piece of paper with two lines and a chalk mark of a third one started, so that the end of that table was clear for somebody standing at it, and got the near edge of the sheet up off the wood about an inch and dropped it, and got both hands to it and lifted it about an inch and dropped it again, and put his right forearm flat on the top of the sheet and slid it nine inches along the wood and left it there. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has lost the use of both hands cannot slide five lines of print nine inches along a board and that he has not refused to do it and cannot be counted either way, and that a sheet of paper is not a counter and is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing about that at about twenty past twelve, out loud, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fifty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A woman came up that lane this morning on her own two feet and a man asked her one question in front of about nineteen people and she said no in about four seconds, and I have read that back in my own head four times since and it was the same no and the same four seconds and I am not going to say anything about her reason because it is hers and I did not hear all of it and I have not asked for the rest of it. What I am going to say is that he asked her once, and that is the first time in a month that anybody in this yard has asked anybody anything, and that a yard is a room with no roof and a place where a thing gets said out loud, and a room with no roof cannot refuse anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that a yard cannot refuse anybody has not refused anybody and cannot be counted either way, and that a yard is a room with no roof and a place where a thing gets said out loud, and that it did not refuse anybody on the fifth of the eighth month, and that the record about the not refusing says not asked.

---

The rest of that day went the way the fourth of it went. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud once at about half past four, and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said one thing at about five to about four people and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted, and that what he said was that he had been watching that lane since about half past nine and that the woman had come in one way and would go out one way and that he was not going to be the man who was standing at that gap in the wall at the time.

The man of about sixty-four was in that yard at about ten and not in it at any hour after about one, and he had gone down that lane then and had come back to the foot of the low wall at about half past eight after the light had gone, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district fifteen days and was not inside that gate for about seven and a half hours of the fifteenth, and that a day a man is not in this district is not a day he has been here, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book went out of that yard by the gap in the wall about four feet wide at about half past five and went down that lane with her weight on the outside of her left boot, and nobody followed her and nobody watched her go past the foot of the third house, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard watched her go and that a person who has gone has not been refused anything by leaving and cannot be counted either way, and that a route is not a road and is not a keeper of anything and has not been asked to be.

What had not moved on the fifth of the eighth month went in at the end of the day in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and twenty-seventh days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and sixth days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for. The rival record has been in force sixty-five days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered. Four columns of not-askings and she did not rule one, and a column for a person who keeps a road is not a column of not-askings and was not ruled and is not a fifth of anything. Three documents this district does not own, and a word in a man's mouth is not a fourth of them and a refusal is not a document and did not become one.

The day came down about half past eight and the eighth month was five days old, and a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book had said no in about four seconds in a yard a stranger can walk into, and had said why, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE FIFTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-ONE WHO KEEPS A ROUTE FOR PEOPLE WHO ARE NOT IN ANY BOOK CAME UP THAT LANE ON HER OWN TWO FEET AND NOBODY WENT WITH HER, AND A MAN ASKED HER ONE QUESTION IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE AND SHE SAID NO IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS WITH A REASON OF HER OWN, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT A REFUSAL WITH A REASON IS NOT ONE OF THE EIGHT REFUSALS WITH NO REASON A CLERK OF A HOUSE HAS GIVEN AND THAT THE NINE AND THE EIGHT AND THE SEVEN ARE THREE FIGURES AND ARE NOT ADDED TOGETHER, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
