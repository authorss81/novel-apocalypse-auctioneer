# Chapter 331: Nine Inches of Rope

---

The twentieth of the eighth month was hard and clear off the low ground behind the bank, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went out past the loams at about half past four with his left hand in a cloth and his right hand bare, and nobody in that yard asked the man where he was going and the record about the not asking says not asked.

"**Two hundred and thirty-nine days on the board and five hundred and fifty-five days on the train. The days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and sixty-nine, and two hundred and thirty days separate the second of January and this morning. The table against that wall stood up for the forty-fifth time this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that nineteen days past is a figure about a ladder that is finished and is not a term and is not going to be given one. A man of about sixty-four came into that gate on the morning of the twenty-first of the seventh month and has been in this district thirty days, a stay being counted without the day he arrived on, and he has slept on that low wall on thirty of the thirty nights of that run. She entered that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning, and that keeping a house is not the same work as being a clerk of a house, and that a knot in a rope is not a figure about a man.

---

Four miles out past the loams the frame of oak stands with two uprights about four feet apart and a beam across the top of it, and the bell in that frame is about two feet across the mouth and is cracked from the rim up about a third of the way on the side that faces the loams, and the clapper is in it, and about nine inches of rope has been hanging inside that beam since the night of the twenty-fourth of the sixth month at a height that a man standing on the ground cannot reach.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing got up it the way he got up it the first time, off the low brace at about the height of his hip, and then up the upright, and then along the upright to where the beam is, and the grain of that oak has gone soft at the top in nine years and he was careful with his right elbow and did not trust his left for anything. He got his right hand up inside the beam and had hold of the rope, and it came down about a hand's breadth and stopped, and there was all the rope there is ever going to be.

Nine inches of rope is not nine minutes of rope, and there is no way to hold nine inches of it in a fist for nine minutes, and he has a left hand that has not closed since the eleventh of the sixth month, so he put the end of that rope into the fingers of his right hand, took a turn round his thumb, drew it out a hand's breadth, and made a knot in it with three fingers and a thumb and the heel of his palm, and then he put the loop of that knot over the inside of his right wrist where the pulse is and let the rest of the rope hang.

She entered that a knot in a rope is a knot and is not a job and is not a decision, and that the reason for that knot is in the binding and not in anybody's head, and that a man who tied one this evening has not been asked to tie it and has not been asked to leave it, and that a bell does not need a person and a person standing on a frame is not standing in the bell's place.

He came down off it at about ten past six with the knot at the height of his chest in about nine inches of rope and he did not pull it, and he stood on the ground at the foot of that frame in the last of the light and looked up at it for about four minutes and did not go up it again, and then he walked the four miles back with the cloth in his left hand and the right hand bare and swinging.

He said one thing out loud at about eight that evening in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and eleven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I went out to that frame this afternoon and I got up it and I put a knot in the rope and I did not pull it, and I am going to say those four things in that order because in the other order it sounds like a decision. The knot is in it because I cannot make a fist in this hand and you cannot hold nine inches of anything for an hour and a half, and if that rope is ever held again the weight of it comes on a wrist and not on a hand. I held that rope the first time in the sixth month and I have not asked anybody to hold it and nobody has asked me, and if it goes tomorrow then it has gone for reasons that are in the wood and not in a man. I am not a founder of anything and I am not a keeper of that frame and I have not been asked to be one and I am not going to be one. And I am not going to stand in that yard in the next four days and tell anybody what I have done up there, because what I have done up there is a knot.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard what he did and what he did not do has made a list and not a finding, and that a man who reads things out loud in that yard is not a keeper of a frame and has never been asked to be one, and that a rope with a knot in it is not an instrument and is not a seventh of the six this district has built and not named.

---

The building with two doors at the near end of the lane behind the bank had its plank door standing open on the hasp that has not moved since the eleventh of the sixth month, and the curtain on the nail was hanging out of the way of it the way a man hangs a curtain when he wants a doorway to be a doorway, and the two sheets of print lay flat side by side on the top of the table with four legs against the outside wall under a stone about the size of a fist, and about nine feet of brick with no window in it had a bench and a stool and two buckets of water in it.

She entered that the third of the three things a clerk wrote down on the seventeenth of the sixth month before this district will call a sounding safe is a community providing a shelter and a way to take the record off, and that a building with two doors and a bench and a stool and two buckets of water is not a shelter and has not been called one by anybody in this district, and that a man of about thirty-eight who cuts reeds said so at a table on the eleventh of this month in about four seconds, and that the third of the three is not true and is not made true by a table with four legs outside a door.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was at the bank from about ten until the light went and his right hand has not closed properly since the tenth of this month, and the clerk of nineteen years has not asked him about it and the record about the not asking says not asked, and he has not been in a bed since the night of the fourteenth and he told nobody that and the record about the not telling says not asked. He said one thing out loud at about seven, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and fourteen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have got a hand that will not close and I have not slept in six nights and there are nine feet of brick at the end of this lane with a bench in it and a table outside it with two sheets of print on it, and if a man in this district were to stand in that lane tomorrow night at two in the morning with a lantern in that hand he would be standing in it as a convenience and not as a body, and I have thought about that every day this week and I have not got a better answer than the one I had this morning. I convened nine settlements in five months and I got none of them to a second morning, and I have got a building that a reed man calls a good shed, and I am not going to stand in a yard and make those two things into one because I would sleep better. If anything is done in this lane at two in the morning it will be done by a person who was asked to do it or it will be done by nobody, and I am not asking anybody this evening and I am not going to ask anybody tomorrow.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard what he has got and has not got has made a list and not a finding, and that the man who said it has not asked one person in that yard a question about that lane and has not asked anybody else to ask one, and that he is not a bearer of that lane and is not a keeper of that building and has never been asked to be either.

---

The things that did not move on the twentieth of the eighth month went in at the end of the day in the same hand as the rest and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and forty-second days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and a knot in nine inches of rope four miles out past the loams is not a line on a board. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and twenty-first days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for. The rival record has been in force eighty days, read out in that yard seven times, its eighth reading refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month and unanswered, and a knot is not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and a roll and a pair of terms on a table outside a door are not a fourth of them. Five protected things and no sixth, and a frame eleven feet high is not a sixth and a bell is not a sixth. Four conditions with no end on it and no fifth was proposed on any day of this month. Four columns of not-askings, and a column with the word NOT ASKED over it and nothing under it is not a fifth of them and is not the same figure as the four. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week, eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and the three are three figures and are not added together, and none of the three moved on any day of this month. The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved, and the figure has stood at four hundred and eleven since the first of the sixth month, which is eighty days this morning, and that is a figure about the figure and is not a day-count for the sheet.

The light went off the low ground about half past eight, and the first day of the eighth month was nineteen days past. About nine feet of brick stood at the end of a lane with a curtain on a nail and two buckets of water in it, and a knot in nine inches of rope was hanging inside a beam four miles off, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE TWENTIETH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO MENDS FENCING WENT FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS AND WENT UP A FRAME OF OAK ABOUT ELEVEN FEET HIGH AND TIED A KNOT IN ABOUT NINE INCHES OF ROPE WITH THE FINGERS OF HIS RIGHT HAND, BECAUSE HIS LEFT HAND HAS NOT CLOSED SINCE THE ELEVENTH OF THE SIXTH MONTH, AND HE DID NOT PULL IT, AND NOBODY DECIDED THAT IT SHOULD BE PULLED AND NOBODY ASKED HIM TO TIE THE KNOT AND THE REASON FOR THE KNOT IS IN THE BINDING. THE THIRD OF THE THREE THINGS IS NOT TRUE AND WAS NOT MADE TRUE BY ANYBODY THIS DAY. THE SHEET ON THAT GATEPOST STILL SAID FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
