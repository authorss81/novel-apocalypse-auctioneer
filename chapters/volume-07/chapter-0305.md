# Chapter 305: The Edge of a Sound

---

The twenty-fifth of the seventh month was the third hot day running and the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was not in the yard of Lot Seventeen at ten in the morning and had not been in it since about ten to five the day before, when he went out of that gate with a coat over his arm, and about nineteen people were in that yard, and the man of fifty-six read the board out from under the east wall and got all three of the figures and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said them out loud afterwards for the people who had come in off the lane since he had read them.

"**Two hundred and thirteen days on the board and five hundred and twenty-nine days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and forty-three, and two hundred and four days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is seven days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is ninety-six days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and twenty-three days, and the table against that wall stood up for the nineteenth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood ninety-three days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that seven is six days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man who went out of that gate at about ten to five on the morning of the twenty-fourth has been gone for two days, and that two days is a figure about two days and is not a figure about what he did in them, and that he was not in that yard on the twenty-fourth, on the twenty-fifth, or on either of the two nights, and that a day a man is not in this district is not a day he has been here.

The man of fifty-six said one thing about that at about eleven, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and sixty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A man went out of that gate yesterday morning with a coat over his arm and nobody sent for him and nobody asked him where he was going, and a clerk four hundred yards away has entered that nobody in this yard knows where he is this morning, and that is the correct entry and I want to say so in front of about nineteen people rather than have it read out later. I have been in this yard five months saying that a door is not a keeper and a wall is not an address, and the honest version of all of that this morning is that I do not know where he is, I cannot go and look, and if I did go and look I would not be able to say what I was looking at. I am not frightened of that. I am saying it because I have found that saying it is the only part of it I can do.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has said out loud that he does not know where another man is has not refused to be told and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

He had gone out of the gate of the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten to five on the morning of the twenty-fourth with his coat over his arm and nothing else in his hands, and he had got out to that frame four miles past the loams by about two in the afternoon, and he had gone on outwards from it in daylight until the light went off the ground, and he had stopped there and slept, or not slept, and this morning at about ten to six he had gone on outwards again from wherever the day before had finished putting him.

On the first of the two days he had crossed a turning that is not on any sheet in this district and about which nobody in this basin has ever been asked, and he had stopped at it for a few minutes and looked back the way he had come and could not see the frame of oak at all, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a turning that is not on a sheet is a turning and is not an address and is not a document, and that nobody asked him whether he was going to write it down and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The ground he was on was ground he had never walked and it had been that since about the middle of the afternoon on the twenty-fourth and it went on being that for a long way, and he did not mark any of it and did not count any of it and told nobody he was counting it. At about eleven on the twenty-fifth he came to the far end of a field about two hundred yards across and the ditch was at the far end of it, about three feet deep where he had last seen it in the sixth month, with the sides cut straight in and about a foot of standing water in the bottom.

He went down into it. That is the whole of what he did and there was nobody watching him do it. He got himself down the near side with one hand on the lip of it and stood in about a foot of standing water on a bottom that was soft enough to take a boot print and did not come out again the way he came in, and from the bottom of it he could see the far side of the field and a low line of something about a mile off that he could not tell what it was.

He could not get up the first time. He tried it twice with his left leg and once with his right and his left arm went into the water to the elbow for the purpose and he came out of the ditch on the second attempt about nine seconds after the first one, and he stood on the lip of it afterwards for about a minute with his hands on his knees, and his coat was on the grass and he picked it up with his right hand and not with his left.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who cannot get out of a ditch the first time has not refused the ditch and cannot be counted either way, and that the getting up the second time is not entered as a first time and is not entered as a second time either, and that a ditch about three feet deep at the far end of a field about two hundred yards across is a ditch and is not a document and the two are not added together, and that a leg and a boot and about nine seconds are three things about a man and are not three figures about anybody and are not a price of anything, and that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said in that yard on the nineteenth of last month that a habit and a figure are the same thing after a month and that he looked for a test for six days and did not find one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He walked back in from about half past eleven and it took him the rest of that day and most of the following night, with two stops of about nine minutes each in the same places he had stopped going out, and about half past six in the morning of the twenty-sixth he came through the gate of the yard of Lot Seventeen with his coat over his arm and sat down at the foot of the low wall along the lane without saying one word to anybody, and there were about four people in that yard at that hour and none of them asked him anything.

---

The afternoon of that day went the way the afternoon of the twenty-third went. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud twice. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was at the foot of the trestle table with his right arm hanging and did not try to lift anything and nobody asked him to. The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was asked about his tent by a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat at about half past four, and he said four or five words back, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that the tent was still rolled on the back of that hand-cart and had been since the thirtieth of last month, and that the twentieth and the twenty-first of this month came and went and the record about the two of them says not asked.

At about half past six on the twenty-fifth the man of fifty-six was at the gate of that yard on his own and about four people were still on the low wall along the lane, and he said one thing to those four people, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fifty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**There have been about nineteen people in this yard today and none of them has been told anything, and about four of the four people on this wall would have asked him this evening if they had thought of it, and I have been in this yard five months and I have watched a number of people ask a man a thing and get an answer and I have never once watched a yard take a thing off a man. He is not in this district and he is somewhere on ground a man in this yard has never walked, and there is nobody here who can go and see, and that is not a thing I am going to have a clerk enter as a failure. What I want entered is that nobody asked him one question this morning and that nobody has gone out after him and that wanting is not asking.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that about four people who would have asked is a figure about about four people and not about the man, and that a man who has said out loud that he will not go and ask has not refused to be asked and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and a man of fifty-six is not a clerk of a house. She entered that a man who is somewhere on ground a man in that yard has never walked has not refused to be found and cannot be counted either way, and that nobody was sent.

She entered what had not moved on the twenty-fifth of the seventh month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and sixteenth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood ninety-three days and nobody has sat in it on any of them. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the ninety-five days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that out of a ladder and did not ask him for it, and he is a man of about forty-eight and not thirty-four and has said of himself that he is fifty-one. The rival record has been in force fifty-four days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of last month, and nobody has answered it. Three documents this district does not own and no document came into that yard today. Five protected things and no sixth, and a ditch is not a sixth and a turning is not a sixth and a field is not a sixth. The instruments this district has built and not named are six, and a tent is not a seventh of them and a table is not a seventh. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week, seven refusals about the ninth holding and eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and the three are three figures and are not added together, and none of the three moved on the twenty-fifth of the seventh month. No number on the sheet at that gatepost moved today.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was twenty-five days old and the first day of the eighth month was seven days off, and a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was somewhere between a field about two hundred yards across and a gate that takes two goes, with a figure he had not said and a coat over his arm.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO KEEPS A ROAD WENT DOWN INTO A DITCH ABOUT THREE FEET DEEP AT THE FAR END OF A FIELD ABOUT TWO HUNDRED YARDS ACROSS AND COULD NOT GET UP THE FIRST TIME AND GOT UP THE SECOND TIME, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT THE GETTING UP THE SECOND TIME IS NOT ENTERED AS A FIRST TIME AND IS NOT ENTERED AS A SECOND TIME EITHER, AND NOBODY IN THAT YARD ASKED HIM ANYTHING AT ALL ON THAT DAY BECAUSE HE WAS NOT IN IT.**
