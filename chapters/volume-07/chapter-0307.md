# Chapter 307: Nine Days of Nothing

---

The twenty-seventh of the seventh month broke grey over the loams and did not get any cooler after eleven, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was already sitting at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his heels on the ground and his coat folded beside him.

"**Two hundred and fifteen days on the board and five hundred and thirty-one days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and forty-five, and two hundred and six days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is five days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is ninety-eight days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back and a hundred and twenty-five days, and the table against that wall stood up for the twenty-first time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood ninety-five days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that five is four days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man who came into this yard on the twenty-first of this month has been in it six days this morning, a stay being counted without the day he arrived on, **and that six days is a figure about a stay in this district and is not the nine days he gave about his own walking on the twenty-first, and that the two are two figures and are not added together and do not make fifteen.**

At about ten the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them said one thing at the end of that trestle table, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and eighty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have got two things and I have not got a third one and I have said that in this yard on the fifth of this month and again on the nineteenth and again on the twentieth, and I am not going to say it again this morning, and I am going to say the shape of it instead because I have had it in my mouth for six days and it is coming out sideways. The shape of the third thing is a question. Not a sheet and not a line of print and not a table and not a tent. A question, asked out loud, of somebody who is standing there, and an answer given back. I have not asked anybody one in this yard in three weeks and nobody has asked me one, and I have not described the one person who stood at that table to eleven miles off and I am not going to, and that is my two things and I have made neither of them into a market. That is the shape. That is all of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who puts tables up is not a keeper of a table and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a shape is not a finding and a shape is not a figure, and that a man who has said out loud the shape of a thing he has not got has not got it and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking him says not asked.

---

The heat came up off the floor of that yard about one and about half of the people in it went and sat on the low wall along the lane. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came through the gate at about half past one with a basket on the back of his cart and put it down inside the gate and went up the lane, and about four people took things out of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a basket was put down inside that gate and that four people took things out of it and that the four is a figure about four people and not about the basket and not about anybody who did not take one.

At about two the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came back down the lane and asked the man of about sixty-four one question, out loud, in the ordinary voice, which was whether he would carry a parcel up the eleven mile road to a turning about a mile short of a wall and take it back down again, and the man of about sixty-four said no in about four seconds, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and twenty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**No. I am not going to be a man who takes two things. I have got one and it is in my head and it has been in my head for a long time, and I am not going to take a parcel off a man who has got a cart for the purpose and give him a reason he can put in a book. And I am not saying no because I think you are after something. I am saying no because a man who carries things gets asked to carry more things, and I have not got room for the one I have got, and that is the whole of my reason and I would like it taken as a reason and not as a mood.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that a man who drives a cart is not a clerk of a house and is not a bearer of a refusal, and that the eight did not move at about two in the afternoon on the twenty-seventh of the seventh month, and that a man who carries a message from one place to another on his own two feet is not a keeper of it and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud twice that afternoon, once at about two and once at about four, and the man of about sixty-four read them over about nine feet of the yard both times and came no nearer the second time than he had the first.

At about three a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat came up the lane with a kid about the size of a dog against her leg and stopped at the gate and asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing one question, which was whether anybody in that yard had walked out from that bell in the last three days, and he said one man had gone out of that gate on the twenty-fourth of this month and had not come back into it yet, and that was the whole of the answer, and she said right and went back down the lane, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that one man and two days is a figure about one man and is not a figure about a bell and is not a finding, and that nobody in that yard asked her anything at all and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past three the man of about fifty-six was at the top of the bank with the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, about six feet apart, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the two of them were not in that yard and that about six people in it knew where they were and that nobody was sent. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing up there and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that about six people at the bottom of that bank could not hear it.

"**He has been in this district six days and he has not asked anybody one question and he has not been asked one, and I have been standing in a yard for five months telling anybody who will stand still that a yard is a place where a thing gets said out loud, and it turns out a man can stand in one for six days and be a stranger in it and be a stranger in it by something he is carrying and not by anything anybody in this yard has done. I am not going to ask him. I am telling you on top of this bank about six feet from him that I am not going to ask him, and I would like that entered, because I have been the man in this basin who asks everybody everything and today I am not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to ask has not refused to be asked and cannot be counted either way, and that two men on the top of a bank are not a meeting and did not become one.

---

At about half past four the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them said the shape of it a second time at the end of that trestle table, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fifty-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The shape of the third thing is a question, and I said that at about ten this morning, and I am saying it again at about half past four because I have not said it once in between and I have thought about nothing else since about eleven. I am not going to say it a third time. There is a man sitting at the foot of that wall who has not been asked one question in six days and I have not asked him one and neither has anybody else, and I am not going to stand in this yard and make that into a reason. It is a shape. I have said it twice. Somebody in this basin is going to notice that twice is not a finding and I would like a clerk to write that down before I say it a third time and make it one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a shape noticed a second time is not a finding and a third time is a habit, and that a man who says a thing a second time because it has not been said since the twentieth of this month is not making a habit of it and has not been asked to stop, and that a shape is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and that the six did not move on the twenty-seventh of the seventh month.

She entered what had not moved on the twenty-seventh of the seventh month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and eighteenth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood ninety-five days and nobody has sat in it on any of them. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the ninety-seven days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she entered that out of a ladder and did not ask him. The rival record has been in force fifty-six days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of last month, and it is unanswered. Three documents this district does not own, and no document came into that yard today, and a basket put down inside a gate is not a document. Five protected things and no sixth, and a wall a man sleeps against is not a sixth and a shape is not a sixth. Four conditions with no end on it, thirteen refusals to join two things and the registrar's own count of twelve, nine refusals to read with the departure unspent and no date in the ledger, nine refusals of eleven coppers a week and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and the seven and the nine are two figures and are not the same figure. No number on the sheet at that gatepost moved today and four hundred and eleven is four hundred and eleven.

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was twenty-seven days old and the first day of the eighth month was five days off, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him and nothing in his hands.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SEVENTH OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SEVEN WHO PUTS TABLES UP SAID THE SHAPE OF THE THIRD THING HE HAS NOT GOT TWICE IN ONE YARD, THAT THE SHAPE IS A QUESTION, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT A SHAPE NOTICED A SECOND TIME IS NOT A FINDING, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR WHO DRIVES A CART ASKED A MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-FOUR TO CARRY A PARCEL UP THE ELEVEN MILE ROAD AND WAS REFUSED IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS WITH A REASON.**
