# Chapter 302: Three Dates

---

The twenty-second of the seventh month was the same sort of day as the day before it and a little worse, and the man of fifty-six read the board out from under the shade of the east wall at about ten minutes past ten and got all three figures, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the board flat on the end of the trestle table in front of him the whole time he was reading it, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

"**Two hundred and ten days on the board and five hundred and twenty-six days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and forty, and two hundred and one days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is ten days off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is ninety-three days back, and the table against that wall stood up for the sixteenth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood ninety days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that ten is nine days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a figure somebody hands to a clerk on a board in a yard is a figure about that board and is not a finding, and that the records about the not asking say not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them came through the gate with his hand-cart at about eleven, which he does most mornings, and pushed the cart out from under the wall into what shade there was and stood with one shoulder against it and looked up the lane, and nobody asked him what he was looking for, and the tent was still rolled on the back of it where it has been rolled since the thirtieth of last month.

Then the man of fifty-six stood at the end of that trestle table and worked three dates out loud in the ordinary voice to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got two hundred and thirty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The first of them is the first day of the eighth month and it is ten days off counted off this morning and it is the day on that sheet at that gatepost. The second of them is the twenty-fourth of last month at about two in the morning, and that bell was pulled for about nine minutes and nobody has written down who heard it, and there is a piece of paper in a room four hundred yards from here with about nine names on it in about nine hands and that piece of paper is a record of the people who came into a building and it is not the record. The third of them is a month after the twenty-fourth of last month, and a month after the twenty-fourth of last month is the twenty-fourth of this month, and that is the earliest day that bell may be pulled again, because the second of the three things a bell has to have is that a bell rung when there is nothing to answer it cannot be rung again for a month. Nobody in this yard has asked me for any of those three. I have said all of it out loud this morning because I am a man who stands at a gate with a board and I would rather be wrong out loud than be asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that three dates said out loud in a yard are three dates and are not a finding, and that the record about the not asking him says not asked, and that a piece of paper with about nine names on it in about nine hands is not the record of a bell and the two are not added together, and that a bell does not need a person and a person standing next to a bell is not deciding what it rings.

At about half past twelve a man at the back of that yard, who was about forty and had a cart of his own somewhere off the lane and had been in the yard about an hour, said the word courier out loud in the ordinary voice to the man of about sixty-four, and meant it about a man who takes a parcel for a shop and gets paid for it at the end of the week, and the man of about sixty-four turned round off the trestle table and said that was not what he was, and then said what he was, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and thirty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That is not what I am. That is a man who takes a thing from one place to another place for money and is paid at the end of a week and stops when the shop shuts. I carry one thing from one place to another place on my own two feet and nobody pays me and there is no week. I have never written it down. It is not in a case and it is not on a sheet and it is not in a book and I could not put it in a case if I had a case, because a case is a thing you can pick up and I have not been able to pick anything up for longer than most of the people in this yard have been standing in it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a word and a thing are two things, and that a word somebody has been given is not a description of anybody, and that a man who has said out loud that a word about a shop does not describe him has not refused the word and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who carries a thing is not a keeper of it and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that the record about the not asking him what he carries says not asked.

The man of about fifty-six said four or five words back to that about a word being a thing that gets said out loud, and they were not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that they were not counted and that about four people heard them.

---

About four of the nineteen went up the lane in the middle of the afternoon and the rest of them did not. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud twice more, once at about two and once at about half past three, and the man of about sixty-four stood about four feet off and read them over his shoulder both times and came four feet nearer the second time and no nearer than that.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the foot of that trestle table for about nine minutes at about half past three with his right arm hanging and his left hand in his belt, and he did not say anything, and about four people stood about twenty feet off with nothing to do, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that nine minutes is a figure about nine minutes and not about a man and not about a hand and is not a finding.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had been on the low wall along the lane from about one until about a quarter past three, came in off the lane at about four and put his hand-cart against the wall inside the gate where it was in the morning, and said one thing to nobody in particular, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fifty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been sitting on that wall next to him since about one this afternoon and he has not asked me one question and I have not asked him one, and I have noticed that I have not asked him one, and I am telling you I noticed it rather than sitting on it. I do not know what to do with a man you cannot ask anything. I have got a table eleven miles off that two men put down and neither of them can put up, and I have got a fifth line of my own that is not true and I put it there on purpose, and this morning I have got a third thing I did not have yesterday and it is a man on a wall and it is not a third thing and I am not going to stand in this yard and call it one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who says out loud that a thing is not a thing has not consented to it and has not refused it and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who notices that he has not asked a question has not asked it, and that the instruments this district has built and not named are six and that a man on a wall is not a seventh of them and did not become one, and that the six did not move on the twenty-second of the seventh month.

About half past five a woman came up the lane and asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing to read the five lines to her and to a boy about four years old who came up the lane behind her holding on to the back of her skirt, and he read them out loud in the ordinary voice at about four feet back from the end of the trestle table, and the man of about sixty-four read them over the woman's shoulder at the same time and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two people read the same five lines at the same moment about nine feet apart and that is a figure about a yard and not a finding, and that a boy about four years old is not the boy of nine whose name is in a column in a building two days of road away and the two are not the same person and are not added together.

The man of about sixty-four said four or five words at about six, to nobody in particular, and it was not counted.

"**I came into this district yesterday morning and a gate has taken me about nine minutes once and a woman has put a bucket of water out for me without being asked and a man has read five lines out loud about four times, and nobody has asked me one question and I have not said one thing that anybody here has written down, and I would like that entered as two things about me and not as one thing about this yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who came into this district on the twenty-first and is standing in it on the twenty-second has been here one day, a stay being counted without the day he arrived on, and that one day is a figure about a man and not about a district and not about a yard, and that a bucket of water put out by a woman who was not asked is not a fifth of anything and is not a sixth of the five protected things, and that the record about the not asking him anything says not asked.

She entered what had not moved on the twenty-second of the seventh month in the same hand and not as a paragraph. The pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is three months back, and that is a hundred and twenty days. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and thirteen days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood ninety days and nobody has sat in it on any of them, and a man sat down on the end of that table on the sixteenth of this month and not in the chair, and those are two different things. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the ninety-two days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and she did not ask him for that figure and he said it on none of the days of this block. The rival record has been in force fifty-one days and has been read out in that yard seven times, and nobody has answered it. The instruments this district has built and not named are six. The documents this district does not own are three, and the five lines of print on the end of that table are not a fourth of them, and the two lines on the piece of paper that has lain there since the fifth of this month are not a document and are not a copy of the five.

The day came down about half past seven and the seventh month was twenty-two days old and the first day of the eighth month was ten days off, and the earliest day on which that bell may be pulled again was two days off and neither of those two facts had anything done to it.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-SECOND OF THE SEVENTH MONTH A MAN OF FIFTY-SIX WORKED THREE DATES OUT LOUD IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN THAT NOBODY HAD ASKED HIM FOR, AND THE THIRD OF THE THREE WAS THE TWENTY-FOURTH OF THAT MONTH, WHICH IS THE EARLIEST DAY THAT BELL IN THE OAK FRAME FOUR MILES OUT PAST THE LOAMS MAY BE PULLED AGAIN, AND A MAN SAID THE WORD COURIER IN THAT YARD AND MEANT IT ABOUT A MAN WHO CARRIES A PARCEL FOR A SHOP, AND WAS TOLD THAT WAS NOT WHAT HE WAS.**
