# Chapter 336: Said Once, in the Ordinary Voice

---

Nothing happened on the twenty-fifth of the eighth month until about half past four in the afternoon. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table, the man of fifty-six read the three of them out loud off the board over his shoulder and got two right, and there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the thirty-sixth night and was gone from that wall at about half past two and was not asked where he had gone.

"**Two hundred and forty-four days on the board. It is two hundred and thirty-five days from the second of January to this morning, and two hundred and seventy-four days since the twenty-fourth of November on which anybody entered anything. The first day of the eighth month is twenty-four days past this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that twenty-four days past is a figure about a ladder and is not a term, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district thirty-five days and has slept on that low wall on thirty-four of the thirty-five nights of that run, and that he was not on that wall this afternoon and that nobody asked where he had gone, and the record about the not asking says not asked. She entered that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning, and that nobody in this district who keeps a house is a clerk of a house.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him one question in that yard at about ten in the morning, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted.

"**You are going to hear a name in a room this afternoon and you are not going to say it out loud in this yard afterwards, and I want to ask you one thing about that while there is still daylight. When you have heard it, what are you going to do with it. And I am asking it in this yard in front of about nine people because if I ask it in that room this afternoon then you will have to answer a woman of fifty-eight instead of a yard, and a man answers a yard worse than he answers a room and better than he answers himself.**"

He did not answer him. He stood about four feet off the end of that trestle table for about eleven minutes without saying anything and then went down that lane with the sheet of five lines of print still folded inside his coat, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has not answered a question in a yard has not refused to answer it and cannot be counted either way, and that a question asked in a yard is a question a person can refuse and that this one was asked in a yard, and that nobody in that yard has asked him about that room since.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall from about ten until about half past two with his coat folded beside him and his back to the sun, and about nine people came and went in that lane in front of him in that time and not one of them said anything to him about the night. At about eleven he turned his head to the left and held it there, and it did not get any better that way, and he turned it back to the yard. Nobody in that lane asked him what he was listening with and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The room is about nine feet by nine at the top of the lane, past the third house from the scale, with a stool by the wall and a window about a foot and a half square and a board nailed across the outside of it, and there is no paper in that room and no ink in that room and there never has been, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against and a man of about sixty-four were in it from about half past three, and a woman of fifty-eight came into it at about half past four and stayed about twenty minutes.

She entered that nothing went into that room in anybody's hands and nothing came out of that room in anybody's hands and that nobody wrote anything down in it, and that a clerk of nineteen years was four hundred yards away in a yard and was not told about it until about six and did not go up that lane, and the record about the not going says not asked, and that a name was said once in that room and a clerk may enter that a name was said and not what it was.

The man of about sixty-four said one thing out loud at about ten past four before he said the other thing, in the ordinary voice, to two people in a shut room, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Eighteen years. I have carried it from a place where people write things down to a place where they do not, and I have carried it on my own two feet, and I have never once put it on anything, and I have told nobody, and there is one more in that night behind the twenty-fourth of the sixth and I am not going to say tonight whether that one is a name or a hand. I am going to say this one because a man in this district has asked me a question about it every day for a fortnight by standing nine feet off me and not asking it, and because I said on the twenty-ninth of the seventh month in a yard that I would rather everybody knew on a day I named. The day is today. Say it to her and not to him, because she has been standing at the edge of that ground for nineteen years without putting a single question into it and I would rather a name went into a person like that than into a man who convenes things, and I am not going to explain that any further than that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who carries a message from one place to another on his own two feet is not a keeper of it and does not become one, and that a man who has carried something for eighteen years and said it out loud once in a room has been delivered and is not a failure and is not a tragic figure and is not redeemed, and that a man whose trade is over is not a figure about anybody, and that the record about the not saying the second one says not asked.

Then he said the name. It was said once, in the ordinary voice, in a room with a shut door in it, and it is not on this page.

She entered that the name is one of the four hundred and eleven and that the four hundred and eleven is a count of the people who answered a door, and that a name said out loud is a consent the person it belongs to did not give, and that binding cannot create consent and neither can a certificate and neither can a bell, and that the person it belongs to has not been asked and has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that no place is named with it and no body is named with it and it is not entered in either of the two books four hundred yards from that gate and is not on any document in this district.

The woman of fifty-eight did not say it back. She stood by the stool with her hands on the back of it for about twenty minutes and did not repeat a word of it and did not write it on anything, and she asked him nothing about it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who does not repeat a name she has been given has not refused it and cannot be counted either way, and that nobody asked her to repeat it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that lane at about five and found his room empty and stood in the doorway of it for about nine minutes and said nothing to anybody, and then said one thing out loud in the yard at about six, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and seventy-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I lent that room for twenty minutes this afternoon and I did not go back up that lane and I did not stand at the bottom of it, and I have not asked him what was said in there and I am not going to ask him tonight or tomorrow or any day this month, and I am not going to be the man who says in this yard that I know a name. I have been asked eleven words once in that same room and I did not say what they were and nobody made me, and a name is a different thing from eleven words, and I do not know what the difference is worth and neither does anybody else in this basin. All I know is that I am the man in this district who reads things out loud and whatever is in my head now is not a thing I read out loud unless the person it belongs to is standing in front of me when I do.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who reads things out loud in yards is not a keeper of a name and is not a bearer of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that the eleven words are not repeated and are not asked for, and that a name in the head of a man who reads things out loud is a figure about a man who reads things out loud and is not a name in a record, and that a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on.

Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and forty-seventh days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and a name said once in a room is not a line on a board. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and twenty-fourth days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for, and he has said of himself that he is fifty-one. The rival record has been in force eighty-five days and is unanswered, and a name is not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and a name said in a room is not a fourth of them and did not become one. Five protected things and no sixth, and a name is not a sixth. Four conditions with no end on it and no fifth was proposed on any day of this month. Nine refusals to read, the departure is unspent and no date in any ledger carries the next reading, and the fourth line of that offer was not read out loud on any day of this month.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood at the end of that trestle table for about nine minutes on his own and then went down the lane. The light came down at about half past eight and the first day of the eighth month was twenty-four days past, and that gatepost still had four hundred and eleven on a sheet nailed to it.

**AND ON THE TWENTY-FIFTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A NAME WAS SAID ONCE, IN THE ORDINARY VOICE, IN A ROOM WITH A SHUT DOOR IN IT, TO ONE PERSON, AND IT IS THE NAME OF SOMEBODY WHO IS ONE OF THE FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN, AND IT IS NOT WRITTEN ON ANY DOCUMENT IN THIS DISTRICT AND IS NOT IN EITHER OF THE TWO BOOKS AND IS NOT NAMED WITH ANY PLACE AND NOT NAMED WITH ANY BODY, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS MAY ENTER THAT A NAME WAS SAID AND NOT WHAT IT WAS. THE SHEET ON THAT GATEPOST SAID FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN AT HALF PAST EIGHT AND SAID IT AT TEN.**
