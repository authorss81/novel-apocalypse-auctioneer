# Chapter 317: Four Seconds Again

---

The sixth of the eighth month came in about the same and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book was already at the end of that trestle table when he put them there, having come in by the gap in the wall about four feet wide and gone round the ditch at the end of the bank again.

"**Two hundred and twenty-five days on the board and five hundred and forty-one days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and fifty-five, and two hundred and sixteen days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is five days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and eight days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and thirty-five days, and the table against that wall stood up for the thirty-first time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood a hundred and five days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that five days past is five days past and is not a term in a ladder, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book came into that yard on the sixth of the eighth month for the second time in two days and that two days is a figure about two days and is not a figure about her.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked her one question at about half past ten, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and the question was whether he might read the five lines of print out loud in a room where she lived, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked a woman one question in a yard on the sixth of the eighth month and that the yard is a room with no roof and is a place where a thing gets said out loud, and that a document read out loud in a yard on a man's request has been read out loud in that yard and is not read out anywhere else by being read out in that yard.

She said no in about four seconds, and the reason was her own, and a man of about nineteen counted what she said and got a hundred and thirty-five, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**No, and it will take about four seconds again, and I would like it entered that it took about four seconds again. A thing said out loud in a yard is said out loud in a yard, and anybody who wants to hear five lines of print can come and stand in this one, and about nineteen of them did yesterday. A room is a different thing and I do not keep anything in a room. I have not kept anything in a room in eleven years and I am not going to start, and the first thing anybody could say about me after that is that I had started keeping a list, and they would be right. You have got a yard. Read it in the yard. Do not go looking for a room.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason given on the sixth of the eighth month is not the same refusal as the one given on the fifth of it, and that it is of a different thing and from a different person and with a different reason, and that neither of the two was asked for twice and neither of the two was taken back afterwards, and that a woman who keeps a route for people who are not in any book is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a room and is not a keeper of a list and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that the two refusals are two refusals and are not added to each other and are not added to any of the figures on the end of that trestle table.

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing at about eleven, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and forty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I asked her one question yesterday and a man in this yard asked her another one this morning and she has said no twice in about four seconds each and I have not been told her reasons because I was not standing close enough to hear either of them. I am not going to ask her a third question in this yard and I am not going to ask anybody else to ask her one on my behalf, and I am not going to go round her, and I said in a lane that I would not and a clerk wrote it down and here I am saying it again in daylight. I have said my piece and I am not going to stand in this yard and add to it. That is the whole of what I have got and I am not asking her for anything else.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he is not going to ask a person a question again has not been refused a question and cannot be counted either way, and that he sent nobody, and that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked his own question and was not sent, and that the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road and the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart and the man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing were not sent to anybody's door by anybody in that yard, and that a going round a person is a going round a person and a refusal with a reason is a thing that happened and not a wall.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said four or five words back to that, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was not counted, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was not counted and that he said that he had asked it himself and that nobody asked him to.

---

The afternoon of that day is the answer to a question nobody in that yard asked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it as one line and not as a paragraph. A yard cannot refuse anybody, and a yard is a room with no roof, and a room with no roof is a place where a thing gets said out loud and nothing else, and on the sixth of the eighth month the thing got said out loud at about half past ten in front of about nine people and the room with no roof did what a room with no roof does, which is that it went on holding about nineteen people for the rest of the day. Nobody left because of the no. Nobody stayed because of it either. The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat was at the foot of that trestle table for that one reading and had not been in that yard on the previous day at all. The man of about forty with a cart of his own was in that yard at about half past two and did not say anything to anybody about what he had said in that yard on the twenty-second of the seventh month and had not been asked to. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was at the end of that table at about four and asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, in the ordinary voice and to nobody in particular, whether a thing read out in a yard was the same thing in a room, and was told that he did not know and that he was not going to guess, and a clerk entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he does not know about a thing has not refused to find out and cannot be counted either way.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was at the foot of the low wall along the lane the whole of that afternoon and did not come into that yard at all, and a clerk entered that a man who stays outside a gate on a day a thing is said inside a gate has not agreed with it and has not disagreed with it and cannot be counted either way.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that yard was given a sentence that settled anything on the sixth of the eighth month, and that a thing said out loud in a yard is not settled by being said out loud, and that four seconds is a figure about four seconds and is not a figure about a person, and that two refusals on two days, of two different things, asked of one person by two different men, are two refusals and are not a figure about anybody and did not become one.

About four people were still in that yard at six. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud once at about half past four and nobody asked him anything about them and nobody asked her anything about them. The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the end of that table with the five lines of print nine inches further along the wood than they had been on the previous day and did not move them again. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was not in that yard and nobody said where he had gone. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane for the sixteenth night and was about nine feet off the end of that trestle table and neither of them spoke to the other, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two men about nine feet apart did not speak to each other for about seven hours and that a distance of nine feet is a figure about nine feet and is not a figure about either of them.

What had not moved on the sixth of the eighth month went in at the end of the day in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and twenty-eighth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood a hundred and five days and nobody has sat in it on any of them. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and seventh days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for. The rival record has been in force sixty-six days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered, and a second refusal about something else is not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and a refusal is not a document and did not become a fourth of them. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and neither figure is the other figure and the two are not added together and neither of them is the figure of the two refusals given in that yard in two days.

The day came down about half past eight and the eighth month was six days old and the two things that had been asked of a woman of about thirty-one who keeps a route for people who are not in any book had both been no, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE SIXTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH THE SAME WOMAN REFUSED A SECOND THING IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS WITH A REASON OF HER OWN, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT NEITHER OF THE TWO WAS ASKED FOR TWICE AND THAT NEITHER WAS TAKEN BACK, AND THAT A YARD IS A ROOM WITH NO ROOF AND A PLACE WHERE A THING GETS SAID OUT LOUD AND CANNOT REFUSE ANYBODY, AND THAT NOBODY IN THAT YARD WAS GIVEN A SENTENCE THAT SETTLED ANYTHING, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
