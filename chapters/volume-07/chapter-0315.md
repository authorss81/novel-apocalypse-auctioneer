# Chapter 315: How Many People Are in the Yard

---

The fourth of the eighth month came in about the same and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table at ten and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen and had been since about a quarter past nine, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the fifteenth night.

"**Two hundred and twenty-three days on the board and five hundred and thirty-nine days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and fifty-three, and two hundred and fourteen days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is three days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and six days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and thirty-three days, and the table against that wall stood up for the twenty-ninth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood a hundred and three days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that three days past is three days past and is not a term in a ladder, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district fourteen days, a stay being counted without the day he arrived on, and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning on the fourth of the eighth month, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that a man or a woman who keeps a scale is not a clerk of a house either.

---

She came up the lane at about half past ten with the blanket of the scale still folded over her arm, which she does not do, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a scale came up a lane with her blanket folded over her arm and that is a thing she has not done before in this district, and that one morning is not a habit and is not a finding.

She stood at the end of the trestle table and looked round that yard for about nine seconds and then said, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, how many people were in it.

The man of about nineteen who counts took the whole of that yard in without a piece of paper or a chalk or a finger on a person and said it out loud, and a man of about thirty-seven counted what he said and got a hundred and twenty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I counted every head in that yard once, from the gatepost round, and I did not count the man of about sixty-four because he is outside the gate on a low wall and the question was about the yard. There are nineteen. I counted them standing still and I counted them moving and I did not count anybody twice, and a woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat is one of the nineteen and a man of about forty-five who digs and fills in holes is one of the nineteen and a man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing is one of the nineteen. It is nineteen. I am not going to say what it is about and I have not been asked what it is about.**"

He took the whole of that yard in from the gatepost, going along the east wall first and then across the open middle and then back up the west side to the trestle table, and he did it again about four minutes later, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who counts a yard twice has not made a check and a check on a figure is not a second figure and is not added to the first, and that the second count of that yard on the fourth of the eighth month was nineteen as well, and that two counts of the same yard are two counts and are not added to one another and do not make thirty-eight.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nineteen is a figure about a yard between about a quarter past nine in the morning and about half past eleven on the morning of the fourth of the eighth month, and that it is not a figure about any of the nineteen people in it, and that a head counted in a yard in daylight in front of about nineteen people is a figure about a yard, and that the same is true of a head counted at a gate, and that a head counted at a gate is a figure about a gate.

---

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing about the number, and one thing about herself, and the two were not said at the same time and about nine people heard both of them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two things were said and that they are two things.

"**Nineteen. I asked for that because I wanted to know it, and I have got it, and it is a number about that yard and not about one of the people in it, and I am not going to put it beside the four hundred and eleven on that post or beside the nine names on that paper in the room four hundred yards off, because those are three different numbers about three different things and the third one of them is about a building on one night in the sixth month and not about this yard this morning. Nineteen and about nine are two figures. They are not added together. They do not make twenty-eight, and nobody in this yard is a twenty-eighth anything.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a count of the people who answered a door is a count of the answerers and not of the people, and that a count of the people in a yard in daylight is a count of a yard and not of anybody, and that nineteen and about nine are two figures and are not added together and do not make twenty-eight, and that a yard a stranger can walk into is a room with no roof and is a place where a thing gets said out loud and did not ask anybody a question before this morning.

"**And the other thing, which I have not said before in this yard and am saying because I asked a question this morning and got an answer and a man has not had a chance to ask me anything. I have four empty houses and they are not going to be offered again before the first day of the season after this one, and that is my own statement about my own four houses and not a rule and not a price and not a thing anybody has agreed to. And the word for what is in those four houses is stock, and stock is a word about what a person has and not a word about what a person is, and I have got a stock of nothing in four houses and a stock of about nine sacks a day at a scale, and those are two stocks and they are not added together and neither of them is four houses.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that *stock* is a word about a thing a person has and is not a rate, and that a woman who keeps a scale has not said the words *rate* or *even* this morning and that the two of those are words she said in this yard on the third of the seventh month and the twenty-sixth of the seventh month, and that this is a third word out of her and is not either of the other two, and that four empty houses are four houses and not four people, and that nobody has counted the people who have left those four houses and the record about the not counting says not asked, and that a house that stands empty is not a sixth of the five protected things in this district.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing about the counting at about ten past twelve, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and ninety-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A man counted nineteen heads in this yard this morning in about four minutes and he did it without touching anybody, and I have watched that yard five months and I can tell you the whole of what I saw him not count. He did not count the man of about sixty-four, because that man is outside the gate on a low wall. He did not count the man of about thirty-seven, because that man is at the foot of the east wall with his back to about half of them. He did not count me, because I am the one reading the board out and a man reading a board out loud is not standing still, and a head that is moving is not a head, and that is not a rule anybody made up, that is just what happens. A count of a yard in daylight is a count of a yard in daylight. It is not the people in it, and I have been putting those two things next to each other in my own head since about a quarter past ten and I am going to stop.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that a head that is moving is not a head has not been asked a question about it and cannot be counted either way, and that a count of a yard in daylight is a count of a yard and not of anybody, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud twice that afternoon, once at about two and once at about half past four, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who read nothing out loud on the second of this month read two things out loud on the fourth of it, and that one morning is a morning and is not a habit and does not make a rule.

The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat was at the foot of that table for both readings and said afterwards, to nobody, that a thing read out loud twice in one day is a thing somebody in that yard is waiting for, and a clerk entered that a woman who keeps a goat is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of anything, and that a person waiting is not a person refusing and cannot be counted either way.

About four people were still in that yard at six. The man of about sixty-four was not one of them and was still at the foot of the low wall along the lane where he had been since before seven and had not come in through that gate at all that day, and a clerk entered that a man who is outside a gate all day is not a figure about what is inside it, and that he has been in this district fourteen days, counted without the day he arrived on, and that nobody asked him to come in and the record about the not asking says not asked.

What had not moved on the fourth of the eighth month went in at the end of the day in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and twenty-sixth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood a hundred and three days and nobody has sat in it on any of them, and a man sat down on the end of that table on the sixteenth of the seventh month and not in the chair, and those are two different things. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and fifth days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and did not ask him. The rival record has been in force sixty-four days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered, and a figure of days about it is not an answer to it.

The day came down about half past eight and the eighth month was four days old and about nineteen people had been in the yard of Lot Seventeen this morning and one of them had asked how many of them there were.

**AND ON THE FOURTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A WOMAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-SIX WHO KEEPS A SCALE ASKED HOW MANY PEOPLE WERE IN THE YARD OF LOT SEVENTEEN AND A MAN OF ABOUT NINETEEN COUNTED THEM AND SAID NINETEEN, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT A HEAD COUNTED IN A YARD IN DAYLIGHT IS A FIGURE ABOUT A YARD AND IS NOT A FIGURE ABOUT ANYBODY, AND THAT NINETEEN AND ABOUT NINE ARE TWO FIGURES AND WERE NOT ADDED TOGETHER AND DID NOT MAKE TWENTY-EIGHT, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
