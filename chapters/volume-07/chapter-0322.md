# Chapter 322: A Table With Four Legs

---

The eleventh of the eighth month came in about the same, and by about ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen and about nine feet of brick at the near end of the lane behind the bank with the sheet of print still lying on a bench against the end wall of it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the twenty-second night.

"**Two hundred and thirty days on the board and five hundred and forty-six days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and sixty, and two hundred and twenty-one days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is ten days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and thirteen days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and forty days, and the table against that wall stood up for the thirty-sixth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table was sat in once, yesterday, and had stood a hundred and nine days before that, and one is not a figure and is not a habit.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that ten days past is ten days past and is not a term in a ladder, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man of about sixty-four came into that gate on the morning of the twenty-first of the seventh month and has been in this district twenty-one days and has slept on that low wall on twenty-one of the twenty-two nights of that run, and that nobody asked him about the one he did not sleep on it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

There is a table with four legs against the outside wall of that building and it has been against that wall for longer than anybody in this yard has been counting, and about ten past eleven the man the figure of twenty-one years is against put his hand flat on the top of it and said to a man who was standing at the end of the lane that he wanted to ask him one question, in daylight, and could say no.

The man was about thirty-eight and he cuts reeds out of the ditch behind that building, and he had a hook in his hand and a bundle about nine inches long over his shoulder, and he had not been asked anything by anybody in that lane before this morning and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The question was whether he would come into that building at two in the morning if it went off, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a table with four legs outside a door is not a counter until somebody asks somebody a question at it, and that it became a counter at about ten past eleven on the eleventh of the eighth month, and that a question is not a form and a form is not a consent.

He said no in about four seconds, and the reason was his own, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and forty-seven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**No. I have got no boots that will go over that ditch in the dark and that ditch is about three feet deep with about a foot of standing water in it, and I have lived on the edge of it for six years, and you have not asked me to come and I am not going to be one of the men who stands in that water at two in the morning because a man asked me at a table at eleven. You have a table and a chair and a door and a curtain on a nail. You have not got a stove. You have not got a floor I have seen. You have not got water. That is a shed with a door on it and it is a good shed and I am not standing in it telling anybody it is a house.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal with a reason is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that a man who cuts reeds is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a ditch and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a shed with a door on it is not a shelter and a shelter is not a shed, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against wanted the yes. He wanted it the way he had wanted the yes all the morning and the morning before, and he caught himself wanting it about two seconds after it had not come, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has wanted an answer and got one is not a man who was represented by the answer, and that wanting an answer and getting one are two things and are not added together, and that a man who cuts reeds said no to a building and not to a district.

He said one thing at about half past twelve, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people at that table with four legs, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and twenty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.
"**That is the first no anybody has said to me in this lane in a month and I asked for it, and I am not going to go round him. I have been calling that building a shelter since yesterday morning and I have been calling it a shelter out loud, and it is a shed with a door on it and he has just told me that in front of about nine people and he is right, and the sheet of print inside it is not a house. A clerk wrote down three things on the seventeenth of the sixth month that a bell wants before this district will call a sounding safe, and the first of them is that a bell that is rung records everybody who hears it for one night, and the second is that a bell rung when there is nothing to answer it cannot be rung again for a month, and the third is that a community has to provide a shelter and a way to take the record off. I am not saying anything about a bell. I am saying the third one is not true and a charter does not make it true, and he has just told me why, and he told me in four seconds and he was not being clever at me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the three things a sounding is to be called safe against are written once in this district and are entered once on this page and are not to be written again, and that a clerk who has written them out loud is not a clerk who has made a finding, and that a charter is a document and the third of the three is a thing that is either true or not true and a document does not make it true, and that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against is not a clerk of a house and has not been asked about any of the three by anybody in this lane and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that there are about nine inches of rope left inside a beam in a frame of oak about eleven feet high four miles out past the loams, and that a rope is a rope and is not a job and is not a decision, and that a bell does not need a person and that a person standing next to a bell is not deciding what it rings, and that nobody in this lane has asked whether it should be pulled and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that it has not been pulled since the night of the twenty-fourth of the sixth month.

---

His right hand had not closed properly since about half past two yesterday afternoon. He put his right hand out to pick a sheet of print off the bench at about two and the clerk of nineteen years had to open it with her fingers, one at a time, the way a person opens a hand that has gone shut, and he said that it was nothing and that it was from yesterday afternoon and that it would come back, and a clerk entered that a hand that will not close is a figure about a hand and is not a figure about a document, and that this one is not the fencer's left hand and is not the hand of the man of about twenty-three who copies for nothing and is not the arm of the man of about thirty-four who digs loam, and that four hands and an arm in this district are five different things and are not added together.

He carried two buckets of water in from the top of that lane at about half past two and had to make two trips because the right hand would not hold the handle of a full one, and put them down inside that building against the end wall where the chair had stood that morning, and stood looking at them for a while. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a bucket is not a stove and not a bed and not a figure of anything this district counts, and that two trips with one in each hand is a figure about two trips and is not a figure about anybody, and that nobody in that lane asked him why he was carrying water and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The rest of that day went the way the days of this month have gone. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read five lines of print out loud in that yard at about half past four and got through all of them. The man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing was at the end of that trestle table at about five and did not take a piece of paper. The tent was still rolled on the back of that hand-cart against the wall inside that gate, and a clerk entered that a tent rolled on a hand-cart is the same this evening as it was on the thirty-first of the seventh month and that neither of them is a figure about a man and that neither of them is a figure about a building with two doors.

What had not moved on the eleventh of the eighth month went in at the end of the day in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and thirty-third days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and a question asked at a table with four legs is not a line on a board. The sixth of the same shape of thing with a word over it and nothing under it was ruled yesterday inside a building and is not filled and is not a fifth column of not-askings. The rival record has been in force seventy-one days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week, eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and the three are three figures and are not added together, and the no at that table this morning is none of the three and is not a ninth or an eighth or a seventh of anything.

The day came down about half past eight and the first day of the eighth month was ten days past, and there were about nine feet of brick at the near end of that lane with two buckets of water and a stool and a sheet of print on a bench in it, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE ELEVENTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH THE TABLE WITH FOUR LEGS AGAINST THE OUTSIDE WALL OF THAT BUILDING BECAME THE PLACE A QUESTION WAS ASKED AT, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-EIGHT WHO CUTS REEDS OUT OF THE DITCH BEHIND IT SAID NO IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS WITH A REASON OF HIS OWN AND CALLED IT A SHED WITH A DOOR ON IT, AND THE MAN THE FIGURE OF TWENTY-ONE YEARS IS AGAINST SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE WAS WRONG ABOUT IT AND DID NOT GO ROUND HIM, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
