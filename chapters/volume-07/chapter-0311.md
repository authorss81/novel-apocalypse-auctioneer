# Chapter 311: One

---

The thirty-first of the seventh month came in white and did not get any cooler after eleven, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him, on the same shaded side, in the same place, for the eleventh night running.

"**Two hundred and nineteen days on the board and five hundred and thirty-five days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and forty-nine, and two hundred and ten days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is one day off counted off this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and two days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and twenty-nine days, and the table against that wall stood up for the twenty-fifth time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood ninety-nine days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that one is no days to the end of the seventh month plus the first of the eighth, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that the day after the one is the first of the eighth month and does not have a figure in front of it at all, **and that a man of about sixty-four came into that gate on the twenty-first of this month and has been in this district ten days this morning, a stay being counted without the day a man arrived on, and that ten is one more than the nine that was on that table yesterday and that the two are two figures about the same man and are not added together and do not make nineteen.** She entered that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning on the thirty-first of the seventh month, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that one is a figure about a ladder and is not a figure about anybody and is not a finding. She entered that nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and twenty-second days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that the board outside the room four hundred yards from that gate still carries the twenty-fourth of December under the dust and was not washed on the last day of the seventh month. She entered that the man of about sixty-four came into that gate on the morning of the twenty-first of this month and has slept on that low wall on ten of the eleven nights of that run and has not slept on it on one of them, and that nobody in that yard has asked him about the one he did not sleep on it and the record about the not asking says not asked. She entered that the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and first days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and that she entered that figure out of a ladder and did not ask him for it, and that he is a man of about forty-eight and not thirty-four and has said of himself that he is fifty-one. She entered that the rival record has been in force sixty days and has been read out in that yard seven times and that its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and that it is unanswered, and that the last day of the seventh month did not answer it.

The man of fifty-six said one thing about the calendar at about half past ten, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fifty-two, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**This yard says last month and it has meant a different month every time the calendar has come round, and it has done it three times in this basin, and I have watched all three and two of the three happened in the night while nobody was looking. This morning in this yard last month is the sixth month. From tonight it is the seventh, and the month before last goes from the fifth to the sixth, and the third month goes from three months back to four, and four is the right number and three is the number this district has been saying since the first of the sixth month, and a clerk is going to write four on this page and is not going back to the old pages to change them. I would like that entered. I said it out loud in a yard and not in a room.**"

A clerk of nineteen years wrote the whole of it down in one hand, on one line, with the words *last month* and *the month before last* set out in a column of their own, and she entered that a relative month is a figure about words and is not a figure about a date, and that this district has turned that word three times since the first of the sixth month, and that it did it in the night the first two times and it did it again in the night last night, and that nobody in this district was awake to see any of the three except the one this district is in, and that a word somebody has been given is not a description of anybody, and that the third month is four months back from the seventh month and was entered as three months back on every page since the first of the sixth month and has not been altered on any of them and was not altered this morning either.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing about the ladder at about a quarter to eleven, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and sixty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**The whole of that ladder is one. I read the eight of them out loud in this yard on the twenty-eighth of the seventh month with the day named against each one, eleven down to four, and it has been three a day since, and this is the last of them and the whole of that ladder ends at one, and there is nothing behind it at all. The day after this one is the first of the eighth month and it does not have a figure in front of it at all, and I have been looking for that figure on four mornings and it is not there and it is not going to be there tomorrow either, and a ladder that runs out is not a ladder that has been broken. It is a ladder that has arrived. I do not know what that is worth and I am not going to guess at it in a yard.**"

At about one the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale had four people being weighed one after another at the bank about two hundred yards down that lane, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam went down the lane at about half past ten to help her lift a sack off the ground and could not get his right arm above the level of that shoulder to take the weight of it, and took the weight of it with his left and with his knees and put it down on the blanket, and she said nothing to him about it and he said nothing to her, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two people carried one sack between them at the bank on the last day of the seventh month and that nobody asked either of them whether they wanted the other one there.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a ladder is a figure about a ladder and is not about anybody, and that a man who has said out loud that he does not know what a figure is worth has not withdrawn the figure and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The heat broke about two in the afternoon and came back by four. The boards at the near end of that lane lifted at four joints by ten and had gone down again by themselves twice, and about nine people put their coats on the end of that trestle table and stood on the shaded side of it, and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the foot of the east wall with his hand-cart against it and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and a clerk entered that it has been rolled since the thirtieth of the sixth month and is not a lean-to and did not become a sixth of the five protected things.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud twice that afternoon, once at about two and once at about half past four, and the man of about sixty-four read them over about nine feet of that yard and came no nearer the second time than he had the first, and a clerk entered that nine feet is a figure about nine feet and is not a figure about the distance between them.

A woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat came down to the foot of that gatepost at about four with a bucket in each hand and stood there for about four minutes looking at the two sheets on the post, and did not read either of them out loud and did not ask anybody what was on them, and then went back up that lane to the third house from the scale, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who keeps a goat came and looked at a sheet and asked nobody what was on it, and that she is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of anything, and that nobody asked her afterwards what she thought of it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past five the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was at the end of that table with his leg out in front of him under it and had been there about four hours on his own two feet, and a clerk entered that four hours is a figure about four hours and is not a figure about a man and is not the second payment of anything, and that a leg is not a figure and a leg is not on a board.

About four of the nineteen went up the lane at about six. Both of the two sheets on that gatepost were still on it at the height of a man's shoulder, one above the other, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put his hand flat on the wood beside them and left it there for the length of a breath and took it away again, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has put a hand on a gatepost beside a sheet and taken it away has not touched the sheet and has not been asked to and cannot be counted either way, and that the number on the top one of those two sheets is four hundred and eleven and has been four hundred and eleven on every morning a man has read it out in this yard since the first of the sixth month, and that no day this district has ever named is the day that whoever nailed it in did anything. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print out loud a third time at about six for three people who had come in off the lane late and had not been in that yard all day, and the man of about twenty-three who sells nothing and copies for nothing stood at the foot of the table and did not take a piece of paper this time, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who copies for nothing did not copy on the last day of the seventh month and that a copy is not a document and did not become a fourth of the three, and that nobody has asked him whether he would.

A man of about forty with a cart of his own came up the lane at about six and stopped about nine feet off the end of that table and said one thing out loud to nobody, and it was not counted, and a clerk entered that it was not counted, and that what he said was that he was going to take his cart up the eleven mile road in the morning and look at the far end of that road, and that a man who says out loud what he is going to look at tomorrow has not been asked to and cannot be counted either way, and that nobody in that yard told him not to.

A man of about forty-five who digs and fills in holes for anybody who asks him came up that lane at about six with a bar of iron in one hand and nothing in the other and stopped at the gate and asked one question in the ordinary voice, which was whether anybody in that yard had lost a stake, and nobody had, and he said all right and went up the lane. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he had asked one question of about nineteen people and that about nineteen is a figure about about nineteen people, and that a man who digs and fills in holes and asks whether anybody has lost a stake is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of anything and has not been asked to be one.

The day came down about half past eight and the seventh month was thirty-one days old and the first day of the eighth month was one day off, and the rule changed in the night, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE THIRTY-FIRST OF THE SEVENTH MONTH THE LADDER OFF THE FIRST DAY OF THE EIGHTH MONTH STOOD AT ONE, AND THE DAY AFTER THE ONE WAS THE FIRST OF THE EIGHTH MONTH AND DID NOT HAVE A FIGURE IN FRONT OF IT AT ALL, AND A CLERK WROTE DOWN IN ONE HAND THAT LAST MONTH IN THAT YARD MEANT THE SIXTH AT TEN IN THE MORNING AND THE SEVENTH FROM TONIGHT, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
