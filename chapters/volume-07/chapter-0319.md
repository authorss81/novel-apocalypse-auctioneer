# Chapter 319: The Fifth of the Same Shape

---

The eighth of the eighth month came in about the same and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the sheet of five lines of print with a word over a line on it was still on the end of that trestle table where it has lain since about ten yesterday.

"**Two hundred and twenty-seven days on the board and five hundred and forty-three days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and fifty-seven, and two hundred and eighteen days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is seven days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and ten days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and thirty-seven days, and the table against that wall stood up for the thirty-third time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood a hundred and seven days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that seven days past is seven days past and is not a term in a ladder, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that a man of about sixty-four has been in this district eighteen days, a stay being counted without the day he arrived on, and that he came into that gate on the morning of the twenty-first of the seventh month and has slept on that wall on eighteen of the nineteen nights of that run and has not slept on it on one of them, and that nobody asked him about the one he did not sleep on it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

She entered the five in the same hand and not as a paragraph, because it is the only thing on the page this morning that is a set of five. **One: the printed sheet nailed to a gatepost, which carries a word over a column and has nothing under it, and a number under nothing, and has stood at the height of a man's shoulder since the first of the sixth month. Two: the third column of a covenant in a book a stranger may walk up to, ruled and empty on its face, and it has been empty on every day since the tenth of the sixth month, which is the first day a clerk of nineteen years entered it. Three: a line under a heading on the ninth of the nine charters in nine rooms, and nobody has been put under it, and the record about the not asking says not asked. Four: the ninth place on a page that came out of a cave, ruled, and empty, and not filled on any day since the tenth of the sixth month. Five: the sheet that came off a stake in that gatepost yesterday morning, with a word over a line and nothing on the line.** A clerk of nineteen years entered that the five are the same shape of thing, and that none of the five is a person, and that the five are not added together, and that a column of a thing with a word over it is not a column of people, and that a document which names a job that does not exist has found nobody, and that two of them were ruled and left empty in this district and are still empty this morning, and that neither of them has been filled on any day since the tenth of the sixth month, which is fifty-nine days back this morning.

---

The man of fifty-six said one thing about that at about half past ten, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and eighty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Five of them. I have been in this yard five months saying that a door is not a keeper and a wall is not an address, and I never once counted the shapes, and this morning a clerk has counted five of them and has written them all down, and I am going to say the only thing I have got about it and then I am going to stop. Four of those five have been empty for a long time. A long time is not a figure. Two of them were ruled by somebody and left empty, and one of them had a name put over it and nothing under it, and one of them came out of a hole in a wall. Not one of the five has got anybody in it and not one of the five has ever been anybody's job, and I have never seen a man or a woman put under a heading in this basin because they said yes. That is not an argument. I have not got an argument. I have got five shapes and they are all the same shape.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six is not a clerk and has said so about himself about a dozen times in four months, and that a man who has said out loud that he has got five shapes and not got an argument has not made a finding and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who has counted five shapes has not counted a single person while he was counting them, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing about the sheet at about eleven, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and eighty-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That sheet came into this yard yesterday off the top of a stake and the second line of it names a book four hundred yards from this gate, and I have read that sheet out loud to about nineteen people in this yard, and there is one person in this basin who may write in that book, and I have not asked her anything about it, and I am not going to, and nobody in this yard has asked her anything about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked. On the twelfth of the seventh month she said out loud in a lane that she was not going to be asked about it. I was in that lane. I did not ask her anything then either. A sheet that names a book is not a person in it, and a person who may write in a book has not refused to and has not agreed to and cannot be counted either way, and nobody in this yard has put a name in that column and nobody is going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a man who mends fencing is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a book and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a sheet which names a book four hundred yards from a gate is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own and did not become one.

---

The room four hundred yards from that gate is about nine feet by eleven with a shut door and a handle and a step of two stones and a fanlight over the door that lights nothing, and there is a shelf in it and there are two books on the shelf and one of them has been open since the sixth of the sixth month with nothing entered in it, and nobody in this district has asked the one person who may write in them for anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of that on the eighth of the eighth month and entered that a shut door is not a refusal and is not a consent and cannot be counted either way, and that a fanlight that lights nothing is a piece of glass and is not a keeper of a room, and that a step of two stones is a step and is not a threshold and is not a document.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing about that room at about half past twelve, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about four people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and twenty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Four hundred yards. I have walked up that bank about nine times in a month and down it about nine times and I have not knocked on that door in any of them, and I am not going to today, and nobody has asked me to. There is one person in this basin who can write in that book, and there is a sheet of print lying on the end of that table that names it, and I have read that sheet out loud and I have not handed it to her, and I am not going to, and I would like that entered as a thing a man did not do and not as a thing a man did.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who reads things out loud in a yard is not a courier and is not a bearer of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to carry a sheet four hundred yards to a door has not refused to carry it and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The rest of that day went the way the seventh of it went. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart read the five lines of print out loud in about nine feet of lane outside that room to about four people at about two, for a woman who had come down the lane on hearing that there was a new one, and got nothing wrong the second time either, and a clerk entered that a thing read back right twice is two readings and is not a habit and is not a finding. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the end of that trestle table at about four and put his right hand flat on the ruled line under the third of the five lines and left it there for about a minute and did not put anything on it, and a clerk entered that a man who puts his hand on a ruled line and leaves it there has not written on it and has not been asked to and cannot be counted either way.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the bank with about four people being weighed one after another and did not come up the lane, and the tent was still rolled on the back of that hand-cart against the wall inside that gate, and a clerk entered that a tent has been rolled since the thirtieth of the sixth month and is not a lean-to and is not a shelter and is not a sixth of the five protected things in this district, and that it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month.

What had not moved on the eighth of the eighth month went in at the end of the day in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and thirtieth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The chair at the end of that trestle table has stood a hundred and seven days and nobody has sat in it on any of them. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and ninth days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for. The rival record has been in force sixty-eight days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered, and a fifth of a shape of thing is not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and a column with a word over it and nothing under it is not a fourth of them and is not a document that registers anybody.

The day came down about half past eight and the eighth month was eight days old, and the second book in the room four hundred yards from that gate was open where it has been open since the sixth of the sixth month, and nothing has been entered in it, and nobody has been asked.

**AND ON THE EIGHTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT A COLUMN OF A THING WITH A WORD OVER IT IS THE FIFTH OF THE SAME SHAPE OF THING IN THIS DISTRICT AND THAT NONE OF THE FIVE IS A PERSON AND THAT THE FIVE ARE NOT ADDED TOGETHER, AND THAT TWO OF THEM WERE RULED AND LEFT EMPTY IN THIS DISTRICT AND ARE STILL EMPTY, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
