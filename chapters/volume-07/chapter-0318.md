# Chapter 318: A Word Over a Line

---

The seventh of the eighth month came in about the same and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right, and there was a folded sheet of print on the end of that trestle table that had not been on it yesterday.

"**Two hundred and twenty-six days on the board and five hundred and forty-two days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and fifty-six, and two hundred and seventeen days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is six days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and nine days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and thirty-six days, and the table against that wall stood up for the thirty-second time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table has stood a hundred and six days and nobody has sat in it on any of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that six days past is six days past and is not a term in a ladder, and that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning on the seventh of the eighth month, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that a document arrived in that yard this morning and is not a fourth of the three this district does not own and is entered as not a fourth.

---

He had it in his hand at about ten. He was a man of about forty-five who digs and fills in holes for anybody who asks him, and he had been in that yard four days before asking whether anybody had lost a stake, and this morning he had taken the top of a stake out of the soft ground by that gatepost at about six, because it had gone in loose in the night and was standing at an angle, and the sheet had been folded over the top of it in two and had come away with it, and he had brought it up that lane in his left hand and had not read it and had not folded it any tighter than it was folded.

"**I do not know who left that. I took the top of a stake out at about six this morning because it was standing at an angle and it came away with about a sheet of print folded over it, and I have brought it up here because I do not know what anybody in this yard wants me to do with it, and I am not going to stand in this yard and guess. I dug that out of the ground. Nobody asked me for it, and that is the whole of what I have got to say about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who digs and fills in holes for anybody who asks him has dug this one and has not been asked for it, and that whoever folded that sheet over the top of a stake in that gatepost is not in this district and has never been asked about and is not on the sheet and has never been on any sheet in this district, and that a nail in a gatepost is not a keeper and whoever drove one in is not on the sheet, and that a person who leaves a thing at a gate is not a party of anything and has not been asked to be one.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the end of that trestle table at about half past ten and asked for the sheet to be read out loud, in daylight, in that yard, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a document is introduced as a thing somebody reads out and is read back afterwards because that is the rule of the counter, and that the request was made by a man standing in that yard and not by anybody four hundred miles off, and that no body has been asked for it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read it out loud once, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and it is five lines of print with a ruled line under the third of them, and it reads as it stands.

> **A FIGURE OF THE PEOPLE IN THE GROUND IN THE SHADOW OF A BUILDING ELEVEN MILES OFF THIS DISTRICT IS TO BE COMPLETED.**
>
> **THE FIGURE IS TO BE COMPLETED IN THE SECOND BOOK OF THE ROOM FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM THE GATE AND IS NOT TO BE ENTERED TWICE.**
>
> **COMPLETED BY**
>
> **_____________________________**
>
> **A FIGURE THAT IS TO BE COMPLETED IS NOT A RATE AND THE FIGURE ON A SHEET AT A GATEPOST IS NOT TO BE ENTERED AGAIN AND IS NOT TO BE ASKED FOR A SECOND TIME.**

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart read all five of those lines back from the top in the ordinary voice to about nine people and got nothing wrong, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the rule of the counter is that a thing read back wrong is read back again from the top and not from the word, and that it was read back right the first time, and that getting it right once is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about anybody.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that that sheet names no person, and names no body, and names no place, and names one building eleven miles off this district and nothing else, and that it carries no name and no seal and no signature and nothing at the bottom of it, and that a word over a line with nothing on the line is a column of a thing and is not a column of people, and that a document which names a job that does not exist has found nobody, and that nobody in this district is to be entered in that column and did not ask to be, and that the five lines of print on the end of that trestle table and the piece of paper with two lines and a chalk mark of a third started are not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own and this one is not a fourth of them either, and that three documents this district does not own is three and is not four.

---

The rest of that day went over the sheet. It lay on the end of that trestle table from about half past ten until the light went, and about nine people looked at the third of its five lines and the ruled line under it and said nothing about it to each other, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that about nine people looking at a thing and not saying anything about it is a figure about about nine people and is not a figure about the thing and is not a finding.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the end of that table at about one and looked at the ruled line under the third of the five lines for a while and then said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and fifty-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That is a line with a word over it and nothing on it, and a line like that is a line any one of us could put a name on with a piece of chalk, and everybody in this yard has seen where the chalk is because it is tied to the leg of that board. I am not going to be the one. I have got an arm that has not gone above my shoulder since the first of the seventh month and I could not get a sheet of paper off this table with it on the second of this month, and I am not going to reach over this one and put a name on a line that somebody four hundred miles off ruled for me. That is the whole of what I have got and I am not going to make it into a reason for anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who digs loam is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to put a name on a line has not refused to put one and cannot be counted either way, and that a mark may not carry a job and a printed line may not either, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was asked by the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, in the ordinary voice and to nobody in particular, whether a person does anything to a line, and said that a person does not do anything to a line, and that a line is a mark somebody else made, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that a person does not do anything to a line has not refused to do anything and cannot be counted either way, and that a line is a mark somebody else made and neither of them is a seventh of the six instruments.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing about it at about half past four, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and forty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**There is a word over a line on that sheet and there is nothing on the line, and I have read that sheet out twice this afternoon for people coming in off the lane, and both times the first two lines went by and about nine people stood there and then somebody said the word *completed* and then there was nothing for about nine seconds. A word over a line with nothing on it is not a column of people. It is a line with a word on it, and I have read it out twice, and neither of those two times has put a name near it, and I am not going to say what it is for, because I have not been asked and because a man who reads a thing out loud is not the man who made it and has not been asked to be.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who reads a thing out loud in a yard is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of a document and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he is not going to say what a line is for has not refused to say it and cannot be counted either way, and that about nine seconds is a figure about about nine seconds and is not a figure about anybody.

What had not moved on the seventh of the eighth month went in at the end of the day in the same hand and not as a paragraph. Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and twenty-ninth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and a sheet of print that has come into a yard and been read out is not a line on a board. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and eighth days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder and not asked for. The rival record has been in force sixty-seven days and has been read out in that yard seven times and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and it is unanswered. Five protected things and no sixth, and a line with a word over it and nothing under it is not a sixth, and a stake is not a sixth, and a sheet folded over a stake is not a sixth. The instruments this district has built and not named are six, and a printed line may not carry a job and this one does not carry one, and nothing in that yard today became a seventh of them.

The day came down about half past eight and the eighth month was seven days old and a folded sheet of print with a word over a line and nothing on the line was lying on the end of that trestle table, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE SEVENTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A FOLDED SHEET OF PRINT CAME OFF THE TOP OF A STAKE IN THAT GATEPOST AND WAS READ OUT ALOUD IN DAYLIGHT AT A MAN'S REQUEST AND READ BACK FROM THE TOP AND GOT NOTHING WRONG, AND IT NAMES NO PERSON AND NO BODY AND NO PLACE AND IT CARRIES A WORD OVER A LINE WITH NOTHING ON THE LINE, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT A COLUMN OF A THING WITH A WORD OVER IT IS NOT A COLUMN OF PEOPLE AND THAT A DOCUMENT WHICH NAMES A JOB THAT DOES NOT EXIST HAS FOUND NOBODY, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
