# Chapter 328: The Proof Is Unavailable

---

About nineteen people were in that yard by ten on the seventeenth of the eighth month and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the board out over his shoulder and got the other two right. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the twenty-eighth night, and the blanket from the bedroll at the foot of that wall had been folded and unfolded twice that week and was damp on the underside.

"**Two hundred and thirty-six days on the board and five hundred and fifty-two days on the train, and the days nobody has entered since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and sixty-six, and two hundred and twenty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is sixteen days past this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and nineteen days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is four months back and a hundred and forty-six days, and the table against that wall stood up for the forty-second time this morning, and the chair at the end of this table was sat in once on the tenth of this month by a man who had not been asked to, and one is not a figure and is not a habit.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the eighth month is not stated in this district and was not used this morning, and that sixteen days past is a figure and is not a term. A man of about sixty-four has been in this district twenty-seven days and has slept on that low wall on twenty-seven of the twenty-eight nights of that run, and nobody asked him about the one he did not and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked him one question at about half past ten, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got eighty-four words, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Eight lines and a stone on the corner and two terms and a room a stranger can walk up to. How do we know whether any of it works. I am not asking you whether you think it works, because a man thinks his own document works and that is a figure about the man. I am asking you how anybody in this basin finds out, on some morning in six months, whether that sheet did the thing or did not do the thing.**"

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against went round the back of that building and looked at the ditch for about nine minutes and came back in and stood about four feet off the end of that trestle table for about twenty minutes without saying anything at anybody, and then he said one thing, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and eighty-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**There is no way. I have gone through it in my head twice this morning and there is no way and I am going to say so out loud in daylight. The only people who can tell you whether that roll works are the people it is for, and not one of them has agreed to be told anything, and I am not allowed to ask, and if I asked then the answer would be an answer to my question and not a finding. A man could stand in that yard every morning for a year counting how many names go on that line and he would have counted how many names went on a line. The people it is for are not in the room, they are the ones we have not asked, and they are the only ones whose answer would be worth anything, and we have not got it and we are not going to get it. The proof is not unavailable today. It is not going to become available. That is not a gap that closes. That is what it is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a document built for persons who have not been asked has not been tested against anybody, and that it is being signed anyway, and that this district has entered that on the seventeenth of the eighth month with the signer's hand on the bench beside it, and that a proof being unavailable is not a finding and is not a failure and is not a promise about six months, and that this is what a convening is, and that a convening is not the same thing as a safe thing, and that the two words are not the same word, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

He went into the building with two doors at about half past three and ruled a line at the foot of that sheet about two inches long and put his name on it, in chalk, with his left hand, because his right hand would not close at all by then and there is no ink in that building and never has been, and the mark came out small and pale and about a sixteenth of an inch off the line at one end, and he looked at it for a while and did not rub it out.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the line at the foot of that roll carries one name on it and that the name is his own and that the name is his own on his own line, and that a clerk of nineteen years did not write it and has not been asked to and is not going to, and that a name on a line in a room a stranger may walk up to is a name a stranger can be shown, and that the two pieces of paper in the book four hundred yards from that gate are not corrected by anything written this month and are not taken out of it, and that one name in a room four hundred yards from a gate is not one name added to the four hundred and eleven and does not make four hundred and twelve, and that the four hundred and eleven is a figure about the people who answered a door and a name on a line is a figure about a man.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a line with a name on it is not a line with a word over it and nothing under it, and that the six shapes of a thing in this district are the six and this is not a seventh of them, and that a mark may not carry a job and that this mark carries no job, and that the man who put it there is not a keeper of that roll and does not become one and has not been asked to be one.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was in that yard at about half past four and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was told by the man the figure of twenty-one years is against that the roll had been signed and that nobody asked him to carry that news to anybody and that he did not carry it anywhere and told one person and that one person was the man of about fifty-six, and that a thing heard by two people is not a record and is not a document and did not become a fourth of the three.

He went up the bank to the room four hundred yards from that gate at about five with the sheet of five lines of print in his coat, and stood on the step of two stones where a man of about sixty-four had laid it flat on the ninth of the eighth month, and did not pick it up and did not knock, and stood there for about eleven minutes with a figure going up by one every morning in his head that had nothing to do with him, and understood that a document a stranger may walk up to and read is worth more to him than a document in a room, and that he is the registrar of this district's records office, and that a registrar who would rather have a document left on a step outside a shut door than carried into a room four hundred yards from a gate is a man whose whole trade has been turned over in a fortnight, and that nobody asked him about it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood on that step at about five on the seventeenth of the eighth month for about eleven minutes and did not pick up the sheet on it and did not knock, and that a sheet on a step is a state and not a place, and that where a sheet is is not an answer to anything, and that a man standing on a step is not holding the sheet and is not holding the door and is not holding anything, and that a person standing in a doorway is not holding the door.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing one question at about half past six in the ordinary voice, in about nine feet of lane outside that room, which was whether a document that has not been tested against anybody is a document or is a piece of paper, and was told that he did not know and that he was not going to guess again, and a clerk entered that it was the second time in eleven days that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had been asked a question of that kind and had not answered it, and that a man who has said out loud that he does not know a second time has not made a habit of it and has not made a finding and cannot be counted either way.

The rest of that day went over the signature. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that lane at about five and stood at the end of that trestle table and did not say anything and went back down the lane, and a clerk entered that a man who has come and said nothing and gone has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way. The woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat was at the foot of that gatepost at about six and read the two sheets on that post and went back up the lane, and a clerk entered that a woman who keeps a goat came and read a sheet and asked nobody what was on it.

A name written in chalk on a sheet on a bench is not a line on a board, and nobody has put one on any of the hundred and thirty-ninth days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the hundred and eighteenth days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, entered out of a ladder, and nobody has asked him for anything on any day of this month. There are nine refusals of eleven coppers a week, eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given and seven refusals about the ninth holding, and they are three figures and are not added together, and none of the three is a tenth or a ninth or an eighth of the thing that was written this month, and the three refusals given in that yard this month are not a figure about any of them. The rival record has been in force seventy-seven days and is unanswered, and a signature is not an answer to it. Three documents this district does not own, and a roll this district made is not a fourth of them and is not a document nobody owns. Five protected things and no sixth, and a name is not a sixth and a piece of chalk is not a sixth.

The day came down about half past eight and the first day of the eighth month was sixteen days past, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the figure in this district that a document is supposed to be worth is still the one it was at ten in the morning.

**AND ON THE SEVENTEENTH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH SOMEBODY ASKED A QUESTION OF A DOCUMENT AND THERE WAS NO WAY OF FINDING OUT WHETHER IT WORKED, BECAUSE THE ONLY PEOPLE WHO COULD TELL YOU ARE THE PEOPLE IT IS FOR AND NONE OF THEM HAS AGREED TO BE TOLD, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT A DOCUMENT BUILT FOR PERSONS WHO HAVE NOT BEEN ASKED HAS NOT BEEN TESTED AGAINST ANYBODY AND IS BEING SIGNED ANYWAY, AND THAT IS WHAT A CONVENING IS, AND A CONVENING IS NOT THE SAME THING AS A SAFE THING, AND THE TWO WORDS ARE NOT THE SAME WORD. THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
