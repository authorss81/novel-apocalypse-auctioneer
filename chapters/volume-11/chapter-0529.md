# Chapter 529: He Will Not Be The Reason There Are Three

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was asked, out loud, in front of about nineteen people at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the twenty-fifth of the second month, whether he would take that square of paper up the lane to the first channel and nail it on that post, and he said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and nobody asked him a second question, and about nine people have said since that a man who has been asked three questions about one sheet of paper in a fortnight and has answered all three the same way has now been asked a fourth one and got a different answer.

It was the twenty-fifth morning of the second month and there was a thin white frost on that ground at about seven that had gone off the boards before about half past seven, and the light came up off the south end of that ground flat and grey, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and sixty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fifty-fourth of them, and that about four of them have said that a man who said on the twentieth morning of this month that he had never seen anybody make anything has not been asked about it again by anybody.

"**The board carries four hundred and twenty-seven, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and forty-three days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and fifty-seven, and four hundred and eighteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He was asked it at about eleven and he said it in about four seconds without stopping what he was doing with the strap, and about nineteen people heard the whole of it and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got sixty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. I have carried one of them up that lane once because a person I did not know told me to and I have not stopped thinking about it for a month, and I am not going to be the reason there are three of them. If that paper goes on that post it is because somebody who has not been asked wants it there.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who has answered three questions the same way gave a fourth answer, and entered that she is not entering that as a change of mind, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was in that yard at about ten and said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Then it is going to be a woman with a ladder and a bucket of something, or a man who has not been asked, and about four of us have worked out that the second one is cheaper and about four of us have worked out that the second one is how the last one got here, and I have a ladder and I am not going to use it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk is not entering either of the two things about four people have worked out, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the man of about sixty-four a question, out loud, at about nine feet off that low wall, in front of about nineteen people, and about nine people within four feet of that wall have said since that is the second time in six volumes anybody has asked that man anything at all and it is the first time anybody has asked him about a sheet of paper.

A clerk of nineteen years said one thing, in the ordinary voice, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have four figures about you on that page and one of them is this morning's and I want to know one thing that is not about you, and I am asking you in front of these people because I asked you something about yourself a fortnight ago and you told me you were not thanking me for it, and this one is not that either.**"

He looked at her for about four seconds and then he answered, in the ordinary voice, in about nine seconds, and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have not seen that paper before this morning. I have not seen the one on that table either. I come up that lane twice a day and I go past that yard and I do not stop, and nobody has ever asked me what is on either of those tables, and you are the first one and you have asked me twice.**"

Then he said one more thing, in about four seconds, and about nine people heard it and the boy counted it and got nineteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not thanking you for it and I am not going to do anything different because of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she had asked a man a question in that yard for the second time in six volumes and entered that asking is not offering, and entered that she entered the same sentence about it a fortnight ago and that she is entering it again because it is the same sentence and not because it is a new one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and said one thing after it in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got seventy-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a man at the foot of that wall who has walked past that table twice a day for a month and has never once looked at it, and about four of you have been asking me who put that paper there, and there is a man in that yard who has not looked at it either, and that is the same thing twice and I am the only one who has said so.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a finding about anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man who comes up that lane twice a day and a man who walks past that yard twice a day are two of the same sort of person in that district and that about four of them have said so out loud in a yard this morning. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at that first table have said since that a man who said no to a question this morning did not stop at that first table at three, and that the two of those are the first and the fourth time. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody had anything to give him, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and a clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and sixty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and seventy-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and thirty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and thirty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and ten days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and forty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Eighty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are seventy-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man has said out loud in that yard that he will not be the reason there are three of a thing, and that the two out figures are still out. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man saying he will not carry a thing is not a thirteenth, and about four people at that first table have said since that this is the first refusal this month that anybody in that yard has heard from a man who was asked a question he could have said yes to. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and a man at the foot of that low wall was asked this morning for the second time in six volumes and said he has not looked at either table in a month. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about six people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a woman with a ladder she is not going to use is not one of the six.

About half past five the two buckets went down that bank empty and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near the top of that bank have said since that two men went past that shop this evening without stopping and that a man who was asked a question there in the morning is one of them. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall for about a minute afterwards without moving.

A man who was asked whether he would nail that square of paper on a post at the first channel said no in about four seconds and said he is not going to be the reason there are three of them, and a woman with a ladder said she is not going to use it, and a man at the foot of that low wall was asked for the second time in six volumes and said he has not looked at either table in a month, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the twenty-ninth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
