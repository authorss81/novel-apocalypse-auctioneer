# Chapter 521: He Put The Post Up And Not The Paper

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said, out loud, in front of about nineteen people at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the seventeenth of the second month, that he has seen that shape on a post at the first channel, and that he put that post up himself in the last month of last volume, and that he put nothing on it, and about nine people have said since that this is the first sighting of that shape anywhere that is not one table or another table.

It was the seventeenth morning of the second month and the ground under that yard had been wet since about four in the morning and had not dried at the south end by about eleven, and the boards of that second table were dry because they are off the ground on four legs he built himself, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and sixty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and forty-sixth of them, and that he has not been down that bank since the sixteenth morning of this month.

"**The board carries four hundred and nineteen, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and thirty-five days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and forty-nine, and four hundred and ten days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He said it at about eleven with a length of batten over his shoulder and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have seen that shape on a post at the first channel. Two lines and nothing in the third, and a tear on one edge, and it has been on that post since before the last month ended, and I want to say two things about it and both of them are about me. I put that post up. I took the old one out and I set a new one in and I drove two nails in it in about a quarter of an hour, and I put nothing on it and I have put nothing on it since.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts things up has now said out loud that he put one of the two places and not the other, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the boy of about nineteen asked him, out loud, in front of about nine people, who has been near that post, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**If you are asking me who nailed that paper up then I am going to tell you that I do not know and that is not a figure and I am not going to give you one. If you are asking me who walks past it then I cannot tell you that either, because about forty people walk up that bank to the washing step every morning and I am one of them and I do not count the others.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering forty, and entered the reason, which is that a man saying he does not count the others is not a count, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally, who has stood at the east end of that yard for about four hours a day for about thirty years, said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, from about nine feet off that first table, and the boy counted it and got fifty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have kept a tally in this district for about thirty years and I have never in my life had a column for how many times anybody has walked past a thing, and if I ever do it will not be for a piece of paper somebody has nailed to a post.**"

About nine people at the east end of that yard have said since that a man who has said one whole sentence out loud in that yard in six volumes has now said two, and that the second one was four seconds long and was not addressed to anybody, and that nobody in that yard is going to ask him a third question about it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he was not asked for it and entered that she did not ask.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and said one thing after it in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A post at the first channel and a post at the second channel and a woman who has taken in washing at the second channel for about nine years, and this district has one figure for how many days a man has said four things out loud and has not got one for how many posts there are.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that there are three places now where that shape has been seen and that a clerk has entered all three of them and has entered no figure for any of the three, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten and said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man who puts things up has just told this yard that he put up a post and did not put up a page, and I want it entered that a man who mends fencing heard that and did not say one word about it until after the man who keeps a tally had spoken, and that is the first time this month I have waited for somebody else to say a thing in that yard before I said mine.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a concession, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel was in that yard at about ten and did not go down to the first channel, and a clerk entered that she was in that yard and entered that she did not go and entered that she did not ask and entered the reason, and about four people at that first table have said since that a woman who has been up that lane six times this month did not go and look at a post this morning, and that about four of them have said that not going to look is a different thing from not being able to.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that the first channel is on the far side of where the eleven miles end, and that everybody in that yard knows that and nobody has said it out loud in that yard. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at that first table have said since that he came up that lane with a strap in his hand and did not stop at that first table either, and that two of those people have said that the man of about twenty-nine is the only person in this district who goes to the first channel and does not stop. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man at the foot of it has now been about eleven feet off two things said out loud in a yard and has been asked nothing on any of the two mornings. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and sixtieth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and sixty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and twenty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man and that date is on a counter in a shop in a hand that is not his. A hundred and twenty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and thirty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Seventy-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are sixty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and a boy asked in a yard on the twenty-eighth morning of last month for one and got nobody knows, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man who puts things up has this morning put a third place on that list and still nothing has been counted. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a post at the first channel is not a thirteenth and a sighting is not an instrument. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and there is now a fourth place in this district where those three lines have been read by somebody. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and two men said whole sentences out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a post somebody has not put anything on is not one of the six.

At about half past five both buckets went down that bank and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read those three lines in the book standing up as she went by and said nothing to anybody, and nobody said a word to her, and about four people near the bottom of that bank have said since that a woman who reads those three lines has been walking past a page in a shop twice a day for nine days and has not looked at it once. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and did not move away from that wall for about a minute and a half afterwards.

A shape that has been on a table in that yard for eighteen mornings and on a counter in a shop for five has now been seen on a post at the first channel, and the man who put that post up has said out loud in front of about nineteen people that he put the post up and did not put the page up, and a clerk entered that and entered that nobody in that district can say how long a sheet of paper has been anywhere, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the twenty-first morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
