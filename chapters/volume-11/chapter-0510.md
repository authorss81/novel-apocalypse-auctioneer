# Chapter 510: The Same Answer Twice

The boy of about nineteen asked the man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart a second question, out loud, in front of about nine people, at about eleven on the morning of the sixth of the second month, which was whether the person who gave him that sheet said anything else at the gate, and the boy counted what he said and counted what the man said the day before and got two different numbers out of the two of them, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both numbers and entered the difference and entered that she is not entering what the difference is.

It was the sixth morning of the second month and the light came up off the south end of that ground about a quarter of an hour later than it had been doing and there was a low bank of cloud sitting on the north end and did not move all day, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and forty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and thirty-fifth of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and eight, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and twenty-four days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and thirty-eight, and three hundred and ninety-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He asked it at about eleven and the man answered without stopping what he was doing with the strap, and about nine people heard the whole of it, and the boy counted it and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been asked two questions about that piece of paper in two days and I have given the same answer to both of them and both of my answers were the same length, and that is because I have got one answer and I am not going to be able to get a second one by standing here longer, and about four of you are waiting for a second one and there is not going to be one.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy asked a man a question he had been asked yesterday and that the two answers were not the same number of words, and entered that she is not entering that as a figure and entered the reason, which is that two answers being a different length is not a check on either of them and that this district has one instrument for checking and it is a wall, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally opened the flat book under his left arm for about nine seconds at the east end of that yard, in the open, with nobody standing near him, and closed it again, and about nine people within four feet of that end of the yard saw him do it and not one of them said a word about it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man opened a book and closed it again and entered no figure about either, and entered that a man who said a whole sentence out loud in that yard two days ago opened a book this morning for longer than he has ever opened it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten with his left hand in its cloth and said nothing at all, and about four people at that first table have said since that a man asked the man who keeps that tally a question about that column two days ago and got nine seconds of an answer and has not asked him anything since, and that the man who keeps that tally has not looked at him once this morning.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with the last of that day's flour on her hip, read those three lines in that lot book standing up, read the three lines on that sheet standing up, and then said one thing out loud to about nine people about the two men who have answered the same question the same way twice, and the boy counted it and got forty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man who says the same thing twice has not remembered it twice, he has got one thing and he is giving it away, and this district has spent six volumes writing down things people said once and then counting them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a finding about the man who drives that cart, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice and about nine people heard all of it, and he said one thing after it in about four seconds and the boy counted it and got thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Two people have now said the same thing twice in that yard and neither of them has changed a word and I am the only one in this yard who has been in a ditch.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a joke and entered the reason, which is that a man who has said a thing twice is not a figure and a man in a ditch is not a figure either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are fifty-three of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table for about nine minutes after she had said her thing and did not go back up that bank, and about four people near her have said since that she did not go back up because she wanted to see what a man who keeps a tally did next, and that she is not going to be asked about it because about four people have already asked each other about it.

About nine people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man who keeps a tally opened that flat book this morning for about nine seconds and that it is the fourth time anybody in that yard has seen him open it, and that about four of them have said out loud that a man who keeps a tally opens a book when he is about to say something and has said nothing four times, and that nobody in that yard has asked him and that about four other people are going to.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and nobody in that yard went out to the lane. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and he did not stop, and about four people have said since that he did not look at the man of about fifty-six either. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that nobody in that yard has ever moved a stool over to him. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and forty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and forty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and fifty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and thirteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and eighteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and eighty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and ninety-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and twenty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Sixty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are fifty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month it is living in now, and about nine people have said since that a boy found one of the two and a clerk entered the other. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and two answers of the same length is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people have said since that two men have now said the same thing twice in that yard about a thing neither of them has read out loud. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about nine people have said since that two answers of a different length did not go into it either. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and two answers of the same length are not one of the six.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and nobody there said one word to her and she did not wait to be spoken to. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about four people at that first table have said since that a boy counted two answers out loud in that yard this morning and got the same number for both of them, and that this is the only check anybody in this district has run on anybody's memory this month and that it checked nothing.

Two people in that yard have now been asked the same question twice and have answered it twice in the same words, and the man who drives that cart said out loud in front of about nine people on sixth morning of the second month that he has got one answer and is not going to get a second one by standing there longer, and a clerk entered that the two answers were the same number of words and entered that she is not entering that as a figure, because two answers being the same length is not a check on either of them, and the third line of that sheet has been a ruled line with nothing on it for the tenth morning and nobody in that yard has taken it off that table.
