# Chapter 545: The Woman With The Scale Asked What It Would Cost

A dog nobody in that yard knew came up that lane at about eight in the morning of the thirteenth of the third month and lay down in the dry corner at the foot of that low wall and stayed there until about the middle of the morning, and about four people in that yard looked at it and about nine of them walked round it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about four of those had come up that bank to look at the dog before they did anything else.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and eighty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the sheet is the only figure in this district that has never been checked by anybody and has never been wrong either, and that this district has never worked out how that is possible.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and seventieth of those mornings and that a dog came up that lane this morning and lay down at the foot of that wall and the man did not look at it.

"**The board carries four hundred and forty-three, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fifty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seventy-three, and four hundred and thirty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came up that bank at about nine with a counter weight in both hands, which is a thing she has carried up that bank about four hundred times, and set it down on the far end of that second table about a foot from the sheet that has been lying on it for forty-five mornings, and left it there.

She said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**On the eighth I shut my shop for a morning and I told everybody why before I did it. I am asking the same thing this morning. If a clerk puts a fourth line on that book on the end of that table, what does this district have left that it does not have now, and I want it said out loud before it is done and not after.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman asked a question in that yard and entered the question, and entered that the question was what this district would have left if a fourth line went on that book, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud from the east end of that yard, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**One thing. In six volumes this district has made one thing that went out of date on its own, and it is the third line of that book, and the reason it went out of date is a man in a ditch. Put a fourth line on that page and this district gives up the only instrument it has that stops being true by itself, and then everything else in here has to be asked about by somebody forever.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of things this district has made moves from twelve to thirteen on the day a fourth line goes on that book, and entered that the thirteen is smaller than the twelve, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**And two more things nobody has said out loud yet. Anything copied out of that book after this goes on the admission and not on the figure, and an admission is the easiest thing in the world to copy and it costs whoever copies it nothing. And after this, this district can never again stand in front of a stranger and say that the other one is a copy, because we will have said out loud in public that our own third line is about somebody we are not naming.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered both of the two things he named, and entered that a copy made after a fourth line is a copy of an admission and not of a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing out loud then, without moving her weight off that table, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Then I want it entered that I asked the question and got an answer in front of all of you before anybody did it, and that my answer is I still think it is right, and I am not going to say that twice.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A weight has been on that table all morning and about four people have come round it, and if that sheet had been a book instead of a copy I would not have been able to do that, and I do not know what that means and I have not been asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of things this district has made is still twelve this morning and that a woman put a weight on that table about a foot from that sheet and it is not a thirteenth, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a woman asked in that yard this morning what this district would lose and a man told her in front of about nineteen people that it would lose the only thing it has that stops being true by itself. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and had to swing wide of that second table because a counter weight was sitting on the far end of it, and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and a dog that had been lying in that corner since about eight got up and moved about nine feet along the wall and lay down again, and nobody offered the man anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a whole yard has spent this morning working out what it would lose and that the dog knew it was a quiet morning before anybody did. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and fifty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month, and it is the shortest of the four that this district keeps, and the other three are longer. A hundred and ninety-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and forty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. Two hundred and twenty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and twenty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and sixty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are eighty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and a clerk has entered this afternoon that a man said out loud in that yard this morning that this district would give up its one instrument that goes out of date on its own, and has entered that the count of things this district has made moves from twelve to thirteen on the day it happens and not this morning. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and the page says twelve this afternoon, and about four people in that yard have said since that a man said a figure out loud this morning that the page has not got yet.

The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people in that yard have said since that a man has said out loud this morning that the fourth thing in that book is the only thing in this district that stops being true by itself, and that a fourth line is what would take that off it, and that about four of them have said that is not the same as saying the fourth thing was wrong. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and about six people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it, and a clerk has entered that she entered two costs this morning on a page and neither of them was asked for. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and a woman asking what a thing would cost before it is done is not one of the six.

Nobody noticed the dog leaving the corner at the foot of that low wall until it had gone about nine feet along it, and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that the buckets went down that bank with nothing in them at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and that the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past while the dog was still lying in that corner, and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her, and that she was in that yard for about two hours this morning, and that a counter weight was sitting on that table and a man was at the foot of that wall and neither of them knew she had been there. At about four the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a figure got in a mouth at about ten this morning and that the page at the near end of that table still says twelve.

A woman who keeps a scale carried a counter weight up that bank at about nine and set it on the far end of that second table about a foot from a sheet that has lain there for forty-five mornings and left it there all day, and asked out loud in front of about nineteen people what this district would have left if a fourth line went on that book, and a man who keeps a tally told her in the ordinary voice that it would have given up the one thing in six volumes that goes out of date on its own, and a man who mends fencing said out loud that after that this district could never prove which of two sheets was its own, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the forty-fifth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and nothing has been put on that page today.
