# Chapter 512: Where Were You On The Last Two Mornings

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, at about eleven on the morning of the eighth of the second month, where he had been on the last two mornings of last month, and about nine people at the near end of that first table looked at him while he thought about it, and about four of them have said since that she had been not asking him for eleven days and that this was the eleventh day of it.

It was the eighth morning of the second month and the light came up off the water behind that yard about half an hour before it had been doing and there was no wind in it after about eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and fifty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and thirty-seventh of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and ten, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and twenty-six days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and forty, and four hundred and one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She asked it at about eleven with the last of that day's flour still on her hip and she had put it down at the low step before she asked and about nineteen people heard the whole of it and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got sixty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You were not in that yard on the last two mornings of last month and there are about four of us who noticed and about nine of us who did not, and I have been not asking you about it for eleven days, and I am asking you now in front of everybody, and you do not have to answer me and I am asking anyway.**"

Nobody said anything at all for about four seconds. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman asked a question out loud in that yard and entered the question, and entered that she had not asked it on the eleven days before this one and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then he answered, in the ordinary voice, in about nine seconds, with his left hand in its cloth, and about nineteen people heard it and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I was not in that yard and I am not going to say where I was. You are not going to ask me a second time and I am not going to be asked one, and that is not a refusal of you. That is the whole of what I have got, and I want it entered that I answered and not that I told anybody anything.**"

Nobody asked him anything else. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man answered a question and told nobody anything, and entered that she is not entering where he was, and entered that nobody is going to ask him a second time.

Then the boy of about nineteen started to ask him something and did not finish the question, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud before the boy had got to the end of it, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got forty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You are going to be the fourth one today. Do not ask me, and I am telling you not to ask me so that you can say afterwards that you were told not to ask, which is a different thing from not asking.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy did not ask a question he had started to ask, and the record about the not asking says not asked. About four people at that first table have said since that a man who has refused to be asked a second time has just refused a boy before the boy finished, and that about nine people have said that this is the third time in eleven days that somebody in this district has been asked a question and then told not to ask it again, and that one of those three has been about the column and one of them has been about the last two mornings of last month.

The man of about fifty-six was at that wall and did not move while any of that was being said, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that a man who has read four figures off that wall every morning for two hundred and thirty-seven mornings heard somebody ask another man a question in that yard this morning and did not look up from the wall, and that he has never once done that before, and that nobody in that yard has asked him about it.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice and said one thing after it in about four seconds and the boy counted it and got thirty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man can be in a place and not be in it, and the only way anybody finds out is by asking him, and he has just told us he is not going to be asked twice.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a finding about anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten, about nine feet from where the question had been asked, and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are fifty-five of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was within about nine feet of a question that was asked in that yard this morning and entered that he did not move and entered that she is not entering that as a finding, and about four people at that first table have said since that a man who cuts reeds has not moved away from a conversation in that yard in six volumes and that nobody in that yard has worked out whether he does that on purpose. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and a clerk entered that a man said a whole sentence out loud in that yard three days ago and has said nothing at all since.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he went on up it. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and he did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people have said since that he was not asked anything this morning either. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and fifty-first night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and fifty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and sixty days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and fifteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and twenty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and ninety-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and twenty-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Sixty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are fifty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month it is living in now, and the fourth of the four is out by a figure this district does not have and has never once been out by a day. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a question asked after eleven days of not asking it is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people have said since that a book with a third line about a person is the only instrument this district has that goes out of date on its own. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and a man answered a question in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a question asked after eleven days of not asking it is not one of the six.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that she did not stop at either table this evening. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about four people at that first table have said since that he did not look up from that wall while a woman asked a man a question in that yard and that is the only thing he has done differently in six volumes that anybody has noticed.

A woman who keeps a scale asked a man where he had been on the last two mornings of last month, in front of about nineteen people, on the morning of the eighth of the second month, after eleven days of not asking him, and he answered in about nine seconds and did not say where he had been and said out loud that nobody was going to ask him a second time and asked for it to be entered that he answered and not that he told anybody anything, and a clerk entered all of it and entered no place, and that sheet has been on the end of that second table for the twelfth morning with nothing in the third line of it.
