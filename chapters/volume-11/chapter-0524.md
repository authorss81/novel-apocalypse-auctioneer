# Chapter 524: Who Said A Thing Has To Be In Writing

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the twentieth of the second month, at about a quarter past eleven, who said a thing has to be in writing to be true, and nobody in that yard had ever heard the question before, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam answered it in about eleven seconds, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of the answer except one word in it.

It was the twentieth morning of the second month and the light came up off the south end of that ground flat and grey and there was no wind in it at all, and the boards of that second table were warm by about half past eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and sixty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and forty-ninth of them, and that he was at that wall for the whole of what was said this morning and did not turn round.

"**The board carries four hundred and twenty-two, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and thirty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and fifty-two, and four hundred and thirteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He asked it standing at the near end of that second table with his right hand off the boards for once and hanging at his side, and about nineteen people heard every word of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got forty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Who said a thing has to be in writing to be true? Not in this yard. Anywhere. In six volumes of people saying things out loud at each other in the open and a clerk writing them down because they said them. Who said that?**"

Nobody answered him for about four seconds and then nobody answered him at all, and about nine people at the near end of that first table have said since that a yard of about nineteen people has gone twenty mornings without one word about a sheet of paper and has now been asked a question none of them had a shape for, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked a question out loud in that yard and entered the question, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and he did not sit down on the low step, and about nine people have said since that a man who has sat on that step on about nine mornings of this month stood in the middle of that yard this morning instead, and he said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said the fifteenth of the tenth month out loud in that yard and a clerk wrote it down because I said it, and not one person in this district has ever stood in front of anybody and said a thing has to be in writing to be true, and so if somebody has taken a copy of that book and left out the line that goes out of date, then nobody has been robbed of anything, because there was never anything there to take. The forgery, if there is one, is not a figure that is wrong. It is a figure nobody said out loud.**"

Nobody said anything at all in that yard for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that there is one word in the thing that she is not entering, and entered the reason, which is the reason she gave out loud on the eleventh morning of this month, which is that a clerk who has to write a word down can only write down words she can put a figure to, and entered that she is not entering that she is going to think differently about it on another morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people within four feet of that first table have said since that the yard has just been told out loud that the thing it has been looking for for twenty mornings is not a thing anybody can take, and that about four of them have said that a clerk has written down the whole of a sentence and left one word out of it, and that about four of them have said that a book made out of things people said out loud cannot be made to say a word back.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was in that yard at about ten and said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have weighed flour in front of customers for eleven years and the reason they come back is not the scale. If what I wrote on that page four mornings ago is a false figure then a clerk in this yard can tell me, and if it is not a false figure then a clerk in this yard cannot tell me anything at all, and I would like somebody here to say which of those two is worse.**"

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud from the east end of that yard, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have kept a tally in this district for about thirty years and there has never once been a column in it for what a thing is for, and about four of you have found out this morning that the whole of what this district does in a yard is done by people saying things out loud, and that is not a figure and it is not going to be one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two people said things out loud in that yard and entered both of the things, and entered that she is not entering either of them as a finding, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one more thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have spent six volumes asking people in this yard to be told things out loud in front of each other and I did it because it works, and a man has just said out loud that it works because nobody in this district has ever required the other kind, and I am not going to argue with him in a yard, and I am not going to stand here this afternoon and pretend that this morning did not happen.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has now said out loud that a thing he has spent six volumes doing is the whole of the method, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the end of that second table and said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said one word out loud in that yard nine mornings ago and nobody asked me a second question and a clerk did not write it down. That man has just said a whole sentence with the same word in it and she has not written that word down either, and I have been standing in this yard for both of those and I am the only one who has, and I am not going to say what I think that means.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk entered a sentence this morning with one word out of it, and that the same word was left out on the eleventh morning of this month, and entered that she is not entering that as a rule and entered that she is not entering it at all.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are sixty-seven of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The boy of about nineteen wrote nothing in the right half of the sheet of his own all morning and a clerk entered that and entered that she did not ask him about it, and about four people at that first table have said since that a boy who has written a date into that half once in about a month wrote nothing into it on the morning the whole of that yard found out what it had been doing for six volumes.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane in the middle of a thing nobody else in that yard had ever been asked and went on up without stopping, and that about four of them have said that this is the second time this month that a question in that yard has gone past him and that he has never once been asked a question in this district in six volumes. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man who had said a thing out loud in that yard at about half past twelve did not stop at that first table at three and that is the first time in this month he has not. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people within four feet of that wall have said since that about nineteen people said things out loud about four feet off him this morning and a man on those stones has not moved and has not looked up. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and sixty-third night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and seventy-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and twenty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and thirty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and forty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Eighty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are sixty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and about nine people at that wall have said since that a man read four figures off it this morning and a clerk wrote one of them down with a word missing out of it, and that the figure on the wall won anyway. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a question nobody in a district has ever been asked is not a thirteenth, and a clerk has entered a sentence this morning and has left one word out of it and has entered that she is leaving it out. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people at that first table have said since that the whole of what this district has for six volumes is things people said out loud in a yard and that this morning somebody said the third of those three lines out loud in a yard and a clerk wrote it down because he said it. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and about six people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a sentence a clerk cannot finish writing is not one of the six.

Empty at about half past five and full on the way back, the two buckets went down that bank and up it, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that she was about nine feet from the man who had said the third of those three lines out loud in that yard this morning and that neither of the two of them said one word to the other. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and did not turn round from that wall at any point after about a quarter past eleven.

A yard of about nineteen people was asked this morning, out loud, in the ordinary voice, who said a thing has to be in writing to be true, and a man who has been in that ditch every morning since before the second of the twelfth month answered it in about eleven seconds and said that a figure nobody said out loud is not a figure that is wrong, and a clerk entered the whole of that answer and left one word out of it and entered that she is leaving it out, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the twenty-fourth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
