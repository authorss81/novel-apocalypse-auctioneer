# Chapter 514: The Third Line Left Out On Purpose

A clerk of nineteen years read two sheets of paper line by line out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about eleven on the morning of the tenth of the second month, standing at the near end of that first table in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people, and then said out loud that a line is left out on purpose or it is left out by accident, and nobody in that yard answered her, and about nine people have said since that it is the first time anybody in that yard has said out loud what that piece of paper is for.

It was the tenth morning of the second month and the ground under that yard had been wet since about four in the morning and had not dried at the south end by about eleven, and the boards of that second table were dry because they are off the ground on four legs a man of about thirty-seven built, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and fifty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and thirty-ninth of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and twelve, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and twenty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and forty-two, and four hundred and three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She read both of them out loud at about eleven and it took about forty seconds and the third line of the one on that first table is a date and the third line of the one on that second table is a ruled line with the paper still on it, and about nineteen people heard every word of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got fifty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Whoever made that sheet copied two lines and stopped. A person copying a book stops where the lines stop. The third line of that book is the only line in it that goes out of date on its own, and I do not think anybody left it out because they did not know what it said.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that that sheet is not a forgery, and entered the reason, which is that a forgery is a figure that is wrong and that the third line of that sheet is not a figure at all, and entered that she does not know what it is and is not going to guess at it in a yard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she turned round and asked the man of about sixty-four a question, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, and about nine people within four feet of that low wall have said since that nobody in that yard has ever asked that man anything at all.

A clerk of nineteen years said one thing, in the ordinary voice, standing about nine feet off that low wall, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have to write down two figures about you this morning and I have got one of them off the top of my own head and I have not got the other one, because I have got a night-count and I have not got the night. I am going to ask you a question and you do not have to answer it, and I am asking it in front of these people because I have entered about nine things this month that are not figures and I have entered no figure about any of the nine of them.**"

Nobody said anything. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He looked at her for about four seconds and then he answered, in the ordinary voice, in about nine seconds, and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy counted what he said and got forty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I sit on the stones and I do not sleep until it gets late and I sleep about four hours and I get up when the light comes. That is what you are asking and I am telling you because you have asked me in front of them.**"

Then he said one more thing, in about four seconds, and about nine people heard it and the boy counted it and got twenty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody has asked me that before and I am not thanking you for it and I am not going to do anything different because of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she had asked a man a question this morning for the first time in six volumes, and entered that asking is not offering, and entered the reason, which is that the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given and that today something was asked and nothing was given and that both of those are in the same entry, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten and heard all of it and said nothing at all, and about four people at that first table have said since that a man who has refused to be asked a second time about where he was stood about eleven feet off a man being asked a question for the first time in six volumes and did not say one word about it, and that nobody in that yard has asked him whether he noticed.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and about nine people have said since that a man read a third line out loud in that yard this morning that has nothing in it, and that it is the first time in this volume that anybody has read that empty third line out loud in this yard.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are fifty-seven of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and he did not open it this morning either, and a clerk entered that he has now opened that book four times in six volumes and has said nothing on any of them.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that this district has a figure for how many days that train has stood and no figure at all for how many times he has come up that lane. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and fifty-third night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and sixty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and seventeen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and twenty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and ninety-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and thirty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Seventy-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are fifty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month it is living in now, and about four people have said since that a man who has been told twice about his own figures has still not been asked one question. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and an answer given to a question that was asked is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and a clerk entered that there is no fourth line in it and that she is not going to rule one this morning. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and a man was asked a question in that yard for the first time in six volumes and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about nine people have said since that she read those three lines tonight on a book that has had four mornings of somebody else standing at it. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people at that first table have said since that a man who has never been asked anything in six volumes was asked something this morning in front of about nineteen people and gave an answer in nine seconds and about four of them have said that it was the first thing that has been asked of him in this district and that he did not take the asking as a gift either.

A clerk of nineteen years read two sheets of paper line by line out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people on the morning of the tenth of the second month and said out loud that whoever made that sheet copied two lines and stopped, and that a person copying a book stops where the lines stop, and that the third line of that book is the only line in it that goes out of date on its own, and then asked a man of about sixty-four a question at the foot of that low wall for the first time in six volumes and entered the answer and entered that asking is not offering and entered that nothing was given, and that sheet has been on the end of that second table for the fourteenth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
