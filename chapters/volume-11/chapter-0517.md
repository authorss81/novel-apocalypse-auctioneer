# Chapter 517: He Went Down The Bank To Read A Date

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam went down that bank on foot at about half past ten on the morning of the thirteenth of the second month, which is about two hundred yards, and stood at the end of the counter of the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale for about forty seconds, and read the three lines on a square of her own paper standing up, and said one thing out loud in about four seconds to about nine people in that shop, and about nine people have said since that a man who has not been off that bank in this month went down it to read a date.

It was the thirteenth morning of the second month and the wind had gone round to the south in the night and that yard had gone from cold to warm in about an hour after nine, and the boards of that second table had a skin of water on the underside and not on the top, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and fifty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and forty-second of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and fifteen, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and thirty-one days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and forty-five, and four hundred and six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and then he did not sit down on the low step, which he has done on about nine mornings of this month, and went down that bank instead, and about four people near that step have said since that a man who has a minute to sit in every morning went past his own step.

Nobody followed him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man went down that bank on foot and entered that she did not follow him and entered that about nine people saw him go and entered that nobody asked him where he was going, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At the counter he stood at the end of it, about four feet off the woman of about thirty-six, and read the three lines on her square of paper standing up, and he read them the way he reads them at that second table, which is slowly, and he said the word with each of them, and then he said one thing in about four seconds, and the boy of about nineteen, who was inside that shop, counted it and got forty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is my day in her hand and I never asked her to write it and I have never asked anybody to write it down twice in my life, and I am not going to say it is mine again in a second place.**"

Nobody said anything at all in that shop for about four seconds. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in a shop and entered the thing, and entered that a date is on a counter and is a figure of a man and that the man has not been asked about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the boy of about nineteen asked her, out loud, in front of about nine people, whether the second line on her paper is the same as the second line in the yard, and she said one thing out loud without being asked twice, and the boy counted it and got fifty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is the same one, and it is the wrong one, and I wrote it in about four minutes yesterday morning from a book a stranger can walk up to and read, and I did not know it was wrong, and I still do not know whether it is wrong.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in a shop and entered the thing, and entered that a person has now said in a shop that the second line is wrong who is not one of the about four people in that yard who knew it, and entered that she is not entering that as a finding and entered the reason, which is that a person saying a thing is wrong is not a check on the thing.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the end of that first table from about half past ten and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I went down that bank this morning at about eleven because I had stood in front of both of those books two mornings ago and could not tell, and I wanted to stand in front of the third one as well, and there is a difference between a man going to look at a thing and a man going to take a thing, and I did the first one and about four of you have worked out that I did not do the second.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has now been in a shop and in that yard in one morning and has said a thing out loud in both, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam did not come back up that bank for about nine minutes, and when he did he went straight to that second table and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice again, and about nine people heard all of it, and he said nothing at the end of the third one and sat down on that low step for about a minute and a half, which is longer than the minute he sat for yesterday. A clerk entered that a man sat on that step for about a minute and a half and entered that she is not entering what the extra half a minute is.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten with his left hand in its cloth and said nothing at all while any of that was being said, and about nine people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man has now gone down that bank twice in two mornings, once yesterday and once this morning, and that on the first of the two times he stood at that counter for about nine seconds and on the second one he was not in the shop at all and nobody has said out loud why that is a difference.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale put the square of paper back on her counter about two feet further along than it had been on yesterday, and about four people in that shop have said since that she did that on purpose and about four have said that she did it because the first place was where people leaned over it, and that both of those are the same size of thing and that nobody in that shop is going to be asked which of them she did.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he went on up it without stopping. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that he looked at that man coming up the bank and did not look at him. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man has now sat on those stones for two mornings and a clerk has entered a night-count for both of them and has not asked him for either. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and fifty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and sixty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and twenty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man and that date is on a counter in a shop in a hand that is not his. A hundred and twenty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and ninety-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and thirty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Seventy-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are sixty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and about four people at that wall have said since that a date is on a counter in a shop and neither of those two out figures has moved an inch because of it. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man walking down a bank to read a page is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and the person has now been asked about a figure of himself and has said one thing about it and has not said it again. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and a man read his own date out loud in a shop this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man going down a bank to read his own date is not one of the six.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near that first table have said since that she has walked past that counter every evening since the paper went on it and has not looked down at it once. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall for about a minute afterwards without moving, and about nine people near enough to see his face have said since that he did that on a morning when a man he has read four figures beside for two hundred and forty-two mornings was standing about nine feet off that wall.

A date that this district has printed in a book a stranger may walk up to and read is on a counter in a shop this morning in a different hand, and the man it is about went down that bank and read it and said out loud in about four seconds that he never asked her to write it and that he has never asked anybody to write a thing down twice, and a clerk entered the whole of that and entered no figure about how many times that date has now been written, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the seventeenth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
