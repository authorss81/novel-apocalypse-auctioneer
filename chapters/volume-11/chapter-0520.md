# Chapter 520: He Went Down The Bank For Four Minutes

The man of about fifty-six went down that bank on foot at about half past eleven on the morning of the sixteenth of the second month, for about four minutes, and stood at the end of the counter of the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale and read the three lines on a square of her own paper standing up, and came back up that bank and said the four figures off that wall twice as he does and said nothing else at all all day, and about nine people have said since that is the first morning in six volumes that he has left that yard and come back without saying one word about it.

It was the sixteenth morning of the second month and the light came up hard and clean off the south end of that ground before seven and did not change all day, and there was no wind in that yard at any point after about eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and fifty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and forty-fifth of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and eighteen, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and thirty-four days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and forty-eight, and four hundred and nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

Nobody in that yard saw him leave it, which is a different thing, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that it is the first morning anybody has been in that yard for two hundred and forty-five mornings and has not seen where he went. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man went down that bank at about half past eleven and entered that she did not follow him and entered that nobody asked him where he was going, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He was gone for about four minutes. He read the one page on that counter standing up and he did not put a hand on it and he did not turn it over, and about nine people in that shop have said since that a man who reads four figures off a wall standing up every morning read one page off a counter standing up and did not ask anybody a single question about it, and that about four of them have said that if he had wanted to know he could have asked the woman and that he is a man who has never asked anybody anything.

Then the boy of about nineteen asked him, out loud, at that wall, in front of about nineteen people, which of the three pages in this district is the one this district made, and the boy counted what he said and got thirty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You have been in that yard every morning for about two hundred and forty-five mornings and there are three pages in this district with the same three lines on them. Which of the three did this district make?**"

The man of fifty-six looked at the boy for about four seconds and then he said it in the ordinary voice in about nine seconds and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have never once seen anybody make any of them. I have read that wall every morning and I have read that book every morning and I have got down that ditch with a man in it and I have not seen one single person in this district stand in front of a table and make a thing, and that is the whole of what I have got and I am not going to stand here and make it sound like more than that.**"

Nobody said anything at all to that for about a minute. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has said two other things out loud in that yard this month and that this is the third and that she is not entering the three of them as one thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was in that yard at about ten and said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got fifty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He stood at my counter this morning for about four minutes and read what I wrote and went away again, and I have weighed about four hundred sacks of flour in front of customers for eleven years and I have never once had a man read a page of mine the way that man reads a wall.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the comparison in it as a finding, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and said one thing after it in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About two hundred and forty-five mornings and about nineteen people a day and not one of them has ever seen anybody make anything, and I have been in the ditch the whole time, and that is not anybody's fault and it is the only thing anybody in that yard can say out loud today.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten with his left hand in its cloth and said one thing out loud at about a quarter past one, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in that yard on about two hundred and forty-five of those mornings and I did not know that until about a minute ago, and I have been asking people all month who put a sheet of paper on a table, and there is a man in that yard who can tell me that nobody saw anybody make any of them, and he has been in that yard since before the middle of the sixth month.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said out loud that he has been asking the wrong question, and entered that she is not entering that as a finding and entered the reason, which is that a man saying he has been asking the wrong question is not a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are sixty-three of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and about four people near him have said since that he has now been within about eleven feet of a man saying that nobody has ever watched anybody make anything, and that he did not open the book.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that two men who had both been down that bank that morning came up it inside about a quarter of an hour of each other and neither of them said one word to the other. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about nine people within four feet of that wall have said since that this is the third morning that a man at that wall has said a thing out loud and a man about nine feet off the stones has been told about one of the three. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and fifty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and sixty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and twenty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man and that date is on a counter in a shop in a hand that is not his. A hundred and twenty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and thirty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Seventy-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are sixty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and about nine people at that wall have said since that a man who has been standing at it every morning for six months has said this morning that he has never seen anybody make anything, and that the two of those facts are about nine feet apart. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man walking down a bank and a man saying he has been asking the wrong question are not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people at that first table have said since that a man who has never been asked a question in two hundred and forty-five mornings was asked one today and gave an answer in nine seconds. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about four people at that first table have said since that this is the first morning in this month where a man answered a question in that yard and nothing went into it and the column is the same size it was this morning. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man saying out loud that he has been asking the wrong question for a month is not one of the six.

Empty, the two buckets went down that bank at about half past five and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that a man of fifty-six had been down that bank twice in one day by then. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall for about a minute afterwards without moving, and about four people have said since that this is the first afternoon of this month that he has not looked down that bank once, and that a man who had been down it at about half past eleven was about four feet off him at the time. About nine people near enough to see his face have said since that he said the fourth of them without stopping to think about it, and that on about nine mornings out of the last two hundred and forty-five he has stopped to think about it.

A man who has said the same four figures off that wall every morning for about two hundred and forty-five mornings said this morning, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, that he has never once seen anybody make any of the three pages in this district, and a clerk entered that and entered that she is not joining it to anything, and the yard has been asking who made a sheet of paper for nineteen mornings and a man who has been in that yard for all nineteen of them has now said out loud that nobody saw it made, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the twentieth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
