# Chapter 528: He Opened The Book For Nineteen Seconds

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally opened the flat book under his left arm at the east end of the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the twenty-fourth of the second month, in the open, at about eleven, for about nineteen seconds, which is longer than he has ever opened it in six volumes, and read two lines out of it, and said one thing out loud afterwards, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the two lines and entered that the second of them is not a figure anybody in that yard can check, and entered that she is not entering the first of them as a thing this district made.

It was the twenty-fourth morning of the second month and the light came up clean off the south end of that ground before seven and there was a wind out of the east for about an hour after nine, and the boards of that second table were cold to the hand until about half past nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and sixty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fifty-third of them, and that about four of them have said that a man who stands at that wall every morning has not asked anybody for a figure in about two hundred and fifty-three mornings and has not been asked for one either.

"**The board carries four hundred and twenty-six, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and forty-two days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and fifty-six, and four hundred and seventeen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He opened it and he read two lines out loud in the ordinary voice and about nineteen people heard both of them and the boy of about nineteen counted what he read and got sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Six mornings of a month, from the first, and it was in the last month of the year before, and I wrote it down off a list and I did not count the marks off that board, and I have kept this book for about thirty years and there is not one other thing in it that I know to be wrong.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man read two lines out loud in that yard and entered the two lines, and entered that a clerk is entering them as a thing a man kept and not as a count, and entered that the length of the month they name is not on any page in this district and that it is the second of the two months this district has not counted, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then he said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to say that the two of those lines are the same as the two on that table. I am not going to say they are not either. I have said four things in that yard in about a fortnight and I said this morning that I was going to stop, and I have come back into it once and this is the once, and after this I am done and there is no column for it and I am not going to ask for one.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who said four mornings ago that he was going to stop came back into it once, and entered that she is not entering that as a change of heart, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen then asked him, out loud, in front of about nine people, whether the first of the two lines is a figure anybody can check, and he answered without being asked twice and the boy counted it and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. It is off a list. A person handed me a list and I wrote down what was on it and I have never seen that person since, and about four of us have known for a month that whatever is on that list is not what that board says, and none of us has counted the marks, because counting them is a day's work and the month is not ours.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk is not entering that the list is wrong and entered the reason, which is that a figure somebody entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day, and that she has now entered that sentence in this yard for the second time, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten with his left hand in its cloth and said one thing out loud at about a quarter past one, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got seventy-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a figure on a list in this district that is about a month and no board in this district has ever been counted off in daylight in front of anybody, and I have known about both of those for about a month, and I am the man who put the second of those two things on a table in the open, and I am not going to say anything else about it today.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it with the two lines a man read out of a book this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and said nothing at the end of the third one and went and sat down on the low step. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are seventy-one of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man has been in that yard every morning this month and has not looked at the book once, and that about four of them have said that is because he does not read and not because he is not interested. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at that first table have said since that a book was open in that yard for about nineteen seconds this morning and a man with a strap went past the end of that table while it was open and did not look at it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man has now been within about four feet of a book that was open in the open for about nineteen seconds this morning and has not asked what was in it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and sixty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and seventy-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and thirty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and thirty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and forty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Eighty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are seventy-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and a man read two lines out of a book this morning that name a month and a clerk entered both of them and entered no figure for the length of that month. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a book a man has kept for about thirty years is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people at that first table have said since that a man who has stood in that yard for about thirty years has now put a figure from a list into that yard's mouth and that nobody in that yard is going to be able to get it back out. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a man read two lines out loud in that yard this morning and a clerk entered both of them and put nothing in it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a figure somebody entered off a list is not one of the six.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read those three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that a man at the east end of that yard read two lines out of a book he has carried under his arm for about thirty years and that neither of them said one word to the other. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a book was opened in that yard in the open for about nineteen seconds and a wall a man reads every morning was about four feet off it throughout.

A man who has kept a tally in this district for about thirty years opened his book in the open this morning and read two lines out of it, and one of the two names a month and the other says the month is the first of one, and a clerk of nineteen years entered both of them and entered that the length of that month is not on any page in this district and that it is the second of the two months nobody here has counted, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the twenty-eighth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
