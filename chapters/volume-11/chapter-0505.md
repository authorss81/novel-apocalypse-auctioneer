# Chapter 505: Four Feet Off A Blank

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that lane at about a quarter past eleven on the morning of the first of the second month with his left hand in its cloth, stood at the end of that second table about four feet off that sheet of paper, did not touch either table, said one thing in about four seconds, and about nineteen people in that yard heard every word of it and none of them had seen him in that yard for seven mornings.

It was the first morning of the second month and the sun came over the roof of the building with the two doors at about half past seven and there was no wind at all in that yard by about nine, and the boards of that first table were warm by about half past eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and forty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and thirtieth of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and three, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and nineteen days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and thirty-three, and three hundred and ninety-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He said it at about a quarter past eleven with his right hand hanging at his side and his left hand in its cloth and he did not look at anybody while he said it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got sixty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody has told me where I was on the last two mornings of last month and I have not been asked and I am not going to be. I came up this lane because there is a piece of paper lying on that table and I would like to know what it is, and it is not that book, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he was not in that yard on seven mornings and is in it this morning, and entered that she is not entering a reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the boy of about nineteen asked him, out loud, in front of about nine people, whether he thinks somebody in this district made that sheet, and he answered without being asked twice and the boy counted it and got twenty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. I do not think so and I am not going to say why, and the yard can do what it likes with that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering a refusal, and entered that a man may say no without a reason and that she has entered nine of those this month and has entered a reason for none of the nine, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten and was about nine feet from the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing for about nine seconds at about a quarter past eleven, and he put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are forty-eight of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. A clerk of nineteen years entered that both men were standing in that yard within about ten feet of each other and entered that nobody asked either of them about the other one and entered that she is not going to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with the last of that day's flour on her hip, read those three lines in that lot book standing up, walked over to that second table, read the three lines on that sheet standing up, and this time she said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Same two lines. Still no third.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that six words is not a finding and entered that she is not entering it as one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and did not go over to that second table at all, and about four people have said since that he has read that sheet out loud on four mornings and has not gone near it today, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what that means and nobody is going to.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and about nine people within four feet of that end of the yard have said since that a man has come back to that yard after seven mornings and that the man has not gone to the man who keeps the tally, and that the two of them were about eleven feet apart for about half an hour.

About half past one the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing walked down that bank about forty feet and stopped at the point where the two buckets came up and stood there for about a minute and a half with his back to that yard, and nobody followed him down there and nobody asked him what he was doing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man walked down that bank and stood with his back to a yard and entered that she did not ask and entered the reason, and about four people at that first table have said since that he was not waiting for anything and that he was not going to be asked anything and that those are two different things and that nobody has worked out which of them he was doing.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he did not stop at either of those two tables. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and he went on up the lane past both tables. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who is given nothing every morning does not stop coming. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and forty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and forty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and fifty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and thirteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and eighty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and eighty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and twenty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Sixty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are forty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month it is living in now, and one of the two did not start again when this district went into a new year. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man coming back into a yard after seven mornings is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and a man who has refused to read a page of it out loud in that yard for two volumes is in that yard this morning for the second morning since he stopped coming. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about four people have said since that a man came back into that yard this morning after seven mornings away and no name went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and about four people have said since that six things in that yard are not a payment and one of the six is a man.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about nine people near that first table have said since that she has never once said what is in it. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about four people at that first table have said since that a man who was not in that yard for seven mornings stood at the end of the second table for about nine minutes and did not touch anything on it and did not go and look at the wall.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that lane on first morning of the second month and stood about four feet off that sheet of paper and said out loud in front of about nineteen people that nobody has told him where he was on the last two mornings of last month and that he has not been asked and is not going to be, and that he came up because there is a piece of paper lying on that table and it is not that book, and a clerk entered that he was not in that yard on seven mornings and is in it this morning and entered that she is not entering a reason, and the third line of that sheet is a ruled line with the paper still on it on the fifth morning it has been lying on that table.
