# Chapter 509: I Was Given It

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was asked, out loud, in front of about nine people, at about eleven on the morning of the fifth of the second month, who told him to put that sheet of paper on that table, and he said he was given it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of that and entered that she is not entering the person who gave it to him.

It was the fifth morning of the second month and the sky was low and grey and stayed low and grey until about two in the afternoon and did not rain, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and forty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and thirty-fourth of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and seven, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and twenty-three days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and thirty-seven, and three hundred and ninety-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He said it at about eleven with the near wheel still dragging behind him and he had a strap in his hand and about nine people heard the whole of it and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got ninety-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I was given it. That is the whole of what I have got and I have said it once and I am not going to say it better the second time. I was given it at a gate after dark on the night before that, by a person I did not know and I was told to take it up that lane and put it on the far table and not to stop, and I did all three of those things and I did not ask him anything and he did not tell me anything.**"

Nobody said anything at all to that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a person was given a sheet of paper at a gate after dark and was not asked about it, and entered that the record about the not asking says not asked, and entered that she is not entering the person and is not entering the gate.

The boy of about nineteen asked him, out loud, in front of about nine people, where the gate was, and he answered without being asked twice and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A gate with a bar across the top of it and a hinge that has been off for about a year and a half. There are about nine of those in this district and I did not ask which one and I am not going to be asked which one now.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering nine gates because there is no figure on any page in this district for how many gates there are, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten and said one thing in about four seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got forty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He is the only person in this district who has been asked twice this month and both times he has said the same thing, and about four of us have been watching for a different second time and there is not going to be one.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering what anybody has been watching for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came up that lane at about half past twelve with a basket on her hip and read the three lines on that sheet standing up at the end of that second table, and then read them a second time standing up, and then said one thing out loud to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got sixty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been up that lane twice this month and I have read that paper standing up six times now and it has not changed once, and the paper I saw on that post at the second channel has not changed either, and I do not know what that means and I am not going to tell anybody what I think.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman has read a sheet of paper standing up six times and it has not changed, and entered that she is not entering what a woman thinks it means, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice and about nine people heard all of it. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are fifty-two of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and a clerk entered that a man said a whole sentence out loud in that yard yesterday and said nothing at all today and entered that she is not entering either of those as a finding.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was four feet off that sheet for about four hours and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven and said nothing at all, and a clerk entered that she did not ask him whether the paper on that table was his and entered that she is not going to, and about four people near that table have said since that a man who has said nothing in that yard for six volumes has been sitting four feet off somebody else's paper for four hours and that nobody in that yard has ever had to work out what to do about that.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people have said since that he comes up that lane in the morning and down it in the afternoon and that nobody in that yard has ever watched him do either. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and he did not stop, and about four people at that first table have said since that he has now given the same answer twice. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man who says a whole sentence out loud once in this district is a man who has been let alone. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and forty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and forty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and fifty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and twelve days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and seventeen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and eighty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and ninety days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and twenty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Sixty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are fifty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month it is living in now, and about four people have said since that this district has never once in six volumes written one of the four down. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man being asked a question he has already been asked is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people have said since that a book a stranger can walk up to and read has been standing on that table since the second of the twelfth month. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about four people have said since that a man has now said a thing out loud in that yard twice and the column is the same as it was this morning. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and about nine people have said since that a man being handed a thing by somebody he did not know is not one of the six.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that a woman who reads those three lines has never been asked whether the third one is right. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people at that first table have said since that the man who drives that cart has now said one thing in that yard and that the thing he said was that he was given it, and that this district does not have a figure for how many people have been given something, and that it has a figure for how many marks a man has cut off a board since the first of the twelfth month, and that the two of those are the same size of thing and that one of them is a thing people can check.

A man who drives that cart up that lane twice a day said out loud in a yard in front of about nine people on the morning of the fifth of the second month that he was given a sheet of paper at a gate after dark by a person he did not know and was told to take it up that lane and put it on the far table and not to stop, and said that he did all three of those things and did not ask anything, and a clerk entered the whole of it and entered that she is not entering the person and is not entering the gate, and that sheet has been on the end of that second table for the ninth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and nobody in that yard has asked him anything else.
