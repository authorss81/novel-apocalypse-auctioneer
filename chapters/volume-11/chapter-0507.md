# Chapter 507: The Eleven Miles Asked About

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the road keeper, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, at about half past one on the morning of the third of the second month, whether anybody in this district was ever going to ask him about the eleven miles, and the road keeper stopped on that lane where he was and answered in about nine seconds, and nobody asked him a second question.

It was the third morning of the second month and there was a hard clean light on that ground from before seven and the boards of that second table were warm by about nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and forty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and thirty-second of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and five, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and twenty-one days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and thirty-five, and three hundred and ninety-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He asked it standing four feet off that lane and the road keeper had about four feet left to walk to the gate and he stopped and turned round and did not come any further back, and about nineteen people heard the whole of it and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got sixty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody has asked me about the eleven miles in six months and I walk them twice a day and I have got to the end of them about four hundred times and stood there, and the answer you want is on the far side of them and I am not going to walk them to find it out for a yard.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he was asked and that he answered and that nobody asked him a second question, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then he said one more thing, in about four seconds, from where he was standing, and about nine people heard it and the boy counted it and got thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Four hundred times I have been asked by people who do not use the word, and about nine of those were children, and children are the only ones who ever asked me the actual question.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering four hundred times because there is no figure on any page in this district for how many times a man has been asked a question, and about four people at that first table have said since that this district has a figure for how many days a man has said four things out loud and does not have one for how many questions anybody has asked him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and he said one thing out loud after it in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got twenty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The end of them is the ninth mile and the ninth mile is a marker somebody cut, and I have never once been asked about that marker either.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that there are two things in this district that nobody asks anybody about and that she has entered both of them this month and has entered neither of them as a finding, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of fifty-six said one thing, from about nine feet off the near end of that first table, in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard it and he was about four feet from the road keeper when he said it and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have said four things off that wall every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and a man has just said that about four hundred people have asked him and nobody has ever asked me, and that is not a complaint, it is a thing I have noticed and done nothing with.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a grievance and entered the reason, which is that a clerk is not the person anybody owes a question to, and about four people at that first table have said since that a man who has been told one of his four figures is out, and told twice, has said one thing out loud in that yard about being asked nothing, and that the two of those facts have not been joined in that yard by anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood where he had asked the question from and said nothing else at all, and about nine people at that first table have said since that he had walked up that lane to ask it and had not come up that lane for that purpose, and that a clerk entered that a man walked up a lane and entered that she is not entering why.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are fifty of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume, and a clerk entered that count and entered that it is a count of marks and is not a figure about a month. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and about nine people within four feet of that end of that yard have said since that they have never once heard him say a whole sentence.

About four people at that first table have said since that the man of fifty-six waited until the road keeper had finished answering and then said his own thing, and that about nine people have said that he has not said one word out loud in that yard since before the middle of the sixth month until third morning of the second month, and that in between those two mornings a clerk told him two of his four figures were out and he said nothing at all both times, and that nobody in that yard has put those three facts together out loud.

At about two the cart came up with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people have said since that he was not asked anything about the two figures that came back down this month. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and forty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and forty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and fifty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and ten days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and fifteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and eighty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and eighty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and twenty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Sixty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are fifty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been corrected and the figure on the wall is the figure, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month it is living in now. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a question asked on a lane is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and there is a second sheet on a second table in that yard with the same three lines on it and two of them filled. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about nine people have said since that a man who says one word out loud in that yard this month has still not got a name beside it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and about four people have said since that a question asked on a lane is not one of the six.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that she went past that table at the same speed she goes past it every evening. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall for about a minute afterwards without moving, and about four people near enough to see his face have said since that a man who has been asked nothing in two hundred and thirty-two mornings was in a yard this morning where somebody asked somebody else a question.

A road keeper who has walked eleven miles twice a day for about six months was asked about them out loud in front of about nineteen people on the morning of the third of the second month and answered in about nine seconds and said that the answer everybody wants is on the far side of them and that he is not going to walk them to find it out for a yard, and then said one more thing, which is that about four hundred people have asked him and about nine of those were children, and a clerk entered all of it and entered no figure about how many questions anybody has ever been asked in this district, because there is no figure on any page in this district for that and there is one for how many mornings a man has said four things out loud, and a boy of about nineteen wrote the figure for the second of those two things on his own sheet in the left half of it and did not read it back to himself, which is the first time in seven days that he has not done that with a number he counted.
