# Chapter 535: The Boy Asked A Clerk How Many Figures She Had

The clerk of nineteen years asked the man of about sixty-four a third question in six volumes at about eleven on the morning of the third of the third month, at about nine feet off that low wall, in front of about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and it was not about a sheet of paper and it was not about a night, and he said one thing out loud, and a clerk entered that she had asked a question and entered that she is not entering what he said, and the boy of about nineteen then asked her, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, how many figures she kept about that man, and she said the word and nothing else, and about nine people in that yard have said since that this is the first morning in six volumes on which a clerk of this district left two things out of a page on purpose and said out loud that she was going to.

It was the third morning of the third month and the light came up clean and hard off the south end of that ground before seven and the boards of that second table were cold to the hand until about half past nine, and the row of cuts on that board had about four inches of shadow across the bottom of it until about a quarter past ten, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and seventy-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and sixtieth of them, and that about four of them have said that a man who reads the same four figures every morning has never once asked whether anybody else in that yard is keeping any.

"**The board carries four hundred and thirty-three, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and forty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and sixty-three, and four hundred and twenty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

She asked it standing up, with her page in her left hand and her pen not out of her apron, at about nine feet off that low wall, and about nineteen people heard every word of it, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I keep four figures about you. I am not going to read them to you this morning and I am going to go on keeping them. I am asking you one thing and it is the third question anybody in this district has put to you in six volumes, and it is not about a sheet of paper and it is not about how many nights. Is there a thing you would rather I did not keep?**"

He looked at her for about four seconds and then he answered, in the ordinary voice, in about eleven seconds, and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy counted it and got ninety-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. Keep them. I have never asked you for one of them and I am not going to start this morning. But I would rather you did not put a third question in a yard, and I will tell you why, and it is not because of me. The first two were about a sheet of paper. The one you have just asked me is about whether I mind. About four of the people standing behind you have not noticed that those are not the same question, and you have noticed it, because you asked it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that she asked a man a question in that yard for the third time in six volumes and entered the question, and entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard, and entered that she is not entering the thing, and entered the reason, which is that a figure somebody entered about a man in a yard in front of about nineteen people is asking with a column, and that she is not entering his answer because she has no figure to put to it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard said anything at all for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall, and about nine people within about nine feet of that low wall have said since that a man who has been given nothing on a hundred and seventy-four mornings has just told a yard of about nineteen people that the third thing anybody did to him this morning was a question about himself and not about his nights, and that about four of them have said that the two of those are not the same kind of thing and that the man on those stones is the one who said so.

The boy of about nineteen then asked the clerk, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, how many figures she kept about the man at the foot of that wall, and she said one word and nothing else and the boy did not count it, because there was nothing in it to count, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked a question out loud in that yard and entered the question, and entered that she answered it, and entered that she is not entering the answer, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, standing where she had been standing, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and forty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have entered that sentence in this yard more than once in six volumes and it has not once been about the right thing, and the reason I have not noticed that until this morning is that a sentence you say often stops being a sentence and becomes a way of doing things. I said on the second morning of this month that a clerk who cannot answer a question in a yard is not going to be able to count it off a board. I said it two mornings ago that a figure somebody entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day. I have said both of those out loud in this yard and both of them were about me and about my page and not about a single person standing in it.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk has said out loud in a yard that a sentence she has entered before has not been about the right thing, and entered that she is not entering that as a change in what this district is, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that first table at about half past eleven for about four minutes and said one thing out loud to about nine people, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said one word out loud in that yard on the eleventh morning of last month and a clerk did not write it down, and a clerk wrote down a whole sentence this month with one word left out of it, and a man stopped in the middle of a sentence, and this morning a clerk did not write down what a man said to her face. That is four of them. I have been standing in this yard for all four and I am the only one who has, and I am not going to say what I think that means, and I have not been asked.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who has been standing in that yard for four things that are not on a page has now said so out loud for the second time, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk has stood about nine feet off a man at the foot of that low wall and asked him a question about himself this morning, and that the road keeper came up that lane in the middle of it and went on without stopping, which is what he does. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had been at the other end of it at half past eleven. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who was asked a question at about eleven this morning has been sat on those same stones for about four hours and has not said one word to anybody since, and that a clerk has entered none of it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and eighty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and thirty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and forty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and thirteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and sixteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Ninety-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are seventy-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and a clerk entered this morning that she is not entering what a man said to her face, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of any month. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a clerk leaving two things out of a page on purpose is not a thirteenth, and a man counting four occasions in a yard is not one either. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people at that first table have said since that a yard of about nineteen people has spent a month finding out that a page cannot say something, and that this morning it has found out that a clerk cannot either, and that the two of those are the same size of thing.

Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that it is empty on the first morning in six volumes on which she entered less than she heard, and she entered that she is not entering that on this page either, and about five people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it, and two of the five were said at about nine feet off a man who does not stop in that yard twice a day. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man saying that he would rather somebody did not ask him a third question is not one of the six.

That morning is the first in six volumes on which a clerk of this district entered less than she heard, and she said out loud, in front of about nineteen people, that a sentence she has entered before had not been about the right thing, and a man who has been given nothing on a hundred and seventy-four mornings said out loud that about four people behind a clerk had not noticed that a question about a man is not a question about a page, and about nine people in that yard have said since that nobody in it has joined those two.

The buckets went down that bank empty and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near the foot of that bank have said since that she came up it about two minutes behind a man who had just been asked a question about himself in a yard and had not been asked one for a hundred and seventy-four mornings before that. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that he read them at about a quarter to four, and that a boy asked his own clerk a question about a man this morning and got one word, and that neither of those two things was entered.

A clerk asked a man at the foot of that low wall for the third time in six volumes whether there was a thing she would rather she did not keep, and he said no and said he would rather she had not, and a boy asked her how many figures she had about him and she said one word, and a clerk entered that she was asked a question and entered that she is not entering the answers, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the thirty-fifth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and two things that were said out loud in that yard this morning are on no page of this district.
