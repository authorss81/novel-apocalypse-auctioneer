# Chapter 550: The Fiftieth Morning Of This Volume

The stone on the corner of that book was cold enough at about nine on the eighteenth of the third month that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing would not put his hand flat on it, and about four people in that yard noticed that and about nine of them walked round that corner, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about four of those had come up that bank on the morning before and about four had not come up that bank in a fortnight.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and eighty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that she had entered that figure on every morning of this month and on the last morning of this volume, and that she did not look up out of that yard while she entered it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and seventy-fifth of those mornings, and that a clerk entered the four of them on her own page at about a quarter to eight this morning and did not look up, and that about four of them have said that is the only thing she did not look up for.

"**The board carries four hundred and forty-eight, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and sixty-four days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seventy-eight, and four hundred and thirty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about ten, and put his right hand flat on the boards, and left it there, and did not put it on the stone lying face up beside them and did not put it on the sheet of paper lying beside that, and about nine people in that yard have said since that he has been in that yard on every morning of this volume and that this is the first morning anybody has watched where his hand was.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man put his hand flat on the boards of that second table this morning and did not put it on that stone and did not put it on that sheet, and entered that she is not entering that as anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and ten and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That book has four lines on it and the other one has three and there is a copy lying next to that stone, and about nine people in this yard can now tell you which of the two of those has a line with nothing on it, and about four of them would not have been able to do that nine days ago, and not one of us can tell you which of the two this district made, and about four of them have said that is the first thing this yard has ever said out loud about a page and it is not a way of telling a stranger.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he was not asked for it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that second table at about eleven and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I read that sheet out loud two afternoons ago in the middle of that yard and the third line of it is still not there, and I have not read it again, and I am not going to read it again, and about four people have asked me since whether I would and I have said the same thing to all four of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a sheet in that yard has been read out loud once and is not going to be read out loud again, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about half past eleven with her shop open behind her, and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**My shop is open and I lost a morning of trade ten days ago, and a book in this yard has four lines on it that it did not have, and I have not been thanked and I am not going to be, and about four of you have been putting those two things next to each other all week and they are not next to each other.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about one, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got sixty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have counted nine words on that page and I counted them out loud the day before anybody wrote them down, and I have counted ninety-three marks in chalk along the edge of that table, and about four people in this yard have said that this district can now say who is not in a book and cannot say who is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nine words were said out loud in that yard on the fifteenth of this month and were not on a page until the sixteenth, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a book in that yard has said out loud that its own third line is a person and that the man who comes up that lane twice a day has read four off that wall on every morning of this volume and has never read that book. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and he had read that copy out loud in that yard on the afternoon of the sixteenth and has not read it since. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nineteen people in that yard have been saying things out loud all month and that not one of them has said one to the man on those stones. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and ninety-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and fifty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and fifty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and twenty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and thirty-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and sixty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are ninety-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and four hundred and eleven did not move on one of the fifty. The count of things this district has made is thirteen, and the thirteen is smaller than the twelve, and a clerk entered that on the sixteenth of this month and not before.

The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and the fifth of them is a remedy, and a fourth line is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and a clerk entered that on the last morning of this volume. A clerk entered that on the last morning of this volume and entered the reason. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and a sheet of that copy was read out loud on the sixteenth of this month at about half past four and was not read again, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk is not entering the name of the man who read it. About six people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and a stone with a hollow in it and a mark in chalk and a fourth line are not one of the six.

There is a fourth line on a book in that yard and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that a book in that yard has had a fourth line on it for two days and that the woman who has read it standing up has not looked at that line, and that the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full, and that the woman of about fifty-eight, going past as she always does, read the three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her, and that about four of them have said she reads the first three and goes on, and that that is the whole of what she has ever done with that book. The man of fifty-six read the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and seventy-fifth morning and that a man at the end of that second table has had his right hand flat on the boards all day and not on the stone and not on the sheet.

A yard on a bank has two tables in it, and on the first of them a book lies under a stone with no wear on it, and that book has four lines on it and the fourth of them is nine words in a clerk's own hand, and on the second of them a stone lies face up with a hollow in it one inch deep in the middle and nothing at either end, and a sheet of paper lies beside that stone with three lines on it and nothing in the third, and it is on that table for the fiftieth morning and it has been on that table for fifty days, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the count of that board on her own page this morning without looking up, and a man of about sixty-four is at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones and has been given nothing, and is on his hundred and eighty-ninth night of that run and has slept on a hundred and eighty-eight of them, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing is at the end of that second table with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on the copy.
