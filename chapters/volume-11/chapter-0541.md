# Chapter 541: Nobody Could Say What The Copy Was For

A smell off the river end came into that yard at about half past eight on the ninth of the third month and stayed in it until about eleven, and about four people in that yard noticed it before anything else happened and none of them said one word to anybody about it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those had come up that bank on the eighth of this month for a count and had not come up that bank on any other morning of this volume.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and eighty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and sixty-sixth of those mornings, and that the yard smells of the river this morning and the man at the wall has not turned round.

"**The board carries four hundred and thirty-nine, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fifty-five days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and sixty-nine, and four hundred and thirty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came up that bank at about nine with a basket on her hip, and set the basket down on the boards of that second table because the step into that yard is two inches higher than the bank and she has been coming up it for eleven years, and then stood there with her hand still on the rim of it.

She said one thing out loud to about nineteen people, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read all four of those pages and I said in a shop once that I could not tell which came first, and I have not stopped reading them since, and I have put a basket on that table this morning because the step is two inches, and I am asking the clerk what the second one is for.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman asked a question in that yard and entered the question, and entered that the question was what a sheet of paper on a table is for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud then, standing at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand, in about four seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I do not know.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything at all for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a page in this district now carries three words about a document that has been lying in that yard for forty-one mornings, and entered that this district has not had that on a page before, and entered that she does not know what it means, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A thing is for whoever has to find out what it was for. Forty-one mornings and nobody has had to, and that is not because it is no use. It is because it has not finished being wrong yet, and I am not saying that in a way that is a rule.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man at the end of that second table said a thing that is not a rule and is not an admission, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud from the east end of that yard, where he has stood for about thirty years with the flat book under his left arm, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted it and got thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A document is for the day somebody needs it. I have carried a book under this arm since the spring and there has not been one day in it, and I would not burn it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally has said in a yard that a book with no day in it is not a book to burn, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that first table at about eleven with the rein over his forearm and said one thing out loud in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put that sheet on that table on the first morning of this volume and nobody asked me to, and I have not read one word of it since. About four people in this yard have asked me twice this month what it is, and the answer is the same both times and it is that I know where it came from.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who put a sheet on a table in that yard in the open is not a man who keeps a copy of anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three then took her basket off those boards, which took her longer than putting it on, and went back down that bank, and about nine people in that yard have said since that she asked the question nobody had asked and about four of them have said that the two words she got back are the first time a page in this district has said that it does not know what a thing on a table is for.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk said four words in that yard this morning about four hundred yards from where he walked. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had been at the near end of that first table at about eleven. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a figure this district could not get was got in that yard two mornings ago and that nobody has thought to tell the man on those stones about the four words either. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eightieth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and eighty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or a page. A hundred and forty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and forty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and nineteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and twenty-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Ninety-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are eighty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and about nine people at that first table have said since that a yard can hold a count of a month and a does-not-know in the same morning and has not worked out yet which of the two it would rather be able to take back. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a sentence admitting that this district does not know is not a thirteenth.

The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people at that second table have said since that a book a stranger can walk up to and read is on its fourth thing, which is a man, and that this morning the same book was asked what it was for and got three words back from the woman who keeps it. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that she does not know what a document is for and did not read that out of a book to anybody, and about six people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and a woman asking a question at a table is not one of the six.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and about four people at the foot of it have said since that the buckets went down that bank with nothing in them and came back up it full, and that the woman of about fifty-eight came up behind them reading the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, without stopping, and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her, and that she was not told the four words this morning and did not ask for them. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall again at about four and got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a sheet of paper with three lines on it has been on the end of that second table for forty-one mornings and that this is the first morning anybody has asked what it is for, and that the answer was four words and the four words are on a page and the sheet is not.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman asked in that yard what a sheet with three lines on it is for, and entered that a clerk answered out loud that she does not know, and entered that a page in this district now says so, and entered that the sheet has not moved and is not going to, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the forty-first morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and a count of a month was got off that board two mornings ago, and about nineteen people in that yard have heard that a figure was got and have not heard what it is for.
