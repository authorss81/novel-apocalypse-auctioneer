# Chapter 542: Two Sheets Held Up To The Light

The second board of that second table from the far end had lifted about an inch at its loose corner sometime in the night, and about four people in that yard noticed it before anything else happened and about nine of them stepped over it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the tenth of the third month, and about nine of those had been in that yard on the morning before and had come back without being asked to.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and eighty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that a figure gets older while it stands still, and that this is the whole of what this district knows about it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and sixty-seventh of those mornings and that about four of them have now watched two sheets of paper be held up in front of a wall he has spent two hundred and sixty-seven mornings in front of.

"**The board carries four hundred and forty, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fifty-six days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seventy, and four hundred and thirty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The boy of about nineteen and the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up each took one of the two sheets off that second table at about ten and held one of them up in the light off the river end, about four feet apart, at the same height, and about nineteen people stood in front of them and looked at both.

Nobody in that yard said anything at all for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall, and about nine people within about nine feet of that second table have said since that about four of them put their heads on one side and about five put their heads on the other, and that nobody has yet been able to say which of those two sides was right.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up said one thing out loud, holding his sheet up at arm's length the whole time, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have put tables up for about nineteen years and I have never in my life needed to know which of two boards was mine. I am holding one of them and I cannot tell you which one I am holding, and I am not going to put either one down until somebody else has held it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two men held two sheets up in that yard in the open and entered that about nineteen people looked at both, and entered that a man who puts tables up for about nineteen years said he could not tell, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud then, with both sheets still up, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are three more of these in this district and I have found all three, and one of them is at a shop and one of them is on a wall nine hundred yards off and one of them is in a house where a woman does not let anybody read it. That is four places and about nineteen people, and the four of them are the same three lines and one of them is ours.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the count of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four, and entered that about nine people in that yard have known that since the fourth morning of this volume and that about four of them have been in two of the four.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is one way to tell a stranger which of these two is ours and it is not looking. It is the person who made the book standing next to it and saying something about himself, and if he does that, then the other one is a copy, and if nobody does that, then both of them are documents and one of them is a thing that was made by somebody and this district cannot say which.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man at the end of that second table said out loud that the only way to tell a stranger which of two sheets is this district's own is for a person to admit something about himself, and entered that no person has done that, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about eleven, where she has stood on and off for about a month, and said one thing out loud in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A shopkeeper is told about a thing by the person who made it. Nobody has ever come in here and told me which of those two was made here, and I have not asked, and I am not saying that I would not ask.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman who keeps a scale has said out loud in a yard that she has not asked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the two sheets went back onto that second table, about four feet apart where they have been for forty-two mornings, and about four people in that yard have said since that a man of about thirty-seven held his up for eleven minutes and a boy held his up for eleven minutes and that about nine people looked at both of them and that nobody in that yard can now tell anybody which is which, and that about four of them have said that the man who could tell is standing at the end of that table with his left hand in its cloth.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk entered an entry this morning about a figure getting older while it stands still, which is the only thing anybody has entered in that yard this month about the figure on that sheet. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and did not look at the two sheets. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nineteen people in this yard have now looked at two sheets of paper and could not tell them apart, and that the man on those stones has been in this yard for every one of those mornings and has not looked at either sheet. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-first night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Three hundred and twenty-three days is how far back the ninth of the nine printed nights is, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A hundred and ninety days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and forty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and fifty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and twenty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifty-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Ninety-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are eighty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the count of places in this district where three lines can be read is four, and about nine people at that second table have said since that a figure which can be checked has one place and a figure which cannot be checked has four, and that the two things which look most alike in that yard are the two sheets and a thing nine hundred yards off that this district has not used as a device in a long time, and that about four of them have said the two of those are the same size of problem. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and holding two things up to the light is not a thirteenth.

The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people in that yard have said since that the fourth thing a document that sets a lot out has to say is a person and that a document which is held up to the light in a yard of about nineteen people is being asked to say the fourth thing out loud with nothing on it. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and about seven people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it, and a clerk has entered that a reading is not a document and a document is not a reading. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and a man saying out loud that the only way to tell a stranger is to stand next to your own work and say something about yourself is not one of the six.

The man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall again at about four and got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that two sheets of paper were held up in front of him this morning and that he read them off at the same speed he read them at about half past eight, and that about four of them have said that is the whole of what he has to say about it. Before that both buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight was on the top of that bank before they were two feet up, reading the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near the foot of it have said since that she is not one of the about nineteen people who spent eleven minutes looking at two sheets this morning.

Two men held two sheets of paper up in that yard this morning, about four feet apart, for about eleven minutes, and about nineteen people looked at both of them and not one of them could say afterwards which of the two was the book, and a man at the end of that second table said out loud that there is one way to tell a stranger and it is a person standing beside his own work saying something about himself, and nobody in that yard did that, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the forty-second morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and there are four places in this district where a stranger may now read the same three lines and be told nothing.
