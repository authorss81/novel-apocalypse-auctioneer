# Chapter 522: Two Figures In That Yard Came Out The Same

The boy of about nineteen found out at about a quarter past eight on the morning of the eighteenth of the second month that the fourth of the four figures the man of fifty-six says out loud in that yard and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost are the same figure this morning, four hundred and eleven, and said so out loud in front of about nine people, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that two figures in that yard have come out equal and entered that neither of them is the other one and entered that this district has never had a rule about it.

It was the eighteenth morning of the second month and there was a hard clean light on that ground from before seven and the boards of that second table were warm by about half past eight, and the stone lying face up on the end of that second table had been warm by about nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and sixty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and forty-seventh of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and twenty, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and thirty-six days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and fifty, and four hundred and eleven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He found it at about a quarter past eight with his own sheet against the wall and he said it out loud to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Those are the same figure. That one is the age of a figure on a sheet nine hundred yards off and this one is a count of days from the second of January on a wall, and they have come out the same number this morning, and about four of us have been reading both of them for a month.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that two figures in that yard are the same figure and that neither of them is a copy of the other one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the rest of it out loud, standing at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand and not on the boards, in about nine seconds, and about nineteen people heard it, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering that two of the figures in this district have come out equal by accident and I am entering that this district has never once in six volumes had a rule about two figures coming out equal, and I am not entering that it is a coincidence, because I do not know what a coincidence is in a place with about nineteen people in it and a wall a man reads every morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk has entered that she does not know a word, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel was then asked, out loud, in front of about nine people, whether the paper on the post at the first channel is the same paper as the one on the counter in the shop, and she answered without being asked twice and the boy counted it and got seventy-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to answer that and I am not going to pretend I did not hear it. I have looked at a post and I have looked at a counter and there are two of them, and the tear is on the same edge of both, and I could not tell you today and I could not have told you a month ago and nobody in this district has a way of telling me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering a tear as a check on anything, and entered the reason, which is that a torn edge is not a date and a date is the only thing in this district anybody can check, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was at the end of that second table from about ten and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have said in this yard that I put up a post and not a page, and I am not going to say anything else about the page, and about four of you have been asking me since I said it whether I would put a post up again if somebody asked me, and I am telling you here that I would not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who has put up about four hundred things in this district has now said out loud that he would not put up one of these, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and said nothing after the third one and went and sat down on the low step, and about four people at that first table have said since that a man read three lines out loud in that yard this morning and said nothing at all after them and that this is the second morning running and that about four of them have said that they think somebody in that yard is about to be asked the thing he keeps not saying.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten with his left hand in its cloth and said nothing at all all morning, and about four people near him have said since that a man who has now said things out loud in that yard on three mornings of this month has said nothing on four mornings running, and that about four other people have said that a man who waits is not the same as a man who has nothing to say, and that nobody in that yard is going to be the one who settles it.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he read all four of them at about the same speed as he reads them every morning and did not stop on the last one, and that this is the first morning in this month that he has not stopped on the last one. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man who has answered the same question three times has now not been asked anything for five mornings, and that nobody has noticed that either. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about nine people within four feet of that wall have said since that a man has now sat on those stones for five mornings and has not been asked one thing on any of the five. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and sixty-first night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and sixty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and seventy days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and twenty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and thirty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and thirty-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Seventy-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are sixty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and a clerk entered this morning that two other figures in this district have come out equal and entered no reason for it. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and two figures coming out equal is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people at that first table have said since that there are now three places in this district with those three lines on them and this district has one way of making them and no way of telling them apart. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about four people at that first table have said since that a clerk has entered eleven sentences this month and has entered the one word in one of them and nothing in the column. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a clerk who does not know a word is not one of the six.

Down went that bank at about half past five with two buckets empty and up it came with them full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near the bottom of that bank have said since that she has been past that counter nine times in the last ten evenings and has not looked at it on any of them. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near enough to see his face have said since that he said the last of the four without stopping to think about it for the second morning running.

The fourth of the four figures a man of fifty-six says out loud in that yard came out this morning the same figure as the one on the sheet at that gatepost, which is four hundred and eleven and has not moved in six volumes, and a boy said so out loud in front of about nine people and a clerk entered that neither of the two is a copy of the other and entered that this district has no rule about it, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the twenty-second morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
