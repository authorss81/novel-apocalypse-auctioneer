# Chapter 538: He Began A Question And Did Not Finish It

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing began a question to the man of about fifty-six at about two in the afternoon of the sixth of the third month in the yard of Lot Seventeen, got about six words into it, and did not finish it, and the man of about fifty-six said one thing out loud before the boy had counted anything, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man began a question and did not finish it and entered that nobody asked him to finish it, and about nine people in that yard have said since that this is the second time in a fortnight a man has stopped in the middle of a thing in that yard, and that the two of them stopped for opposite reasons.

It was the sixth morning of the third month and the light came up off the water behind that yard before seven and went flat by about nine and stayed flat, and the boards of that second table had a skin of water on the underside of them and not on the top, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and seventy-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and sixty-third of them, and that about four of them have said that he has been asked nothing since the twenty-sixth morning of last month and has said nothing to anybody about being asked nothing.

"**The board carries four hundred and thirty-six, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fifty-two days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and sixty-six, and four hundred and twenty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He got as far as the sixth word and stopped, and about nine people within about nine feet of that wall have said since that a man who has asked this yard a question on about nine mornings in a month stood at about two in the afternoon in front of a man at a wall with his mouth open for about four seconds and then shut it, and that about four of them have said that a man who stops in the middle of a thing in that yard usually stops because he does not want to finish it, and that this one did not look like that.

The man of about fifty-six turned round from that wall, which he has not done at a question in about two hundred and sixty-three mornings, and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You have been standing there with a question in your mouth for about a minute and you have not asked it, and I would like it entered that I noticed, and I am not going to help you with it, and I am not going to refuse it either, because a refusal out of me this afternoon in front of all of these is a figure about me in a yard, and I have had one of those a month and it was not pleasant and I am not having a second one on a morning when a clerk is going to count something.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man turned round from that wall for the first time at a question in about two hundred and sixty-three mornings, and entered that she is not entering the six words, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said it out loud then, in front of about nineteen people, that a man had begun a question and had not finished it, and the boy counted what he said and got thirty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man stood there with a question and he did not finish it and nobody is going to make him and I am saying it now so that about nineteen people have it and not just me.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man began a question and did not finish it, and entered that nobody asked him to finish it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about a quarter past two, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got ninety-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I was not going to ask him for a figure and he is right and he has said it before I have said it. I was going to ask him whether a man who reads four figures off a wall every morning for about two hundred and sixty-three mornings knows which of them he would lose first, and I stopped, because on a morning when a clerk is going to count a row of marks out loud I am not going to stand in that yard and be the second person asking for a number.**"

About nine people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man who has spent a month asking this yard questions said out loud this afternoon that he stopped asking one on purpose, and that about four of them have said that a man at that wall has now told a yard twice that he is not going to be the one who hands anybody a number, and that the two of those are the same sentence said by two men four weeks apart. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not joining those two sentences, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was at the end of that second table at about eleven and said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody is going to hold anything on that morning. A table is not a stand and a man is not a weight for a page. I put the second one up in about eleven minutes six volumes ago and I have watched four people put things on it since and the only one of them who is still there is a sheet of paper with a line on it that nobody has filled in.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the four things, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man who has not been asked anything in about a fortnight asked a yard not to ask him a question, which is the first time anybody in this district has asked a man to leave a thing alone. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and did not look at the board, and about four people at the end of that second table have said since that is twice in a month that man has come past a piece of wood with marks in it and not looked at it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who has asked to be left alone this morning and a man at that wall who nobody has asked anything are about nine feet from each other at about half past three and that neither of them knows it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and eighty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and forty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and forty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and sixteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and nineteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Ninety-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are eighty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and a man who mends fencing said out loud in that yard this afternoon that he stopped in the middle of a question on purpose, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of any month. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man saying he stopped asking is not a thirteenth, and a man saying a table is not a stand is not one either. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people at that first table have said since that this yard has now had two men refuse to be asked for a number in a fortnight and that about four of them have said that neither man was asked for one by the other.

Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that it is empty on a morning when a man asked this yard not to ask him a question, and entered that a question is not a reading, and about four people said something out loud in that yard this afternoon and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man asking a yard not to ask him a question is not one of the six.

Two men in that yard stopped in the middle of a thing this month and neither of them was asked to go on, and one of them stopped because he would not and one of them stopped because he decided not to, and a clerk entered both of those and entered that she is not entering which of the two is the braver, and about four people near that first table have said since that the only figure either of them gave this month was a refusal.

At about half past five the buckets went down that bank empty and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near the top of that bank have said since that she read the whole of it tonight without stopping on any of the three, which she has not done since before the middle of the sixth month. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that he turned round from that wall at about two this afternoon and faced about nine people for about nine seconds, and that about four of them think he is going to be asked again before the eighth.

A man who mends fencing got six words into a question this afternoon and stopped, and a man at that wall turned round and said out loud that he was not going to help him and was not going to refuse him either, and a clerk entered that she is not entering the six words, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the thirty-eighth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and there are two mornings left.
