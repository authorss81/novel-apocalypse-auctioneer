# Chapter 501: A Second Book On The Second Table

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart put a sheet of paper on the end of the second table in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about ten past eight on the morning of the twenty-eighth of this month, with the near wheel still dragging behind him, and there are three lines on it and the first two of them are the first two of that lot book and there is nothing at all in the third of them, and about nineteen people saw him do it and nobody in that yard asked him one word about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

It was the twenty-eighth morning of the first month of the nineteenth year and the frost was off the boards of that first table before about half past seven and the light came up off the south end of that ground flat and grey and without any wind in it, and the stone lying face up on that second table had been cold until about nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and forty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, and a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and twenty-sixth of them.

"**The board carries three hundred and ninety-nine, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fifteen days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and twenty-nine, and three hundred and ninety days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The paper is not the paper that book is on. That book is a board with a hinge and a stone on the corner of it and has had three lines on it since the second of the twelfth month. What is lying on the end of that second table is a single sheet with a torn edge on the side nearest the yard, and the first line of it is the first line of that book word for word and the second line of it is the second line of that book word for word, and the third line of it is a ruled line with the paper still on it.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart said one thing out loud at about ten past eight with his hand still on the paper and then he went up that lane on foot, and about nine people heard the whole of it and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got forty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I was told to put it on the far table and not to stop, and I have done both of those things and I am not going to be asked where I got it, because I was not asked where I got it either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a person was told to put a sheet of paper on a table and was not asked where it came from, and entered that she is not entering the person who told him and entered the reason, which is that nobody has asked her anything, and about nine people have said since that he did not stop at that table and about four people have said since that he did not look at the wall on his way past it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen came to the near end of that first table at about a quarter past nine and looked at the sheet on the second table for about four seconds and then came back to the first one and looked at that book for about four seconds and did not say anything at all to anybody, and a clerk entered that a boy looked at two books and entered no figure about either of them and entered that she is not going to.

Then he asked, out loud, in front of about nine people, how long this month is, and the man of fifty-six said the whole of what he knows about it, which is that he does not know, and that nobody in that yard knows, and that the last time anybody in this district counted a month off that board was on the last day of the last one and it took about an hour and a half and about nineteen people stood at that wall for it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked a question out loud in that yard and entered that no figure was given, and entered that a figure somebody entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day, and said that out loud in the ordinary voice before she wrote it down, and a clerk entered that she said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and about four people at that table have said since that a clerk has now written that sentence down three times in six volumes and that the second time was a habit and a habit is a finding.

Nobody answered her. The record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard at any point on that day. A clerk of nineteen years entered that he was not in that yard and entered that she did not ask and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked. He has now been out of that yard on four mornings running, three of them in the last block of the last volume, and about nine people at that first table have said since that they have not worked out when he stopped coming and that four of them are not going to ask.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past twelve with the last of that day's flour on her hip, read those three lines in that lot book standing up, walked over to that second table, and read the three lines on the sheet standing up, and stood at about four feet off the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds and did not say one word about either of them.

Nobody asked her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman read two books standing up and said nothing, and entered that she is not going to enter a comparison, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and then he stood at the near end of that second table and read the three lines on that sheet in the ordinary voice, slowly, and about nine people heard all of it, and he said nothing at the end of the third one and went and sat down on the low step at the end of that first table for about a minute, and nobody asked him what the third one said.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and nobody said a word to him all morning.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and a clerk entered that she did not ask him about anything else either. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at that first table have said since that he did not look at the paper on the second table on his way past. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about nine people within four feet of that wall have said since that they have never once seen anybody sit down beside him. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and fortieth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and thirty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and forty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and seventy-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and eighty-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and eighteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Fifty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are forty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a sheet of paper with two lines on it is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of them is a person, and that book has no fourth line in it. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about four people have said since that it has been named on every morning of this district for a month and has had nothing in it on every one of them. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count of the five is still five with no sixth in it, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that she reads the third line about half a second slower than the first two and has never said so. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and did not go and stand at the end of the second table.

A piece of paper with two lines on it and nothing in the third of it has been on the end of the second table in the yard of Lot Seventeen since ten past eight on the morning of the twenty-eighth of this month, and nobody in that yard asked the man who put it down one word, and nobody in that yard has taken it off that table, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that neither of those two things happened and entered that she did not ask about either of them, and the third line of that sheet is a ruled line with the paper still on it and nobody has written anything in it, and about nineteen people have stood in that yard and looked at it and not one of them has said out loud which of the two books on two tables this district made.
