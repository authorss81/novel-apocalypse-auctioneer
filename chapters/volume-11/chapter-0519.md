# Chapter 519: I Wrote It, And You Watched Me

The boy of about nineteen asked the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, out loud, in front of about nineteen people at the near end of that first table on the morning of the fifteenth of the second month, who made that square of paper, and she said that she did, in about nine seconds, and about nine people have said since that this is the first question anybody in that yard has asked this month that got a true answer straight back.

It was the fifteenth morning of the second month and the sky was low and stayed low until about two in the afternoon and did not rain, and the boards of that second table were warm by about half past nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and fifty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and forty-fourth of them, and that he has not once looked down the bank.

"**The board carries four hundred and seventeen, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and thirty-three days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and forty-seven, and four hundred and eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He asked it at about eleven with his own sheet in his left hand and the right half of it folded over, and she had come up the bank with nothing in her hands on purpose, and about nineteen people heard the whole of it and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I wrote it, at about a quarter past ten three mornings ago, at the end of your own second table, in about nine minutes, with a pencil stub off the top of your own scale, and about nineteen people in that yard watched me do it and about four of them have said since that I got the second line right, and that is the whole of what is known about that paper and it is known because everybody was looking.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a page was made in the open in that yard and entered that she is not entering it as a thing this district made, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the boy asked the second half of it, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and she answered without being asked twice and the boy counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Then who made the other one, because you have just told me about yours and nobody in that yard has told me about theirs, and you said on the first morning of this month in a shop that you could not tell which of the two of them this district made, and you said it in front of about nine people and about four of them have said it since.**"

The boy counted what she said and got forty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said I could not tell and I still cannot, and I am not going to be able to after what I have just said, and the difference between those two things is about nine minutes of a woman's handwriting and there is nothing else in it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the difference between the two pages as a finding, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was then asked a third question, out loud, in front of about nine people, by the boy, at about a quarter past eleven, which was whether he had ever carried anything for anybody in that yard besides that sheet, and he answered without stopping what he was doing with a strap and the boy counted it and got fifty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. I have got one answer and this is the third time I have said it and it is going to be the same number of words as the other two times, and about four of you have been waiting for a different third time and there is not going to be one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a third answer of the same length is not a check on the first two and is not a check on itself, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that this district has now run one check on one man's memory three mornings running and it has returned the same figure three times, and that a check that cannot come out a second way is not a check.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and said one thing after it in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About four of us have known that second line was wrong since before the last volume began, and about four of you have known nothing, and that is the reason that page in the shop is as good as the two in this yard, and nobody has to be a liar for that to happen.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a finding about anybody in that yard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten with his left hand in its cloth and said nothing all morning, and about four people near him have said since that he asked one question yesterday and has not asked one today, and that about four other people have said that a man who asked a question he was not asked to ask is the sort of man who asks one and then stops, and that both of those sets of people are describing the same two mornings.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are sixty-two of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and about four people near him have said since that he has now been within about eleven feet of four questions this month and has answered one of them.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel was not in that yard this morning and a clerk entered that she was not in it and entered that she did not ask and entered the reason, and about four people at that first table have said since that she has been in that yard on six mornings out of the last sixteen and has not been in it on ten and that nobody in that yard has worked out what a person does with ten mornings.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he did not stop, and about four people at that wall have said since that a yard of about nineteen people has asked about nine things this month and the one person who walks past it twice a day has been asked none of them. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and did not look at the boy. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about nine people within four feet of that wall have said since that four mornings running is the longest anybody in that yard has gone without saying one word to one another. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and fifty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and sixty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and twenty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man and that date is on a counter in a shop in a hand that is not his. A hundred and twenty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and thirty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Seventy-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are sixty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and nobody in that yard has said out loud that the month it is living in now is any length at all. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a person saying out loud that they made a thing is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people at that first table have said since that this is the only one of the three lines that anybody in this district can say who wrote. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and a man said a third thing out loud this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man who has said the same thing three times is not one of the six.

The buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read those three lines standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said a word to her, and about four people near the top of that bank have said since that a woman of about thirty-six came up it four minutes ahead of the buckets and did not stop at the first table. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall for about a minute and a half afterwards without moving, and about four people near it have said since that a man who has read four figures off it for two hundred and forty-four mornings has not this month said one word about where a date went.

A page in a shop was made in the open in that yard three mornings ago in about nine minutes by a woman who can tell which of two books she has been reading and not which one this district made, and this morning that woman said out loud in front of about nineteen people that she wrote it and that about nineteen people watched her, and the yard's question about that page is finished, and the yard's question about the other one is exactly where it was nineteen mornings ago, and a clerk entered that one of the three pages in this district can be traced to a person in a room and that the other two cannot, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the nineteenth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
