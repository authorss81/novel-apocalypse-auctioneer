# Chapter 548: She Wrote Nine Words In Her Own Hand

The light came across that first table from the river end at about ten past nine on the sixteenth of the third month and laid itself along the top of that lot book, and about four people in that yard noticed that a stone was on that book and about nine of them noticed it come off, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those had come up that bank on the morning before to hear nine words that were not on a page yet.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and eighty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that a clerk has entered that figure on eight mornings running and that she will go on entering it after this month is out.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and seventy-third of those mornings and that about four of them have been at that first table since about nine.

"**The board carries four hundred and forty-six, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and sixty-two days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seventy-six, and four hundred and thirty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

A clerk of nineteen years took the stone off the corner of that lot book at about ten with both hands, set it down on the boards of that first table about two feet away, did not move the book, and about nine people in that yard have said since that nobody in that yard has taken a stone off a book with two hands before.

She said one thing out loud, standing there, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am writing it now, in my own hand, at nobody's dictation, with about nineteen people standing at this table. If it is wrong I will take it out and there is nobody here who can stop me, and if it is right nobody here will ever know that I was frightened, because I am not going to say so.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud from the end of that second table, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**She has not asked one of us anything, and about four of us have stood here since about nine waiting for her to, and I am not going to be the one who says so.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and that nobody was asked before that book was written, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

She wrote the nine words in the front of that lot book with her own hand, in about a minute, and about nine people in that yard watched a pen do it and nobody spoke while it was happening.

Then the clerk of nineteen years read what she had written out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The line above is a person, not named here.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything at all for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk wrote a fourth line in that book in that yard in daylight in front of about nineteen people, and entered the nine words, and entered that the count of things this district has made moves from twelve to thirteen today, and entered that the thirteen is smaller than the twelve, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that no column was ruled for the fourth line and that none is going to be, and entered the reason, which is that a fourth line is not a column and a column is something a person looks down and a line is something a person reads, and entered that a person who copies that book after today is copying an admission and an admission is easier to copy than a figure and costs whoever copies it nothing, and entered that this district cannot now stand in front of a stranger and say that the other sheet is a copy, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of that ditch at about half past ten, came to the end of that second table, and put his own thumb into the hollow in the middle of that stone lying face up on it, and pressed, and took his thumb out, and did not say one word about it, and about nine people in that yard have said since that he did it and said nothing, and that about four of them do not know whether he agreed.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man put his thumb in the hollow in that stone this morning and entered that he said nothing, and entered that he was not asked whether he agreed and was not asked whether the nine words are true, and entered that a clerk is not entering whether he agreed either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I shut my shop for a morning eight days ago and I would do it again, and about four people here have been saying all morning that it cost me a morning of trade and that you got a fourth line out of it, and I would like both entered in the same breath.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered both halves of that together, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud from the east end of that yard, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got thirty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That book had one thing in it that went out of date by itself. Now it has none, and I have been in this yard about thirty years and I have never seen one do that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody has read that sheet out loud and that it has lain on that table for forty-eight mornings, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a book on that first table has had four lines on it since about ten this morning and that he came up that lane an hour and a half after it and was not told. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm, and did not go up that bank, and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that the man on those stones was not told about the fourth line until about a quarter to four, and that he did not look up. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

At about half past four the man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart came up that bank on foot, took the sheet off the end of that second table with his right hand, and stood in the middle of that yard with it held out at arm's length in front of his own face, and about nineteen people turned round and looked at the back of it.

He said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have not read this before and I am reading it now. The first line is about a foot of standing water in a ditch. The second line is a figure of how far behind that is. The third line is ruled and it is empty. That is the book. This is not the book. On this one the first two are the same and the third one is not there, and I am saying that out loud in front of you because that is the whole of what is different between them.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything at all for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall, and about nine people in that yard have said since that a sheet with three lines on it has been read out loud for the first time in six volumes, and that about four of them have said a line nobody filled in is now a thing that was said out loud.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man read a sheet of that copy out loud in that yard in the open, and that it is the first time that sheet has been read out loud by anybody, and that a reading is done by whoever is standing there and that standing in a yard is not a post, and that she is not entering the name of whoever read it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart said one thing out loud after that, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put this on that table on the first morning of this volume and I have stood next to it every morning since, and I could not tell you this morning which one of these two was ours, and neither could about nine other people, and I have said a thing out loud about that sheet this afternoon and not about myself, and it is still not the book.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a sheet in that yard has now been read out loud and has not thereby become the book, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past five, at the near end of that first table, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is the whole of what this yard has been doing since the first morning of this month, and I am entering it as the whole of it. Nobody had to be got. Somebody had to say it out loud, in a yard, where about nineteen people could hear it, and about half past four this afternoon a man who has stood next to that sheet for forty-eight mornings stood in the middle of that yard and said the third line of it was not there.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody went up that bank after the buckets on the afternoon a fourth line went on a book, and about four people at the top of it have said since that a book in that yard has had a fourth line on it since about ten this morning and that the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full, and that a woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody and that nobody there said one word to her, and that she came past it this evening and did not look at it. The man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall at about four and got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that the column was ruled and empty this morning and was ruled and empty this evening, and that about four of them have said a man read a sheet out loud in between and nothing went into it.

A hundred and ninety-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and fifty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and fifty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and twenty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and twenty-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and sixty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it. A hundred and five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are ninety-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and four hundred and eleven did not move.

The count of things this district has made is thirteen, and the thirteenth is smaller than the twelve, and a clerk entered that at about ten this morning and not before, and about four people in that yard have said since that a district which has gone six volumes without a thing that goes out of date on its own has now said out loud that its own third line is a figure of a person it is not going to name. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and the fifth of them is a remedy, and a fourth line is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and a clerk entered that on the morning it went on. A clerk entered that this morning and entered that she is entering the reason. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today except a sheet of that copy at about half past four, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that she is not entering the name of the man who read it, and about nine people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and an admission a clerk makes about her own book is not one of the six.

A clerk of nineteen years wrote a fourth line in that lot book on the sixteenth of the third month in the open, in daylight, in front of about nineteen people, in her own hand and at nobody's dictation, and it is nine words and it says that the line above is a person and that he is not named here, and a man who digs loam put his thumb in a hollow in a stone and said nothing, and at about half past four a man who has stood next to that sheet for forty-eight mornings read it out loud and said that its third line is not there, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the forty-eighth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and about four people in that yard have said since that a man stood in front of both of those things and said a thing out loud about one of them, and that that is not a person saying something about himself.
