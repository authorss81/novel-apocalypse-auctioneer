# Chapter 511: The Wrong Figure Copied Faithfully

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up out of that ditch at about ten on the morning of the seventh of the second month, put that sheet of paper on the end of that first table beside that lot book with his right hand, read the first line of one of them out loud and then the first line of the other one out loud, then the second line of one of them and then the second line of the other one, and they are the same, and about nineteen people in that yard heard all of it and about nine of them have said since that they did not know they were listening to the wrong figure being proved right.

It was the seventh morning of the second month and the frost was on the low step at the end of that first table until about nine and the boards of that second table were cold to the hand until about half past nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and fifty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and thirty-sixth of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and nine, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and twenty-five days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and thirty-nine, and four hundred days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He said it at about a quarter past ten with his right hand flat on that sheet and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got sixty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said that day out loud in that yard and a clerk wrote it down because I said it. Whoever wrote that sheet copied that line as well, and if they had made it up they would have got it right, because there are about four of us who know it is wrong and the rest of this district has never had a reason to look.**"

Nobody answered him at that point. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the second line of that sheet and the second line of that book are the same, and entered that both of them are wrong in the same way, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the rest of it out loud, standing at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand and not on the boards, and about nineteen people heard it, and the boy counted what she said and got fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A copy that carries the error cannot be told from an honest copy, and this district has never been able to tell a forgery from a mistake, and we have never needed to, because our only defence against somebody faking us has been that this district does not do that. That is about conduct and not about a page.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a defence about conduct is not a check anybody can run, and entered that she is not entering the word forgery beside it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen asked the man of about thirty-four who digs loam, out loud, in front of about nine people, whether that third line is his day, and he answered without being asked twice and the boy counted what he said and got twenty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That line is mine on the other one. On that one there is nothing and I have not asked what the nothing is and I am not going to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the third line of that book is a date and that the date is his, and entered that he has not asked about the empty third line of that sheet, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten and watched a man put that sheet down on the first table and take it back again and said nothing at all about either of those two things, and then said one thing about the third line in about four seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got thirty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are two books on two tables in this yard and the difference between them is the only line anybody has been talking about for eleven days, and that is the line that goes out of date.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman said on the twenty-ninth morning of this month that the third line is the only line of the three that is about anybody, and entered that the two of those sentences are about the same line and that she is not joining them, and about four people at that first table have said since that he did not stop him.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are fifty-four of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume, and the sheet was not on that table for about nine seconds this morning and came back to it. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and about nine people within four feet of that end of the yard have said since that a man who opened that book for about nine seconds three days ago has not opened it since, and that one of those people has said out loud that a man who keeps a tally does not open a book to count with and that everybody in that yard knows it and nobody has ever asked him.

About nine people at that first table have said since that a man who has been in that ditch every morning since before the second of the twelfth month put that sheet down on a book a stranger can walk up to and read, and took it off again, and that nobody in that yard asked him why he did that and that about four of them have said out loud that a man who is careful with a stranger's book is not careful with his own, and that nobody has asked him about either.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about nine people have said since that a man who reads four figures off a wall on his way past and a man who is never asked about eleven miles are two different kinds of person and that this district has both of them. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and he did not stop and did not look at that sheet on his way past. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything about the two books. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and fiftieth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and forty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and fifty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and fourteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and nineteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and eighty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and ninety-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and twenty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Sixty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are fifty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month it is living in now, and neither of them is about a person and both of them are on a wall a man reads every morning. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a copy that carries the error is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and the man the third line is about is the only person in that yard who has ever read it. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and a man read a third line out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a copy that carries the error is not one of the six.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about nine people have said since that two books went past her on that path this evening and she read both. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall for about a minute afterwards without moving, and the fourth of those four is four hundred this morning, and nobody in that yard has said anything about that figure being the first round one in six volumes.

A man who has been in that ditch every day since before the second of the twelfth month put a sheet of paper down beside that lot book on the morning of the seventh of the second month and read the first two lines of each of them out loud and they are the same, including the one that is wrong, and said out loud in front of about nineteen people that if whoever wrote it had made it up they would have got it right, because about four people in this district know that figure is wrong and everybody else has never had a reason to look at it, and that sheet is back on the end of that second table for the eleventh morning with nothing in the third line of it.
