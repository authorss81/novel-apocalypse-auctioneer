# Chapter 525: The Question On The Page Changed This Morning

A clerk of nineteen years entered, out loud, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the twenty-first of the second month, that the question being asked in that yard changed on the twentieth morning of this month, and entered who asked it, and entered that nobody answered it, and entered that she is not entering an answer because there is not one, and about nine people have said since that this is the first time a clerk has entered a change in what a yard is asking rather than a change in what it has got.

It was the twenty-first morning of the second month and there was a low grey sky with no rain in it and the boards of that second table were warm by about half past eight, and the ground under that yard had gone from dry to damp in about half an hour after seven, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and sixty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fiftieth of them, and that he has now said them every morning of a week in which that yard asked a different question three times.

"**The board carries four hundred and twenty-three, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and thirty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and fifty-three, and four hundred and fourteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She said it at about eleven with her page in her hand and not on the boards, and about nineteen people heard it and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got ninety-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The question in this yard changed on the twentieth morning of this month. A man asked what that sheet of paper is for. Nobody answered. Then the same man asked who said a thing has to be in writing to be true, and a man who digs loam answered it, and I entered the answer and left one word out of it. I am entering all three of those things and I am entering that the yard is not asking who made that paper any more and I am not entering why, because I do not know why.**"

Nobody said anything at all to that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk has entered a change in what a yard is asking, and entered that this is the first of its kind in six volumes, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about a quarter past eleven, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I asked that question and I do not want it entered as mine, and I have come up here this morning to say that in front of these people so that it is said, and a clerk is going to write down that a man said he does not want it entered as his and she is going to have to do that anyway, and I would like everybody in this yard to know that I knew that when I asked it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she entered it, and entered that a man said in advance that she would have to, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and said one thing after it in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got sixty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About four of you are going to ask me this morning whether I meant it, and I am telling you now that I meant it and that it changes nothing about the third line being my day, and that a man who has not asked me anything since the fifteenth morning of last month is not going to start this morning because I said a sentence.**"

About nine people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man who has been asked nothing in about thirty mornings has been asked about his own sentence and has told them in advance not to, and that about four of them have said that this is the third time in a fortnight that somebody in that yard has told a yard not to ask them something, and that a clerk has entered all three of those and joined none of them. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it with the other two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel was in that yard at about ten and said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read that page on a post at the first channel and I have read a page on a counter in a shop and I have read a book on a table in that yard and I have read a sheet on the other table, and that is four places, and about four of us have read all four, and I am not going to tell anybody which one I think came first.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that there are four places in this district with those three lines on them and that a clerk has entered all four and has entered no figure about which of them is oldest, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are sixty-eight of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and a clerk entered that a man who said three whole things out loud in that yard in about a fortnight said nothing on this morning, and entered that she is not entering that as a finding.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man who has walked those eleven miles twice a day for about six months came up that lane and read a wall a man had just been asked a different question at and did not stop, and that about four of them have said that if anybody in that yard is ever going to ask him anything it is going to have to be somebody who can walk as far as he does. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people near that first table have said since that a man who put a sheet of paper on a table in this yard a month ago at ten past eight has now gone up that lane about a hundred mornings without stopping at either table. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about nine people within four feet of that wall have said since that this is the ninth morning and that nothing has been offered to him on any of the nine and that on the fourth of them a whole yard found out what it had been doing for six volumes about nine feet away. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and sixty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and seventy-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and twenty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and thirty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and forty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Eighty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are sixty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk entered a change in what a yard is asking this morning and did not enter a change in a single figure, and that about four of them think that is the right way round. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a clerk entering that a yard has changed its question is not a thirteenth, and about four people at that first table have said since that the change is on a page now and that is the only one of the four things about those three lines that is in this district's own hand. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people at that first table have said since that a district which cannot date a sheet of paper can record a change in what it is asking inside a morning. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk read a thing out loud in that yard this morning and put nothing in it, and a man asked in front of about nineteen people not to have a thing entered as his and it was entered. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man who said in front of about nineteen people that he does not want a thing entered as his is not one of the six.

At about half past five the buckets went down that bank empty and came back up it full, and on her way past the woman of about fifty-eight read those three lines standing up and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near the top of that bank have said since that a man who was in that yard at about a quarter past eleven has not gone down that bank in about a fortnight, and that nobody has asked him why. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall for about a minute afterwards without moving, and about four people near it have said since that a man who has not been asked anything in about a fortnight was asked something this morning and answered it, and that the two of those are the same wall.

What a yard is asking is on a page for the first time in six volumes, entered this morning out loud by a clerk of nineteen years who entered who asked it and entered that nobody answered it and entered that she is not entering an answer because there is not one, and a man who asked it came up the next morning and said in front of about nineteen people that he did not want it entered as his, and a clerk entered that too, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the twenty-fifth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
