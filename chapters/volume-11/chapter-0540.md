# Chapter 540: She Counted The Marks Off The Board With Her Finger

A clerk of nineteen years counted the marks off that board in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the eighth of the third month, at about ten, in the open, in daylight, with her own finger on the wood, in front of about nineteen people, and said the figure out loud, and a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally opened his flat book in the open afterwards and read a figure out of it, and about four people in that yard have said since that this is the first morning in six volumes on which a figure this district could not get was got, in the open, off a piece of wood, in front of everybody.

It was the eighth morning of the third month and the light came up off that ground clean and came across that board from about nine forty and lay along the row of cuts so that every one of them had a shadow on the far side of it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard, about nine of whom could hear a number that morning and about ten of whom could not.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and seventy-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and sixty-fifth of them, and that he said all four of them this morning before the count and again at about four after it and did not turn round from that wall at any point on the day.

"**The board carries four hundred and thirty-eight, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fifty-four days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and sixty-eight, and four hundred and twenty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was in that yard at about ten with her own shop shut and said one thing out loud before anything else happened, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I opened at about eleven instead of seven and I have lost a morning of trade and I am standing in a yard instead of behind a counter, and I am saying that first so that nobody can say afterwards I did not know what I was doing. I still do not know what I was doing.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was shut, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years then went and stood at the end of that board with about nine people behind her and said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted it and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to count the cuts from the first of the ones that are not like the others to the next one that is not like the others, and say the number once, and then not talk about it until this afternoon. Nobody is to stop me and nobody is to help me and nobody is to stand closer than about four feet.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that about nineteen people stood where they were standing while she counted, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

She put her finger on the second of the deeper cuts in that row at about ten and moved it along the wood one cut at a time, counting them out loud in the ordinary voice, and when her finger came to the next cut that was not like the others she took it off the wood and said it out loud, and the boy counted what she said and got three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is thirty-one.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything at all for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk counted the marks off that board in the open and in daylight with her own finger on the wood in front of about nineteen people, and entered the figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally took the flat book out from under his left arm at about ten past ten in the open and opened it on the boards of that first table, and put his own finger on a line, and read one line out of it out loud in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy counted what he said and got fifty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Thirty. From the first. I wrote that down off a list in the year before last and I have never counted the marks off that board, and I am reading that line out in this yard this morning because I said in this yard four mornings ago that I would.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man read a line out of a book in that yard in the open and entered the line and the figure, and entered that the figure came off a list and the count came off a board, and entered that she is not joining the two of them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said it out loud then, in front of about nineteen people, in about nine seconds, and counted what he said and got fifty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is the month I asked about on the twenty-sixth morning of last month. I asked in that yard and nobody told me and the clerk told me she was not going to be able to tell me either, and it has taken ten mornings and a piece of wood.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a figure has been on a page in this district since about a quarter past ten this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was up that bank at about ten and came down to the edge of that yard and said one thing out loud to about nine people, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I cut those marks and I have never read one of them and I have not helped her with a single one. About four people in this yard have been waiting for me to be the man who counted it and I am not going to be.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the man who cut the marks did not stand at the count, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about fifty-six stood at the end of that row of cuts for about a minute and a half afterwards without saying one word and without looking up, and about nine people within about nine feet of that board have said since that he had asked, out loud, the day before, for it to be entered that he would not look up, and that he did not.

Then he said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read four figures off that wall every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and two of them are out and have been out for about a month, and I have said nothing about that twice and I am not saying anything about it this morning. I want it entered that I did not look up while she did it, and that is the whole of what I have got.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not look up, and entered that she is not entering why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said out loud two mornings ago that a number would not tell a stranger which of those four pages was the one this district made, and this morning a clerk counted a month off a piece of wood in front of all of you, and nobody has shown me I was right and nobody has shown me I was wrong, and I am not going to stand here this afternoon and pretend that one of those has happened.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the four pages, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and read all four of his at the same speed for the first time in about a fortnight. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and did not look at the board. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a figure this district could not get was got in that yard at about ten this morning and that nobody thought to tell the man on those stones. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

At about half past three the clerk of nineteen years stood at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand and said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure somebody entered because they had to enter one is not a promise about a later day. I have said that sentence in this yard more than once and this afternoon it is the reason and not the answer, and the difference between the figure I counted off that board this morning and the figure that man read out of his own book this morning is a day. I am entering it as a difference and not as an error, and a figure off a list is a figure off a list, and it stays on the page it is on.**"

A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and one figure was counted off a piece of wood in that yard this morning in daylight in front of about nineteen people, and a clerk has entered the difference between that figure and a figure off a list as a day. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that two of them are out and have been out for about a month, and that this yard is now holding two kinds of wrong and has never been told which is worse.
