# Chapter 516: A Third Sheet On Her Own Counter

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale wrote three lines out on a sheet of her own at the end of that second table in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about a quarter past ten on the morning of the twelfth of the second month, in about nine minutes, with a pencil stub and a square of the paper her sacks come in, and carried it down that bank in her left hand and put it flat on her own counter where her customers stand, and said out loud in front of about nine people in that shop that she cannot tell which of the two books on two tables in that yard is the one this district made, and about nine people in that shop could not tell either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

It was the twelfth morning of the second month and the light came up off the water in the low ground behind that yard about ten minutes later than it had been doing and there was a wind out of the north-east for about an hour after nine, and the boards of that second table were cold to the hand until about half past nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and fifty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and forty-first of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and fourteen, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and thirty days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and forty-four, and four hundred and five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She used the low step at the end of that first table to rest the paper on and she asked nobody for the pencil and she was given none, and about nineteen people watched a woman who keeps a scale write a book out in the open in about nine minutes without once looking at either of the two things she could have copied it from.

Nobody stopped her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person wrote three lines out on a sheet of her own in the open and entered that she is not entering why and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The first line went down in about a minute and the second line in about four, and the boy of about nineteen, who was standing about nine feet off with his own sheet in his hand, said out loud to about four people that she was getting the second one right, and a clerk entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing.

She wrote the third line in about a second. The boy did not see the whole of that one and about four people at the end of that second table have said since that they did not either, and a clerk entered that a person wrote a third line and entered no figure about what was in it and entered that she did not ask.

Then she put the pencil stub in her apron, folded the sheet once, said nothing to anybody, and went down that bank on foot, and the whole of that walk was about two hundred yards and took her about four minutes, and about nine people at the near end of that first table watched a thing leave that yard on it for the first time in a month.

At her own counter, with about nine people in the shop, she put the sheet down flat and squared it with the side of her hand and said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard every word of it and the boy of about nineteen, who had come down that bank about a minute behind her, counted what she said and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read that book standing up every working day since the day it came out to that table and I have read the sheet that is lying on the other one, and I have written those three lines out on my own paper this morning, and I still cannot tell you which of the two of them this district made. Two people in this shop have been in that yard and I would like one of them to come here and tell my customers which one is ours.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in a shop and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the two of them as a pair, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

One of the two was the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up, who had come down that lane at about eleven for about four minutes on a morning he had no business in that shop, and he looked at both of the two sheets standing up for about nine seconds each and then said one thing out loud in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put that second table up in this yard in about eleven minutes and nobody has asked me one word about it since, and I have stood in front of both of those books this morning along with the rest of you, and I am not going to be the one who says which of them is ours. The day I say that I am a man who made a thing, and I have not made anything in six years.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in a shop and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts things up said he is not going to say a thing, and entered no reason for it beyond the one he gave in a mouth.

The other of the two was the woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel, who was in that shop with a basket on her hip and had been up that lane four times this month, and she said one thing out loud before she went, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got sixty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I did not know until about a quarter past eleven this morning that anybody had taken those lines off that table and written them out again, and I am not going to say whether that is stealing, and I am not going to say it in a shop either, and that is the only reason I have said anything at all.**"

Nobody answered her either, and she went up the bank with the basket on her hip, and a clerk entered that two of about nine people in a shop had been in that yard and entered that she is not entering a comparison between the two of them.

Then the boy of about nineteen asked the woman of about thirty-six a question, out loud, in front of about nine people, and she answered without being asked twice, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Which one of the two did you write it from, and I am not asking that as a trap, I am asking it because I have stood in front of both of them every morning this month and I cannot tell either, and I want to know how a person who has read them every day since the day they came out knows.**"

She said it in about four seconds and the boy counted it and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I do not know either. I wrote it off the one I have been reading and I have been reading the same one every day since the second of the twelfth month, and if that is the wrong one then I have been standing in the wrong yard for four hundred working days and nobody in this district has ever come and told me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in a shop and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as an admission and entered the reason, which is that a woman saying she might have been wrong is not a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and then he read the three lines on that sheet standing up in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard all of it, and he said nothing at the end of the third one and nothing after it, and went and sat down on the low step for about a minute. A clerk entered that a man read three lines out loud in that yard and entered that he said nothing after the third of them for the first time this month, and about four people at that first table have said since that the man who reads that sheet out loud every morning stopped talking this morning and that nobody in that yard has worked out why and that nobody is going to.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten with his left hand in its cloth and watched the whole of it and said nothing at all, and about four people near him have said since that he did not go down that bank after her, and that about four other people have said that he went down it about ten minutes after the boy did and stood at the end of her counter for about nine seconds and came back up without saying one word to anybody.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are fifty-nine of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and about four people near him have said since that he moved his head about four inches to the left at the moment she got up off that step and did not move it again.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and a clerk entered that he was not asked anything else on that lane either. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that he looked at the square of paper on the low step for about four seconds on his way in and did not look at it on his way out. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about nine people within four feet of that wall have said since that a man who has been given nothing every morning for a fortnight has now been given nothing on a morning when a whole yard was saying things out loud. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and fifty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and sixty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and nineteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and twenty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and ninety-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and thirty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Seventy-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are fifty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and a boy asked in a yard on the twenty-eighth morning of last month for one and the whole of what he got was nobody knows. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a square of paper that a person wrote out with a pencil stub in about nine minutes is not a thirteenth and is not the count moving. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and that book has no fourth line in it. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about four people at that first table have said since that about nine people in a shop heard three things said out loud this morning and not one of them went into a column. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a sheet written out in a shop is not one of the six.

The two buckets went down that bank at about half past five and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and she went up the bank about four minutes after the woman of about thirty-six had come down it. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and did not go and stand at the end of that second table.

Three lines went onto a counter in a shop this morning in a hand that is not the hand on either of the two books in that yard, and about nine people in that shop looked at all three of them and could not say which of the three was the one this district made, and a clerk entered that a page in this district now has a date on it that nobody put there and entered that the person that date is about was in a ditch until about ten and came up and read three lines off a different table and said nothing, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the sixteenth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
