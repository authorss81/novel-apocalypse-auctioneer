# Chapter 544: She Wrote Three Sentences And Refused All Three

The clerk of nineteen years was at the near end of that first table by about half past seven on the twelfth of the third month, before about nine people had come up that bank, and she wrote on her own page three times in an hour and turned the page over three times, and about four people in that yard have said since that they watched her do it and that she never once turned it back, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those had been in that yard on the second morning of this month for the same reason.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and eighty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that a clerk has entered that figure on four mornings running and is going to go on entering it after this month is out, and that nobody has asked her to.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and sixty-ninth of those mornings and that a clerk stood in front of that table at half past seven this morning with her pen in her hand and did not say anything to anybody until about eight.

"**The board carries four hundred and forty-two, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fifty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seventy-two, and four hundred and thirty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about nine, in front of about nineteen people, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to try to say on a page the thing this yard has been circling for ten mornings, and I am going to say out loud why each one of the three is not it, and I am turning the page over after each one so that nobody in this yard can read a thing I am not putting there.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk is going to write three sentences in that yard this morning and is going to refuse all three out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

She wrote the first one and read it out loud in the ordinary voice, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The third line above is a person.**"

Then she said one thing out loud about it, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got fifty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is not it. A stranger standing in front of that book reads that and thinks the page is about a kind of person and not about this district, and it is worse than nothing, because a book that says a person and does not say who has told the stranger that there is somebody to ask.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk wrote a sentence this morning and refused it out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

She turned that page over, wrote the second one, and read it out loud, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got ten and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The third line above is the man who digs loam.**"

Then she said one thing out loud about it, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is not it either. There is nowhere in this district for a name to go, a man who keeps a tally has said that out loud twice, and a man who has not been asked one question in six volumes is not going to be found by a page that has been in four places in this district for a month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk wrote a sentence this morning naming a man and refused it out loud in front of about nineteen people, and entered that she has entered that she wrote it and not what she wrote, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

She turned that page over, wrote the third one, and read it out loud, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody.**"

Then she said one thing out loud about it, and about four people at that first table have said since that she said it faster than the other two and that her hands were not steady, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is the worst one and I wrote it last, and if that book said nobody then a man who has been standing in a ditch in every morning of this volume would be a nobody in a book a stranger can walk up to, and a page that says nobody about a man is the last thing this district is going to put in a book.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk wrote three sentences in that yard this morning and refused all three out loud in front of about nineteen people, and entered that a fourth line is not a column and that a column is something a person looks down and a line is something a person reads, and entered that no column is going to be ruled for it and that she is not going to rule one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she put her pen down flat on the boards of that first table, the way a person puts down a thing they have finished with, and about nine people in that yard have said since that she has not picked it up again.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**None of those three is it and I know what the fourth one has to be, and it is not a sentence about a line. It is a sentence a person says about himself, and if it is not that, then whoever writes it is writing a rule and this district has enough of those to last it a year.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man at the end of that second table has said out loud in a yard that what is wanted is an admission and not a rule, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud then, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**She has written three sentences this morning and not one of them is on a page, and I have counted all three of them, and about four people in this yard have said that this district can make a thing it cannot hold up in the light.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that three sentences a clerk wrote this morning are not on any page of this district, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk stood at that table from half past seven this morning and that he came up that lane at about the time she put her pen down. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and looked at the pen lying on the boards of that first table as he went past and did not say anything about it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk has written three sentences in that yard this morning and not one of them is on a page, and that the man on those stones has not been told any of that. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-third night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and sixty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it. A hundred and ninety-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and forty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and fifty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and twenty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and twenty-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named.

A hundred and one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are eighty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and about four people at that first table have said since that a clerk has now written three sentences about a fourth line in a morning and has entered the refusals and not the sentences, and that the refusals are longer than anything this district has entered about anything. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a sentence that has been refused is not a thirteenth, and three of them are not three.

The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people in that yard have said since that a clerk has said out loud this morning that the fourth thing is a person and that a page cannot say a person and not say who, and that about four of them have said that is the whole of the trouble with the fourth thing. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that she read three sentences out loud in that yard this morning and that the column for the name of whoever reads a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and about seven people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and a clerk turning a page over so that nobody can read what she has not put there is not one of the six.

A clerk's pen lay flat on the boards of that first table from half past eleven until about half past three, and about four people near that first table have said since that the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full, and that by the time they were two-thirds of the way up the woman of about fifty-eight was already at the top of it, reading the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her, and that she was not told about the three sentences and did not ask, and that she has been past that book four hundred and some working days and has not once said what it is for. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall again at about four, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is a yard where a thing being put down is now a thing somebody can see.

A clerk of nineteen years wrote three sentences in that yard this morning about the third line of that lot book and refused all three of them out loud in front of about nineteen people and entered the refusals and not the sentences, and said out loud that a fourth line is not a column and that a column is something a person looks down and a line is something a person reads, and put her pen down flat on the boards at about half past eleven and did not pick it up again that day, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the forty-fourth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
