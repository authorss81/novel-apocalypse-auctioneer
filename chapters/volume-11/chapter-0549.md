# Chapter 549: Nobody Could Say Whether He Agreed

There was mud in that lane from the night and about nine people came up it on the seventeenth of the third month and printed the whole way, and about four of them had been up that bank on the two mornings before this one and about five had not, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about four of those were standing at the far end of that first table looking at a book with four lines on it.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and eighty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that a clerk has entered that figure on every morning of this month and is not going to stop on the first of the next one, and that nobody has thanked her for it on any of them.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and seventy-fourth of those mornings and that about four of them have been at that first table since about nine looking at a fourth line and that the man at the wall has not looked at it once.

"**The board carries four hundred and forty-seven, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and sixty-three days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seventy-seven, and four hundred and thirty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up put the stone back on the corner of that lot book at about half past eight, with both hands, without being asked, and said one thing out loud while he was doing it, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That book had its stone off it all day yesterday and it is a book anybody can walk up to and read, and it has had four lines on it since about ten yesterday morning. I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to be asked what it is for.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a stone is on the corner of that book again, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing out loud at about ten, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Eight days ago I shut my shop for a morning and I told everybody before I did it and I would do it again. What I want said this morning is that a yard of about nineteen people got a fourth line out of a morning of my trade and about four of them have been saying so in that yard as if it was a bargain, and there was nobody on my side of it, and I have not been thanked and I am not asking to be.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman shut her own shop for a morning eight days ago and has not been thanked for it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his hands behind his back and said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I read that sheet out loud yesterday afternoon in the middle of that yard and I said its third line is not there, and about four people in this yard have been saying since that that is the end of it. It is not the end of it. I have now read it out loud once, and it is still a sheet of paper with three lines on it, and I still cannot tell you which of those two is the one this district made, and neither can anybody standing here.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a sheet in that yard has been read out loud once and has not thereby become the book, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then a person in that yard asked a question out loud in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people, and about nine of them heard every word of it, and it was not asked of anybody in particular, and the boy counted what was said and got forty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**So then who is the person. The book says there is one and it will not say who, and I have been in this yard every morning of this month and there is not one name on any page in this district, and where is it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the question was not asked of anybody in particular, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four people at that first table then looked at the end of that second table, and nobody said anything to anybody, and about nine people in that yard have said since that a question was asked in that yard this morning and that the answer to it is a book on that table with nine words on it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have never been on a page in six volumes and neither has anybody else, and that is not the same as not having one, and nobody in this yard asked me a question this morning and I am not going to wait about for one. I mend a fence and I do it in the ordinary way and I have about nine lengths to do before the water turns.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody asked him a question, and entered that a clerk is not entering what a man said as a name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years then said one thing out loud at the near end of that first table, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody asked him anything this morning and I am not going to be the one who starts it now that four people have looked at him. There is a question in that yard and it has not been put to anybody and I am going to leave it where it is, and I am entering that I have said that out loud.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that about four people at that first table have said since that they do not know whether the man who digs loam agreed to a fourth line, and entered that a clerk was not asked that question and did not ask it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane through the mud, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a book in this yard has said out loud yesterday that its own third line is a person, and that the man who comes up that lane twice a day has read four off that wall on every morning of this volume and has never once read that book. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and did not go up that bank. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that this is the one hundred and eighty-eighth morning of that run and that about four of them have noticed that nobody in that yard has thought to tell the man on those stones anything at all this month. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and ninety-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and fifty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and a clerk has said out loud in a yard that the third line of that book is a person and has entered no name. A hundred and fifty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and twenty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and thirty days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and sixty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are ninety-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and about four people at that first table have said since that a book in that yard has now said out loud that its own third line is a person and that two figures on a wall nine feet away are still out and neither of them has been told why, and that about four of them have said that is two kinds of wrong in one yard. The count of things this district has made is thirteen, and the thirteen is smaller than the twelve, and a clerk entered that at about ten yesterday morning and not before.

The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and the fifth of them is a remedy, and a fourth line is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and a clerk entered that on the morning after it went on. A clerk entered that on the morning after the fourth line went on and entered the reason. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and a sheet of that copy was read out loud yesterday at about half past four and was not read again, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that she is not entering the name of the man who read it and the reason, which is that a reading is done by whoever is standing there and standing in a yard is not a post. About six people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it, and one of the six was a question that was not asked of anybody. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and an admission is not one of the six, and neither is a man not being thanked.

A question was asked in that yard this morning and nobody answered it, and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full, and that the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and that a book in that yard has had a fourth line on it for a day and a night, and that not mentioning that fourth line is the only thing she has ever done with that book and that it is not nothing. That man said the four off that wall again at about four and got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that he is the only person in that yard nobody has looked at twice this month.

A question was asked in that yard on the morning of the seventeenth of the third month in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people, and it was not asked of anybody in particular, and about four people at that first table looked at the end of that second table and nobody said anything to anybody, and a man who mends fencing said one thing out loud and it was not his name, and a clerk entered that nobody asked him anything and that she is not entering what he said as a name, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the forty-ninth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and a book on that first table has four lines on it and the fourth of them is nine words and this district has still not worked out what it is going to do about the other one.
