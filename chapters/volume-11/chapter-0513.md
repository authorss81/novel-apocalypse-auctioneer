# Chapter 513: What He Said About It

The man of fifty-six said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the ninth of the second month, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, about nine feet off the near end of that first table, and the thing he said was about being told twice that one of his four figures is out and about not having been asked one question in two hundred and thirty-eight mornings, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it and entered that she is not entering it as a grievance.

It was the ninth morning of the second month and the light came up hard and clean off the south end of that ground before seven and did not change all day, and the boards of that second table were warm by about nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and fifty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

He said the four off that wall twice as he does, in the ordinary voice, and got all four of them both times, and a clerk entered them once, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and thirty-eighth of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and eleven, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and twenty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and forty-one, and four hundred and two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He said it at about a quarter past ten and about nine people had already been in that yard for about an hour and about nine people have said since that he waited until they were there and did not say it to one person, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got those four off that wall every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and I have got all four of them every morning. On the thirtieth morning of this month a boy told me in this yard that two of them are out. That is the second morning anybody has told me anything about those four. I have not been asked one question about them in two hundred and thirty-eight mornings and I am not asking anybody for one.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he has been told twice and has asked nothing and that the two of those facts are in the same entry and are not joined, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the clerk of nineteen years asked the boy of about nineteen, out loud, in front of about nine people, whether he is going to say that thing about the second of January again, and he said one thing in about four seconds and the boy counted it and got thirty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. I said it out loud once and that is the time. I am not going to be the man who says it every morning until somebody puts it right.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy has said he is not going to say a thing twice, and entered that she is not entering that as a promise, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing anything at all this morning and he was at the end of that second table from about ten with his left hand in its cloth and said nothing, and about four people at that first table have said since that a man who was asked a question yesterday and refused to be asked a second time has now not been asked once this morning, and that about four of them have said that they do not know whether that is on purpose and that they are not going to ask.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice and said one thing after it in about four seconds and the boy counted it and got eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**This district has nobody to give it to.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a finding about anybody, and entered that the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is a column and a question is not a column and there is no column in this district for questions anybody has not been asked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came up that lane at about half past twelve with a basket on her hip, read the three lines on that sheet standing up, and said one thing out loud to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got sixteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody has asked me anything in about nine years and I did not think of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a third person in that yard has said out loud this month that nobody asks them anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are fifty-six of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and he did not open it on this morning either.

The boy of about nineteen read the number back to himself in a low voice and then wrote it in the left half of the sheet of his own, which is the fourth number this month that has gone into that half and none into the right, and a clerk of nineteen years looked at the sheet for about four seconds and entered that a boy wrote a figure down and entered that she is not asking him what the other half of the sheet is for.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he did not stop for anybody. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and he did not stop, and nobody in that yard asked him anything. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and a clerk entered that nothing was asked and nothing was given and that four nights running is not a figure. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and fifty-second night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and sixty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and sixteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and twenty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and ninety-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and thirty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Seventy marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are fifty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month it is living in now, and a man has been told about one of the two and has said nothing and has not been asked a second time. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man saying a thing out loud once is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and nobody in that yard has read it out loud this month. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and a man said a thing out loud about not being asked anything and nothing went into it, and a man said a thing out loud in that yard this morning that was about not being asked a question and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man saying out loud that nobody asks him anything is not one of the six.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and she went up that bank and nobody in that yard watched her go. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people at that first table have said since that a man who has said four figures out loud every morning for two hundred and thirty-eight mornings said one other thing out loud this morning and that the other thing was about the four figures and nobody in that yard asked him a question about any of it.

The man who reads those four figures off that wall said one thing out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people on the morning of the ninth of the second month, and it was that he has got all four of them every morning since before the middle of the sixth month, and that the thirtieth morning of this month a boy told him in that yard that two of them are out, and that this was the second morning anybody has told him anything about those four, and that he has not been asked one question about them in two hundred and thirty-eight mornings and is not asking anybody for one, and a clerk entered all of it and entered that the two facts are in the same entry and are not joined, and that sheet has been on the end of that second table for the thirteenth morning with nothing in the third line of it.
