# Chapter 547: Nine Words Said Out Loud And Not Written Down

The stone on the corner of that lot book had been cold enough at about nine on the fifteenth of the third month that the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up could not keep his hand on it, and he said so out loud to about nine people, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about four of those had come up that bank on the three mornings before this one with a question they had been told the answer to the day before.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and eighty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that a stone has been on the corner of that book since the second of the twelfth month and that nobody has ever put a thumb in the other one, and that this is the first fact about a stone anybody in this district has entered.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and seventy-second of those mornings and that about four of them have been up that bank since about eight watching a man clear a place on that first table with a knife.

"**The board carries four hundred and forty-five, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and sixty-one days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seventy-five, and four hundred and thirty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up worked along the front edge of that first table from about eight with a short knife of his own, and took one splinter out of a board about a foot from the corner where that book lies, and put the shaving in his pocket, and said one thing out loud about it to about nine people, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That book has had a stone on the corner of it since before the frost came and I am clearing a place beside it for a hand, and I am doing it in my own time and I am not going to be thanked and I am not going to be asked what it is for.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a sheet lying on a table in that yard has still not been read out loud by anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got forty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Put this on it. The third line is a person, and this district will not say who. That is what I have got and I have had it since the ninth morning and I have not offered it to anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that about four people in that yard said out loud, in about four seconds each, that it was not it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud from the east end of that yard, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got thirty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No, because a line that will not say who is a promise about the future and this district does not make those and I have been the man who says so here for about thirty years.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It has to be a thing a person would say about himself, out loud, standing next to it, with his hands doing nothing. If it is about the line then somebody is writing a rule about a line and we will be reading it in about four years and asking who put it there, and I am not going to be on either side of that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man at the end of that second table has said out loud in that yard that a fourth line has to be an admission and not a rule, and entered that the reason a column is not going to be ruled for it is on a page and she entered it two mornings ago, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years stood at the near end of that first table at about twenty past twelve with her page shut in her apron and said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The line above is a person, not named here.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything at all for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall, and about nine people within about nine feet of that first table have said since that nine words were said out loud in that yard this morning and about four of them have said that they are going to be on a page tomorrow and that the page is not written yet.

Then the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not writing it this afternoon. I am writing it tomorrow morning in daylight with that book uncovered and about nineteen people standing at that table, and it is going in my own hand and it is not going into a page off anybody's mouth, and if it rains I am going to write it in the rain.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nine words were said out loud in that yard this morning and that a clerk is not entering them this afternoon, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that first table at about half past one and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put that sheet on that table on the first morning of this volume and I have not read it once, and I have thought about that this morning for about an hour, and I am not going to say what I thought, and I would like it entered that I have not read it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has said out loud in that yard that he has not read that sheet, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about one, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nine words, and I counted them twice, and about four people in this yard have said that a thing of nine words is going on a page tomorrow and that nobody in this district has ever put anything that short on a page that a stranger can read.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nine words were said out loud in that yard and are on no page of this district at this hour, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk said nine words in that yard at about twenty past twelve and that he came up that lane about ten minutes later and was not told about any of them. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had been at that second table at about half past one that afternoon saying he had not read it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk has put a day out loud in that yard again and that the man on those stones was not told what is on it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and fifty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and fifty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and twenty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and twenty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and sixty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are ninety marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and about four people at that first table have said since that a clerk has said nine words out loud in that yard and has entered that she is not writing them until tomorrow, and that about four of them have said that this is the first morning in six volumes that a thing has been said out loud in that yard and then put off. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a sentence that is not on a page yet is not a thirteenth, and one that names a person and does not name him is not one either.

The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and a clerk has said out loud in that yard this morning nine words about that line, and about nine people in that yard have said since that the fourth thing a document that sets a lot out has to say is a person, and that a document which says a person on a fourth line is not doing the fourth thing twice, it is doing it in front of a stranger. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and about eight people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it, and a clerk has entered that nine of those eight had one sentence each. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and a clerk putting a morning off is not one of the six.

A stone on the corner of that book was cold enough at about nine this morning that a man could not keep his hand on it, and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that a book in that yard is about to have something said about it on a page and that the buckets went down that bank at about half past five with nothing in them and came back up it full, and that the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her, and that she is the one person in that yard who has read it four hundred working days standing up and she was not told. The man of fifty-six read the four off that wall again at about four and got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a clerk said nine words in that yard at about twenty past twelve and that a man at the foot of that wall read four off it at about four, and that about four of them have said the two numbers are not the same size of thing.

A clerk of nineteen years said nine words out loud in that yard at about twenty past twelve on the fifteenth of the third month and told about nineteen people in the ordinary voice that she was not writing them this afternoon and was writing them in the morning in daylight with that book uncovered and in her own hand and not out of anybody's mouth, and about four people refused a different set of words said out loud in that yard before hers, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the forty-seventh morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and a book on the first table has three lines on it this evening and had three lines on it when that yard was empty.
