# Chapter 515: One Blank And One Date, In The Open

Nobody took either sheet off either table in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the eleventh of the second month, and nobody in that yard said out loud that they were not going to, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that both of them lay where they lay on the day they arrived and entered that she did not ask anybody to leave them and entered the reason, which is that nobody has asked her anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

It was the eleventh morning of the second month and there was a clean hard light on that ground from before seven and the boards of that second table had been warm since about half past eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and fifty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fortieth of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and thirteen, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and twenty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and forty-three, and four hundred and four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

Then the boy of about nineteen read the two third lines out loud, standing between that first table and that second table at about eleven, one after the other, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and about nine people have said since that it is the first time that anybody in that yard has read the two third lines against each other out loud, on purpose, with both of them in his mouth at once.

"**The figure on the second line above stopped being true on the fifteenth of the tenth month.**"

Then he said what the other one was, and the boy counted what he said and got thirty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**And that one is nothing, and I have read it out loud, and it is still nothing, and it is going to be nothing this afternoon and it was nothing yesterday morning.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that an empty line was read out loud in that yard in front of about nineteen people and that reading it out loud did not make it a figure, and entered that she is not going to enter that it did not make it a figure either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart said one word out loud, in the ordinary voice, at about a quarter past eleven, to about nine people, and the boy counted what he said and got one, and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Forgery.**"

About nine people within four feet of that first table have said since that a man who has been asked two questions about that piece of paper in fifteen days and has given the same answer to both of them said one word out loud this morning that nobody else in that yard has said, and that the word has not been on any page in this district in six volumes.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a word out loud in that yard and entered that she is not entering the word, and entered the reason, which is that there is no figure on any page in this district for the word and that a clerk who has to write a word down can only write down words she can put a figure to, and about four people near that table have said since that a clerk has entered eleven things in this yard this month that are not figures and has not entered a word, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen then put both of those third lines in the left half of the sheet of his own and put nothing in the right half of it, and about four people at that first table have said since that a boy who has been ruling that sheet for a month has never once written in the right half of it and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him what the right half of it is for.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table and said one thing, in about four seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got forty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is the first morning anybody in this yard has said out loud what that piece of paper is, and it has been on that table fifteen days, and the fifteen days were not nothing. The fifteen days were two people standing still and reading two lines.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a sheet of paper with two lines on it has been on that second table for the fifteenth morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about fifty-six was at that wall and said nothing at all this morning, and about nine people near that wall have said since that he has now said two things out loud in that yard in a fortnight and that both of them were about not having been asked something, and that nobody in that yard has worked out whether he is going to say a third one.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice and said nothing at the end of the third one and went and sat down on the low step. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are fifty-eight of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and about four people near that end of that yard have said since that a man who says nothing every morning said something once this month and has not done it since.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale read those three lines in that lot book standing up at about a quarter past twelve and did not walk over to that second table, and about nine people at that first table have said since that she has walked over to it on six mornings out of the last fifteen and that this is the ninth morning she has not, and that nobody in that yard has asked her why on any of the nine.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about nine people at that wall have said since that he has said four figures to himself in that yard and nobody has ever asked him to. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and he did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day and did not look at the paper on the second table. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about nine people within four feet of that wall have said since that a man who was asked a question two days ago and a man who was not asked this morning are in the same place at the same hour, and a clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and fifty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-three of them, and that this is the first morning since the tenth morning of this month on which nothing was asked of him at all.

---

A hundred and sixty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and eighteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and twenty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and ninety-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and thirty-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Seventy-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are fifty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month it is living in now, and about nine people at that first table have said since that one of the two has been out since before the last volume began, and a boy asked for one in a yard on the twenty-eighth morning of this month and the whole of what he got was nobody knows. The count of things this district has made is twelve. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people at that first table have said since that the reading has stood at that fourth one for a month and has not moved. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about nine people at that first table have said since that this column has now been named on fifteen mornings of this month with nothing in it on fifteen of them. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man reading an empty line out loud is not one of the six.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that she has read that book about four hundred times and has never once been asked to say what is in it. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about four people at that first table have said since that this is the fifteenth morning that two books have been lying on two tables in that yard and that nobody has taken either of them off and that about nine of those fifteen mornings nobody in that yard said one word about either of them.

A piece of paper with two lines on it and nothing in the third of it has been on the end of the second table in that yard for the fifteenth morning, and a boy read its empty third line out loud in front of about nineteen people this morning and it was still nothing when he had finished, and a clerk said out loud on the tenth morning of the second month that whoever made it stopped at the line that goes out of date, and a man who was in the ditch when that figure went out of date read that empty line out loud on the fifteenth morning and said nothing at the end of it, and nobody in that yard has written the word forgery on any page in this district, and nobody in that yard has said out loud that there is not one.
