# Chapter 503: Two Of The Four Are Out

A boy of about nineteen walked from the near end of that first table to that wall at about a quarter past eight on the morning of the thirtieth of this month, read the fourth of the four figures off it, read it again, and said out loud in front of about nine people that it counts from the second of January and that this district went into a new year on the first morning after the count and this figure did not start again, and about nineteen people heard every word of it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of that and entered the difference as a day and entered that a figure may not be corrected.

It was the thirtieth morning of the first month and the light came up off the water in the low ground behind that yard about ten minutes later than it had been doing and there was a wind out of the east for about an hour after nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and forty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and twenty-eighth of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and one, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and seventeen days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and thirty-one, and three hundred and ninety-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He said it at a quarter past eight and he was not asked to and he had the fourth figure in his own hand on a sheet of his own, and about nine people heard the whole of it and the boy counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That one counts from the second of January and we went into a new year on the first morning after the count and it did not start again. It should be a small number this morning and it is three hundred and ninety-two, and that is not me being wrong about it, that is the wall being right and the wall being right about a day that has already gone.**"

Nobody answered him at that point. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the fourth of the four figures on that wall counts from the second of January and did not count again on the first of this year, and entered that there is a difference between the two of them, and entered that a figure may not be corrected and may not be struck and may not be replaced, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said the rest of it out loud, standing at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand and not on the boards, and about nineteen people heard it, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and thirty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering that two of the four figures on that wall are out and that they are out in two different ways and that I am entering both. The third of the four has been out by a day since the second of the twelfth month and nobody has put it right because a figure may not be put right. The fourth of the four has not been out by a day. The fourth of the four has not started again, and the difference between the two is a figure this district does not have, because the length of this month has never been counted by anybody, and I am entering that there is a difference and I am not entering what it is, and it is mine and not his.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk has entered a figure that is about a figure on a wall and not about a person, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she walked the four feet to that wall and told the man of fifty-six, in the ordinary voice, with about nine people standing close enough to hear both of them, and he listened to the whole of it and looked at the fourth of the four for about four seconds and said nothing at all, and a clerk entered that she told a man a figure of his own was out and entered that he said nothing and entered that she is not going to ask him again and entered the reason.

About nine people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man who has said those four off that wall every morning for two hundred and twenty-eight mornings was told one morning that one of them was three hundred and ninety-two days out and did not say one word, and that he was told a thing like it in the last month of the last volume and did not say one word then either, and that he has now been told twice and nobody in that yard has asked him a question either of the two times.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and then he read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard all of it, and he said one more thing out loud after it in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got thirty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Four lines on that wall and nobody in this yard has ever written one of them down, and I have been in that ditch for every one of the days they have been out there.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a finding about the man who is in the ditch and entered the reason, which is that the third line of that book is a figure of him already and one figure of a man is enough for this district in one month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard at any point on that day for the sixth morning running, and a clerk entered that he was not in it and entered that she did not ask and entered the reason.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten, four feet off that sheet, and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are forty-six of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and nobody said a word to him all morning.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that nobody in that yard has ever asked him about anything at all. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and he did not stop and did not look at either table. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and nobody in that yard went over to him at any point in the afternoon. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and forty-second night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and forty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and fifty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and eleven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and eighty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and eighty-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and twenty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Sixty marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are forty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and both of them are entered as out, and one of them has been out by a day since the second of the twelfth month and the other of them did not start again on the first morning of last month, and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall is the figure. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and two figures that are out are not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and a clerk entered that the third line of that book is a figure of a man and that it is the only line in it that goes out of date on its own. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about nine people at that first table have said since that nobody in that yard has ever been asked whether they would hold it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and nobody in that yard proposed a sixth this morning.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and nobody at that table looked up at her and she did not look up at anybody. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall for about a minute afterwards without moving.

Two of the four figures a man of fifty-six says out loud in that yard every morning have been found out in the same morning in front of about nineteen people, and one of them has been out by a day since before the last volume began and the other of them has not been out by a day at all and did not start again when this district went into a new year, and a clerk entered both and entered that a figure may not be corrected, and a man was told and said nothing, and about nine people have said since that he has now been told twice and that nobody in that yard has asked him a question either of the two times.
