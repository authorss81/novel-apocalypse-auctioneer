# Chapter 518: Nobody Asked Her To Take It Off

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale, out loud, at the near end of that first table at about eleven on the morning of the fourteenth of the second month, in front of about nineteen people, whether she would take that square of paper off her own counter, and she said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and nobody asked her a second question, and it is the first thing that man has said out loud in that yard this month.

It was the fourteenth morning of the second month and there was a thin frost on the low step at the end of that first table that had gone off the boards before about half past seven, and the light came up off the south end of that ground flat and grey and without any wind in it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and fifty-seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and forty-third of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and sixteen, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and thirty-two days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and forty-six, and four hundred and seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He asked it standing at the low step with his left hand in its cloth and she was about eleven feet off him at that first table with nothing in her hands, and about nineteen people heard the whole of it and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You put a date on your own counter yesterday morning and that date is about a man who was in a ditch at the time, and this district has spent six volumes saying that a figure goes on a page with a person and a reason in the room, and there was nobody in your shop but about nine customers. I am asking you whether you will take it off.**"

She said no in about four seconds and the boy counted what she said and got thirty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. And I am going to tell you why in four seconds and then I am not going to say anything else about it, and if you ask me a second time I am going to keep saying no.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man asked a question out loud in that yard and entered that a woman refused it with a reason and entered that nobody asked her a second question, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then he said the rest of it, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I was not asked to say that and I am not going to take it back either, because the rule about a person and a reason in the room is a rule about this yard and you are not in this yard, and I have watched you write those three lines out in about nine minutes in front of about nineteen people and there is a person and there is a reason and neither of them is me.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a rule and entered the reason, which is that a clerk who entered a rule in a yard would be entering a post, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was asked nothing else for about an hour and a half, and about four people near her have said since that a woman who said no in four seconds and then gave a reason in nine seconds has not been given the third thing, which is somebody repeating the question, and that about four other people have said that a man who asked a question he was not asked to ask has now been in that yard for two mornings and has said one thing.

The boy of about nineteen then wrote something in the right half of the sheet of his own, at the near end of that first table, for about nine seconds, and it was the first thing that has gone into that half of it for about a fortnight, and a clerk of nineteen years looked at it for about four seconds and entered that a boy wrote in the right half of that sheet and entered that she did not ask what it was and entered that she is not going to.

About nine people near that first table have said since that what the boy wrote was four words and that two of them were a month and a day and that the other two were not anything at all, and that about four of them have said that he copied a date out of a shop into his own book this morning and that nobody has asked him why and that he is not going to be asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and said one thing after it in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man asked a woman in that yard this morning whether she would take a page off her own counter, and about four of you have already worked out that there is nothing in that yard that can make her do it, and the only thing that can is somebody being asked in a shop, and nobody has done that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering what about four people at that table have worked out, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are sixty-one of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and a clerk entered that a man who has kept a tally in this district for about thirty years was about eleven feet off a question this morning and did not open his book, and entered that she is not entering that as a finding.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that he came up that lane at about half past one and went down it at about half past two and did not stop anywhere, which is the first time in this month he has gone back down it in the same hour. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man who puts a sheet down does not stop for a man who asks a woman to take one off. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who asked a question in that yard this morning and got no for an answer from a woman has not asked that man anything and has not gone near him. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and fifty-seventh night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and fifty-six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and sixty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and twenty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man and that date is on a counter in a shop in a hand that is not his and the man has not been asked about it twice. A hundred and twenty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and ninety-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and thirty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Seventy-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are sixty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man was asked this month in a yard whether he would rule a column and said he would not. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man asking a woman a question in a yard is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people at that first table have said since that the fourth of the five is the only one of the five that this district has anybody in. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and a man said a whole sentence out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a rule about a person and a reason in the room is not one of the six.

Down that bank at about half past five the two buckets went empty and came back up it full, and past the first table the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that she now reads two things on that path in the evening and one of them is two feet lower down and in a shop. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people at that first table have said since that he has not been asked one question in two hundred and forty-three mornings and that this morning somebody in that yard asked a question and it was not his.

A man asked a woman in that yard this morning whether she would take a page off her own counter and she said no in four seconds and gave a reason in nine and nobody asked her a second question, and a clerk entered that a man asked and a woman refused and entered that the rule about a person and a reason in the room is a rule about that yard and not about a shop, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the eighteenth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
