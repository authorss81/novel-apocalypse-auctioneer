# Chapter 533: The Boy Counted A Row Of Cuts Out Loud

The month turned on the morning of the first of the third month and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it turned and entered no figure of any kind about how long it had been, and a boy of about nineteen counted the row of cuts on that board out loud at about ten past ten, in the open, in front of about nineteen people, and got ninety, and a clerk entered that a boy counted a row of marks in that yard and entered the figure and entered that it is a count of marks and not a count of a month, and that the whole of what this yard has to go on is now ninety marks and a man who cut them.

It was the first morning of the third month and the light came up off the water behind that yard before seven and came over the top of the boards of that second table about ten minutes later than it had been doing all of last month, and the boards were cool to the hand until about a quarter to nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and seventy-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fifty-eighth of them, and that the first morning of a month is the first morning this volume in which he has said the fourth of them and then gone and stood at the end of the row of cuts for about four seconds before saying the first of them again.

"**The board carries four hundred and thirty-one, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and forty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and sixty-one, and four hundred and twenty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The boy went at the board first, before the light was properly on it, and he put his own hand flat on the wood at the end nearest the wall and worked his way down the row with his thumb, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people stood and listened to a boy count a piece of wood, and the number he read back to himself in a low voice at the end of it was ninety.

"**Ninety. I have counted them twice and the first time I got eighty-nine and I went back to the beginning and did it again with the other hand, and there is a mark at the end of it that is not like the others and I counted it, and I would like a clerk to write down that I counted it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy counted a row of marks in that yard in the open and entered the figure, and entered that a boy got a different figure the first time he counted it and entered no figure for that one because he did not ask her to enter it, and entered that a count of marks is not a count of a month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and then went and stood at that board and put the flat of his hand on the wood about a foot from the end of the row, and said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted it and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is more than one of them and I am not going to count them for you. A count that a man gives you is a count you did not do. I cut those. I cut the ones I cut, and I have never asked anybody for anything back, and a clerk can put a finger on that wood and find them herself in about four seconds, and she does not need me to stand here and show her which ones they are.**"

About nine people at the end of that board have said since that a man who has said no to two questions in two days went and put his own hand on the wood before he answered a third one, and that about four of them have said that a man offering to be shown and a man offering to be found are two different things and that this yard has not had the second of them before. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering what a mark is for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years then said one thing out loud, standing at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and twenty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to count anything today, and I want it entered that I said that out loud this morning before anybody asked me to. A count of a month is not a thing you do at ten past ten with people standing about who want an answer, and I have now said the sentence about a figure somebody entered because they had to enter one not a promise about a later day twice in six volumes, and I am telling this yard that it is the wrong sentence for this morning. The problem this morning is not that anybody entered a figure. The problem is that a page in this district has ninety on it and a man has none.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk has said in a yard that a sentence she has entered before is the wrong sentence for this morning, and entered that she is not entering that as a change, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about a quarter past one, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and fourteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been asking this yard for a number for a month and I have got nobody knows, and a man has stood at that board this morning and told us there is an instrument in it. I am not going to say that I am glad, because the day this yard can count a month is the day after which it will want to count a second one, and I do not want to be the one who asks for that, and if anybody in this yard thinks a man can hand a yard an instrument and then walk away from it, that man has not been in this district for six volumes.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a plan and entered that she is not entering what a second count would be of, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the end of that second table at about half past twelve for about four minutes, on his way to somewhere else, and said one thing out loud to about nine people in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got seventy-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put that sheet on that table on the first morning of this volume and I have not read it once. I have watched a boy count a row of marks in the open this morning and I stood about four feet off him doing it, and I am not going to tell anybody what I thought about it, and that is a thing I am allowed to do in a yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put a sheet of paper on a table in the open in this volume and has never read it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a boy counted a row of cuts in the open this morning and a man who comes up that lane twice a day read four figures off a wall on his way past and neither of them stopped. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had already been at the other end of it at half past twelve. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a clerk stood about nine feet off that man this morning and told about nineteen people that a page in this district has four figures on it about a man who has none, and that nobody in that yard joined the two of those. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-second night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and eighty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and thirty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and forty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and eleven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and fourteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Ninety marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are seventy-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and a clerk said out loud in that yard this morning that the sentence she has entered twice in six volumes is the wrong sentence for this morning, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of any month. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a boy counting a row of marks in the open is not a thirteenth, and a clerk saying out loud that she is not going to count anything today is not one either. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people at that first table have said since that a yard of about nineteen people has been asking who is going to do the work for a month and the answer to that question has been a boy's thumb and a clerk saying no.

Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that she looked at it twice and entered the two of those as two, which is the first time she has entered a count about her own looking, and about five people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man saying that a clerk can find a thing in the wood herself is not one of the six.

The month turned this morning and nobody in that yard made anything of it, and a man cut a mark in a board at about ten this morning that is deeper than the others, and a clerk entered that the month turned and entered no figure about it, and about four people near the end of that board have said since that this is the second morning in a row on which the last thing anybody in this district did about a month was to refuse to do anything about it.

The two buckets went down that bank empty and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that she read the second of those three lines before the first this evening, which she has not done before. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man who has read four figures off it every morning for two hundred and fifty-eight mornings stood this morning at the end of a row of cuts for about four seconds, and that about four of them think he was counting.

The first morning of the third month came into that yard the way every other morning comes into it, and a boy counted ninety marks out loud and got it right the second time, and a man told a clerk she could find the ones that are not like the others with a finger in about four seconds, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the thirty-third morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and there is now a figure on a page in this district for the number of marks in a board and none at all for the number of days in a month.
