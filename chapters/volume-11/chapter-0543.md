# Chapter 543: The Man Who Digs Loam Said What A Date Is

The wind came round to the north side of that yard about half past eight on the eleventh of the third month and the dust that has banked against the south side of that low wall for about two months went the other way instead, and about four people in that yard noticed it and about nine of them did not, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and the boards of that second table were dry for the first morning this month.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and eighty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that a clerk has now entered the age of that figure on three mornings running and has not been asked for it on any of them.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and sixty-eighth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man came up that bank this morning with a spade and did not stop at the wall.

"**The board carries four hundred and forty-one, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fifty-seven days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seventy-one, and four hundred and thirty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up out of it with his spade over his shoulder, and set the blade of it flat into the ground at the edge of that yard and stood with his weight on the heel of it, and said one thing out loud, before anybody had said anything to him, in about eleven seconds, and about nineteen people heard every word of it, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A date is the only kind of figure I have ever been in. I have been in two. The first was a figure of my hands and nobody told me that either, and the second is a date on a line in a book a stranger can walk up to and read, and I did not ask to be in that one and I have never once been asked whether I am.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything at all for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and set it down as it was said, and entered that a man said it before anybody had said anything to him, and entered that nobody asked him anything before he said it and nobody asked him anything after, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said out loud in that yard that he has been a figure in two things in his life, and entered that the second of them is the third line of that lot book, and entered that a man has said in a yard that he was never asked whether he is, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at the end of that second table at about half past ten, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody is going to ask him. Not today and not on any morning this yard has left, and I want it entered that I have said that out loud before anybody decides to do it, because whatever goes on that line cannot be a thing this yard went and got off a man standing in a ditch with a spade.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has said out loud in a yard that a man who digs loam is not going to be asked whether the third line of that book is about him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud from the east end of that yard, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I keep a tally of a man and I have never once asked him for a word, and there is a reason and the reason is that a tally is a thing a man does between himself and a page and the day he has to say it out loud it is not a tally any more. That is all of it. He can stand at that end of my yard for the rest of the year.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally has said out loud in a yard that he has never asked a man for a word, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud then, standing four feet off that man with the spade, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He said two sentences this morning and I have counted both and I am the only one who has, and about four people in this yard have been waiting for somebody to ask him something so that I could write it down.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody asked that man anything this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man who digs loam then took his spade out of that ground, shouldered it, and went back down that bank, and about nine people in that yard have said since that he came up out of that ditch at about ten and said two sentences he had not been asked for and went back down into it inside a quarter of an hour, and that about four of them have said that a man who has been a figure in two things in his life has now said both of them out loud in a yard, and that neither of them was a complaint.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that the wind came round to the north this morning and that he came up that lane through the dust on the other side of it, which is the first time in about a month that anything has come up that lane from the north. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had not been up that bank at all that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man said out loud this morning that he has been a figure in two things in his life, and that there is not one of the two that this district put him in on purpose. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-second night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and forty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and ninety-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and fifty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and twenty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and twenty-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and sixty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are eighty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and about four people at that second table have said since that the man who cut a hundred of those marks came up out of the ditch this morning and said out loud that nobody has ever asked him what they are for and then said a second thing about himself that nobody had asked him for either. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man saying out loud that he has been a figure twice is not a thirteenth.

The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and that person was in that yard this morning with a spade on his shoulder and said two sentences about it, and about nine people in that yard have said since that the fourth of the five things has now been said out loud by the man it is about, in a yard, in the ordinary voice, and that about four of them have said that the fourth thing has been said and the fifth thing has not, and that the fifth thing is a remedy. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that a man said a thing out loud this morning that a document in that yard has been saying since the second of the twelfth month, and about five people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and a man refusing to be asked whether a line is about him is not one of the six.

About four people at the top of that bank have said since that a man came up out of a ditch this morning and said two sentences nobody had asked him for, and that the buckets went down that bank at about half past five and came back up it full, and that the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past. About four people near the top of that bank have said since that she has never once said whether she knew, and that she has read that book every working day for about four hundred working days. She said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her. The man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a hundred marks have been cut off that board and that nobody has ever asked any of the nine what the marks are for, and that the one man who has been asked has said no.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up out of a ditch at about ten on the eleventh of the third month and said out loud, before anybody had said anything to him, that a date is the only kind of figure he has ever been in, and that he has been in two, and that nobody has asked him whether he is in either, and he went back down that bank inside a quarter of an hour, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the forty-third morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and about nine people in that yard have said that a book in it has had a man in it since the second of the twelfth month and that this is the first morning the man has said so himself.
