# Chapter 534: A Count Of A Month Will Not Tell A Stranger Anything

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the second of the third month, whether a count of the length of a month was going to tell a stranger which of the four pages in this district was the one this district made, and the clerk answered her in about four seconds in the ordinary voice that it was not, and a clerk entered that she had been asked a question in that yard and entered the answer, and about nine people at that yard have said since that the thing this yard has been given to look forward to was never going to answer the question this yard has been asking all month.

It was the second morning of the third month and the sky was low and grey and stayed low until about half past three, and the boards of that second table had a skin of water on the underside of them and not on the top, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and seventy-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fifty-ninth of them, and that about four of them have said that the only person in that yard who has read anything anybody else wrote this week is a clerk.

"**The board carries four hundred and thirty-two, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and forty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and sixty-two, and four hundred and twenty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She asked it at about eleven standing beside the near end of that second table with her own basket on her hip, and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I wrote those three lines out myself on my own paper in about nine minutes with a pencil stub, and I have stood in my own shop and looked at my own page and the page in that yard and I could not tell you which one this district made, and I have been not telling for a fortnight. Now there is going to be a number about a month. Is that number going to help me?**"

The clerk of nineteen years looked at her for about four seconds and then answered, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about four seconds, and about nineteen people heard it and the boy counted it and got four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. It will not.**"

Nobody said anything at all to that for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman asked a question out loud in that yard and entered the question, and entered that the whole of what this district has spent a month on is not what the number is going to be about, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one more thing out loud after that, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Then I want it entered that I asked, and that I was told, and that I am not going to be one of about nine people in this yard who spend the next two weeks pleased about a number about a month while four pages sit in four places and not one person in this district can put a hand on the right one of them. A page with a month counted off a board is not a page anybody can check. I have a scale and I have never once been able to tell anybody anything by looking at it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the scale, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years then said one thing out loud, standing at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to name a day in this yard. A clerk who names a day in a yard is a clerk who has made a plan in the open, and this district has not had one of those in six volumes and I am not going to be the first, and I would like it entered that a woman has asked me for a number and I have said no to the day as well as to the help.**"

The boy asked her then, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, on what day it would be if not a day she had named, and about four people at that first table have said since that a boy of about nineteen has now asked a direct question in this yard and got a direct answer and that the answer is that nobody knows. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked a question out loud in that yard and entered the question and entered the answer, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got ninety and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is the whole trouble and she is the only person in this yard who has said so. A yard can be watching on purpose on any morning it likes. What a yard cannot do is decide on a morning that everybody in it is watching on purpose, because about nine of the people in this yard would come for a fight and about nine would come for a funeral and one would come because she has a shop to open, and all three of them are the same morning.**"

About nine people near the end of that second table have said since that a man who mends fencing has said out loud in that yard that a yard cannot choose a morning, and that about four of them have said that a yard can, and that a clerk said no to naming one, and that a woman said no to the point of it, and that about four people in that yard have said since that this is the first time in six volumes that two people in that yard have said no to the same thing in the same morning and neither of them said the word no to the other. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the three kinds of people, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen then went and counted the marks in chalk along the edge of that second table out loud, standing up, in the open, in about nine seconds, and got seventy-six, and a clerk entered that a boy counted a row of marks in that yard and entered the figure, and entered that a clerk has entered the figure of a row of chalk on the same page as the figure of a row of cuts in wood and entered that she is not entering the two of them as a pair, and entered that the row of chalk does not reach back as far as the row of cuts and entered no reason why. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are seventy-seven of them in a row going back through the last volume and into this one, and he did not look up when the boy counted them.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and did not stop at that board, which he has never done. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at the end of that second table have said since that the page lying on it has now been on it for the thirty-fourth morning and that nobody has taken it off and that a woman with a shop of her own has just told a yard of about nineteen people that a number will not fix it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that two people said no in that yard this morning and that a man on those stones has not been asked about either of them. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-third night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and eighty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and thirty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and forty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and twelve days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and fifteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Ninety-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are seventy-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and a clerk told a woman in that yard this morning in about four seconds that a count of a month will not tell a stranger which of the four pages in this district is the one this district made. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a clerk saying no to a day is not a thirteenth, and a woman saying she is not going to be one of about nine people pleased about a number is not one either. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people at that first table have said since that a yard which has spent a month asking what a page is for has now been told that the thing it is about to be given is about a different thing altogether, and that about four of them have said that is the first time anybody has said so in that yard.

Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that it is empty on a morning when a woman asked her for a number and got two of them, and a clerk has entered that the column is not one of the two, and about five people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man saying that a yard cannot choose its own morning is not one of the six.

That morning two rows of marks in one yard were counted out loud in the open by a boy of about nineteen and got a figure each, and one of the two rows is in wood and goes back past the turn of the year and the other is in chalk and does not, and a clerk entered both figures and entered that she is not entering the two of them as a pair, and about four people near that first table have said since that this is the first morning in this volume on which a person has said out loud what instrument this district has to count with.

At about half past five the buckets went down that bank empty and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read those three lines standing up on her way past without stopping, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that she has read that book every evening for about four hundred working days and that the whole of what she has never said about it in six volumes is nothing. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a clerk told a yard this morning that a number would not help, and that the only thing in that yard with a figure about it in front of a person is four figures off a wall and a boy with a thumb.

A woman with a scale asked a clerk in that yard this morning whether a count of a month was going to tell her which of four pages was the one this district made, and a clerk said no in four seconds, and a clerk also said no to naming a day, and a man who mends fencing said out loud that a yard cannot choose a morning, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the thirty-fourth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and nobody in that yard has said what day it is going to happen on.
