# Chapter 502: A Woman Of Thirty-Six Reads Both

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale read that lot book and the sheet on the second table standing up, one after the other, on the morning of the twenty-ninth of this month, and said out loud in front of about nineteen people that she can tell which one she has been reading and cannot tell which of the two this district made, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of that and entered that a woman who has read those three lines standing up every working day since the day that book came out has said out loud that she cannot tell which of them is the district's.

It was the twenty-ninth morning of the first month and the ground was soft under that yard from about two in the morning and the boards of that second table had a skin of water on the underside of them and not on the top, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and forty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that a figure about a figure is not a figure about a sheet and that there is no day-count for the sheet.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and twenty-seventh of them.

"**The board carries four hundred, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and sixteen days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and thirty, and three hundred and ninety-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She did it at about a quarter past twelve with the last of that day's flour still on her hip and she did not put the flour down, and about nineteen people heard the whole of it, and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read that book standing up every working day since the day it came out to that table and I can tell you which one I have been reading. I cannot tell you which one you made, and the reason is that in six volumes nobody in this district has ever stood in front of me and made anything.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the second half of it is a finding about conduct and not about a page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the boy of about nineteen asked her, out loud, in front of about nine people, how long she has been reading them, and she answered without being asked twice, and about nine people heard it and the boy counted it and got forty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Four hundred working days, about two seconds of it every time, and I have not missed one and I have not been asked for one of them, and that is the answer you are getting and I am not going to give you a better one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering four hundred days as a figure about a column, and entered the reason, which is that a woman standing at a table is not standing in a post, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and then he read the first two lines on that sheet out loud to himself and not to anybody, and then he said one thing out loud in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got thirty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Whoever wrote that was standing at a table and not at that book, and the third line is the only one of the three that is about anybody, and that is the line somebody left out.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the third line of that book is a date and that the date is a figure of a man, and entered that she is not entering the two sentences as one finding, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard at any point on that day, and a clerk entered that he was not in it and entered that she did not ask and entered the reason, and about four people at that first table have said since that this is the fifth morning running and that one of them counted them.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten until about two and had that sheet of paper about four feet in front of him for about four hours, and he put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are forty-five of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. A clerk entered that he was four feet off that paper for about four hours and entered that nobody asked him whether the paper was his and entered that she is not going to.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and about nine people within four feet of him have said since that they have never once heard him say a whole sentence and that nobody in that yard has ever asked him for one.

The boy of about nineteen ruled a line down the middle of his own sheet before about ten and put the third of this morning in the right half of it and nothing in the left half, and a clerk of nineteen years looked at it for about four seconds and entered that a boy ruled a line and entered that she is not ruling it and is not refusing it and entered that she is not going to enter what the left half of it is for.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and a clerk entered that he was not asked about anything else on that lane this morning. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and he did not stop for anybody at that first table either, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that he looked at that sheet for about four seconds on his way past and did not touch it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about nine people within four feet of that wall have said since that they stopped looking at him about a month ago.

A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and forty-first night of that run and that he has slept on a hundred and forty of them, and said the next of it out loud before she wrote it down, and about nine people within hearing of that first table heard all of it.

"**The figures I entered for that man on the twenty-seventh morning after the count were one high on both of them, and I entered them, and I am not going to correct them, and a figure may not be corrected, and this morning is a hundred and forty-one and a hundred and forty and those are the figures on this page.**"

Nobody said anything at all to that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that two figures about a man in this district are a day out and that nobody asked that man anything about either of them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

A hundred and fifty days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and ten days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and eighty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and eighty-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and nineteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Fifty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are forty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a woman reading two books standing up is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and that book has no fourth line in it. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a clerk entered on the twenty-eighth morning of this month that naming it out loud is not paying it.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about nine people at that first table have said since that nobody in this yard has ever asked her to read it out loud. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood about nine feet off the end of that second table for about a minute afterwards without touching anything.

A woman who has read those three lines standing up for about four hundred working days said out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen people on twenty-ninth morning of the first month that she can tell which of the two books she has been reading and cannot tell which of them this district made, and a clerk entered that and entered that the second half of it is a finding about conduct and not about a page, and the paper with two lines on it is on the end of the second table for the second morning and the third line of it has a ruled line and nothing on the ruled line, and nobody in that yard has touched it since a man of about twenty-nine put it down with a hand that had been on a cart.
