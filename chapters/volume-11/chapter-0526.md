# Chapter 526: He Began A Sentence And Stopped

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing began a sentence out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen at about a quarter past eleven on the morning of the twenty-second of the second month, in front of about nineteen people, and got about nine words into it and stopped, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man began a sentence and did not finish it and entered that nobody asked him to finish it, and about nine people have said since that a man who has said a thing out loud in that yard on five mornings of this month stopped in the middle of one for the first time, and that he stopped at the same four words that another man said in that yard two mornings ago.

It was the twenty-second morning of the second month and the light came up hard off the water behind that yard before seven and did not change, and the boards of that second table were warm by about half past eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and sixty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fifty-first of them, and that about four of them have noticed that he has not turned round at that wall once this week.

"**The board carries four hundred and twenty-four, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and forty days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and fifty-four, and four hundred and fifteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He got nine words into it and stopped, and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A figure nobody said out loud is not a—**"

He stopped there. Nobody said anything to him and about nine people have said since that a man who has put a sentence into a yard on five mornings of this month stopped in the middle of the ninth word of one and stood there with his mouth shut for about four seconds, and that a clerk counted the words and read the number back to himself and did not finish the sentence for him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man began a sentence and did not finish it and entered that nobody asked him to finish it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she said one thing out loud about it, standing at the near end of that first table with her page in her hand, in about nine seconds, and about nineteen people heard it and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering that a man began a sentence and stopped, and I am not entering why, and I want everybody to hear me say that I have now used that sentence three times this month and that on the first of those three I entered a thing, and on the second of them I entered a change in what a yard is asking, and on this one I am entering a man stopping.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the three of those are three different shapes of a thing and that a clerk who joined them would be entering a rule, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and said nothing at the end of the third one and nothing after it, and about four people at that first table have said since that the man who said a whole sentence in that yard two mornings ago read three lines out loud this morning and stopped, and that about four of them have said that a man who has told this yard not to ask him anything since the fifteenth morning of last month is the only person in that yard who could have finished that sentence and did not.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was in that yard at about ten and said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A clerk in that yard wrote down a whole sentence four mornings ago and left one word out of it, and a man stopped in the middle of a sentence this morning, and I have been weighing flour in front of customers for eleven years and I can tell you the two of those are the same size of thing, and neither of them is a mistake.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the comparison in it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have said three things in that yard in about a fortnight and about four of them are things I have been holding for thirty years, and I am going to stop, and that is not a rule and there is no column for it.**"

About nine people at the east end of that yard have said since that a man who has kept a tally for about thirty years has told that yard in four seconds that he is going to stop talking in it, and that about four of them have said that a man saying he is going to stop is the first thing anybody has said in that yard this month that is about himself and not about a sheet of paper, and that a clerk entered it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a refusal of anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one more thing out loud at about a quarter past one, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got thirty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I know how that sentence ends and I am not going to say it here, and I have not been asked not to, and that is the whole of what I have.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has now said out loud that he knows how a sentence ends, and entered that she is not entering what the sentence is, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he did not stop for anybody. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and nobody in that yard has said one word to him about a sentence that was not finished this morning, and he has not been in that yard at any point since about half past twelve. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man stopped in the middle of a sentence about nine feet from that wall and a man on those stones did not look up. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and sixty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and seventy-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and twenty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and thirty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and forty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Eighty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are sixty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man got nine words into a sentence this morning and a clerk entered the nine of them and has entered that he stopped. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man stopping in the middle of a sentence is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people at that first table have said since that a man who has never once been asked a question in this district has now said out loud that he knows how a sentence ends and is not going to say it. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a man said nine words out loud in that yard this morning and a clerk entered the nine words and entered that the rest of it was not said, and about four people at that first table have said since that this is the first time in this month that a clerk has entered part of a thing and named the size of the part. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man saying he knows how a sentence ends and is not going to say it is not one of the six.

The buckets went down at about half past five and came back up full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up as she went past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that she went up that bank about a minute and a half behind a man who had stopped in the middle of a sentence in that yard four hours earlier. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near enough to see his face have said since that he has now said those four for about a week without turning round once and that about four of them think he is waiting for something, and that a man at the foot of that wall has now been nine feet off a thing nobody in that yard had ever been asked before.

A man in that yard began a sentence this morning at about a quarter past eleven, got nine words into it and stopped, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the nine words and entered that a man stopped and entered that nobody asked him to finish it, and the man said afterwards in four seconds that he knows how the sentence ends and is not going to say it, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the twenty-sixth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
