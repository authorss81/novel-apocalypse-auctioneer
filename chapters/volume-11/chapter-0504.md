# Chapter 504: The Shape Is Older Than The Yard

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came up that lane at about eleven on the morning of the thirty-first of this month with a basket on her hip and said one thing out loud in front of about nineteen people, which was that she has seen that shape before and has seen it on a post, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of it and entered that the shape is older than that yard and entered no figure about how much older.

It was the thirty-first morning of the first month and the wind had gone round to the south in the night and that yard had gone from wet to dry in about an hour after nine, and the stone lying face up on the end of that second table had been warm by about half past nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and forty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and twenty-ninth of them, and that he has said nothing else in that yard since a boy told him on the thirtieth morning of this month that one of his four was out by a figure this district does not have.

"**The board carries four hundred and two, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and eighteen days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and thirty-two, and three hundred and ninety-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She said it at about eleven and she put the basket down at the low step at the end of that first table before she said it and picked it up again afterwards, and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy of about nineteen counted what she said and got sixty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have seen that shape before. I have seen it on a post at the second channel, going on for two years, with two lines on it and nothing in the third, and the woman who kept that laundry then used to say it was the shape and not the thing, and she never once said who put it up and nobody ever asked her.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the shape on that table has been on this district before and that this district did not put it there, and entered that she is not entering two years because there is no figure on any page in this district for how old a sheet of paper is, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the boy of about nineteen asked her, out loud, in front of about nine people, whether the thing on that post at the second channel is the same thing as the sheet on that second table, and she answered without being asked twice, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I do not know and that is the whole of what I have got. I have taken in washing at that channel for about nine years and I could not tell you whether it was the same paper and I could not tell you whether it was the same words, and I have never once read the second of those two lines anywhere but on that table.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman has read two lines four hundred working days and has never read them anywhere else, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and he read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice and about nine people heard all of it, and he said one thing after it in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got twenty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The tear on that edge is old. I have been in that ditch long enough to know what paper looks like when it has been wet and dried twice.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering old, because there is no figure on any page in this district for old, and entered the reason, which is that a torn edge is not a date and a date is the only thing in this district anybody can check, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard at any point on that day for the seventh morning running, and a clerk entered that he was not in it and entered that she did not ask and entered the reason, and about nine people at that first table have said since that they have started saying the number out loud to each other on that wall in the morning and that nobody in that yard has said the number out loud where he could hear it.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table from about ten and had that sheet of paper about four feet in front of him again, and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are forty-seven of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said nothing at all, and about nine people within four feet of that end of that yard have said since that he has never once been asked for a figure off it.

About nine people at that second table have said since that a woman who takes in washing at the second channel read that sheet standing up twice and put her basket down before she said anything and picked it up again afterwards, and that nobody in that yard has worked out whether she put it down for the asking or for the answer, and that about four of them have said that a woman who puts a heavy thing down before she says a thing out loud in a yard is not doing it for the yard.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he read them without stopping, the way he does. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at that first table have said since that he has not stopped in that yard on any morning of this month. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people have said since that he has not asked anybody for anything in this district in six volumes. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and forty-third night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and forty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and fifty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and twelve days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and eighty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is two hundred and eighty-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and twenty-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Sixty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are forty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected and the figure on the wall is the figure, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month it is living in now, and a boy asked for one in a yard on the twenty-eighth morning of this month and the whole of what he got was nobody knows. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a shape that has been on a post for two years is not a thirteenth and is not a fourteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and it has not been read out loud in that yard in six volumes. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and it has been named out loud in that yard six times this month and filled on none of them. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and it has not been said out loud in a mouth anywhere in this district this month.

Both buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people have said since that she has not missed a working day in about four hundred. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and nobody in that yard has asked him a question since the thirtieth morning of this month and about four people have said that they have not asked him either and that they have thought about it.

A woman who has taken in washing at the second channel for about nine years stood at the low step at the end of that first table on the morning of the thirty-first of this month and said out loud in front of about nineteen people that the shape of that sheet has been on a post at the second channel going on for two years with two lines on it and nothing in the third, and that the woman who kept that laundry then said it was the shape and not the thing, and a clerk entered that the shape is older than that yard and entered no figure about how much older, because there is no figure on any page in this district for how old a sheet of paper is, and the third line of that sheet is a ruled line with the paper still on it on the fourth morning it has been lying on that table.
