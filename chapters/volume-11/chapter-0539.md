# Chapter 539: The Woman With The Scale Shut Her Shop For The Morning

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said out loud, in front of about nineteen people, in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the seventh of the third month, that she was shutting her own shop for the morning of the eighth and had not opened it for anything but a delivery in eleven years, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that and entered that it is the first thing anybody in that yard has put on the table in a month, and about nine people at that yard have said since that a shutter going down over a counter where four pages of this district are lying is a bigger thing than anything anybody has said out loud there in three weeks.

It was the seventh morning of the third month and the light came up off the south end of that ground white and hard and did not change all morning, and the boards of that second table were hot to the hand by about nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that the heat in that yard on the two mornings before a count is the one thing in this district that has got on with the day without being asked.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and seventy-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and sixty-fourth of them, and that about four of them have said that this is the last morning before a clerk says a number out loud in that yard and that he read the fourth of his four a half beat slower than the other three for the second morning running.

"**The board carries four hundred and thirty-seven, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fifty-three days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and sixty-seven, and four hundred and twenty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She said it standing at the end of that second table at about eleven with her basket already on her hip, and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am shutting my own shop for the morning of the eighth. Not the day. The morning. I have not shut that shop for a morning in eleven years unless a man came with a delivery I could not do without, and there is nothing coming on the eighth, and I have told the two people who come in before I open on a market day and I have not told anybody else, and I would like it entered that I said it out loud in here first and not in my own shop.**"

Nobody said anything to that. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that it is the first thing anybody in that yard has put on the table in a month, and entered that she is not entering the eleven years as a cost, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, at about half past eleven, what she was going to do if the number she counted was not the number the man at the east end of that yard was going to read out of his own book, and she answered him in about nine seconds and the boy counted it and got one hundred and seventeen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to enter the figure I count and I am going to enter the line he reads and I am going to enter that there is a difference if there is one. I am not going to enter what the difference is until there is one, and I am not going to enter which of the two of us is right, because a clerk who says which of two numbers is right in a yard is a clerk who has turned a count into a verdict, and this district has never had a verdict and I am not going to build one on a morning when there is a hand on a piece of wood.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she has said out loud in that yard what she will do and has said out loud in that yard what she will not do, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard and said one thing out loud at about a quarter past twelve, in about four seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to shut that book this morning before she counts and put it under my arm, and it is going to stay shut until she has said her number, and then I am going to open it and read one line out of it in the open, and if about nineteen people have heard me say that a fortnight ago and have been waiting for me to be a man with something useful then they have been waiting for the wrong man, because I have not got anything except a line I wrote down off a piece of paper somebody handed me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering what a clerk is going to do with a book, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and said one thing after it in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to stand at the front of that yard on that morning. I cut those marks and I have never read one of them and I am not going to be standing four feet off a clerk's hand being told what my own wood says. I will be up that bank at about ten because that is where I read those three lines and I am not going to stop doing that for anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the marks as his, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy asked the man of about fifty-six, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, at about ten past one, whether he was going to be at that wall on the morning of the eighth, and he said one thing out loud in about four seconds and the boy counted it and got fifty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am at that wall at the hour I am at that wall every morning. I am not coming earlier and I am not coming later and I am not going to stand anywhere else, and if a number comes out of that yard on that morning I would like it entered that I did not look up.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that this is the last morning before a clerk says a number out loud in that yard and that a man who comes up that lane twice a day has not been told about it and would not come early for it if he had been. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at the end of that second table have said since that the page lying on it has been on it for the thirty-ninth morning and that nobody has put anything on that table to hold a page down. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who has been asked nothing for about a week has not been asked about the eighth either, and that nobody in that yard has suggested that anybody should. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and eighty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and forty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and forty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and seventeen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and twenty days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifty-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Ninety-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are eighty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and a clerk told a boy in that yard this morning what she will enter and what she will not enter about a count, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of any month. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a woman shutting her own shop for one morning is not a thirteenth, and a man saying he will not stand at the front of a yard is not one either. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people at that first table have said since that tomorrow morning is the first morning in this volume on which about nineteen people in that yard are going to be in it for a stated reason.

Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that it is empty the morning before a count, and entered that she has entered that sentence on about nine mornings now and that she is not going to enter it again today, and about four people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a woman shutting a shop is not one of the six.

The morning before the morning a clerk has said out loud that she will count a row of marks in that yard went almost like every other morning in it, and a woman said out loud that she is shutting her own shop for it, and a clerk said out loud in front of about nineteen people exactly what she will enter about it and exactly what she will not, and a man with a book under his arm said he is going to keep that book shut until she has spoken, and about four people in that yard have said since that this is the first time this district has entered, out loud and in advance, the shape of a thing it is going to find out.

The buckets went down that bank empty and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near the top of that bank have said since that she is the only person in this district who will be in that yard tomorrow morning without having been asked to be. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that he has now told about nineteen people where he will be standing tomorrow morning, and that about four of them have said that a man who has said nothing in a month has just made an arrangement with a yard, and that he arranged it in such a way that he cannot be moved.

A woman with a scale said out loud in that yard this morning that she is shutting her own shop for the morning of the eighth and has not done that in eleven years, and a clerk said out loud what she will enter and what she will not, and a man at that wall said he will be at the hour he is always at that wall and would like it entered that he did not look up, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the thirty-ninth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and there is one morning left.
