# Chapter 536: She Named The Eighth Morning Out Loud

The clerk of nineteen years said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the fourth of the third month, that she was going to count the marks off that board on the eighth morning of that month, in the open, in daylight, with her own finger on the wood, and that the only thing she would say when she had done it would be the figure, and a clerk entered that a clerk named a day out loud in a yard and entered that this district has not had one of those in six volumes, and about nine people in that yard have said since that the yard which spent a month refusing to put a figure on a month has now got a morning in it with a figure at the end of it.

It was the fourth morning of the third month and the light came up off that ground flat and grey and there was a wind out of the east for about an hour after nine that took the sound off that yard and put it back at about ten, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and seventy-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and sixty-first of them, and that about four of them have said that on the morning a clerk named a day he said the fourth of his four a half beat slower than the other three, which he has not done since before the middle of the sixth month.

"**The board carries four hundred and thirty-four, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fifty days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and sixty-four, and four hundred and twenty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

She said it at about a quarter past ten with the boards of that first table under her own page and about nineteen people standing in front of her, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**On the eighth morning of this month. In this yard, in the open, in daylight, with my own finger on the wood, and about nineteen people standing where you are standing now. I will count the marks from one I can find with my hand to the next one I can find with my hand, and I will say the number out loud in the ordinary voice, and that will be the whole of what I say. I am not going to write it down first. I am not going to write it down at all until I have said it.**"

Nobody said anything at all to that for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk named a day out loud in a yard, and entered that this district has not had that in six volumes, and entered that she is not entering it as a plan and entered that a plan is a thing with a date on it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy asked her then, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, why the eighth and not another morning, and she answered him in about nine seconds and the boy counted it and got ninety and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Because a finger on wood has to be able to see what it is doing. That board faces east and the light comes across it from about ten on the eighth of a month and not before, and on any other morning the cuts are in their own shadow and a clerk would be counting with her thumb in the dark, and a figure you cannot see is a figure somebody has to take on trust, and I am not going to ask this yard to take one on trust.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the light is not a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and fifteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have kept this book for about thirty years and I have read four things out of it in that yard in about a month and I have not read the line this is about out loud in front of anybody, not once, in thirty years. I am going to read it out loud on that morning, in the open, after she has said her number and before anybody says anything else, and I am not going to be asked to and I am not going to be thanked for it, and a clerk can enter that I said I would do it and she can enter that I did not say what it is.**"

About nine people at the east end of that yard have said since that a man who has said he was going to stop talking in that yard has just said out loud that he is going to talk in it on a morning four days from now, and that about four of them have said that a man with a figure in a book is the only person in that yard who can put something next to what a clerk counts, and that about four other people have said that this yard has spent a month refusing every offer of a figure and has now been given two inside four days. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about a quarter past one, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and nineteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have asked this yard for a number for a month and I have got nobody knows, and I would like it entered this morning that the man who asked for it is now the only person in this yard who wishes the number were not going to come. I am not going to be the one who asks her to do it. If a month in this district has a figure on it in the open then a stranger can read it, and if a stranger can read it then a stranger can ask what a page is for, and this yard has not got an answer to that and it has had a month to get one.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering what a page is for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a clerk has named a morning in that yard and that the man who comes up that lane twice a day is the only person in this district who will be doing exactly what he did this morning on that morning as well. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that four mornings from now a clerk is going to say a number out loud in that yard, and that a man who comes up that lane twice a day will be about four hundred yards off when she does it, and that nobody has offered to tell him. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and eighty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and thirty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and forty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and fourteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and seventeen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Ninety-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are seventy-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and a clerk named a morning in that yard this morning and entered that she is not writing the figure down first, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of any month. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a clerk naming a day is not a thirteenth, and a man saying he will read a line out of his own book on a morning four days off is not one either. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people at that first table have said since that the yard has now got two things it is waiting for and both of them are numbers, and that about four of them have said that a yard waiting for two numbers is a yard that has stopped asking what a page is for.

Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that it is empty four days before a morning she has named, and entered that a clerk who names a morning is not a clerk who has filled a column, and about four people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man saying he is not going to be the one who asks is not one of the six.

Four mornings from that morning a clerk of nineteen years is going to put her own finger on a board in that yard and count a row of marks out loud in front of about nineteen people, and a man with a book under his arm has said he will read a line out of it afterwards, and about four people in that yard have said since that this is the first time in six volumes that two people have told this yard what they are going to do in the open and on a morning that has a date on it, and that neither of them said what the figure is.

The buckets went down that bank at about half past five empty and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that she is the only person in this district who comes up that bank every evening without being asked, and that a clerk has just told about nineteen people the morning she is going to count a month, and that the two of those facts have never been in the same entry. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man who has read four figures off it every morning for two hundred and sixty-one mornings is going to be standing at that wall on the eighth morning of this month, and that nobody asked him whether he would.

A clerk named the eighth morning of the third month out loud in that yard this morning and said that the only thing she would say when she had done it would be the figure, and a man with a book under his arm said he would read a line out of it in the open after her, and a man who has asked this yard for a number for a month said he wished the number were not coming, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the thirty-sixth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and there are four mornings left.
