# Chapter 530: How Long The Month Before This One Was

The boy of about nineteen asked, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the twenty-sixth of the second month, how long the month before this one was, and nobody in that yard answered him, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked a question out loud and entered that no figure was given and entered that she is not going to be able to give one either, and about nine people have said since that a yard of about nineteen people has spent a month finding out that a thing cannot be made or taken back, and has ended it by asking for a number that everybody in it has been getting wrong for a month without knowing it.

It was the twenty-sixth morning of the second month and the light came up off the south end of that ground flat and grey and there was no wind in it, and the boards of that second table were warm by about half past eight and the stone lying face up on the end of it had been warm by about nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and sixty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fifty-fifth of them, and that nobody in that yard has asked him for a figure in two hundred and fifty-five mornings.

"**The board carries four hundred and twenty-eight, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and forty-four days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and fifty-eight, and four hundred and nineteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He asked it at about eleven with his own sheet open on the boards at both halves, and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**How long was the month before this one? I have been in this yard every morning for a month and there is a man in that ditch who was in it every morning, and the four figures on that wall are up every morning, and about nine of us have been using those figures to work out things for a month, and nobody in that yard has ever said the number.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked a question out loud in that yard and entered that no figure was given, and entered that she is not going to be able to give him one either, and entered the reason, which is that a clerk who cannot answer it in a yard is not going to be able to count it off a board, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about fifty-six said one thing out loud, from that wall, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got seventy-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to answer that and I want it entered that I was asked it and did not answer, because the last time anybody told me a figure of mine was out I said nothing for a week and this time I am saying that I am not going to be the one who hands anybody a number, and that is different and about four of you can decide for yourselves whether it is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he was asked a question about a month and not about one of his four figures, and entered that she is not entering the difference between those two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and said one thing after it in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in that ditch every morning since before the second of the twelfth month, and the days I have been in it come off that wall every morning for about two hundred and fifty-five mornings, and nobody has ever counted them off the wood in front of anybody, and that is the only thing I have got to say about a month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk is entering it as a thing a man has said and not as a count, and entered that the two of those are the same sentence in this district and always have been, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten with his left hand in its cloth and said one thing out loud at about a quarter past one, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got ninety-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a board in this yard with a mark cut off it for the first of the twelfth month and about nine people have been cutting marks off it for a month, and there is a man in that ditch who has been in it every morning for a month, and about four of us have said out loud in this yard this month that nobody has counted any of it in daylight, and that is the whole of what is left and I am not going to say a number.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said a word in that yard this morning that he has not said there before, which is the word for a morning, and entered that she is not entering it as a plan, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table and put his own mark in chalk on the edge of that table at about a quarter past eleven in the ordinary way and said nothing at all, and there are seventy-three of them in a row going back to the ninth day of the last volume. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and did not open it, and a clerk entered that he said on the twenty-fourth morning of this month that he was done and has not said anything in that yard since and entered that she is not entering five mornings as a figure.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that the fourth of those four figures is a day out in a way nobody can put a figure to and that the first three of them are fine, and that a yard of about nineteen people has known that for a month and has not said one word about it out loud. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that the square of paper on that counter in that shop has been there for a fortnight and that nobody has taken it off and nobody has put another one there. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and sixty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and seventy-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and thirty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and thirty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and eleven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and forty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Eighty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are seventy-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and a boy asked in that yard for one on the twenty-eighth morning of last month and again on this morning and the whole of what he got both times was nobody knows. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a page in a shop and a page on a post and a page on a second table and a book on a first table are not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people at that first table have said since that this yard has not moved one of the four things it counts in a month and has found out three things about a page, and that about four of them think those are the same size of thing. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about four people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a boy asking for the length of a month nobody has counted is not one of the six.

The buckets went down that bank at about half past five, empty, and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near that first table have said since that this is the thirtieth evening in a row and that she has not missed one. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near enough to see his face have said since that he was asked a question this morning and said no to it, and that a man who has said the same four figures for two hundred and fifty-five mornings has now said no to the first person in that yard who ever asked him anything.

A boy of about nineteen asked this morning, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, how long the month before this one was, and no figure was given, and a clerk entered that she is not going to be able to give one either, and a man of about fifty-six who has read four figures off that wall every morning for two hundred and fifty-five mornings was asked a question this morning and said no to it and gave a reason, and the sheet on the end of that second table has been on it for the thirtieth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and there are now four places in this district where those three lines can be read and nobody in this district can say which of the four it made.
