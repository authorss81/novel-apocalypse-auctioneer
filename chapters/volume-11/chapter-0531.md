# Chapter 531: A Month You Are Living In Cannot Be Counted

The boy of about nineteen asked, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the twenty-seventh of the second month, who was going to count the marks off that board, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing told him in about nine seconds that a month a person is living in cannot be counted by anybody, and that the month this yard is living in ends in two mornings, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of that and entered that it is the reason nothing was counted on that morning, and that the reason is on a page for the first time in six volumes.

It was the twenty-seventh morning of the second month and the light came up off the south end of that ground flat and grey and there was no wind in it at all, and the boards of that second table were warm by about half past eight and the stone lying face up on the end of it had been warm by about nine, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and seventy days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fifty-sixth of them, and that about four of them have said that a man who has said no to one question in a month said it in four seconds and did not look round afterwards.

"**The board carries four hundred and twenty-nine, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and forty-five days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and fifty-nine, and four hundred and twenty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He asked it at about eleven with his own sheet open on the boards at both halves and about nineteen people heard every word of it, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fourteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I asked in this yard on the twenty-sixth morning of last month how long the month before this one was, and nobody told me, and the clerk told me she was not going to be able to tell me either, and I have been thinking about that for two days. I am not asking for the figure. I am asking who is going to do the work, because there is a board in this yard with a mark cut off it for every morning since the first of the twelfth month and about nine of us have been cutting them, and nobody has ever counted any of them in daylight in front of anybody.**"

Nobody answered him. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked a question out loud in that yard and entered the question, and entered that a boy asked it in the ordinary voice and entered that she is not entering the asking of it as a second asking of the same thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about half past ten and said one thing out loud before anybody else had, in about four seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. I put the post up at the first channel and I put nothing on it and I have said so in this yard once. I put tables up. I have never in my life counted a table, and if a yard wants the marks off that board counted then a yard wants a person who has spent thirty years counting marks and there is not one of those in this district either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts things up said no to a question nobody had put to him, and entered that she is not entering the no as a refusal of a post, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said it, and he said it standing at the end of that second table with his right hand in the cloth it has been in since the eleventh of the June and his left hand flat on the boards, and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A month you are living in cannot be counted. That is not a rule anybody made, it is the way a month is. You cannot count the days off a month you are in the middle of, because every morning you would have to decide whether today is in it, and the man who decides that is the man who is being counted. That month ends in two mornings. And there is one before it that ended a long while back, and a month that has ended can be counted, and that is the whole of what I have.**"

About nine people at the near end of that first table have said since that a yard of about nineteen people has been asking a question for a month and a man who mends fencing has told it, out loud, that the question it is asking is a question about a month it is standing inside. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the thing is the reason nothing was counted on that morning, and entered that a reason is not a rule and that she is not entering it as either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and stood at the end of that row of cuts on the board with his back to about nine people for about half a minute, and then said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**They are not all the same. I have been looking at that row since about a quarter past ten this morning and I am not going to say anything else about it today, and I am not going to be asked.**"

The boy asked him anyway, out loud, in front of about nineteen people, and got an answer in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No, I am not.**"

Nobody said anything else about the row of cuts for the rest of that morning. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said no to a question, and entered that she is not entering what a mark is, and entered that about nine people were standing at that board at about eleven and that nobody put a finger on any of it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was in that yard at about ten and said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and fifteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I buy what I sell by the month and I have never once known how long a month is, and I have been doing it for eleven years, and I am telling you that this morning because I have found out that a person can be doing a thing for eleven years and not know one thing about it, and I did not find that out this morning. I found that out a fortnight ago. I am saying it now because a man in this yard has just said out loud that a month cannot be counted and I would like somebody to check whether that is the only thing that is true about them.**"

Nobody answered her. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a clerk is not entering the eleven years as a finding about anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a board with a mark cut off it for every morning since the first of the twelfth month stands about nine feet off the end of that wall and that a man has been reading figures off one of them for two hundred and fifty-six mornings without ever looking at the other one. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and did not look at the board either. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that the whole of a question about a month was asked and answered at about nine feet from that man this morning and that he has not moved his hands. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventieth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and seventy-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and thirty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and thirty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and twelve days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and forty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Eighty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are seventy-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of any month, and a boy asked in that yard this morning who was going to count the marks and a man told him that a month a person is living in cannot be counted by anybody. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a reason a man gave out loud in a yard is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people at that first table have said since that about nine people in this yard have been cutting a mark into a board every morning for about three months and that this is the first morning any of them has asked who is going to read them back.

Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that it is empty for the fourth time in a month and has entered no figure for that, and about seven people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man saying out loud that a month cannot be counted is not one of the six.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud in that yard this morning that a month a person is living in cannot be counted, and a man of about thirty-four who digs loam stood at the end of a row of cuts for about half a minute and said that they are not all the same and would not say anything else about it, and the boy of about nineteen asked him a second question and was told no, and a clerk entered the reason and did not enter a figure.

The two buckets went down that bank empty and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past without stopping, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near the foot of that bank have said since that she is the only person in this district who has looked at a page in that yard every evening for about four hundred working days and that nobody has ever asked her what she thinks of it. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that he has now said no to one person in a month and has said yes to nothing in six volumes, and that the two of those are not in the same sentence.

A boy asked in that yard this morning who was going to count the marks off that board and a man told him that a month a person is living in cannot be counted, and a man at the end of that board said the marks are not all the same and would say nothing else about it, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the thirty-first morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and a board in that yard has eighty-eight cuts in it and has never been read back by anybody.
