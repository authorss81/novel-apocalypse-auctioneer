# Chapter 546: The Copy Was Lifted Off That Table And Put Back

The far end of that second table has been soft under the hand since about the middle of the morning of the fourteenth of the third month, and about four people in that yard noticed it before anything else happened and about nine of them kept their weight off it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about four of those had come up that bank on the two mornings before this one to ask the clerk a question and had been told no both times.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and eighty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the board that carries that figure has a name and the sheet does not, and that this district has never before had to decide which of the two is the one it means.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and seventy-first of those mornings, and that a man came up that lane at about a quarter to ten with both arms out and picked a sheet of paper up off a table and put it down again about an inch along.

"**The board carries four hundred and forty-four, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and sixty days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seventy-four, and four hundred and thirty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart came up that lane at about a quarter to ten with his arms out, and lifted the sheet off the end of that second table with his right hand, and held it flat against his own chest for about a minute, and put it back down about an inch from where it had been lying for forty-six mornings.

Nobody in that yard said anything at all for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall, and about nine people within about nine feet of that second table have said since that about four of them looked at the boards and about five of them looked at the man's hands.

He said one thing out loud then, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That end of that table is going. I have seen it going for about four days. I am not going to be the man whose sheet is the reason a table a man of thirty-seven built in about eleven minutes goes soft, and I have put it back and it is on the same table and it is on the same morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man took a sheet of paper off the end of that second table in that yard in the open at about a quarter to ten, and entered that he put it back on the same table on the same morning about an inch from where it had been, and entered the reason, which is that the far end of that table is going, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I asked him in front of all of you this morning last week whether I could have that sheet for my counter, because a person is allowed to ask, and he said no. He has now picked it up with his own hands in front of about nineteen people and carried it against his chest for about a minute. Nobody in this yard is going to tell me those are the same size of thing and I am not going to stand here and be the one who says they are not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman who asked for that sheet eleven mornings ago and was refused has said out loud that she is not going to be the person who settles what a man carrying a sheet for a minute means, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years then said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering that it moved and I am not entering where it went. A thing goes onto a table in this district with a person and a reason in the room and that has held for forty-six mornings, and this morning a thing came off one and it held this morning as well, and an inch is not a distance anybody can enter, and if it were, then I would have to enter a figure for how far along that table a sheet may be, and I am not going to rule that either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering a distance, and entered that a distance a sheet of paper may be moved along a table is not a figure and is not a rule, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A copy that has never moved is a copy that is easy to say is a copy, because nobody has ever picked it up. It has now been picked up by the man who put it there and put back by the same pair of hands, and about nine people watched, and about four of us cannot tell whether that is better or worse, and I am one of the four.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy counted that row of chalk marks twice this morning and did not put one there in between, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud at about one, without stopping what he was doing to the edge of that table, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That sheet was on the corner and now it is not on the corner, and I have been putting my mark on the edge of that table about two inches from where it is, and I am not going to change where I put mine, and nobody has asked me to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who cuts reeds has said out loud that he is not moving his own mark to meet a sheet of paper, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past one, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted eighty-nine chalk marks on that edge at about eight this morning and I counted them again at about one and it was eighty-nine both times, and I did not put one there in between, and about four people in this yard have asked me whether I did.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a figure a boy counts with his own eyes is not a figure anybody can check, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a sheet of paper came off that second table at about a quarter to ten this morning and went back on it, and that a man who comes up that lane twice a day has now been that yard twice on a morning when a thing on a table moved. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had been at that table at about a quarter to ten. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a sheet of paper has moved once in this yard in a month and that the man on those stones has not been told that a clerk has entered that it moved. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and eighty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and forty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and fifty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. A hundred and ninety-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred and twenty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and twenty-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and sixty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are eighty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and a clerk has entered this afternoon that a sheet of paper moved about an inch along a table this morning and that she is not entering the distance. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a sheet moving is not a thirteenth, and about four people in that yard have said since that this district has spent a month with a thing on a table and has now worked out what happens to a table under it.

The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about nine people in that yard have said since that the only difference anybody in this yard has ever been able to find between the book and the sheet is that one of them has a thing in the third line, and that about four of them have said that a sheet that moves in a yard in front of about nineteen people stops being a thing anybody could have made in secret, and that this is not a defence and nobody has entered it as one. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that a man carried a sheet of paper against his own chest for about a minute this morning and that she is not entering that as a reading, and about seven people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and a distance a sheet may be moved along a table is not one of the six.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds did not stop what he was doing when that cart came up at about three, and about four people at the top of that bank have said since that a sheet of paper on a table in that yard has moved for the first time in a month and that the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and that the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, without stopping, and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her, and that she came past that table this evening without looking at it. The man of fifty-six said those four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man carried a sheet of paper against his chest at about a quarter to ten and that a clerk entered that it moved and did not enter where it went, and that about four of them have said that is the nearest thing this district has come to saying a thing was in two places at once.

A man of about twenty-nine took a sheet of paper off the end of that second table in the open at about a quarter to ten on the fourteenth of the third month, held it against his own chest for about a minute, and put it back about an inch along, and gave a reason out loud in front of about nineteen people, and a clerk entered that it moved and entered that she is not entering where it went and entered that an inch is not a distance anybody can enter, and the sheet is on that table for the forty-sixth morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it and has been moved once about an inch along it, and it is not in the same place on that table that it was in yesterday, and nobody in that yard can say which of the two places is the one it was on.
