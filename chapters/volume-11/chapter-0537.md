# Chapter 537: The Man Who Cuts Reeds Said One Thing

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the fifth of the third month, at about a quarter past eleven, about four feet off the end of that second table and about a minute after he had put his own mark in chalk on the edge of it in the ordinary way, and it is the first thing he has said in that yard in six volumes, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said nothing said a thing and entered the thing, and about nine people in that yard have said since that a yard of about nineteen people has spent a month asking what a page is for and has not once asked the man who has been marking the edge of its furniture in front of all of them.

It was the fifth morning of the third month and the light came up off the water in the low ground behind that yard about ten minutes later than it had been doing and there was no wind in it, and the boards of that second table were warm by about half past eight, and the chalk on the edge of it had gone soft overnight and set again hard, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and seventy-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and sixty-second of them, and that about four of them have said that on the morning a man who has said nothing in six volumes said something, the man at that wall said all four of his without stopping and then stood at that wall and did not turn round.

"**The board carries four hundred and thirty-five, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and fifty-one days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and sixty-five, and four hundred and twenty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He put the chalk down where he always puts it, at about a quarter past eleven, and about four people near that second table had already stopped talking, which they had not done before, and then he said one thing out loud in the ordinary voice, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have cut a mark on that edge every morning for about two months and I have never once known what it was for and I am not going to stop now because somebody in this yard has started counting them. I cut reeds. A reed is a thing that comes up out of the ground about the same time every year and goes down about the same time every year, and a man who cuts them does not need to know how long a year is. He needs to know what the water did last week.**"

Nobody said anything to that for about four seconds. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who has said nothing in that yard in six volumes said a thing, and entered that she is not entering what a mark is for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that ditch until about ten and came up and got the four figures at the near end of that first table and said the word with them four times, and read the three lines on that sheet out loud in the ordinary voice, and then went and stood at the end of that board and said one thing out loud to about nine people, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got fifty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He is right about that and I have known it was right for about as long as he has, and the two of us have never once said it to each other, and about nine people in this yard think they are the only two who work the ground in it.**"

Nobody said anything to that either. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the two of them as a pair, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about a quarter past one, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got sixty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody has ever asked that man what that mark is for. I have stood in this yard every morning for a month and I did not ask it this morning, and I am not going to ask it now, because if I ask it now, three days before a clerk counts a row of marks out loud, then it will look like a thing I want.**"

About nine people at the end of that second table have said since that a man who has asked this yard a question every morning for a month said out loud this afternoon that he is not going to ask a question, and that about four of them have said that is the first thing he has ever said in that yard that is about what he has not done. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel was in that yard at about eleven with a basket on her hip and said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in about nine seconds, to about nine people, and the boy counted it and got one hundred and twenty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have read all four of those pages. I have read them in a shop and in a yard and on a post and on a table and I have said once, out loud, in a shop, that I could not tell which one came first, and I have not stopped reading them since. I am going to be in this yard on the morning she counts. I am not going to miss it, and I am not going to be asked to say anything, and if somebody counts a month in front of me and it comes out the same as what I have been reading for a fortnight then I would like to be told that in a yard and not in a shop.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the four pages as a pair and entered that the fifth is a post and not a page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man who cuts reeds stood in that yard this morning and said a thing about the water, and that the road keeper comes up that lane past a board with ninety-four cuts in it and has never once looked at it. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at the end of that second table have said since that the mark that man put on that edge this morning is the eightieth in a row and that it is the first one anybody in this yard has watched him put down. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man who has been given nothing on a hundred and seventy-six mornings has sat within about nine feet of the first thing a man who has said nothing in six volumes has said in that yard, and that nobody has joined the two. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and seventy-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and seventy-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and eighty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and forty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and forty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and fifteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and eighteen days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and fifty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Ninety-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are eighty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and a man who has said nothing in six volumes said a thing in that yard this morning about what he does not need to know, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of any month. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a man saying that he does not need to know how long a year is not a thirteenth, and a man saying that he is not going to ask a question is not one either. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and about four people at that first table have said since that this yard has asked about nine questions in a month and has not asked one of a man who makes a mark every morning in front of it.

Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk has entered that it is empty on the morning the first man in six volumes said something in that yard, and entered that a sentence is not a name, and about four people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a man saying he is not going to ask a question is not one of the six.

That morning the man who cuts reeds put his eightieth mark in chalk on the edge of that second table and said his first thing in six volumes, and a man who digs loam said he had known the reed cutter was right for as long as he had, and a man who mends fencing said he was not going to ask a question three days before a count, and about nine people in that yard have said since that four people said four things about the same piece of wood in one morning and that not one of the four of them was about a sheet of paper.

At about half past five the two buckets went down that bank empty and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near the top of that bank have said since that she reads about four hundred working days' worth of evenings and that a man who cuts reeds has now said in the open that a man who counts does not need to know how long a year is, and that the two of those things have never met. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall without turning round while a man who has never said a word in that yard finished being spoken about about two hundred yards off.

A man who has said nothing in six volumes said one thing this morning about what a man who cuts reeds does not need to know, and a man who mends fencing said out loud that he is not going to ask him a question about it, and a woman who has read all four of those pages said she will be in that yard on the morning of the count, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the thirty-seventh morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it, and there are three mornings left.
