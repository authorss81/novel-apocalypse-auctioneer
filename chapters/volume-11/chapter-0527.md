# Chapter 527: The Only Thing Anybody Can Say About It

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said, out loud, in front of about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the morning of the twenty-third of the second month, the only thing anybody in that district can say about a sheet of paper with three lines on it and nothing in the third, which is that it does not go out of date, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the whole of that and entered that it is the only sentence anybody in that yard has about that sheet and entered no reason why.

It was the twenty-third morning of the second month and the sky was low and grey and stayed low and grey until about two in the afternoon, and the boards of that second table had a skin of water on the underside of them and not on the top, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in the yard of Lot Seventeen, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and sixty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the age of a figure is a figure about the figure and that the sheet has no day-count of its own and never has had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that he has said those four every morning since before the middle of the sixth month and that this is the two hundred and fifty-second of them, and that about four of them have said that he has been at that wall every morning this week and has not once looked at anybody who spoke.

"**The board carries four hundred and twenty-five, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and forty-one days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and fifty-five, and four hundred and sixteen days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

He said it at about eleven, standing, and about nineteen people heard every word of it and the boy of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and sixteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The only thing anybody in this district can say about that sheet is that it does not go out of date. The book goes out of date, because the third line on it is a date and I am the date. That sheet has nothing in the third line, so there is nothing in it that can go out of date, and a thing that cannot go out of date is not a better thing. It is a thing that will be there in nine years and so will this yard, and none of the three of you will be able to tell me in nine years which of the two of them this district made.**"

Nobody said anything at all to that for about four seconds. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that it is the only sentence anybody in that yard has about that sheet, and entered no reason why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen asked him then, out loud, in front of about nine people, what happens to a page that does not go out of date, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**You have just told this yard that a page with nothing in the third line cannot be got rid of by getting old, and I have been asking that yard who made it for twenty-three mornings, and I want to ask you whether anybody ought to be going round taking them off.**"

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam looked at the boy for about four seconds and then said one thing, in about four seconds, and the boy counted it and got forty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. Nobody ought to be going round taking them off, and I am the wrong man to ask, and I have been in a ditch every morning since before the second of the twelfth month and I have not gone and looked at one of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has now said out loud that he has not gone and looked at one of them, and about four people at that first table have said since that a man who has told that yard not to ask him anything since the fifteenth morning of last month was asked a direct question this morning and answered it, and that about four of them have said that the two of those facts are in the same morning and that nobody in that yard has joined them. A clerk of nineteen years entered that she is not joining them either, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About nine people at the near end of that first table have said since that the yard asked a boy a direct question this morning and got a direct answer from a man who has refused to be asked one thing since the fifteenth morning of last month, and that about four of them have said that a refusal to be asked is not the same as a refusal to answer, and that about four other people have said that in six volumes nobody has found out which of those two it was until this morning.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was in that yard at about ten and said one thing out loud at about half past twelve, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted it and got sixty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Then somebody has to be able to tell a stranger which one is ours, and about four of us have now tried, and the only thing any of us can do is say that the third line is a date, and that is the line that is not on it, and about four people in that yard know why and about nine people in that yard do not.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table from about ten with his left hand in its cloth and said nothing at all all morning, and about four people near him have said since that a man who stopped in the middle of a sentence yesterday has said nothing today, and that about four other people have said that a man who has said nothing for two mornings has still not taken a hand out of the cloth, and that nobody in that yard has asked him about either.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and he did not stop. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people have said since that a boy asked that man to nail a page on a post at the first channel this morning and was told no, and that this is the first time anybody has asked that man for anything at all. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about nine people within four feet of that wall have said since that a man has sat on those stones for eight mornings and has now been within about nine feet of a question two mornings running without being asked it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and sixty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and sixty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

A hundred and seventy-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and thirty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man. A hundred and thirty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and forty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched any of it.

Eighty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are seventy marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither of those two figures moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and there is still no figure on any page in this district for the length of the month that came before this one, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man has said out loud this morning that a sheet with nothing in the third line cannot be got rid of by getting old, and that the figure on the wall cannot either. The count of things this district has made is twelve, and a sentence about a page not going out of date is not a thirteenth. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and a clerk has entered this month that the third line of that book is the only line in it that goes out of date on its own, and that a sheet with nothing in the third line has nothing in it that can. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk looked at it before she left and put nothing in it, and about nine people at that first table have said since that a boy asked a man a direct question this morning and got a direct answer and that neither of the two of them is on a page. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, the count is still five, and six things stand in that yard that are not a payment and none of the six is one, and a page that cannot be got rid of by getting old is not one of the six.

Half past five, and the two buckets went down that bank empty and came back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people near the bottom of that bank have said since that she read the third of those three lines about half a second slower than the first two and that she did that tonight as she has done every evening for about four hundred working days. After that the man of fifty-six said the four figures off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood at that wall for about a minute and a half afterwards without moving, and about four people near it have said since that he said the fourth of them without stopping to think about it and that about nine mornings out of the last twenty he has not.

The only sentence a yard of about nineteen people has about a sheet of paper with three lines on it and nothing in the third is that it does not go out of date, and a man who has been in that ditch every morning since before the second of the twelfth month said it out loud this morning and a clerk entered it and entered that it is the only one anybody there has, and the sheet on the end of that second table is on it for the twenty-seventh morning with a ruled third line and nothing on it.
