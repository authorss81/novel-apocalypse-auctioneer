# Chapter 350: The Ninth of the Ninth

---

The ninth of the ninth month came in bright and the yard of Lot Seventeen had about nineteen people in it by ten, the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of that trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the bottom one of the three out loud over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the fiftieth night and has been in this district forty-nine days.

"**Two hundred and fifty-eight days on that board and five hundred and seventy-four days on the train, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and forty-one days back, and the table against that wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood sixty-four mornings.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved on any of the ten days of this month, and that its own age as a figure about the figure is ninety-nine days, and that it is a figure about a figure and is not a day-count for the sheet, and that a clerk who could have entered it on nine days out of ten and did not is a clerk who was not afraid of it.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart went out of that lane before six in the morning and was four miles off by the time the light came up, and nobody in that yard asked him to be there this evening and nobody said anything about the fact that he would not be. About nine people looked down the lane at about five and then looked away.

Nothing was on the end of that trestle table. It had been nothing on the end of it on nine of the last ten days, and a bid had been opened on the first of the ninth month at about six in the evening in front of about nineteen people and had not been called for since, and there is no figure on the page for how many days that is and a ladder is a figure about a ladder.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and seventeen, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Nothing has happened this week. I opened a thing in this yard on the first of this month with nothing in my hands and I have not run it since, and I am not going to go up that bank tonight and write it up as a week. A man said no to me in this yard with a reason on the second and I did not argue with him. A man of about nineteen stood in this yard on the third and said a word out loud that about nineteen of us have been getting wrong for a hundred and forty days, and a clerk wrote it down and did not go back and alter the old pages. I said out loud on the fourth that I am afraid of sixty-one days, and about nine of you stood there and let me say it. On the seventh I said out loud that this bid has no way to pay anybody in it and I counted four of five things on my fingers and could not fold the fifth. That is a week. It is not a settlement and it is not a defeat and I am not going to stand here this morning and tell about nineteen people what it means, because I do not know.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he does not know what a week meant has made a list and not a finding, and that six things a man has said out loud in ten days are six things and are not a figure about the six things and are not a figure about the man, and that a thing a district has built and not run is a thing a district has built, and that this is entered as a fact about a thing and not as a judgement about anybody.

The light came off the low ground at about one and the yard went hard and bright after the dark morning, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood about nine feet off the end of that trestle table with his right hand in his coat and his left hanging, and about nine people in that yard came and went behind him and not one of them said anything to him, and he did not go up that bank and he did not knock and he did not send anybody.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about two, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I said in this yard in the sixth month and again in the seventh that the figures on that table were not going to be any different tomorrow, and I have not said it since and I am not going to say it again today. What I will say is that I bent about nine feet of wire this week for nobody. I did not know what it was for at the start of this week and I have not known what it is for today and I bent it with one hand because the other one has not closed since the eleventh of the sixth month. It is the first work I have had in this district that nobody asked me for and I have not charged anybody for it, and I am not going to be a man in this yard who stands about in a thing other people are building. That is all.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he did nine feet of wire nobody asked him for and charged nobody for has not been given a job and has not been given a post, and that a thing done for nothing is not a document and is not a rate and is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a man who has done work and not been paid for it is not a figure about a way of paying anybody.

---

---

The morning went like a morning. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing bent wire at the end of that trestle table with the fingers of his right hand and the cloth still on his left, and the man of fifty-six read the three figures over his shoulder at about ten and again at about one, and about nine people came and went off that lane and none of them came in with a question, which had not happened in that yard in nine days.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was at the top of that ditch at about eleven with a bar of iron he had not come to use, and the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of it was in it with his hook and his bundle until about one. The plank door in the end wall of that building stood open on its hasp, and the nail in that hasp has not moved since the eleventh of the sixth month, and the curtain on the nail inside it moved about four inches in the wind and came back. About four people stood in that building and about nine stood outside it and one of the people outside it said the word out loud, and the man who cuts reeds said no in about four seconds, and the thing he said is not on this page and is not going to be on this page, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said a thing once in four seconds and has now been asked about it by a stranger is not a figure about that building and is not an instrument this district has built and not named.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody in that building was asked to be anything on this day and that the two buckets of water inside it were put there by a woman who has carried two buckets a day into that building for nine years and who is not a keeper of a bucket and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that the bench and the stool inside it were sat on this morning by two men who were not asked to sit on them and did not stay.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them came into that yard at about three with his hand-cart and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and nobody asked him to put up a table and he did not offer, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the table against a wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood sixty-four mornings and is a figure about a table and is not a figure about the man who puts tables up, and that his tent has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month and is not a shelter and is not a sixth of the five protected things, and that nobody asked him about either of those and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past four with two empty buckets and filled them at the trough outside that wall and did not carry them up. She stood at the foot of it for about four minutes with the buckets full beside her and about nine people in that yard, and she asked one of them, out loud, in the ordinary voice, whether the water in that building was going to keep being put there this winter if that building got a term put on it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said he did not know, and the man of fifty-six said that he did not know either, and about four other people in that yard said nothing at all, and she said that was all right and picked the buckets up and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a question about a bucket is a question and is not a bid and is not an offer and is not a refusal, and that a person who asks a question in a yard in front of about nineteen people and gets no answer has not been refused and cannot be counted either way, and that she has asked that woman about a bucket twice in nineteen years and both times about a bucket, and that nobody in that yard asked her anything back and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid on the ninth of the ninth month and the count is still five. The rival record has been in force ninety-nine days, has been read out in that yard seven times, had its eighth reading refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and is unanswered. A clerk entered that a bid a district runs on its own building is not a reading of that record and is not a ninth reading and is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that the fourth line of the offer that has been on a table in this district for seven months is still not read out loud, and that the refusals to read are nine and the departure is unspent and there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the first day of the eighth month is thirty-eight days past this morning, and that nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and sixty-first days since the first of the fourth month, counting the first day in, and that a line in a book and a line in a yard and a line on a board are three different things. Six instruments this district has built and not named, and a bid, a lot, a toll, a reading, a second reader, a column, a book and a mark are all entered as not a seventh of them. Five protected things and no sixth, and the tent rolled on the back of that hand-cart is not a sixth. The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and forty-one days back and nobody in this district can say whether that night happened, and a plank does not keep nights.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall for the whole of the evening and nobody said one word to him about what he was going to do with his hands, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man whose trade is over is not a failure and is not a tragic figure and is not redeemed, and that a clerk may not say that about him and may not say the other thing either.

The light went at about half past eight. Nothing had been settled that day. The day after this one is the tenth of the ninth month and nobody in this district has said how long that month is, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE NINTH OF THE NINTH MONTH A BID HAD BEEN OPEN IN THAT YARD FOR NINE DAYS AND WAS NOT RUN ON ANY OF THEM, AND A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT ASKED ONE QUESTION ABOUT A BUCKET IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE AND WAS ANSWERED BY NOBODY, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
