# Chapter 343: Two Figures About One Refusal

---

The second of the ninth month came in with a wind off the low ground that had been getting up since before light, and the yard of Lot Seventeen was full of it by ten. About nineteen people were standing in it. Nothing was on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read all three figures out loud over the mender's shoulder and got two of them right and stopped on the third and read it again and got it, and the mender never turned round and never said so. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded beside him, on the forty-third night of his run and forty-two days in this district.

"**Two hundred and eighty-one days since the twenty-fourth of November and two hundred and forty-two days from the second of January, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and thirty-four days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is a hundred and sixty-one days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved on either of the two days since that bid was opened, and that its own age as a figure about the figure is ninety-two days, and that a sheet at a gate is a state and not a place.

---

The mender had swept the boards at about half past eight because the wind had got at the dust on them, and he did not say that he had, and about nine people in that yard looked at the bare end of that table before they looked at anything else. A table with four legs and nothing on the end of it is a table with four legs. A counter is a place where somebody asks somebody else a question, and no question was asked at the end of it this morning.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that yard from about nine and did not leave it until about one. He has not got his right arm above the level of that shoulder since the first of the seventh month, and it was in the same place on this morning that it was on the first of that month, and he carried it the way a man carries a thing he has stopped expecting to be asked about.

At about eleven the man the figure of twenty-one years is against asked him one question in that yard, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people. He asked whether he would hold that building for a month if the bid ever came to him.

The loam man said no in about four seconds, and the reason was his own, and it came out of him already made, which is what a reason sounds like when a man has had it in his mouth for a week.

"**No. That is the only thing in this district a person can get out of the weather in, and you are standing in a yard asking me what it is worth for a month, and if it goes to whoever says the highest thing in this yard tonight then on the next wet night there is one person inside it under a term and everybody else is in the rain, and I am not going to be one of the people who says a number out loud about that. Put it on me and I will dig you footings and I will not hold it.**"

The wind took about four seconds of the end of it and nobody in that yard said anything. About nine people looked at the ground. One of them said that it was only a shed, and the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of the ditch behind that building was about nine feet off him at the time and did not say it again, and has not said it since, and nobody has asked him to.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who has refused has not been asked to say why and has, and that a refusal and a reason are two figures about one thing and are two figures. A refusal with a reason of its own is not one of the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and those eight did not move this morning and are still eight, and a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house. She entered that a man who digs loam has not been given a post and has not been given a column and has not asked for either, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about half past ten with two buckets and carried them into that building and put them down inside it where they went every morning, and she was in that yard when the man said no. She stayed about four minutes with her hand on the frame of the door, not doing anything with the door, and then she filled the buckets at the trough outside that wall and carried them up the two goes of the bank again.

Nobody in that yard said one word to her about a refusal given in her hearing. She has carried two buckets a day into that building for nine years, which is about six and a half thousand bucketfuls, and she is not a keeper of a bucket and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and the two times in nineteen years that she has asked the woman at the end of that bank about water, both times she asked about a bucket and not about the building the buckets went into.

The man of fifty-six said one thing out loud at about one, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and seventy-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been in this basin a long time and I know the shape of the edge of a thing, and the edge of this is that every one of us in this yard has known since we were children that building was the place you went when it was wet, and none of us has ever been asked to say so out loud, and this district is now going to stand in a yard and put a term on it. A man said no this morning with a reason, and a reason is a thing a person can carry about with them and be asked for again. Eight refusals in this basin have come with no reason at all and a man or a woman who keeps a house gave every one of those and I have never once known what a house has to do with them, and the eight have not moved. Two figures about one refusal is two figures and I am not going to add them together for them.**"

A man who has said out loud that a reason is a thing a person can be asked for again has not offered anybody his reason and has not been asked for it, and nobody in that yard asked him what his reason was worth.

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood at the end of that table from about half past one until about four and did not write anything down and did not ask anybody for a figure. He had a night of it in front of him, because he had promised the yard at about six the evening before, and he had no figure to bring to it, and a man with no figure who has promised a yard something is the loneliest kind of man in a district of nineteen people.

He said one thing out loud at about half past four, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and the minute took one hundred and fifty words of it, in his own words.

"**A man said no to me in this yard at eleven this morning in about four seconds and he gave a reason, and the reason was about that building and not about a bucket and not about a house, and I am not going to stand here this afternoon and pretend that is a small thing that happened. That is the answer to the question I asked in this yard yesterday afternoon, which nobody answered. It came back the next morning in four seconds from a man who works in the ground. It is not a figure and it is not a price and I am not going to write it on the end of that table, because a table in the open in a yard a stranger can walk into is not a place to write down that a man will not stand in a building in the wet.**"

A refusal given out loud in a yard is a thing a person said and is not a finding. It cannot be counted and it cannot be uncounted, and a man who has heard a refusal and has not been asked what he thinks of it has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane at about two with the bed of it empty and stopped at the trough, and about nine people in that yard got out of his way the way they always do for a cart that is going somewhere. He was at the end of that table for about four minutes and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not ask him anything at all. A man standing at the end of a table with nothing on it is not a man waiting to be asked and is not a man who has been asked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At the foot of the east wall, the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the brick and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month. He listened to the whole of the afternoon from about nine feet off and said nothing about the refusal. A man who has lost the use of both hands is not a figure about a refusal and is not a reason for anybody to refuse anything, and a tent rolled on a hand-cart is not a shelter and is not a document and is not a sixth of the five protected things.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road walked the lane from about three until about four and came back past the end of that table and went up the bank and did not stop, and nobody asked him one question about the eleven miles he walked out of a sound in the seventh month. A man who keeps a road is not a bearer of that road and has not been asked to be one.

The rival record has been in force ninety-two days and has been read out in that yard seven times, and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and a refused reading is not a reading, and it is unanswered. A refusal given about a building in that yard is not an eighth reading of that record and is not a ninth, and a bid a district runs on its own building is not a reading of it.

The first day of the eighth month is thirty-one days past this morning, and thirty-one is a figure about a ladder and is not a term and is not a figure about anybody.

There are three sets of refusals standing in this district and they are three figures and they are not added together. Nine refusals of eleven coppers a week. Seven refusals about the ninth holding. And the eight that came from a house with a clerk in it, which are the eight that have never moved on any day since the twenty-fourth of the third month and did not move this morning either. Eleven coppers a week is a price a man named out loud and is not a rate. A refusal is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and a reason given in a yard is not a fourth of the three documents it does not own, and a man who will not stand in a building is not a sixth of the five protected things.

The light went at about half past eight and the man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded beside him and nothing in his hands, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was about nine feet off the end of that trestle table and did not go up that bank and did not knock, and he asked for nothing, and that is entered and is not a finding and is not a refusal.

**A MAN REFUSED A BID IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS IN THAT YARD AND GAVE A REASON THAT WAS ABOUT THE BID. NOTHING CAME OUT OF A HOUSE WITH A CLERK IN IT TODAY. THE SHEET FOUR HUNDRED YARDS UP THE LANE STILL SAID FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
