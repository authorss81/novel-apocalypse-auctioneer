# Chapter 343: Two Figures About One Refusal

---

The second of the ninth month came in with a wind off the low ground and the yard of Lot Seventeen had about nineteen people in it by ten, the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of that trestle table and the man of fifty-six read all three out loud over his shoulder and got two of them right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the forty-third night and has been in this district forty-two days.

"**Two hundred and eighty-one days since the twenty-fourth of November and two hundred and forty-two days from the second of January, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and thirty-four days back, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is a hundred and sixty-one days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved on either of the two days since that bid was opened, and that its own age as a figure about the figure is ninety-two days, and that a sheet at a gate is a state and not a place.

---

Nothing was on the end of that trestle table this morning and about nine people looked at the bare boards of it before they looked at anything else. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had swept it at about half past eight because the wind had got at the dust on it, and he said nothing about having swept it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a table with four legs and nothing on the end of it is a table with four legs and is not a counter, because a counter is a place where somebody asks somebody else a question and no question was asked at the end of it this morning.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that yard from about nine and did not leave it until about one. He has not got his right arm above the level of that shoulder since the first of the seventh month, and his right arm was in the same place on this morning that it was on the first of that month, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that an arm that has not gone above a shoulder since the first of two months past is not a figure about a bid and is not an argument against one.

At about eleven the man the figure of twenty-one years is against asked him one question in that yard, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people. He asked whether he would hold that building for a month if the bid ever came to him.

He said no in about four seconds, and the reason was his own.

"**No. That is the only thing in this district a person can get out of the weather in, and you are standing in a yard asking me what it is worth for a month, and if it goes to whoever says the highest thing in this yard tonight then on the next wet night there is one person inside it under a term and everybody else is in the rain, and I am not going to be one of the people who says a number out loud about that. Put it on me and I will dig you footings and I will not hold it.**"

The wind took about four seconds of it and nobody in that yard said anything. About nine people looked at the ground. One of them said that it was only a shed, and the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of the ditch behind that building was about nine feet off him at the time and did not say it again, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who said in about four seconds what a building is once, on the eleventh of the eighth month, and has been asked nothing about it since, is not a second refusal of anything and has not refused a second time.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who has refused has not been asked to say why and has, and that a refusal and a reason are two figures about one thing and are two figures, and that a refusal with a reason of its own is not one of the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that those eight did not move this morning and are still eight, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house. She entered that a man who digs loam has not been given a post and has not been given a column and has not asked for either, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about half past ten with two buckets and carried them into that building and put them down inside it where they went every morning, and she was in that yard when the man said no. She stayed about four minutes with her hand on the frame of the door and then she filled them at the trough outside that wall and carried them up the two goes of the bank again. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman who has carried two buckets a day into a building for nine years is not a keeper of a bucket and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and that she has asked that woman at the end of that bank about a bucket twice in nineteen years and both times about a bucket, and that nobody in that yard said one word to her about a refusal given in her hearing, and the record about the not saying says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said one thing out loud at about one, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and seventy-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been in this basin a long time and I know the shape of the edge of a thing, and the edge of this is that every one of us in this yard has known since we were children that building was the place you went when it was wet, and none of us has ever been asked to say so out loud, and this district is now going to stand in a yard and put a term on it. A man said no this morning with a reason, and a reason is a thing a person can carry about with them and be asked for again. Eight refusals in this basin have come with no reason at all and a man or a woman who keeps a house gave every one of those and I have never once known what a house has to do with them, and the eight have not moved. Two figures about one refusal is two figures and I am not going to add them together for them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that a reason is a thing a person can be asked for again has not offered anybody his reason and has not been asked for it, and that nobody in that yard asked him what his reason was worth.

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood at the end of that table from about half past one until about four and did not write anything down and did not ask anybody for a figure. He said one thing out loud at about half past four, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A man said no to me in this yard at eleven this morning in about four seconds and he gave a reason, and the reason was about that building and not about a bucket and not about a house, and I am not going to stand here this afternoon and pretend that is a small thing that happened. That is the answer to the question I asked in this yard yesterday afternoon, which nobody answered. It came back the next morning in four seconds from a man who works in the ground. It is not a figure and it is not a price and I am not going to write it on the end of that table, because a table in the open in a yard a stranger can walk into is not a place to write down that a man will not stand in a building in the wet.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal given out loud in a yard is a thing a person said and is not a finding, and that it cannot be counted and cannot be uncounted, and that a man who has heard a refusal and has not been asked what he thinks of it has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane at about two with the bed of it empty and stopped at the trough, and about nine people in that yard got out of his way the way they always do. He was at the end of that table for about four minutes and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not ask him anything at all, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man standing at the end of a table with nothing on it is not a man waiting to be asked and is not a man who has been asked, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the foot of the east wall with his hand-cart against it and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month. He listened to the whole of the afternoon from about nine feet off and said nothing about the refusal, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has lost the use of both hands is not a figure about a refusal and is not a reason for anybody to refuse anything, and that a tent rolled on a hand-cart is not a shelter and is not a document and is not a sixth of the five protected things.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road walked the lane from about three until about four and came back past the end of that table and went up the bank and did not stop, and nobody asked him one question about the eleven miles he walked out of a sound in the seventh month, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who keeps a road is not a bearer of that road and has not been asked to be one and cannot be counted either way.

The rival record has been in force ninety-two days and has been read out in that yard seven times, and its eighth reading was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and a refused reading is not a reading, and it is unanswered. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a refusal given about a building in that yard is not an eighth reading of that record and is not a ninth, and that a bid a district runs on its own building is not a reading of it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the first day of the eighth month is thirty-one days past this morning, and that thirty-one is a figure about a ladder and is not a term and is not a figure about anybody.

The ninth refusals of eleven coppers a week and the seven refusals about the ninth holding and the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given are three figures and are not added together, and eleven coppers a week is a price a man named out loud and is not a rate. Six instruments this district has built and not named, and a refusal is not a seventh. Three documents this district does not own, and a reason given in a yard is not a fourth. Five protected things and no sixth, and a man who will not stand in a building is not a sixth.

The light went at about half past eight and the man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded beside him and nothing in his hands, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was about nine feet off the end of that trestle table and did not go up that bank and did not knock, and a clerk entered that he asked for nothing and that that is entered and is not a finding and is not a refusal.

**AND ON THE SECOND OF THE NINTH MONTH A MAN REFUSED A BID IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS IN THAT YARD AND GAVE A REASON THAT WAS ABOUT THE BID, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT A REFUSAL AND A REASON ARE TWO FIGURES ABOUT ONE THING, AND THAT THE EIGHT REFUSALS WITH NO REASON A CLERK OF A HOUSE HAS GIVEN DID NOT MOVE AND ARE STILL EIGHT, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
