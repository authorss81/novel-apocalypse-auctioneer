# Chapter 372: A Day Built For and Not Used

---

The thirty-first of the ninth month came in white and then grey, and it was the last day of that month and the last day of that run, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten because it is the last day of anything and the yard fills on the last day of anything.

The boards at the end of that trestle table carried four figures this morning and had carried four the day before and the day before that. The man of fifty-six read one of them out loud over the shoulder of the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and got the other three, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said nothing at all this morning, which he does not do.

"**Two hundred and eighty days on that board and five hundred and ninety-six days on the train on the siding, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is a hundred and ninety days, and the table against that wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood eighty-six mornings.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet at that gatepost says four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the term on that reading ended at about six last night and is one day past and did not renew, and that the five lines in the lot book on the end of that trestle table have one line in them and have had one line in them since about two yesterday afternoon, and that nobody was asked to put a second one in and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

Nothing happened on the thirty-first of the ninth month, and it happened the whole day, from about ten in the morning until the light went, and about four people in that yard said afterwards that they had come down that lane expecting a thing and could not say what, and about four other people said that they had not come expecting anything at all, and those are two different things and neither of them is a finding.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a day on which a term ended the evening before is a day and is not a finding, and that thirty days is a figure about the days and is not a figure about the people who spent them.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the end of that trestle table from about ten until about twelve with his left hand in its cloth and his right hand empty, and there was nothing for him to read. He has stood at that table most mornings of that run and said the other half of whatever somebody has just said, and this morning nobody said anything that wanted an other half, and he did not go and make one up, and he did not leave either.

About four people in that yard noticed him standing there with nothing to do and one of them said out loud that there must be something in the book for him to read, and he said out loud that there was one line in it and he had read it at two yesterday afternoon and he was not going to read it again to fill the morning.

The man of about nineteen who counts what people say in daylight yards came down that lane at about eleven with his own page under his arm and stood about nine feet off the end of that trestle table, and he did not count anything at all today. Not the figures, not the four, not the sentences anybody said in that yard, not the man of fifty-six reading off the boards. He stood there until about half past twelve and then he went up the lane and did not come back before the light went, and he did not say to anybody that he was not going to count, and two or three of them said afterwards that they had not known he did anything but count.

A man who has not counted a thing has not refused to count it and goes in nobody's count on either side, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about it, and that is what the record says about him today.

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came down that lane about one in the afternoon.

He had not stood in that yard since the twenty-ninth of the eighth month, and he came down that lane on the last day of the ninth month and stood at the end of that trestle table with a flat book under his arm and the sleeves of his coat turned back, and he shifted his weight from one boot to the other about as often as a man does when he has come a long way on the strength of something that is not going to happen.

Nobody asked him for his figure. He did not offer it. He stood there until about half past two, looked at the one line in the lot book standing up the way anybody can, and said out loud that a line is a line and that he keeps six households and that is all he has come to say, and half of them said good afternoon to him and he said good afternoon back to those and to nobody else, and he went back up the lane the way he had come.

The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved on any of the days since the twenty-first of the fourth month, and that figure came out of a ladder and not out of his mouth this afternoon, and he was not asked for it. A man who walks into a yard and looks at a book and goes away again is a fact about a yard and is not a finding about the man.

---

At about half past two the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing took the wire out of the right pocket of his coat, which is lighter than it was this morning because he gave nine inches of it to rule that book with yesterday afternoon, and went down the lane to the gate at the head of the loam. About four people in that yard watched him go and not one of them said anything about where he was going.

The gate at the head of the loam has nine feet of wire in it with a knot at the top of it, and about three feet of wire in the latch of the second gate beside it, and both of them went there by a man who was not asked and did not offer, and the gate has been shut and opened on ordinary days since and has not been looked at by anybody in this district on any of them. There is no figure on any piece of paper in this district that either of those could be paid out of, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid and the count is still five.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures off those boards again at about half past two into a yard that had about half the people standing in it that it had at ten, and got the same four, and nobody asked him to read them again and he did not say that he had read them before. About four people in that yard said the second one to each other behind him and got it right.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and was not in that yard at any point on this day, and four or five others there said that he would be in the yard on one of the other days of this week, and nobody in that yard said what for.

---

The man who digs loam came in at about half past three and went out at about four and read the one line in that book off the boards with his right arm held down at his side and said nothing to anybody, and nobody asked him anything.

The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about four with his boots black to the ankle, read the four figures off the boards of that trestle table as he went past, got all four, and went on up the lane, and nobody in that yard asked him to and nobody in that yard asked him about the eleven miles.

Between about two and about four that yard held about six people and nothing else that was not already standing in it. The boards at the end of that trestle table were four inches from the edge of the lane and those six went past them about nine times in two hours, and about four of the nine went past without looking at them, and nobody in that yard said one word to anybody about anything at all, and the light came off the roof of that building about a foot at a time and went down the east wall.

A man who keeps a road is not a bearer of it and has never been asked to be one, and the eleven miles of that road have been on the page in a lane since the twenty-sixth of last month and the man they belong to is not on any page at all, and about four people in that yard said today that they had stopped expecting him to stop and about four of them said they had not, and neither of those is a finding.

The woman of fifty-eight was at the trough at about half past four with two empty buckets and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself. She read the one line in that book standing up on the way, the way she read the page under the stone before it, and she said nothing, and nobody in that yard asked her anything.

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the east wall of that building with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and he was not asked for a table by anybody in that yard on the last day of a run, because there was nothing for a table to be for. He said out loud that a table is for two people who want to lean on something and that this yard has not wanted that today.

The man who cuts reeds was in the bottom of that ditch with his hook and his bundle until the light went off the low ground, and the ditch is about three feet deep with about a foot of standing water in it, and he came up the bank past that door without saying one word about the building, and nobody in that yard asked him.

The page with the twenty-first of the ninth month on it was still on the end of that trestle table under a stone, and it had been there ten days, and about nine people in that yard had read the top line of it standing up and about four of them had read the whole of it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, will come round in a ledger tomorrow and will not be paid, and the count is still five, and nobody in that yard said one word about that either.

Along that lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and he was there when that yard filled in the morning and he was there when it emptied, and three or four of the others said goodnight to one another on their way out and said it to him as well this time, and he said it back.

The day went off about half past six. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet at that gatepost is still four hundred and eleven, and that the term on that reading is one day past and has not renewed, and that the reading of that lot is begun and is not finished, and that the bid is open thirty days and was not run on one of them and that nobody proposed anything about closing it, and that there is a book on the end of that trestle table with five ruled lines in it and one line written in it, and that this district built a thing for this morning and has not used it.
