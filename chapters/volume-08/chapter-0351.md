# Chapter 351: A Figure About a Lot

---

The tenth of the ninth month came in with the first cold of that run that got through a coat and stayed in it, and by ten the yard of Lot Seventeen had about nineteen people in it and about four more than the day before it. The three figures were on the end of the trestle table where they had been every morning of that run. The man of fifty-six read the middle one of the three out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other two, and the mender did not turn round. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the fifty-first night of his run and fifty days in this district.

"**Two hundred and fifty-nine days on that board and five hundred and seventy-five days on the train on the siding, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and forty-two days back, and the table against that wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood sixty-five mornings.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move this morning, and that its own age as a figure about that figure is a hundred days, and that a figure about a figure is not a day-count for a sheet and is not a promise about a later day, and that the rival record has been in force a hundred days and is unanswered, and that a record in force and a figure's own age are two figures counted off one day and are not one figure.

---

The reading began at about half past ten and the man who convened the bid was the one who said it out loud, in the ordinary voice, in daylight, with about nineteen people in that yard and the wind off the low ground going through all of them.

He had said the same description of the same building on the first of this month at about six in the evening and had said it three times to himself before that, and this time he was slower and he got to the end of it, and when he came to the end he did not stop, which is the thing a man cannot teach himself to do.

The man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and fifty-eight, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A lot is a thing a bid is for and I am describing the same one again and it is the same lot. About nine feet of brick at the near end of the lane behind that bank, no window in it, a plank door in the end wall, a nail in a hasp that has not moved since the eleventh of the sixth month, a curtain on a nail, a bench, a stool, two buckets of water, a table with four legs against the outside wall, and a ditch behind it about three feet deep with about a foot of standing water in it. That is the lot and I have not read a figure off it in the nine days it has been open, and I am going to read one off it this morning, and I want everybody in this yard to hear where I got it from before I say it. It came out of the water. That woman has carried two buckets a day into that building for nine years, and two a day for nine years is about six and a half thousand bucketfuls, and every one of them went through that door. That is a figure about the lot. It is not a figure about her, and I have not asked her for it, and I am not going to, and if I had asked her for it and she had said a figure it would have been a different thing and it would not have been worth standing here for.**"

The yard was quiet for about four seconds. The woman of fifty-eight was in it, at the trough side with a full bucket in each hand, and she had come down that bank at about half past nine and had not come further in. She put the two buckets down one after the other, which took her longer than it usually takes her, and then she stood there with her hands empty and did not say one word.

About nine people in that yard looked at her and then at the man the figure of twenty-one years is against. One of them was the man of fifty-six and he did not look at her at all, he looked at the three figures on the end of that trestle table, and then he read the middle one of the three out loud again on his own and got it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure about a lot said out loud in a yard in daylight is a figure about the lot and is not a figure about anybody standing in that yard, and that a figure may be arrived at from a person's work and still be a figure about a thing, and that the person it was arrived at from was not asked for it and the record about the not asking says not asked. She entered that a woman who has been in the sound of a figure being said about her own carrying has not agreed to the figure and has not refused it and cannot be counted either way.

---

He went on to the term, because the term was the next thing and he had decided in the lane on the way down that he would not stop in the middle.

He said out loud that the term on that lot was a month, and that the figure of it was a month, and that the reason it was a month had been entered in a book four hundred yards up that bank on the first of this month and had not been argued with since. Then he stopped again, in the middle of a yard, on purpose this time, with about nineteen people standing there and the wind going through it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the term on the lot is a month, and that the figure of the term came off the reading this morning in the ordinary voice in a yard, and that the reading of the lot is begun and is not finished, and that the reading has got as far as the term and has not got to the three other things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and that a clerk entered the day at the end of that term out of a ladder on the day the term was set and has not said it out loud to anybody, and that a ladder is a figure about a ladder.

He said one more thing out loud at about a quarter to eleven, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and what he said was counted and got a hundred and thirty-four, and the man of about nineteen had it in his own words.

"**I said on the first of this month that a reading is a thing said out loud and then read back from the top by somebody who is not the man who said it, and a thing read back wrong is read back again from the top and not from the word. That is the whole of it and there is no second half to it that anybody can do on their own. There is nobody in this yard reading it back this morning and I did not ask one, because the second person has to be a person and there is no figure on any page in this district for who. So this morning I said a figure about a lot out loud in front of about nineteen people and it binds nobody.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything back. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was in it until about eleven, four days in a week he is out on that road and this was one of the other three, and nobody in that yard asked him to do anything at all while he was standing in it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The rest of the morning went the way that morning went. The mender bent wire at the end of that table with the fingers of his right hand and the cloth still on his left, and there was about nine feet of it done and a knot in it about the length of a finger, and nobody in that yard asked him what the wire was for. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came into that yard at about one with a bar of iron he had not come to use and went out again without saying anything, and the woman of fifty-eight carried her two buckets up the two goes of the bank at about a quarter to one, on her own, the way she has carried them for nineteen years.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about two, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people. The count came to a hundred and sixty-five, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**That is the first figure anybody has said out loud in this yard about a thing that is not a fence or a bucket, and he got it out of the water that went into that building, and he did not ask the woman who carried it. I want to say the other half of it so that nobody in this yard goes home with the first half. A figure said out loud about a thing in daylight in front of people is not a price and is not a toll and is not a rate, and it does not bind anybody, and I have been in this basin since I was nine and I have never heard anybody say one out loud about a thing on purpose. I do not know what it is worth and neither does he and neither does anybody standing here, and I am not going to be the man in this yard who tells them it is a step.**"

A figure that is said out loud about a lot and is not a toll is not a rate, and a rate is a thing that is already true of you whether anybody says it or not, and a figure that is to be completed is not a rate. Nobody in that yard put a figure on the toll of that lot, and nobody proposed one, and a figure somebody might complete later is not a rate today.

---

At about four the light came off the low ground and went hard, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood about nine feet off the end of that trestle table with his right hand in his coat and his left hanging at his side, and about nine people came and went behind him and none of them said anything to him. He did not go up that bank and he did not knock and he did not send anybody.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane at about five and went into that yard and stood at the end of that table for about a quarter of an hour without saying a word, and nobody asked him one question about eleven miles, and he went out again. The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the brick at the foot of the east wall with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and nobody asked him about either of those things.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid this morning and the count is still five, and a bid in a yard on ten days is not a market, and the word market is not on anybody's page this month except in a clerk's denial of it.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank again at about half past five with two empty buckets and stood at the foot of it with them for about four minutes, and she did not ask anybody anything, and nobody asked her anything, and about nine people in that yard watched her stand there and not ask.

The light went at about half past eight. The bid was still open and had not been called for again since the first of this month, and the reading of the lot had got as far as the term. The man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody in any yard this month has said one word to him about what he is going to do with them, and a man whose trade is over is not a failure and is not a tragic figure and is not redeemed, and a clerk may not say that about him and may not say the other thing either. The number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**A FIGURE WAS SAID OUT LOUD ABOUT A BUILDING IN THAT YARD AND NOBODY IN THIS BASIN HAD EVER HEARD ONE SAID ABOUT A THING ON PURPOSE, AND IT CAME OUT OF THE WATER, AND THE WOMAN WHO CARRIED THE WATER STOOD THERE WITH HER HANDS EMPTY AND SAID NOTHING, AND A THING READ BACK BY NOBODY BINDS NOBODY.**
