# Chapter 356: Thirty-One

---

The fifteenth of the ninth month came in clear and hard with the first of the light on the roof of that building, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten and the lane was busy both ways with carts. The three figures were on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read the middle one of the three out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other two. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the fifty-sixth night of his run and fifty-five days in this district, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and sixty-four days on that board and five hundred and eighty days on the train on the siding, and the rival record has been in force a hundred and five days, and the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved for a hundred and forty-six days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that she did not ask the man of about forty-eight for that figure at any point this morning and did not go and look for him, and that his figure came out of a ladder, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a ladder is a figure about a ladder.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the question at about half past ten, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people. What he said was counted and got a hundred and fifty-two, and the man of about nineteen had it in his own words.

"**I have got the term of that lot in my head as a month and I have had it in my head since the first of this month and I cannot find the end of this month anywhere in what I know. I am not going to count that board. She counted that board once, on the thirtieth of the last month, with both days named out loud while she did it, and what she got is the only counted length of a month this district has got. I am asking her, in this yard, in front of all of you, how long the ninth month is, and I want her to say the day she counted from and the day she counted to, because the last time anybody said a month out loud in this yard they said eight of them and nobody in this district has ever said this one.**"

The clerk of nineteen years was about nine feet off with the day's page under her arm and she did not answer him straight away. She got a pencil out of her coat and looked at it and then put it back, which is a thing she does when she is deciding whether a thing is going to be a page or not.

She said one thing out loud, and the man of about nineteen counted what she said and got a hundred and seventy-nine, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**Thirty-one, and here is where it comes from and it is not out of a list. I counted the marks on that board from the mark for the first of the eighth month to this morning's mark on the thirtieth of the last month and I got thirty for the eighth month, and I named both days while I was counting because a count with no day named in it is a count of something. The ninth month is the month after the eighth and this month began on the first of the ninth, which is a day this district has a figure for because a bid was opened in this yard on it at about six in the evening, and the month that followed the eighth is one day longer than the eighth. Thirty, and one more than thirty, is thirty-one. I am entering that and I am not standing here asserting it. A month nobody has said was short is a month nobody has checked, and I am not going to say that it has been checked.**"

Nobody in that yard moved for about four seconds. The man of fifty-six asked her whether she would say it again and she said no, and said it in the ordinary voice, and said that she had said them once in that yard, on the thirtieth of the last month, and was not going to do it again, and that she had written it on a page four hundred yards up that bank and that anybody who wanted to check it could come and check it, and that a person who does not want to come and check a figure is not a person being lied to.

About four people in that yard looked up at that bank when she said where the page was. None of them went up it. The bank is two goes with a trough at the bottom of it and there is a house at the top of it with a shut door, and about four people stood and looked at it and then looked at the ground, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and nobody in that yard asked the clerk to bring the page down and she did not offer to.

The man of fifty-six asked her, in front of about nine people, to write that one figure again on a line she could put on the end of that trestle table, and she wrote it there in a hand about half the size of the three that were already on the boards, and he read it back off the wood himself before he went off to the fence end of the lane.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the ninth month has thirty-one days, and that the figure is arrived at from a count of marks off a board from the first of the eighth to the thirtieth of the eighth, with both days named, and that the ninth is one day longer than the eighth because the eighth has thirty, and that she entered the figure and did not assert it, and that the page it is on is four hundred yards from that gate and a mark on a page is not a line on a board.

The man of fifty-six said one thing out loud at about a quarter to twelve, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and the count came to a hundred and twenty-eight, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**Fifteen days I have been reading three figures off that table every morning and never once asked whether the year they are counted in has a shape. There is a figure on that page now for how long this month is and I am the man who reads figures out loud in this yard and I had not asked for it, and that is not a finding about her and it is not a finding about me. I am going to go and read it off the table tomorrow morning along with the other three, and anybody who wants to hear a fourth figure read out loud in this yard can stand there tomorrow and hear it, and I am not going to be asked anything about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he had not asked for a figure has not refused one and cannot be counted either way, and that four figures read out loud over the edge of one table in a morning is a figure about that table and is not a finding about the men who read them, and that the fourth of them was not read out loud this morning and will be on the table in the morning whatever anybody does about it.

---

The mender did not take the figure. He counted it.

He did it standing at the end of that trestle table at about eleven, with the fingers of his right hand on the edge of the boards, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and it took him about nine minutes, and nobody helped him and nobody in that yard asked him what he was doing. He got to thirty-one and he stopped with his hand where it was.

Then the man of fifty-six said it out loud after him, once, and got the same figure, and about nine people in that yard heard two men say the same number in a yard inside about four minutes of each other.

The clerk of nineteen years wrote the whole of that down in one line while the two of them were still standing at the table, and then she read it back to herself with her lips not moving, the way she does, and about four people in that yard watched a woman of nineteen finish a line in about nine seconds. It says that two men counting one figure out loud in a yard in one morning and arriving at the same number is two figures about a count and is neither of them a finding. It says that a man getting a figure right is not a finding and not a habit and not a figure about the man who got it right, and that a man who got one figure right once was entered on the twenty-ninth of the eighth as not a finding either, and that the two of those are two figures about two days and are not added together. And it says that nine minutes of a hand on the edge of a table is a figure about nine minutes and is not about the man.

Then the mender asked about the next month, and the clerk of nineteen years said the thing that made the rest of that morning.

She said that this district has one counted length of a month and it is the one she counted, and that for every other month of that year the length came off the month before it, and that the month after this one would be thirty on that method, because the month before it has thirty-one, and that she was going to enter that on the page with the day it was taken from, and that she was not going to stand in that yard and check a month that has not started and cannot be checked by anybody standing in it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the month after this one has thirty days on the figure this district has used for it, which is the figure of the month before it, and that a figure taken off the month before it is a figure about a list and is not a figure about a date, and that nobody in this district has checked it and that she is not going to check a month that has not started, and that no day of this month or the next stands on the month after that and no figure in this district may use a length for it, because a day this district cannot name is a day it cannot count to the end of.

---

The rest of that day did what the rest of those days did. The reading of the lot is begun and not finished and the man who convened the bid was not at the end of that trestle table at any point in the afternoon and nobody in that yard asked him why.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came in at about one and went out at about half past one and said nothing to anybody. The man who keeps a road came through at about two and stood at the end of that table with his back to it and read the four figures off the boards over his own shoulder without saying anything to anybody, which is a thing he has done once before this month, and getting a figure right twice is not a habit and is not a finding and is not a figure about the man who got it right.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past four with two empty buckets and filled them at the trough and put them inside that building, and she stood at the foot of the bank for about four minutes with about nine people in that yard and she did not ask anybody anything and nobody asked her anything.

At about half past seven the yard was down to about four people and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat beside him, and the man who convened the bid was about nine feet off the end of that table and did not go up that bank and did not knock and did not ask for anything from anybody in this district on this day.

The light went off that yard at about half past eight. The number on that sheet was still four hundred and eleven. The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and forty-seven days back and nobody in this district can say whether that night happened, and a plank does not keep nights.

**A MONTH WAS SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD AND NAMED FROM THE DAY IT WAS COUNTED FROM, AND A MAN COUNTED IT AGAIN ON THE EDGE OF A TABLE WITH ONE HAND AND GOT THE SAME FIGURE, AND THE MONTH AFTER THAT ONE WAS ENTERED OFF THE MONTH BEFORE IT BY A CLERK WHO SAID OUT LOUD THAT SHE WAS NOT CHECKING IT.**
