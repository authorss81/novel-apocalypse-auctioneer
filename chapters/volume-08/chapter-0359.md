# Chapter 359: What a Road Is For

---

The eighteenth of the ninth month came in with the last of that warm spell in it and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, standing about in the shade of that building because it was the only shade in that yard. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the top one of the four out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other three. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the fifty-ninth night of his run and fifty-eight days in this district, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and sixty-seven days on that board and the days nobody has entered anything since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and ninety-seven, and two hundred and fifty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning, and nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and seventieth days since the first of the fourth month, counting that first day in.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that a bid was opened in that yard on the first of the ninth month at about six in the evening and has been open seventeen days, and that a figure about how long a bid has stood is a figure about a bid and is not a figure about anybody standing in that yard, and that nobody in that yard proposed anything this morning about closing it.

---

Nobody proposed anything about closing it, and about four people in that yard said so out loud to one another in the ordinary way, and the mender said that a bid that nobody has proposed to close is not a bid that has failed, and the man of fifty-six said that it was a bid that had not been run, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that those were two different things and that he did not know which one it was, and about four other people in that yard said nothing at all.

A bid a district runs on its own building is not a market, and a sheet at a gate in twenty days is not a market, and the word market is on nobody's page this month except in a clerk's denial of it, and a yard is a room with no roof and a yard is where a thing gets said out loud.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have was said out loud in that yard for the third time in a fortnight this morning, by the person who convened the bid, and it is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and it is still not paid, and the count is still five, and a district that has built an instrument and is using it every morning and cannot pay anybody in it is a district with a thing that works and nobody in it.

What the four figures on the end of that trestle table are for got said out loud four times that morning in about nine minutes, and the four answers were not the same and none of them was wrong.

The mender said that they were for knowing what day it was, and that that was what he had used them for every morning of that run and that he had not known it was a use until somebody asked. The man of fifty-six said that they were for a man standing at a table to have something in front of him that he could not argue with. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam, from the top of that ditch, said that they were for a stranger, because a stranger could walk up to that table and read four numbers off the end of it and go away again, and that nothing else in this district could be walked up to and read. The man who convened the bid said that they were for nothing, and that he had put the idea of them there in the first week of this month, and that a thing a man has made for nothing is a thing a man has made for nothing.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that four men said four things out loud about what four figures on a table are for and that a figure about what an instrument is for is a figure about the instrument and is not a finding about the men, and that a thing a man says he has made for nothing is a thing made for nothing and is not a thing owed to anybody, and that an instrument this district uses every morning and has no way to pay anybody for using is not a failure and is not a market.

Nobody in that yard agreed with anybody else. The man of about nineteen counted the four answers and got four figures where he had expected one number of them, and he wrote the four down and did not add them together, and a figure that is a count of what people said in a yard is a count of the people who said it and is not a count of the thing.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane at about eleven and went and stood in that yard, and he had been out on the road since before the light and his boots were black to the ankle, and nobody in that yard asked him a question and he did not wait to be asked.

He said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and twenty-seven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A road is not for the people who know it. That is the whole of what I have come down here to say and I have said it in this yard because a yard is where a thing gets said out loud. Eleven miles of it from the gate past the loams and along that lane, and I know where every yard of it goes and what is at the end of it, and that is no good to anybody, because knowing where a road goes is not holding it. A road is for somebody who has not been on it. A man comes down that lane he has never walked, in the dark, in the wet, and he has to be able to find out where it goes without asking me, because I am not going to be standing at the end of it every time for the rest of my life, and I have not been asked to and I am not offering. I have never held that road and nobody in this yard has ever handed it to me, and if a document came along with my name on it for that road I would tear it up, and I am saying that in daylight so that there is a minute in this yard where a man said it before anybody thought of it.**"

Nobody in that yard moved for about four seconds. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that he had been in that yard every morning of that run and had not thought of it, and the man of fifty-six said nothing, and the man who convened the bid said nothing, and the woman of fifty-eight was at the trough side with a full bucket in each hand and looked up for about two seconds and then looked back down at the water.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who knows where a road goes is not holding it, and that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he has never held it and would tear up a document that said he had has not refused a post and cannot be counted either way, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a road is not a lot and a lot is not a road and the two are not added together, and that a person standing in a doorway is not holding the door and a person standing at a gate is not holding the gate.

At about four men in that yard said, one after another and in the ordinary way, that a road a stranger could walk up to was the thing this district had made that nobody had thought about, and the man who keeps it said the same four words to all four of them in about four seconds each time, which was that he was not going to be asked about it, and he went out of that yard and up the lane, and nobody sent anybody after him.

The same answer given four times in an afternoon is a shape noticed four times and is not a finding and is not a habit, and saying a thing out loud in a yard before anybody has thought of it is a thing a man has said and not a document, and a thing said in a yard cannot be carried away and cannot be read back from the top by anybody and therefore binds nobody, and that is not a fault in the yard and is the way a yard is.

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the top of that ditch at about two with a bar of iron in his right hand held down at his side, and he was not at the end of that trestle table and did not go to it, and about four people in that yard who were talking about the rule that came into a room on the thirteenth of this month came out to the ditch to say it to him, and he listened to all four of them standing at the top of that bank with his iron in his hand and then said one thing back to the four of them.

He said it in about four seconds, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got ninety-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**A rule that costs a man nothing to keep is a rule nobody can be made to keep, and if a thing only holds while somebody reads it again, then what it actually is, is a thing that one man is carrying for a district, and it is going to keep being that whatever is written down about it. I am not saying that to be clever. I am saying it because a man cut nine feet of wire this month for nobody and I have not asked him for one inch of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud that a rule nobody can be made to keep is a thing one man is carrying has not refused the rule and cannot be counted either way, and that a rule that costs nothing is a rule nobody can be made to keep, and that the wire is not a lot and is not a toll and is not a figure about anybody, and that the man who bent it was not asked for it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The rest of that afternoon was the yard doing what it does. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was in the bottom of it until about four. The man who puts tables up stood at the foot of the east wall with his hand-cart against the brick and the tent rolled on the back of it, and nobody asked him anything. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and was four miles off before the light came up, and nobody in that yard said one word about whether he would be in it in the evening.

The reading of the lot stands at the third of five things a document that sets a thing out has to say and has not got to the fourth of them, which is a person, and it is not finished, and no name went into the column that is ruled and empty on any of the days of that run.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself, and she did not ask anybody anything and nobody in that yard asked her anything.

The light had gone off that yard by about half past eight. The man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded beside him and his hands where he keeps them, and nobody in any yard of this month has said one word to him about what he is going to do with them. The number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, the bid in that yard has been open seventeen days, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is still not paid and the count is still five.

**A MAN WHO KEEPS A ROAD SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD THAT A ROAD IS FOR SOMEBODY WHO HAS NOT BEEN ON IT, AND THAT KNOWING WHERE A ROAD GOES IS NOT HOLDING IT, AND HE HAS NEVER HELD IT AND NOBODY ASKED HIM TO, AND THE BID IN THAT YARD IS STILL OPEN AND NOBODY PROPOSED ANYTHING ABOUT CLOSING IT.**
