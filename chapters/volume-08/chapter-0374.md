# Chapter 374: A Room With Two Doors and No Answer In It

---

The second of the tenth month came in with rain that came and went three times before ten and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, four of them in out of it. The man of fifty-six read the top one of the four figures on the boards at the end of the trestle table out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other three, and then he went off up that lane to the fence end without stopping, which he does most mornings.

"**The rival record has been in force a hundred and twenty-three days and the figure about that figure is a hundred and twenty-three days, and those are two figures and not one figure. The six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved for a hundred and sixty-four days, and the first day of the eighth month is sixty-two days past this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the rival record and the age of the figure about that figure are counted off one day and one exclusion and land on one interval, and that a record in force and a figure's own age are not the same object even when they age alike, and that a clerk may not print one of them as the other.

The rest of that morning before eleven went the way it goes. The man who digs loam was in the bottom of that ditch with a bar in his right hand held down at his side and worked his way along it and came up the bank at about ten and went out of that yard. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in the same ditch with his hook and his bundle and came up past that door and did not look in.

The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the east wall with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and nobody in that yard asked him about it and he was given nothing.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about ten with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, carried them up the two goes herself, and read the two pages lying under that stone standing up on her way past, which is the page with the twenty-first of the ninth month on it and the page a boy put down there yesterday, and she said nothing about either of them and nobody in that yard said anything to her.

---

At about eleven the man the figure of twenty-one years is against went into that building with two doors and left the plank door open behind him, and about four minutes later the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went in after him with his left hand in its cloth, and the door stayed open, and nobody in that yard said one word about either of them going in.

They were in there from about eleven until about half past two. What they said in there was said in a room about nine feet by eleven with a bench about nine feet long against one wall, a stool, two buckets of water, a table with four legs against the outside wall, a curtain on a nail and about nine feet of brick and no window, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing in it, and a man of about nineteen who was standing at the end of that trestle table with his own page under his arm counted what he said and got two hundred and eight, standing at the open door of that building.

"**You said a thing out loud in this yard on the twentieth of this month and it has been eleven days on the page and nobody has done one thing about it since, and I said it and not you, and I have not done one thing about it either. I have stood at the end of that table every morning of that run saying the other half of whatever somebody has just said, and that is what I am for, and on the four days a week that man with the cart is out on that road, I am the other half of nothing, because there is no other half of anything until somebody has said the first half, and on those four days nobody says the first half. You are the man who convenes things. You told me on the twenty-ninth that you were not going to write a rule about it and not going to ask him and not going to name anybody, and I have thought about that for eleven days and you are right and I want it said that you are right, and I am still not able to do one thing about it, and I do not know anybody who can.**"

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing back in that room at about twenty past twelve, and the boy counted what he said and got two hundred and three, from the open door as well.

"**You are right and I have been right for eleven days and neither of us has moved, and that is the whole of what a room is for. I am not going to write it down, because a rule nobody can be made to keep is a thing one man is carrying, and you said that at the top of that ditch and it is true, and if I write it down it will be a page with a thing on it and a page with a thing on it is not a rule, it is a thing somebody said once. I am not going to ask that man with a cart to read a thing back from the top. If I ask him, he will say yes, because he says yes, and then it will be one man saying yes to another man in a yard, and that is worse than four days in seven, because it will look like it works. And I am not going to name him to it, in a yard or in a room, and I am not going to name anybody to anything, and the man with the cart does not know I am in here.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a reversal was said out loud in that yard on the twentieth of this month and has been on the page eleven days, and that it was dealt with this morning in a room and was not settled, and that nothing was said in that room that either of those two men has not already said in that yard.

---

About nine people in that yard came to that open door over that three hours and about four of them stood at it long enough to hear what was being said in there and about four of those four went away again, and nobody in that yard said that a room is a yard, and nobody in that yard said that what was said in that room had been said out loud.

At about one the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up the bank past that door with his hook in his hand and the door was open and both of them were standing in there, and he went past and did not stop and did not say one word about it, and about four people in that yard said afterwards that they had seen them in there through the door and that it had not looked like a row.

About four minutes after the mender had gone in, the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had come back out and stood at the end of that trestle table for about four minutes and then gone in again, and about four people in that yard said that he had gone in twice and not one of them said why.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing about the two doors while they were in there, and the count of what he said came to eighty-one.

"**There are two doors on that building and there has been a curtain on a nail on the inside of one of them since the eleventh of the sixth month, and a yard is the only place in this district where a thing gets said out loud, and I have used this room twice this month and both times I have said less in it than I would have said outside it, and that is not a fault of the room.**"

Two doors on a building are two doors and are not a second person and are not a second reader, and a person standing in a doorway is not holding the door, and the cost of a thing being dealt with in a room on this morning is that it was not dealt with at all and the yard has a figure going round it that is wrong by four.

At about one, with both of them in that building, the second of the four figures on the end of that trestle table was said out loud in that yard by a man at the back and was wrong.

It was wrong by four, and two or three people heard it, and about four of them said it after him in that yard over the next half hour, and it went on going round that yard that way until about half past two. The man of about nineteen was standing at the end of that trestle table about nine feet from the boards with his own page under his arm, and he heard it said four times and did not say one word, and nobody in that yard had asked him to say one word, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six was up that lane at the fence end. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was in a room. The man who keeps a road was in that yard and heard it and looked at the boards and looked away again and did not say anything, and nobody asked him about the eleven miles or about anything else, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past two the man the figure of twenty-one years is against and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came out of that building together and stood about nine feet off the end of that trestle table, and the second of the four figures was said out loud in that yard again while they were standing there, wrong by the same four.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing listened to it going round, and he did not go to the boards and he did not say the figure, and about nine people in that yard watched a man who reads figures every morning of a run hear one said wrong and not say it, and none of them said anything to him about it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the end of that trestle table and let the wrong figure go round that yard three more times while he was standing there, and about nine people watched a man who reads figures out loud every morning of a run hear one said wrong and not say it, and none of them said anything to him about it, and he has not gone back to it since.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who catches a mistake and has not been asked to catch one is not a reader and is not a keeper of anything and is nobody's clerk, and that the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was empty at the end of this day, and that a mistake said out loud in a yard and not stopped is a mistake and is not a finding.

Nobody in that yard was told the figure by anybody, and nobody in that yard was asked anything about it, and the record of it says not asked.

The rest of that afternoon went past without either of those two men going back into that building. The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about four with his boots black to the ankle, read the four figures off the boards as he went past, got all four, and went on up the lane, and nobody in that yard asked him to. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and was not in that yard at any point today.

The man of fifty-six came back down that lane at about five and half of them told him, one after another and in the ordinary way, that the second of the four figures had been going round wrong all afternoon, and he listened to all four of them and did not say what the figure was, and four or five others there noticed him not saying it and said nothing about that either.

About half past six the last person standing at that end of the table said the second of the four figures out loud one more time, to nobody, with the boards in front of him, and it was wrong by the same four, and the boards said what they have said every morning of that run and the yard said what it said.
