# Chapter 379: An Ordinary Day at the Foot of a Low Wall

---

The seventh of the tenth month came in with the first of the light straight down that lane and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The man of fifty-six read the top one of the four figures on the boards at the end of that trestle table out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other three, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at that table with his left hand in its cloth and both his boots through with the wet of the fourth gate.

"**The rival record has been in force a hundred and twenty-eight days and the age of the figure about that figure is a hundred and twenty-eight days, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and seventy days back, and nobody has put a line on a board in a hundred and ninety days counting the first of the fourth month in.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the rival record and the age of the figure about that figure are two figures counted off one day and one exclusion and are not added together, and that a record in force is not a finding and the ninth of the nine printed nights is still open and was named in that yard this morning and closed in none of them.

Along that lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the seventy-eighth night of his run and seventy-eight days in this district, and he has slept on that low wall on seventy-seven of the seventy-eight nights of that run and did not sleep on the one of the twenty-first of the eighth month, and there was nothing in his hands.

The four figures on those boards were the same four as yesterday and the day before that, and the man of fifty-six went off up that lane to the fence end at about ten without stopping, which he does most mornings, and the yard got on with the day without him at that end of the table and about four people in that yard said the top one of the four to each other and got it right.

---

Nothing about that was said out loud in that yard today by anybody, and that is the whole of the day and it is not a finding and it is not a habit.

Nobody asked him anything. Nobody offered him anything. Nobody said one word to him about his hands, and nobody said one word to him about the seventy-eight days, and nobody said one word to him about the night of the twenty-first of the eighth, and about four people in that yard said good morning to him as they came down the lane and about four of them said good morning to each other instead. The record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a stay is a figure about a stay and is not a figure about a man, and that he is not a keeper of a lot and not a bearer of the reading and not the second reader and not a post and not a steward and not a job nobody asked him for, and that a clerk is not going to say what he is going to do with his hands because nobody asked her.

The rest of that day was ordinary and two or three of them said afterwards that it had been an ordinary day, and half of them said that it had not been ordinary and could not say what had been different about it, and neither of those is a finding.

At about one, at the end of that trestle table, two people had a short conversation about the man of about sixty-four that was not about his hands and was not said to him. One of them said out loud that it was not right that a man had sat at the foot of that wall for seventy-eight days and nobody had asked him one thing, and the other one said out loud that there was nothing to give him, and both of them said it quietly enough that he could hear it and neither of them looked at him while they said it, and he heard both of those and neither of them was about him.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two people in that yard said a thing out loud about a man at the foot of that wall at about one this afternoon and that what they said was not said to him, and that nobody gave that man a thing, and that a thing a person is not given is not a thing a person has refused, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the four figures out loud at about one and at about half past three, the second one from the top both times, and four or five of them said the top one of the four to each other behind him and got it right both times.

The man who digs loam was in the bottom of that ditch with a bar in his right hand held down at his side, before nine and until about ten, and again from about two until about half past four, and he said out loud to a few people who asked him whether the water in that ditch was up that it was the same as it had been all month and that he had not measured it and was not going to.

The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about eleven with his boots black to the ankle and again at about four, read the four figures off the boards both times as he went past, got all four both times, and went on up the lane both times, and nobody in that yard asked him about the eleven miles on either occasion, and nobody was asked and the record says so on both occasions.

The man who cuts reeds was in that ditch from about nine until about half past four with his hook and his bundle, and came up the bank past that door without saying one word about the building.

About four people in that yard started to say something to the man of about sixty-four between about eleven and about four and did not finish it, and three or four more of them started and did finish it and what they finished it with was the weather, and the record of it says not asked and the record about the weather says the weather.

What the reading of that lot would take to finish is a person and a remedy, and this district has neither of those, and as many again said that today at about half past four as though they were talking about something else, and nobody in that yard said the word reading at any point in the whole of this day, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the reading of that lot is begun and is not finished and that nobody in that yard said so today and that a clerk saying it is not the same as anybody in that yard saying it.

At the foot of the east wall the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart set against that wall with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and he was not asked about it and he was given nothing, and a few of them said that a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them is a man this yard ought to be using this week, and he said that there is nothing this week to put a table under, and that was the whole of what he said about it.

The man of about nineteen was at the end of that trestle table with his own page under his arm from about ten until the light went, and he counted four things out loud in that yard today, all four of them figures, and he wrote all four of them down in his own hand, and he put nothing else on that page. The first of the four was the man of fifty-six reading the figures off those boards at ten, and he counted it and got fifty-two.

"**Two hundred and eighty-seven days on that board and six hundred and three days on the train on the siding, and the days nobody has entered anything since the twenty-fourth of November stand at three hundred and seventeen, and two hundred and seventy-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

He wrote that one down as well, and the other three he counted were said by other people at other ends of that yard, and he wrote all four of them down, and two or three of them said that a man who writes down every figure anybody says is going to fill that page, and he said that it is his page and that nobody has asked him to stop and nobody was asked and the record says so.

Nothing was proposed in that yard today about the bid, which is open and was not run, and nothing was proposed about the term, which is eight days past and did not renew, and nothing was proposed about the reading, which stands where it stopped, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six and nothing went into it by anybody, and the lot book on the end of that trestle table has five ruled lines in it and one line written in it, and nobody in that yard asked for a second line in it.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in that yard from about ten until about five and said two short things out loud, one at about eleven about a page and one at about five about a gate, and nobody counted either of them and nobody has claimed either of them since.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough and carried them up the two goes herself, and on the way up she read the two pages under that stone and the one line in that lot book standing up, the way anybody can, and she said nothing to anybody and nobody in that yard said anything to her.

That is the whole of what a woman of fifty-eight did in that yard today. She carried two buckets up two goes of a bank and she read three things other people had written, standing up, on her way past, and she asked nothing, and nobody asked her anything, and that is the ninth day of a run in which she has done that and not one other thing, and a figure about a run of mornings is a figure about mornings and is not a figure about the woman.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them untied his hand-cart from that wall at about six and wheeled it four feet along the wall to get it out of the wet and left it there, and that was the only thing anybody moved in that yard all day that was not moved on purpose, and half of them said goodnight to him and he said it back and went up the lane with the tent still rolled on the back of it.

The first day of the eighth month is sixty-seven days past. Nobody in that yard said one word about that this morning and nobody in that yard said one word about it this evening, and the line on the sheet at that gatepost says that the people in the ground in the shadow of a building eleven miles off are to be moved on or before that day, and that figure was arrived at by counting the people who answered a door, and it is four hundred and eleven and it did not move.

By the time the light had gone in that yard, at about half past eight, the man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall until it had gone with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and four or five others there said goodnight to one another on their way out and said it to him as well, and he said it back, and nobody in that yard said one word about anything else, and the term on that reading is eight days past, and the reading of that lot is begun and is not finished, and the bid is open and was not run on this day.
