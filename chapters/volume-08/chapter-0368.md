# Chapter 368: What a Second Reader Is

---

The twenty-seventh of the ninth month came in with the first of the light on the roof of that building and about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table and a stone on the corner of the day's page. The man of fifty-six read one of the four out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other three, and about four people in that yard were saying the fourth one of the four to each other and getting it right. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the sixty-seventh night of his run and sixty-seven days in this district, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and seventy-six days on that board and five hundred and ninety-two days on the train on the siding, and nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and seventy-ninth days since the first of the fourth month, counting that first day in, and the rival record has been in force a hundred and seventeen days and is unanswered.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that four hundred and eleven is the figure on the sheet at that gatepost and did not move this morning, and that a figure about a sheet at a gate is a figure about a sheet at a gate and is not a figure about a man of about nineteen standing in that yard with a page under his arm, and that the man of about sixty-four was not asked anything and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man of about nineteen asked one question out loud in that yard at about half past ten, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and it was the second question he has asked in that yard in a month and the first one anybody has asked in that yard that was not about a bucket, a fence, a figure or a building.

The count came to fifteen and the boy wrote it out in his own hand.

"**What has a person got to be able to do, to be a second reader?**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that trestle table with the cloth on his left hand and nothing in the fingers of his right one, and he said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and thirty-seven, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**You have to not be the man who said it. That is the whole of it and there is no other half of it. It is not a skill and it is not a wage and it is not nine feet of wire and it is not something anybody can learn in a fortnight by standing at that table with a cloth on one hand. You have to be a second person in a yard at a different hour, with the sentence still in your head and no way of getting at the page it came off, and if you get it wrong you have to say so and be got right by the man who said it. That is a second reader. Everything else is a man reading a sentence twice and calling it a check.**"

About nine people in that yard looked at the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing when he had finished, and about four of them looked at the man who convened the bid, and nobody in that yard said one word.

The man of fifty-six said one thing out loud at about a quarter to eleven, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and it was not counted, because about four people in that yard were saying the fourth figure to each other at the time. He said that a man who has been asked a question in a yard and has answered it has not been given a post and has not refused one, and that answering a question about what a second reader is is not being a second reader, and that if anybody in that yard thinks it is then they have understood nothing at all about this district and they should go and sit down somewhere.

A man who has said out loud in a yard what a second reader has to be able to do has not been given a post and cannot be counted either way. About nine people in that yard looking at him afterwards is nine people, and is not a finding and is not a habit, and he was not asked and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man of about nineteen did not write a figure down.

He stood about nine feet off the end of that trestle table with his own page under his arm and he wrote a sentence instead, in his own hand, and it says that a second reader is a person who is not the man who said it and is in a yard at a different hour and cannot see the page. Then he stopped, and he read it back to himself with his lips not moving, and then he went to the end of that table and asked the clerk of nineteen years, out loud and in front of about nine people, where a sentence goes.

She said that it goes on the page with the rest of the day's line, and that a clerk writes what was said and where it stopped, and that a sentence and a figure are two different things and a page can hold both of them.

He said that everything in that book was a figure and that he had never once in twenty days put anything else in it, and that he had got about nine figures in his own hand that afternoon and did not know what to do with a sentence, and that he was not going to put it in a book four hundred yards up that bank because he was nobody's clerk and it was not his book.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about nineteen who counts what people say in daylight yards and lanes in front of about nineteen people wrote a sentence and not a figure on his own page on this afternoon, and that a sentence is not a figure and is not a habit and is not a finding, and that he was not asked for it and did not offer it, and that nothing went into any book in this district because of it and nothing went anywhere.

About four people in that yard said, one after another and in the ordinary way, that a man who writes a sentence down ought to be a clerk, and the man of about nineteen said no to all four of them in about four seconds each time, and said that the last time he had been offered a post in this district by anybody was the third of this month and he had said no to that one as well, and that he counted things in yards for nothing and was going to go on counting things in yards for nothing.

The same answer given four times in an afternoon is a shape noticed four times and is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about a man who has said it four times, and nobody was offered anything and nobody was refused anything, and a post that nobody has offered is not a post that a man has turned down.

A man who counts what gets said out loud in a yard is nobody's clerk and has said so out loud in that yard twice this month. A man who has been offered a post four times by people standing about in a yard has not been given one by anybody with the standing to give one.

---

Then the man of about nineteen asked a second question out loud in that yard at about half past one, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and it was counted and got nine, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**What happens on a day this district cannot name?**"

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put one thing out loud in front of about nine people in the ordinary voice, and it came to a hundred and ten words, and the boy had it in his own hand.

"**It goes past. That is what happens in it. There is no figure on any page for that day and there is nobody standing in this yard to put one there, and the day does not stop for it and the ditch behind that building does not stop filling for it. A question about what happens on a day like that is a question and not a bid and not an offer, and I have answered it and I am going back to that gate with the broken latch on because a man told me about it yesterday in a lane and I have not done anything about it yet.**"

A question about what happens on a day this district cannot name is a question and is not a bid and is not an offer and is not a refusal. A person who has asked a question in a yard and has been answered has not agreed to anything and cannot be counted either way.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went up that lane at about two and was not in that yard again that day, and about nine feet of nothing was in the first gate and about three feet of nothing was in the second one by the time the light went, and about four people in that yard said that was a day's work done for nothing for the second time in a week, and about four other people in that yard said nothing at all.

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the top of that ditch from about three until about five. The man who keeps a road was not in that yard on this day and was out on that road and got back to that low wall before the light went, and his boots were black to the ankle, and about four people in that yard said that he had told them eleven miles of it yesterday and had not told them a single thing about himself, and nobody in that yard asked him for a second thing.

That man said one thing out loud at about four, from the head of that lane, to about four people who had walked up it with him, and it was not counted, because he was about a quarter of a mile off and the man of about nineteen could not get one figure out of it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the two men of about thirty-seven in this district were not asked anything this afternoon and were given nothing, and that the tent on the back of that hand-cart is still rolled and has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and that a tent that has not gone up is not a shelter and is not a sixth of the five protected things this district has.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was not in that yard at any point on this day and about four people in that yard said that he would be in on one of the other three days of that week, and nobody in that yard said what for and nobody in that yard asked him anything and he was not asked to say what for.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself, and nobody in that yard asked her anything and she did not ask anybody anything.

The reading of that lot is begun and is not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and the fourth of the five is a person, and the man who convened the bid was about nine feet off the end of that trestle table all day and did not go to the lane and did not go up that bank.

The bid in that yard had stood open twenty-six days and had not been run on one of them and no offer had ever stood on it and nobody had proposed anything about closing it. The light went off that yard at about half past eight and the figure on that sheet is the figure it was at ten that morning, and it has not changed.

**A MAN OF ABOUT NINETEEN ASKED A QUESTION IN THAT YARD ABOUT WHAT A SECOND PERSON HAS TO BE ABLE TO DO AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR ANSWERED IT, AND NINE PEOPLE LOOKED AT HIM, AND A BOY WROTE A SENTENCE DOWN INSTEAD OF A FIGURE FOR THE FIRST TIME IN TWENTY DAYS.**
