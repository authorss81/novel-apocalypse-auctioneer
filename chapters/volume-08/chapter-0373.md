# Chapter 373: Nobody Watched the Gatepost

---

The first of the tenth month came in with the light coming off the low ground earlier than it had all month and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. There was a stone on the corner of the day's page where it lay on the end of that trestle table, and beside it under the same stone lay the page with the twenty-first of the ninth month on it. The man of fifty-six read one of the four figures on the boards over nobody's shoulder and got the other three, because the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the other end of that table with his arms folded and his right hand round a length of wire he had not started on.

"**Two hundred and eighty-one days on that board and five hundred and ninety-seven days on the train on the siding, and two hundred and seventy-two days separate the second of January and this morning, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is a hundred and ninety-one days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move on the first morning of a month, and that nobody in this district has checked whether this month has thirty days, and that the figure for the length of this month was entered on the page on the fifteenth of last month off the month before it, and that a figure taken off the month before it is a figure about a list and not a figure about a date, and that she is not going to check a month by standing in it.

---

At about half past ten the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud in that yard, and the man of about nineteen counted it and got eighty-five.

"**Nobody in this district has ever checked how long this month is. The figure for it came off the month before this one, which is a figure about a list and not about a day, and I am not going to go and check it and I would like it said out loud in this yard that I have not, because a clerk who has entered a figure because she had to enter one has entered a figure about entering and not about the month.**"

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said the rest of it at about eleven, in front of the whole of that yard, and the boy counted what he said and got a hundred and ninety.

"**Sixty-one days. That is the interval between the day that sheet was found on that post, which was the first of the sixth month, and the day that sheet named, which was the first of the eighth. Sixty-one days on from the first of the eighth is this morning, because the eighth has thirty days and the ninth has thirty-one. I said that figure out loud in this yard on the fourth of this month and I have said it since and I am saying it now with the day on it. A figure about how often a body four hundred miles off prints is a figure about a habit and not about a body, and nobody has promised me anything on this morning, and I have not asked anybody for a promise and the record about the not asking says not asked. I am going to stand at the end of that table until the light goes and I am not going to say one word about it, and if you want to say something, say it, and if you do not, then that is what this morning is.**"

About nine people in that yard said nothing at all, and about four of them looked at the lane, and nobody in that yard went up that lane toward that gatepost, and nobody in that yard said out loud that they were going to go and look.

---

The morning before that was ordinary and the district is good at ordinary. The man who digs loam was at the top of that ditch before nine and came down into it with his right arm held at his side and worked along the bottom of it with a bar of iron, and the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in the same ditch about nine feet further along with his hook and his bundle, and the two of them did not say one word to each other and about four people in that yard said that they had seen them both in there and had not known what to make of it.

The man who keeps a road went out of that yard at about ten and up that lane and did not stop, and nobody in that yard asked him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At the foot of the east wall the man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the brick with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and nobody in that yard asked him about it and he was given nothing. About four people in that yard told him that a man who puts a table up for anybody ought to be the first to be asked about a day like this one, and he said that a table is for two people who want to lean on something and that nobody in that yard wanted to lean on anything this morning, and that was the whole of what he said about it.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past ten with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough and carried them up the two goes herself, and she read the top line of the boy's page under the stone standing up on the way past and said nothing to anybody about it.

---

Nothing came on the first of the tenth month.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood at the end of that trestle table from about eleven until about four with his right hand in the front of his coat and said nothing whatever in that yard, and the wire the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was holding at the other end of that table was still not started on at four, and the man who digs loam stood in that ditch behind that building with his hook and came up the bank at about half past three and went straight out of that yard.

At about half past three the man the figure of twenty-one years is against got as far as the fourth of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, out loud, at the end of that table, with about nine people close enough to hear it, and stopped there, and did not go on to the fifth of them, and did not start again.

He has got to that fourth twice in this month and stopped both times, and both of the times he stopped where a man stops when there is no table to put it on and no second person standing in that yard to read it back from the top, and about nine people in that yard heard him stop and not one of them asked him to go on.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a reading that was standing ready this afternoon was not used, and that a reading nobody was asked for is not a reading, and that a reading that is not used is not a finding and is not a failure and is not a success.

At about four the man who keeps a road came up that lane with his boots black to the ankle and said one short thing at the end of that table, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got twelve.

"**A figure that does not move is a figure about a count.**"

At about half past four the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up out of that ditch with his bundle over his shoulder and said one thing in that yard on his way out, and the boy counted what he said and got twelve.

"**I was in that ditch and I did not see it move.**"

At about five the man of fifty-six said one thing at the end of that table and nobody had asked him for it, and the count of what he said came to twelve.

"**It is four hundred and eleven and it has not moved today.**"

Then two people at the back of that yard had the whole of the rest of the argument about where a second figure would go if one came, and they had it in the ordinary way, and about four other people joined in and about four more left again. The places they named were that board outside the room four hundred yards up that bank, the first of the two books in that room, the second of the two books in that room, the page on that trestle table, the lot book with one line in it, and a new piece of paper that nobody in that yard had. Not one of those six was agreed on and not one of them was ruled, and nobody in that yard was asked to say where a figure ought to go, and the record of it says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure that has not come has not got a place, and that a place is not a reason, and that the six places those people named this afternoon are six opinions and not six instruments, and that none of them is a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and that nobody was asked to hold a place for a figure that did not come and the record says not asked.

The three of them were standing at three different ends of that yard and the boy went along behind all three of them and wrote the three figures down in a column on his own page, and the three figures were the same figure.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that getting a figure right three times is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about the man who counted it, and that getting a figure right once was not a finding either and that was entered on the twenty-ninth of the eighth.

---

The man of about nineteen put his own page on the end of that trestle table about ten minutes before the light went.

He did not ask anybody whether he could and nobody stopped him, and he put it under the same stone as the page with the twenty-first of the ninth month on it, and his own page had three short sentences on it in his own hand and three figures that were all the same, and he put it face up where anybody walking up to that table could read it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a page a person can walk up to and read is a page and not a book, and that a page on a table in a yard is a page in the rain, and that the page the boy put down this evening is not a day's page and is not the bid's record and is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a boy who writes down what people say in daylight yards is nobody's clerk and has not been asked to be one and nobody was asked and the record says so.

She did not say one word to him about it, and he said one word to her, which was that it was going to rain.

The first day of the eighth month is sixty-one days past this morning, and a figure about how often a body four hundred miles off prints is also sixty-one days, and a clerk entered that two figures of the same length are two figures and are not added together and that a clerk who could have made a finding out of them did not and that is not a clerk who has found nothing.

The bid in that yard is open thirty days and was not run on one of them and nobody proposed anything about closing it. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is ruled and was empty at the end of this day, and it is the seventh of that shape of thing in this district and it is not added to the nine instruments, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart is out on that road and was not in that yard at any point today and was not asked to be and cannot be counted either way.

Nothing came on the first of the tenth month and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is still four hundred and eleven, and the reading of that lot is begun and is not finished, and a boy has put his own page on a table in that yard where anybody can read it.
