# Chapter 353: What a Name Is Worth

---

The twelfth of the ninth month came in flat and grey with the sky the colour of the mud in that lane, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten and about four of them came in out of the wet. The three figures were on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read the bottom one of the three out loud over the mender's shoulder, and got the other two, and said nothing further, because saying them was the job and there was no pay in it. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the fifty-third night of his run and fifty-two days in this district, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and sixty-one days on that board and five hundred and seventy-seven days on the train on the siding, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is a hundred and seventy-one days, and the first day of the eighth month is forty-one days past this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the pool last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which from the eighth month onward is five months back, and that a refusal with a reason of its own is not one of those eight, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that those eight did not move this morning and are still eight.

---

The man of fifty-six had not slept well and it was in him from about half past ten, and it came out the way it comes out of him, which is that he says a thing in a yard in the ordinary voice that he has been carrying, and then stands there while about nine people decide what to do about it.

He said it at about eleven, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and seventy-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Yesterday morning a man said a figure out loud about a building in this yard and it was the first one anybody has ever said in here on purpose, and I have been stood at the end of that table since about ten o'clock yesterday thinking about what it means that a figure can be said out loud at all. So here is mine, and I have not got it from anybody and I have not asked anybody for it. I am going to put a figure on what the name of a person in a list of hearers is worth to somebody four hundred miles off who is holding a count of the people who answered a door. I am going to say it is worth exactly as much as the paper, and I am saying it out loud because I would rather it was a number in this yard than a number in my own head, and I am not asking anybody to agree with it and I am not going to be asked.**"

The man who convened the bid said nothing. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said nothing, and bent the wire he was holding with the fingers of his right hand, and the cloth was still on his left. About nine people in that yard said nothing back, which is what about nineteen people do when a figure has been said out loud in front of them that nobody can check.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure named out loud in a yard by a man who is not a clerk is a price and is not a rate, and that a clerk does not price it and does not correct it and does not carry it into any column, and that the man who named it was not asked for it and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the man who named it has not said that it is afraid of the morning, which it has said three times, and a clerk entered that the fourth was not said.

---

The woman of fifty-eight was in that yard at about half past eleven because she was in it every morning of that run, and she had her two empty buckets with her and she had set them down by the trough and she was straightening up again when the man of fifty-six got to the end of what he had to say.

She said one thing out loud. She said it to about nineteen people, in the ordinary voice, and she said it in about four seconds, and the man of about nineteen counted what she said and got a hundred and fifty, and it went in the minute in her own words.

"**No. I will not have my name on that for a figure, and that is all I am going to say about it. I have carried two buckets up that bank for nineteen years and I have not said my name in this yard once in that time and I am not going to start this morning because a man has said a number out loud. And you cannot get it back off afterwards. That is the whole of my reason and it is about me and not about that building and not about that list. A name that is on a piece of paper is not a person and the person cannot get it off it by saying she would rather it was not there, and I know that because a thing I said once in a room went into a book and nobody asked me whether it should.**"

She picked her buckets up off the ground while she was saying the second half of it and had them in her hands before she had finished the sentence, and about four people in that yard watched a woman of fifty-eight refuse a figure with a reason and then go back to the water, and not one of them said one word to her about it.

The man of fifty-six stood where he was for about a minute after she had gone. Then he said out loud, to about nine people, that he had said a number that a woman had just told him to keep off her, and that he wanted it struck out of what the boy had, and he said it in the ordinary voice and it was not a question and nobody answered it.

The man of about nineteen said that he could take a number out of his own head and that he had never been able to take one out of his own head before, and that he was not going to promise a person he did not know that he could do it, and that he would try. The man of fifty-six said that was not good enough and that he had said the number in a yard in front of about nineteen people and it was in about nineteen sets of ears that were not his own.

Then the man of fifty-six said the other half of it, to nobody in particular, and it is the only thing he said that day that has not been counted by anybody, because about nine people in that yard had already gone off down the lane in the wet.

"**A number a man has said out loud in a yard cannot be unsaid by him afterwards and a clerk cannot strike it, because a clerk strikes a line and a line is not what I said. I have been in this basin a long time and I have got a name in my own mouth that I have never given to anybody, and I have never had to think about what a name is worth to somebody four hundred miles off, and today I put a figure on it in a yard like a man putting a price on a length of wire, and a woman told me in about four seconds that I could not take it off her again, and she was right and I have no way of showing her that I have understood it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure a man names out loud in that yard cannot be taken back off anybody afterwards, and that a clerk may strike a line on a page and may not strike what a person said in a yard, and that a memory is not a page, and that a figure a man has said out loud is a figure about that man and not about the person he said it about, and that the person it was said about has said she would rather it was not there and has not asked for anything.

The clerk of nineteen years wrote that down at about half past twelve, standing at the end of that trestle table with the wind getting at the page, and she did it in one line and it came to this. A price for a name was refused out loud with a reason, by a person who was not asked for it and who cannot be counted either way. She entered the refusal and she did not enter the name, and she entered that no column was ruled this morning and that nothing was put in the column that is ruled and empty. She entered that this is a refusal with a reason of its own and is not one of the eight refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given, and that those eight did not move and are still eight. And she entered that neither this refusal nor the figure the man of fifty-six named is a ninth of anything, and that a ninth of what is not stated.

---

What the rest of that day was made of was the rain coming back.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came into that yard at about one and stood under the eaves of that building with his right arm down at his side and his left holding his coat off the drip, and about four people in that yard asked him, one after another and in the ordinary way, what he thought a term of a month was worth, and he told all four of them that he did not know, in about four seconds each time, and the same answer given four times in an afternoon is a shape noticed four times and is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about him.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was in the bottom of it until about half past three in the rain with his hook and his bundle, and about nine people in that yard asked him before he went down whether that building was going to be standing in the winter, and he gave them the same answer in about four seconds each time and went down the bank.

At about four the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was at the mouth of that lane in the rain with his shafts down and about nine people in that yard watched him not come in, and nobody in that yard sent anybody out to him and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The reading of the lot stands where it stopped, with the term said out loud in it and no holder after it, and the man who convened the bid did not go on with it this afternoon and nobody in that yard asked him to.

At about half past seven the rain stopped. The man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded beside him, and about four people in that yard said goodnight to him and he said goodnight back, and nobody said one word to him about what he was going to do with his hands, and a man whose trade is over is not a failure and is not a tragic figure and is not redeemed, and a clerk may not say that about him and may not say the other thing either.

The light went early off that ground with the cloud still on it. The number on that sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the rival record has been in force a hundred and two days and is unanswered, and the reading of the lot is begun and not finished, and the bid in that yard has now been open eleven days without anybody offering anything at all for it.

**A FIGURE WAS NAMED OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD FOR WHAT A NAME PUT DOWN IN WRITING IS WORTH TO A BODY FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF HOLDING A COUNT OF ANSWERERS, AND A WOMAN OF FIFTY-EIGHT REFUSED IT IN ABOUT FOUR SECONDS WITH A REASON OF HER OWN, PICKED UP HER BUCKETS AND WENT BACK TO THE WATER, AND NO NAME WENT ANYWHERE.**
