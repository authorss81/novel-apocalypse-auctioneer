# Chapter 388: Five Ninths on One Page

---

The sixteenth of the tenth month came in with the first of the light on the wet boards and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the four figures off those boards out loud to three people who had come in behind him and got all four.

"**The number of days between the second of January and this morning is two hundred and eighty-seven, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is two hundred and six days, and the term on that reading is seventeen days past and did not renew, and nobody in this district has counted how many columns there are anywhere.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that a column is a column and not a person, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the east wall with his cart and two people in that yard told him that ruling a page is a thing he could do, and he said out loud that a straight edge is a straight edge whoever holds it and that he had not been asked to rule anything and would not rule anything he had not been asked to rule.

Two people in that yard said that is the least anybody could have offered him and three of them said that is exactly what a man who put a chair down without being asked would say, and he said that both of those were about the chair and neither of them was about him.

The man of about fifty-six came up that lane at about half past nine, read the four figures off the boards before the mender got to them, got all four, and then went and read them again out loud for the three people who had come in behind the mender, and two of them said that was the first time in five months he had read the same four numbers twice in one morning.

---

At about half past ten the clerk of nineteen years ruled a page.

She did it with a straight edge and a pencil and she did it on her own knee at the near end of that trestle table, and half that yard came and looked at it, and she wrote the five headings at the top of the five columns and then she wrote nothing else at all.

"**The ninth line of the old charter. The third column of the covenant. The ninth place on the page out of the cave. The ninth of the eleven houses. The ninth of the nine printed nights.**"

She read those five headings out loud once, in that yard, in daylight, in the ordinary way, and everybody present heard all five of them and about four of them wrote nothing down and the rest of them wrote all five down.

Under the five of them she ruled four more columns, and over the four she wrote a word, and the four of them are the four columns of not-askings this district has had since before the first of the ninth, and all four of them were empty. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is the seventh of that shape of thing in this district and is not one of the four and is not on this page, and it is ruled and it is empty and it has been since the thirtieth of the ninth.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that five different ninths in this district are now on one page in one morning, and that none of the five is filled, and that a page with five headings and nothing under the headings is not a list of anybody and is not a record of hearers and is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own.

Then two people in that yard asked her, in the ordinary way and all at once, for a sixth column, for the person who read the line back from the top on the eleventh of this month, and the man of about nineteen looked up off his own page.

She said one thing back to them in about four seconds, and the page has every word of it on it and nobody has read it since.

"**You are asking me for a new column of the same shape. It is not an empty column and it is not the same column as any of the four, and it is not added to the four, and four is a figure about four columns, and a fifth of a shape of thing is not a column of that shape. I am not going to rule it and you are not going to get it by asking me twice.**"

Two people in that yard said that the woman of fifty-eight had done a thing on the eleventh of this month and that a thing somebody does ought to be written down somewhere, and what the book says about it is that a thing somebody did is not a column and that a person who has not been asked to be in a column cannot be put in one, and that the woman of fifty-eight was asked on the eleventh of this month and said out loud that she is not going to do it again and nobody has asked her to since.

---

At about one the man of about nineteen copied the five headings onto his own page in his own hand, in about four minutes, sitting on the end of that trestle table with the page flat on his knee.

He got four of the five right. He got the third one wrong, and he got it wrong by leaving a word out of it and not by changing a figure, and the clerk of nineteen years was about nine feet off and looked at what he had written at about two and did not tell him, because he is nobody's clerk and she is not his clerk and a clerk is not going to correct a page she does not keep, and she saw it and did not say anything and was not asked to.

He found it himself at about four. He put his finger on the line and said one thing out loud to nobody in particular, and nobody counted it.

"**That is wrong. I have left a word out of the third one and I have had it in front of me for three hours and nobody in this yard told me, and they were all standing about nine feet off, and I do not know whether that is a thing about me or about this yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy left a word out of the third of five headings on his own page and found it himself three hours later, and that nobody in that yard told him because nobody in that yard was his clerk, and that a page with a word missing out of it is a page and not a record, and that the record about the not telling says not asked.

Three people in that yard asked him, in the ordinary way, whether he was going to write the five headings out again, and he said that he was going to leave the word out of it and that a page of his with a word missing out of it is the truest thing on that table this morning, and a page is a page and is not a document this district owns and did not become a fourth of the three.

Three people in that yard said out loud that a clerk standing nine feet off could have said one word and did not, and a clerk of nineteen years said out loud, in about nine words, that she could have and did not and would not have been wrong to, and that a person who is not a clerk of a house is not a keeper of anybody's page.

---

At about two the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane, read the four figures off the boards as he went past and got all four, and then stopped at the end of that trestle table and read the five headings on the clerk's page standing up, and two people in that yard watched him get to the fifth one.

Then he said one thing out loud in that yard and the man of about nineteen counted it and got ninety-three, and the lane heard it.

"**That fifth one has been open for a hundred and seventy-nine days and I have never once been asked about it and I am not going to pretend I have not just read it. I have kept a road for nine years and I know what a thing that is still open looks like and that is one, and I am not asking anybody to close it and I am not asking anybody to tell me about it, and that is the whole of what I have come down this lane to say.**"

Then he sat down in the second chair for about a minute and got up again, and nobody in that yard asked him why he sat in it and he was not asked, and a sitting is a figure about a sitting and is not a figure about the man and is not entered either way.

Nobody closed the fifth of those five. The ninth of the nine printed nights is now written on a page in a yard as well as on a page in a book four hundred yards from that gate, and the clerk's page says so, and that a thing on two pages is not a thing that has been closed, and that a page a stranger cannot walk up to and a page a stranger can walk up to are two pages and are not one page, and that nobody in that yard has been asked to close it and she was not going to ask.

---

The rest of that day went ordinary and the ordinary part of it was longer than it has been for a fortnight. The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in the bottom of that ditch before nine and came up the bank at about ten and was in it again from about two until about half past four, and the water in that ditch is about four inches lower than it was on the fifteenth of this month and nobody in that yard has altered a figure anywhere. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in that ditch with his hook and his bundle until about half past four. The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the east wall with his cart and was given nothing and was not asked about it.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane at about half past four with his shafts down and did not come into that yard, and the yard watched him not come in, and nobody said a word to him about a page with nine columns on it.

That bid stands at forty-six days and was not run today and nothing has been said about closing it. The first day of the eighth month is seventy-six days past. The man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall on the eighty-seventh night of his run and eighty-seven days in this district, with nothing in his hands, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about five ninths.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, carried them up the two goes, and read the clerk's page standing up on her way past the way anybody can, and she got to the fifth heading and stopped there for about two seconds and went on up the bank, and nobody in that yard said one word to her about stopping and the page says not asked.

By the time the light had gone in that yard the clerk's page was on the end of that trestle table under the same stone as the boy's page and the page with the twenty-first of the ninth month on it, and it had nine columns ruled on it and nothing written in one of them, which is the same as it was at about two this afternoon, and the boy's page was under the same stone with a word missing out of the third of five headings on it and nobody in that yard had corrected it and nobody had been asked to.
