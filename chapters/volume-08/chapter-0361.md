# Chapter 361: Four Days in Seven

---

The twentieth of the ninth month came in with the first hard frost of that run standing in the mud of that lane and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, standing in the one place where the light got past the shadow of that building. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read the top one of the four out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other three, and about four people in that yard were saying the second one of the four to each other behind him and getting it right. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the sixtieth night of his run and sixty days in this district, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and sixty-nine days on that board and five hundred and eighty-five days on the train on the siding, and the rival record has been in force a hundred and ten days and is unanswered, and the table against that wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood seventy-five mornings.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move this morning, and that its own age as a figure about that figure is a hundred and ten days, and that a record in force and a figure's own age are two figures counted off one day and are not one figure, and that the man of about sixty-four was not asked what he is going to do with his hands, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about a quarter past ten a cart came up that lane and stopped at the head of it with the shafts down, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart got off and stood at the front of it, and about nine people in that yard saw him do it and none of them said one word to him about it. He was not four miles off before the light that morning, because this was one of the three days in a week he is in the district and not out on that road, and he had come up that lane at about six with the cart loaded, and there is nowhere in that yard to put a cart, because a yard is a room with no roof and it is not a room a cart fits in.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said it. He said it standing at the end of that trestle table with the cloth still on his left hand and about nine inches of fresh wire in the fingers of his right one, and the count came to two hundred and fifty and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have read more out loud in this yard this month than I have read anywhere in my life and I am going to say the thing underneath all of it, and I have been sitting on it since the seventeenth. A reading is not a thing a man says. A reading is a thing a man says and then a second person reads it back from the top, and if the second person reads it back wrong the whole of it gets said again from the top and not from the word, and if there is no second person then the man who said it is standing in a yard talking to himself. And there is one person in this district who has ever read a thing back from the top. He is about twenty-nine and he drives a cart, and he is standing at the head of this lane at this moment, and he is gone four days in a week, and on those four days there is nobody in this basin who can do it at all. So the whole of what we have got rests on one man with a cart, and it rests on him three days in seven, and on four days in seven nothing in this district binds. A thing that does not bind on four days in seven is a thing that has been agreed to and not kept, and an agreement nobody can be made to keep is not a term.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything back for about four seconds. The cart at the head of that lane did not move, and the man at the front of it put his hands on his knees.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and forty-four, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been making a reading in that yard every morning since the tenth of this month out of my own two hands and I did not know that was what I was doing until about a minute ago, and I am not going to stand here and ask that man to do it. If I asked him and he said yes, then the whole of this lot and the whole of the term on it would stand on a man who is gone four days in a week, and I would have done that to him in a yard in front of about nineteen of you. He has not been asked and I am not going to ask him, and I have not got a second half to this and I am not going to pretend to anybody standing here that I have.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he is not going to ask a person to read a thing back from the top has not refused to ask and cannot be counted either way, and that the man at the head of that lane was not asked and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that the column on that page with a word over it and nothing under it is still ruled and still empty and nothing went into it this morning.

---

The cart stood at the head of that lane with the shafts down for the best part of three hours.

Nobody in that yard moved out of its way on purpose. About nine of them were standing where they had been standing since ten, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart did not shout at anybody and did not drive a cart through about nineteen people standing in the mud of that lane, and he sat on the front bench of it with his hands on his knees from about half past ten until about one, and then got up and backed it out to the corner, and it was a quarter past two before the wheels were on that road again.

About nine people in that yard watched a loaded cart stand still for three hours because a thing was being said out loud in it, and about four of them did not know they were standing in the way. Nobody in that yard paid for those three hours and the man who lost them did not say he had, and nobody in that yard asked him about any of it, and he was not asked about any of it either.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has lost three hours at the head of a lane has not given anything and has not had anything taken off him, and that a cost nobody in a yard agreed to is still a cost, and that it is not a toll, because a toll is a thing a holder pays under a term and there is no holder in that yard.

---

The man of fifty-six did not say one word about any of it until about half past four, and then he said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people. The count came to a hundred and forty-three and the boy wrote it out in his own hand.

"**I have stood at the end of that table most mornings of that run with four figures on it and I have read them out loud over another man's shoulder for nothing, and I have never once asked myself who was standing on the other side of the thing I was reading. I have not got anything clever to say about that. I would have been the same man reading four numbers off a wall this morning if that man with the cart had never come up that lane in his life, and that is what I have been reading them for, and I would like somebody standing in this yard to understand that I am not a part of this. I am a man reading numbers. That is the whole of what I have been doing since the first of this month.**"

About four people in that yard said nothing back, and about four others said that he had read them well, and he said thank you to all eight of them and nothing else. He has not given up a post and cannot be counted either way, because there was no post and there is not one now and there never was, and a man who reads four numbers off the end of a table is not a reader and is not a second reader and nobody has ever asked him to be one.

---

The rest of that day went the way the days of that run went. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the wire into the pocket of his coat on his right side and went to the fence end of that lane and was not back in that yard after one o'clock, and about four people in that yard noticed he had gone and did not ask him where to. The man who digs loam came in at about two with a bar of iron in his right hand held down at his side and went out again at about half past two without saying one word to anybody, and his right arm did not go above the level of that shoulder. The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about four with his boots black to the ankle, read the four figures off the boards of that trestle table as he went past it without stopping, got all four, and went on up the lane, and nobody in that yard asked him to and nobody in that yard said to him that he had just done a thing.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was in the bottom of it with his hook and his bundle until about half past four. At the foot of the east wall the man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the brick with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and nobody in that yard asked him about it.

About four people in that yard were still talking about the mender's sentences at about five. Two of them had it that the man with the cart had said he would do it and one of them had it that nobody had asked him at all, and both of those were said out loud in that yard in the ordinary way, and neither of them was true, and two figures of what a man said in a yard are two figures about what a man said and are not one figure and are not added together, and the man they were about was gone up that road before either of them was said.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself, the way she has carried them for nineteen years, and she did not ask anybody anything on this day and nobody in that yard asked her anything, and every question anybody has asked in that yard this month has been about a bucket.

The reading of that lot stands where it stopped on the sixteenth, at the third of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and it has not got to the fourth of them, which is a person, and the fifth of them, which is a remedy, has not been said either. The bid in that yard was open on the twentieth of the ninth month and had stood open nineteen days and had not been run on one of them, and no offer had ever stood on it, and nobody had proposed anything about closing it.

The cart was not at the head of that lane at about six and about nine people in that yard looked up the lane and saw the ruts of it going out past the corner. The light went off that yard at about half past eight and the number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and it will be the same figure in the morning, and a figure that does not move is a habit and not a finding, and a clerk has entered it and has not found anything in it.

**THE WHOLE OF WHAT THIS DISTRICT HAS GOT RESTS ON ONE MAN WITH A CART, WHO STOOD AT THE HEAD OF THAT LANE FOR THREE HOURS BECAUSE NOBODY IN THAT YARD SAID A WORD BACK, AND ON FOUR DAYS OUT OF SEVEN NOT ONE THING IN THIS BASIN BINDS, AND NOBODY ASKED HIM TO DO ANYTHING AT ALL.**
