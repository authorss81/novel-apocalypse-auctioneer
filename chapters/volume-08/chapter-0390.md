# Chapter 390: Nine Sittings and Nine

---

The eighteenth of the tenth month came in with the first of the light on the roof of that building and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The man of fifty-six read the four figures off the boards at the end of that trestle table out loud over the mender's shoulder, the way he has read them over somebody's shoulder for five months, and got all four, and then he did not go up that lane to the fence end at about ten, which he does most mornings, and stayed at that table.

"**Nothing has been entered for three hundred and twenty-eight days, the board carries two hundred and ninety-eight and the train on the siding carries six hundred and fourteen, the table against that wall eleven miles off has stood a hundred and four mornings, and the pool of refusals is two hundred and eight days and last moved in the third month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the old charter in the book four hundred yards from that gate is not corrected by anything said in this yard and is not replaced by it and is not taken out by it, and that its fifth line is still wrong on its face in a book a stranger may walk up to, and that a wrong count is not struck by writing a correct one beside it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at that table with the cloth on his left hand and about nine inches of wire in the right pocket of his coat, and he bent that wire all morning without using it, and two people in that yard said that a man of his age ought to be able to put a fence up in a morning and he said that he can put a fence up in a morning and that is a different sentence and neither of them is about the second chair.

---

At about eleven the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing asked the people in that yard, in the ordinary way, to count the four things out loud themselves.

Nobody had asked them to before. About nine people in that yard counted, out loud, over about twenty minutes, and they got the same four figures, and the man of about nineteen counted all four of those figures as well and wrote them down and got the same figure off every one of the four, and two of them said that four people getting a figure right is not a finding, and the man of fifty-six said it out loud before they said it.

They counted the figure on that gatepost and got four hundred and eleven. They counted the days that record has been in force and got a hundred and thirty-nine, and they counted the days the term on that reading has been past and got nineteen, and they counted the days that bid has stood open and got forty-eight. And the man of fifty-six said out loud that he had not said a word while they did it, and two of them said that was the point of it and three of them said it was not the point of anything.

Then two people in that yard counted the four columns as well, out loud, and they are the ninth line of the old charter, the third column of the covenant, the ninth place on the page out of the cave, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and all four of them are ruled and empty, and the fourth of them has been ruled since the thirtieth of the ninth month and the other three have been empty for longer than anybody in that yard has been keeping figures, and four is a figure about four columns, and no fifth of that shape was proposed, and a column with a word over it is not a column of people.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid on this day and the count is still five, and that a day's work went into the fourth gate on eleven miles of road on the sixth of this month and went into an outlet at the low end of a ditch on the fifteenth of this month and neither of those two things has been paid for, and that this district has had two lots in a book for four days and has not paid anybody for either of them.

About four people in that yard said out loud that a district which has counted its own figures for the first time in a month ought to write them down somewhere anybody can walk up to, and the four figures are on the page in the clerk's own hand, and the page is hers and is not a book a stranger may walk up to, and a page a clerk keeps is not a page anybody can walk up to, and this district has one of those and not the other and did not set out to have either.

About four people in that yard asked the man of fifty-six, in the ordinary way and all at once, what he thought the district should do about a bid that has stood open forty-eight days, and he said one thing out loud and about nine people heard it and nobody counted it.

"**I have read the figures on that board for five months and I do not know when a term runs out and I was not going to be told, and I have not been told, and a bid that has stood open forty-eight days is a thing that has not been run on one of forty-eight days, and that is a figure about a bid and not about anybody standing in this yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a bid in a yard on forty-eight days is not a market and that a market is a place where a person is asked a question, and that this yard has been asked questions for five months and that is a yard and not a market, and that a person who has not been asked about a bid has not refused one and cannot be counted either way.

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in the bottom of that ditch before nine and came up the bank at about ten and was in it again from about two until about half past four, and his right hand did not go above the level of that shoulder on any part of this day. The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about eleven and again at about four, read the four figures both times and got all four both times, and nobody in that yard asked him about the eleven miles and nobody in that yard said one word to him about counting.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in that ditch with his hook and his bundle until about half past four, and the man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the east wall with his cart and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and he was given nothing and nobody asked him about the tent, and two of them said that a man who put a chair down ten days ago is the man anybody in this district ought to be asking about nine sittings, and he said that nine is a figure about sittings and that he is a man who puts tables up and that is the end of what he knows.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in that yard from about ten until about four and said one thing out loud at about one, and nobody counted it and nobody has claimed it since.

"**The rule got said in this yard on the tenth of this month and nobody was made to keep it and it has been kept once in the eight days since that, by a person who is nobody in particular and who was asked, and I am not going to say that out loud again because the day I say it is the day it is a post.**"

At about half past four the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and did not come up that lane, and two of them said out loud that on nine of the ten days of this month nothing in this district has bound at all, and about four of them said that on four days in a week that is simply a fact about a man with a cart, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud that a fact about a man with a cart is a fact about a man with a cart and not a rule, and nobody counted either of those.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, carried them up the two goes, and read the two lines in that lot book and the nine columns on the clerk's page standing up on her way past, and she said nothing to anybody and nobody in that yard said one word to her.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was not in that yard and was not asked, and his six households have not moved for a hundred and eighty days and that figure came out of a ladder and he has not said it out loud and was not asked for it on this day.

At about half past five the man of about nineteen wrote nine at the top of his own page, in his own hand, and turned the page round and showed it to two people standing near the end of that trestle table, and then he said that the man of fifty-six was the only person in that yard who counts figures and that the man of fifty-six ought to count this one, and the man of about fifty-six counted it and got seventy-nine.

"**That is nine sittings and I have been putting a name to every one of them since the eighth day of this month and I have just taken nine names off that page, because nine sittings is a figure about nine sittings and I do not know that they are nine people and neither do you, and there is one of me on that page nine times over and that is the most I am going to put down.**"

About nine people in that yard looked at that page. About four of them said out loud that he had just made a worse record than the one he had, and about four of them said that it was the first thing anybody in this district has ever made worse on purpose, and what the book says about it is that nine sittings is a figure about nine sittings and is not a figure about nine people, and that whether the nine are nine people or one person nine times is not a question the clerk of nineteen years has been asked and is not going to answer, and that the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six and no name went into it by anybody.

Along that lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall on the eighty-ninth night of his run and eighty-nine days in this district, with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and about four people in that yard said good evening to him as they came down the lane and about four of them said good evening to each other instead, and nobody in that yard asked him to sit in the second chair and he was not asked and he was not given anything and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past six the man of fifty-six finished at the end of that trestle table, looked at the second chair for about a second and a half, and sat down in it.

The whole of that yard saw a man who has read the figures off those boards every morning for five months sit down in a chair that a man with no use in either hand put down on the eighth of this month, and nobody in that yard said one word about it, and nobody counted how long he was in it, and nobody in that yard said whether he was going to be the second reader of anything, and the chair was at the end of that trestle table with somebody in it.
