# Chapter 366: The Reason Twice

---

The twenty-fifth of the ninth month came in with the light coming off the low ground early and going flat by eleven, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read the middle one of the four out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other three, and about four people in that yard were saying the fourth one of the four to each other behind him and getting it right this time, because it had been put right in front of all of them the day before. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the sixty-fifth night of his run and sixty-five days in this district, and he had slept on that low wall on sixty-four of the sixty-five nights of that run, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and seventy-four days on that board and five hundred and ninety days on the train on the siding, and two hundred and sixty-five days separate the second of January and this morning, and the table against that wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood eighty mornings.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and that there is no figure on that sheet and no figure on the page in her own hand that is about anybody standing in that yard this morning, and that the man of about sixty-four was not asked which of the sixty-five nights of that run he did not sleep on and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said it at about eleven, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people. The count came to a hundred and seventy-six and the boy wrote it out in his own hand.

"**A man said a figure out loud in this yard on the twelfth of this month for what a name in a list of hearers is worth to somebody four hundred miles off, and a woman of fifty-eight told him in about four seconds that she would not have her name on it for a figure, and he has not got over it and I have not got over it, and I am going to put a figure on the same thing this morning because I have got one and because there is nowhere in this district to put a thing like that except a yard. My figure is not the paper. My figure is a day's work. That is what a name in a list of hearers is worth, and it is worth exactly that and not a half of it, because a day's work is the only thing anybody in this basin has that a person four hundred miles off could actually use, and a person who cannot use it has not got anything.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road was in that yard at that moment. He had come up that lane at about half past ten with his boots black to the ankle and he had been standing about nine feet off the end of that trestle table for about half an hour without saying anything, and he said one thing out loud, in about four seconds, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got thirty-nine, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**No. A road a stranger can walk up to has not got my name on it, and neither has a man, and the day something wants a name put on it is the day it stops being a road.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything back for about four seconds. About nine people in that yard were standing where they had been standing and about four of them looked at the man who had said the figure and about four of them looked at the man who had refused it, and about four people in that yard said afterwards, in the ordinary way, that they had not expected either of those two men to do that, and that was four people saying a thing and not a finding and not a habit.

A price for a name is a figure and not a rate, and a figure a man has named out loud in that yard cannot be taken back off anybody afterwards. A clerk may strike a line on a page and may not strike what a person said in a yard, and a memory is not a page.

---

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing, out loud, to about nine people, and nobody took a figure of it, because about four people in that yard were saying the fourth number to each other at the time.

He said that a man has to be able to give a reason twice for it to be a reason, and that a thing said once in a yard is a thing a man said, and that a thing said twice in a yard in daylight in front of about nineteen people is two things a man said, and that he was asking for it in the open and not as a favour, and that nobody in that yard had asked that man to say anything and he was not asked twice for the same thing.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road looked at him for about four seconds and then said the same thing again, in the same words, at the same speed, standing where he was.

The man of about nineteen counted what he said the second time and got the same figure he had got the first time, and wrote both of them down in his own words and did not know what to do with either of them, and about four people in that yard saw a man count the same sentence twice and get the same figure and saw a clerk of nineteen years look at both of the figures he had written.

A reason given twice out loud in a yard in daylight is two reasons and is not one reason given twice. Two figures of the same length are two figures and are not added together, and a clerk who could have made a finding out of that has not made one. The man who gave the reason was not asked to give it a second time and cannot be counted either way, and the second time was asked for out loud in front of about nine people and is not a favour.

---

The cost of that morning was a number in a man's mouth and it was the man who had said it.

About four people in that yard came up to the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing one after another between about half past eleven and about one and asked him, in the ordinary way, what his figure was, and he told all four of them. He told the first one a day's work. He told the second one a day's work. He told the third one a day's work.

The fourth one asked him what a day was in that. He stopped for about four seconds with his right hand on the edge of that trestle table and about nine people in that yard watched him stop, and then he said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and it came to ninety-five words, and the boy had it in his own hand.

"**A day is a day. I have said a day's work in that yard four times this morning and I have said it four times the same, and you are asking me the fifth question about the same figure and I am going to answer it once and then I am not going to answer it again, and a man who cannot give a figure five times has not changed his mind about it. Go and ask him. He has got one and he has said it out loud this morning in about four seconds.**"

A man who has said a figure out loud in a yard four times and will not say it a fifth time has not withdrawn the figure and cannot be taken back off anybody. A figure a man names out loud in that yard is a figure about that man and is not about the person he said it about, and four people asking a man what a figure is, is four people and is not a finding and is not a habit.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two prices for a name have now been refused out loud in that yard with a reason, the first of them by a woman of fifty-eight on the twelfth of this month and the second of them by a man of about thirty-four who keeps a road this morning, and that neither of them is a ninth of anything. The pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which from the eighth month onward is five months back, and did not move this morning, and a refusal with a reason of its own is not one of those eight, and those eight are eight. She entered that a third would be a thing this district is doing on purpose, and that a thing a district is doing on purpose is a habit, and that nobody in that yard was asked for a name and no name went anywhere.

---

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was at the top of that ditch from about two until about five with a bar of iron in his right hand held down at his side, and the man who keeps a road went out of that yard at about one and up that lane and did not stop on the eleven miles of it, and about nine people in that yard watched him go. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and was not in that yard at any point on this day, and about four people in that yard said at about four that he would be in on one of the other days of that week, and nobody in that yard said what for, and nobody in that yard asked him anything and he was not asked.

The man of fifty-six said one thing out loud at about half past four, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and nobody took a figure of that one either, because about four people in that yard were still saying two of the four numbers to each other at the far end of that trestle table.

He said that this district has now refused a price for a name twice out loud with a reason, and that the first time a woman of fifty-eight did it and the second time a man who keeps a road did it, and that both of them said the same shape of thing in about four seconds each, and that the reason one is not the reason the other one gave and that a clerk will enter both of them and will not join them up. He said that a district which refuses the same thing twice out loud has not found a habit, and that if anybody in this yard hears it a third time then somebody in this yard has started doing it on purpose, and that a thing this district does on purpose is not a rule and is not a custom and is a habit, and that he is the man who reads four figures off a board and he is not going to be the man who starts one.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was in the bottom of it until about half past four. At the foot of the east wall the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up had his hand-cart against the brick with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and he was not in that yard again after about eleven and nobody in that yard asked him where he had gone to.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself, and nobody in that yard said one word to her about the twelfth of this month or about this morning, and she did not say one word about either of them.

The reading of that lot is begun and is not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and the fourth of the five is a person. The bid in that yard had stood open twenty-four days and had not been run on one of them and no offer had ever stood on it and nobody had proposed anything about closing it.

The light went off that yard at about half past eight and the number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the two refusals of a price for a name are two, and a third was not manufactured this month.

**A MAN NAMED A SECOND FIGURE OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD FOR WHAT A NAME IN WRITING IS WORTH TO A BODY FOUR HUNDRED MILES OFF, AND A MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-FOUR GAVE HIS REASON TWICE IN THE SAME WORDS, AND THE FIGURE IS TWO AND A THIRD WAS NOT MANUFACTURED.**
