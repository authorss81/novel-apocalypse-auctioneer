# Chapter 385: What a Holder Owes and Who Is Not One

---

The thirteenth of the tenth month came in with the cloud sitting low over the low ground and the standing water in the ditch behind that building about a foot deep where it had not been a foot deep two days ago. About nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that trestle table with the cloth on his left hand and his right one flat on the boards, doing nothing with it.

"**Two hundred and eighty-four days separate the second of January and this morning, and two hundred and ninety-three days on that board, and a table against a wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood ninety-nine mornings, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and seventy-six days back and is still open.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the third of the three things this district has to have before it will call a sounding safe became true in that yard on the eleventh of this month, and that it is on the page this morning, and that no part of it is printed on a sheet this district makes.

The man of about nineteen counted three figures out loud before eleven that morning and wrote all three down, and two people in that yard said that he counts a different four every day and that a figure is a figure whichever four it comes in on.

---

At about half past ten the man the figure of twenty-one years is against picked the lot book up off the end of that trestle table.

He did it with his left hand. He has not got a use in the other one and has had none since the tenth of the eighth month, and it opened on that table yesterday, and about nine people in that yard watched him do it, and he got the book up and turned it about to face himself and put it down in front of the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, and it went down the wrong way up.

The clerk of nineteen years said one word out loud from about nine feet off, in the ordinary way, and about nine people in that yard heard it.

"**Backwards.**"

He turned it round. Then he took his right hand out of his coat and put it in his coat again, and it did not come out again for the rest of that day, and what the book says about it is that a man who put a book down in front of another man and was told which way up it goes has not been asked for a figure and has not given one.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read that line out loud from the top, in that yard, in daylight, with the whole of that yard standing in it, and he was not the man who convened it and he said so before he started, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got seventy-two.

"**I did not open this and I am not the one who asked for it and I am going to read it because a thing said by the man who asked for it is a thing he already believes, and this is the third time it has been read out in this yard and on the two other times the man who asked for it read it and nobody read it back.**"

He read the first of the five things, and the lot. Then he read the second, and the term, and he read it the way he reads everything, which is as though there were nine people standing in front of him and there were four. Then he came to the third, and the man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and seventy.

"**The third of the five is the toll, and I am going to say the toll out loud, and here it is: a toll is a thing a holder pays under a term, and there is no holder of that lot, and there has never been a holder of it, and so there is no toll on it, and that is not a figure of nothing and it is not a figure at all, it is a thing that is not there. A man is a person and a building is a building and a building cannot pay anybody, and if the building were a holder then the toll would fall on whoever is standing inside it, and inside it there is a woman who carries water to eleven houses, a child, and about four other people, and not one of them has been asked whether they would take that building on, and I am not going to be the one who says a name in this yard for that.**"

About nine people in that yard were standing still. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said one thing out loud and it was short and nobody counted it.

"**A road is a thing a man looks after and a man is a holder of it. If a man can be a holder of a road then a man can be a holder of that building and somebody has to pay somebody.**"

And the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing back, in about four seconds, and three people in that yard said afterwards that it was the shortest thing anybody had said about a toll in five months.

"**Then somebody has to pay somebody, and that somebody is inside a building, and you cannot ask a building who is in it.**"

Nobody in that yard read any of it back from the top. That has happened on three days in this month now. The woman of fifty-eight was not asked and has not offered and said out loud at the top of that bank on the eleventh of this month that she is not going to do it again, and nobody asked her to, and the page in her own hand says that the rule costs nothing to keep and was not kept on this day, and that nobody can be made to keep it, and she was not asked to say so and did not offer.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid and the count is still five, and that a toll is a thing a holder pays under a term, and that there is no holder, and that a day somebody spends on a thing in this district is not a toll and did not become one today.

---

At about two the clerk of nineteen years wrote a second line in that lot book and about nine people in that yard watched her do it, and the figure in it came out of the mouth of the man of about thirty-one who digs loam and out of nobody else's.

He had said at about one that there was about a foot of standing water in that ditch at the near end and about two inches at the far end, and that he had put his bar into it twice this month, and that the outlet at the low end has been silted up since before he came to that ditch. Nobody asked him for a figure and he gave one, and two people in that yard said out loud that is the first time anybody has said a figure about a thing in that yard on purpose and that the thing is a ditch, and he said that a ditch is a thing a man can put a bar into and that is the whole of what he knows about it.

She wrote it down and put the stone back on the corner and the page lifted at two joints in the wind the way it does.

> **One lot. A ditch about three feet deep behind a building with two doors. About a foot of standing water in it this morning. No person in it.**

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the lot book has two lines written in it out of five ruled ones, and that both of them give a figure of a lot and not one figure of a person, and that the figure on the second of the two came out of the mouth of a man who is not in either line, and that a lot book is the tenth of what this district has made and is not a seventh of the six it has built and not named, and that the man who gave the figure was not asked to sign it and has not signed anything.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road read that second line out loud standing up at about half past two and got it right, and nobody read it back from the top, and three people in that yard said out loud that there were two lines in that book now and that a book with two lines in it is still a book with nothing in it to hold, and he said that he had not said that and had only read it.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the end of that trestle table for about four minutes after that and said out loud, to nobody in particular, that a figure about a ditch is a figure about a ditch and that his name is not on it and he would like it to stay off it, and he was not asked to sign it and did not offer to, and a man who would like his name kept off a page has not been entered in one.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds came up out of that ditch at about half past four, wet to the waist, with his bundle in his right hand and his hook in his left, and put both of them down against the wall, and crossed that yard and sat down in the second chair for about a minute, and got up again, and picked his hook and his bundle up off the ground where he had put them, one in each hand.

About nine people in that yard watched him do it and about four of them said nothing and three of them said that is the first one to sit in that chair today, which is a figure about a chair and is not a figure about the two men in it.

Nobody in that yard ran the bid and nobody proposed anything about closing it and nobody said the word out loud today, and it stands at forty-three days and was not run. The term on that reading is fourteen days past and did not renew. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read three things out loud in that yard today and no name of his went into it and he did not ask for one.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, carried them up the two goes, and read both lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, and said nothing to anybody, and nobody in that yard said one word to her about any of it, and the page says not asked.

Along that lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall on the eighty-fourth night of his run and eighty-four days in this district, with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and he was not asked about the toll and was not asked about the holder and was not asked to be either, and a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way.

That night the second chair was still at the end of that trestle table and the lot book was under the stone with two lines in it out of five, and two people in that yard said out loud on their way out that a district with two lots in a book and no holder for either one had got further along in a month than it had in a year, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud that it had not got further along and that two lines is two lines, and nobody counted either of those and nobody has claimed them since.
