# Chapter 357: Five Things to Say

---

The sixteenth of the ninth month came in with the light coming off the low ground before the cloud had finished going, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table that morning instead of three, and the fourth was at the end of the line in a hand about half the size of the other three, and about nine people in that yard looked at it before anybody said anything about it. The man of fifty-six read the middle one of the four out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other three. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the fifty-seventh night of his run and fifty-six days in this district, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and sixty-five days on that board and two hundred and ninety-five days that nobody has entered anything, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is a hundred and seventy-five days, and two hundred and fifty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that a fourth figure on the end of a table is a figure about a table and is not a figure about the month it is a figure of, and that the fourth of them was put there on the fifteenth of this month at the request of a man who reads figures out loud and not by a clerk, and that a figure a man has asked for is not a figure the man holds.

---

He got to the end of his own reading at about eleven and it took him about eleven minutes, and nothing in that yard said at the time that it was going to be the last day of that run on which anybody got to the end of it.

A document that sets a thing out has five things in it. He said them out loud first, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people. What he said was counted and got a hundred and eighty-four, and the man of about nineteen had it in his own words.

"**The lot, the term, the toll, the holder and the remedy. That is the whole of it and I have got through two of them in six days and I am going to try to get through the rest of them this morning in daylight in front of all of you, and I am telling you now that I do not think I am going to be able to, and I would rather say that at the start than be found out at the end. The lot is that building. The term is a month, and I have said that out loud in this yard twice and the reason for it is in a book four hundred yards up that bank. The toll is a thing a holder pays under a term, and there is no holder, so the third one of the five is a thing that cannot be said, and a document that sets out a toll with nothing to fill it in is a document with a hole in it and a hole is not a figure and not a rate.**"

About nine people in that yard were standing in the sun. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the wire on the boards and had stopped moving his hand.

Then he came to the fourth of the five, and he stopped, and he did not start it.

He stood at the end of that trestle table for about four seconds and then he said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, that he was not going to say the fourth thing and was not going to say the fifth one either, and he gave the reason for both in the same breath. The count came to a hundred and eighty-two and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**The fourth one is the holder, and a holder is a person, and there is no person in this yard to hold anything. I could say a word there and about nineteen of you would take it for a name, and a name put on a figure cannot be taken off it by the person it is put on, and I said out loud in this yard in the first week of this month that I have not given anybody one word to call me by and I am not starting now for a building. And the fifth one is the remedy, which is what a holder can do about a toll, and if there is no holder there is nobody to do anything and a document that sets out a remedy that nobody can use has found nobody. So I have got two of the five out loud and I am going to stop in front of about nineteen people, and I would rather be a man who stopped than a man who read a thing out that is not there.**"

The yard was quiet after that for about four seconds. Nobody said anything. About nine people looked at the ground and two of them looked at the woman of fifty-eight, who was in that yard with two empty buckets by the trough side, and then looked away again.

The clerk of nineteen years was about nine feet off and had her pencil out before he had finished the second half of it, and she wrote for about two minutes, and the man who convened the bid stood where he was and watched her do it, and about nine people in that yard watched both of them. What went on the page is that the reading of that lot is begun and is not finished, and that it has got to the third of the five things such a paper has to carry, and that the fourth of the five is a person and there is no person in that yard. She put down that she did not enter a name and no name went anywhere. She put down that the fifth of the five is a remedy, and that a document which names a remedy that does not exist has found nobody, and that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he is not going to say the rest of his own reading has not refused to say it and cannot be counted either way.

He asked her about the page, in that yard, in front of about nineteen people, at about half past eleven.

He asked her what a clerk writes under a reading that stopped, and she said that she writes what was said and where it stopped, and that she would write the word, because a clerk writes what was said and not what was meant. He said that he did not want the word written down. She said that he had said the word out loud in daylight in a yard in front of about nineteen people and that she was not going to be the second half of a thing that happened, and that a clerk does not do what a person asks her to about a page when the person is standing in front of her saying it, because a page is not a yard and a yard is where a thing gets said out loud and a page is where it is put down, and that a thing said out loud in a yard and then taken back off a page is a thing that was never said, and that it was said.

About nine people in that yard were listening to two people disagree about a word, and the man of about nineteen counted both sides of it and got two figures where he had expected one, and he wrote them both down and did not know what to do with either.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who has asked a clerk not to write a word and has been refused has not been given a post and has not refused one, and that a page is not a yard and is not a counter and a table with four legs outside a door is not a counter until somebody asks somebody a question at it, and that the word is on the page and a word on a page is not a signature and is not a consent and carries no job.

---

The rest of that morning was the argument about what had just happened, in the way that a yard is a room with no roof and a yard is where a thing gets said out loud.

The man of fifty-six said that a reading that stops in the middle is not a reading, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that a thing said out loud in a yard and not finished is not a reading and is not a document and is not a fifth column of not-askings, and the man of fifty-six said he had not said it was a fifth column of anything and that he was saying it was not a reading, and the two of them stood about nine feet apart at the end of that table and neither of them said anything else about it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two men in a yard who disagree about whether a thing that stopped in the middle is a reading have both said a thing out loud and neither of them has read a thing back from the top, and that a reading is read back from the top by a second person and a thing read back by nobody binds nobody, and that a party is not a person and a figure is not a person and has no way of saying no.

At about one the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went up the lane to that building with about four people and came back with the two terms that are on the table with four legs against its outside wall, which are two lines on one sheet dated the thirteenth of the last month, and one of the two is about a sound, and he put them back on that table in about four minutes and said nothing about either of them and nobody in that yard asked to hear either of them read out.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that two terms on a table were carried four feet and put back down and that neither of them was read out loud in that yard, and that a term is a figure about a term, and that a pair of terms is a document and is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own, and that a term with no end written on it is a condition with no end on it and there are four of those and this is not a fifth.

The offer that has been on a table in this district for seven months has four lines in it and the fourth of them has not been read out loud in that yard today or on any day of this month, and the refusals to read are nine, and the departure has not been spent, and there is no date in any ledger for the next reading.

At about three the man the figure of twenty-one years is against went up the lane about nine feet past that building and did not go into it and did not go further up the bank, and came back down, and about four people in that yard saw him do it and none of them asked him where he had been.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane at about half past three with the shafts down and about nine people in that yard watched him not come in, because a yard is a room with no roof and a cart does not fit in one, and he stood at the mouth of the lane for about ten minutes and then went on up the road, and nobody in that yard sent anybody out to him and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past four with two empty buckets and filled them at the trough and put them inside that building, and she was in that yard at about the time he had stopped and she said nothing about it then and she has said nothing about it since.

The light had gone off the ground by about half past eight. The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat beside him and his hands where he keeps them, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about what he is going to do with them. The number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven and the bid in that yard has been open fifteen days and the reading of the lot has got to the third of five things and is not finished.

**TWO OF THE FIVE THINGS A DOCUMENT THAT SETS A THING OUT HAS TO CARRY WERE SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD AND THE THIRD COULD NOT BE SAID AT ALL, AND THE FOURTH, WHICH IS A PERSON, AND THE FIFTH, WHICH IS WHAT A PERSON COULD DO ABOUT IT, WERE NOT SAID, AND HE SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO SAY THEM.**
