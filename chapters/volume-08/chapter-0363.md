# Chapter 363: Read Back From the Top

---

The twenty-second of the ninth month came in clear and hard with the light coming straight off the low ground and about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, standing in the shade of that building because there was no other. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read the bottom one of the four out loud over the mender's shoulder, got the other three without stopping on them, and then read the bottom one again on his own and got it the same. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the sixty-second night of his run and sixty-two days in this district, and he had slept on that low wall on sixty-one of the sixty-two nights of that run.

"**Two hundred and seventy-one days on that board and five hundred and eighty-seven days on the train on the siding, and nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and seventy-fourth days since the first of the fourth month, counting that first day in, and the table against that wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood seventy-seven mornings.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and is the same figure it was at ten the day before, and that the man of about sixty-four was not asked about the night he did not sleep on and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that this clerk has that night out of a ladder and a ladder is a figure about a ladder.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane at about eleven with the shafts down and stopped at the head of it, and about nine people in that yard saw him stop and none of them said a word to him about it. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that trestle table with the cloth on his left hand and he had about nine feet of wire in the right pocket of his coat and about nine inches of fresh wire in his fingers, and he put one thing out loud into that yard in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people. The count came to a hundred and forty-one and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I want to say one thing in that yard and I am not going to say it twice. A fence is not a wall. A fence is a thing a man puts up so that a thing on the far side of it stays where it is, and it does not keep anything in, because anything that wants to get past a fence can get past it, and what it does is tell a person standing on the near side that there is a far side. And that is all a yard is. Four figures on the end of a table and a man saying them out loud so that a person standing in it can find out what day it is, and then going away again. That is the whole of the trade and there is nothing else in it.**"

Then the man at the head of that lane read it back from the top.

He read it back from the top, in the ordinary voice, standing where he was, at about nine feet from the end of that trestle table, and he got most of it. He left out the part about a fence not keeping anything in, and he turned the last sentence round into a question, and about nine people in that yard heard him do it and nobody in that yard said one word while he was doing it.

Nobody had asked him. He had not said that he would. He was a man of about twenty-nine with a cart who had been in a yard in this district three times this month and had not been asked to do anything at all on any of them.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a second person read a thing back from the top in that yard in daylight this morning, and that it is the first time that has happened in this district, and that nobody in that yard asked the person who read it back to do it at all, and the record about the not asking says the same thing it says every day, which is not asked, and that somebody who has done that is not a keeper of it and is not a second reader and has not been given a post, and that nothing went into the column that is ruled and empty and nothing went anywhere.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said the whole of it again, from the top, and not from the word.

He did not look at the man at the head of the lane and he did not look at the man of about nineteen and he did not say that the first one had been got wrong. He went back to the first word and said the same thing again in the same voice at the same speed, and the man of about nineteen counted it a second time and got a hundred and forty-one, which is the figure the first time, and wrote it down twice.

About nine people in that yard watched a man say a thing twice because a man with a cart had read it back from the top and had not got all of it, and about four of them looked at the man the figure of twenty-one years is against while he was doing it, and he said nothing.

A thing read back wrong is read back again from the top and not from the word. A man who says the same thing twice out loud in a yard has not lost anything and has not been given anything, and about nine people in that yard heard a man be wrong about a fence, and nine people hearing a thing is not a finding and is not a habit.

Then the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart spoke, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and eighty-four, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**I have read things back from the top twice before this month and both of those were in the seventh month and neither of them was in a yard. I am telling you now that I do not know whether I had that right just now, because a man who reads a thing back from the top does not know. That is the whole of what a second person is for. If I knew whether I had it right I would not be a second person, I would be the same man again. So do not go away from here this morning thinking a thing has been settled, because a man in a coat at the end of a lane has read a sentence about a fence back to a man about nine feet off a table and one of us was not sure. I am not saying no to anything. Nobody has asked me anything. I have a cart standing at the head of this lane with four miles in front of me and I would rather go and do the four miles.**"

About nine people in that yard said nothing at all. The man of fifty-six said that it was the fourth morning of that run on which somebody in that yard had said a thing and had not finished it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that it was the fourth and that he was the one who had said it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man has said out loud in that yard that he does not know whether he read a thing back correctly, and that he has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and that a second person who does not know whether he is right is still a second person and is not a reader and is not a keeper of a reading, and that nobody in that yard was asked to be one and nobody in that yard offered to be one.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart backed out of that lane at about ten past twelve and there was no cart at the head of it after that. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the wire into the right pocket of his coat at about one o'clock and went up that lane on foot to the head of the loam and was not back in that yard at any point after that.

He set nine feet of it into the gate at the head of the loam, with a knot in it about the length of a finger, and it took him the rest of that day. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was at the top of that ditch with a bar of iron in his right hand held down at his side and he watched a man with one hand put wire into his own gate for about nine minutes and then went back to the loam, and he did not say one word about it then and he has not said one word about it since, and on the eighteenth of this month he said out loud in that yard that he had not asked that man for an inch of it.

About four people in that yard said, one after another and in the ordinary way, that the man who mends fencing had done a day's work and ought to be paid for it, and the man of fifty-six said that a day of work is not a toll and that there is no way in this district to pay a person who is not in a household, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and it is still not paid, and the count is still five.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has done a day's work in this district for nothing has not been given a post and has not refused one, and that a day's work is not a lot and not a toll and is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and that he was not asked for the wire and he did not offer it and nobody in that yard asked him for an inch of it.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane at about four with his boots black to the ankle and said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and what he said was counted and got a hundred and forty-seven, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I walked eleven miles of that road before the light and I am going to say one figure out loud in this yard and I am not going to be asked about it. Eleven. That is a figure about a road. It is not a figure about me and it is not a figure about my boots and it is not a figure about the four gates off it, and there is nothing else in this district this month that eleven is a figure about. I am not a man with eleven miles. I am a man who knows where that road goes and I have said in this yard that knowing where a road goes is not holding it and I have not changed my mind about that in four days, and I am not going to be asked about it today by anybody standing here.**"

About four people in that yard said nothing back and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in it to hear it. Eleven is a figure about a road and is not a figure about a man, and the man who said it told that yard in advance that he was not going to be asked about a figure, and he has not refused to answer and cannot be counted either way, and he was not asked and did not have to answer a question about a figure.

---

The reading of that lot stands where it stopped on the sixteenth, at the third of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and it has not got to the fourth of them, which is a person, and the fifth of them, which is a remedy, has not been said either. The bid in that yard had stood open twenty-one days on the twenty-second of the ninth month and had not been run on one of them and no offer had ever stood on it, and nobody had proposed anything about closing it.

The light went off that yard at about half past eight and the figure on that sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and will be the same in the morning, and the first thing anybody read back from the top in this district was read back from the top on this day by a man with a cart who said afterwards that he did not know whether he had got it right.

The boy of about nineteen had that minute open on the boards of that trestle table by about one and shut it again with no figure in it, because a man with a cart had said out loud that he did not know whether he had read a thing right and there was nothing to write down. He put the minute under his arm and went up that lane, and nobody in that yard asked him what he was going to do with it.
