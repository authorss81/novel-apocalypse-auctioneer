# Chapter 364: The Fourth of the Five

---

The twenty-third of the ninth month came in with the cloud sitting low over the low ground and about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the top one of the four out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other three. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the far end of that table with the cloth on his left hand and about nine feet of wire in the right pocket of his coat and nothing in his right fingers, because he had put all of it into a gate at the head of the loam the day before and had said out loud in that yard that he was not a man who fixes things for a district. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the sixty-third night of his run and sixty-three days in this district.

"**Two hundred and seventy-two days on that board and five hundred and eighty-eight days on the train on the siding, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is a hundred and eighty-two days, and the rival record has been in force a hundred and thirteen days and is unanswered.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on that sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and that it is a figure about the people who answered a door and says nothing about anybody standing in that yard this morning, and that its own age as a figure about itself is a hundred and thirteen days, and that a record in force and a figure's own age are two figures counted off one day and are not one figure, and that the man of about sixty-four was not asked anything and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against went to the end of that trestle table at about half past ten and did not stand about nine feet off it.

He said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in daylight, in front of about nineteen people, and the count came to two hundred and forty-six and the boy wrote it out in his own hand.

"**I am going to go back to that reading in front of about nineteen of you and I am going to say the fourth one and the fifth one, and I want everybody in this yard to know before I start that on the sixteenth of this month I said out loud that I was not going to say either of them, and I have not changed my mind about what they are. The fourth of the five is the holder, and a holder is a person, and there is no person in this yard to hold this lot. The reason there is no person is that I have put this lot in front of about nineteen people for twenty-two days and not one of them has come forward, and I am not going to read out a name, and I am not going to read out a word and let about nineteen of you put a name to it. The fifth of the five is the remedy, which is what a holder can do about a toll, and there is no holder, so there is nobody to do anything, and a document that sets out a remedy that nobody standing in it can use has found nobody. So I have got three of the five out loud and I am going to stop there again, in daylight, in front of all of you, and I would rather do that twice than say the fourth thing.**"

He stopped. The yard was quiet for about four seconds. Nobody in that yard said anything, and about nine people in it looked at the ground, and about four people in it looked at the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, who was standing at the far end of that table with nothing in his right fingers, and then looked away again.

About four people in that yard said, one after another and in the ordinary way and not all at once, that the fourth of the five ought to be a man who looks after that building, and that somebody has to be named for it, and that a building in this district with a term on it and no name against it is a building nobody will see to in the winter. None of those four people said the word out loud, and all four of them meant it.

Four people in a yard saying that a document ought to have a person in it is four people saying a thing and is not a man being offered anything and is not a man being asked anything. A document that names a job that does not exist has found nobody, and a building is not a lot and a lot is not a building and the two are not added together.

---

The man of about nineteen did not count it.

He had counted every thing said out loud in that yard since the first of this month. He had it in his own words on about nineteen separate pages and two of those pages are in a book in a room four hundred yards up that bank. He stood about nine feet off the end of that trestle table with his own page under his arm and he did not count what that man had said, and afterwards he wrote one line on the page in his own hand, and it says that he did not count it and that he does not know why he did not count it.

About four people in that yard saw a man of about nineteen not write a figure down and none of them said one word to him about it. He was nobody's clerk and he had not been asked to be anybody's clerk and he was not asked for the figure and did not give it, and nobody in that yard asked him for it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of about nineteen who counts what people say in daylight yards and lanes in front of about nineteen people did not count a thing said out loud in a yard this morning and entered that he did not count it and does not know why, and that a man who does not know why he did not do a thing has not refused it and cannot be counted either way.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the reading of that lot has now got to the fourth of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and that the fourth of the five is a person and there is no person in that yard to say, and that the fifth of the five is a remedy and there is nobody who can use one, and that a man who has put the five things out loud in that yard twice in seven days and stopped in the same place both times has not refused to say the rest of it and cannot be counted either way, and that no name went into any column this morning.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about a quarter to twelve, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and the count came to a hundred and sixty-nine and the boy wrote it out in his own hand.

"**Nobody in this yard has asked that man to let anybody else read a word of it back from the top, and nobody in this yard has asked me either, and I want it written down that neither of those things happened today, because in a week somebody is going to come in here and say that a man in a cloth stood at this table and read a lot out loud in a yard and that is the same as reading it back. It is not. I said the inside of that building in a lane on the fourteenth of this month because a man could not hold a door. I have said four sentences about a fence in this yard. That is all I have done in that direction and I am not going to do one inch more of it because about nineteen of you have asked me nothing, and a man who is asked for nothing has not refused and cannot be counted either way.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that nobody has asked him for anything has not refused a post and cannot be counted either way, and that nobody in that yard asked him to be anything, and that the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is ruled and was empty at the end of that morning, and that the whole of what that column would have to hold is one man and there is no figure on any page in this district for who.

---

The word came up in that yard at about four and it came up because the man of fifty-six put it there, and he put it there carefully.

He said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and seventy-seven, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been in this basin a long time and I have never once heard the word keeper said out loud in a yard about a building, and I have got it in my head this morning because four people have just said a thing four times without saying it. I am going to say it once so that it is on a page somewhere and then I am going to leave it alone. A keeper is a person a thing is kept by. That is the whole of the word and there is no other half of it. There has never been a keeper of that building in this district on any page anybody has ever read, and there never was one, and if a sheet went up in this yard tomorrow with a job on it and no name under the job, then that sheet would not have found anybody, and I would rather be the man who said the word once than the man who had it written down twice with nothing under it.**"

About nine people in that yard said nothing back. A keeper is a person a thing is kept by, and no keeper of that lot is on any page in this district and there never was one. A printed line may not carry a job and a mark may not carry a job, and a column with a word over it and nothing under it is a question this district asked itself on the eighth of this month and has not answered, and nobody in that yard asked the man of fifty-six to say that word.

---

The rest of that day was the day going. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came into that yard at about one and went out at about half past one and said nothing to anybody, and his right arm did not go above the level of that shoulder. The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about three with his boots black to the ankle, read the four figures off the boards of that trestle table as he went past it, got all four, and went on up the lane without stopping, and nobody in that yard asked him to and nobody in that yard said to him that he had done a thing. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and was not in that yard at any point on this day, and about four people in that yard said at about four that he would be in on one of the other days of that week, and nobody in that yard said what for, and nobody in that yard asked him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself, and she did not ask anybody anything on this day and nobody in that yard asked her anything about the page that is still on the end of that trestle table under a stone.

The term on that lot is a month, and a month has an end on it, and a clerk entered that end out of a ladder on the day the term was set and has not said it out loud to anybody in that yard on any day of that run, and a ladder is a figure about a ladder and not a figure about a term and not a figure about anybody.

The light went off that yard at about half past eight and the number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven. The bid in that yard had stood open twenty-two days and had not been run on one of them, and no offer had ever stood on it, and the reading of that lot has now been said out loud three times in a yard and has got to the fourth of the five things and has not got past it, and a man of about nineteen did not count the second time and does not know why.

**A MAN SAID THE FOURTH OF THE FIVE THINGS OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD AND THE FOURTH OF THE FIVE IS A PERSON, AND A MAN OF ABOUT NINETEEN WHO COUNTS EVERYTHING SAID IN THAT YARD DID NOT COUNT IT, AND NOBODY WAS ASKED TO READ IT BACK.**
