# Chapter 346: A Day the Machinery Stood In

---

The fifth of the ninth month came in hot by eleven and the yard of Lot Seventeen had about nineteen people in it by ten, the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of that trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the middle one out loud over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the forty-sixth night and has been in this district forty-five days.

"**Two hundred and fifty-four days on that board and five hundred and seventy days on the train, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and thirty-seven days back, and the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved for a hundred and thirty-six days, and the first day of the eighth month is thirty-four days past this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved, and that its own age as a figure about the figure is ninety-five days, and that the man of about forty-eight was asked for that figure on no day this week and is in no day of this week, and that a clerk has it out of a ladder and did not ask him for it.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart went out of that lane before six in the morning with eleven sacks in the bed of it and was four miles off by the time the light came up. He is out on that road four days in a week and this was one of the four. About nine people in that yard asked after him during the morning, in the ordinary way, and nobody asked him to be there, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man being asked after is not a man being asked for and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was in that yard from about ten until about six and nobody in it asked him to do anything at all. He had the cloth on his left hand and has had it since the eleventh of the sixth month, and that hand has not closed since that day and did not close today. He took a length of wire off the end of that trestle table at about ten, where it had been lying since the first of the sixth, and bent it round a form with the fingers of his right hand, and set the form on the boards, and picked up another length, and did that for about seven hours with about four minutes' rest in it, and nobody asked him what the wire was for and he did not say.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road walked the lane from about eleven until about two and came back past the end of that trestle table and went up the bank and did not stop in that yard, and at about four he walked it again and came back at about six. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who walks a lane twice in a day is not a bearer of that lane and has not been asked to be one, and that eleven miles walked out of a sound in the seventh month and four hundred yards of this lane walked today are two figures and are not added together.

---

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the five lines of print on the end of that trestle table out loud twice in the afternoon, once at about two and once at about half past four, for the people who had come in off the lane and had not been in that yard all day. He read them with his right hand flat on the boards to keep the page still. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was four miles off and was not in that yard for either reading, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a document read out loud twice to two different sets of people in one afternoon is a document that has been said out loud and is not a thing read back from the top by a second person, and that the rule of the counter is not a rule about a man with a cart and has never once been.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against is the man who convened this bid and is not the man who reads in it, that he lost the reading on the twenty-third of the eighth month and did not ask for it back, and that he has not asked for it back since and that asking for a reading back is not a figure about anybody and is not a figure about a hand.

---

At about one the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the ninth length of wire down on the boards of that table and rubbed his right thumb and the first two fingers of that hand together for about a minute, and then picked up the tenth length and bent it, and about nine people in that yard watched him do the rubbing and not one of them said anything about it.

At about half past three the man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood at the end of that trestle table and found that there was nothing on it. A bid was opened on the first of the ninth month in that yard at about six in the evening in front of about nineteen people and it has not been called for since, and this morning it was not called for either, and there was a chair at the end of that table and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a chair is a chair and that nothing was done with it this morning and that a chair is not a ladder.

He said one thing out loud at about half past three, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and seventy-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**This yard has had a bid in it for four days and on five of those six days there has been nothing whatever on the end of that table, and I have been in this yard every one of them, and this morning a man has been bending wire at that end of it for seven hours and nobody asked him what it was for, and a man I need for the third of the three reasons this bid cannot close is four miles off on a road with eleven sacks in his bed, and I did not send for him and I am not going to send for him. A district that builds an instrument and then stands about in it on one day out of ten is not a district that has built nothing. It is a district that has built something it has not got a reason to run yet, and I would rather say that out loud in this yard this afternoon than have about nineteen people find it out for themselves in a month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he has built something he has not got a reason to run has not withdrawn the thing and cannot be counted either way, and that a figure about how often a district stands about in an instrument is a figure about a district and is not a figure about any man in it, and that a figure of mornings is a figure about a morning and is not a figure about the man who stood at it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and thirty-seven days back and was named in that yard this morning and closed in none of the ten days of this month, and that a plank does not keep nights, and that the refusals to read are nine and there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said one thing out loud at about half past five, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and forty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have walked that lane four times today and I know every soft place in it between the trough and the top of that bank, and I have been asked in that yard this month by three different people whether I would stand in a lane at night, and I have said no to all three with a reason of my own, and I am not going to be asked a fourth time and I am not going to stand in that yard this evening and be looked at. If that building needs a person in it in the wet then the person in it is going to be somebody who has asked to be in it and not somebody a yard has found standing about in it. That is all I have got and I am saying it once.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he will not stand in that yard and be looked at has not refused to be a holder of anything and cannot be counted either way, and that a man who knows where a road goes is not holding a road and is not a keeper of one, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about half past five the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the last length of wire down on the end of that table and could not get the fingers of his right hand straight to undo his own boot lace, and stood there with his foot up on the crossbar of the trestle for about a minute. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came over and undid it for him in about nine seconds and neither of them said one word about it and about nine people in that yard watched it happen.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who undoes another man's boot lace has not taken on a post and has not been given one, and that a hand that cannot close on a lace after seven hours of bending wire is not a figure about an instrument, and that a clerk may not say that a man whose trade is over is a failure and may not say the other thing either.

---

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came into that yard at about one and went out again at about two and said nothing to anybody. The man of fifty-six was at the end of that table from about ten until about three and then went and sat down on the low wall along the lane at the far end of it, where about four people were already sitting, and he did not say anything out loud all afternoon except the three figures over the mender's shoulder in the morning, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man of fifty-six who has said the same standing sentences in every month of this basin has not said one this afternoon and that a sentence not said is not a finding and cannot be counted either way.

The woman of fifty-eight went down that bank with two buckets at about eleven and again at about four, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that she was not asked about anything on this day and that she has asked that woman at the end of that bank about a bucket twice in nineteen years and both times about a bucket, and that nobody in that yard asked her about the bid.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the foot of the east wall of that yard with his hand-cart against it and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month. He asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing one question at about four about the wire, and the answer was that it was wire, and he said that was all right and put his hands in his coat. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has lost the use of both hands asking another man one question about a length of wire is not a figure about the wire, and that a tent rolled on a hand-cart is not a shelter.

The light was off the yard by half past eight and the end of that trestle table was bare, and the sheet on that gatepost four hundred yards up the lane had not moved off four hundred and eleven.

**AND ON THE FIFTH OF THE NINTH MONTH A DISTRICT BUILT AN INSTRUMENT AND STOOD ABOUT IN IT, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT A FIGURE OF MORNINGS IS A FIGURE ABOUT A MORNING AND IS NOT A FIGURE ABOUT THE MAN WHO STOOD AT IT, AND A MAN WHO UNDID ANOTHER MAN'S BOOT LACE WAS NOT GIVEN A POST FOR IT, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
