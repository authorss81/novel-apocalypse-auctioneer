# Chapter 346: A Day the Machinery Stood In

---

The fifth of the ninth month came in hot by eleven and there was no cloud on it by noon. The yard of Lot Seventeen had about nineteen people in it by ten. The three figures were on the end of the trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the middle one out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other two right, and the mender, who had a length of wire in his right hand from about ten, read them with the corner of his eye. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded beside him, on the forty-sixth night of his run and forty-five days in this district.

"**Two hundred and fifty-four days on that board and five hundred and seventy days on the train, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and thirty-seven days back, and the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved for a hundred and thirty-six days, and the first day of the eighth month is thirty-four days past this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved, and that its own age as a figure about the figure is ninety-five days, and that the man of about forty-eight was asked for that figure on no day this week and is in no day of this week, and that a clerk has it out of a ladder and did not ask him for it.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart went out of that lane before six in the morning with eleven sacks in the bed of it and was four miles off before the light came up. He is out on that road four days in a week and this was one of the four. About nine people in that yard asked after him during the morning, in the ordinary way, the way you ask after a dog, and nobody asked him to be there, and a man being asked after is not a man being asked for.

The mender was in that yard from about ten until about six and nobody in it asked him to do anything at all. He had the cloth on his left hand and has had it since the eleventh of the sixth month, and that hand has not closed since that day and did not close today. He took a length of wire off the end of that trestle table at about ten, where it had been lying since the first of the sixth, and bent it round a form with the fingers of his right hand, and set the form down on the boards, and picked up another length, and did that for about seven hours with about four minutes' rest in it. Nobody asked him what the wire was for and he did not say.

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road walked the lane from about eleven until about two and came back past the end of that trestle table and went up the bank and did not stop in that yard, and at about four he walked it again and came back at about six. A man who walks a lane twice in a day is not a bearer of that lane and has not been asked to be one, and eleven miles walked out of a sound in the seventh month and four hundred yards of this lane walked today are two figures and are not added together.

The mender read the five lines of print on the end of that trestle table out loud twice in the afternoon, once at about two and once at about half past four, for the people who had come in off the lane and had not been in that yard all day. He read them with his right hand flat on the boards to keep the page still, in between lengths of wire, without breaking the pace of either. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was four miles off and was in that yard for neither reading, and a document read out loud twice to two different sets of people in one afternoon is a document that has been said out loud and is not a thing read back from the top by a second person. The rule of the counter is not a rule about a man with a cart and has never once been.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the man the figure of twenty-one years is against is the man who convened this bid and is not the man who reads in it, that he lost the reading on the twenty-third of the eighth month and did not ask for it back, and that he has not asked for it back since, and that asking for a reading back is not a figure about anybody and is not a figure about a hand.

---

At about one the mender put the ninth length of wire down on the boards of that table and rubbed his right thumb and the first two fingers of that hand together for about a minute, and then he picked up the tenth length and bent it, and about nine people in that yard watched him do the rubbing and not one of them said anything about it.

At about half past three the man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood at the end of that trestle table and found that there was nothing on it. A bid was opened on the first of the ninth month in that yard at about six in the evening in front of about nineteen people and it has not been called for since, and this morning it was not called for either. There was a chair at the end of that table, and a chair is a chair, and nothing was done with it this morning, and a chair is not a ladder.

He said one thing out loud at about half past three, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and the minute took one hundred and seventy-nine words of it, in his own words.

"**This yard has had a bid in it for four days and on five of those six days there has been nothing whatever on the end of that table, and I have been in this yard every one of them, and this morning a man has been bending wire at that end of it for seven hours and nobody asked him what it was for, and a man I need for the third of the three reasons this bid cannot close is four miles off on a road with eleven sacks in his bed, and I did not send for him and I am not going to send for him. A district that builds an instrument and then stands about in it on one day out of ten is not a district that has built nothing. It is a district that has built something it has not got a reason to run yet, and I would rather say that out loud in this yard this afternoon than have about nineteen people find it out for themselves in a month.**"

Having said out loud in a yard that he has built something he has not got a reason to run has not withdrawn the thing and cannot be counted either way. A figure about how often a district stands about in an instrument is a figure about a district and is not a figure about any man in it, and a figure of mornings is a figure about a morning and is not a figure about the man who stood at it.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against had watched the whole seven hours of that from about nine feet off, and had gone up the bank once at about noon and come back, and had not asked the mender a single question about any of it. He had a use for the answer if the mender had offered one, and he had worked out somewhere around the fourth length of wire that he was not going to be given it, and that the best thing he could do for that man all day was to stand about nine feet off him and not interrupt him, which was a thing he had learned in this district the hard way and had no name for and was not going to look for.

That night of the twentieth of the fourth month is a hundred and thirty-seven days back, it was named in that yard this morning, and it is closed in none of the ten days of this month, and a plank does not keep nights. The refusals to read are nine and there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said one thing out loud at about half past five, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and forty, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have walked that lane four times today and I know every soft place in it between the trough and the top of that bank, and I have been asked in that yard this month by three different people whether I would stand in a lane at night, and I have said no to all three with a reason of my own, and I am not going to be asked a fourth time and I am not going to stand in that yard this evening and be looked at. If that building needs a person in it in the wet then the person in it is going to be somebody who has asked to be in it and not somebody a yard has found standing about in it. That is all I have got and I am saying it once.**"

Saying out loud in a yard that he will not stand in that yard and be looked at is not a refusal to be a holder of anything and cannot be counted either way, and a man who knows where a road goes is not holding a road and is not a keeper of one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about half past five the mender put the last length of wire down on the end of that table and could not get the fingers of his right hand straight to undo his own boot lace, and stood there with his foot up on the crossbar of the trestle for about a minute, working at it. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came over and undid it for him in about nine seconds, and neither of them said one word about it, and about nine people in that yard watched it happen.

A man who undoes another man's boot lace has not taken on a post and has not been given one, and a hand that cannot close on a lace after seven hours of bending wire is not a figure about an instrument, and a clerk may not say that a man whose trade is over is a failure and may not say the other thing either.

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came into that yard at about one and went out again at about two and said nothing to anybody. The man of fifty-six was at the end of that table from about ten until about three and then went and sat down on the low wall along the lane at the far end of it, where about four people were already sitting, and he did not say anything out loud all afternoon except the three figures over the mender's shoulder in the morning. A sentence not said is not a finding and cannot be counted either way.

The woman of fifty-eight went down that bank with two buckets at about eleven and again at about four, and she was not asked about anything on this day, and nobody in that yard asked her about the bid.

At the foot of the east wall the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the brick and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month. He asked the mender one question at about four about the wire, and the answer was that it was wire, and he said that was all right and put his hands in his coat. A man who has lost the use of both hands asking another man one question about a length of wire is not a figure about the wire, and a tent rolled on a hand-cart is not a shelter.

By six the nine forms stood in a row along the end of that table under the man's right forearm, all of them the same bend and none of them any use, and the wire that was left over was about four inches, and he put that in his pocket. Nobody asked him to keep them, nobody asked him to take them away, and about four people in that yard who had watched the whole day said nothing about any of it and went home.

The light was off the yard by half past eight and the end of that trestle table was bare, and the sheet on that gatepost four hundred yards up the lane had not moved off four hundred and eleven.

**A DISTRICT BUILT AN INSTRUMENT AND STOOD ABOUT IN IT, AND A MAN UNDID ANOTHER MAN'S BOOT LACE AND WAS NOT GIVEN A POST FOR IT, AND NINE FEET OF WIRE WENT ONTO A FORM IN ONE HAND OVER SEVEN HOURS FOR NOBODY.**
