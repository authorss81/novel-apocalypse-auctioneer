# Chapter 389: Four Accounts of One Sentence

---

The seventeenth of the tenth month came in with the low ground steaming off it and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The man of fifty-six read the four figures off the boards at the end of that trestle table out loud over nobody's shoulder and got all four, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the fence end of that lane and did not come back to that table until about one.

"**A hundred and thirty-eight days is how long that record has been standing in force and a hundred and thirty-eight days is how old the figure about that figure is, and two hundred and eighty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is seventy-seven days past and nobody in this yard has said one word about it today.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that nobody has put a line on a board in two hundred days counting the first of the fourth month in, and that a line in a lot book is a line about a lot and is not a line on that board.

---

Two people in that yard said out loud, between about ten and about twelve, that they were not there on the fourth of this month.

That is not an unusual thing to say in that yard and two more people said that they had been told about the fourth of this month and had not been there for it. About four people in that yard were not present on the fourth of this month, and being absent from a day is not a refusal of it, and the page in her own hand says that and not anything else, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in the bottom of that ditch before nine and came up the bank at about ten and stood at the end of that trestle table for about two minutes reading what the clerk of nineteen years had ruled on her page, and two people in that yard said that a man who reads a page standing up in a yard is reading a page he could walk up to, and he said that it was the first page in this yard he has been able to read without somebody standing in front of it.

The man of about nineteen counted two figures out loud before eleven and wrote them down, and two people in that yard said he has not counted the two on his own page for two days now and he said that a page of his own with a word missing out of the third of five headings on it is worth more to him than two figures, and three of them said that is a thing a man says and not a thing a clerk enters.

So between about one and about three, those people asked the man the figure of twenty-one years is against what he had said on the fourth of this month, out loud, in daylight, in front of about nine people, and he said it, and about four people heard it.

He said it in sixty-eight words, and it is the same answer he gave on the fourth of this month and it is shorter than that answer, and the man of about nineteen counted that answer at the time and the page for that day has two hundred and eighty-two words on it, and what was said today is not the same words as those and is the same answer, and nobody counted this one and nobody has claimed that count since.

"**This district has one book and it is on the end of that table and any person can walk up to it and read it, and there is one line in it, and the line gives a figure of that lot and not one person in it, and it is smaller than what that record has, and I am not going to pretend they are the same size.**"

Then the man of about nineteen told them the other thing, and he said that on the day the man of fifty-six read that line out loud nobody read it back from the top, and two people said that they had heard something different about that, and about four other people said that they had heard nothing about it at all, and one of the people who was not there on the fourth said out loud that what she had been told was that nothing in that yard had been said about that record at all.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that an account of a thing said in a yard on a named day is a figure about the accounts and is not a figure about the thing, and that a yard is a room with no roof and what gets said in one cannot be got back out of it, and that four people in that yard have four accounts of one morning and that none of the four is a record and that a clerk has not been asked which of the four is right and is not going to say.

---

At about half past two the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was told all four of the accounts, one after another, by about four different people, standing at the end of that trestle table, and he listened to all of them and then said one thing out loud, and the man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and twelve.

"**I am not going to correct one word of any of the four of them. What I said on the fourth of this month is what I said on the fourth of this month and I said it once and I am not going to say it again in this yard so that somebody can get it right this time, and a thing that four people have four accounts of is not a wrong thing, it is what a yard is. If one of you wants the right one then one of you has to have been there, and about four of you were not, and that is the whole of it.**"

The whole of that yard went still. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud one thing about that, and about four people heard it.

"**That is the first sensible thing anybody has said in this yard about a thing being said in it, and I have been in this yard five months and I have never once thought of it that way.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man let four wrong accounts of his own sentence stand in a yard on purpose, and that he was not asked to do that and did it anyway, and that a sentence he said once and will not say again is not a document and is not a ninth of anything, and that a record set against four places four hundred miles off has not been read out in this yard on this day and is in force a hundred and thirty-eight days and was answered once, out loud, on the fourth of this month, and is still in force.

---

At about four the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane with his shafts down and came into that yard.

The yard noticed it and two of them said something about it and about four of them said nothing. Nobody asked him anything. He was not asked to read the figures and he did not read them. He was not asked to read the line in the lot book and he did not read it. He crossed that yard and sat down in the second chair at the end of that trestle table for about a minute, and the yard watched the only man in this district who has ever read a thing back from the top sit down in a chair and read nothing at all, and about four of them said out loud, in the ordinary way, that is a thing to be looked at.

Then he got up and went out of that yard and up the road, and nobody in that yard asked him where he was going and he was not asked, and the page in her own hand says that a man who has read a thing back from the top once sat in a chair for a minute and read nothing, and that a sitting is a figure about a sitting and is not a finding and is not a habit.

Two people in that yard said out loud, while he was still in it, that the man who reads things back from the top is the man this district has been standing in a yard about for a month, and three others said out loud that he had just sat down for a minute and read nothing, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said that reading nothing for a minute is a thing he has never heard of anybody doing and that it may be the first honest thing in this month, and two people in that yard said that a man sitting in a chair is not a first honest thing and he said it to the boards and not to them.

---

The figure the man in that yard is afraid of is sixty-one days, and it is the interval a body four hundred miles off prints at, and it was named on the page in a yard on the first of this month with the day on it, and nothing came on that day and nobody went up that lane to look at that gatepost then and nobody has been up it since. The interval has been named out loud three times in a yard and is written on a page in a book four hundred yards from that gate. A body which prints does not arrive, and it has not arrived on any day of this month, and a thing which does not arrive has no face, and the clerk of nineteen years is not going to write one for it and has not been asked to.

The rest of that afternoon went ordinary. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in that ditch until about half past four and came up the bank with his right hand in his pocket. The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about five, read the four figures as he went past, got all four, and went on up, and nobody in that yard asked him about the eleven miles or about anything else. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in that ditch with his hook and his bundle until about half past four. The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the east wall with his cart and was given nothing.

That bid stands at forty-seven days and was not run today and nobody in that yard has said one word about what ought to be done about it. The term on that reading is eighteen days past and did not renew. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read nothing out loud after about one today, and two people in that yard said that a man who reads the most in this district going four hours without saying a figure out loud is doing something today and nobody in that yard asked him what. The man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall on the eighty-eighth night of his run and eighty-eight days in this district with nothing in his hands, and nobody in that yard or in that lane said one word to him about any of it.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, carried them up the two goes, and read the two lines in that lot book and the nine columns on the clerk's page standing up on her way past, and said nothing to anybody and nobody said one word to her.

Three people in that yard told one man what he had said and he did not put one of them right, and the clerk of nineteen years shut her page at about seven and put it inside her coat, and about nine people in that yard said good evening to one another on their way out. A clerk's page is the only account that yard has ever been allowed to keep and there was no page in it on the fourth of this month, and the four hundred and eleven is four hundred and eleven and did not move and is not a figure about anybody standing in that yard, and the page says not asked.
