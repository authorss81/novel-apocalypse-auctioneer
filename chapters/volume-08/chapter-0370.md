# Chapter 370: A Thing Read Back by Nobody

---

The twenty-ninth of the ninth month came in with the first of the light straight down that lane and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and it was the last day of that run. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read one of the four out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other three, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing, who had not been in that yard at all the day before, bent about nine inches of fresh wire at the end of that table with the fingers of his right hand and said nothing to anybody. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the sixty-ninth night of his run and sixty-nine days in this district, and he had slept on that low wall on sixty-eight of the sixty-nine nights of that run, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and seventy-eight days on that board and five hundred and ninety-four days on the train on the siding, and two hundred and sixty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and sixty-one days back.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet at that gatepost says four hundred and eleven, that it did not move this morning, and that she expects it to say the same on the day after this one, and that nobody in that yard asked the man of about sixty-four anything about the sixty-ninth night, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and a figure about a man who has been in this district sixty-nine days is a figure about a stay and not about a man.

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in the ordinary voice, in daylight, in front of about nineteen people, and it came to three hundred and twenty-one words, and the boy had it in his own hand.

"**I have stood in that yard every morning since the tenth of this month saying figures out loud and I have worked out two things about it and I am going to say both of them this morning in front of about nineteen of you, and then I am going to stop, and I am not going to stop because I have run out of things to say. The first is that a reading is not a thing a man says. It is a thing a man says and a second person reads back from the top, and there is one man in this district who has ever read a thing back from the top, and he is gone four days in a week, and on four days in seven there is nobody in this basin who can do it at all. The second is that I have known the first of those two things since the first of this month, and I said it out loud in that yard on the twentieth, and I have not done one single thing about it since, and neither has anybody else, and that is what twenty days of a district looking at its own instrument comes to. I am not going to write a rule about it. I am not going to ask that man to do it. I am not going to name anybody to anything. A rule nobody can be made to keep is a thing one man is carrying, and a man who digs loam said that out loud at the top of that ditch on the eighteenth of this month and nobody asked him whether it was true, and it is true, and I am not going to do one thing more about it this month, and I am saying that in front of about nineteen of you so that there is no mistake about it in this yard.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything back for about four seconds. The man of about nineteen wrote one line on his own page and read it back to himself with his lips not moving.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man has said out loud in that yard that he is not going to do anything about a thing he has found out, and that he has not refused the thing and cannot be counted either way. The price of a rule nobody can be made to keep is that one man ends up carrying it on his own, and a man who has said out loud that it is true has not been asked and was never asked and the record about the not asking says not asked. This is the second time that sentence has been said out loud in a yard in this district, and a sentence said twice in a yard is two sentences and not one sentence said twice. No rule was written, no column was filled, no name went into any column, and nobody in that yard was appointed to anything and nobody in that yard was offered anything.

---

The clerk of nineteen years wrote the day's entry on the page on the end of that trestle table at about half past eleven with a stone on the corner of it, because the wind was coming up that lane at about the rate it does on a day with nothing in it.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against put his right hand flat on the boards of that table to hold the corner of the page down while she got to the end of the line. His right hand has not closed properly since the tenth of the eighth month and it did not close on those boards, and it did not stay where he put it, and it went about four inches across the wet wood with the page going with it, and the corner of the page came off the stone and lifted about an inch in the wind.

About nine people in that yard saw that happen. The clerk of nineteen years did not stop writing, and did not say anything, and did not have to be asked, and she finished the line and then put the flat of her own left hand on the corner of the page and kept it there until the light on the mud of that lane changed about four minutes later, and neither of the two men who had a hand on that table said one word to the other about it before, during or after.

A man whose hand will not close on a wet board is not a figure about that board, and a hand that goes four inches across a table is not a signature and carries no job. A person who has had a page held down for him by somebody else has not given that person a post and has not given up one.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about half past four, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and ninety-three, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**I have bent about twelve feet of wire in that yard this month. Nine feet of it went into a gate at the head of the loam and about three feet of it went into a latch on a second gate, and I have not been asked for one inch of either of those and I have not offered one inch of either of them, and nobody in this yard has ever said my name with either of them in the same breath. I have stood at the end of that table most mornings of that run saying the other half of whatever it is somebody else has just said, and that is what I am for, and I am not going to stand here on the last morning of it and make it sound like more than it is. A district that has built one thing and cannot get it to do anything has still built it. I am not going to make that sound better than it is and I am not going to make it sound worse. I am going to put the wire in my pocket and go home.**"

About nine people in that yard said nothing at all, and about four of them said goodnight to him, and he said goodnight back to about four of them and to nobody else.

---

The rest of that day went the way the last day of a run goes. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the wire into the right pocket of his coat and went to the fence end of that lane. The man who digs loam came in at about two and went out at about half past two and said nothing to anybody. The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about four with his boots black to the ankle, read the four figures off the boards of that trestle table as he went past, got all four, and went on up the lane, and nobody in that yard asked him to.

The reading of that lot is begun and is not finished. It has got to the fourth of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and the fourth of the five is a person, and the fifth of them is a remedy and there is nobody in this district who can use one, and the man who convened the bid said the fourth of the five out loud in that yard on the twenty-third of this month and stopped there, and nobody in that yard asked him to go on with it at any point in the six days after that.

The bid in that yard was still open on the twenty-ninth of the ninth month. It had stood open twenty-eight days and had not been run on one of them and no offer had ever stood on it, and nobody proposed anything about closing it, and nobody proposed anything about it at all.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself, and she did not ask anybody anything on this day and nobody in that yard asked her anything, and the page with the twenty-first of the ninth month on it was still on the end of that trestle table under a stone.

At about six the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane with his shafts down and stopped at the head of it, and about nine people in that yard saw him stop and watched him not come in, and nobody in that yard sent anybody out to him and nobody in that yard said one word to him about anything, and he was not asked to do one thing or say one thing. He sat on the front bench of it with his hands on his knees for about nine minutes and then backed it out of that lane and went up the road, and about four people in that yard said afterwards that he would be in on one of the other days of that week, and nobody in that yard said what for.

The light went off that yard at about half past eight and the fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid and the count is still five. The number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the rival record has been in force a hundred and nineteen days and is unanswered, and the term on that lot has an end on it and nobody in that yard has ever said the date of it out loud, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is ruled and was empty at the end of that day and nothing went into it by anybody.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall until the light had gone with his coat folded beside him and nothing in his hands, and about four people in that yard said goodnight to one another on their way out and none of them said it to him and all of them would have said it to him if he had looked up.

**A MAN SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD THAT HE WAS NOT GOING TO DO ANYTHING ABOUT THE ONE INSTRUMENT THIS DISTRICT HAS, AND HE DID NOT, AND THE READING OF THAT LOT IS STILL BEGUN AND NOT FINISHED, AND THE BID IS STILL OPEN, AND A THING READ BACK BY NOBODY BINDS NOBODY.**
