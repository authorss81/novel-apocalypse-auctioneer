# Chapter 369: An End on It

---

The twenty-eighth of the ninth month came in with the cloud sitting on the low ground and no sun in that yard until about eleven, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, standing in about nine inches of mud that had come up the lane in the night. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read the bottom one of the four out loud over nobody's shoulder, because the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was nowhere in that yard that morning, and about nine people in that yard worked out that he was not coming before anybody said a word about it and none of them said a word. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the sixty-eighth night of that run and sixty-eight days in this district, and sixty-seven of those sixty-eight nights have been spent on that low wall.

"**Two hundred and seventy-seven days on that board and five hundred and ninety-three days on the train on the siding, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is a hundred and eighty-seven days, and the table against that wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood eighty-three mornings.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that four hundred and eleven is the figure on the sheet at that gatepost and did not move this morning, and that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not in that yard at all this morning, and that nobody in that yard asked him where he had gone and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a man not being in a yard is a fact about a morning and is not a finding about the man.

---

The man of fifty-six said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and the count came to a hundred and thirty-seven and the boy wrote it out in his own hand.

"**There is a term on that lot. It has an end on it, the way a term does, and I have read four figures off the end of that table most mornings of that run and I have never once been asked when it runs out, and I have never once offered to say. I am going to say this morning that I do not know, in front of about nineteen of you, and that I am not going to be told, and that a man who reads numbers off a board all morning is the last man in this basin anybody should ask about anything that is not on the board. A term is a figure about a term. I have never seen a day come round for one and I am not starting this month.**"

About nine people in that yard said nothing back, and about four of them looked at the man who convened the bid, and he was about nine feet off the end of that trestle table with his right hand in his coat and he said nothing at all.

The term on that lot has an end on it, and a clerk entered that end out of a ladder on the day the term was set and has not said it out loud to anybody in that yard, and that a ladder is a figure about a ladder and a term is a figure about a term and neither of them is a figure about anybody, and that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he does not know when a thing ends has not refused to be told and cannot be counted either way.

Nobody in that yard said one word about the end of that term, on this morning or at any point in that day, and about four people in that yard said afterwards, out loud and in the ordinary way, that they had not thought about it, and about four other people in that yard said that they had not thought about it either, and neither of those is a finding and neither is a habit.

---

The bid was still open. It had been opened in that yard on the first of the ninth month at about six in the evening in front of about nineteen people on a building with two doors, and it had stood open twenty-seven days and had not been run on one of them, and no offer had ever stood on it, and about four people in that yard said the words nothing has been offered and about four other people in that yard said the words nobody has run it, and those are two different things and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said so on the eighteenth of this month when he was in that yard and was not in that yard to say it again this morning.

Nobody in that yard proposed anything about closing it. Nobody in that yard proposed anything about opening it. Nobody in that yard proposed anything at all, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a bid which has stood open twenty-seven days without an offer on it and without anybody proposing anything about closing it is a bid and is not a market, and that a figure about how long a bid has stood is a figure about a bid and is not a figure about anybody standing in that yard.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came into that yard at about one with a bar of iron in his right hand held down at his side and stood at the end of that trestle table for about nine minutes and read the four figures off the boards on his own and got all four, and said out loud that his right arm does not go above the level of that shoulder and has not since the first of the seventh month, and did not point at them.

Then he said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and ninety-three, and it is in the minute in his own words, and he said the whole of it with his right arm held down at his side.

"**Twenty-seven days. That is a figure about a bid and it is not a figure about the four people who opened it and it is not a figure about the man standing at that table, and every one of you has been treating it as though it were a figure about one of you since the first of this month, and it is not. There is a ditch behind that building that is about three feet deep and there is about a foot of standing water in it, and nobody has asked me about it and nobody has paid me for it and it is not a lot and it is not a toll and it is not anybody's. A thing that stands still and gets wetter is not a thing that anybody is keeping. I am not saying close it. I do not know who could close it and I have not been asked and I am not offering. I am saying that twenty-seven days is a figure about a thing, and that a figure about a thing is not a reason for about nine people to be unhappy in a yard.**"

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane at about three with his boots black to the ankle and went out of that yard and up that road and did not stop, and nobody in that yard asked him anything and he was not asked about the eleven miles again, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was in the bottom of it with his hook and his bundle from about eleven until about half past four and came up the bank past the door of that building without saying one word about the building, and about nine people in that yard asked him, one after another and in the ordinary way, whether that building was going to be standing in the winter, and he gave them the same answer in about four seconds each time and went on up the lane, and the same answer given nine times in an afternoon is a shape noticed nine times and is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about the man who gave it.

At the foot of the east wall the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the brick with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and nobody in that yard asked him about either of those things and he was given nothing, and about four people in that yard said in the ordinary way that a man who puts a table up for anybody who will use it ought to have one of his own, and he said that he had one on a hand-cart and that it was rolled up, and that was the whole of what he said about it.

The page with the twenty-first of the ninth month on it was still on the end of that trestle table under a stone, and it had been there seven days, and about nine people in that yard had read the top line of it standing up, and about four of them had read the whole of it. A page a person can walk up to and read is not a book and is not the first of the two books four hundred yards up that bank, and a page on a table in a yard is a page in the rain, and it was still there.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and was not in that yard at any point on this day, and about four people in that yard said that he would be in the yard tomorrow because tomorrow was the other of his three days, and nobody in that yard said what for and nobody in that yard asked him anything, and about four people in that yard said the same thing tomorrow that they had said today and that is four people and not a finding.

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against went up the lane to the foot of that bank at about half past five and stood there for about four minutes and then turned round and came back down into that yard.

He had not gone up that bank since the thirteenth of this month. He went up it on that day and there was a rule in the room and he argued with it in that yard afterwards and lost, and he has not gone up it since, and this morning he stood at the foot of it for about four minutes and did not go up, and about four people in that yard saw him do that and none of them said anything to him about it, and nobody in that yard asked him where the woman who keeps those two books was, and he did not say, and that is the arrangement between the two of them and it is not on a page.

A man stood at the foot of a bank for about four minutes and did not go up it, and he has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way. The second of the two books was not touched and was not named and a clerk did not put that down either, and the woman who keeps those two books was not in that room and was not asked about.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself, and she went past him on the second of the two goes and neither of them said one word to the other, and she has gone up that bank about six thousand times and has never once asked him anything.

The reading of that lot is begun and is not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and the fourth of the five is a person, and the fifth of them is a remedy and there is nobody who can use one. The light went off that yard at about half past eight and the number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the first day of the eighth month is fifty-seven days past.

**A TERM ON THAT LOT HAS AN END ON IT AND NOBODY IN THAT YARD SAID ONE WORD ABOUT IT ALL DAY, AND A MAN WENT UP THE LANE AS FAR AS THAT BANK AND STOOD THERE AND THEN CAME BACK DOWN AGAIN, AND THE BID STOOD OPEN TWENTY-SEVEN DAYS.**
