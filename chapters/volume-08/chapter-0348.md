# Chapter 348: The Fifth of the Five

---

The seventh of the ninth month came in low and grey over the ridge, the kind of morning that does not so much begin as arrive, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The three figures were on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read the middle one of the three out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other two right. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded beside him, on the forty-eighth night of his run and forty-seven days in this district.

"**Two hundred and fifty-six days on that board and five hundred and seventy-two days on the train, and two hundred and forty-seven days from the second of January, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and thirty-nine days back, and the six households have not moved for a hundred and thirty-eight days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved, and that its own age as a figure about that figure is ninety-seven days, and that the man of about forty-eight who keeps those six households was not asked for that figure on this day and is in no day of this week.

---

At about half past ten the woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with two buckets and put them inside that building and came back out, and about nine people in that yard saw her do it and nobody said anything to her about it. She was not asked about the bid and has not been asked about it on any of the days it has been open, and a person who carries water to eleven houses is not a keeper of a bucket and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against put his left hand flat on the end of that trestle table and counted on the fingers of his right hand, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people. He counted to four and stopped.

He had done it once before, privately, on the bank, and the four had come out clean. What he had not allowed for was that the hand would do it differently in front of a yard than it had done on a bank in the rain, and it did, and there was nobody to ask about it.

He said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and the minute took two hundred and twenty words of it, in his own words.

"**There are five things this district does not have and I have counted four of them on my fingers and I am going to say the fifth one out loud because every person in this yard already knows it and nobody has said it and I am the one who has to say it. This bid has no way to pay anybody in it. That is the fifth of the five and it is the one this district has never once written down anywhere, and I have found that out this month by looking, and I am not going to pretend I have known it for a year. A toll is a thing a holder pays under a term, and there is no holder in that yard tonight and there is no toll and there is no figure on one, and if somebody writes a figure on one later that will be a price a man named out loud and a price is not a rate. So I am saying it here: a bid with no way to pay anybody in it is not a bid, and it is not a market, and it is not a settlement, and I opened it anyway on the first of this month with nothing in my hands, and I would do it again.**"

He got to four on his fingers and stopped because his right hand has not closed properly since the tenth of the eighth month and could not fold its last finger over, and he said the fifth thing out loud without a finger on it. A hand that cannot close on a fifth thing is not a refusal of it and is not a count of it, and the phrase was at zero in this district for a hundred and forty days and is on the page today.

About nine people in that yard did not move. Nobody argued with him. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing looked at the boards of that table and not at him, and the man of fifty-six said one word out loud, which was no, and then said nothing else. A man who has said one word out loud in a yard has not agreed with a figure and cannot be counted either way.

---

About half past twelve the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch came up the bank past the end of that trestle table with his bundle on his shoulder and his hook in his hand, and about nine people in that yard looked at him, and he did not stop and did not say anything, and about four of those nine people looked at the man the figure of twenty-one years is against instead, in the way that people in a yard look at one another when a thing is being said in it.

About four people in that yard asked him, one after another and in the ordinary voice, what a holder would have to pay, and whether it could be eleven coppers a week. He told all four of them that he did not know, and that eleven coppers a week is a price a man named out loud in an earlier month, and that a price is a thing you are told and a rate is a thing that is already true of you whether anybody says it or not, and that there is no rate in this district that turns a year into coppers and there never has been one. He did not name a figure for the building at any point in the afternoon.

Four questions asked in a yard is a figure about four questions and is not a figure about anybody, and the man who was asked them four times answered them four times and asked for nothing in return, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam came into that yard at about four with the bar of iron again and stood at the end of that table for about a quarter of an hour and said one thing, out loud, in the ordinary voice: that the shortest interval this district has evidence for is a month, because of a bell, and that a month is a length, and a length is not a promise. Then he went out of that yard and down the lane. Saying out loud that a length is not a promise has not withdrawn the term, and his right arm is in the same place it was on the first of the seventh month and has not been spent again this week.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane at about half past four with the bed of his cart empty and stopped at the trough, and asked the mender one question about whether the water in that trough was being drawn down, and was told that it was not, and did not ask anything else. A man who has asked one question about a trough and got an answer has not been asked anything about a bid.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about one, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and forty-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Three people in this district have asked me in the last month whether I would stand in that building in the wet, and I have said no three times and given a reason three times, and I am not standing here this morning to be asked a fourth time. I want the other half of it said out loud as well. There is no way in this basin to pay a person who is not in a household, and that means that building has no price on it, and a thing with no price on it is a thing somebody is going to be given. It is the only building in this district that can hold people out of the weather. Whoever ends up standing in it in the wet is going to be doing it because a yard looked at them and nobody looked away.**"

Not refusing the bid and not agreeing with it, and it cannot be counted either way. A figure that is to be completed is not a rate, and no rate turning a year into coppers has ever existed in this district and none exists now. The man the figure of twenty-one years is against heard it and did not answer it, and the not answering is not an agreement either.

The man of about nineteen had his flat stone at the foot of that trestle table from about ten until the light went and did not use it once. In the first four days of that bid he had a page of figures and a speech a day to put in the minute, and since the second of the month there had been two things said out loud in that yard in a week, and today there was one, and he had it, and it was already written down by somebody else before he had finished holding it in his head. He had been asked twice on the fourth what he was going to count and had told them he did not know yet, and it had taken him four more days to find out that a man does not get to choose what his day is going to contain.

---

The first day of the eighth month is thirty-six days past this morning, and a ladder is a figure about a ladder and is not a figure about anybody. A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth thing this district does not have was named out loud in that yard on the seventh of the ninth by the man who convened the bid, and that it had not been named on the page of that yard in the hundred and forty days before it, and that the count is still five and the fifth is still not paid.

That night of the twentieth of the fourth month is a hundred and thirty-nine days back. It is named in that yard and it is closed in none of the ten days of this month, and a plank does not keep nights. A toll nobody can pay is not a sixth of the five protected things and not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and no fifth column of not-askings was ruled today and a clerk did not rule one.

About four of the nineteen people in that yard left it at about four and went up the bank past that building with two doors, and two of them came back down at about half past four. One of those two was the man of about thirty-four who digs loam, who stood at the top of that ditch and looked down at the foot of standing water in it and did not go down, and the other was a man nobody in that yard had a descriptor for that day, and both of them went back down the lane without saying one word about the five things this district does not have. Four people leaving a yard is a figure about four people and is not a refusal and is not an agreement, and a man with no descriptor in that yard on this day is not a person who has been asked for one.

The man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall for the whole of the afternoon and did not stand up, and nobody said one word to him about what he was going to do with his hands, and a man whose trade is over is not a failure and is not a tragic figure and is not redeemed, and a clerk may not say that about him and may not say the other thing either.

The woman of fifty-eight was at the trough outside that wall at about half past seven with the two buckets full beside her and about four people still in that yard, and about nine people in this district know that she has carried two buckets up that bank for nineteen years and that nobody has ever paid her for it and that she has never once asked anybody to pay her. She is not a clerk of a house and she is not a keeper of a bucket, and she was in that yard all morning while the fifth of the five things this district does not have was said out loud in it and was not asked about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The light had gone off that yard by about half past eight and the bid on that building had not been called for since the first of the ninth month, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven.

**THE FIFTH OF THE FIVE THINGS THIS DISTRICT DOES NOT HAVE, WHICH IS A WAY TO PAY A PERSON WHO IS NOT IN A HOUSEHOLD, WAS SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD BY THE MAN WHO CONVENED THE BID AND IS STILL NOT PAID, AND HE COUNTED FOUR OF THE FIVE ON THE FINGERS OF A HAND THAT WILL NOT CLOSE.**
