# Chapter 377: A Page Lifted With Two Full Hands

---

The fifth of the tenth month came in with cloud coming up off the low ground all morning and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The man of fifty-six read one of the four figures on the boards at the end of that trestle table out loud over nobody's shoulder and got the other three, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the other end of that table with a knot in about nine inches of fresh wire in the fingers of his right hand, working it and not using it for anything.

"**Nobody has entered anything for three hundred and fifteen days since the twenty-fourth of November, and two hundred and seventy-six days separate the second of January and this morning, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is a hundred and ninety-five days, and that table against a wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood ninety-one mornings.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which is seven months back this morning, and that the figure for it is a figure about words on a page and is not a figure about a date, and that it is a figure about the pool and is not a figure about the eight.

---

The rain came in at about half past two and went on for about four hours.

The page with the twenty-first of the ninth month on it was on the end of that trestle table under a stone and had been there fifteen days, and about nine people in that yard had read the top line of it standing up over those fifteen days and about four of them had read the whole of it. The boy's page was under the same stone beside it. The water came at the corner of the stone first and then under it, and the top corner of the clerk's page went soft and began to lift about an inch.

Nobody in that yard did anything about it for about four minutes.

Then the woman of fifty-eight put one of her two buckets down in the mud where she had set it, picked the page up off the stone with both hands, shook it once against her thigh, and put it back under the stone, and picked her bucket up out of the mud again, and went on up the two goes of that bank with the other bucket still in her other hand.

The clerk of nineteen years was about nine feet off the other end of that trestle table and she saw the whole of that and she did not say one word about it, and nobody in that yard said one word to the woman of fifty-eight about it, and about four people in that yard saw it and said afterwards that anybody can walk up to a page and read it and that a page in a yard is a page in the rain.

The clerk of nineteen years was about nine feet off the other end of that trestle table and she saw the whole of that and she did not say one word about it, and two hands were needed for that page and one of them was holding a bucket.

A page that has been on that table for fifteen days was picked up this afternoon by a person who is not a clerk and put back under the same stone, and the page is soft at one corner and can be read, and a bucket is not a document and a page is not a book.

Then she said one thing out loud in that yard, in front of about nine people, and the man of about nineteen counted what she said and got two hundred and forty-three.

"**I am going to say one thing out loud in this yard and it is about my own pages and nobody asked me to say it. The old pages in that book four hundred yards up that bank carry three words on them that they should not carry this morning. They carry three months back and they carry four months back and the pages I have written since the thirtieth of the eighth month carry five months back, and the third month is seven months back today and every one of those pages is now carrying the wrong figure, and I am not going back up that bank to alter one line of one of them. I did it the other way once. I wrote the new figure on a new page and I left the old one exactly as it was, and a clerk of nineteen years asked that day whether that was not the same as correcting them and I said no, and she was right and I was right, and here is the reason I did not and will not. A correction that goes back and edits the thing it corrects cannot afterwards be checked, because after the edit there is nothing on the page that shows anybody what it said before, and this district has spent seven volumes arguing about what it may write down, and the one thing it may not do is make its own past unreadable.**"

About nine people in that yard listened to that. About four of them said afterwards, in the ordinary way, that they had not known the pages said anything else, and about four of them said that a page is a page, and neither of those is a finding.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a relative month is a figure about words and is not a figure about a date, and that the third month is seven months back as of this morning, and that the pages carrying three and four and five months back are not altered and are not struck, and that a wrong figure on a page somebody wrote months ago is not a mistake anybody made this morning.


---

The rest of that afternoon went past in the rain. The man who digs loam was in the bottom of that ditch with a bar in his right hand held down at his side and the rain coming down the bank into the ditch and about a foot of standing water in it, and he came up the bank at about four and went out of that yard with his coat on and the water standing in the legs of it.

The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about three with his boots black to the ankle and lower than that, read the four figures off the boards as he went past, got all four, and went on up the lane, and nobody in that yard asked him about the eleven miles and nobody in that yard asked him what he thought of what the clerk had said about her own pages, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in that ditch from about nine until about half past four and came up past that door with his hook over his shoulder and did not say one word about the building, and about four people in that yard asked him in the ordinary way whether that building was going to be standing in the winter, and he gave them the same answer in about four seconds each time.

The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart set against the east wall with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and the rain went through the canvas on the way and he did not put it up, and nobody in that yard asked him to and nobody in that yard said one word about the tent.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood at the end of that trestle table through all of it with the rain going past the edge of the boards and did not go up that bank and did not ask anybody to go up it for him, and two or three of them noticed that he did not and said nothing to him about it.

Along that lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall in the rain with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and he was there in the morning and he was there in the rain and nobody in that yard said one word to him about any of it.

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally, and has said of himself that he is fifty-one, was not in that yard on this day and was not asked for his figure, and his six households have not moved for a hundred and sixty-seven days, and that figure came out of a ladder and not out of his mouth.

---

The day's page goes in the first of those two books. The clerk of nineteen years carried a page up that bank three times on the twenty-first of the ninth month and did not go up it the fourth time, and she has not gone up it since, and half of them have not thought about that once in the fifteen days those pages have been lying on that table.

Nobody carried a page up that bank this afternoon. The rain was still going and four or five others there knew where that room was and about four of those four knew the woman's name is not on anything anybody has said out loud, because nobody has said it.

The second of those two books has one line in it, in her own hand, and nobody asked her to write it. Nothing touched it today, nothing opened it, nothing was read out of it, and a clerk did not enter that it was not touched, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against has not gone up that bank since the thirteenth of the ninth month and did not go up it today.

The two sheets of print on that step of two stones were on it this morning and were on it this evening, and nobody has moved them and nobody has been asked about them.

At about five the man of about nineteen asked the clerk of nineteen years a question at the end of that trestle table in front of whoever was standing there, and the count of what he asked came to ninety-three.

"**Does a page go in that room four hundred yards up that bank? Not your page, not anybody's, just a page. Because there is one under that stone with the twenty-first of this month on it, and there is one of mine under it as well, and the three of them are the only written things in this district that a person who is not a clerk can walk up to and read, and I do not know whether that is a thing that happens or a thing that does not happen here.**"

She said one thing back to him and the count of what she said came to a hundred and forty-five.

"**A page goes in that room when somebody carries it up there and puts it on the shelf and opens the first of the two books and writes the day on a page in that. I carried three pages up that bank on the twenty-first of this month and did not go up it the fourth time, and I have not gone up it since, and nobody has asked me to and I have not offered, and the record about that says not asked. A page on a table is a page a person can read. That is what you said and it is true and it is not the same as a page in a room, and I am not going to tell you one is better than the other, because I have not got a figure that would tell me which one is.**"

The answer she gave him was not a refusal and not an offer and not a document, and a page on a table and a page in a room are two pages and are not the same page and are not added together, and nobody in that yard asked her to carry a page up that bank.

At about six the man of about nineteen said one thing out loud to nobody in particular at the end of that trestle table, in the ordinary way, and nobody counted it but him.

The rain stopped at about half past six. The corner of that page is soft and the words on it can still be read standing up, and three or four of the others read it again after the rain stopped, and the woman of fifty-eight did not come back down that bank that day and nobody in that yard said why.

"**You put that page back under the stone in the rain with a bucket in your other hand and you did not ask anybody to help you and you did not say one word about it, and that is the fourth thing that has happened in that yard this month that nobody counted, and I am counting it now, on my own page, and I am not calling it a finding.**"
