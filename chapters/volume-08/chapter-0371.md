# Chapter 371: A Term and a Voice That Would Not Come

---

The thirtieth of the ninth month came in with the wind gone round to the west in the night and the mud of that lane gone hard on top and soft underneath, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. Four figures stood on the boards at the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read the top one of them out loud over nobody's shoulder, because the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had gone down to the gate at the head of the loam at eight with a coil of wire in the right pocket of his coat, and got the other three without stopping on them. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the seventieth night of his run and seventy days in this district, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and seventy-nine days on that board and five hundred and ninety-five days on the train on the siding, and nobody has entered anything for three hundred and nine days since the twenty-fourth of November, and the first day of the eighth month is fifty-nine days past this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move this morning, and that the term on that reading is a month and runs out at about six this evening, and that she has had the end of it out of a ladder since the first of this month and has not said it out loud to anybody until this morning, and that nobody in that yard asked her for it and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about half past ten the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came back up that lane from the fence end with his right pocket heavier than it had been and said one thing to the clerk of nineteen years in that yard, in front of whoever was standing at the end of that trestle table, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and twenty-five.

"**I have read every figure that has gone on that table this month and I have read two of them out loud more mornings than I can count on my fingers, and there is one figure on that lot that none of us has ever heard anybody say out loud, and I am asking her for it in this yard, in front of all of you, because I would rather be refused in daylight than go on not knowing it. I am not asking her for it for me. I am asking her for it so that it is in a yard instead of in a book four hundred yards up that bank, where a thing like that goes and does not come back out.**"

She wrote one line on her own page and read it back to herself with her lips not moving, and then she said it out loud, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-two.

"**The term on that lot is a month. It was set at a month on the first of this month and it ends at about six o'clock this evening, and I have not said that out loud in that yard before this morning, and I am saying it now because I have been asked in a yard and not in a room.**"

Nobody said anything back for a while. The man of fifty-six stood where he had been standing and said that he had been asked by about four people in the last quarter of an hour what a term was and that he had told all of them the same thing, which is that he does not know when a term runs out and is not going to be told, and he said it out loud again, and nobody in that yard told him that a woman had just told them.

Nine or ten people in that yard stood and listened to a woman of nineteen years say the date of something out loud in the middle of them, and about four of them looked at the man of fifty-six while she said it and about four of them did not.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a term is a figure about a term and a ladder is a figure about a ladder and that neither of them is a figure about the man standing at the end of the table, and that a man who has said out loud that he does not know when a term runs out has not refused to be told and goes in nobody's count, and that nobody in that yard was asked whether they wanted to hear it.

---

The lot book was ruled at about two in the afternoon on the end of that trestle table, in that yard, with about nine people standing round and looking at it being done.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing took about nine inches of wire out of the right pocket of his coat and put it on the boards and said nothing at all, and the clerk of nineteen years ruled five lines with it and a straight edge she had brought up in her own hand, and he did not stay while she did it and went back down the lane to the gate. Nobody asked him to rule anything and nobody asked him to leave. Nobody said one word about nine inches of wire.

The first line in the book is this, and it is all that is in it:

"One lot. A building with two doors. About six and a half thousand bucketfuls of water. No person in it."

Nobody in that yard asked her what the second line was going to be for and she did not offer one, and the book lay on the end of that table with five ruled lines in it and one line written in it and the rest of the afternoon to wait.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a lot book gives a figure of a lot and not of a person who is in it, and that a lot book this district makes is the tenth of the instruments it has made and is not a seventh of the six it has built and not named, and is not a copy of anything and not a fourth of the three documents it does not own, and that a line in a lot book is a line about a lot and is not a line on a board.

---

The bid went on standing. It had been opened in that yard on the first of this month at about six in the evening on a building with two doors, and it had stood open twenty-nine days, and it had not been run on one of those days, and no offer had ever stood on it. Nobody in that yard proposed anything about closing it and nobody proposed anything about opening it and nobody proposed anything at all, and about four people in that yard said the words nobody has run it and about four of them said nothing has been offered, and those are two different things.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a bid which has stood open twenty-nine days with nothing on it is a bid and is not a market, and that twenty-nine is a figure about a bid and is not a figure about the people standing in the yard.

The reading of that lot is still where it stopped. It has got to the fourth of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and the fourth of the five is a person, and the fifth of them is a remedy, and there is nobody in this district who can use one. The man who convened that bid said the fourth of the five out loud in that yard on the twenty-third of this month and stopped there, and nobody asked him to go on with it at any point in the seven days after that, and he has not gone back to it.

The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is ruled and was empty when he looked at it at about four, and it is the seventh of that shape of thing in this district and it is none of the six instruments and it is not added to them, and nothing went into it by anybody.

---

The rest of that afternoon went the way a day goes when the end of a term is in it and everybody knows it and nobody will be the one to say so.

A man at the back asked the clerk of nineteen years whether a second line was going into that book, and she said out loud that she was not going to rule one and that nobody in that yard was going to make her and that if a line came along it would come along on its own. About four people in that yard said afterwards that they had expected a second line, and about four of them said they had not, and neither of those is a finding.

The man who digs loam stood at the end of that table for a while with his right arm held down at his side and read the five lines of that book off the boards, being the first person in that yard to read anything out of it, and said that there was one line in it and that a line is a line. He has not been asked to read anything out of a book in this district and he read one out because he was standing there.

The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about three with his boots black to the ankle, went past the yard, and did not stop, and nobody in that yard asked him anything and nobody asked him about the eleven miles.

At the foot of the east wall the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the brick with the tent still rolled on the back of it. It has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and he was not asked about it, and about four people in that yard told him that a man who puts up a table for anybody who will use one ought to be the first man to be asked about a book, and he said that a table is for two people who want to lean on something and that a book is not that, and that was the whole of what he said about it.

The man who cuts reeds was in the bottom of that ditch with his hook from about eleven until the light went, and the ditch is about three feet deep with about a foot of standing water in it, and nobody in that yard asked him about the building and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and was not in that yard at any point on this day, and nobody in that yard sent anybody out to that road to look for him.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past four with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself. She stopped for a moment at the end of that trestle table on the way up and read the one line in that book standing up, the way anybody can, and she said nothing about it and nobody said anything to her about it.

---

At about ten to six the man the figure of twenty-one years is against came to the end of that trestle table and said out loud, for everybody in that yard to hear, that the term on that reading was up in about ten minutes.

Then he said he was going to say the figure of it, and he could not get it out.

He stopped. He started again about four seconds later and got about half of it out and stopped again, and then he said the whole thing a third time and what came out of him the third time was not a figure at all. About nine people in that yard were close enough to hear all three attempts and not one of them helped him and not one of them said anything. He put his right hand into the front of his coat, where it has been going since the twenty-ninth of this month, and he has not been able to close that hand properly since the tenth of the eighth.

The man of fifty-six was asked twice by people at the back of that yard and said nothing either time, and four or five others asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and he said out loud that he had not been asked anything and had said nothing and that they could put that down.

Then the clerk of nineteen years, who had said a month out loud in that yard at half past ten without any trouble at all, was asked what the term was by a man at the back, and she opened her mouth, and nothing came, and she shut it, and she looked at the page in front of her and then she said the words a term is a figure about a term, and that is not the figure.

It went off that yard by about half past six. Three people in that yard had tried to say that figure out loud since six o'clock and not one of them had managed it, and the clerk of nineteen years wrote it down out of a ladder at about ten past seven while the man who had said it out loud in the morning stood about nine feet off with his right hand in his coat and watched her do it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that nobody was able to say the figure out loud after the term ended, and that a term that has ended is a figure about a term and is not a figure about the man who could not say it, and that the term did not renew and nobody in that yard proposed anything about renewing it.

He could not say it a second time. About nine people heard him try three times and one of them was the man of about nineteen with the pencil, and the number of attempts is three and a clerk entered it and nobody in that yard wrote it down twice.
