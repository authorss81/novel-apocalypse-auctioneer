# Chapter 367: Eleven Miles

---

The twenty-sixth of the ninth month came in with the sky the colour of the mud in that lane and about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, four of them in out of the wet. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table with a stone on the corner of the day's page. The man of fifty-six read the top one of the four out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other three. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the sixty-sixth night of his run and sixty-six days in this district, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and seventy-five days on that board and five hundred and ninety-one days on the train on the siding, and the days nobody has entered anything since the twenty-fourth of November stand at three hundred and five, and the rival record has been in force a hundred and sixteen days and is unanswered.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and is the same figure it was yesterday, and that a figure about the people who answered a door is not a figure about a man who knows where a road goes, and that the man of about sixty-four was not asked anything this morning and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane at about eleven with his boots black to the ankle and about nine other people in that yard went out of it with him, and that is not a thing anybody called for.

He took them up the lane past that building and about four hundred yards on, and he stopped at a gate, and he put one thing out loud into that lane in the ordinary voice in front of about nine people. The count came to two hundred and eighty-two and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**That gate is the first one off this lane and it is about four feet off the mud and it does not shut, and there is wire in it that a man set in on the twenty-second of this month, for nothing, and I am not going to say whose name goes against that wire because there is no name against anything in this district and that is not a complaint. Past that gate the road goes up about a mile and it is the first half mile anybody has walked this month that I have not been out on, and then there is a second gate, and the second gate is shut, and the latch on that one is broken and has been since the seventh month. Past the second gate it is a mile and a half to the third one, and the third one is the only gate on that road with a roof over it, and there is nothing under the roof. Past the third gate there is a mile and a half more and then the fourth one, and at the fourth one the road stops being a road and becomes two wheel tracks in a field, and a man standing at that fourth gate who wants to go anywhere else has to ask somebody. Eleven miles, counting the mile and a half from the top of that bank down to this yard. I know where every yard of it goes. I said in this yard on the eighteenth that knowing where a road goes is not holding it, and I have not changed my mind, and I am not going to be asked about it today.**"

About nine people in that lane stood there for about four minutes and said nothing, and then the man who mends fencing said that he had not known about the latch on the second gate, and the man who keeps a road told him that was the point of the morning, and about four people in that lane laughed and about four of them did not.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put his right hand on the wire in that gate and said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and seventy-five, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**That is nine feet of wire with a knot in it that I put in on the twenty-second of this month for nothing, and it is the first thing on that road a stranger would put his hand on, and there is nothing else on eleven miles of it that a stranger could put his hand on. I have been thinking about that for four days and I have got to the end of what I have got about it, and this is the end of it. A wire in a gate is not a page and it is not a figure and it does not say on anybody's behalf that they have been here. It is nine feet of wire, and a man set it in a gate in a week when another man said out loud in that yard that he had not asked him for an inch of it, and that is the whole of the reason and there is not one more word to be said about it in a lane.**"

About nine people in that lane said nothing at all. The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road said that there was a great deal not to be said about a lot of things and went on up the lane.

Nine feet of wire is the whole of the reason a man gave out loud in a lane, and he has not been given a post and cannot be counted either way, and that the wire was not asked for, and that a wire in a gate is not a written thing at all and is not a seventh of the six instruments this district has built and not named, and that a mark on a gate is not a signature and a wire is not a mark.

---

They came back down that lane into that yard at about half past one and the man who convened the bid was standing about nine feet off the end of that trestle table with the day's page under his right arm. About nine people came into that yard behind them and stood about in it, and about four of them were still saying the second gate and the third one to each other, and the lane was busy both ways with carts and none of the carts stopped.

He put one thing out loud in front of about nineteen people in the ordinary voice, and it came to a hundred and ninety-five words, and the boy had it in his own hand.

"**I have got a sheet of paper under my arm with four things written on it that a clerk put down this morning, and I have had it in my hand since about ten, and I was going to give it to that man in the lane at the first gate, and I did not, and I want the whole of that said out loud in a yard in front of about nineteen of you rather than kept. I did not give it to him because a man said in this yard on the eighteenth of this month that if a document came along with his name on it for that road he would tear it up, and I have thought about that for eight days and I have not found out a way round it, and I am not going to put a page in front of a man and ask him whether he minds. Nobody has asked him to sign anything this morning and nobody is going to. The page is still under my arm and it is going to be in a room four hundred yards up that bank at about six.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said out loud in that yard that he meant to give a sheet of paper to a person and did not, and that he has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way, and that the person he did not hand it to was not asked and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a sheet of paper with four things on it and no name on it is not a document this district owns and is not a fourth of the three documents this district does not own.

---

The rest of that day was the man of about thirty-four who keeps a road standing in that yard until the light went.

He came back into that yard at about half past one and about four people in that yard asked him where a stranger would find water, and he said the trough at the bottom of that bank, and about four more asked him where a stranger would find the loam, and he said past the fourth gate and left, and about four more asked him where a stranger would find that ditch, and he said it is behind that building and about three feet deep and there is about a foot of standing water in it at this time of year.

Then the man of fifty-six asked him, in the ordinary voice and in front of about nine people, where a stranger would find the man of fifty-six.

He said that he would not answer that one. He said it in about four seconds and he did not smile, and about nine people in that yard laughed anyway, and about four of them did not, and the man of fifty-six said nothing back and went to the fence end of that lane.

A man who has answered nine questions about a road in a yard has not been given a post and is not a keeper of that road and cannot be counted either way, and that he was not asked about the ninth question and would have said the same thing if he had been, and that a man who walks a road is not a keeper of it and a man standing in a gateway is not holding a gateway.

He was not in that yard again after about half past four and he got back to that low wall at about half past nine at night, which is the first time this month that he has come back to it after the light had gone, and his boots were black to the ankle, and the man of about sixty-four was asleep on it when he got there and did not wake up, and about nobody said one word to either of them about that.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the top of that ditch from about two until about half past five with a bar of iron in his right hand held down at his side, and he was not in that lane at the first gate at any point this morning and nobody in that lane said anything about where he was instead. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was in the bottom of it with his hook and his bundle until about four. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and was not in that yard at any point on this day, and nobody in that yard said one word about whether he would be in it in the evening. At the foot of the east wall the man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the brick with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and nobody in that yard asked him about it and he was given nothing.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself, and nobody in that yard said one word to her about a page or a road or a name, and she did not say one word about any of them.

The reading of that lot is begun and is not finished and stands at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and the fourth of the five is a person. The bid in that yard had stood open twenty-five days and had not been run on one of them and no offer had ever stood on it and nobody had proposed anything about closing it.

The light went off that yard at about half past eight and the number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved for a hundred and fifty-seven days, and he was not in that yard and was not asked for that figure, and the figure was not got by asking him, and it came out of a ladder, and a ladder is a figure about a ladder.

He stood in that yard until the light went and then went out of it and up that lane, and nobody handed him anything on the way out and nobody asked him to sign anything, because there was nothing to hand him and nothing to sign. Eleven miles of a road is in a minute now, and a minute is not a road, and knowing where a road goes is not holding it.
