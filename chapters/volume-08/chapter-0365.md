# Chapter 365: A Figure Got Wrong

---

The twenty-fourth of the ninth month came in with a wind off the low ground that had no grit in it and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read one of the four out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other three and went off up the lane to the fence end without stopping, which he does most mornings. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the sixty-fourth night of his run and sixty-four days in this district, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and seventy-three days on that board and five hundred and eighty-nine days on the train on the siding, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and fifty-six days back, and the first day of the eighth month is fifty-three days past this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that four hundred and eleven is the figure on the sheet at that gatepost and did not move this morning, and that the man of about sixty-four was not asked anything about the sixty-fourth night and the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a thing this district does not have that is not one of those five is not a thing, and that there are five of them and there is no sixth.

---

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them came off the brick at the foot of the east wall at about half past ten and came and stood at the end of that trestle table.

Nobody asked him to. He had not been in that yard at that end of that table in three weeks, and about nine people in that yard looked up and then went on with what they were doing, and he stood there for about four minutes before he said anything at all.

Then he read the four figures out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people. The count came to fifty-five and the boy wrote it out in his own hand.

"**Two hundred and seventy-three, five hundred and eighty-nine, a hundred and fifty-six, and thirty. I have been in this basin since the sixth month and that is the first time I have stood at that end of that table and I am not going to pretend to anybody standing here that I have been practising.**"

Then the man of fifty-six said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and it was not counted, because about four people in that yard said it over the top of him and nobody could get one figure out of that.

"**The fourth of them is thirty-one and not thirty. It is the length of this month, and a clerk of nineteen years said it out loud in this yard on the fifteenth of this month and would not say it again, and I have read that board most mornings since and I am not going to be wrong about it in front of about nine of you, and you are not in any trouble and I have said it out loud so that about nine other people in this yard who heard you get it wrong do not go off this morning with it.**"

That man said thank you. He said it in the ordinary voice and he said nothing else, and he stayed at the end of that trestle table for about nine minutes afterwards with his hands where he keeps them and did not go and get his hand-cart and leave.

About four people in that yard who had heard the fourth figure said thirty went on saying the other three to each other and then one of them stopped, and about nine other people in that yard stood about in the sun and waited to see whether anybody standing at that table was going to say anything else, and nobody did.

A man who reads a figure wrong in a yard in front of about nine people has not made a finding and has not made a habit out of it. A man getting a figure right is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about the man who read it, and a man getting a figure wrong is not a finding either, and the two are not added together, and nobody was found. Four people in a yard going on with three of four figures after the fourth has been put right in front of them is four people and is not a figure about the four figures.

The man of fifty-six came back into that yard at about three and stood about nine feet off the end of that trestle table and said one thing out loud to about four people, and it was not counted, because about four other people in that yard were saying the figures to each other at the time and nobody could get one figure out of it.

He said that he had said it out loud in that yard because a yard is a room with no roof and that is the only place in this district where a thing gets said out loud, and that if he had said it to one man at the fence end of that lane then that man would have gone off that lane with it and about nine other people would have gone off this yard with a wrong figure in their heads, and that he is not a reader and has never been one and reads four numbers off a board because that is what he does, and that a man who is not a reader can still be the man who is standing in a yard when a thing is got wrong. He said that last part twice, in the same words, because the wind took the first one.

---

He went back to the foot of the east wall at about twenty to twelve and put his hand-cart against the brick, and the tent was still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and nobody in that yard asked him about it and nobody in that yard was given anything and he was given nothing.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about one, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and thirty-four, and it is in the minute in his own words.

"**I have got one of those four wrong twice this month going past that table and nobody has ever told me which one. I worked it out on my own the second time and I have not said one word about it to anybody, and I am not going to say anything about it now, and I want that written down. A figure a man has got wrong in his own head and has not said out loud is a figure about his own head. It is not in that yard and it is not on any page in this district and there is no figure on any page anywhere for it, and I have had a fortnight to say it out loud and I have not said it, and that one is mine.**"

About nine people in that yard said nothing back and about four of them said that it did not matter, and he said that he knew it did not matter and that was not the point of it.

A man who has said out loud in a yard that he got a figure wrong in his own head and did not say so for a fortnight has not refused anything and cannot be counted either way. Getting a figure right is not a finding and getting a figure wrong is not a finding, and the man who got a figure wrong in that yard this morning and the man who has been getting one wrong going past that table are two figures about a morning and are not one figure and are not added together.

---

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against said one thing out loud at about four, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and it came to two hundred and twelve words, and the boy had it in his own hand.

"**A man has been corrected in that yard this morning in front of about nine people and he said thank you and he stayed at that table for about nine minutes afterwards, and I want it said that this district has spent a long time building fences around people so that nothing could be corrected in front of about nineteen of them without their asking for it first, and that is what happened this morning and nobody asked for it and nobody arranged it. I am not going to stand here and say thank you to the man of fifty-six for it, because thank you said out loud in a yard is a thing said out loud in a yard and it is not the same as the thing I mean, and there is no column for it and there is not going to be one. I am going to say that a yard is the only instrument this district has that can do that, and that about nine people in it this morning had a number put right and did not have to be asked, and that a book four hundred yards up that bank could not have done it and would not have done it and is not going to.**"

About nine people in that yard said nothing back, and the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was at the foot of the east wall by then and did not hear it, and nobody in that yard told him about it afterwards, and a clerk entered that he was not there to hear it and that nobody told him afterwards.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a yard is a room with no roof and that nothing in this district gets said out loud anywhere else, and that a table with four legs in a yard is not a counter until somebody asks somebody a question at it, and that no question was asked at that table this morning and four figures were read off the end of it and one of them was put right out loud by a third person, and that a man who was not there to hear a thing said about him has not been given anything and cannot be counted either way.

---

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was at the top of that ditch from about two until about five and did not come to that trestle table at all, and his right arm did not go above the level of that shoulder. The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about three with his boots black to the ankle, read the four figures off the boards of that trestle table as he went past, got three of the four right and the fourth one wrong, and went on up the lane without stopping, and about four people in that yard noticed which one he had wrong and said so to each other while he was still going, and he did not stop and did not turn round and was not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and was four miles off before the light came up, and about four people in that yard said that he would be in on one of the other days of that week, and nobody in that yard said what for, and nobody in that yard asked him anything and he was not asked to be in it again.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was in the bottom of it with his hook and his bundle until about half past four and came up the bank past the door of that building without saying one word about it. The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself, and she did not ask anybody anything on this day and nobody in that yard asked her anything.

The reading of that lot is begun and is not finished and has got to the fourth of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and the fourth of the five is a person, and the man who convened the bid was in that yard from about ten until about two and did not go to the end of that trestle table this morning at all, and nobody in that yard asked him why and he was not asked.

The bid in that yard had stood open twenty-three days and had not been run on one of them and no offer had ever stood on it and nobody had proposed anything about closing it. The page with the twenty-first of the ninth month on it was still on the end of that trestle table under a stone, and nobody in that yard had asked anybody about it.

The light went off that yard at about half past eight and the number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and that night in the fourth month is a hundred and fifty-six days back and nobody in this district can say whether it happened, and a plank does not keep nights.

Nobody found anything. A number was put right in that yard by a man who had not wanted it put right, and he said thank you and nothing else, and about nine people said nothing back, and by the end of that day there was a clerk's line saying he was not there to hear the rest of it. A fortnight of one man's own head came to that, and nothing came of it after.
