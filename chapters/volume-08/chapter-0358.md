# Chapter 358: The Other Reader

---

The seventeenth of the ninth month came in warm again after two hard days, and the yard of Lot Seventeen had about nineteen people in it by ten. There were four figures on the end of the trestle table. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of it with the cloth still on his left hand and a wire in the fingers of his right one, and the man of fifty-six had not come in by ten, which had not happened once in that run before, and about nine people had worked that out inside about four minutes without one of them saying a word about it. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the fifty-eighth night of his run and fifty-seven days in this district, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and sixty-six days on that board and five hundred and eighty-two days on the train on the siding, and the ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and forty-nine days back, and the first day of the eighth month is forty-six days past this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on that sheet is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the man of fifty-six was nowhere in that yard at ten this morning and nobody asked him why, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that a man not being in a yard at ten is a fact about a morning and not a finding about anybody in it.

---

He came to the end of that trestle table at about ten and read the four figures out loud, on his own, to about nine people. What he said was counted and got a hundred and eleven, and the man of about nineteen had it in his own words.

"**Two hundred and sixty-six on that board, five hundred and eighty-two on the train, a hundred and forty-nine back to that night in the fourth month, and thirty-one for the month we are standing in. I am going to say those four numbers in this yard in the ordinary voice and I am not going to point at them, because my right arm does not go above the level of this shoulder and has not since the first of the seventh month, and I am not going to stand here in front of all of you putting my hand in the air to help myself to a number off a board.**"

About nine people in that yard stood still while he said it. He took about four minutes over the four figures, which is longer than the man of fifty-six takes, and he got all four, and he said the fourth one twice because he lost his place on it the first time, and got it the same both times.

Then the man of fifty-six came into that yard at about eleven and came and stood at the end of that trestle table about nine feet off, and he did not say one word about it.

He waited there for about a quarter of an hour. The mender bent wire. The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank with two empty buckets and set them by the trough. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam said that he was going to the loam and went out of that yard up the lane, and the man of fifty-six watched him go and said nothing, and it was not said out loud whether the two of them had said one word to each other at all, and a clerk did not enter it.

At about one the man of fifty-six read the four figures out loud over the mender's shoulder, and got all four, and about nine people in that yard heard him do it, and he read them in the ordinary voice and at the speed he always reads them.

The clerk of nineteen years was about nine feet off with the day's page and she wrote that entry in one hand on one line, and read it back to herself with her lips not moving before she put the pencil away. It says that two men read the same four figures out loud in one yard in one morning and both of them got them, and that two men reading the same four figures is two figures about a morning and is not one figure and is not a finding. It says that a man arriving at a right number is not a finding and not a habit and not a figure about the man who arrived at it, and that a man who got one figure right once was entered on the twenty-ninth of the eighth as not a finding either, and that those are two figures about two days and are not added together.

About four people in that yard said, one after another and in the ordinary way, that the man who digs loam could read those figures every morning if he wanted to. He said no to all four of them, in about four seconds each time, and gave the same reason to each of them, which was that he had said on the second of this month that he was not going to stand in that yard and be looked at, and that a man who reads a number off a board every morning in front of nineteen people is being looked at.

The same answer given four times in an afternoon is a shape noticed four times and is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about him, and saying a thing could be done is not offering to do it and is not refusing a post that does not exist, and no post was offered to anybody this afternoon and no column was filled.

---

What the man of fifty-six did about the morning is the part of it that is not on any page.

He was in that yard from about eleven until the light went. He read the four figures at about one, because that is what he does. He read them again at about four, because about nine people in that yard had started saying them to each other and getting two of the four wrong, and he said that out loud, to about four people, and got them right again.

He did not say one word to the man who convened the bid about who had read them at ten. He did not say one word to the mender. He stood at the end of that trestle table at about half past three with his back to the boards and looked at the lane for about nine minutes, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was about nine feet off the other end of it, and neither of them said anything to the other, and about four people in that yard noticed both of them standing there and said nothing at all.

At about four he said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and the man of about nineteen counted what he said and got a hundred and ninety-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Since one o'clock this afternoon about nine people in this yard have been saying those four figures to each other and two of the four are being got wrong, and I am not going to be the man who stands in this yard correcting people about numbers. If a number is going to be read out loud in a yard it should be read by whoever happens to be standing at the table, and that is all. A man standing at a table is not holding the table and a man reading a figure off a board is not keeping the figure, and I have been reading four numbers off that table every morning of this run and not one of them is mine and not one of them is anybody else's. The fourth one is the length of a month and a clerk wrote it and a man who digs loam read it out loud this morning and I read it at one o'clock, and I want that written down in that order and I want it written down that I did not hear him do it until about eleven.**"

It was entered that a man who says out loud in a yard that he did not hear another man read a figure until eleven o'clock has refused nothing and cannot be counted either way, and that the order two figures were read in on one morning is a figure about a morning and is not about either of the two men, and that a person standing at a table is not holding it and a person standing in a doorway is not holding the door.

A person who reads a figure out loud every morning of a run and finds the figure read out loud by another man one morning is not a keeper of it and has not lost a post, because there was no post and there is not one now, and a figure is not a post and a chair is a chair and a table is a table. He has nothing to be relieved of and no one has relieved him of anything, and about nine people in that yard watched a man stand with his back to a board for about nine minutes and not one of them asked him what he was doing there.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said nothing all afternoon has not withdrawn anything and has not given anything, and that a column with a word over it and nothing under it is still ruled and still empty, and that a man reading four figures off a table is not a reading, and that a reading read back from the top by a second person is not something that happened this morning, and that a thing read back by nobody binds nobody.

---

The rest of the day went like the rest of that week. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was up the lane in a field from about half past two until about five. The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about three, read the four figures off the boards of that trestle table as he went past it without stopping, and got three of the four right and the fourth one wrong, and went on up the lane. About four people in that yard noticed which one he had wrong and said so to one another while he was still going, and he did not stop and did not turn round and did not come back and was not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who reads a figure wrong going past a table has not made a finding and has not made a habit out of it and the wrong number is not a figure about the man who read it, and that about four people in a yard noticing which of four numbers a man got wrong is a figure about those four people and not about the numbers, and that a man who knows where a road goes is not holding that road and was asked no question about eleven miles this morning.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was in the bottom of it with his hook and his bundle until about four. At the foot of the east wall the man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the brick and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and nobody asked him about either of those things.

The reading of the lot stands where it stopped yesterday, at the fourth of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and the man who convened the bid did not try to go on with it this afternoon and nobody in that yard asked him to.

The woman of fifty-eight filled her two buckets at the trough at about half past five and carried them up the two goes of the bank on her own and about four people in that yard watched her do it and none of them offered to take one, and she has not asked for one in nineteen years.

The light went off that yard at about half past eight. The man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded beside him, and nobody in any yard of this month has said one word to him about what he is going to do with his hands, and a man whose trade is over is not a failure and is not a tragic figure and is not redeemed, and a clerk may not say that about him and may not say the other thing either. The number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the bid in that yard has been open sixteen days and no offer has stood on any of them.

**THE FIGURES ON THAT TABLE WERE READ OUT LOUD AT TEN IN THE MORNING BY A MAN WHO DIGS LOAM AND CANNOT RAISE HIS RIGHT ARM AND HE GOT ALL FOUR OF THEM, AND THE MAN WHO READS THEM EVERY MORNING CAME IN AT ELEVEN AND SAID NOTHING ABOUT IT AND READ THEM AGAIN AT ONE, AND A CLERK ENTERED THAT GETTING A FIGURE RIGHT IS NOT A FINDING.**
