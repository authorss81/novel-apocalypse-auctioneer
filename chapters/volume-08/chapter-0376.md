# Chapter 376: What a District Says Back

---

The fourth of the tenth month came in with a hard clean wind off the low ground and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The man of fifty-six read the bottom one of the four figures on the boards at the end of the trestle table out loud over the shoulder of the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing and got the other three, and the mender was back at that table this morning and nobody in that yard said one word to him about being back at it.

"**Two hundred and eighty-four days on that board and six hundred days on the train on the siding, and the rival record has been in force a hundred and twenty-five days and the age of the figure about that figure is a hundred and twenty-five days, and those two are two figures.**"

The rival record is a sheet of paper and it came into this district on the first of the sixth month, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read it out in that yard seven times in seventeen days for a yard that wanted to hear a piece of paper, and on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month somebody at the back asked him to read it once more and he said no in about four seconds and gave a reason, and a refused reading is not a reading and nothing has been said about it since.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the rival record is unanswered.

The morning before that was ordinary. The man who digs loam was in the bottom of that ditch before nine with a bar in his right hand held down at his side. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in the same ditch about nine feet further along with his hook and his bundle, and the two of them did not say one word to each other. The man who keeps a road went out of that yard at about ten and did not stop in it.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was not in that yard on this day and nobody in that yard said where he was, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The page with the twenty-first of the ninth month on it was on the end of that trestle table under a stone with the boy's page beside it under the same stone, and it had been there fourteen days, and the wind that morning was the kind that gets under a stone at one corner and lifts it.

---

At about half past eleven the man the figure of twenty-one years is against went down on his right knee in the mud at the foot of that trestle table and got the rival record out from under the leg of it.

It is a sheet of paper and it has been under that leg since the first of the sixth month and it has taken the shape of the leg, and when he came up with it in his hand it unfolded about halfway and then would not unfold any further, and he did not shake it and he did not read it. He knelt there in the mud about four seconds longer than he had to, and then he tried to get up in one movement the way a man gets up, and he could not, and he got up in about four seconds on his own with his left hand on the boards and nobody in that yard offered him an arm and about nine people watched him do the whole of it.

Then he stood at the end of that trestle table with the sheet in his left hand and said one thing out loud, in the ordinary way, in that yard, in daylight, in front of about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got two hundred and eighty-two.

"**That record is set against four places four hundred miles away and it has been in force a hundred and twenty-five days and nobody has answered it, and I am going to answer it now, and I am going to say first that nobody in this yard asked me to and I am not a holder of it and I am not a party to it and I have no power over it and I am not the registrar of anything this afternoon. This district has one book. It is on the end of that table and any person can walk up to that table and read it, and there is one line in it, and the line gives a figure of that lot and not one person in it, and it took about four seconds to write and it took nine inches of wire to rule. I am not going to tell you it is the same size as that. It is not the same size as that and it is smaller and I know it is smaller. It is the only thing this district has that a stranger can walk up to and read, and the reason it is the only thing is that this district has had seven volumes of people telling it not to write anybody down, and they were right about that and I am not arguing with them about that today. I am answering that record with a book with one line in it, and the line is not an answer, and I am not saying the book is an answer either, and I am putting this down as one and I am going to stop.**"

The man of fifty-six read the one line of the lot book out loud in that yard about a minute later, standing up, the way he reads figures off boards, and what he said was this, word for word, and nobody read it back from the top, and about four people in that yard said afterwards that the line was the line: One lot. A building with two doors. About six and a half thousand bucketfuls of water. No person in it.

Nobody said anything back for about four minutes. The man the figure of twenty-one years is against put the sheet back under the leg of that trestle table, where it has been since the first of the sixth month, and it went back flat, and the shape of the leg is in it and will be in it when it comes out again.

Then about four people at the back of that yard asked him what that book was worth, in the ordinary way, one after another, and he said one thing back to all four of them and the boy counted it and got a hundred and nineteen.

"**I am not going to put a figure on that book and you are not going to get one out of me this afternoon, and the reason is not modesty. A figure is a thing somebody is told. Nobody in this district has told me what anything is worth to a body four hundred miles off, and if I gave you a number this afternoon it would be a number I made up in a yard, and a number a man made up in a yard is a figure about that yard and not about a body, and I would rather have nothing on that page than a figure I cannot stand behind in front of about nineteen people.**"

Four people in that yard asked for a figure this afternoon and did not get one, and a man who has not given a figure has not refused one and goes in nobody's count on either side, and nothing this district has made in this yard is a market, and a yard is a room with no roof and a yard is where a thing gets said out loud.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against put the sheet back under the leg of that trestle table, where it has been since the first of the sixth month, and it went back flat, and the shape of the leg is in it and will be in it when it comes out again.

Nobody in that yard was appointed to anything this afternoon and no name went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, which is ruled and was empty at the end of this day, and about four people in that yard said afterwards that a thing said out loud in a yard is in the mouths of the people who heard it and that is all it is, and that is not nothing and it is not a record either.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a record set against four places four hundred miles away was answered once this afternoon in that yard in daylight out loud, and that what was said was not a figure and not a number and not a reading of that record and not a ninth reading, and that the eighth reading of that record was refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month and this afternoon was not that eighth reading either, and that a record answered in a yard is answered for the people standing in that yard and not for a body four hundred miles off, and that the man who answered it was not asked to answer it and did.

---

About four people in that yard asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing what he thought of that, and he is the man who read that record out seven times and refused the eighth one out loud with a reason of his own. He said one thing about it, and the boy counted it and got a hundred and forty.

"**I have read that thing out loud to this yard seven times and I said no to the eighth one in about four seconds, and what has just happened in this yard is not the eighth and I want that written down, because if it is the eighth then I have been wrong for a month and a bit. That record is set against four places and I have never seen one of them. This yard has a book with one line in it and I have seen the line. I am not saying one is better than the other. I am saying they are not the same size and I have been careful about that for seven readings and I am not going to stop being careful about it now that somebody has said it out loud in daylight.**"

The man who refused that record out loud on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month has said today that what was said in that yard is not the eighth, and a man who has been careful about a thing for seven readings is not a keeper of it and is not a bearer of it and is not a party to it, and he was asked what he thought of it and he answered and was not asked to.

The rest of that day went as days go. The man who digs loam was in the bottom of that ditch with a bar in his right hand held down at his side. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in the same ditch with his hook and his bundle.

The man who keeps a road came up that lane at about three with his boots black to the ankle, read the four figures off the boards as he went past, got all four, and went on up the lane, and nobody in that yard asked him about the eleven miles and nobody in that yard asked him what he thought of what had been said in it, and the record of it says not asked.

The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart set against the east wall of that building with the tent still rolled on the back of it. About four people in that yard said to him that if a book was going to be on that table every day then somebody would want a table to read it on, and he said that a person can read a book standing up and that a table is for two people who want to lean on something, and that was the whole of what he said about it.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, carried them up the two goes herself, and stopped at the end of that trestle table on the way up and read the one line in that lot book standing up, the way anybody can, and said nothing about it and nobody said anything to her.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and was not in that yard at any point on this day, and a record set against four places four hundred miles away is not made better or worse by a man being out on a road.

About half past seven the man of about sixty-four got up off that low wall for the first time in a run and stood about nine feet along it and then sat down on the stones again with his coat folded beside him, and two or three of them saw him do that and none of them said anything to him about it, and he has seventy-five days in this district and nothing in his hands and is not a holder of that record and is not a party to it and is not a keeper of a lot and has not been asked to be anything.

The last of the light went off that yard about half past eight. The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is still four hundred and eleven and has not moved today, and the rival record has been answered once out loud and is still in force, and the term on that reading is five days past, and the reading of that lot is begun and is not finished.
