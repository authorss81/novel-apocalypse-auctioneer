# Chapter 349: A Word Over a Ruled Line

---

The eighth of the ninth month came in clear and cold for the hour, the kind of cold that gets into a coat in about ten minutes and then stops, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The three figures were on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read the top one of the three out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other two right. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded beside him, on the forty-ninth night of his run and forty-eight days in this district.

"**Two hundred and eighty-seven days since the twenty-fourth of November and two hundred and forty-eight days from the second of January, and the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is a hundred and sixty-seven days, and the first day of the eighth month is thirty-seven days past this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved, and that its own age as a figure about that figure is ninety-eight days, and that the rival record has been in force ninety-eight days and is unanswered, and that those two are two figures counted off one day and are not one figure.

---

By about half past ten the man of about nineteen had the flat stone at the foot of that trestle table and about nine people in that yard had come and gone. The woman of fifty-eight went down that bank with two buckets at about eleven and put them inside that building and came back out and filled them at the trough, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came into that yard at about one and went out again at about half past one and said nothing to anybody, and neither of them was asked anything about the bid and nobody in that yard said one word to either of them about it.

There are two sheets of print on the top step of those two stones outside that door, and they have not been moved. The second of the five lines on that sheet is still unanswered on the eighth of the ninth month, and a document that has been on a step in the open for a fortnight and a half is not a document anybody has answered and is not a document nobody owns and is not a fourth of the three.

---

The room is about nine feet by eleven at the top of that bank, off a lane, and it has a shelf in it and two books on the shelf and a step of two stones outside the door and a fanlight over the door that lights nothing. The man the figure of twenty-one years is against went up that bank at about two and did not knock, because the door was open and a clerk of nineteen years was in the room with a page under her arm.

The woman who keeps those two books was not in that room and did not come into it while he was in it. He did not ask anybody where she was. He had known for about four months that she was in and out of that room at hours that were not on any page, and he had worked out over the winter that the correct thing to do about that was to arrive when the door was already open and never to ask, and he had not decided that on any authority. It was his own and it was the only decision he had made in this district that he could not have explained to a committee.

The first of the two books was open on the shelf at a page with a ruled line on it and a word over the line and nothing under the line. The clerk of nineteen years turned the pages back to it with two fingers and then took her hand off and stood about nine feet off and did not say anything.

He said one thing out loud at about ten past two, in the ordinary voice, to the one person in the room, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and seventy-three, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**There is a word over that line and there is nothing under it and I have been in this basin a long time and I have never once seen anybody put anything under it. It is the seventh of that shape of thing in this district. The other six are not this one and this one is not them and the seven are not added together, and a column with a word over it and nothing under it is a question this district asked itself and did not answer, and a column of a thing with a word over it is not a column of people. A reading needs a second person to read it back from the top, and the reader has to be somebody, and there is no figure on this page for who, and there is not one on any page in this district. I am not going to be that person and I have not been asked to be, and my name does not go in there this afternoon.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a room that his name does not go in a column has not withdrawn his name from anything, because his name was never in it, and that a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way, and that what is ruled on that page is a heading and not a list of persons, and is not a fifth column of not-askings, and no fifth column of not-askings was ruled in that room this afternoon.

He put his right hand flat on the shelf beside that book while the clerk of nineteen years turned the pages forward and then back again, and the hand did not close on the edge of the shelf at any point, and it stayed flat on the wood for about nine minutes after the book had been shut. The clerk of nineteen years did not offer to do anything about it and did not say anything about it and neither did he.

What the nine minutes were, in the order they went, was this. The first two were the pages going forward. The next three were the pages coming back, and his hand stayed where it was on the shelf through all of them, and he watched the clerk's two fingers and not the book. The last four were a shelf and a wall and the edge of the step outside the door and the sound of a bucket going up a bank somewhere below, and the hand was still flat on the wood at the end of them, and he took it off himself, and put it in his coat.

There was dust on that shelf where the hand had been. He had left a print of it about the size of a hand, and the clerk of nineteen years looked at the print for a moment and then wiped it off with her sleeve, and neither of them said anything about that either, and the wipe is not on any page in this district and no clerk entered it.

A hand flat on a shelf for about nine minutes is not a signature and is not a name and is not a consent, and a person standing next to a book is not holding the book, and a person standing in a doorway is not holding the door, and nobody in that room asked either of them one question about it and nobody in that room said one word about it, and neither of them wrote anything in that column, and a column with nothing under it this afternoon has nothing under it tomorrow.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against was in that room for about an hour of the daylight. He did not ask for the column to be filled and he did not ask for it to be taken out of that book, and he did not say one word about the man of about sixty-four or about the bid or about a name, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he asked for nothing, and that that is entered and is not a finding and is not a refusal and is not a going-away.

---

He came back down that bank at about ten past three and went straight into that yard and stood at the end of that trestle table, and about nine people in it looked at him and none of them asked him where he had been. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures still on the boards and a wire he was bending with the fingers of his right hand, and he put the wire down when the other man came to the end of the table and picked it up again about four minutes afterwards without either of them having said anything.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was at the top of it for about an hour in the afternoon with a hook and a bundle, and about four people in that yard asked him, one after another and in the ordinary way, whether he thought that building was worth anything, and he gave the same answer to all four of them in about four seconds each. The same answer given four times in an afternoon is a shape noticed four times and is not a finding and is not a habit, and a man who says in four seconds that a thing is not a shelter is not a figure about a shelter and is not an instrument this district has built.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the term on the reading that was set on the first of the ninth month has a day at the end of it, and that she entered that day out of a ladder on the day the term was set, and that nobody in that yard has said the figure out loud at any point this week, and that a ladder is a figure about a ladder, and that the term is a figure about a term and is not about the man who convened the bid and is not about anybody who is not in the building.

At the foot of the east wall the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the brick and the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and he was not asked anything about the column and said nothing about it and offered nothing.

---

At about five the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud in that yard, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and the minute took one hundred and twenty-six words of it, in his own words.

"**I have read things out loud in this yard eleven times since the first of the sixth month and not one of the eleven was a reading, and a reading is a thing said out loud and then read back from the top by somebody who is not the man who said it. I have been asked to do the first half of that eleven times and I have never once been asked to do the second half, and I am not standing in this yard this afternoon offering to be the second half. I want that entered in the words I said it in. If somebody in this district ever does get asked, I hope it is a person nobody has spent anything on yet.**"

A man who has said out loud in a yard that he is not offering to read a thing back has not refused to read a thing back and cannot be counted either way, and the record about the not asking says not asked, and a second reader is nobody in particular and is not a post and is not a column and is not a keeper and has not been proposed in that yard by anybody.

At about half past four the man of fifty-six read the three figures on that table out loud one more time, on his own, with nobody over his shoulder, and got all three right, and about nine people heard him do it. A man reading three figures out loud on his own is a man saying a thing out loud and is not a reading, and getting three figures right is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about the man who read them.

At about half past seven the yard emptied down to about four people and the man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded beside him and nothing in his hands, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was about nine feet off the end of that trestle table and did not go up that bank again and did not knock.

The light went at about half past eight and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and it will be still four hundred and eleven in the morning, and nobody in that district has said otherwise this week.

**A COLUMN WITH A WORD OVER IT AND NOTHING UNDER IT WAS LOOKED AT IN DAYLIGHT IN A ROOM FOUR HUNDRED YARDS FROM THAT GATE BY TWO PEOPLE AND NEITHER OF THEM WROTE ANYTHING IN IT, AND IT IS THE SEVENTH THING OF THAT SHAPE IN THIS DISTRICT AND IS NOT ONE OF THE SIX INSTRUMENTS.**
