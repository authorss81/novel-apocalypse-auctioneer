# Chapter 344: Five Months Back

---

The third of the ninth month came in clear and the yard of Lot Seventeen had about nineteen people in it by ten, the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of that trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the top one of the three out loud over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the forty-fourth night and has been in this district forty-three days.

"**The train on the siding has stood five hundred and sixty-eight days and the days nobody has entered anything since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and eighty-two, and the first day of the eighth month is thirty-two days past, and the table against that wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood fifty-eight mornings.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved, and that its own age as a figure about the figure is ninety-three days, and that it is not a day-count for the sheet and that a sheet at a gate is a state and not a place.

---

The man of about nineteen spends most mornings at the foot of that trestle table with his back to the wall and his eyes on about nineteen people, and what he does is count what gets said out loud in that yard in daylight. He has done it since the second of the sixth month and nobody in this district has ever asked him to and he has never asked anybody to let him. This morning he came in at about ten with a flat stone in his coat pocket that he keeps the day off the wall on and he put the stone down, and then he did not pick it up again for half an hour, and about nine people in that yard noticed him not picking it up and nobody said anything about it.

The man of fifty-six was at the end of that table when the man of about nineteen said the thing, and he did not move while it was being said and he did not say anything after it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said the same kind of thing out loud in a yard once before, on the thirty-first of the seventh month, and has not been asked whether he agrees with the second one, has not refused the second one and cannot be counted either way, and that being right about the edge of a thing is not a post.

At about half past ten the man of about nineteen stood in that yard in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people and said one thing out loud, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got one hundred and seventy-six, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**From this morning the third month is five months back and the page has said four since the thirty-first of the seventh month. Four is wrong by one and has been wrong by one since the first of the eighth, and three was what this district said from the first of the sixth and three was wrong by one from the twenty-first of the seventh. The man of fifty-six stood in this yard on the thirty-first of the seventh and said the last part of that out loud and got four then, and he is in this yard this morning and I have not asked him whether he agrees with me and I am not going to. This word is a figure about words and it is not a figure about a date. A clerk is going to write five on this page this morning and is not going back to the old pages to change them, and I would like that entered. I said it out loud in a yard and not in a room.**"

A clerk of nineteen years wrote the whole of it down in one hand, on one line, with the words *last month* and *the month before last* set out in a column of their own. She entered that the third month is five months back from the eighth month onward, and that it was entered as four months back on every page since the thirty-first of the seventh month and as three months back on every page before that, and that no page has been altered. **SHE ENTERED THAT A CORRECTION THAT GOES BACK AND EDITS THE THING IT CORRECTS CANNOT AFTERWARDS BE CHECKED, AND THAT A RELATIVE MONTH IS A FIGURE ABOUT WORDS AND IS NOT A FIGURE ABOUT A DATE, AND THAT THIS DISTRICT HAS TURNED THAT WORD FOUR TIMES SINCE THE FIRST OF THE SIXTH MONTH AND THAT TWO OF THE FOUR HAPPENED IN THE NIGHT WHILE NOBODY WAS LOOKING.**

Nobody may go back and alter an old ledger page. The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood nine feet off the end of that table while she wrote and did not say anything, and afterwards he put his right hand flat on the page she had written on to hold it against the wind, which came off the low ground, and his hand has not closed properly since the tenth of the eighth month and it lay flat on that page for the length of the writing and did not close on the edge of it at all.

He said one thing out loud at about eleven, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and sixty-one, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Those three and four figures on the old pages are mine. I wrote them. I have not gone back to them this morning and I am not going to, and a stranger who walks up to that room in a year's time is going to read four on a page and then read five on the next one and be right to ask which of them was wrong, and the answer is that both were wrong and one of them is nearer and that is all. A man of about nineteen has said this out loud in a yard and I am going to say the other half of it in a yard. Binding cannot create consent and a certificate is not consent, and a correction is not consent either, and a figure nobody has checked is not a figure about a date. I asked for nothing this morning and I got nothing this morning and both of those go down.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has said out loud in a yard that he wrote a wrong figure and is not going back to it has not withdrawn the figure and cannot be counted either way, and that a hand flat on a page is not a signature and is not a name and is not a consent.

---

The light went flat about one and the wind came round the corner of that bank and took the corner of the page on the end of that trestle table, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the stone on it with the fingers of his right hand and the page went flat and stayed flat. About four people came up that lane in the afternoon and read both of those pages standing up. One of them asked which of the two was right, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against told him that neither one was. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who has told a stranger in a lane that neither of two figures is right has not withdrawn either of them, and that both of them are still on their own pages, and that a stranger may read both of them tomorrow because neither of them is being taken out of that room.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about half past ten and again at about four and put the two buckets inside that building both times, and the man of about thirty-four who digs loam came at about three and stood at the top of the ditch behind it for about a minute and did not go down into it, and the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was in it with his hook and his bundle and the two of them said nothing to each other at all. A clerk of nineteen years entered that two men at the two ends of a ditch are not a meeting and are not a second refusal of anything, and that nobody asked either of them why.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read the new page out loud twice in that yard, once at about two and once at about half past four, for the people who had not been in it at half past ten, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a page read out twice in a yard in daylight is a thing that has been introduced and is not a thing that has been checked, and that the man who read it has a cloth on his left hand since the eleventh of the sixth month and read all of it with the other one.

The pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, which from this morning is five months back and a hundred and sixty-two days, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the figure and the two words together, and that it did not move this morning and is a hundred and sixty-two days, and that a figure about a day a clerk's entry last changed and a figure about the month it names are two figures and are not added together.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk who could have corrected the old pages and did not was asked and did it, and that a clerk who could have made a finding out of the disagreement between the two pages and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a lot is not a place and the yard of Lot Seventeen is not a number of lots, and that no seventh instrument was proposed in that yard this morning.

The wind came round that bank again at about three and about nine people in that yard got up off the low wall along the lane and went and stood behind that building with two doors out of it, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went with them and stood at the corner of the nine feet of brick with his back to the lane. He put his right hand flat on the outside wall of it for the length of about nine breaths and took it off again and put it in his coat, and about four people who had come in off the lane after that asked each other, in the ordinary way, whether it was warm, and nobody answered them, and nobody said anything else about it, and the wall stayed warm after his hand came off it.

Nothing was on the end of that trestle table for the second day running and the bid was not called again and nobody had called for it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a bid that was opened on the first of the ninth month and has not been called for since is a thing that has been done once, and that a thing done once is not a habit and is not a finding and is not a figure about anybody.

The woman of fifty-eight was at the trough outside that wall at about half past seven with the two buckets empty beside her and about four people still standing in that yard. She asked one of them, out loud, in the ordinary voice, whether the water in that trough was going to be drawn down this week or whether the man who digs loam had it, and the man of fifty-six told her that he did not have it and did not know who did, and she said that was all right and filled the first bucket. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a question about a trough is a question about a trough, and that nobody in that yard asked her a question back and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The light went off the ground at about half past eight and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against took his hand off the boards of that table at about half past four and put it in his coat and did not go up that bank and did not knock, and the man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall with nothing in his hands, and a clerk entered that a man whose trade is over is not a failure and is not redeemed and a clerk may not say that about him and may not say the other thing either.

**AND ON THE THIRD OF THE NINTH MONTH THE THIRD MONTH WENT FROM FOUR MONTHS BACK TO FIVE MONTHS BACK, SAID OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD BY A MAN OF ABOUT NINETEEN WHO ASKS FOR IT TO BE ENTERED, AND A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS WROTE FIVE ON THAT PAGE IN ONE HAND ON ONE LINE AND DID NOT GO BACK AND ALTER THE PAGES THAT CARRY THREE OR FOUR, AND THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
