# Chapter 360: A Habit Four Hundred Miles Off

---

The nineteenth of the ninth month came in with a low sun and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, and the lane was busy both ways, and there were four figures on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read one of the four out loud over the mender's shoulder, got the other three, and said nothing further. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him, on the sixtieth night of his run and fifty-nine days in this district, and there was nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and sixty-eight days on that board and five hundred and eighty-four days on the train on the siding, and the rival record has been in force a hundred and nine days and is unanswered, and the table against that wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood seventy-four mornings, and the man of about sixty-four has slept on that low wall on fifty-eight of the fifty-nine nights of that run.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that its own age as a figure about that figure is a hundred and nine days, and that the rival record and that age are two figures counted off one day and are not one figure, and that the man of about sixty-four was not asked which of the fifty-nine nights he did not sleep on, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

He said it at about eleven, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and he did not do the arithmetic, and that was the whole of what the morning was.

What he said was counted and got two hundred and fifty, and the man of about nineteen had it in his own words.

"**Sixty-one days. I am not counting it out loud this time and I want that written down as a thing I chose not to do and not as a thing I could not do. I counted it in this yard on the fourth of this month, in daylight, in front of about nineteen of you, and I did the sum out loud as I said it, and I got the same figure I have just said, and a figure that reproduces is a figure about a count and is not a finding and I have had two of them now and I have learned nothing from the second one. So here it is without the sum. Sixty-one days is a figure about how often a body four hundred miles off that has never once shown its face in this basin decides to print. A figure about how often a body prints is a figure about a habit and not about the body, and I am afraid of a habit and not of the body, and I am not going to say the day that the sheet on that gatepost names, because a clerk four hundred yards up that bank has that day on a page and has entered it out of a figure about me and not out of a promise by anybody, and a figure about a habit is not a promise about a later day and I will not stand in this yard and make it into one.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything back for about four seconds. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one sentence, out loud, that a clerk could not use, and it was that he had heard it, and he did not say it a second time and he did not say it to anybody else.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a figure somebody has entered because they had to enter one is not a promise and is not a figure about the body that printed the first one, and that she entered the interval on a page and does not believe it and entered it because a ledger with no date in it is not a ledger, and that a figure about how often a body is a figure about a habit and a habit is not a promise about a day.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane at about half past eleven with his boots black to the ankle again and went into that yard and stood at the end of that trestle table, and this time he waited.

He waited until about ten past twelve, with about nine people in that yard, and then he asked one question out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people. The count came to thirteen words and they are in the minute in his own words.

"**Is anybody going to be able to sleep in that building this winter?**"

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against did not answer it straight away, and the answer came about four minutes later. It came to two hundred and nineteen words and they are in the minute in his own words.

"**I do not know, and I have got three things to say about it and none of them is an answer. It is nine feet of brick with no window and the light that gets into it comes off the mud of that lane, and a man who cuts reeds out of that ditch said in about four seconds on the eleventh of the last month what it is, in front of about nineteen of us, and I am not going to say it for him and he is not in this yard today. There is a term on it and a term is a figure about a term and a term is not a shelter. And there came a rule into a room on the thirteenth of this month that says a use ends when its term ends and that whoever held it has to have it read again, and I have argued with that rule in this yard in front of all of you and I lost, and I am not going to pretend this morning that I have another answer than the three of those, because a person who has been asked a question about where people are going to sleep this winter and gives them a speech instead of an answer has not answered it.**"

About nine people in that yard heard that and about four of them said nothing at all, and the man who keeps a road said thank you and nothing else and went out of that yard and up the lane.

A person who has been asked a question in a yard and has answered it is nobody's post and nobody's refusal, and a question about where a person is going to sleep this winter is a question and not a bid and not an offer, and a clerk did not ask him anything and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The rest of that day was the last day of that run and it went the way the days of that run go.

The reading of the lot stands where it stopped on the sixteenth, at the third of the five things a document that sets a thing out has to say, and it has not got to the fourth of them, which is a person, and the man who convened the bid was at the end of that trestle table at about two and said nothing out loud, and about nine people in that yard came and went behind him, and nobody asked him to go on with it and nobody asked him to stop.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came in at about three and went out at about half past three and said nothing to anybody. At about half past three the man who convened the bid went up the lane on his own to that building and stood in the doorway of it, with the plank door back about four inches on the nail in the hasp, and looked in at the bench and the stool and the two buckets of water and the table with four legs against the outside wall, and he did not go in and he did not put a hand on anything.

About four people in that lane saw him standing in it and none of them said anything to him about it, and he stood there for about nine minutes and then came back down the lane to that yard and stood about nine feet off the end of that table again.

A person standing in a doorway is not holding the door and is not holding what is in the building, and a man who has been asked whether anybody is going to sleep in a building and has answered that he does not know, and has then gone and stood in the doorway of it for about nine minutes without going in, has not been given a post and has not refused one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing put the wire down at about half past four and did not pick it up again that day, and the nine feet of it with the knot in it went into the pocket of his coat on his right side, because his left hand is in a cloth and has not closed since the eleventh of the sixth month, and about four people in that yard watched him do it and none of them asked what the wire was for.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and was not in that yard at any point on this day, and about four people in that yard said at about four that he would be in on the other three days of that week, and nobody in that yard said what for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was in the bottom of it until about half past four. The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart against the brick at the foot of the east wall with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and it is not a shelter, and nobody in that yard asked him about any of that.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes of the bank herself, the way she has carried them for nineteen years, and she did not ask anybody anything on this day and nobody in that yard asked her anything, and there have been three questions asked in that yard this month and every one of them was about a bucket.

---

The light went at about half past eight and the number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and it will be the same figure in the morning, and it was arrived at by counting the people who answered a door, and the sheet's own age on that post is not a figure this district has and no clerk has entered one.

The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and fifty-one days back and nobody in this district can say whether that night happened, and a plank does not keep nights. The fourth line of the offer that has been on a table in this district for seven months is still not read out loud, the refusals to read are nine, and the departure is unspent, and there is no date in any ledger for the next reading.

The bid in that yard was still open on the nineteenth of the ninth month. It had been open eighteen days and it had not been run on any of them and nobody had proposed anything about closing it, and no name went into the column that is ruled and empty on any day of that run, and the reading of that lot is begun and not finished.

The man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of that low wall until the light had gone with his coat folded beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody in any yard of that run said one word to him about what he was going to do with them, and about four people in that yard said goodnight to one another on their way out and none of them said it to him and all of them would have said it to him if he had looked up.

**THE READING OF THAT LOT IS STILL BEGUN AND NOT FINISHED, THE BID IS STILL OPEN, NOBODY PROPOSED ANYTHING ABOUT CLOSING IT, AND A MAN SAID THE FIGURE HE IS AFRAID OF OUT LOUD IN THAT YARD FOR THE SECOND TIME AND DID NOT COUNT IT, AND THE NUMBER ON THAT SHEET WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN.**
