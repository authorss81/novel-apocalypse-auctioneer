# Chapter 347: What Happens If Nobody Ever Answers It

---

The sixth of the ninth month came in with the wind round from the east and it had not dropped by ten, and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen with their coats buttoned. The three figures were on the end of the trestle table. The man of fifty-six read the top one of the three out loud over the mender's shoulder and got the other two. Along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded beside him, on the forty-seventh night of his run and forty-six days in this district.

"**The rival record has been in force ninety-six days and the days since anybody wrote a line on a board are a hundred and fifty-eight, and the table against that wall about four feet high eleven miles off has stood sixty-one mornings, and the first day of the eighth month is thirty-five days past.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and has not moved, and that its own age as a figure about that figure is ninety-six days, and that the rival record and that figure about that figure are two figures counted off one day and are not one figure and are not added together.

---

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was in that yard from about nine until about one with a bar of iron he had not come to use. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds out of that ditch was not in that yard at all that day, which is the first time anybody in that yard had noticed it.

The mender was at the end of that trestle table from about nine until about one with nothing in his hands for the first time this month. He turned the three figures on the boards so that they faced out into the yard instead of along it, and then stood about nine feet off and looked at them from where a stranger would stand. About four people came and read them from there, and none of them asked him what they were, and he did not offer.

About four people went into that building with two doors between eleven and one and stood in it, and one of them lifted the hasp and let it down again, and the nail in that hasp has not moved since the eleventh of the sixth month. The curtain on the nail inside the door moved about four inches in the wind and came back. Nobody put anything on the bench or on the stool and nobody was asked to.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank at about half past ten with two buckets and put them inside that building and came back out and filled them at the trough and did it again at about four. She has asked that woman at the end of that bank about a bucket twice in nineteen years and both times about a bucket, and nobody in that yard said one word to her about the bid all day.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten this morning and are a hundred and sixty-five days, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that the man who refused the bid with a reason of his own did not refuse anything else this week.

The wind had been round from the east since the light came up and the boards of that trestle table had dust on them and nothing else on them. About four people in that yard had been asking each other, in the ordinary way, whether anything had happened this week, and nobody had answered them, and nobody had said the word out loud. There has been no line on a board in this district on any of the hundred and fifty-eight days since the first of the fourth month, and that figure is about boards and not about books or yards, and no line was put on one today.

---

He came into that yard at about ten past twelve with the pair of terms still nailed up on the table with four legs against the outside wall of that building with two doors, under a stone about the size of a fist, and he read both of them out loud in the ordinary voice to about nine people who had asked him to, and the second of them, which is the one about a sound, he read twice. He did not read the fourth line of the offer, which is on a table in this district and has been for seven months, and nobody in that yard asked him to.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against stood about nine feet off the end of that trestle table for the whole of the reading, and did not read either of the two terms himself and did not offer to. The refusals to read are nine and were nine this morning, there is still no date in any ledger for the next reading, the departure is unspent, and he was not asked to read and was not asked not to.

The man of fifty-six said one thing out loud at about half past three, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and fifty-four, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**I have been asked that question before in this basin, in a yard and once in a room, and the last time I answered it I answered it about a sound and the answer turned out to have been about a sound the whole time. Ninety-six days is a figure about how long that record has been in force and it is not a figure about one of you. What happens if nobody ever answers it is that a thing nobody answers stops being a question and turns into a wall, and a wall is a thing a man can lean a fence against, and this district has been leaning fences against that wall since the first of the sixth month. I am not saying it ought to be answered. I am saying what it is, and I have not got a better one than that and neither has anybody standing in this yard.**"

---

At about two the man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart put the shafts of his cart down in that yard and asked one question out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people. He asked what happens if nobody ever answers it.

Nobody answered him. About nine people looked at the man of fifty-six, and the man of fifty-six looked at the boards of that trestle table, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing looked at the man of about twenty-nine, and about four people who had come in off the lane went back out of the yard. A man who has asked a question in a yard in daylight and got no answer from about nineteen people has not been refused, and a person who has not answered a question asked of them has not refused it and cannot be counted either way, and no name was taken and no column was ruled and nobody in that yard was written down anywhere that afternoon.

The man of about twenty-nine stood there with his hands on the shafts for about a minute and a half after that. He had driven four days a week for eleven weeks and he had a delivery at the far end of that road due at about three, and he was the only person anybody in that yard had ever seen read a thing back from the top, and he had done it twice and got it right both times, and he had not said so and was not going to. About nine people in that yard understood what he had just done and about nine people in that yard understood what he could have done about it, and neither set of nine said anything at all.

A woman of about thirty-four who keeps a goat came down that lane at about three with a pail in each hand and stopped at the foot of that gatepost, about nine feet off the two sheets of print on it, and looked at both of them for about four minutes, and did not read either of them out loud and did not ask anybody what was on them, and then went back up the lane. About nine people in that yard watched her do it and about four of them looked away before she had finished. A woman who keeps a goat is not a clerk of a house and is not a keeper of anything and has never been asked to be a keeper of anything, and nobody in that yard asked her afterwards what she thought of it.

The cart was due at the far end of that road at about three and it went out of that lane at about ten past four instead, and the sacks in the bed of it stood in the sun for an hour longer than they should have been. An hour of a carting day is a figure about an hour and is not a figure about a question that nobody answered, and nobody in that yard paid for that hour and nobody in that yard lost anything by it, and the man who lost it did not say he had.

---

Saying out loud in a yard that an unanswered thing turns into a wall has not answered anything and cannot be counted either way, and a shape noticed a second time is not a finding and a third time is a habit, and the standing sentence about being afraid of the morning was said in that yard three times and was not said a fourth time that afternoon.

About four of the people who had come in off the lane went up to that building with two doors at about four and stood on the ditch side of it and looked at the water in it, and the man who works in that ditch was not there to tell them anything, and one of them said out loud that it was about a foot deep, and nobody contradicted him and nobody told him anything. They stood there for about nine minutes and then went back up the bank in ones and twos, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against was at the trough with an empty bucket he had not filled and did not fill it while they were standing there. A man holding an empty bucket at a trough in front of about nineteen people is not a figure about water and is not an offer and is not a holder, and nobody in that yard asked him why he was not filling it.

He said one thing out loud at about six, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and the minute took one hundred and seventy words of it, in his own words.

"**Nobody is going to answer that question tonight and I am not going to answer it either, and I want that entered in the plain words it was said in. I have asked this yard for a figure four times in five days. I got one answer and it was about a fence, and one refusal with a reason, and one page that a man of about nineteen said out loud so that everybody would hear it, and one nothing at all. A record that has been read out in this yard seven times and had its eighth reading refused out loud with a reason on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month is not a thing this yard has answered and is not a thing this yard is going to answer because a building got a term put on it. This bid is not an eighth reading of that record and it is not a ninth, and I am not going to let anybody in this district call it one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the rival record has been read out in that yard seven times, that its eighth reading was refused aloud on the twenty-fourth of the sixth month, and that a refused reading is not a reading. **SHE ENTERED THAT THE LIST OF ABOUT NINETEEN NAMES READ OUT ON THE TWENTY-THIRD OF THE EIGHTH IS NOT AN EIGHTH READING AND IS NOT A NINTH, THAT A BID A DISTRICT RUNS ON ITS OWN BUILDING IS NOT A READING OF IT, THAT A THING THIS DISTRICT MADE IS NOT A DOCUMENT NOBODY OWNS, AND THAT NOBODY PROPOSED A READING IN THAT YARD ON THIS DAY.**

The man of fifty-six and the man of about sixty-four stood at the foot of that low wall together for about nine minutes at about half past five and neither of them said one word and neither of them moved, and about nine people in that yard looked at the two of them and then looked away. Two men standing at a wall is not a conversation and is not a reading, and a man who has been told nothing three times was told nothing a fourth time on this day.

The woman of fifty-eight was at the trough outside that wall at about half past six with the two buckets full beside her and about four people still in that yard, and she asked one of them, out loud, in the ordinary voice, whether that record had ever been read out in this yard before the summer, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said he had been in this basin since he was nine and did not know, and she said that was all right and carried the buckets up the two goes of the bank. A question about a record is not an eighth reading and is not a ninth.

The cart had gone out of that lane at about ten past four and the mender put his right hand flat on the boards of that trestle table and left it there for the length of a breath while the wind took the dust off them, and about four people still in that yard watched him do it and not one of them said anything. A hand on a table is not a figure about a table and is not a reading, and the wind on that lane has taken a page off a flat table more than once.

The light went off the low ground at about half past eight and that gatepost still said four hundred and eleven, and about four people were still in that yard.

**ONE QUESTION WAS ASKED IN THAT YARD IN DAYLIGHT AND ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE DID NOT ANSWER IT, AND AN HOUR OF A CARTING DAY WENT OUT OF THAT LANE LATE, AND NOBODY PROPOSED A READING.**
