# Chapter 387: A Day's Work and What It Is Worth

---

The fifteenth of the tenth month came in with a hard clear light and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam read the four figures off the boards at the end of that trestle table on his way up the bank and got two of them, and the man who mends fencing told him the other two from about nine feet off without looking up, and he said them back and got both, and two people in that yard said that is the fourth time that has happened in this yard and that it is not a clerk and is not a reader and is a man counting other men's figures for his own convenience.

"**Two hundred and eighty-six days from the second of January and this morning, and the days nobody has entered anything stand at three hundred and twenty-five, and the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved for a hundred and seventy-seven days, and that last figure came out of a ladder and he was not asked for it and is not going to be.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that a man who says a figure out loud because somebody else told it to him is not a reader and is not a clerk and is not holding the boards, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

At about eleven the woman of fifty-eight asked the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds for a day's work, in that yard, in the ordinary way, in front of whoever was standing there, and the man of about nineteen counted what she asked and got a hundred and forty-eight.

"**The outlet at the low end of that ditch behind the building with two doors has been silted up since before I started carrying water out of it and I know that because I have been carrying it for nineteen years and I have watched that ditch fill from the bottom up for about eleven of them, and I am not able to get into it because I am fifty-eight and I have two buckets and a bank, and if you clear it today the water goes down the low ground tonight instead of standing in there, and I am not able to pay you and I am not able to be paid, and I am asking you because you are in that ditch every day and I am in that yard twice a day, and I am telling you both of those things before you answer me.**"

He said yes in about four seconds, and nobody counted it and nobody has claimed it since, and he put his hook and his bundle down against the wall where he puts them every morning, and asked for nothing, and went at the outlet at about a quarter past eleven.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person was asked for a day's work in that yard and said yes, and that the asking was done by a person who cannot do the work and not by anybody holding anything, and that a day's work is a day somebody spent and is not a toll and is not a bid and did not become either of those today, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard said out loud that this district has five things it does not have, and three people in that yard said that a man doing a day's work in a ditch is not one of the five, and a day somebody spent is not a thing this district does not have, and the count of the five did not move because nobody counted anything this morning.

He was in that ditch until about half past four. The outlet at the low end of it is about four feet down in a three foot ditch, and to get at it a man has to go down into about a foot and a half of standing water and then put his hands in the silt at the bottom of it, and he had to do that with his own hands and no third thing to stand on and nobody to hold him. About nine people in that yard watched him go down into it and about four of them said out loud that nobody was going to be able to get him out of it if he went under, and about four of them said that he had not asked anybody to get him out of it.

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about one with two empty buckets while he was down in it, filled them at the trough, and carried them up the two goes, and she said one thing out loud to nobody on her way past about the water in that ditch being the same water she carries and it having been in there eleven years. About four people in that yard heard her and about four of them did not, and she was not asked and did not offer, and the page says not asked.

Nobody did go down into it after him. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam stood at the top of that bank for about nine minutes at about one with his bar in his right hand and did not go down, and a man who stands at the top of a bank for nine minutes and does not go down it has not refused to go down it and is not entered either way.

He came up that bank at about half past four wet to the chest, and he could not get the last two feet of it with his legs, and he got up it on his hands and his knees in the mud and nobody offered him an arm and he did not ask for one. He stood at the top of that bank for about a minute with the water coming off him and said one thing out loud, and the man of about nineteen counted it and got ninety-eight.

"**That is a day I have spent and I am not going to be paid for it and I would have said no to a figure, and if anybody in this yard wants to tell that woman of fifty-eight that I did it for her then I am going to tell them that I did it because that water was going to be in that ditch until the winter and I have to stand in it every day. That is not a favour and it is not a kindness and it is not anything, it is a ditch.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid and the count is still five, and that nobody has been paid anything today, and that a person who says out loud that he would have said no to a figure has not refused a figure and cannot be counted either way.

---

The man of about thirty-four who keeps a road came up that lane at about eleven and again at about four and read the four figures both times and got all four both times, and nobody in that yard asked him about the eleven miles. The man who mends fencing went up the lane to the fence end at about ten and came back at about two and did not read the figures out loud at either end of it. The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the east wall with his cart at about two.

He crossed the yard and sat down in the second chair for about a minute, and the yard watched a man sit down in a chair he brought down that lane himself on the eighth of this month, and two of them said out loud that this is the one that counts, and three of them said out loud that he brought it here and he is the last one anybody should be using it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a chair is a chair, and that a man sitting in a chair he brought is a figure about a sitting and is not a figure about the man, and that nobody in that yard asked him why he sat in it and he was not asked and has not refused and is not entered either way.

The man of about nineteen counted four things out loud in that yard today and all four of them were figures, and he wrote all four down in his own hand and put nothing else on that page. The first of the four was the two men getting the boards right between them at ten. The second was the woman of fifty-eight asking her question at eleven and he counted that one as well, which is the first sentence he has ever put in the same column as a figure. The third was the man who keeps a road reading the boards at four. The fourth was the four figures the man of fifty-six read out at about half past four, and the boy got a different figure for that one from the same four numbers than he got from the same four numbers at ten, and three of them said that two men reading the same four numbers in one day and getting two different counts out of them is not a mistake in the numbers.

---

About four in the afternoon the woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough at the near end of that yard, and carried them up the two goes herself, and at about half past four the water in the ditch behind the building with two doors went down about four inches in about half an hour and went along the low ground, and everybody in that yard saw it go and said nothing about it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing spent the whole of that afternoon on the fence at the head of that lane with about nine inches of wire and a knee, and nobody asked him to and nobody offered him anything for it, and two people in that yard said out loud that a man with one hand that closes is the man they ought to be asking to do everything in this district, and he said out loud that they had not asked him and he would have said yes to being asked and that is two different things and neither of them has happened.

The second line in the lot book says that there was about a foot of standing water in that ditch this morning, and that was true at about two this afternoon and is not true at about six, and nobody altered the line and nobody wrote a second figure beside it, and what the book says about it is that a figure in a lot book is a figure about a lot at the time it was taken and is not a figure about that lot afterwards, and that a line that has stopped being true is not a wrong line and is not a mistake anybody made.

That bid stands at forty-five days and was not run today and no one has proposed anything at all about what to do with it. The term on that reading is sixteen days past and did not renew. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and was empty at about six and nothing went into it by anybody, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing read nothing out loud at all today and did not offer to. The man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall on the eighty-sixth night of his run and eighty-six days in this district with nothing in his hands and nobody in that yard said one word to him about any of it.

That night the man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them said one thing out loud at the end of that trestle table to nobody in particular, and nobody counted it and nobody has claimed it since.

"**I put that chair down on the eighth of this month for nobody, and a man of fifty-eight asked another man for a day's work in this yard today, and neither of those two things has anything to do with each other and I am saying so out loud before about four of you put them together.**"
