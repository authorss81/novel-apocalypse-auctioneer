# Chapter 341: Thirty

---

The thirtieth of the eighth month came in about the same as the day before it. About nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten, the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of that trestle table and the man of fifty-six read the middle of the three out loud over his shoulder and got the other two right, and the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of the low wall along the lane with his coat folded beside him for the forty-first night and has been in this district forty days and there is nothing in his hands.

"**Two hundred and forty-nine days on that board and five hundred and sixty-five days on the train on the siding, and the days nobody has entered anything since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and seventy-nine, and two hundred and forty days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-nine days past.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and that it is ninety days this morning as a figure about the figure, and that it is not a day-count for the sheet, and that a nail in a gatepost is not a keeper. She entered that a man of about sixty-four has slept on that low wall on thirty-nine of the forty nights of that run, and that nobody asked him about the one he did not and the record about the not asking says not asked.

---

The board stands outside that room four hundred yards from that gate, against the wall, under a fanlight that lights nothing. It has carried the twenty-fourth of December under three winters of dust and has not been washed, and it carries one mark a day, and a clerk has been putting them on it for years.

At about ten the clerk of nineteen years took a stick of chalk off the sill and started at the top of it and came down. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that lane behind her, because his left hand has not closed since the eleventh of the sixth month and is in a cloth, and he cannot run a finger along a board and feel where one mark stops and the next one begins. So she counted and he read the groups out loud off the wood, and every eighth one he said the number of, out loud, in the ordinary voice, so that two people were saying the same thing about the same mark and neither of them was doing it on her account.

By about half past ten her right hand had gone from holding the chalk to gripping it, and she took the stick over into her left and went on. Near the twelfth of the month there were two marks closer together than the rest, and he said thirty-four, and she said thirty-three, and they went back to the wall and stood in front of it for about four minutes and found that one of the two marks had been put on by somebody who was not the clerk and had put it on small, and that the clerk had counted it and been right.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank with two buckets and stopped at the foot of it, because the house at the end of that bank had had no water yet and she was not going to carry two empty buckets back up in front of about nineteen people. She stood there for the length of the count. She put one of them down in the dust and stood with her hands on her hips and looked at the back of a woman counting a board, and about nine people in that yard came out and looked at the same thing, and one of them asked what she was doing and she told him, in about four seconds, and then went back to standing.

---

The clerk of nineteen years counted from the mark for the first of the eighth month to this morning's mark. She named both days out loud while she counted them, because the first of the eighth is a day nobody had put a figure in front of at all four days ago, and a count with no day named in it is a count of something.

"**First of the eighth, and this morning, and the thirtieth of the eighth, and there is no mark for the thirty-first of the eighth on that board because nobody has got to it yet.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the eighth month has thirty days, and that the first has thirty-one and the second had twenty-eight and the third has thirty-one and the fourth has thirty and the fifth has thirty-one and the sixth has thirty and the seventh has thirty-one, and that the second is the only month any person in this district has ever said was short. **SHE ENTERED THAT A MONTH NOBODY HAS SAID WAS SHORT IS A MONTH NOBODY HAS CHECKED, AND THAT THIS IS THE FIRST TIME THIS DISTRICT HAS CHECKED ONE.** She entered that the board stands at two hundred and forty-nine, and that the figure on the board and the count off it are one figure, and that **A FIGURE THAT REPRODUCES IS A FIGURE ABOUT A COUNT AND IS NOT A FINDING**.

She entered how the figure on that board is arrived at, in words, for anybody who wants to check it after her. Seven days from the twenty-fourth of December to the first of January, and then thirty-one, twenty-eight, thirty-one, thirty, thirty-one, thirty and thirty-one, and the thirtieth of the eighth month. That comes to two hundred and forty-nine, and she entered that the two ways of getting at it are two ways of getting at the same figure and are not two figures.

---

About half past ten the woman of fifty-eight filled the first of the two buckets at the trough outside that wall and stood with it in both hands without lifting it, and about nine people in that yard watched her do it, and then she lifted it and carried it up the two goes of the bank. She did it again at about eleven and the second one went up at about half past eleven, and the first bucket of water on that bank that day arrived about two hours later than the first bucket of water on any of the days before it.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about eleven, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and ninety-five, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Yesterday was the last day of a month this district had any way of standing on. There is not a figure anywhere in this basin for how long the eighth month is, and there was not one four days ago either, and on that day a clerk stood in that yard and wrote down that the length of it is not stated here and was not used, and that page is not wrong and nobody is to go back and take it back, because a day this district could not name is a day it could not count to the end of. The thirtieth of the eighth is the first day a figure for that month can be used on, and this morning is the first time this district has counted the length of a month instead of taking it off the month before it. The second is the only month anybody in this basin has ever said was short. A month nobody has said was short is a month nobody has checked. I have been in this basin since I was nine and I have never once known how long any of them were.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a page written four days ago saying a length is not stated is not wrong and is not to be altered, and that what a district cannot name it cannot check, and that a figure about the length of a month is a figure about a month and is not a figure about anybody who has to get through it.

She entered that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten in the morning, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that a refusal with a reason of its own is not one of those eight, and that a clerk of nineteen years did not ask anybody for a figure today and did not get one.

**A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS ENTERED THAT NOBODY HAS PUT A LINE ON A BOARD ON ANY OF THE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-SECOND DAYS SINCE THE FIRST OF THE FOURTH MONTH, COUNTING THE FIRST DAY IN, AND THAT A STICK OF CHALK AGAINST A WALL IS NOT A LINE ON A BOARD, AND THAT THE MARKS ON THAT BOARD WERE GOING ON BEFORE THE FIRST OF THE FOURTH AND HAVE GONE ON EVERY DAY SINCE, AND THAT THOSE ARE TWO DIFFERENT THINGS AND THE FIGURE ABOVE IS ABOUT THE SECOND ONE.**

---

The woman of fifty-eight filled the first bucket at the trough outside that wall at about half past eleven and carried it up the two goes of the bank, and she filled the second after that, and the water got to the house at the end of that bank about two hours later than it had got there on any of the days before it. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person standing in a yard while a figure is counted is not a figure about the count, and that nobody asked her whether she minded standing there, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that two hours is a figure about a day of work and is not a figure about a board.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man who is afraid of a figure is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about the figure, and that a relative month is a figure about words and is not a figure about a date, and that a clerk who could have made a finding out of a day counting to itself and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing.

Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and fifty-second days since the first of the fourth month, counting that day in, and a stick of chalk against a wall is not a line on a board. Three documents this district does not own, and a count of marks is not a fourth of them. Six instruments this district has built and not named, and a count of marks is not a seventh. Five protected things and no sixth, and a board is not a sixth and was not this morning. Four conditions with no end on it, and a month with an end on it is not a fifth of them and is not added to them. Four columns of not-askings, and no fifth was ruled today and a clerk did not rule one.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against came up that lane at about half past eleven and stood at the foot of that board and looked at it for about nine minutes without touching it. His right hand has not closed properly since the tenth of the eighth month and he kept it in his coat the whole of it.

He said one thing out loud at about twenty to twelve, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people, and a man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Tomorrow evening, at about six, I am going to stand in that yard and ask about nineteen people one question about a building with two doors at the near end of the lane behind that bank. A lot is a thing a bid is for, and that building is the thing, and I am not going to put a figure on it tonight and I am not going to put a figure on it tomorrow either. The term on it is a month. Nobody has asked me for a longer term and a clerk has not offered me one, and when somebody in that yard asks me tomorrow why a month, I will answer it out loud, and I am saying all of it now so that nobody can say afterwards that they did not know it was coming.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a bid was called for the evening of the first of the ninth in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that the thing it is called for is a building with two doors at the near end of that lane, and that the term on it is a month, and that a clerk has entered that figure and has not entered a reason for it because nobody has said a reason out loud yet, and that a figure about a term is a figure about a term and is not a figure about anybody.

---

The ninth of the nine printed nights was the twentieth of the fourth month and is a hundred and thirty-two days back, and nobody in this district can say whether that night happened, and a plank does not keep nights. A clerk entered that a clerk who could have made a finding out of a night in the fourth month and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing mended the end of a fence by the trough from about two until about four and was not asked to by anybody and said nothing about it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man mending a fence nobody asked him to mend is not a person doing a job and has not been given one, and that nobody in that yard asked him what the fence was for. Three documents this district does not own, and a stick of chalk is not a fourth of them. Six instruments this district has built and not named, and a count of marks is not a seventh. Five protected things and no sixth, and a board is not a sixth and was not this morning.

About half past seven the man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of that low wall and the light went off the ground at about half past eight. The figure on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and the day after this one is the first of the ninth, and nobody in this district has said how long that month is.

**AND ON THE THIRTIETH OF THE EIGHTH MONTH A CLERK OF NINETEEN YEARS COUNTED THE MARKS ON THE BOARD OUTSIDE THAT ROOM FROM THE MARK FOR THE FIRST OF THE EIGHTH TO THIS MORNING'S MARK AND ENTERED THIRTY, AND NAMED BOTH DAYS WHILE SHE COUNTED THEM, AND ENTERED THAT THE FIGURE ON THAT BOARD AND THE COUNT OFF IT ARE ONE FIGURE AND THAT A FIGURE THAT REPRODUCES IS A FIGURE ABOUT A COUNT AND IS NOT A FINDING. THE FIGURE ON THE SHEET AT THAT GATEPOST WAS STILL FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN AT HALF PAST EIGHT, AND THAT IS NINETY DAYS THIS MORNING AS A FIGURE ABOUT THE FIGURE.**
