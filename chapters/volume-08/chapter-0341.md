# Chapter 341: Thirty

---

The thirtieth of the eighth month came in much as the day before it, flat light off the low ground and no wind to speak of, and the dust settled on every flat thing in the yard of Lot Seventeen and stayed there. About nineteen people were in by ten. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing had the three figures on the end of the trestle table where they had been every morning of that run, and behind him the man of fifty-six read the middle one of the three out loud over his shoulder, got it, got the other two, and said nothing further, because saying them was the job and there was no pay in it.

At the foot of the low wall along the lane the man of about sixty-four sat with his coat folded beside him on the stones. That was the forty-first night of his run and the fortieth day since he came through the gate. There was nothing in his hands, and he had not put anything in them, and nobody had asked him to.

"**Two hundred and forty-nine days on that board and five hundred and sixty-five days on the train on the siding, and the days nobody has entered anything since the twenty-fourth of November stand at two hundred and seventy-nine, and two hundred and forty days separate the second of January and this morning, and the first day of the eighth month is twenty-nine days past.**"

The clerk of nineteen years came out of the room with the day's page under her arm and stood a moment in the doorway with the fanlight over it not lighting anything behind her. She had a way of writing that other people in that yard had stopped noticing, which was to write the whole of a thing in one line and then read it back to herself with her lips not moving. She wrote this one standing up.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and that it is ninety days this morning as a figure about the figure, and that it is not a day-count for the sheet, and that a nail in a gatepost is not a keeper. She entered that a man of about sixty-four has slept on that low wall on thirty-nine of the forty nights of that run, and that nobody asked him about the one he did not, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she went and got the stick of chalk off the sill.

---

The board stands outside that room four hundred yards from the gate, against the wall, under a fanlight that lights nothing. It has carried the twenty-fourth of December under three winters of dust and it has not been washed. It carries one mark a day, and a clerk has been putting them on it for years.

She started at the top and came down. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up the lane behind her, because his left hand has not closed since the eleventh of the sixth month and it is in a cloth, and he cannot run a finger along a board and feel where one mark stops and the next one begins. So she counted and he read the groups out loud off the wood, and every eighth one he said the number of out loud in the ordinary voice, so that two people were saying the same thing about the same mark and neither of them was doing it on her account.

By about half past ten her right hand had gone from holding the chalk to gripping it, the way a hand does when it has forgotten it is holding anything, and she moved the stick over into her left and went on. Down near the twelfth of the month there were two marks closer together than the rest. He said thirty-four. She said thirty-three. They stood in front of the wall for about four minutes and found that one of the two had been put on by somebody who was not the clerk, and put on small, and that she had counted it anyway and had been right.

The woman of fifty-eight came down the bank with two buckets and stopped at the foot of it. The house at the end of that bank had had no water yet and she was not going to carry two empty buckets back up in front of about nineteen people, and there was nowhere else to stand with them. So she stood there for the length of the count. She set one down in the dust and stood with her hands on her hips and looked at the back of a woman counting a board. About nine people in that yard came out and looked at the same thing. One of them asked her what she was doing, and she told him in about four seconds, and then went back to standing.

---

The count ran from the mark for the first of the eighth month to this morning's mark. She named both days out loud while she did it, because the first of the eighth was a day nobody in this district had put a figure in front of at all four days ago, and a count with no day named in it is a count of something.

"**First of the eighth, and this morning, and the thirtieth of the eighth, and there is no mark for the thirty-first of the eighth on that board because nobody has got to it yet.**"

The mender put his right hand flat on the boards of the trestle table to keep the page from going, and asked her, out loud, in front of everybody, how long the eighth month was. He asked it the way a man asks a thing he already suspects is not there.

She said thirty. Then, because he had asked in front of nineteen people and a thing said once in a yard is a thing that has to be finished, she said the rest of it. Thirty-one for the first. Twenty-eight for the second, and the second was the only month any person in this district had ever said out loud was short. Thirty-one for the third, thirty for the fourth, thirty-one for the fifth, thirty for the sixth, thirty-one for the seventh, and thirty for the eighth. The man of fifty-six asked her to say that again and she said it again, and got the same nine numbers, and about nine people in that yard were listening to a woman recite the lengths of the months they were living in.

Nobody had ever asked her to. The question had been on a page four days ago saying the length of the eighth month is not stated here and was not used, and that page was not wrong, and it was not to be altered, because a day this district could not name was a day it could not count to the end of.

She got the stick of chalk and wrote the nine numbers on the end of the trestle table under the three figures, in a line, and then wrote how the figure on the board is arrived at, in words, for anybody who wants to check it after her. Seven days from the twenty-fourth of December to the first of January. Then thirty-one, twenty-eight, thirty-one, thirty, thirty-one, thirty, thirty-one, and the thirtieth of the eighth month. That comes to two hundred and forty-nine, and the figure on the board and the count off it are one figure and not two, and a figure that reproduces is a figure about a count and is not a finding.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about eleven, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people, and the minute took one hundred and ninety-five words of it, in his own words. He said it to the yard and not to her.

"**Yesterday was the last day of a month this district had any way of standing on. There is not a figure anywhere in this basin for how long the eighth month is, and there was not one four days ago either, and on that day a clerk stood in that yard and wrote down that the length of it is not stated here and was not used, and that page is not wrong and nobody is to go back and take it back, because a day this district could not name is a day it could not count to the end of. The thirtieth of the eighth is the first day a figure for that month can be used on, and this morning is the first time this district has counted the length of a month instead of taking it off the month before it. The second is the only month anybody in this basin has ever said was short. A month nobody has said was short is a month nobody has checked. I have been in this basin since I was nine and I have never once known how long any of them were.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a page written four days ago saying a length is not stated is not wrong and is not to be altered, and that what a district cannot name it cannot check, and that a figure about the length of a month is a figure about a month and is not a figure about anybody who has to get through it.

She entered that the refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given did not move at about ten this morning, and that a man or a woman who keeps a house is not a clerk of a house, and that a refusal with a reason of its own is not one of those eight, and that she had not asked anybody for a figure today and had not got one.

---

The woman of fifty-eight filled the first of the two buckets at the trough outside that wall and stood with it in both hands without lifting it, which she does when she is deciding whether to be angry. About nine people in that yard watched her do it. Then she lifted it and carried it up the two goes of the bank. She did it again at about eleven and the second bucket went up at about half past eleven, and the first water of the day reached the house at the end of that bank about two hours later than it had reached it on any of the days before it.

A person standing in a yard while a figure is counted is not a figure about the count. Nobody asked her whether she minded standing there.

The refusals with no reason did not move. A man who is afraid of a figure is not a finding and is not a habit and is not a figure about the figure. A relative month is a figure about words and is not a figure about a date. And a clerk who could have made a finding out of a day counting to itself and did not is not a clerk who has found nothing.

---

Nobody has put a line on a board on any of the hundred and fifty-second days since the first of the fourth month, counting that day in. A stick of chalk against a wall is not a line on a board. There are three documents this district does not own and a count of marks is not a fourth of them. There are six instruments this district has built and not named and this is not a seventh. There are five protected things and no sixth, and a board is not a sixth and was not this morning. The four conditions this district has got written down have no end written on any of them and a month is not an end, and the four columns of not-asking are still ruled and three of them are still empty, and a clerk ruled no fifth of them this morning and has not been asked for one.

The man the figure of twenty-one years is against came up that lane at about half past eleven and stood at the foot of the board and looked at it for about nine minutes without touching it. There was a figure on nine hundred and thirty feet of it and he had counted most of those nine minutes twice in his life. His right hand has not closed properly since the tenth of the eighth month, and it stayed inside his coat the whole of the time he stood there. He had come up that lane with a decision in him and the board took the decision out of him, the way a thing you have already said out loud takes it out of you the second time.

He said one thing out loud at about twenty to twelve, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people. The man of about nineteen counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-nine, and it went in the minute in his own words.

"**Tomorrow evening, at about six, I am going to stand in that yard and ask about nineteen people one question about a building with two doors at the near end of the lane behind that bank. A lot is a thing a bid is for, and that building is the thing, and I am not going to put a figure on it tonight and I am not going to put a figure on it tomorrow either. The term on it is a month. Nobody has asked me for a longer term and a clerk has not offered me one, and when somebody in that yard asks me tomorrow why a month, I will answer it out loud, and I am saying all of it now so that nobody can say afterwards that they did not know it was coming.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a bid was called for the evening of the first of the ninth in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and that the thing it is called for is a building with two doors at the near end of that lane, and that the term on it is a month, and that she had entered that figure and had not entered a reason for it, because nobody had said a reason out loud yet, and that a figure about a term is a figure about a term and is not a figure about anybody.

---

The ninth of the nine printed nights was the twentieth of the fourth month and is a hundred and thirty-two days back, and nobody in this district can say whether that night happened, and a plank does not keep nights.

From about two until about four the mender worked on the end of the fence by the trough, at the post where the ground had gone out of it, and nobody asked him to, and he said nothing about it, and about four people walked round the far side of that fence instead of the near side for the rest of that week without one of them saying why. A man mending a fence nobody asked him to mend is not a person doing a job and has not been given one. Nobody in that yard asked him what the fence was for.

About half past seven the man of about sixty-four was still at the foot of the low wall with his coat beside him, and the light went off the ground at about half past eight. The number on the sheet at that gatepost was still four hundred and eleven, and it would be four hundred and eleven in the morning, and the day after this one is the first of the ninth month, and nobody in this district has said how long that month is.

**THIRTY, COUNTED OFF THE BOARD IN A YARD WITH BOTH DAYS NAMED WHILE IT WAS COUNTED, AND A WOMAN'S WATER TWO HOURS LATE FOR IT. THE SHEET FOUR HUNDRED YARDS UP THE LANE SAID FOUR HUNDRED AND ELEVEN AT HALF PAST EIGHT, AND THAT FIGURE WAS NINETY DAYS OLD AS A FIGURE ABOUT ITSELF.**
