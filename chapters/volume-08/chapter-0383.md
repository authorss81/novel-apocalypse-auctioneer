# Chapter 383: Asked in a Yard in Daylight

---

The eleventh of the tenth month came in with a low sun and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The mender had gone up the lane at about ten without stopping, so the man of fifty-six stood at the boards at the end of that trestle table with nobody over his shoulder at all and read the four figures out loud in a yard with about four people near enough to hear him, and got all four.

"**The ninth of the nine printed nights is a hundred and seventy-four days back and the table against that wall eleven miles off has stood ninety-seven mornings, and the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved for a hundred and seventy-three days, and the first day of the eighth month is seventy-one days past.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that the pool of refusals with no reason a clerk of a house has given is two hundred and one days and last moved on the twenty-fourth of the third month, and that that is a figure about words on a page and is not a figure about a date.

The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was in the bottom of that ditch before nine and came up the bank at about ten and went out of that yard, and the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in the same ditch with his hook and his bundle from about nine until about half past four, and neither of those two men said one word to the other on this day either.

---

At about half past ten the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said out loud, with about nineteen people standing in that yard, that the building with two doors at the near end of the lane behind the bank is held for a term of a month on the two terms that are lying on the table against its outside wall, and that anybody may go into it, and that the one thing nobody may do in it is be entered in a record of this district by having heard a sound.

About four people in that yard said that is not a new thing, and about four of them said that it has never been said out loud in a yard before, and the man the figure of twenty-one years is against said that it had been written on a sheet and that a sheet weighted with a stone is not the same as a thing said in a yard, and that he was not writing anything and was not asking anybody to sign anything and had not got a page with his name on it.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a building is not a person and cannot hold a lot and cannot pay a toll, and that what is held is held on the two terms on that table and not on anybody, and that the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of fifty-six read the one line out of the lot book standing up, the way he read it once before, and put the book back down under the stone and did not read it back himself.

"**One lot. A building with two doors. About six and a half thousand bucketfuls of water. No person in it.**"

Nobody read it back from the top. That has happened twice before now in this yard and both times nobody did it, and two people in that yard said out loud on this morning that there was nobody to do it, and about four others said that the man who reads the most in this district went up that lane at about ten and has not come back to that table.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud at about a quarter past eleven that he would have read it back and that he has said out loud twice this month that he cannot make anybody keep a thing, and that reading one line back from the top is not keeping a thing and is the only part of it he could actually do.

---

At about twelve the woman of fifty-eight came down that bank with two full buckets and stopped in that yard, because the man of fifty-six was still at that table with the four figures written out in front of him and was saying them over to himself, and everybody in that yard stops when a man is saying figures over. She set the two buckets down in the mud where she set them, and the whole of that yard looked at her and nobody said anything for about a minute.

Then the man the figure of twenty-one years is against asked her, out loud, in that yard, in daylight, in front of about nineteen people, and the man of about nineteen counted what he asked and got seventy-nine.

"**Will you read that one line back from the top, please, out loud, and if you get it wrong I will have it said out loud again from the top and not from the word, and nobody is going to write your name on anything and there is no figure for this and you will not be paid and I am asking you in a yard because that is the only place a thing gets said in this district.**"

She did not answer for about four seconds. Then she said one thing out loud, in the ordinary way, and nobody counted it and nobody has claimed it since.

"**I have carried water up that bank about six thousand times in nineteen years and I have never been asked for one thing out loud and I am not going to be asked for two.**"

And then she read it back from the top, standing up, in that yard, in daylight, and she got it right.

She wiped her hands on her apron before she touched the book, which took her about a second and a half, and the people nearest her saw her do that and none of them said one word about it. She did not lift the stone. She put her own hand on the page to hold it against the wind the way anybody can, and she read the whole of the line from the beginning without stopping in the middle of it and without looking up at anybody while she was doing it, and this is what that yard heard from her, in that order and with nothing left out: "**One lot. A building with two doors. About six and a half thousand bucketfuls of water. No person in it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the one line of the lot book was read out loud in that yard and was read back from the top in that yard by a person in that yard who is nobody in particular, and that it was got right, and that the record about the not asking says not asked, and that no name went into the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud and that column is ruled and is empty and she is not going to write one in it.

Nobody in that yard said one word about her nineteen years. A clerk of nineteen years did not enter that she was honoured and a clerk may not enter it.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was not asked. The man of about nineteen was not asked. The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and is out on it four days in a week. The man who keeps a road was up that lane at about one and read the four figures as he went past and got all four and was not asked either. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was not in that yard and was not asked, and he has not been asked on any of the ten days of this month, and a person who has not been asked has not refused and cannot be counted either way.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud, to nobody in particular, at about one, that he would have done it and was glad somebody else was asked, and that a man who is glad about that is a man who wanted it, and that is the whole of what he is. About four people in that yard said that it is the first thing anybody in this district has said out loud that they were glad about.

Two people in that yard said out loud, in the ordinary way, that a district which has spent seven volumes arguing about what it may ask a person for has asked the one person in it who was standing in the yard with two buckets. A thing this district has spent seven volumes defending people against is a thing that happened in this yard at midday today with the mud coming up over the bottom of two buckets, and the page in her own hand says she was asked and does not say she was asked twice.

Then the woman of fifty-eight sat down in the second chair at the end of that trestle table for about a minute, and the people nearest that trestle table watched her do it, and she got up again out of it without anybody offering her an arm and picked up both buckets and carried them up the two goes of that bank.

She said one thing out loud at the top of the first go, to nobody, and the man of about nineteen counted it and got thirty-three, and about four people in that yard heard it.

"**I have done that once and I am not going to do it again, and nobody has asked me to do it again, and I want that written down that nobody asked me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a person who has said out loud that she is not going to do a thing again has not been asked to do it again, and that the next time is a different day and a clerk was not asked about the next time and is not going to offer.

---

The rest of that day went the way a day goes after something has happened in it. The man of about thirty-four who digs loam was back in that ditch from about two until about half past four and his right hand did not go up past his shoulder at any point in it. The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up for anybody who will use them was at the east wall with his hand-cart standing against it and the tent still rolled where it has been rolled since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and nobody asked him about the tent and he was given nothing.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures off those boards at about four and got all four, and two people in that yard said the top one of the four to each other behind him and got it right, and one of them said out loud that a man who reads a line out loud and then has somebody else read it back has had his line read twice today and that is the first time that has happened to him in five months.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart came up that lane at about half past five with his shafts down and stopped at the head of it and did not come into that yard, and the yard watched him not come in, and nobody said a word to him about midday and he said nothing to anybody and went back up the road.

The bid stands at forty-one days and was not run today, and nobody in that yard put forward anything about closing it. The term on that reading is twelve days past and has not renewed, and what the reading of that lot has got to is still the fourth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say, and the fourth of those five is a person, and the fifth of them is a remedy and there is nobody in this district who can use one.

Along that lane the man of about sixty-four sat out his eighty-second night of that run at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about what happened at that trestle table this morning, and he was not asked to sit in the chair that has been standing at the end of that table since the eighth of this month.

The light went in that yard at about half past seven and the second chair was still at the end of that trestle table with the stone back on the lot book and the boy's page beside it, and the two buckets were up on the top of that bank where they always are, and the man who put that chair down was up the lane with his tent still rolled on the back of his hand-cart.
