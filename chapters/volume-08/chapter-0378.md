# Chapter 378: The Fourth Gate

---

The sixth of the tenth month came in with the rain gone and the lane drying in patches and about nineteen people were in the yard of Lot Seventeen by ten. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that trestle table with the cloth on his left hand and about six feet of wire in the right pocket of his coat that he had bent in the six days of this month so far, and the man of fifty-six read one of the four figures over his shoulder and got the other three.

"**Two hundred and eighty-six days on that board and six hundred and two days on the train on the siding, and the first day of the eighth month is sixty-six days past this morning, and the six households of the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally have not moved for a hundred and sixty-eight days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and that nobody has put a line on a board in a hundred and eighty-nine days counting the first of the fourth month in, and that a line in a lot book is a line about a lot and is not a line on a board.

---

At about half past ten the man who keeps a road came into that yard instead of going past it, and asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing for something out loud, in the ordinary way, in front of whoever was standing there, and the count of what he asked came to a hundred and sixty.

"**There is a fourth gate on that road about a mile off, at the top of the rise where the ground goes to the water, and the bottom rail is out of it and the latch has nothing to hold it to, and I have been opening it with my boot since the second day of this month and I am sick of it. I am not ordering you and I have nothing to give you and I am not asking you to do it for a figure, because I have no figure to give anybody. I am asking you because you mend fences and I keep a road, and I have not asked anybody else, and if you say no then I will go and do it myself badly with one hand the same as I have been doing it, and I am telling you that before you answer so that it is a question and not an offer.**"

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing back in that yard about two seconds later, and the boy counted it and got a hundred and forty-one.

"**I will do it, and I am not going to name a figure for doing it, and the reason I am not going to name a figure is that a figure is a thing somebody is told, and nobody in this yard has been told what a day's work is worth to anybody, and if I say a number this morning it will be a number I made up standing here, and it will be a figure about me and not about that gate. You have a road. I have not got a road. That gate is on yours and I am going to put something in it that is mine and I am going to leave it there for nothing, and that is the whole of the arrangement and there is nothing in it for either of us to hold.**"

About four people in that yard said that that was not how anything was done. About four other people in that yard said that it was exactly how it had been done at that gate in the first place, in September, when nine feet of wire went into it and nobody asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a question asked in that yard this morning was a question and is not a bid and is not an offer and is not a ninth of anything, and that a man who has said out loud that he will do a day's work and will not name a figure for it has not agreed to a term and is not a keeper of a road.

---

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing went up that road at about eleven and was gone until about five.

He worked on the fourth gate with the fingers of his right hand and his right forearm and his knee, and the latch is held with wire now and the rail is bound with wire, and the gate does not hang straight and everybody who has seen it since has said so. It took him the whole of that afternoon because he can only do about half of what a man with two hands does, and he said out loud to nobody in particular at about two that the ninth of a fence in one afternoon is not a good number and that the fourth of this one is worse.

The man who keeps a road stood at the top of that rise and watched him for about nine minutes and then went down the road, and he did not stay, and he did not say anything about what it had cost, and nobody has asked him since what it cost.

Nobody paid him. There is no figure on any piece of paper in this district that a man who is not in a household could be paid out of, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid and the count is still five. A day's work at a gate is not a toll, because a toll is a thing a holder pays under a term and there is no holder in that yard and there has never been a holder in that yard, and a clerk entered that a day's work is not a toll and is not a bid and is not an offer and is a day somebody spent.

---

The rest of that day in that yard was ordinary and got on with it. The man who digs loam was in that ditch with a bar in his right hand held down at his side and came up the bank at about ten and went out and came back at about three and went out again. The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was in the same ditch with his hook and his bundle and came up the bank at about half past four.

The man who puts tables up for anybody who will use them had his hand-cart set against the east wall with the tent still rolled on the back of it, and it has not gone up on any day since the thirtieth of the sixth month, and nobody in that yard asked him about it today and he was given nothing, and about four people in that yard said to him that a man who can put a gate right in one afternoon ought to be the man they go to about, and he said that he has no use in either of his hands and that was the whole of what he said about it, and nobody in that yard asked him anything else about it.

The man who keeps a road came back up that lane at about half past four, stopped at the fourth gate, and put his boot against the bottom of it the way he has been doing since the second day of this month, and the gate did not give, and he stood on that rise with his boot against it for about four seconds and then took his boot off it and stood there, and nobody was on that road and nobody saw him do any of it and he has not said one word about it since.

The man of about nineteen had both sides of that morning's exchange on his own page by about half past four, the question and the answer, in his own hand, and about four people in that yard said that a page of somebody's asking and somebody's answering is not a record of anything and is not a document and he said that it is a page and that is all it is.

About four people in that yard said out loud, in the ordinary way, that a man who mends fences should be asked what a day's work is worth, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said out loud from the fence end of the lane that nobody in this yard has been told, and that is the whole of the answer he had, and two or three of them said that was not an answer and three or four more of them said it was.

The man of fifty-six read the four figures off the boards at the end of that trestle table at about one and at about four, into a yard with nobody at that end of it, and four or five others there said the top one of the four to each other behind him both times and got it right both times, and nobody asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing to read them because he was not in that yard.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives a cart was out on that road and came past the fourth gate late in the afternoon and stopped, and got down and looked at the wire in it for about four seconds and got back on and went on up the road, and he was not asked to do anything and he did not do anything and he was not asked whether it was good and he did not say.

At about five the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came back down that lane to that yard with about nine inches of wire left in the right pocket of his coat and his hands black to the wrist and the knees of his breeches through with the wet of that rise, and about nine people in that yard looked at him coming in and about four of them said good afternoon and about four of them said nothing.

He put the trestle table's corner with his shoulder to hold it while he got his coat off, and stood at the end of that table for about a minute without saying anything, and then said one thing, and a man of about nineteen counted it and got a hundred and thirty-one.

"**That gate is worse than it was and it is held, and those are two things and I have only got one pair of hands, and I am telling that yard about it because I am not going to stand here being praised for it by people who have not been up that rise. I did not get paid and I was not asked to be paid and I would have said no to a figure, and that is the ninth of nothing and it is not a toll and it is not a bid. A day's work is a day somebody spent. That is all it is and it is not nothing, and I am not going to let anybody in this yard turn it into something it is not.**"

The woman of fifty-eight came down that bank at about half past five with two empty buckets, filled them at the trough and carried them up the two goes herself, and she said nothing to anybody and nobody in that yard said anything to her, and about four people in that yard said that she has carried water up that bank about six thousand times in nineteen years and that nobody in this district has ever thought about the figure except her, and that is a figure about a bank and is not a figure about a woman.

Along that lane the man of about sixty-four sat at the foot of the low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, on the seventy-seventh night of that run and seventy-seven days in this district, and nobody in that yard said one word to him about his hands.

That yard was dark by about half past eight and the last of the light went into the ditch behind that building. A clerk of nineteen years entered that a day's work is not a toll, and that a toll is a thing a holder pays under a term, and that there is no holder in that yard and there has never been a holder in that yard, and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have, which is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, is still not paid and the count is still five, and that a figure about a man who mended a gate for nothing is a figure about a gate and is not a figure about the man. The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is still four hundred and eleven. The rival record has been in force a hundred and twenty-seven days and is unanswered. The term on that reading is six days past and did not renew. The reading of that lot is begun and is not finished. The bid is open and was not run on this day and nobody proposed anything about closing it. The fourth gate on that road has wire in it that is not straight and nobody has paid for it and nobody will.
