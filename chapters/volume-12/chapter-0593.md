# Chapter 593: The Last Morning Of A Month This District Has Never Counted Went Past Without Anybody Putting Its Length On A Page

The light came over that low wall about ten minutes earlier on the thirtieth of the fourth month than it had come over it the morning before, and about four people who had come up that bank before eight and had been standing at the near end of that first table for about a month said out loud that the light was early, and about four of them looked at the boards of that first table to see whether the clerk had opened a new page, and she had, and none of them said one word about it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Three pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for ninety-one mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and thirty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that this is the last morning of a month of days that this district has not counted off a board, and entered that she is not going to count it, and entered the reason for that, which is that a month a person is living in is not a month that has ended, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and eighteenth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man who has read four figures off a wall three hundred and eighteen times has never once been asked what would happen to him if that wall came down.

"**The board carries four hundred and ninety-one, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and seven days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and twenty-one, and four hundred and eighty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up came out of that shed with the straight edge under his arm and laid it across the boards of that second table and took his own hands off it and stood looking at the two long sides of that table and said one thing out loud at about ten past eight, in about twenty-three seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Yesterday a boy counted ten mornings left in this month of days and this morning there are nine, and that is the only number in that yard that moved overnight, and I have set tables for about twenty years and I have never once seen a room where the only thing that changed in a night was a countdown.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not entering nine as a figure about any page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came up that bank at about half past eight and stopped at that wall and looked at the marks cut off that board and then looked at his own book and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-two seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said out loud in this yard a fortnight ago that I would not ask that man anything, and yesterday morning I said a second thing about the same thing and about four of you have not worked out what the second thing was, so here it is in the plainest words I have got. I am not able to ask him. What I am not able to do is stand four feet off him and say nothing while about nine other people do the same.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has now said out loud in that yard twice that he is not able to ask a man a question, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about ten with her own door open behind her and a basket of eggs on her hip and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-eight seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nine people came through my door this morning before the light was properly on that bank and not one of them asked me about a page, and four of them bought nothing, and I have decided that I am going to count that as a good morning and not as a thing that has changed, and I would like a clerk to enter that I have decided it and not that she worked it out.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open and had been open all morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen had been counting the marks in chalk along the edge of that second table with his own finger an inch above them since before the clerk had finished, and he said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about twenty-seven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A hundred and thirty-six of them and I counted them without touching the board, and yesterday it was a hundred and thirty-five, and a man who cuts reeds puts one of those on the underside of those boards most mornings and I have watched him do it about four times this month, and he has never once said what it is for, and I have never once asked him.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered the count, which is a hundred and thirty-six, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the low end of that yard and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-three seconds, to about four people near enough to hear, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is not for anything. I put a mark on the underside of a board in a yard I work in twice a week because I do not trust a number I have not made with my own hand, and about four of you have been counting them from the top of that table where you cannot see half of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and his left hand in its cloth, and he said one thing out loud, in about thirty-eight seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got a number and I have had it since the sixteenth of this month and I said yesterday in this yard that I was not going to say the figure, and I am not going to say the figure today either, and what I am going to say is what kind of number it is. It goes down one every morning. I have been in this yard about a month and I have not worked out what happens when it gets to nothing, and about four of you have been waiting sixteen mornings to find out and so have I.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said he has not found out what happens when his number comes to nothing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the three pages, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past one and stood at the far end of that second table with his back to about nine people who were all standing in about four feet of the length of it, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man stood at the far end of that table and entered that she is not entering that about nine people were standing about four feet off him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that this is the last morning of this month of days and that about four of them have said the road keeper read those four figures on it and went on up the lane the way he does, and that about four of them have said that a man who walks that lane twice a day has not once stopped on the last morning of a month in about seven months. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and nothing on the shafts, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and stood at the top of it for a while before he went on, and nobody in that yard spoke to him. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and thirty-second night of that run and that about four of them have said the man on those stones has now been given nothing on two more month ends than this yard has counted. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and thirty-second night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and thirty-first of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Two hundred and forty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and ninety-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and seventy-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and seventy-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and ten days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and fifty marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and thirty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and the first of those moved this morning, and a clerk entered the person who put it there and the reason he gave out loud, and the count of the marks in chalk along the edge of that second table is a hundred and thirty-six and it moved once on this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and the end of a month of days is not a fourteenth thing and nobody in that yard made anything this morning except a clerk with a pen. Two of the four figures on that wall are out, and neither of them was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and eighty-two.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for ninety-one mornings while two pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and the last morning of a month of days did not advance it, and a clerk entered that out loud at about half past ten with the reason, which is that a month turning is not a reading of anything. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, and about nine things were said out loud in it, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and about four people at that first table have said since that they have started noticing it in the ledger before they get to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the end of a month of days is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and the length of that month is on no page in this district and is going to stay on no page in this district.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full and were stood against the low side of that first table because the boards were wet, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, stopped for about half a second at the third of them, and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a month turned tonight without anybody in that yard noticing except a clerk with a pen, and that about four of them have said the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months read them tonight the same and that is the whole of what a month is. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and eighteenth of those mornings and the last of them in a month, and that about four of them have said a man who reads four figures has not once asked whether the month he is counting them in has a length, and that about four of them have said the answer is that nobody knows and that a clerk has said out loud this morning that she is not going to find out either.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the thirtieth of the fourth month about being able to stand four feet off a man and say nothing, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and about nine feet off a man who had his back to about nine people for about a minute and a half and did not turn round once while it was happening.
