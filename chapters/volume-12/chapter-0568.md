# Chapter 568: It Came In Between A Quarter To Eight And Half Past Eight

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the end of that second table at about a quarter to eight on the fifth of the fourth month with his stub of chalk in his right hand and his left palm flat on the boards, where he puts it every morning of his working life, and about four people in that yard were standing near him at that hour because about four of them had come up that bank early and had nothing else to do before eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those were looking at the end of that table and not at each other.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and seven days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table was two at about a quarter to eight this morning and is three, and entered the person and entered the reason, and entered that she has not been to that end of the table since a quarter to eight and entered why, which is that she has not needed to.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and ninety-third of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man at that table has watched two of these arrive this month and that he has described both of them out loud before a clerk could get a pen to the page.

"**The board carries four hundred and sixty-six, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and eighty-two days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and ninety-six, and four hundred and fifty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud at about half past eight, standing where he had been standing at a quarter to eight, in about thirty-two seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put that mark at a quarter to eight where I always put it and there were two sheets lying at that end of that table and I counted them without wanting to. I have been away from that table twice since and I am not asleep, and I am telling you now that I was not away at all, and if anybody wants to know the other thing then they had better ask me now while I still know that I was not away.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who cuts reeds was at that table at about a quarter to eight and at about half past eight and that the number of sheets on that table was different at the two times, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one more thing from where he was standing, in about thirty-two seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The one that came on the twenty-fifth got here between a quarter to eight and a quarter to nine and this one got here in less than that, and about four of us have worked out that whoever is bringing them is bringing them before this yard fills up, and about four of us have worked that out, and I do not know what the next one of us is going to say about it and I am not going to be the one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering who brings them and entered the reason, which is that she does not know, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that second table at about nine and said one thing out loud, in about thirty seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is the same hand as the one from the twenty-fifth and it is the same hand as the one from the thirtieth, and I can tell you how I know without being a clever man, because the hand crosses itself on the second word every single time, and about four of you have looked at three sheets and not seen that, and it is the only thing I have got that is worth a thing this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who puts a page on that table said out loud this morning that three pages in that yard are in one hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud after that, in about thirty-seven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering three on that table this morning and I am entering the man who counted two at a quarter to eight and the reason he gave out loud in front of all of you, and I am not entering the hand, and the reason I am not entering the hand is that a hand is a thing you cannot check unless you watch a person write, and a woman said that out loud in this yard a fortnight ago and she was right and I am not going to argue with her in a ledger.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a hand is a thing that cannot be checked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about a quarter past ten with a barrow he had not emptied and stopped at the end of that second table, and looked at three sheets of paper lying along the end of it, one after the other, from the far end to the near end, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man looked at three pages in that yard and entered that she is not entering what he made of the third one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about half past ten with her shop open behind her, and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-four seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have had that page on my counter for a fortnight now and about nine people a day come into that shop and about four of them go straight to it before they say what they came for, and I have got a thing to sell on the other side of the same counter, and I have not moved that page an inch and I am not going to, and a shopkeeper who moves a thing off her own counter is a shopkeeper who has decided something about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open, and entered that about four people a day go to a page before they say what they came for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about thirty-six seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is three on that table and one in a shop, and that is four, and I counted them and I am not going to say what the next number is going to be, because I said in this yard on the twenty-ninth of the month before that I was not going to say the second half of anything until somebody else said the first half to me, and nobody has, and I am a man who keeps to what he says out loud even when it is only about a number.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy is not going to say what a number will be, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about half past eleven with his right hand flat on the boards, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-eight seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got the four turns done on that rail and the fifth wants doing in a dry wind and not a wet one, and the wind has gone round to the north in the night, so I have come up here for nothing at all, and about four of you have watched me come up here and stand at this table and not do anything and that is the whole of my morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man came up that bank and did not do the thing he came for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and did not open it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that he was there and that she entered nothing else, and about nine people at that east end have said since that a man with a book under his arm has said nothing out loud in that yard for three mornings running.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that three pages are lying at the end of a table in that yard and none of them has ever come up that lane, and that a man who has walked that lane for about seven months has never once seen a person carrying anything at all up it. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and stopped for about as long as it takes to say nine words, and did not stop for anybody else in that yard on that day, and had said out loud at about nine that three pages in that yard are in one hand. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a page came into that yard this morning and the man on those stones has not been told. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and seventh night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and six of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and sixteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and seventy-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and seventy-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and forty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and forty-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and eighty-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and twenty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred and eleven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning, and one of those hundred and eleven is this morning's. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a page that came into a yard at about a quarter past eight with nobody's hand on it is a page and not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk said that in this yard on the twenty-fifth of the month before and a man who keeps a tally said on the same morning that he has not made a place for it in his own book. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been put right this morning, and the figure on the wall is the one that stands.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and it was two at about a quarter to eight, and it went up while there were four people in that yard and by ten there were about nineteen of them, and the reason is on the page because a man said it out loud before anybody asked him for it, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move and a clerk has said out loud that a page in a hand is not a new kind of thing. The reading of that lot did not move and is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and a page arriving before a yard fills up is not a reading going any further along, and a fifth line is not the fifth thing and did not pay it. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and about nine people said a thing out loud in that yard this morning including a boy who has not filled that column and is not going to.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and three pages lying at the end of one table in a yard is not one of the six things standing in that yard that are not a payment.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full by two women who did not stop at either table, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and did not look at the three sheets at the end of that second table, and nobody there said one word to her. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the third morning this month on which a page came into that yard and that the man who has said four numbers out loud every morning since before the middle of the sixth month did not turn round, and that about four of them have said that he does not turn round any more.

A man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said in that yard this morning that there were two sheets at that table at a quarter to eight and three at half past eight and that he was not away from that table, a clerk of nineteen years entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table went from two to three and entered the person and the reason, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about half past eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the three sheets.
