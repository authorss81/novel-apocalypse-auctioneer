# Chapter 579: He Said The Reason Out Loud Before He Put His Hand On It

There were three sheets lying along the end of that second table at about a quarter to eight on the sixteenth of the fourth month and there had been four the day before, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was standing at the near end of that first table with his cart at the bottom of that bank and nothing in his hands at all, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had counted the sheets from the lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those were facing up that bank.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and eighteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table was four at about a quarter to eight this morning and is three, and entered the person and entered the reason, and entered that she is entering the reason and not a judgement.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fourth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a thing left that yard this morning with a person carrying it and a reason said out loud, and that this is the second time in a month a thing has left that table and the first time it went up a bank with a cart behind it and this one is going up on foot.

"**The board carries four hundred and seventy-seven, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and ninety-three days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seven, and four hundred and sixty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart said one thing out loud at about half past nine, before he had gone near that table, in about forty-four seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to take one of these off that table and put my hand on it in a moment, and I am saying why out loud first, and the reason is that a man was asked in this yard two days ago what it costs to copy a thing, and a stranger is going to be told that there is a man he can come and ask, and he cannot ask a man about one page in a yard and one page in a shop and not ask him about the other one, and about nine people in that shop have already been asked to look at one and could not tell.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the person, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart put his right hand flat on the third sheet along the end of that second table, lifted it, and carried it up that bank in his own two hands, and did not stop for anybody in that yard on the way, and about nine people watched him go up it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a sheet came off the end of that second table in that yard in the open at about half past nine on the sixteenth of the fourth month, and entered that the number of sheets on that table is three and it was four, and entered the person and entered the reason, and entered that she is entering the reason and not a judgement, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

He went up that bank at the pace of a man carrying something he has already decided to carry, and the gap he left at the end of that second table was about the length of his own hand and nobody put anything in it, and about four people at that first table said nothing at all and about four of them said the same nothing at the same moment.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was standing in her own doorway when it came up that bank and said one thing out loud, in about thirty seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are two on my counter now and my door is open and about nine people came through that door before I opened this morning, and I am still standing here saying I cannot tell which of them is ours, and I have said it in public three times now and about four of you have worked out that I have been right all three times, and I would like it entered that I have not stopped saying it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that two pages are on that woman's own counter with the door open, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

That woman had two sheets on that counter where there had been one, and she had not asked for the second one and did not say who had put it there, and the boy of about nineteen looked at the two of them for a while and then looked at the gatepost instead, and the clerk saw him do it and entered nothing about it.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about ten, in about twenty-seven seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is three. It was four at a quarter to eight and it is three now, and a man said the reason out loud before he touched it, and about four of you have said that is the only rule this yard has got about that table and it is about a month old and it came out of a man not being able to be in two places at once.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is three, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-six seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are two pages in a shop and three in this yard and that is five, and the four in my book has not moved and it is not going to, and a page that goes up a bank into a shop is still a page this district cannot account for, and about four of you have wanted me to say that out loud and I have.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally has said out loud that the count of four has not moved, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past ten and stopped at the end of that second table and looked at three sheets lying along the end of it, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man looked at three pages in that yard and entered that she is not entering what he made of them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards, and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-four seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got a rail to do on the high side that has been loose for three days and I have not done it and I am not going to today, and about four of you have worked out that I am not standing here for the rail, and you are right, and I have been at the end of this table every morning for two volumes and I have watched a yard find out what it is after and it has not come to this end of the table yet.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about half past nine in the ordinary way, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the three sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man carried a page up that bank in his own two hands this morning with a reason said out loud first, and that a woman asked that man a real question on that lane two days ago and got a real answer, and that about four of them have said those two things are the only two things that have happened in that yard this month that anybody in that shop would be able to understand. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had carried a page up that bank on foot at about half past nine. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a page went up that bank this morning and that about four of them have said that the man on those stones is going to be asked about one of them one day and has not been told which day. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and eighteenth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and seventeen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Two hundred and twenty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and eighty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and eighty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and fifty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and sixty days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and ninety-six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and thirty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred and twenty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and an eighth mark went on the underside of those boards before about nine. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a page that goes up a bank into a shop is a page and not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered that on the second of the month and it holds this morning as well. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands, and the man who has said one of those four for three hundred and four mornings was not asked this morning anything at all.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and it was four at about a quarter to eight, and it went down while there were about nineteen people in that yard, and the reason is on the page because a man said the reason out loud before his hand went on the sheet, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and there are now two pages on a shop counter in that parish and about nine people a day go into that shop. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five and did not move, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and a page going up a bank on a man's arm is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a woman who has said in public three times that she cannot tell which of them is this district's has a name against nothing.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man carrying a page up a bank in his own two hands is not one of the six things standing in that yard that are not a payment.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full and were carried up past the first table, and the woman of about fifty-eight came up behind them and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a page went up that bank this morning at about half past nine and she did not look at that table at all, and that about four of them have said that a woman who has not looked at a table for a fortnight is a woman who has decided something about it. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this district has moved two pages out of that yard this month and neither time did anybody find out anything except that a counter was bare and a table was not, and that about four of them have said that is the finding and it is not an answer.

A man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart said the reason out loud in that yard at about half past nine before he put his hand on a sheet, carried it up that bank in his own two hands, and put it on a shop counter, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the three sheets.
