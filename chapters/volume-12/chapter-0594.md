# Chapter 594: A Month Turned Overnight And About Nineteen People Came Up A Bank And Not One Of Them Said So

A clerk of nineteen years wrote a date at the top of her own page before about four people in that yard had come up that bank, and about nine of them came up it before eight and went to the ends of the two tables as they have every morning of the last month, and about four of them had come up it meaning to mention that a month had turned and had not managed it by about ten, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Three pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for ninety-two mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and thirty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered the date at the top of her own page, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that a month turned overnight and that nobody in that yard said one word about it before about ten, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and nineteenth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man has now read those four figures off that wall on the first morning of four different months without once being told which month it was, and that neither of those has ever once been said out loud in that yard.

"**The board carries four hundred and ninety-two, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and eight days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and twenty-two, and four hundred and eighty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came up that bank at about half past eight with a basket on her hip and stopped at the near end of that first table and looked at the date at the top of that clerk's page for a while and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-three seconds, to about four people near enough to hear, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got eleven houses and I go to about nine of them before I come up this bank, and not one of the women in those houses has said one word to me this morning about anything, and a month has turned, and the two of those are the same kind of morning and mine are the ordinary ones.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman came up that bank with a basket and looked at a date and went down that bank again, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen had come up that bank before the clerk had finished her entry and had stood at the near end of that first table reading the top of that page over her shoulder, and he said one thing out loud at about ten past nine, in about twenty-nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted nine mornings left in that month yesterday and today it is a new month and there is no number for the new one, and I have been in this yard every morning of the last one and I did not know until about a quarter to eight this morning that it was going, and about four of you did not know either, and a clerk knew at about four in the morning and said nothing.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered that he is not entering a figure for the length of the month that has just ended, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was at the end of that second table with the straight edge in his hand and said one thing out loud, in about thirty seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I laid that straight edge across these boards yesterday and today it is ten minutes of light earlier and nobody has moved a thing, and I have set tables in about nine yards in this parish and I am telling you that a yard does not reset because a clerk turned a page, and a yard resets because somebody in it does something, and nobody in this one has done anything since the twelfth of last month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man is not entering the twelfth of the month as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about ten and stood at the far end of that second table, and about four minutes later he moved about two feet along it and stood at the end of the four pages instead of at the end of the table, and he looked at the fourth line of the near page with his forefinger an inch off it and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man moved along that table and entered that she is not entering two feet, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about half past ten with her own door open behind her and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-two seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A month turned overnight and nine people came into my shop before I had the door open and not one of them mentioned it, and about four of them have been in my shop on the first morning of every month for two years and not one of them has ever mentioned it, and I would like it entered that a shop notices a month turning and a yard does not, and I do not know why and I have stopped guessing in public about that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a shop was open, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about thirty-nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I wrote a date at the top of that page at about four this morning and about nine of you have read it since and not one of you has said one word about it to me or to anybody else, and I am not offended and I am not going to enter it as a finding, and the reason is that a month turning is a thing that happens to about nineteen people in a yard and not one of the nineteen does anything differently on the morning after it, and that is the most ordinary fact I have entered in nineteen years.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing and the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about half past eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and his left hand in its cloth, and he said one thing out loud, in about thirty-two seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I did not know either. I came up that bank this morning with a rail under my arm and I have been in that yard every morning of a month and I did not notice the month end until a clerk put a date on a page, and that is the fourth month of those I have stood in this yard and I have noticed two of them, and I am not going to say anything else about that this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the three pages, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard at about twelve with the flat book shut under his arm and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-one seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a new page. That is all I have got this morning. I have been standing at that wall with a book under my arm for about a month and a new page is the first thing anybody has put in front of me that I have not already seen coming for a fortnight.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man had not been asked anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a month turned in that parish overnight and that the man who walks that lane read the four figures off that wall on the first morning of it and said good morning to about four people,  The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and a coil of rope on the shafts, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and about four people at that first table watched him go up it and did not ask him for the rope. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a month turned overnight and that nobody in that yard said one word about it and that the man on those stones has not been given anything on the first morning of a month for as long as anybody there can remember. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and thirty-third night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and thirty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and forty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and ninety-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and seventy-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and seventy-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and eleven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and fifty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and thirty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and one mark went on the underside of those boards before about nine. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a month turning overnight is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered that out loud at about eleven with the reason, which is that a page turning is not a thing a district makes. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and no figure on it was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and eighty-three.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for ninety-two mornings while two pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a new page at the top of a clerk's book did not advance it, and a clerk entered that out loud with the reason, which is that a page turning in a book is not a reading of anything. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, and about nine things were said out loud in it, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and about four people at that first table have said since that they looked for it in the ledger before they read anything else this morning.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a month turning is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about nineteen people in that yard did not notice a month turning, and that the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months did not notice it either, and that a woman who reads the same three lines every evening for four months is the person in this parish least likely to be surprised by a date. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a clerk wrote a date at the top of a page at about four this morning and a man read four figures off that wall twice in the day and the two of those are the only two things in that yard that knew a month had turned, and that a yard is a place where things get entered and not a place where anybody gets told.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the first of the fifth month about not having noticed that a month had turned until a clerk wrote a date on a page, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about half past eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the three pages.
