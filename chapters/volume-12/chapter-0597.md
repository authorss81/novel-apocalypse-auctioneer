# Chapter 597: A Clerk Of Nineteen Years Entered A Way Of Being Found On A Page In That Yard And Entered That She Is Not Entering His Name And Entered The Reason

The boards of that second table had dried on the north side by about seven on the fourth of the fifth month and were still wet on the south one, and the clerk of nineteen years had come up that bank at about half past six, which is before she has ever come on any morning in about a year, and had put her own book down at the near end of that first table and squared it with the flat of her hand before anybody else was in that yard, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Three pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for ninety-five mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and thirty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that a man said yes in that yard yesterday and that she is entering today a way of being found, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and twenty-second of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a clerk came up that bank at about half past six this morning and squared her own book with the flat of her hand before anybody was in that yard, and that about four of them have said that is the first time a person in that yard has done anything to a page before about nine people got there.

"**The board carries four hundred and ninety-five, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and eleven days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and twenty-five, and four hundred and eighty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

A man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had been standing at the end of that second table with his straight edge in his hand since about seven, watching that clerk write. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about nine and went to the near end of that second table and did not put his hand on the boards, and stood about nine feet off the far end of it, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I asked one question on the second of this month and I stood about nine feet off a man while he said yes to it, and this morning I am not putting my hand on that table for a reason I am not going to explain, and about four of you have noticed that I have not put it down and about four of you have worked out the reason and I am not going to confirm it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man did not put his hand on that table, and entered that she is not entering the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past nine and stood at the far end of that second table, and the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about thirty-seven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Before I write this I am going to say out loud what I am not going to write, in front of all of you, and I have said this in this yard before and I am saying it again because the last time was a fortnight ago and a thing said out loud in a yard is not a thing that stays said. I am not writing his name. There is a reason and it is the only reason I have and I will give it once and then I will not give it again this month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing and entered the reason for it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

"**A page is a thing a stranger can be handed without anybody standing next to it, and the whole of what that page is worth is that it can be handed over, and if his name is on it then a stranger can carry his name about nine hundred yards from this yard without ever meeting him, and I have watched that happen to about four people in two volumes and I have never once been the one who wrote the name.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the reason, and entered that she is not saying it a second time this month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the clerk of nineteen years wrote on that page, in the open, in front of about nineteen people, with about nine of them reading over her shoulder, and the boy of about nineteen read out loud what she wrote, in about thirty seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He digs loam in the low ground at the second channel, and he is there from before eight until the water is over him, and a man is to ask any house on that channel, and he is not to ask at this yard, because a man who is not from this parish cannot find this yard and that is a thing this district found out in a fortnight and it is not a small thing.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy read a thing out loud in that yard and entered that he read it, and entered that the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six and that she is entering him in no column, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about thirty-two seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is a person's whereabouts on a public page and it is the first one this district has written in six volumes, and I want it entered that it is a place and not a name, and that about four of you have worked out within about nine seconds of hearing it what the difference is between those two things, and I am not going to say it a second time this month because I have said the reason once and I said I would.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering a name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about twenty-two seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is where I am and it is on a page in a yard and I did not ask for it and I am not taking it back, and about four of you should know that I said yes to being asked and not to being written down, and the second of those two is what you did.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he did not ask for it and is not taking it back, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard said one word for a while. The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table with her own door open behind her and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-seven seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About four of you ask me things a day and a page went up that bank this morning with a place on it and not a name on it, and the man at the end of that table can now be found by anybody who reads, and not one of the four of you could find him at eight in the morning by asking anybody in this yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the door of that shop was open, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

And then about nine people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man said yes on the third of this month and on the fourth he said he had not asked to be written down and did not take it back, and that about four of them have said this district has now got a man on a page who says the page is not what he agreed to, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what a stranger holding that page is supposed to do with a man who says that.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a page in that yard now tells a man from nine hundred yards off where a man is, and the road on that lane is eleven miles long with a gate at the ninth, and the two of those have nothing to do with each other yet. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man on a page in that yard has a place on it this morning and the man on those stones has not been on a page in about two volumes. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and thirty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and thirty-fifth of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and forty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Two hundred days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and seventy-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and seventy-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and fourteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and fifty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and forty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a way of being found is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a way of being found is not a thing a district makes, it is a thing a district publishes, and those are not the same and the count of the five things this district does not have is still five. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and no clerk in that yard went near either of them this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and eighty-six.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for ninety-five mornings while two pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and writing a person's whereabouts on a page did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a document which says where its person is has still not said what happens when that person says no, and a man said this morning that he did not ask to be written down, and about nine people in that yard have said since that that is what happens when that person says no and that it happened in about nine seconds. Nothing was read out of anything into that book today except a page a clerk had written herself, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a boy read a clerk's own entry out loud in that yard this morning at about half past ten and that column is still empty, and a clerk entered that she is not entering him in it, and the reason is that he read a thing this district wrote about a person and not a thing this district has made.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and publishing where a man is not a payment and did not pay it, and about four people at that first table have said since that a page that says where a man is and not what he is paid is the first thing this district has written that a person could be found with and that nobody in that yard has worked out what he is owed for it.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, put her own finger on the fourth of them for about a second and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man has a place on a page in that yard and the woman who reads those three lines every evening has nothing on any page and has not asked for anything in about four months. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man can be written on a page in about nine seconds and four numbers can be read off a wall in about four, and that about four of them have said this district has never once compared those two speeds and it is not going to start today.

The clerk of nineteen years entered a way of being found on a page in that yard on the fourth of the fifth month at about half past ten in front of about nineteen people, and entered that she is not entering his name, and entered the reason, which is that a page is a thing a stranger can be handed without anybody standing next to it, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that table at about twelve with his right hand in his pocket and not on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the three pages.
