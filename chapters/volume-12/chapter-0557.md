# Chapter 557: There Were Two Sheets On That Table

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the edge of that second table at about a quarter to nine on the twenty-fifth of the third month with his stub of chalk in his hand and his left palm flat on the boards, and there were two sheets of paper lying along the end of that table, and about four people in that yard saw that before anybody had said a word, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those were standing in front of two sheets of paper on a table and about four of them had been in that yard at eight and had seen one.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and ninety-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table was one at about a quarter to eight this morning, and entered that she has not been to that end of the table since and entered the reason for that too, which is that she has not needed to.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and eighty-second of those mornings, and that there are two sheets of paper in that yard this morning and that a man at that wall read four figures off it without stopping and did not turn round.

"**The board carries four hundred and fifty-five, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and seventy-one days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and eighty-five, and four hundred and forty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud at about a quarter to nine, with his hand still on the boards, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said  and got seventy-four  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put that chalk mark at about a quarter to eight this morning where I always put it and there was one sheet on that end of that table. I have been at that table about forty times a day for thirty years and I do not need to count them to know there was one. There are two now. I have not been away from this yard and I have not been asleep.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who cuts reeds was at that table at about a quarter to eight and at about a quarter to nine and that the number of sheets on that table was different at the two times, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years said a second thing out loud after that, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said  and got eighty-one  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of sheets on the end of that second table is two this morning and it was one at about a quarter to eight, and I am entering that, and I am entering the reason a man has just given out loud, and I am not entering where the second one came from, and the number of things this district has made is thirteen this morning and it is thirteen this afternoon and a sheet is not a fourteenth thing.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is two, and entered that she is not entering where the second one came from, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past nine, and the boy counted what he said  and got one hundred and thirty-five  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are four lines on that one. The first two are the same two as on the book and the third is ruled and there is nothing on it, and the fourth has nine words on it and I counted them twice this morning and it is nine, and the nine of them are the same nine that are on that book and they say that the line above is a person and not named here, and about four people in this yard are going to want me to say the nine words out loud and I am not going to, because a clerk refused a reading in this yard a few days ago and I am not about to fill that column on a morning when I do not know what the page is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a sheet with four lines on it came into that yard this morning with a ruled third line and nothing in it and nine words on a fourth line, and entered that those nine words are the nine on the fourth line of that book, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that second table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what she said  and got seventy  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A hand is a thing you cannot check unless you watch a person write it. That is the whole of it and it has been the whole of it since the first day of this month, and about four of us have been looking at two pages and calling it a question, and it is not a question, it is a hand, and nobody in this yard watched a hand.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open, and entered that a hand is a thing you cannot check unless you watch a person write it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that second table at about half past ten and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said  and got fifty-nine  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is not my hand, and I am saying that in front of all of you before anybody has asked me, because I have been standing at this table every morning since the first morning of the month before waiting to be asked and I would rather say it than have somebody say it for me and be right.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who put a sheet on that table on the first morning of the month before said out loud this morning that a second sheet is not in his hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up that bank at about a quarter to eleven and stood at the end of that second table and read the fourth line of that second sheet and said nothing for about a minute, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man read a line in that yard this morning and entered nothing about what he thought of it and entered that nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about eleven the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said  and got fifty-six  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a thing in this yard that this yard did not make, and about four of us have been standing in front of it since a quarter to nine, and I would like it entered that standing in front of a thing is what we have done instead of looking at where it came from.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that his right hand was flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on either sheet,and the record about the not asking says not asked. and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm, and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said  and got fifty-eight  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have three in that book that this district made and cannot account for, and I have not got a place in that book for this one, and I am not going to make a place, because the day I make a place for a page is the day that book stops being a thing you can check.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally has not made a place in his own book for a page that came into that yard this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man has been in that yard every morning for two months and has read four figures off that wall every one of them, and that there is a page in that yard with his own four lines on it and a hand on it that is not his, and that he has not been asked. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had said a thing out loud at that table at about half past ten. A man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and it was at about half past three that a clerk looked over, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a page came into that yard this morning that nobody made and that the man on those stones is the second person in that yard today that nobody has thought to tell anything to. At about four a clerk of nineteen years entered that this is his hundred and ninety-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and ninety-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and sixty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and sixty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and thirty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and thirty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and seventy-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and fourteen marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and a sheet of paper with four lines on it is not a correction of a figure on a wall. The count of things this district has made is thirteen, and the thirteen is smaller than the twelve, and a page that arrives in a district is a page and not a thing anybody has made.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is two and it was one at a quarter to eight this morning, and it moved while about nineteen people were in that yard, and the reason is on the page because a man said the reason out loud in front of all of them, and nobody said a word about the number of documents this district does not own this morning and a clerk did not enter one. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and the fifth of them is a remedy, and a fourth line is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and about four people at that second table have said that a page with a fourth line on it is the only thing in that yard that has a person in it and a person not in it at the same time. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and a boy counted nine words on a line in that yard and did not read them out loud and said why out loud, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk is not entering the name of the man who read a sheet of the other copy out loud once, and is not entering the name of a boy who counted nine words this morning. About nine people said something out loud in that yard this morning, and a page came into that yard in the middle of it, and about four of them have said that the two of those facts belong in the same sentence and that nobody has written that sentence. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a page that nobody made is not one of the six.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and about four people at the foot of it have said since that the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and that the woman of about fifty-eight came up behind them, read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, looked at the fourth line of a second sheet on the end of that second table for about as long as it takes to count nine, and said nothing to anybody, and that nobody there said one word to her. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the first morning in six volumes that a page has come into that yard which nobody in it can say who made, and that about four of them have said that a yard which has spent a month being unable to say which of two pages is its own has now been handed a third page by nobody at all.

A sheet of paper with four lines on it, the third of them ruled and empty and the fourth carrying nine words in a hand that is not this district's, came onto the end of that second table in the yard of Lot Seventeen between about a quarter to eight and about a quarter to nine on the twenty-fifth of the third month, and a man who was standing at that table the whole of that time said so out loud and gave the reason, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that the number of sheets on that table is two, and entered that a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on either sheet, and the number of things this district has made is thirteen.
