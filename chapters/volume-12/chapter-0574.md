# Chapter 574: Everybody In That Yard Got Ready And Nobody Asked Him Anything

The wind had gone round to the north in the night and there was a cold edge on that yard by about half past seven on the eleventh of the fourth month that had not been there in any morning of that month, and the boards of that second table were stiff with it and four sheets of paper lay along the end of them, and about four people in that yard who had come up that bank before eight stayed up that bank longer than they meant to, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those were facing the lane.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and thirteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that about nine people in that yard are standing in a position to see that lane this morning.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and ninety-ninth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a yard which has spent a fortnight being unable to say who made four sheets of paper has spent this morning standing in rows facing a road.

"**The board carries four hundred and seventy-two, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and eighty-eight days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and two, and four hundred and sixty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale had her shop open behind her at about ten and had put her own table out onto the pavement with the leg down, which she does not do before the middle of the year, and about nine people in that yard had seen her do it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a shop table has been put out on that pavement and entered that she is not entering why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was sitting on that first table with his hands on his knees and his cart standing at the bottom of that bank, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man is sitting on that table facing the lane and entered that he has not said anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen walked down that lane at about half past ten and came back up it at about ten to eleven and went past the gate and came back past the gate again, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy walked up and down that lane three times and entered nothing about why, and about four people in that yard have said that a boy of about nineteen does not walk up and down a lane three times in a morning for the pleasure of it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about half past ten with his right hand flat on the boards and about nine feet from the place on that lane where a man who walks it twice a day stops to read a wall, and about four people in that yard counted where he was standing, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man stood at about nine feet from a place on that lane and entered that nine feet is not a figure on any page in this district and is not a distance anybody has entered, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About nine people in this yard have arranged themselves this morning so that a man could be asked a question when he comes up that lane, and I have entered every one of those arrangements, and I am not going to enter the question, because there is no question, and about four of you have been standing in those places for an hour without one of you saying a word to any of the others about what it is for.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that about nine people arranged themselves in that yard and did not say out loud what for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the edge of that second table at about half past eleven and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been at this table every morning for thirty years and I have watched about nine people get ready to say something to a man and not one of them say it, and I am not blaming anybody, and I am telling you that a man who has walked a road every day for about seven months can tell the difference between a road and a road where people are waiting, and I have known the difference since about ten in the morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, and the ink on that page was dry by the time she had finished, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about twelve, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have not asked him anything and I have not been asked to, and I have been standing about nine feet off that lane for an hour and a half, and I have had four different reasons ready and about four of you have watched me have them, and I am not going to say one of them out loud this morning, and that is the whole of what I am going to say about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man stood about nine feet from a place on that lane for about an hour and a half and asked nothing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was at the near end of that second table at about eleven and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A table is a thing you stand at, and about nine of you have been standing at this one all morning in a straight line facing the road, and I built it to be stood at by two men having an argument about a load of hay, and about four of you have used it this morning for neither of those things.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, and with the pen she has had for about four years, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm, and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have not opened that book for three mornings, and a yard that is waiting is not a yard that is doing anything, and about four of you have stood about nine feet off a man for an hour and a half and you could have walked to the water and back twice in that time, and I am not saying that to be unkind, I am saying that nothing in that book this morning would be different if none of you had come up that bank.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, in the ordinary way and with the pen she has had for about four years, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came up that bank at about half past twelve with a basket on her hip and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nine of you are standing in this yard like it is market day and not one of you has a thing to say, and I have washed for eleven houses for nineteen years and I have never once seen nine people wait for a man like this, and I am going to go home now and wash somebody's sheets and I hope the rest of you have something to do this afternoon.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and stopped at that wall for about as long as it takes to say four figures, and about nine people in that yard were standing about nine feet off that lane and nobody in that yard said one word to him, and about four people have said since that this is the first morning in about seven months that a man has stood at that wall longer than it takes, and that about four of them have said that he did it deliberately and that about four of them have said they do not think he did it deliberately, and that nobody has asked him which. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had been sitting on that first table facing that lane since about ten. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nine people stood about nine feet off a man on a lane this morning and not one of them stood near enough to say one word to the man on those stones. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and thirteenth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and twelve of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and twenty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and seventy-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and eighty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and fifty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and fifty-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and ninety-one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and thirty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred and seventeen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning, and a second mark went on the underside of those boards before about nine. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and about nine people arranging themselves in a yard is a thing nine people did and not a fourteenth thing this district has made, and a clerk entered every one of the arrangements without being asked for any of them. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands, and the man who says them stood at that wall at about half past one this afternoon longer than he has ever stood at it while about nine people watched him from about nine feet.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and there is a page in a shop and four in that yard and about nine people in that yard were waiting all morning for a man who was not asked anything, and a clerk has entered that there is no question. The reading of that lot has not moved off the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and a morning about which nothing was asked anybody is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and about nine people said a thing out loud in that yard this morning and none of them said it to a man who came up that lane at about half past one.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and nine people standing about nine feet off a man on a lane is not one of the six things standing in that yard that are not a payment.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full in about nine minutes, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody in that yard, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a yard of about nineteen people spent its whole morning waiting for a man to come up a lane and has not written one word about it anywhere, and that about four of them have said that the four sheets at the end of that table were not looked at by anybody in that yard between about ten and about four. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man stood at that wall longer than he has ever stood at it and about nine people stood about nine feet off him and not one of them said a word, and that about four of them have said that this is the second morning this month on which that wall has been the only thing anybody in that yard could see.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the end of that second table at about half past ten with his right hand flat on the boards and about nine feet from a place on that lane, and at about twelve he said out loud in that yard in front of about nineteen people that he was not going to ask a man anything, and the road keeper came up that lane at about half past one and was not asked about the eleven miles, and a clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and thirteenth night of that run.
