# Chapter 591: The Yard Counted How Many Mornings Were Left In Its Month Of Days And Nobody Had Decided Anything By Nine

The stone on that second table had a skin of water standing in the hollow in the middle of it by about seven on the twenty-eighth of the fourth month, and the boards of that table were dry along the north edge and damp along the south one, and a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had put his own thumb into that hollow at about ten past seven and taken it out and wiped his thumb down his own leg, and about four people standing at the near end of that first table had watched him do it and had not said anything, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Three pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for eighty-nine mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and thirty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and entered that a decision about a person has not been entered in this yard and that she is not entering one this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixteenth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a yard which has decided nothing for sixteen mornings has still got a man reading four numbers off that wall twice a day inside it, and that about four of them have said that is the right way round.

"**The board carries four hundred and eighty-nine, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and five days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and nineteen, and four hundred and eighty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was leaning on the near end of that first table with his hands behind his back when the clerk had finished her morning entry, and he said one thing out loud, in about twenty-seven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in this yard every morning of this month and I have not seen one thing decided in it, and I have set tables for about twenty years and I know what a room looks like when it is waiting for somebody to open their mouth, and this yard has been that room since the twelfth of this month, and it is not going to open on its own.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not entering the twelfth of the month as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen had been standing at the far end of that second table since before the clerk had finished, and he said one thing out loud at about ten past eight, in about seventeen seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got forty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Ten. Counting this morning there are ten mornings left in this month of days, and that is the first number anybody in this yard has counted this month, and about four of you have been counting something else and it has not moved in sixteen days.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered that the count of mornings left in that month of days is ten and that it is not entered as a figure about any page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came up that bank at about half past eight with the flat book shut under his left arm and stopped at the east end of that yard and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-one seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got four things in this book I could not read out in this yard if you paid me, and I told you that a week ago, and the reason I have not opened it in front of you since is that there is now a fifth thing in this parish that nobody has put in a book anywhere, and I am not the man who is going to be the first one to put a person in one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man did not open a book, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about ten with her own door open behind her and a broom in her hand and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-nine seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nine people came into my shop before I had the door open this morning and two of them bought nothing at all, and one of them asked me whether the page on my counter is ours, and I told him I cannot tell, and I have told four of you in this yard the same thing four times this month and I am not going to change the answer for anybody because the month has turned a page.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open behind her, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven and put a rail he had cut the ends off down against the low side of that second table and stood at the near end of it with his right hand flat on the boards, and his left hand was in the cloth it has been in since the eleventh of the June, and he said one thing out loud, in about twenty-nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been counting something since the sixteenth of this month and I have not told anybody in this yard what it is, and I counted it this morning before I came up that bank, and it is a number of days, and about four of you have been waiting sixteen mornings for me to say a number, and I am telling you it is a number of days and I am not telling you the figure.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the three pages, and entered that she is not entering his figure, and entered the reason, which is that he did not say it out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about twenty-nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The fourth of the four figures on that wall is out, and every person in this yard knows it, and I said so out loud in this yard on the twenty-seventh of this month, and I am not going to say it a second time, and I am not going to write it a second time either, and a figure on that wall may not be corrected and it is not mine to correct.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it a second time, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds had been at the low end of that yard since before eight with his bundle of reeds over his shoulder, and he came and stood at the near end of that first table and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-five seconds, to about four people near enough to hear, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in this yard about a month and I have watched a man say one number under his breath every morning and I have never once heard him say the same one twice, and I would like it noticed that a man who counts something in his own head every day for a month has still not told one of us what it is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing and entered the person, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past one and stood at the far end of that second table and read the fourth line of the near page with his forefinger an inch off it, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was in that yard and entered that she is not entering what he made of a number of days, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a boy counted ten mornings this morning in that yard, and that about four of them have said that is the first time in about a month that anybody in that yard has counted forward instead of backward. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the shafts wet, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank without stopping for anybody in that yard on that day, and the near wheel was still dragging when it stopped at the top. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a yard counted ten mornings this morning and that the man on those stones is not one of the ten and was not asked whether he is. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and thirtieth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and twenty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and thirty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and ninety-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and ninety-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and sixty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and seventy-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and forty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and thirty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and two marks went on the underside of those boards before about nine. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a boy counting ten mornings out loud is not a fourteenth thing and did not pay anything, and a clerk entered that out loud at about half past eleven with the reason, which is that a count of mornings left is a thing a person says and not a thing a district makes. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and a clerk has said out loud in that yard that she is not going to say it again, and no figure on that wall was altered this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and eighty.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for eighty-nine mornings while two pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man counting a number of days under his breath did not advance it, and a clerk entered that out loud with the reason, which is that a number a man has not said out loud is not a reading of anything. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, and about nine things were said out loud in it, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a boy counting ten mornings did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full by a woman who said nothing about the weight of them, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past that evening and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a yard counted the end of its own month this morning for the first time in about two volumes, and that about four of them have said the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months is the only person in that parish who has not counted anything today and that about four of them have said that is not a failing. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the one morning of that month when a man in that yard counted forwards, and that about four of them have said that a man who has said four numbers backward three hundred and sixteen times has never once counted what was left.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-eighth of the fourth month about a yard that has not decided one thing in sixteen mornings, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the three pages.
