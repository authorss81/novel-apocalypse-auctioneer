# Chapter 566: A Page In A Shop And A Page In A Yard

The boards of that second table had gone dry on the north side by about half past seven on the third of the fourth month, and the stone lying face up on it had a skin of water across the hollow in the middle of it, and a man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart lifted that skin off with two fingers and set it down again on the boards, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those had been in the shop of the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale before they came up, and about four of them had stood at her counter and looked at a sheet of paper on it and had looked away from it again.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and five days as the age of that figure, and entered the date at the top of her page, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that a page is in a shop this morning and that the shop is up that bank and is not in this yard.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and ninety-first of those mornings, and that a clerk has now written down twice where two pieces of paper are, and has not written down one word about either of them beyond where they are.

"**The board carries four hundred and sixty-four, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and eighty days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and ninety-four, and four hundred and fifty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about ten with her shop open behind her, and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-four seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There have been about nine people at my counter before I opened and two of them stood at it and bought nothing at all, and one of them asked me whether that page on my counter came out of that yard, and I told him I could not say, and he said he had walked four miles to be able to tell people he had asked, and I have got nothing to give him this morning that I did not give you in this yard on the second of the month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open, and entered that about nine people were at her counter before she opened and that two of them bought nothing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that second table at about half past ten and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-eight seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A month and a day ago I carried a page up that bank in my own two hands with the reason said out loud before I touched it, and nobody has asked me one word about it since, and about four of you have worked out by now that I am not waiting, and I am not, and there is a page at the end of this table that came in with nobody's hand on it and it is not my hand and I am not going to keep saying so every morning until one of you believes it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who put a page on that table on the first morning of the month before said out loud this morning that he is not going to say again that a page in that yard is not in his hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about thirty-eight seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is two. It has been two since a quarter to eight and I have looked at that end of that table four times this morning and it is two, and there is one of them up that bank in a shop with a door open, and that makes three in this parish, and about four of you said three when I said the word out loud just now and about four of you said two, and I am going to keep saying two, because two is the number on the table and the other one is not on the table.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is two, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart said one more thing at about half past ten, without stopping where he was, in about twenty seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That copy at the near end of that table has been lying on that wood for sixty-six mornings and it is the only figure in this yard that has not gone up or down once in two whole volumes, and about four of you have never said that out loud and I have.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a copy has been on the end of that second table for sixty-six mornings, and entered that she is not entering that as a figure about the day, and entered the reason, which is that a figure about the day is a different figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about thirty-four seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering that a page is in a shop. I am not entering that shop as a fifth place, and the reason is that a place is a place, and that shop was one of the four this district has counted before any page was put on it, and if I enter it as five I will be entering a page as a place, and a page is not a place, and that is the whole of my reason and it will be the same reason tomorrow.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about eleven with a barrow he had not emptied and stood at the end of that second table and looked at the two sheets lying there for about as long as it takes to say four figures, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man stood at that table and looked at two pages and entered that she is not entering what he made of them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about half past eleven with his right hand flat on the boards, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-seven seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a rail on the low side of that bank that wants five turns and not four, and I did the four yesterday afternoon and I can see the fifth one from here, and I am not doing it in front of about nineteen of you, because a rail that is done in front of people is a rail that gets watched, and I have watched a rail do it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on either of the two sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-eight seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have not opened that book this morning and I am not going to, and there is a number in it that has been the same for a fortnight, and a man with a book under his arm that he does not open in front of about nineteen people is a man telling you the number has not moved, and I would rather tell you that than have you ask me for it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally did not open his own book in that yard this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man has now walked that lane in this district for about seven months and there is a page in a shop in this district and a page on a table in this district and neither one of them has ever come up that lane in his hand. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had lifted a skin of water off that stone with two fingers before eight. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that there is a piece of paper in a shop in this parish and the man on those stones has not been given a cup of water, and that about four of them have said that out loud this afternoon for the first time. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fifth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and four of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and fourteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and sixty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and seventy-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and forty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and forty-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and eighty-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and twenty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred and nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning. Two of the figures on that wall are out, neither of them has been put right, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning of this year so far, and a clerk's reason for not writing the right one beside it has not changed since the day she gave it. The count of things this district has made is still thirteen, and a page in a shop is a page and not a fourteenth of them, and a clerk said that out loud in that yard a fortnight ago and it is as true this morning as it was then.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and one of those two has been on that table for sixty-six mornings while the other arrived in this yard eight days ago, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and a page went up a bank to a shop a month and a day ago and nobody has said out loud in that yard this morning that a shop is not this district. The reading of that lot has not moved off the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and a fourth line is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, so the count of those five is still five and about four people at that first table have said that a page in a shop is not a thing that makes a reading go any further along. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and about nine people said something out loud in that yard this morning and not one of them has a name against it, and a clerk is not entering the name of the man who read a sheet out loud once in this yard in a month.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is still unpaid, and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and there is a page in a shop in this parish this morning and neither of those two things is one of the six things standing in that yard that are not a payment.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full, and about four people at the foot of it have said since that the woman of about fifty-eight came up behind them, read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, and did not look at the two sheets at the end of that second table at all this evening, and that she has not looked at those since the day they came. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this district has now got a piece of paper that is inside its own parish, and that the only man in that yard who says a number out loud every morning has still not been asked what any of them are for.

A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale stood at the near end of that first table with her shop open behind her and a page on her own counter that about nine people had looked at, a man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart lifted a skin of water off that stone with two fingers and set it down again, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is two, one of which has been on that wood for sixty-six mornings, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about half past eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on either of the two sheets.
