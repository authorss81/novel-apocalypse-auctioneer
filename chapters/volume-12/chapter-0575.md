# Chapter 575: Somebody Asked How Much It Costs To Copy A Thing

There was no wind in that yard at all on the twelfth of the fourth month, which was the first still morning in that month, and the boards of that second table were warm by about nine and the four sheets lying along the end of them had lifted at one corner where the night had been dry, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those were standing at the end of that second table and about nine of them had not come up that bank to look at a page.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and fourteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that nobody in that yard has said one word about a page since yesterday.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundredth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said the man who has said four figures off that wall for two volumes said the three hundredth of them this morning and then stood there while about nineteen people did not ask him one thing.

"**The board carries four hundred and seventy-three, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and eighty-nine days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and three, and four hundred and sixty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and opened it for the first time in four mornings at about half past eight, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man opened a book in that yard and entered that she is not entering what was in it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about ten with her shop open behind her, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-four seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have had a page on my counter for a month and a shopkeeper is asked about her scales and her sugar and her oil and about four times a day somebody comes in and asks me about that page instead, and I cannot tell them, and I would like it entered that I have not shut my shop for one morning about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that second table at about half past ten and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-six seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have made one copy of one page in my life and I made it in about a quarter of an hour off the book on that stone, and I have thought about that for a month and I have not once been asked how long it took, and about four of you have been looking at four sheets instead of at the man who made one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering that as a figure about anybody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about ten and stood at the end of that second table and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was in that yard and entered nothing about him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud, at about eleven, in the ordinary voice, in the open, in front of about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**How much does it cost to copy a thing?**"

Nobody answered it for about eleven seconds, and about nine people in that yard have said since that they counted those eleven seconds, and about four of them have said that it was the boy and not a man, and that about four of them have said that this is the first question anybody in that yard has asked out loud in about four months and that nobody answered it.

Then the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud from the east end of that yard, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It costs whatever it costs to find out, and a figure has to be true before anybody can write it down a second time, and the finding out is the whole of the cost, and a man who copies a figure has had to stand in a room with somebody who knew the figure. That is what the third line on that page is, it is the one line anybody cannot copy off anybody, and I have known that since I was about your age.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a figure has to be true before it can be copied, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one more thing out loud from where he was standing, in about twenty-nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**On the twenty-ninth of the month before you said in this yard that you were not going to say the rest of that until somebody else said the first half to you, and nobody has said the first half, and you have stood here and asked it anyway, and I want you to say out loud in front of these people that you asked it in spite of what you said you would do.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, with the pen she has had for about four years, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud in answer to that, in about sixteen seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got forty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Nobody has said the first half, so I have said the first half and the second half together and they are the same question, and I am not going to stand here and pretend to about nineteen of you that it is not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one more thing out loud at about a quarter past eleven, in about thirty-two seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Then a copy of our book would have a date in the third of it, because the third of it is a date, and there is not one of these four with anything in the third, and somebody has had our book in front of them and taken the shape of it and left out the one line that is about anybody and copied the nine words that say the line above is a person, and that is not a forgery, that is a form.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a copy of that book would carry a date in its third line, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy asked a question in that yard at about eleven and entered that nobody answered it for about eleven seconds, and entered that the reason those nine words are on the fourth line of that book is the reason they are on the fourth line of the four pages at the end of the other table, and it is the same reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about twenty to twelve with his right hand flat on the boards about nine feet from a man standing at the end of it, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-one seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have stood about nine feet off that man for a month with a question ready every morning and I am not going to ask it, and I want it entered that I did not ask it this morning either, and that the man in this yard who worked it out is not me.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on that table and not on the stone and not on the four sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud from the edge of that second table at about twelve, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a man in this yard about nine feet from that table who is the third line of that book, and he has been in this yard nine times in a month and read that fourth line every one of them, and about four of us have been waiting a month for somebody to say that out loud, and I am not asking him anything and nobody else is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man named nobody and asked nobody, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard asked him anything, and a man of about thirty-one who digs loam stood at the end of that second table until about a quarter past twelve and then went back down that bank, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man stood in that yard and left and entered that she is not entering whether he heard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a boy asked a question in that yard this morning that nobody answered for about eleven seconds and that the man who answered it has kept a book for about thirty years. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had said out loud at about half past ten that he made one copy off that book in a quarter of an hour. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a yard worked out this morning that a page costs nothing and a man costs something, and that the two have never been put on one page in this district. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fourteenth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and thirteen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and twenty-three days is how long the bid has been open, it was not run today, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or on a page. A hundred and seventy-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth says he is not named here. A hundred and eighty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and fifty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and fifty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and ninety-two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody watched.

A hundred and thirty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred and eighteen marks in chalk along that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a question asked in that yard at about eleven is a thing a boy said and not a fourteenth. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands, and the man who has said one of those four for three hundred mornings was not asked this morning what it is for.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and a clerk has entered that a page with four lines on it and nothing in the third is a form and not a forgery, and that the object of that yard's morning changed at about eleven from a page to a person, and nobody was asked anything. The reading of that lot has not moved off the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and a form is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk is not entering the name of the boy who asked that question and is not ruling a column for the man who answered it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who said out loud that a figure costs somebody something is not one of the six things standing in that yard that are not a payment.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full and were set down inside that gate, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about nine people in that yard this morning were talking about what the third line on that book costs. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a boy asked a question in that yard that nobody had asked before.

A boy of about nineteen asked a question in that yard at about eleven on the twelfth of the fourth month, in the open, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, and nobody answered it for about eleven seconds, and a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally answered it out loud from the east end of that yard and said that a figure has to be true before it is written down a second time, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone or the sheets.
