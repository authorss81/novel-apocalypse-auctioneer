# Chapter 587: Six Pages Went Down The Boards In Front Of About Nineteen People And About Nine Of Them Were Described And Not Read

The boards of that second table had been swept twice by about seven on the twenty-fourth of the fourth month, and the sweepings were gone over the low side of that bank into the wet grass, and a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up was standing at that table with a straight edge in his hands when the woman who keeps a scale came down the bank with two pages under one arm. Four pages were lying along the end of that table and by about ten there were six, and the copy at the near end of the end of them has now been on that wood for eighty-five mornings, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and twenty-six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four at about a quarter to eight this morning, and entered that a woman came down that bank before eight with two pages under one arm, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and twelfth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that two pages came down that bank this morning and that this is the first time anything has come down that bank in about a fortnight.

"**The board carries four hundred and eighty-five, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and one days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and fifteen, and four hundred and seventy-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank with two pages under one arm and stood at the end of the second table and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-two seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am putting these two on that table and I am saying why first, and the reason is that about nine of you have been talking for a week about comparing them and there is one way to compare two of them and it is to have them on the same wood, and my shop is open and it is not going to be a shop nobody can get into after this morning, and I will take them up at about one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the person and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

She put the two pages down at the end of that table and squared all six of them up against a straight edge and nobody in that yard touched one of them at any point in the morning, and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up held the straight edge and then took his hands away and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-five seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have set tables up for about twenty years and I have never once in my life put my hand on a thing to square it, and I have just done six of them with a straight edge, and about four of you have noticed, and I would like it entered that a man can be told not to touch something and still be perfectly useful.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the person, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then about nine people stood along the two long sides of that table and did the thing that about four of them had been proposing for a week, and nobody read one word out of one of the six pages, and what happened instead was that about nine people described in their own words what was on each of them while a clerk of nineteen years wrote it down in a column, and about four of them has said since that a yard comparing six pages by talking about them for an hour is a slower kind of looking than picking one up.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about twenty-eight seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Six. Four lines on every one of them. Nothing in the third of any of them. The same nine words on the fourth of every one of them and not one word different. And about four different hands in the fourth line, which is the only thing about these six that is not the same, and I have got a figure for that, and it is four, and the four hands are not four people.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a comparison of six pages was entered, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came and stood at the top end of that table with the flat book open under one arm and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-six seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is the same as what a man said in this yard on the twentieth of this month, and he got the same answer about four of us gave him about nine days ago, and there is not one of those six pages that has been touched by anybody who could answer for it, and about four of you have just spent a morning proving that the six are alike, and the being alike is not the finding. The finding is that they are alike and there is nobody here to stand beside them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he is not entering the comparison as a finding, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about eleven and stood at the far end of that second table with the six pages on it and looked down the two long sides of it and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was in that yard and entered that she is not entering what he made of six pages on one board, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about twelve with his right hand flat on the boards on the end where the six pages were not and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-four seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been at this end of this table about a month with a hand flat on it about nine feet off a man and I have just spent a morning looking at six pages with a straight edge on them, and I am not saying that is a waste. I am saying that about four of you now know as much about those six pages as anybody in a shop two hundred miles off, and that is a new thing, and I do not know what to do with it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the six sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about one, in about thirty-one seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A comparison is not a test. This district's own test is a man standing beside a page and saying something about himself, and that test has not been run on any of those six this morning and cannot be run on any of them today, and I have entered the comparison because about nine people asked me to and because a thing that has been written down can be looked at again on a morning when somebody has more to say.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, reading it back to herself before she wrote it, that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a comparison is not a test, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about one the woman who keeps a scale took the two pages up off that table in her own two hands and carried them back up that bank to her own counter, and a clerk entered that a woman took two pages up that bank in the open and entered the person and entered the reason, which is that she had said at about eight that she would take them up at about one, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is four. At about four a man of about forty who is not from this parish came up that bank, picked his own page up off the end of that second table, and carried it back down that lane with it in his coat, and a clerk entered that a man took a page off the end of that second table in the open and entered the person and entered the reason, which is that about nine people in that yard had said out loud this morning that his page is the only one of the six that has never been beside the other five, and that he would rather take it home than leave it in a yard, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is three.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that two pages came down that bank this morning and went back up it at about one and that this is the first time in about a fortnight that anything in that parish has gone back up that bank with a person holding it. The cart came up behind the barrow, with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stood there. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nineteen people stood round a table in that yard this morning comparing six pages and that the man on those stones has not been near that table. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and twenty-sixth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and twenty-five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and thirty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and ninety days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and ninety-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and sixty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and sixty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and forty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and thirty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and three marks went on the underside of those boards before about eleven. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a comparison is not a thing made and a sheet that comes up a lane and goes back down it is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered that out loud at about one with the reason, which is that the count is of things this district made and not of things that passed through it. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands where it has stood.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is three and it was six at about ten, and it went to four at about one and to three at about four, and both times the reason was on the page because a person said it in the open before a hand went on anything. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for eighty-five mornings. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and six pages on one board did not advance it, and a clerk entered that out loud with the reason, which is that a comparison of six things that are alike is not a reading of any one of them. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today. About nine people said things out loud in it and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and about nine people in that yard spent a morning looking at six pages instead, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past that evening and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about nine people in that yard have now got the same description of six pages that anybody could get by looking at them, and that about four of them have said the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months has a better description of one thing than any of the nine. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that the comparison was entered and that an entry is not a test and that about four of them have said a yard has now got a page that says all six are the same and that page is worth nothing to anybody outside that yard.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-fourth of the fourth month about four lines on six pages and nothing in the third of any of them and four different hands, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about twelve with his right hand flat on the boards on the end where the six pages had been.
