# Chapter 567: A Fifth One And It Was Not A Place

There was a smell of wet chalk off the boards of that second table by about half past seven on the fourth of the fourth month, and the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds had his stub in his hand and was not at that table, and about four people in that yard said out loud that that was the first morning in a month he had not been at that end of it before eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about four of those had come up that bank with a question they had not asked anybody yet.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and six days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and ninety-second of those mornings, and that about four of the marks in chalk along the edge of that second table have been put there by a man who was not in that yard this morning, and that nobody has worked out yet what that means and about four of them have stopped saying out loud that he is never late.

"**The board carries four hundred and sixty-five, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and eighty-one days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and ninety-five, and four hundred and fifty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past eight, in about forty seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man came up that lane yesterday at about four in the afternoon with a bit of paper in his hand with three lines on it and the third of them ruled and nothing on it, and he asked me whether it came out of this yard, and I said it did not, because I have never been out of this parish, and he said it looks the same as the one he has got at home, and then he went on down the lane, and he did not say one word about where he got it and I did not ask him.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man carried a page up that lane on the afternoon of the day before, and entered that the boy did not know the man and is not entering a description, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four people at the near end of that first table went down that bank to the gate and looked up the lane, and about four of them came back without saying what any of them had seen.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud after that, in about thirty-three seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering that page and I am not entering it as a place, and the reason is that a place is a place you can walk back to on any morning of a week, and that man's hands are not a place and neither is a lane, and if I put it in as a fifth then the four we have counted stop being the four we have counted and I will have made a number up this morning that nothing in this yard can check.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm, and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-eight seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A place is a place you can go back to and I have been to all four of them, and a man carrying a thing away from here down a lane is not a fifth of anything, and there is a list in this district of the places where those lines can be read and what nobody did at them, and a thing that is not a place does not go on that list, and if it did then somebody would be able to say one day that five places went untried when what happened is that one page left.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally has been to all four of the places in this district where those three lines can be read, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about ten with her shop open behind her, and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-five seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He came into my shop yesterday morning before I had the shutter up and he asked me whether anybody in that yard had made the page that is on my counter, and I told him I did not know, and he did not tell me where his came from and I did not ask him, and about nine people have been in that shop since and I have said the same thing to about four of them and I am not going to say anything else about the man.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man came into that shop on the morning before this one, and entered that she is not entering what the man said or did not say, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came up that bank at about half past ten with a basket on her hip, set it down on the boards of that second table because the step into that yard is two inches higher than the bank, and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-five seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He asked me the same thing on his way down and I told him that page is not off any of the eleven houses I wash for, and that is the whole of what I am going to say about it, and I am saying it now before anybody asks me and I am saying it once, and about four of you have been waiting a fortnight for me to be the one who says it and I would rather be the one who says it than the one who does not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman said a thing before she was asked and entered the reason, which is that she would rather say it than not say it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that second table at about eleven and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-two seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in this yard every morning since the first morning of the year before this one and I have watched that lane go past four hundred times and I have never once seen a man come up it with paper in his hand and stop at that table, and I have not said that out loud before this morning and about four of you have never asked me and I am not blaming anybody, I am just saying the shape of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has watched that lane go past four hundred times and entered that she is not entering that as a figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven and stood at the end of that second table with his back to the sheets and looked at the first table instead, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man stood in that yard and did not look at the pages and entered nothing about why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about half past eleven with his right hand flat on the boards, and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-two seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put two posts in at the end of last week and the ground on the low side is stone under about nine inches and everything under that is whatever this valley is made of, and I have been in that yard every morning for two volumes telling about nineteen people about a rail and a post, and this morning is the first one I have had nothing to say when I got here, and I am not going to invent something.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man had nothing to say and did not invent anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.


At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man walked up that lane yesterday with a page in his hand and about four of the people in this yard were standing in it at the time. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up the bank without stopping, and had said out loud at about eleven that he has watched that lane go past four hundred times and never seen a man stop at that table. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a page went out of this parish yesterday afternoon in about four hundred paces and nothing has gone out of it tonight. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and sixth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and five of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and fifteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and seventy days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and seventy-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and forty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and forty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and eighty-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and twenty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred and ten marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning, and the man who puts those marks in chalk was not in that yard before about nine for the first time in a month, and nobody has entered a reason and nobody has asked him for one. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and a page carried down a lane is a page and not a fourteenth, and a clerk entered that on the twenty-fifth of the month before and it holds this morning as well. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and no correct figure has been set beside either of them, and the figure on the wall is the one that stands this morning as it has stood every morning.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and there is a page in a shop up that bank and a page that went down that lane yesterday which is neither of those two things and has not been entered as a third kind, and a clerk gave that reason out loud in that yard in front of about nineteen people this morning. The reading of that lot has not moved and is still the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and a page which has left this parish has not told anybody anything about what a person is, and a fifth line is not the fifth thing and did not pay it. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and about nine people said a thing out loud in that yard this morning and a clerk is not entering the name of the man who carried a page out of this parish yesterday, and there is no column for where a page went either. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who walked four hundred paces down a lane with a piece of paper in his hand is not one of the six things standing in that yard that are not a payment.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were brought back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that book standing up on her way past, said nothing to anybody, and went on, and nobody there said one word to her, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she has now gone past that book for a fortnight without once stopping at the fourth line, and that about four of them have said that a woman who stopped on one morning in this month and has not stopped since is a woman who has finished saying what she came to say. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and stood there afterwards without moving, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man carried a page out of this parish yesterday who had said a page is not in his hand three times in public, and that about four of them have said that is the first time anybody in this district has been the one carrying a page away.

A boy of about nineteen said in that yard this morning that a man carried a page out of this parish yesterday and did not say where he got it, a clerk of nineteen years entered that the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move and gave her reason out loud, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is two.
