# Chapter 576: Nobody In That Yard Looked At A Page This Morning

The four sheets were still lying along the end of that second table at about a quarter to eight on the thirteenth of the fourth month and nobody in that yard went near them before about ten, and about four people who had come up that bank early stood at the near end of that first table instead and looked at each other, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those were standing in a loose half circle with their backs to the second table.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and fifteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that nobody in that yard has looked at a page since about a quarter to nine the day before.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and first of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that on the first morning after a yard worked out a thing, the yard did not work out a second thing, and that about four of them have said that the man who says those four was not asked about the one it worked out.

"**The board carries four hundred and seventy-four, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and ninety days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and four, and four hundred and sixty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book open under his left arm, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-seven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**For a month this yard has been asking who made four sheets of paper, and yesterday morning a boy asked a different question and a man answered it out loud, and the question after that is not who made it, it is who could answer one, and I have got a book, and he is not in it, and that is the whole of what I have come to say.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man says the question in that yard is no longer who made a page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about thirty-two seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering that the question in this yard changed at about eleven yesterday and I am entering the change and not entering a finding, and the reason is that a finding is a thing a person can check tomorrow morning and I have got four sheets of paper that a man can fill in and I have not got a person, and one of those two things can be written down and the other one cannot and I am not going to try.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is entering a change and not a finding, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard had asked what it costs to change the question in it, and the clerk had not said that out loud either, and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally shut his flat book at about that moment and put it back under his arm with the strap done over it, and about four people at the east end of that yard looked at the strap and then at the top of that bank.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about ten, in about twenty-nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A form is a thing anybody can fill in and a person is a thing you have to go and get, and this yard has spent a month looking at a form, and it turns out that the form is the easy half of it, and about four of you have worked out that I know what the hard half is and I am nineteen and I wash for my aunt on the fourth day of a week.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about half past ten with her shop open behind her, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-nine seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Then somebody in this yard is going to have to decide whether a person goes on a page, and I have been standing in my own shop for a month with a page on my counter saying I cannot tell, and a woman who weighs flour for a living can tell you the price of a thing and I have been waiting all month for somebody to work that out for a page.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a decision about a person on a page has not been made, and entered that she is not entering which way it will go, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

That shop stood open all morning and its door was not shut once, and about four of the people who came in and looked at her counter went back out again without buying anything, and she let them go, and the boy of about nineteen watched them go out and did not say a number.

The man of about fifty-six said the four off that wall for the second time that morning at about eleven, in the ordinary voice, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and first morning and that a clerk has entered that nobody in that yard has looked at a page since yesterday and that a man has now read the same four figures twice in one morning for the first time in about two volumes, and that about four of them have said that a man who reads four figures twice in a morning when nothing has changed is a man who has noticed something, and that about four of them have said that nobody has asked him what he noticed, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said four figures out loud twice and entered that nobody in that yard asked him anything and entered that she is not entering that as a question.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven and stood at the end of that second table with his back to the four sheets lying along the end of it, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man stood with his back to four pages in that yard this morning and entered nothing about it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about twelve with his right hand flat on the boards and about nine feet from a man standing at the other end of it, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-four seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A yard that has decided it needs a man is a yard that has to go and stand in front of one, and I have got about nine feet of bank between me and that this morning and I have had that distance for a month, and about four of you have been watching me have it and about four of you have not.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, with the pen she has had for about four years, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the four sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that second table at about twelve and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-seven seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I said in this yard three days ago that I sleep in nine places and none of them is in this parish, and about four of you have been carrying that around since, and I said it because I was tired, and if a person is what this yard is after then I would like it entered that I am not standing here offering myself up like a sack of flour.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man is not offering himself as a thing this district can put on a page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a yard that has decided it needs a person has still not asked the one person it has been looking at for a month, and that about four of them have said the man who comes up that lane twice a day has not been asked either and that is twice now in one day. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had said out loud at about twelve that he is not a sack of flour. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that this district has spent a fortnight deciding that a person is the only thing a page cannot carry, and that the man on those stones is a person, and that about four of them have said that out loud this afternoon for the first time. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and fifteenth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and fourteen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and twenty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and seventy-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and eighty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and fifty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and fifty-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and ninety-three days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and thirty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred and nineteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and a fifth mark went on the underside of those boards before about nine. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man is not a fourteenth thing this district has made. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands, and a man who has said one of those four for three hundred and one mornings read it twice in one morning and was asked nothing.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and a clerk has entered that nobody in that yard looked at a page before about ten, and the reading of that lot has not moved off the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and a decision about a person is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a man said four figures out loud twice in that yard this morning and a clerk entered that he was asked nothing and entered that she is not entering that as a question.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a decision about putting a person on a page is not one of the six things standing in that yard that are not a payment.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full and were stood against that first table, and the woman of about fifty-eight stopped and read three lines in that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a yard which decided yesterday morning that it needs a man spent today with its back to four pages. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and first morning and that about four of them have said this district has a man on a wall and a man at a wall and a man on some stones and has not asked one of them.

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said in that yard this morning that the question is no longer who made a page and that the man who could answer one is not in his book, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about twelve with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the four sheets.
