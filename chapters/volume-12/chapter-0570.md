# Chapter 570: She Asked To Have An Entry Read Out Loud And Was Refused

The boards of that second table were dry all the way across by about eight on the seventh of the fourth month and the stone lying face up on it had a ring of hard water at the edge of the hollow, and the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at that table at his usual hour and put his mark in chalk and said nothing, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those had been in the shop of the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale before they came up and had bought something this time.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that she is not going to enter anything else this morning until somebody asks her for it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and ninety-fifth of those mornings, and that he said them both times this morning in the ordinary voice while about nine people in that yard were talking about an entry on a page and not about a single one of his four.

"**The board carries four hundred and sixty-eight, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and eighty-four days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and ninety-eight, and four hundred and fifty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came up that bank at about nine with a basket on her hip, set it down on the boards of that second table because the step into that yard is two inches higher than the bank, and said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Read out loud what you wrote about me on the twenty-eighth of the month before. I have thought about it for nine mornings and I have not slept properly since and I want to hear a clerk say it in the open in front of these people and not find it on a page at a quarter to eight in the morning when I am not standing here.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman asked for an entry to be read out loud in that yard, and entered the thing that was asked for, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**No. I am not going to, and I am going to give you the reason out loud because you asked me in front of about nineteen of them and you are entitled to one. A thing that gets read out loud in this yard gets a name put against it, and I am not going to put your name against anything, and I wrote it, and it is mine, and it does not turn into a different thing because somebody else says it with their mouth instead of mine.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she refused to read an entry out loud, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing said one more thing out loud from where she was standing, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is the second time in a month somebody has said no to me in that yard and both times it was a woman with a pen in her hand, and I am not going to ask a third time, and about four of you have worked out by now that when I say I am not going to ask again I have already asked in my head about nine times.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering how many times a person has asked in her own head, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that second table at about half past nine and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About four of you have never once heard what that entry says and every one of you knows what it says, and that is the thing about an entry nobody will read out loud, and about four of you have been carrying it around in your heads for nine mornings and it has not once stopped you doing anything.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about ten, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not counting that. If I count refusals then somebody will ask me how many there have been and there is no figure on any page in this district for how many questions anybody has ever been asked him, and I have got a figure for how many mornings a man has said four things off a wall and I have got that one and I am not giving it up for a number about somebody saying no.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy is not keeping a count of refusals, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who puts tables up was at the end of that second table at about half past ten and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put a table up in eleven minutes in this yard in a month of a year I have not counted, and a table is a table, and what sits on it is not my business, and I said that in this yard once already and I am saying it again because about four of you have spent nine mornings on a page and there is still a table underneath it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up that bank at about eleven with his barrow and stood at the end of that second table and looked along the three sheets lying there and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man looked at three pages in that yard and entered that nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about half past eleven with his right hand flat on the boards, and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have got three rails to do on the high side of that bank and one post that wants a foot deeper, and I will be at the water by the middle of the afternoon, and I came up here first because this is where I stand, and that is all the reason there is and I have never had a better one in two volumes.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the three sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a woman in that yard was refused a thing this morning in the open by another woman, and that the man who comes up that lane has been asked nothing out loud in this district in about seven months except once by a man who mends fencing, and that about four of them have said that the two of those facts are on the same wall. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had said out loud at about half past nine that every person in that yard is carrying an entry about a page around in their head. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a woman asked to be read out loud in that yard this morning and a man on those stones has not been asked to be read out loud anything in about two volumes. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and ninth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Two hundred and eighteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and seventy-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and seventy-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and forty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and fifty-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and eighty-seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and twenty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred and thirteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a refusal is not a thing and is not a fifteenth anything, and a boy said out loud in that yard this morning that he is not keeping a count of them and gave the reason, which is that there is no figure on any page in this district for how many times anybody has been asked anything. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been put right, and the figure on the wall won this morning while about nine people in that yard were talking about a page instead of a wall.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and one entry on that first page is nine mornings old and has not been read out loud once and will not be this morning, and about four people at that first table have said that a refusal to read is a thing a yard does and not a thing a page does. The reading of that lot has not moved and is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and a clerk refusing to read her own entry out loud is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today except the nine words of a refusal, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and about nine people said a thing out loud in that yard this morning and one woman was refused and neither of those has a name against it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a woman carrying an entry about a page in her head for nine mornings is not one of the six things standing in that yard that are not a payment.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were brought back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines of that lot book standing up and said nothing to anybody and did not stop, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she has not stopped at the fourth line for a fortnight and that about four of them have said that the only person in that yard who has read that fourth line properly is a woman who read it standing up and went on her way. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that there are two hundred and ninety-five mornings on that figure and not one of them has had anybody in that yard ask him what it is for, and that about four of them have said that is the only figure in that yard that has never once been argued about.

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel asked a clerk of nineteen years in that yard this morning to read out loud what she had entered about her on the twenty-eighth of the month before, and the clerk refused out loud in front of about nineteen people and gave the reason, and the woman did not ask a second time, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about half past eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the three sheets.
