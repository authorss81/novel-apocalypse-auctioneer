# Chapter 585: Somebody Named The Fifth Of The Five Out Loud And Then Nobody Could Say Which Man It Meant

The sun came out on the twenty-second of the fourth month about half past seven and put a hard edge along the top rail of that low wall that had not been there the day before, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone straight to the end of the second table without stopping at the first one, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Four pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for eighty-three mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and twenty-four days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that a clerk has entered the fifth of the five things this district does not have on every morning of this month and that it has not been paid on any of them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and tenth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that the sun came over that wall this morning for the first time in a fortnight and that nobody in that yard looked at it.

"**The board carries four hundred and eighty-three, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and ninety-nine days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and thirteen, and four hundred and seventy-four days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart had the cart standing at the top of that bank with the shafts down and came and leaned on the end of that second table with his back to the yard and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-five seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and it gets said in that yard every morning, and I have listened to it about a month, and this morning I have worked out that nobody in that yard can tell me which man it means. About four of you have been assuming it means the man at the end of this table. He digs loam. He goes home to a house. It might not be him at all.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man named the fifth of the five things this district does not have, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard answered him, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man stood with his back to about nineteen people and asked a question that was not the question, and that about four of them have said that the reason he put his back to the yard was so that he would not have to see whether anybody answered.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten, in about twenty-five seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to say which man he means either, and the reason is that I have had a book for nineteen years and I have entered every fifth thing in this district that anybody named and I have never once entered who a fifth thing was for, because a thing that does not have a person in it is the only kind that stays true.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, standing up, that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the reason, and the reason again, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about half past ten and put a rail he had cut the ends off down against the side of the second table and stood down on the low side of that bank so that he was below the top of it for the first time in a month and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-five seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been standing at the top of this bank every morning for a month telling myself the ground was too high to speak to a man on, and this morning I have come down off it, and I have still not said anything, and I would like whoever is keeping a tally of this written down that the ground made no difference.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man was standing below the top of that bank and not on it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had been in the yard since before eight and had not gone near the end of that second table and said one thing out loud from about four feet off the middle of it, in about twenty-five seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About four of us have been saying for a week that the fifth thing is a payment and about four of us have been saying it in the voice a man uses when he means something else, and this morning I have worked out that nobody here can pay anybody, because nobody in that yard has a wage or a price or anything anybody else would take.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man has not been paid and is not asking, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was standing in her own doorway with the door open and a basket of eggs on her hip and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-three seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got sixty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have a way of paying about nine people in this parish and it is the only way of paying anybody I have got, and it does not pay a man who digs loam, and it did not pay me for the first two years I had that shop, and I am not saying that to make anybody feel better about anything.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, and read it back to herself before she wrote a word of it, that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the shop has been open every morning of this month through all of it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about one, in about twenty-seven seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is four on that table and four in that shop and four in her book and the fifth of the five gets said every morning, and I have got a number for the first two and not for the other, and about four of you have said out loud this month that a figure you cannot write down is not the same as a thing that is not there.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past one and looked at four pages lying along the end of that second table and then at the top of the bank on the other side of it where a man was standing in the sun, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was in that yard and entered that she is not entering that he looked up the bank, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man asked that yard this morning which man the fifth of the five is for, and that about four of them have said the sun came over that wall and made that question the first thing anybody has said there that a person could have answered. The cart came up with the near wheel still dragging and the shafts wet, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop and nobody in that yard asked him to. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a yard has spent a morning working out which man its fifth thing is for, and that the man on those stones is paid nothing and is owed nothing and has not been entered in any of it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and twenty-fourth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and twenty-three of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and thirty-three days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and eighty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and ninety-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and sixty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and sixty-six days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and two days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and forty-two marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and twenty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and naming the fifth of the five out loud for the fifth time this month is not a fourteenth thing and did not pay it. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them was looked at this morning, and the figure on the wall stands.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for eighty-three mornings while two pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five and did not move, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man asking which man the fifth thing is for did not advance it, and a clerk entered that out loud at about ten with the reason, which is that the reading of a lot does not move because somebody asked a question about it. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, and this is the fifth morning of that month on which that is what happened. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and a woman was asked about about four of them this week and it did not help.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, and the fifth of them was named out loud in that yard this morning by a man with his back to about nineteen people, and it was not paid, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read those three lines standing up on her way past that evening and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man asked that yard this morning which man its fifth thing is for and got no answer, and that about four of them have said that the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months is paid in eggs and is not the fifth thing either. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that the sun came over that wall this morning and put a man in it for the first time in a month, and that about four of them have said the man in the sun did not speak to anybody and that about four of them have said that was the point of the sun.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-second of the fourth month about which man the fifth of the five things this district does not have is for, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was standing below the top of that bank with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the four sheets.
