# Chapter 583: A Man Told Nineteen People Exactly What Would Have To Go On A Page And Did Not Ask One Of Them

The wind came off that bank in gusts on the twentieth of the fourth month and took the leaves of the two elms at the top of it in and out of the yard in a way that had about nine people looking up at the trees instead of at the end of the second table for most of the morning, and the copy at the near end of the three sheets lying along the end of that table has now been on that wood for eighty-one mornings. About four people had come up that bank before eight, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and twenty-two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that a board came down out of that yard yesterday afternoon and that nobody is entering the words that were on it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and eighth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man stood at the end of the second table for about an hour yesterday looking at a man at the other end of it and said nothing about it.

"**The board carries four hundred and eighty-one, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and ninety-seven days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and eleven, and four hundred and seventy-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came up that bank with the flat book open in his hand instead of under his arm and put his own hand flat on the boards of the second table to hold it down against the wind and said one thing out loud, in about forty seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Here is what this district would have to put on a page, and I am saying it out loud because about four of you have been guessing at it for a week and the guessing is worse than the thing. It is not a page about a page. It is three things. A third line that is a date. A fourth line saying the line above is a person. And then an instruction to a stranger holding that sheet, telling him who to ask and how he might get to him, and the instruction is not the answer to anything, it is the road to asking him.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man held a book down against the wind with his own hand, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard said anything, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that a man stood about eleven feet off a man at the end of the second table and told about nineteen people what this district would have to write down, and did not turn round at any point while he was saying it, and that about four of them have said that the not turning round was worse than the words.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about twenty-five seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is three things and we have got two of them, and the one we have not got is the only one of the three that costs anybody anything, and about four of you worked that out before I had finished the sentence, and I am not going to be the one who says it out loud because it is not mine to say.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy said he is not going to say a thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about ten and stood at the far end of that second table, and about nineteen people in that yard had been told in the last half hour what this district would have to put on a page, and nobody in that yard looked at him while it was said, and nobody asked him anything.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came up out of her own shop door with the door still open behind her and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-two seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have a page on my own counter with a road on it now, if what he has just said is what this district is going to do, and a road to a man is the one thing I have been able to give about four people this month who wanted to know whether a sheet in their hand came from this parish, and I have been giving them nothing, and I would like it entered that I have been giving them nothing for a month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open behind her and had been open all morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and about nine feet from a man who was leaning on the far end of it with both forearms and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-two seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About four of us have known what this yard is going to have to do since the twelfth of this month and about four of us have not said it in a yard, and the reason I am not saying anything about that is that there is a man in this yard about nine feet from me and he has not been asked whether he agrees to any of it, and I am not going to be the reason he hears about it from a clerk.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the three sheets and that the wind had taken the pages about an inch, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about thirty-five seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not writing any of what he has said on that book and I am not entering it as a line, and the reason is that it is not a line and it is not a fifth line, and the fifth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say is a remedy, and a remedy is what happens when that man says no, and nobody has asked him, so there is no remedy, and a road to a man is not a remedy.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not writing it, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man told that yard this morning what it would have to put on a page, and that about four of them have said the man the third line is about was standing nine feet away for the whole of it. The cart came up in a shower that had stopped halfway up that bank, with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank without stopping for anybody in that yard on that day, and nobody in that yard spoke to him and he spoke to nobody. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a road to a man was described in that yard this morning out loud and that the man it goes to is not that man on those stones and has not been asked. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and twenty-second night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and twenty-one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and thirty-one days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and eighty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and ninety-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and sixty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and sixty-four days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and forty marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and twenty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and three things described out loud are not three things made, and a clerk entered that out loud at about half past eleven with the reason, which is that a thing has to exist before it can be made twice. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and this morning neither of them was corrected, and the wall stands.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for eighty-one mornings while two pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five and did not move, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a road to a man is not the fifth thing and did not pay it. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, and about nine people said things out loud in it. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and nobody in that yard has offered her a name to put in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man describing out loud what this district would have to write down has not paid it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full by a man who said nothing about the weight of them, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man told that yard this morning what it would have to put on a page and a clerk told it the same morning that she is not writing it, and that about four of them have said those two are the same thing said twice. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this district has now worked out what it would have to put on a page and has not asked the man whose line the third one is whether he would let it, and that about four of them have said that is the correct order and that about four of them have said they do not know that it is.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twentieth of the fourth month about what that district would have to put on a page, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and about nine feet off a man who had been at the far end of it since ten.
