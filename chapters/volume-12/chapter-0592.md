# Chapter 592: A Woman Who Keeps A Scale Said Out Loud In Front Of Nineteen People That The Man The Third Line Is About Is The Only Person In That Yard Nobody Has Ever Promised Anything To

The light was low enough on that bank by about eight on the twenty-ninth of the fourth month to come in under the far edge of the boards of that second table and lie on the stones, and the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale had come up that bank with her own broom in her hand and swept her own two feet of boards and nobody else's, and about four people in that yard had noticed which two feet and had not said anything about it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Three pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for ninety mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and thirty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that a woman swept two feet of that table and not the rest of it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventeenth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a woman swept two feet of that table this morning and that is a sentence anybody could read.

"**The board carries four hundred and ninety, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and six days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and twenty, and four hundred and eighty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at the low end of that yard with his binder open, and about nine people were standing about four feet off the far end of that second table, and the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eight, in about thirty-five seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A number of mornings left in a month of days went into that yard yesterday and a man put a number of days in his own head and told the yard he had one and would not say it, and I am not entering either figure, and I am saying out loud this morning that the reason I am not is that a figure about how long a yard has been waiting is not a figure about a person, and about four of you have been treating it as one since yesterday.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale put her broom against the side of that first table and came round the near end of it and stood where about nine people could see her and said one thing out loud, in about forty-four seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and fourteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been in that shop nineteen years and I have never in my life promised a customer anything about a piece of paper, because I have never known, and there is one man in this yard I have never once promised anything to about anything at all, and he is standing at the far end of that table with his finger an inch off the fourth line of a page, and that is not because he is difficult. That is because about nine of you have been wanting him to say something for a month and he has not done it, and you have not gone to him and asked him for it.**"

Nobody in that yard said anything, and about four people at the near end of that first table have said since that a woman said out loud in front of all of them that a man at the far end of that table had never been asked for anything by anybody, and that those two halves of it are the same sentence and it took a woman saying it to make the second one.

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the person it was about, and entered that it is not a name, and entered that she is not entering it as a figure about a man, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table with his right hand flat on the boards when she said it and he did not turn round, and about four people who could see his face have said since that his right hand did not move on the wood while she was saying it, and that a hand that does not move on a board is a hand that is listening.

Then the boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about ten, in about thirty-three seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Four people have said out loud in that yard this month that the man who mends fencing had a choice about it every morning, and about four of you have said he should have gone and asked, and the man at the end of that table has been standing nine feet off a man for a month waiting for one of you to go, and none of you have, and the reason is that none of you have anything to lose by the answer and he has.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first and said it out loud after he had counted it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book open in his hand for the first time in nine mornings and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-three seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have said out loud in this yard that I am not able to ask that man anything, and I said it twice, and about four of you looked at me when I said it and about four of you did not, and I want to say the second part of it this morning before anybody decides what I meant. I said it in front of about nineteen people because I wanted them all to know at once. That is not the same as not being able to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man corrected a thing he had said out loud in that yard, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about ten and stood at the far end of that second table, and at about half past ten he put his own hand flat on the boards on the near side of the pages and left it there, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man put his own hand on that table and entered that she is not entering what he was doing with it there, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about forty-three seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and ten and read the number back to himself in a low voice.
"**A woman has said out loud in this yard this morning that a man at the end of that table has never been promised anything, and about nine of you are standing four feet off him, and I am entering all of it, and I am entering that I am not entering a word of it as a reason for anybody to do anything today, and the reason is that a thing said about a man is not the same as a question put to him and I have watched about nine of those get said in this yard in a month and none of them turned into a question.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering it as a reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud at about twelve from the near end of that second table, in about thirty-two seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**She is right and I have known she was right for about nine days, and I came up this bank this morning to say one thing out loud and it is not about her, and about four of you are going to be disappointed, and I am going to say it anyway, and I am not saying it to him today, and I would like it entered that I said out loud in this yard that I am not saying it to him today.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said he is not saying it to a man, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the three pages, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a woman said out loud in that yard that about nine people had been wanting a man to say something for a month and had not gone and asked him, and the road keeper walked past all of it without stopping, as he has for a month. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the shafts up, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that the man on those stones has had about nine people about four feet off him on two hundred and thirty mornings and not one of them has asked him for anything either. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and thirty-first night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and thirty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and forty days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and ninety-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and seventy days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and seventy-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and forty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and thirty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and four marks went on the underside of those boards before about ten. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a woman saying a thing out loud about a man is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered that out loud with the reason, which is that nothing was made. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither was looked at this morning and neither was corrected, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and eighty-one.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for ninety mornings while two pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a woman saying out loud that nobody has ever promised that man anything did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that what a person has not been promised is not a reading of a lot. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, and about nine things were said out loud in it, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and about four people in that yard have said since that they have noticed it is named more often than it used to be and that nobody has ever been put in it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a woman sweeping two feet of that table did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were brought back up it full and stood against that first table until about dark, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a woman told about nineteen people in that yard this morning that nobody had ever asked a man at the end of that table for anything, and that the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months has never been asked for anything either and reads them anyway. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the second morning in a row on which a person in that yard has said something about a man in it, and that about four of them have said yesterday it was a boy counting backwards and this morning it was a woman standing at a counter, and that neither of those is a question and that nobody has asked that man one in two volumes.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-ninth of the fourth month about a man at the far end of that second table who has never been promised anything by anybody, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that table at about twelve with his right hand flat on the boards and about nine feet off a man who had put his own hand on the wood at half past ten and taken it away again at about eleven.
