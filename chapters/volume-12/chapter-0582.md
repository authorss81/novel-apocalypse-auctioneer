# Chapter 582: The Yard Wrote The Asking Out On A Board To Find Out What It Would Look Like Held Up

It rained a little before seven on the nineteenth of the fourth month and stopped, and the wet came up out of that bank and made a dark stripe along the foot of it that stayed there until about nine, and about nine people came up that bank before eight and went to the low end where a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had stood a board up on end against the side of the second table with a piece of chalk in his hand. The copy at the near end of the three sheets lying along the end of that table has now been on that wood for eighty mornings, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and twenty-one days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that a man stood a board up in that yard before eight with a piece of chalk in his hand and that she has entered that and is not entering what is written on it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and seventh of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a board went up in that yard before eight and that the man at that wall read the four off it and went on up the lane.

"**The board carries four hundred and eighty, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and ninety-six days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and ten, and four hundred and seventy-one days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up rubbed the damp off that board with the side of his hand and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-one seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About four of us were talking at the end of that table on the last morning of that month of days and one of us said that if we could get it down on something and stand it up where people walk past, we would find out in a morning what it would be like to ask it, and I have the board and I have the chalk, and I am not saying any of you asked me to do it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a man stood a board up in that yard and entered the person and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen came round the end of that second table and read the board from left to right twice with his mouth open and his thumb against his teeth and then said one thing out loud, in about twenty-three seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is a notice and it is not a question. A notice says what is the case and a question says what somebody wants to know, and there is nobody in those words who wants anything, and about four of you have read that board and about four of you have read it as a thing the yard has already decided.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy read a thing out of somebody else's mouth before he counted it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came and stood at the end of that board with the flat book shut under his arm for the fourth morning running and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-two seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**We can fix that in a minute by putting a man's name on it, and I am the one who knows which name, and I am telling you before any of you work it out that I have thought about doing it and I am not going to, and the reason is that a name on a board is not this district deciding to publish a man. It is this district publishing a man on the strength of a board nobody agreed to.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said he is not going to do a thing and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came up that bank at about one with a basket on her hip and stopped at the end of that board and read it standing up and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-two seconds, to about four people who were near enough to hear it, and the boy counted what she said and got fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I read that standing up and I have read a good many things standing up in my life, and I am telling you that a board with a question on it and nobody in it is a thing people walk past, and the ones who stop at it are the ones who are looking for something to complain of.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman came up that bank with a basket and went down it again without stopping at either table, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about half past two with his right hand flat on the boards and looked along them at the man standing at the far end of them and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-five seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have had a look at that board from where I am standing and I am not going to say anything about what is on it, and the reason is that the man standing at this end of this table is not going to be able to read it standing where I am standing, and I noticed that this morning before anybody said a word about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, with the pen she has had for about four years, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the three sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about three, in about thirty-four seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A notice is not a line and a line is not a notice and I am not entering that board as either of them, and the reason I am saying it out loud in front of you is that about four of you have been looking at it since eight as though it were going into that book, and it is not going into that book, and I have had a book for nineteen years and I know what a thing goes into when it goes into one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the board as a line, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a board went up in that yard before eight with a question on it and no name, and that a man who has walked that lane twice a day for about seven months looked at it and did not stop. The cart came up ten minutes later than it had come up all week, with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank without stopping for anybody in that yard on that day, and a barrow came up behind it on foot. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a notice went up in that yard this morning and that the man on those stones did not come up to look at it and was not asked to. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and twenty-first night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and twenty of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and thirty days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and eighty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and ninety days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and sixty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and sixty-three days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and ninety-nine days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and thirty-nine marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and twenty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and two marks went on the underside of those boards before about eleven. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a board stood up in a yard is not a fourteenth thing and is not a page, and a clerk entered that out loud at about three this afternoon with the reason, which is that a page is a thing somebody can carry and a board is a thing somebody put up. Two of the four figures on that wall are out, neither was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for eighty mornings while two pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a notice is not the fifth thing and did not pay it. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every morning of this month and it is empty on every one of them, and about four people at that first table read the entry over her shoulder while she wrote it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a board with a question on it is not a payment and is not a way of paying anybody, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and came back up it full and were set down inside that gate, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man worked out what a question looks like on a board and a clerk said out loud that it is not going in her book, and that about four of them have said those two things are the same finding told twice by two people who are not talking to each other. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a board with a question on it went up in that yard at eight and came down at four, and that nobody in that yard can say what the asking is for until somebody who has not agreed to it says it out loud.

The man of about thirty-two who puts tables up took a board down in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the nineteenth of the fourth month that he had stood up at about eight, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about half past two with his right hand flat on the boards and about nine feet off a man who was still standing at the far end of them.
