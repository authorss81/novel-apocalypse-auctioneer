# Chapter 596: A Woman Who Keeps A Scale Said Out Loud What It Would Cost Him And Then He Said Yes In Front Of Nineteen People Before A Clerk Had The Pen Down

The boards of that second table had the damp back in the grain of them by about seven on the third of the fifth month, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eight, which is about two hours earlier than he has come on any morning of two months, and about four people who had come up before him did not go to the ends of the tables when he did, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Three pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for ninety-four mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and thirty-five days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that a man came up that bank at about half past eight and that a question was said out loud in that yard yesterday and that no answer to it has been entered, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and twenty-first of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man came up that bank this morning at about half past eight and stood at the far end of that table for about an hour and a half before anybody said anything to him,

"**The board carries four hundred and ninety-four, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and ten days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and twenty-four, and four hundred and eighty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was standing at the near end of that first table when the man who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eight, and she had her own shop door open behind her and her hands empty, and she said one thing out loud, in about thirty-eight seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to say the price out loud before anybody asks him to pay it, because that is what happens in my shop and I have never once done it anywhere else. Every copy of that book that anybody makes from this morning carries this district's own words about where he is. Not his name. His whereabouts. And about four of you have worked out what that means and about four of you have not, and the ones of you who have not are the ones he is going to think about at about two this afternoon.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a shop on that lane was open behind her, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book shut under his arm and did not open it, which about four people there noticed. The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about ten with his right hand flat on the boards, and he had not said one word since he came up that bank, and the clerk of nineteen years turned her own book round on that table so that the page faced about nine people and not the man who owns the book, and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-three seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am turning this book round so that about nine of you can see what I write and so that the man it is about cannot, and I am saying out loud that I am doing it, and about four of you have said to me in a month that a clerk ought to be visible when she writes about a person, and I have said no four times, and this morning I am saying yes because there is a person in that yard and not a page.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said one more thing out loud from the near end of that first table, in about thirty-two seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The number of copies is not a figure this district can hold. That is not me being dramatic, that is the plain arithmetic of it, and I want it said out loud in that yard today, and I want it said out loud before he says the word and not entered afterwards and said to have been known all along, and about four of you would do that and I have watched you do it about nine times in a month with about nine other things.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the woman said it before the answer and not after it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam was at the far end of that second table through all of that, with his right hand flat on the boards about a foot from the near edge of them, and he had not looked at the clerk's book once, and at about ten past one he said one thing out loud, in about twenty-four seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Yes. I will let a man come up that bank and ask me. I have been in that yard every morning for two months and I have not been asked one thing, and I am not going to be one of these people who makes a speech about it, and I am not going to promise anybody I will like it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, and did not look up while she wrote it, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he had been told the cost out loud before he said it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard said one word for about a minute, and about nine people at that first table have said since that the clerk of nineteen years did not look up while she wrote the answer down, and that a clerk who does not look up while she writes a man's answer is treating it as an ordinary entry, and that this is the first time in about a month she has written anything in that yard that was not about a page and she wrote it the way she writes the weather.

Then the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud, in about thirty-five seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got ninety-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not to be thanked and I have said that in this yard three times and I am saying it again this morning. I asked one question yesterday and I stood nine feet off that man while he said yes, and none of that cost him a thing. I would like it entered that I asked one question and then stood there, and that the standing there was the whole of what I did, and that about four of you looked at me while he was saying it and I did not move.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said he is not to be thanked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past one, in about thirty-five seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering an answer, and I am entering that it was given out loud, and I am entering that a woman said the cost out loud before it was given and not after, and I am not entering where he is, and nobody is to ask me that today, and about four of you have been waiting two months for me to write down where a person is and I am telling you it is not this morning because a man said yes in a yard and did not say where he sleeps.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering where a person is, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that second table at about two with his right hand flat on the boards, and a man of about thirty-two who puts tables up stood at the far end of it and looked down its length, and neither of them said one word, and about four people in that yard have said since that two men stood at the two ends of one table for about forty minutes and said nothing, and that this is the first time those two have been in that yard at the same time without one of them leaving.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man said yes in that yard this morning and the road keeper was not here for it. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and set the shafts down at the top of it and stood there until about half past three. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nineteen people in that yard had a man say yes in it this morning and the man on those stones was given nothing on the morning of it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and thirty-fifth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and thirty-fourth of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and forty-four days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and ninety-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. Two hundred and four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and seventy-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and seventy-seven days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and thirteen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and fifty-three marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and thirty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and two marks went on the underside of those boards before about eleven. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a man saying yes in a yard did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a man saying yes is not a document saying where its person is, and the reason it is still the fourth of the five is that a document that says where its person is has still not said what happens when that person says no, and about four people in that yard said that out loud today and nobody repaired it. Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said yes in that yard this morning and a woman said what it would cost him out loud before he did, and that the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months has not been asked a thing either and reads them anyway. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that this is the first morning of that month on which a man in that yard was told the price of a thing out loud before he agreed to it, and that this has happened in about nine shops on that lane and nine times in that yard in two months, and that the man on those stones has been at the foot of that wall for two hundred and thirty-five nights and has not been asked what anything costs.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the third of the fifth month at about ten past one, after a woman had said the cost of it out loud, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man agreed out loud to be asked and entered that a woman said the cost out loud before he agreed and entered that she is not entering where he is, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the near end of that table at about two with his right hand flat on the boards and about nine feet off a man who had said yes at about nine feet.
