# Chapter 577: A Woman Asked Him A Real Question On That Lane And He Answered It

The wind had gone into the west in the night and there was rain in the far end of that valley by about eight on the fourteenth of the fourth month, and the boards of that second table were wet on the north side again, and the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds was at that table at a quarter to eight and said nothing at all to anybody, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had gone back down it again because of the rain, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those had come up that bank knowing that a man comes up that lane at about half past one every day.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and sixteen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that she has looked at that four again this morning and entered why, which is that a man said four days ago that the four is a kind and not a figure about four things, and she has entered that and is looking at it again.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and second of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a yard which has decided it needs a person has not yet found out how far a person has to come to be found.

"**The board carries four hundred and seventy-five, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and ninety-one days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and five, and four hundred and sixty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past nine, in about twenty-eight seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That four has not moved and I have looked at it twice this morning, and nobody has put a fifth kind of thing in front of me, and I am going to keep saying that until somebody does and I am not going to say this morning what the fifth one would be, because the last time I said what the next one would be in this yard I was wrong inside a week.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-five seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is four because it is a kind and not a figure about four things, and a page a person can account for is a page, and there is not one of those in this yard, and about four of you have been asking me for a fifth for a fortnight and I am telling you the same thing I told the clerk four days ago.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the four is a kind and not a figure about four things, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came up that bank at about one with a basket on her hip, set it down on the boards of that second table because the step into that yard is two inches higher than the bank, and said one thing out loud before she said anything else at all, and about nineteen people in that yard heard it, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and twenty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to say why I am going to ask a man something before I ask it, and it is not about the paper and it is not about my eleven houses and I have said I am not going to say about those and I am not going to. Somebody in this yard said yesterday that they have got to find a man, and a man is not a thing you can put on a shelf, and a woman who takes in washing for eleven houses is the person who knows which house a man sleeps in, and I am not going to tell anybody which house, and I want to know whether one could get here at all.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she gave her reason out loud before she asked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel asked one thing out loud then, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, at about a quarter past one on that lane, and the boy counted what she said and got sixteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Can anybody who does not live in this parish get to that bank without a cart?**"

The road keeper stopped on that lane where he was standing, about four feet short of the gate, and did not come any further back, and said one thing out loud in about thirty-one seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That lane is the only way in. It is eleven miles and I walk it twice a day, and about nine people come up it in a week and about four of them are not people who live in this parish, and there is a gate at the ninth mile, and after that gate the road goes down to the water, and a man on foot who is not from this parish will be told at that gate that the road ends there.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was asked a thing on that lane and entered that he answered it, and entered that nobody asked him a second question, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The road keeper said one more thing out loud from where he was standing, in about twenty-three seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got fifty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**And nobody has ever asked me whether the road ends at the water, and it does not, and there are four more miles of it past that gate, and I have said nothing about that for about six months because there is nobody here to say it to and I am not paid to carry news up a lane.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud on that lane and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering the four miles as a figure, and entered the reason, which is that a figure on a page would be the first thing anybody in this district could put on it about that road, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past one, in about twenty-six seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He has been asked about those eleven miles in six months by about four hundred people and he has never once been asked whether the road ends, and it is the same man and it is a different question, and about four of you have been standing in that yard for a fortnight waiting to ask somebody something and the one you wanted was not on that lane at all.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man on that lane had been asked about a road and had not been asked whether the road ends, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about a quarter to two and stood at the end of that second table and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was in that yard and entered nothing about him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about half past one with his right hand flat on the boards, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-five seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**If the only road to that bank is a lane with a gate on it at the ninth mile, then a man who agrees to be found is a man who agrees to be found on a road, and that is a different thing from agreeing to be found, and about four of you have been using those two words as though they were one all week.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, and she read it back to herself before she wrote it, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the four sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The road keeper came up that lane again at about half past two and read the four figures off that wall as he went by and said good morning out loud to about four people, and about four people have said since that a man who has walked that lane for about seven months has never said good morning on it, and that about four of them have said he said it to the wall. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had said out loud three days ago that he sleeps in nine places and none of them is in this parish. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a woman asked a man a real question on that lane this afternoon and that about four of them have said that the man on those stones is four feet from where she asked it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and sixteenth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and fifteen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and twenty-five days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and eighty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and eighty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and fifty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and fifty-eight days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and ninety-four days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and thirty-four marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred and twenty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and a sixth mark went on the underside of those boards before about nine. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a question asked of a man on a lane is a thing a woman did and not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk has said out loud that she is not going to say what the next kind would be. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands, and the man who says them read them twice as he has every morning and was asked nothing.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and a clerk entered four days ago that the four is a kind and not a figure about four things and entered it again this morning, and the reading of that lot has not moved off the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and a road is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a man was asked a question on that lane in the open this afternoon and answered it and there is no column for the name of whoever is asked either.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who walks eleven miles twice a day and is not paid to carry news is not one of the six things standing in that yard that are not a payment.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five in the rain and were brought back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past with her coat on and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that about nine people in that yard this afternoon found out that the only road to that bank is a lane with a gate on it, and that about four of them have said that nobody in that yard had thought to ask. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man who has said those four figures for three hundred and two mornings has still not been asked what one of them is for, and that about four of them have said that this afternoon a yard full of about nineteen people asked a man who comes up a lane instead.

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel asked the road keeper on that lane at about a quarter past one on the fourteenth of the fourth month whether anybody who does not live in this parish can get to that bank without a cart, and he answered her in about thirty-one seconds and said that there is a gate at the ninth mile and that the road goes on four miles past it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was asked and answered and that the number of documents this district does not own is four, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about half past one with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the four sheets.
