# Chapter 600: A Yard With Two Tables In It, A Book Under A Stone With No Wear On It And Five Lines On The Book, A Clerk Entering A Count Without Looking Up, A Man At The Foot Of A Low Wall Given Nothing, And A Man With His Right Hand Flat On The Boards

The yard of Lot Seventeen was cold on the seventh of the fifth month from before seven, and the first table with that lot book on it had a stone on it that had no wear on it at all, and the second table had its own stone lying face up on it with the hollow in the middle of it about an inch deep and nothing at either end, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had not gone to the ends of anything, and by ten there were about nineteen people in that yard. Two pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for ninety-eight mornings, and three pages were lying on a shop counter up that bank with a door open.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and thirty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is two and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that a book on that first table has five lines on it and that the fifth of them is in her own hand at the dictation of the man the third line is about, and entered that the fourth of them has not been touched, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and twenty-fifth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a book in that yard had five lines on it yesterday for the first time in about six volumes and that a man at that wall read his four figures this morning exactly as he has read them three hundred and twenty-four times before it, and that about four of them have said that a man is not a thing a district can hurry along.

"**The board carries four hundred and ninety-eight, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and fourteen days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and twenty-eight, and four hundred and eighty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eight, in about thirty-four seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That column has been ruled and named and entered as empty on every morning of this volume and it is empty again this morning, and I have been named as a reader out loud more in this volume than in any volume before it and I have not been entered in it once, and that is not a failure of that column. That is the whole work of that column and I would like somebody in this yard to say so out loud before this month of days ends.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that nobody in that yard said one word back to her, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about nine and went to the end of that second table and stood there and did not put his right hand on anything for about a minute, and then put it flat on the boards at the near end of that table, and his left hand was in the cloth it has been in since the eleventh of the June, and he said one thing out loud, in about forty seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and six and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A man is findable now. I have been standing nine feet off a man for two months and there is a line on a book in that yard that says where he is, and about four of you have been standing in this yard for a month wanting that, and I am the one who asked, and I am not going to be thanked, and I have said that in this yard four times now and I am saying it a fifth time because there is a morning at the end of a month of days and about four of you will want to say something.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said he is not to be thanked for the fifth time, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past nine and stood at the far end of that second table and read the fifth line of that book from the near end of that first table with his forefinger an inch off it, from the beginning to the end, and then said one thing out loud, in about thirty-two seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It says where I am and not what I am and I put it there and I would put it there again, and about four of you have worked out that this is the cheapest thing this district has ever written, and about four of you have worked out that it is also the only way any of you can get to me, and those two are the same thing and that is what I said out loud in this yard yesterday.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen stood at the near end of that first table and read that fifth line out loud twice, in about thirty-one seconds, in front of about nineteen people, and said one thing out loud after that, in about twenty-six seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I counted the whole of this yard this morning and it is the biggest number I have counted in two volumes and I am not going to say what it is a number of until somebody else says it, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud is empty and I read that line out loud twice this morning and I am not going in there, and about four of you have all worked out that by now.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that he counted first, and entered that she is not entering him in the column, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about eleven with her own door open behind her and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-one seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are three pages on my counter and about nine people a day come into my shop and about four of them have stopped asking me which one is ours and started asking me where a man is, and I cannot tell them that either, and I am not going to be thanked, and about four of you have worked out that this district has swapped one thing it cannot answer for another and that nobody has asked for the swap.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a shop was open behind her, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years entered at about half past eleven, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that this is the one hundred and twenty-seventh morning after the count, and entered it without looking up, and the pen went on the page and did not stop.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a page in that yard says to go down that lane and through the gate and past the gate to the water, and that about four of them have said the road keeper has not been asked to say whether that is the right way, and that the road keeper goes that lane twice a day and has not been asked about it on any morning of this month. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank and did not stop, and the near wheel was still dragging when it stopped. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man got a line put on a book in that yard this week and that the man on those stones has not been given anything and has not been asked anything on any morning of it, and that about four of them have said that is the correct way round and that about four of them have said the correct way round is not the same thing as the right way round. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and thirty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and thirty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Two hundred and forty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it. Two hundred and three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here, and it is nine words in the same clerk's hand and it has not been touched. Two hundred and eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and seventy-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and eighty-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and seventeen days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and fifty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and forty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a fifth line is not a fourteenth thing, and the count of the five things this district does not have is still five. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands at four hundred and eighty-nine.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and it is not one more than it was yesterday, and the number of documents this district does not own is four, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for ninety-eight mornings while three pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and a fifth line did not pay it, and a document which says where its person is has still not said what happens when that person says no, and that is the reason the reading does not go past the fourth of the five, and about four people in that yard have said that out loud and nobody has repaired it. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a boy read a fifth line out loud twice in that yard on the morning before this one, and about four people in that yard have worked out that it is emptier than it was in the first month of this volume, and that is what that column does and it has done it now in three volumes running, and no person has been appointed to it on any morning of fifty, and it is not a thing to be fixed in a morning.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and the count of the five is still five, and about four people at that first table have said since that a man is findable and is not paid, and that those are two different sentences and only one of them was wanted.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a book in that yard has five lines on it and that the woman who has read three of them every evening for about four months read three this evening and did not read the fourth or the fifth, and that about four of them have said that is the correct way round. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that the figure on that wall is out and has not been corrected, and that the last morning of a month of days is the same as every other morning of it, and that about four of them have said a man who has read four numbers off a wall three hundred and twenty-five times has still not been asked what one of them is for, and that about four of them have said that will now be true for as long as there is a wall.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the seventh of the fifth month, a hundred and twenty-seventh morning after the count, that he is not to be thanked, and a clerk of nineteen years entered the count of the board on her own page without looking up, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is two, and a man of about sixty-four is at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones and still given nothing on his two hundred and thirty-ninth night of that run, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing is at the end of that second table with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on the two sheets.
