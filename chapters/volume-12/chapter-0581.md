# Chapter 581: Six Mornings That A Yard Spent On A Person And Nobody In It Asked Him Anything

The light came over the top of that bank earlier on the eighteenth of the fourth month than it had come on any morning of the month, and it got onto the far end of that second table before the boards had dried, and the copy at the near end of the three sheets lying along it has now been on that wood for seventy-nine mornings. About four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had not gone to the end of that table, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and twenty days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that a person said a thing out loud in that yard yesterday about a man at that wall and that she is entering it as a thing said, and entered that this is the sixth morning since the object of that yard's morning changed from a page to a person, and that nobody has been asked anything on any of the six, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and sixth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that this district has spent six mornings working out that it needs a man and has not once looked at the man at the end of the second table.

"**The board carries four hundred and seventy-nine, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and ninety-five days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and nine, and four hundred and seventy days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds stood with his back to the near end of that first table and turned his knife over in his hand twice while he said one thing out loud, in about forty-four seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and fourteen and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been thinking since the twelfth about what it would cost this yard to ask him, and it is not what anybody in here thinks it is, and I have worked it out by looking at what this yard does at eight every morning. Asking him is not the cost. Putting it out loud in front of you all is the cost, because from the morning we do it this yard is a yard that asked, and every morning after that the not asking is a thing about nine of you chose, and about four of you will not be able to stand at this table without remembering that you stood at it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man turned a knife over in his hand twice while he said it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale had her shop door open behind her and was moving a jar along the back of her own counter with the flat of her hand and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-two seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There are two pages on my counter and there have been two on my counter for four mornings, and about nine people came through that door before I opened this morning and about four of them looked at them on the way past, and I have said in public three times that I cannot tell, and this morning one of the four said the reason out loud for me, which is that a woman who cannot tell should not be holding them at all.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, reading it back to herself before she wrote it, that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a person at that counter said a reason out loud on behalf of a woman, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally had the flat book open on the end of that first table for the second morning running and turned it round so that the page faced about nine people instead of facing him and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-eight seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have had this book open two mornings and shut it the other four, and the reason I shut it is that a book which is open in a yard is a book anybody can look at, and the only page I have not got anybody to look at is the one I would want them to look at, and I am not going to say that out loud twice in one week.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man opened a book in that yard and entered the person and entered the reason, and entered that she is entering the reason and not the page, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart came up that bank at about ten with the near wheel still dragging and stood at the far end of that second table looking at the three sheets without touching any of them and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-seven seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been getting ready to be useful for a fortnight and this morning I have worked out that being useful is a thing you can be asked for, and nobody has asked me for it, and I have found out this morning that I would rather be asked than be ready, and I am not going to stand here and pretend that is about my own work.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said a thing about himself that is not his name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past ten and stopped at the end of that second table with his hands behind his back, and looked along the three sheets from the near end to the far end and then at the boards, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man stood in that yard and entered that nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing came up that bank at about eleven with a rail in the crook of his arm and put it down against the side of the second table and stood at the end of it about nine feet off a man who was already at the end of it and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-six seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have a post out at the low end that has been leaning since before the middle of the month, and I have not been to it for six days, and I have not told anybody that until just now, and about four of you have worked out that I have not been to it because I come up here instead, and you are not wrong about that either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the three sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The two of them stood there. Nobody came over. About four people at the near end of the first table were looking at the two of them and one of them was holding a cup he was not drinking out of, and the boy of about nineteen looked at the boy of about nineteen's own hands and then at the two men and did not say anything, and a clerk entered that nobody said anything and entered the reason, which is that there was nothing to enter.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man at that wall has said four numbers out loud three hundred and six mornings running and that about four of them have said this morning that the cost of asking has been worked out in that yard and has not been said to the man it concerns. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank without stopping for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man worked out this morning what it would cost to ask a man, and that the man he worked it out about was at that wall at the time and did not hear it. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and twentieth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and nineteen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and twenty-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and eighty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and eighty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and fifty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and sixty-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and ninety-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and thirty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and twenty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and a ninth mark went on the underside of those boards before about nine. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a man working out what asking would cost is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered that out loud at about ten this morning with the reason, which is that it is a thing a man said and not a thing anybody made. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for seventy-nine mornings while two pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot stands at the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it has not been paid. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man working out what the asking would cost in public has not paid it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man worked out this morning what it would cost a yard to ask one man one question, and that the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months was not told any of it. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a yard worked out a price this morning and did not name a man at the wall, and that about four of them have said that the price is the finding and that this district has not yet decided whether it is willing to pay it.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the eighteenth of the fourth month about what it would cost that yard to ask the man at the end of the second table, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and about nine feet off a man who had been there since half past ten and had not been asked anything by anybody in that yard.
