# Chapter 563: Nobody In That Yard Could Say Whether There Was Another Morning Of It

The boards of that second table were wet on the north side again on the thirty-first of the third month and about four people in that yard had said out loud that there had been rain in the night without anybody hearing it, and about nine of them had come up that bank in the wet anyway, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about four of those had come up it to ask a clerk whether there would be another morning of this month.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and two days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that she is not entering whether this is the last morning of this month and entered the reason.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and eighty-eighth of those mornings, and that about four of the figures on that wall have been counted through more month ends than this district has ever counted.

"**The board carries four hundred and sixty-one, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and seventy-seven days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and ninety-one, and four hundred and fifty-two days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

A person in that yard asked a question out loud in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people, and about nine of them heard every word of it, and it was not asked of anybody in particular, and the boy counted what was said  and got seven  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Is there another day of this month.**"

There was no answer at all, and about four people at that first table looked at the boards of that table instead of at each other for about as long as it takes to say the four figures off a wall, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a person said a thing out loud in that yard and entered that it was not asked of anybody in particular,and the record about the not asking says not asked. and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years then said one thing out loud to about nineteen people, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said  and got one hundred and nine  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am not going to answer that, and I am going to say why out loud in front of all of you, and it is the same reason I gave a boy on the twenty-fourth. The last morning of this month that this district can name is this one. I am not entering whether it is the last, because a month nobody has counted has not got an end that anybody in this yard is able to put on a page, and if I put one there and it is wrong then a figure on that page is wrong and a figure on that page may not be corrected.**"

A clerk entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the reason for it, which is that a month nobody has counted has not got an end anybody in that yard can put on a page and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm, and said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what he said  and got seventy  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have a page in that book for this month and I turned into it last night and it is blank, and I am not going to put a day on it until somebody in this yard tells me the month has ended, and I have kept that book for about thirty years and I have never once had a blank page in it at this time of the month.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally has a blank page in his own book and is not writing on it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about ten with her shop open behind her, and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what she said  and got fifty-seven  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I add my days up on the last morning of a month and I have not added this one, and I am not going to until somebody tells me it has ended, and about four of you have asked me twice today whether it has, and I have said no both times, and I do not know.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open, and the record about the not asking says not asked. A clerk entered that a clerk has said out loud in that yard that she is not entering whether this is the last morning of this month, and that about four people have asked her twice.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said  and got eighty-five  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been counting the mornings of this month since the first of it and I have got a number and I am not going to say it, and the reason I am not going to say it is that the number I have is the number of mornings I have been in this yard and it is not the number the month has, and if I say mine out loud in this yard then about four people will take it for the other one.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy has a number of his own and would not give it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about eleven with his right hand flat on the boards, and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said  and got sixty-two  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is about nine lengths of fence I have to do and the water turns in this yard on a morning I have never been able to put on a piece of paper, and I have done six of them and I have two posts to set and that is the whole of my morning whatever day it turns out to be.**"

A man's right hand went flat on the boards of that second table at about eleven and a clerk entered that, and that it did not go on the stone and did not go on any of the three sheets, and entered the thing he said out loud,and the record about the not asking says not asked. and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that second table at about half past eleven and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said  and got sixty-eight  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**About four of you have asked me this month to read a copy out loud in this yard and I have said no every time and I am going to keep saying no, and there is a page coming into this yard with my shape on it and somebody else's hand on it and that is a thing I have not got a way of talking about yet.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a reading was refused in that yard this morning for the third time in a month, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a question went round that yard this morning about whether there is another day of this month, and that about four of them have said that a man who comes up that lane twice a day has walked through that yard's mornings without ever needing to know which one is the last of anything. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and about four people at that first table have said that the only thing that has ever come into that yard in a cart is nothing at all. By about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that this is his two hundred and second night of that run and that a yard which cannot say whether it is the last morning of a month has not asked the man on those stones anything for a hundred and ninety-nine of those nights, and that about four of them have said nobody has said that out loud. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and second night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and one of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and eighty days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and a clerk has entered this morning that she is not entering whether this is the last morning of this month, and gave the reason out loud in front of about nineteen people. The count of things this district has made is thirteen, and the thirteen is smaller than the twelve, and a question about the end of a month is not a fourteenth thing.

There was no advance in the reading of that lot and it stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and the fifth of them is a remedy, and a fourth line is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and a clerk entered that a man who keeps a tally has a blank page in his own book and is not writing on it. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and a reading was refused in that yard this morning, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk is not entering the name of anybody. Nine people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it, and about four of them were in that yard because a question had been asked at about half past eight and not answered. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a month this district has not counted is not one of the six.

About four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and that a woman of about fifty-eight went past that book standing up, read the three lines she has always read, said nothing to anybody, and was not asked anything by about four people who were standing close enough to ask her. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man at that wall has read four figures off it on this morning and that nobody in that yard knows whether it is the last of anything.
