# Chapter 590: A Clerk Counted The Third Month Off A Board With A Finger On The Wood And A Man Then Counted The Wall

Word had got round that parish before seven on the twenty-seventh of the fourth month that a clerk of nineteen years was going to count a month off a board in the open, and about nineteen people were at the wall by about a quarter to eight, which has never happened on any morning of that yard, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Three pages were lying along the end of that second table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for eighty-eight mornings.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and twenty-nine days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and that the third month of this year is thirty-one days, counted this morning off a board with a finger on the wood in the open in daylight with about nineteen people at the wall, and that it ended twenty-seven mornings ago, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fifteenth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man counted a month off a board this morning and then about half past nine a man counted the wall, and that about four of them have said those are the only two counts anybody has made in that yard in about a month that were not about a page.

"**The board carries four hundred and eighty-eight, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and four days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and eighteen, and four hundred and seventy-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The clerk of nineteen years put the board up on the low wall outside that yard with its face to the light and put her left forefinger on the first mark of the run and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-eight seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am counting the marks of the third month on this board and I am counting them in front of you because the third month of this year ended twenty-seven mornings ago, and a month that has ended can be counted, and I have never counted a month off a board in daylight before this morning and I am not going to pretend to you that I am not pleased about it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then she ran her finger along that board from the first mark of that run to the last of it, in the open, and about nine people at that wall counted with her out loud, and she stopped with her finger on the last one and said one thing out loud, in about twenty seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got fifty-two and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That is thirty-one. The third month of this year had thirty-one days, and it ended twenty-seven mornings ago on the morning of the thirty-first, and I am entering it, and nobody has to take my word for it, it is on that board and about nine of you watched me do it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that the length of the third month of this year is thirty-one, and entered that it was counted off a board with a finger on the wood, and entered the person, and entered that a month length has now been counted three times in this district, the other two being the twelfth month at thirty and the first month at thirty-one, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About half past nine the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came up that bank with the flat book open in his hand and stopped in front of that wall and looked at the four figures on it for a while without saying anything, and then said one thing out loud, in about fifty-two seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The fourth of those four is out, and I have known for two volumes that it was out and I have not been able to say how far, and I can say how far this morning because she has just counted a month off that board. That figure counts days from the second of January and it has never stopped. It went over the turn into this year without stopping. The year it went over was three hundred and sixty-four days long, and that is a sum of twelve months, and three of those months have been counted by somebody and nine are off a list at the back of my book, and the twelfth was counted at thirty and the first at thirty-one and the third is thirty-one as of a quarter to eight this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the person, and entered the figure, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table when he started and came across the yard and stood at the end of that wall and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-four seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have mended fence for nineteen years and I have never once in my life said a thing out loud in front of about nineteen people about a number on a wall, and I have said one this morning, and I would like the yard to notice that I did it and not to make anything of it, because that man has said four of those numbers every morning for three hundred and fifteen mornings and it has cost him whatever it costs him every one of them.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the three sheets, and entered that a man crossed that yard and stood at that wall, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about ten, in about thirty-eight seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering that a man said the fourth of those four figures is out by a whole year. I am not entering what it ought to say. A figure on that wall may not be corrected and it is not mine to correct, and I am not going to write a number beside four hundred and seventy-nine that says the wall is wrong, because a wall that has been wrong for a year is still standing, and it keeps standing until somebody with more standing than a clerk takes it down, and there is nobody in this parish with that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is not entering a correction, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the clerk of nineteen years went across the yard to that wall and said out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nineteen people, that the fourth of those four figures is out by a whole year, and a clerk entered that she said it and entered the person and entered the date of the saying, and the man of fifty-six was standing at that wall and heard every word of it and said nothing at all, and about four people at that wall have said since that he did not move and did not look at the wall, and that nobody in that yard asked him one thing about it then or afterwards, and that about four of them have said the not asking him is the hardest thing anybody in that yard has done this month.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table with her shop open behind her and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-eight seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have been asked about four times in a week which of two sheets is this district's and I have said four times that I cannot tell, and this morning a man counted a month off a board in front of nineteen people and a man counted that wall, and neither of those two things helps me at all, and I am glad about both of them, and I have not shut my shop.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, reading it back to herself before she wrote it, that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past ten and stood at the far end of that second table and looked at about nine people standing at that wall and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was in that yard and entered that nobody in that yard said one word to him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man read the four off that wall on his way past this morning with about nineteen people standing behind the man reading them, and that about four of them have said he was not asked one thing about a year. The cart came up at about three, late, with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank without stopping, and the near wheel was still dragging when it stopped. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a yard counted a month and counted a wall this morning and that the man on those stones was given nothing for it, and that about four of them have said that is the correct way round. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and twenty-ninth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and twenty-eight of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Two hundred and thirty-eight days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and ninety-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and ninety-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and sixty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and seventy-one days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and seven days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and forty-seven marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and thirty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and counting a month is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered that out loud with the reason, which is that a month is not a thing a district makes and that count is in a different book. **TWO OF THE FOUR FIGURES ON THAT WALL ARE OUT AND NEITHER WAS CORRECTED THIS MORNING, AND THE SIZE OF THE ERROR IN THE FOURTH OF THEM WAS PRINTED ONCE, IN THAT YARD, IN THE OPEN, BY A MAN WITH A REASON IN HIS MOUTH, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-SIX WAS TOLD IN FRONT OF ABOUT NINETEEN PEOPLE AND SAID NOTHING AND WAS NOT ASKED TWICE, AND THE FIGURE ON THE WALL STANDS AT FOUR HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-NINE.**

The number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for eighty-eight mornings while two pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and counting a month did not advance it, and a clerk entered the reason, which is that a reading of a lot does not move because somebody counted a month. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, a month was counted off a board at about a quarter to eight, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a clerk counting a month off a board did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past that evening, after all of it, and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months is the only one of about nine people in that yard this morning who counted nothing at all, and that about four of them have said that is not a failing. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a clerk counted a month off a board this morning at about a quarter to eight and that the whole second half of the dates this district has been printing since the middle of the last month came out right, and that about four of them have said that a man who has said four numbers out loud in that yard for two volumes had a month counted off a board under his own eyes and was not asked one thing about it.

The clerk of nineteen years counted the third month of this year off a board with a finger on the wood in the open in daylight in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-seventh of the fourth month and got thirty-one, and a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally counted the fourth of the four figures on that wall out loud and said the wall is out by a whole year, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about ten with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the three sheets.
