# Chapter 571: It Came Back Down That Bank And Nobody Saw It Come Down

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was standing in her own doorway with her shop open behind her at about a quarter to eight on the eighth of the fourth month and her counter had nothing on it, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank and looked up at her and about four of them had looked at her counter from the lane, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those were at the end of that second table where there had been three sheets at a quarter to eight.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and ten days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table was three at about a quarter to eight this morning and is four, and entered that she does not know who carried it, and entered that she is entering that she does not know instead of a name, and entered the reason for that, which is that a name she has not got is not a name and she is not going to put a mark in the place of one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and ninety-sixth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man who has said four figures off that wall every morning for two volumes read them both times this morning while four pages were lying at the other end of his own yard and he did not look at any of them.

"**The board carries four hundred and sixty-nine, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and eighty-five days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and ninety-nine, and four hundred and sixty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud at about a quarter to nine, in about thirty-one seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I was coming up that bank at about a quarter to nine with a barrow I had emptied and a page came up past me going the other way with a hand on it, and I saw the hand and I did not know the hand, and I let it go because I did not know it, and I am telling you that this morning before anybody has asked me and before anybody has said a word about it to anybody.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the person, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale came down that bank at about half past nine with her shop open behind her and stood at the near end of that first table, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-eight seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**My counter is bare and my door was open from before six and about nine people came in and about four of them stood at the counter and about four of them did not, and I do not know when it emptied and I am not going to stand here and say it was not me, and if it was me I have not noticed and I would like it entered that I said that.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open before six, and entered that she does not know when a page came off her own counter, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The shop had a smell of wet stone in it from that door standing open as long as it had, and the woman who keeps a scale went back inside and stood with her back to the doorway for a moment with her hands inside her apron, and the boy of about nineteen stood with his thumb against his teeth and did not say a number, and the four sheets at the end of that second table had not moved while all of that was going on.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud after that, in about thirty-three seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-four and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am entering four on that table this morning and I am entering the man who saw a hand and the reason he gave out loud, and I am not entering a name for the person who carried it, because I have not got one, and I would rather write down that a clerk did not know than write down something in a clerk's hand that she made up at about half past eight because about nineteen people were standing in front of her.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that she is entering that she does not know instead of a name, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart was at the near end of that second table at about ten and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**Four. It is not my hand and I am not going to keep saying that, and I am going to say the other thing once instead, which is that I have been in this yard every morning for two volumes and there has not been one single morning in all of that time when I would not have taken a page off that table and put it somewhere else if anybody had told me where to put it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past ten, in about twenty-nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is four, and I counted them at a quarter to eight and I have counted them three times since, and about four of you have been looking at a man all morning instead of at that table, and none of you has said out loud which man, and I am not going to be the one who says it either, and I have got a good reason and the reason is that I do not know.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy is not going to say which man, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four of the people at the near end of that first table went back down that bank without saying anything and about four of them did not, and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing shifted his weight off his right foot and put it back again without lifting his hand off the boards.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about eleven and stood at the end of that second table and looked at four sheets of paper lying along the end of it without touching any of them, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man looked at four pages in that yard and entered that she is not entering what he made of them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about half past eleven with his right hand flat on the boards, and said one thing out loud, in about thirty seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A page went up that bank a month ago and came down it this morning, and it went up in a man's two hands in the open with a reason said first, and it came down in somebody's hand in about four hundred paces with nobody watching, and about four of you have said that the two of those are the same journey and I would like to know why we only wrote one of them down.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the four sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a page came back into that yard this morning and a man who walks eleven miles twice a day went past four of them at that wall without being told a word about it, and that about four of them have said that the whole of that yard's attention is standing about four feet from a man who digs and not one word has gone across. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had said out loud at about ten that he would have moved a page anywhere if anybody had told him where to put it. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a page has been in that shop and is now back in that yard and the man on those stones has been in the same place through both of those. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and tenth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and nineteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and seventy-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and seventy-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and forty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and fifty-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and eighty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and twenty-eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred and fourteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning. The count of things this district has made is thirteen, and a page coming back into a yard it went out of is a page and not a fourteenth, and a clerk entered the same thing a month ago in this yard and nobody has argued with her about it since. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither has been set right this morning, and the figure on the wall is the one that stands, and it won on a morning when four pages were lying at the other end of that yard.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and it was three at about a quarter to eight, and it went up while there were four people in that yard, and the reason is on the page because a man saw a hand on it and gave that out loud in front of all of them, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move and a clerk has said out loud that she is entering that she does not know rather than a name. The reading of that lot has not moved off the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and a page that comes back is not a reading going any further along, and a fifth line is not the fifth thing and did not pay it. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and there is a person in that yard who would know more about one of those four pages than anybody else standing there and that person has not been asked and there is no column for the asking.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a page that leaves a shop with nobody watching is not one of the six things standing in that yard that are not a payment.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, looked once at the four sheets lying at the end of that second table, and went on, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that she has not looked at those since the day they came and that this morning she did, and that about four of them have said nothing else about it. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a page went up that bank a month ago and a clerk wrote down the man and the reason and the yard has not written down one word about the page coming back, and that about four of them have said that the second half of that journey is the half that is not on anybody's page.

A man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said in that yard this morning that a page came up past him on that bank with a hand on it and that he did not know the hand, the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and was three at a quarter to eight, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about half past eleven with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the four sheets.
