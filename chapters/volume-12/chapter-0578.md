# Chapter 578: Somebody Said The Fifth Of The Five Out Loud And Nobody Paid It

The rain had gone in the night and left the boards of that second table with a hard edge of dirt along the north side on the fifteenth of the fourth month, and the man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds wiped his chalk mark off and put it back at a quarter to eight, and about four people in that yard had come up that bank before eight and had been talking about a document before they got to the top of it, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about nine of those were standing at the near end of that first table with a book under a stone on it and not looking at it.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and seventeen days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the reading of that lot is the fourth of the five and did not move, and entered that she has entered the fourth of those five every morning of this volume and that this morning is the first one on which she has been asked what the fifth of them is by anybody in that yard.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and third of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a yard which has spent a fortnight deciding that a person is the only thing a page cannot carry has not once this fortnight asked what happens when that person says no.

"**The board carries four hundred and seventy-six, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and ninety-two days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and six, and four hundred and sixty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

The woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel came up that bank at about ten with a basket on her hip and said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A document that sets a lot out has five things to say and the fourth of them is a person and the fifth of them is a remedy, and the remedy is what happens when that person says no, and it is the fifteenth of this month and nobody in this district has ever said what that is, and I have washed for eleven houses for nineteen years and I have watched about nine of them not pay a bill they owed and I know exactly what that is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the fifth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say is a remedy, and entered that it is not paid, and entered the reason, which is that a remedy is what happens after a person has been asked and has answered, and that nobody in that yard has asked, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then the clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-five and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**The fifth of those five is a remedy and it is unpaid, and I am entering that it was named in that yard this morning and not paid, and I am not going to have the two of those confused in my book, because a fifth line is not a remedy and a remedy is not a line, and about four of you have been using the word line this week for something else entirely.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a fifth line is not a remedy, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm, where he has stood every morning of this volume, and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**That reading has not moved since the beginning of this volume and it cannot move until somebody says no or yes to being asked, and about four of you have worked that out this week and about four of you have been using it as a reason not to ask, and I have got a book under my arm with a remedy in it that I have never once been asked to produce.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, and she read it back to herself before she wrote it, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-nine and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is four. It has been four every morning I have been in this yard and I am not going to say the other one, and about four of you have been talking about a fifth all week as though a fifth were a number and it is not a number, it is a thing somebody has to do, and I am nineteen and I have not done it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about half past eleven with her shop open behind her, and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**If somebody in this district is going to be put on that page so that a stranger can come and ask him, then somebody is going to have to tell that stranger what to do when he has said no, and my counter has had a page on it for a month and I have never once been asked what I would do if a stranger asked me something I could not answer.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at about half past nine in the ordinary way, that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-four who digs loam came up that bank at about twelve and stood at the end of that second table and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was in that yard and entered that nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about twelve with his right hand flat on the boards, and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A remedy is a thing you have in your pocket before you need it and this district has not got one, and about four of you have been treating a man standing in a yard as though he were one, and I have been standing about nine feet off him for a month and I have not said a word and I am not going to today.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, the way she has entered things in that yard for nineteen years, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the four sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a woman asked him a real question yesterday afternoon and that he answered it, and that about four of them have said that a yard which has finally learned to ask a man something has not learned how to write down what it is going to do when the answer is no. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a remedy is a thing a person has before he is asked, and that about four of them have said the man on those stones has not had one in about two hundred and sixteen nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and seventeenth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and sixteen of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Two hundred and twenty-six days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and eighty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and eighty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and fifty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and fifty-nine days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and ninety-five days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and thirty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are one hundred and twenty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and a seventh mark went on the underside of those boards before about nine. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a remedy that is not paid is not a fourteenth thing this district has made, and a clerk said so out loud in that yard this morning with a reason. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands, and the man who has said one of those four for three hundred and three mornings was not asked this morning what happens when a person says no.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the fifth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say is a remedy and it was named in that yard this morning and it is not paid, and the count of the five is still five, and the reading of that lot is the fourth of the five and did not move, and about four people at that first table have said that a reading cannot move until somebody is asked and that a remedy cannot be written until somebody has answered. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a woman said a thing out loud in that yard this morning and was not refused, and that is the only thing that has happened on that column this month and the column is still empty.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a remedy is not one of the six things standing in that yard that are not a payment, and about four people at that first table have said that two unpaid things sitting next to each other is the only thing that yard has got today.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines of that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a yard has named the fifth of its five things this month and has not paid it and that about four of them have said that this is the first time in about two volumes that anybody in that yard has said the whole of a thing out loud and then stopped. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a man who has said four figures off that wall every morning for two volumes was not asked this morning what happens when a person says no, and that about four of them have said that a man who says four figures is a man who would have an answer.

A woman of about thirty-three who takes in washing at the second channel said in that yard this morning that the fifth of the five things a document that sets a lot out has to say is a remedy, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that it was named and that it is not paid, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about twelve with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on any of the four sheets.
