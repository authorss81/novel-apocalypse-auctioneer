# Chapter 584: A Man Came Up That Lane With A Page In His Coat And The Count Of Four Did Not Move

It was cold enough on the twenty-first of the fourth month that the wet in the boards of that second table had come back overnight in about nine places, and a man came up that lane at about a quarter to eight with a page in the inside of his coat and stopped at the end of that table and took it out and stood holding it in both hands at his belt while about four people who had already come up that bank looked at it. The copy at the near end of the three sheets lying along the end of that table has now been on that wood for eighty-two mornings, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and twenty-three days as the age of that figure, and entered that a man who is not from this parish came up that lane before eight this morning with a page in the inside of his coat, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four, and entered that it was three, and entered the person and entered the reason, and entered that she is entering the reason and not a judgement, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and ninth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that a man came up that lane with a page this morning and that nobody asked him anything at all.

"**The board carries four hundred and eighty-two, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and ninety-eight days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and twelve, and four hundred and seventy-three days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

---

A man of about forty who is not from this parish said one thing out loud, in about thirty-four seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got eighty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to put this down on that table in a moment and I am saying why first. It has been on my mother's kitchen table since before the frost and I cannot tell where it came from and I cannot tell you whether it is yours, and I have carried it up eleven miles this morning because I could not have it in that house not knowing, and I am not staying, and I am not from this parish, and I am not asking anybody anything.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered the person, and entered the reason, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Then he put the page down on the boards at the end of that second table beside the other three, and squared it with the side of his hand without touching any of them, and went back down that bank without looking at it again, and about nine people watched him go and about four of them looked at the four pages lying along the end of that table afterwards and nobody touched any of the four.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about a quarter past eight, in about forty-two seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got one hundred and eleven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have opened my book on the four and I am not adding anything to it and I am going to say out loud why, because about four of you have been waiting two days for me to open it. A fifth kind of document would be a thing this district could go and get if somebody asked for it. That man came up that lane with it in his coat and nobody asked him for it and nobody here could have got it. It is not a fifth of anything and it is not a fifth place, and I am not entering him and he is not one of us.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, reading it back to herself before she wrote it, that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about nine, in about twenty-six seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**It is four on that table now and it was three at a quarter to eight and it is four in the shop and that is eight, and it is four in the book, and I have got four numbers and they are not about the same thing, and about four of you have been treating them as one number all month and this morning they came apart.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a boy counted first, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that second table at about ten with his right hand flat on the boards and about two feet short of the fourth page and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-nine seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have put a hand on about nine feet of nothing for a month and this morning it is a page that came up eleven miles on a man's coat, and I am not saying that makes it easier. I am saying that about four of you have been talking about asking as though the only difficulty were the words, and this morning the difficulty walked up that bank and put itself down.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table and not on the stone and not on any of the four sheets, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale had her shop open and two pages on her own counter with a space between them and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-eight seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have two on my counter and a clerk has just told about nineteen people that the count of four has not moved, and I would like it entered that I have been the reason that count is four, and not the page on the table, and that I have known that for a fortnight and have not said it until now because nobody asked me and I am not going to start.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a woman said she is not going to start, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds said one thing out loud at about eleven, in about twenty-six seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**He carried it eleven miles to find out whether it was ours and he left it and he did not ask, and about four of us stood in that yard this morning and did not ask, and the only difference between the man on that lane and us is that he is not from this parish, and that is not a difference anybody in this yard would want.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past eleven and looked at four pages lying along the end of that second table instead of three, and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man looked at four pages in that yard and entered that she is not entering what he made of them, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man came up that lane with a page before eight this morning and that the road keeper walked past the gate and past him and did not stop, and that about four of them have said the road keeper has not stopped for a man with a page on that lane in about seven months. The cart came up at about three, quiet, with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank the way he has gone up it every morning of this month. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that a man carried a page eleven miles this morning to find out where it came from and did not find out and went home. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and twenty-third night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and twenty-two of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

---

Two hundred and thirty-two days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and eighty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and ninety-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and sixty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and sixty-five days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and one days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and forty-one marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and twenty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and three marks went on the underside of those boards before about ten. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and a page brought up a lane by a man who is not from this parish is not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered that out loud at about a quarter past eight with the reason, which is that this district did not make it. Neither of the two figures on that wall that are out was corrected this morning, and the figure on the wall stands.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is four and it was three at about a quarter to eight, and it went up while about nineteen people were in that yard, and the reason is on the page because a man said the reason out loud before his hand went on it, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and there are two pages on a shop counter in that parish and about nine people a day go into that shop. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for eighty-two mornings. The reading of that lot stands where it has stood for a volume, at the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and nobody has paid it. Nothing was read out of anything in that yard today. The column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and a page came up that lane this morning and did not fill it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a man who cannot tell where a page came from is not a payment, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and one of them was leaking and left a wet stripe down the middle of those boards, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man carried a page eleven miles to that yard this morning to find out where it came from, and that about four of them have said that the answer to that is four lines on a piece of paper and the paper was in his coat. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a yard has now been told by a stranger what it looks like from eleven miles, and that about four of them have said that the stranger did not put a question to anybody and that this yard has not put one to its own man in six mornings.

A man of about forty who is not from this parish put a page down on the end of that second table in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-first of the fourth month and went back down that bank, and the number of sheets on the end of that second table is four, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about ten with his right hand flat on the boards and about two feet short of the fourth of them.
