# Chapter 551: Nobody Asked One Word About It

The stone on the corner of that book was cold enough at about nine on the nineteenth of the third month that the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing would not put his hand flat on it, and about four people in that yard noticed that and about nine of them walked round that corner instead, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen, and about four of those had come up that bank to stand at the end of that second table and look at a sheet of paper lying on it, which has been on it for fifty mornings and which nobody in that yard has asked one word about this morning.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered two hundred and ninety days as the age of that figure, and entered that a figure about a figure is a figure about the figure, and that the sheet at that gatepost has no day-count of its own and has never had one.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the two hundred and seventy-sixth of those mornings, and that this is the first morning of a month in this yard and that a man at that wall has said four figures off it in two months without once being asked where any of them come from.

"**The board carries four hundred and forty-nine, the train on that siding has stood seven hundred and sixty-five days, nobody has entered anything for four hundred and seventy-nine, and four hundred and forty days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

At about ten past eight the clerk of nineteen years put the number of sheets on the end of that second table on her own page, and then said the thing out loud, and the boy counted what she said  and got eighty-eight  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to write the number of sheets on the end of that table down every morning of this month, and this morning it is one, and I am writing it because a yard that has a sheet of paper lying on a table in it ought to be able to say how many sheets, and I am not writing a word about where it came from, and if the number moves I am not writing why unless a person in this yard says why out loud.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that the number of sheets on the end of that second table is one this morning, and entered that she is going to enter that number every morning of this month and that it is a count of a morning and not a figure about a thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen said one thing out loud at about half past eight, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what he said  and got sixty-nine  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is one sheet on the end of that table and there was one yesterday and I counted it at about eight and again at about ten and it was one both times and I did not put one there in between, and about four people in this yard have said that a count of one is still a count and is not a way of filling a line.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a figure a boy counts with his own eyes is not a figure anybody can check, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart came up that bank at about nine with the near wheel still dragging and stood at the near end of that second table for about a minute and a half without touching anything on it, and then said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said  and got seventy-one  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I put that one there on the first morning of the month before at about ten past eight in front of about nineteen people and nobody asked me one word about it, and I have been standing here fifty mornings waiting for somebody to ask me, and nobody has, and this is the first morning of a new one and I am not going to be asked about it today either.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who put a sheet on a table in that yard in the open fifty mornings ago was not asked about it on this morning, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was at the east end of that yard with the flat book under his left arm, where he has stood for about thirty years, and said one thing out loud, in about eleven seconds, and the boy counted what he said  and got sixty-four  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**There is a month in this year that nobody in this yard has ever counted off a board, and I am in the middle of it, and I am not going to count a month I am living in, and I am saying that out loud in front of all of you so that it is in somebody's ear before the end of it.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man who keeps a tally has said out loud in that yard that there is a month in this year this district has not counted, and entered that she is not entering a figure for it, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about half past nine with her shop open behind her, and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, and the boy counted what she said  and got seventy-four  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**My shop is open and there is a scale in it, and that is the only thing I have ever had that a person can put something on and find out whether it is what I told them it was, and I am not saying a page is a scale, and I am saying that a person cannot check a page, and I have been standing at that counter for twenty years being checked.**"

A woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale said a thing out loud in that yard and a clerk entered the thing, and entered that her shop was open behind her the whole time she was standing there, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years then said one thing out loud at about ten, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said  and got seventy-one  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I wrote two sentences about that sheet before I came out of my own door this morning and I am refusing both of them out loud in front of all of you, and the reason is that the two of them together would tell a stranger where it came from, and this yard is not a place where a thing gets located out loud before anybody has asked it one question.**"

A clerk wrote two sentences this morning before she came out and a clerk entered that, and that she refused both of them out loud, and entered that she has entered that she wrote them and not what they are,and the record about the not asking says not asked. and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-four who mends fencing stood at the end of that second table with his right hand flat on the boards for about four minutes and then took it up and said one thing out loud, in about nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said  and got fifty-five  and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I came up that bank for a length of string, and the second rail of that gate wants three turns put on it before this wind gets at it, and I am going to go and put three on it, and there is about nine lengths to do before the light goes off this yard.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man put his right hand flat on the boards of that second table this morning and did not put it on the stone and did not put it on the sheet, and entered that he said a thing out loud and entered the thing,and the record about the not asking says not asked. and the record about the not asking says not asked.

About four people at that first table looked at the end of that second table after that man said it, and nobody said anything to anybody, and about nine people in that yard have said since that a yard which has spent a month saying things out loud about a piece of paper has not asked one word this morning about the piece of paper itself, and that the man who put it there has been standing next to it since about nine waiting for it.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a sheet of paper has been lying on a table in that yard for fifty mornings and that a man who comes up that lane twice a day has read four off that wall on every morning of both of those months and has never once asked what is on the end of that table. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and did not stop for anybody in that yard on that day, and had stood at that table at about nine. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that this yard has started a count this morning and that not one of the nineteen people in it has thought to tell the man on those stones that there is a count. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his hundred and ninetieth night of that run, that he has slept on a hundred and eighty-nine of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

A hundred and ninety-nine days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and fifty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and fifty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood since the tenth of the tenth month. Two hundred and twenty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and thirty-two days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is a hundred and sixty-eight days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and eight marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are ninety-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither figure moved this morning. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and neither of them has been corrected, and the figure on the wall wins on every morning, and about four people at that wall have said since that a man has read two figures off it this morning that are a day out in different ways and that nobody has asked him which of them he could go and look at. The count of things this district has made is thirteen, and the thirteen is smaller than the twelve, and a number of sheets on a table is not a fourteenth.

The number of sheets on the end of that second table is one this morning and it is the first morning this district has entered that number, and about four people at that table have said since that a count which has been taken once is not yet a count, and that what tells them that is not the number. The reading of that lot did not advance and stands at the fourth of the five, and the fourth of them is a person, and the fifth of them is a remedy, and a fourth line is not the fifth thing and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five, and a clerk entered that this morning and entered the reason. Nobody read an entry out loud in that yard today, and a reading was refused again this morning in front of about nineteen people, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and a clerk is not entering the name of anybody who said a thing in that yard today. About seven people said something out loud in that yard this morning and nothing went into it. The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and a number of sheets is not one of the six.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and about four people at the foot of it have said since that the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and that the woman of about fifty-eight, going past as she always does, read the three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past and said nothing to anybody and nobody there said one word to her, and that a book in that yard has had a fourth line on it for a month now and she has read past it every morning of that month. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a clerk in that yard has begun writing down how many sheets are on a table, and that not one person in that yard has asked what a sheet of paper on a table is for, and that about four of them have said that a thing you count before you know what it is is a thing you have decided to stop looking at.

A clerk of nineteen years entered the number of sheets on the end of that second table at about ten past eight on the nineteenth of the third month and it is one, and a man of about twenty-nine who drives that cart put that one there on the first morning of the month before, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about ten with his right hand flat on the boards and not on the stone and not on the sheet, and about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen have stood within about nine feet of one another all morning and not one of them has asked the man who put it there.
