# Chapter 589: Nine People Said The Asking Out Loud In That Yard And The Man It Is About Was Down The Bank And The Rehearsal Was Entered

There was mist on that bank at about seven on the twenty-sixth of the fourth month and it did not clear until about half past eight, and about four people came up that bank before eight and stood at the end of that second table and waited there, and by ten there were about nineteen people in the yard of Lot Seventeen. Three pages were lying along the end of that table and the copy at the near end of them has now been on that wood for eighty-seven mornings, and the man of about thirty-one who digs loam did not come up that bank at all that morning and nobody in that yard said why.

At about a quarter to eight a clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table, that the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move, and entered three hundred and twenty-eight days as the age of that figure, and entered that the number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and entered that the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and entered that the man the third line of that book is about was not in that yard on this morning and that nobody in that yard has said he was not coming and that she is not entering that as a thing anybody decided, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall in the ordinary voice and got all four of them without looking at the wall, a clerk entered them once, and at about four he said them again and got them again, and about nine people standing within hearing of that wall have said since that this is the three hundred and fourteenth of those mornings, and that about four of them have said that about nineteen people in that yard practised saying a question to each other for about an hour this morning and that the man at that wall read his four figures over the top of it twice.

"**The board carries four hundred and eighty-seven, the train on that siding has stood eight hundred and three days, nobody has entered anything for five hundred and seventeen, and four hundred and seventy-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.**"

The boy of about nineteen stood at the near end of that second table at about half past nine and looked down the line of about nine people and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-three seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to say it and I want to say it exactly as somebody would say it to a man, and I have practised it walking up that bank, and I am saying it now, and about four of you are going to answer it as though it were real and that is the whole idea of this morning.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a boy said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally answered him out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about twenty-six seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got sixty-seven and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I am going to ask you why that line was left off. I am asking because I have got four of them in front of me and I cannot get a straight answer out of a page, and I am not going to ask you twice, and if you do not want to answer me you can say so and I will enter that you said so.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a question was said out loud in that yard in the open at about half past nine, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The man of about thirty-seven who cuts reeds tried it next and got through the first half of it and then stopped about four feet short of the man at the end of that table, and said one thing out loud, in about twenty-eight seconds, and the boy counted what he said and got seventy-three and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I could not get to the end of that. I have said it to three men in this yard this morning and I have got about nine feet further each time and I have stopped further short every time, and I do not think that is because I have not practised enough, and I have practised it about a dozen times and I am not going to say what I think it is.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man stopped about four feet short of a man and said why, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The boy of about nineteen asked the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing at about half past ten and the man of about thirty-four who mends fencing said one thing out loud, in about forty-nine seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what he said and got one hundred and thirty-one and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I will tell you the thing I have not said out loud to anybody. I have got the rail at the low end of that bank that I have not gone to since before the middle of the month, and it is not that the rail is difficult. It is that if I ask him and he says no, then I am a man who asked a man and got a no, and I will not be the man who mends a fence any more, I will be the man who asked. I have been standing nine feet off that table for a month to keep the two things apart, and about four of you can see that, and none of you is going to make me say it to him.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, and the ink on that page was dry before she had finished, that a man said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a man said a thing about himself, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

Nobody in that yard said anything for a while, and about nine people at that second table have said since that a man said out loud in front of all of them why he has not asked, and that about four of them have said it was not a good reason, and that about four of them have said it was the truest thing said in that yard in about a month, and that nobody could tell which of the two of those it was.

The woman of about thirty-six who keeps a scale was at the near end of that first table at about eleven with her shop open behind her and said one thing out loud, in about thirty-one seconds, and the boy counted what she said and got eighty and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**I have watched about nine of you say a question to a person standing nine feet in front of them this morning and every one of you got further than the last one, and the reason is not the words. It is that not one of you has anything to lose by the answer, and the man you are all practising at is not standing here, and a rehearsal of a thing nobody has to live with is a rehearsal.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered, at the near end of that first table in that yard, that a woman said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

The clerk of nineteen years said one thing out loud at about half past eleven, in about thirty-seven seconds, to about nineteen people, and the boy counted what she said and got ninety-eight and read the number back to himself in a low voice.

"**A question was said out loud in this yard this morning nine times and nobody read a thing out of anything, and this column is for the second of those and not the first, and it was ruled at about six this morning and it is empty at about half past eleven this morning, and I am not entering anybody into it and about four of you have worked out that I could if I wanted to and I am not going to, and the reason is that a person who practises a question is not a reader.**"

A clerk of nineteen years entered that a clerk said a thing out loud in that yard and entered the thing, and entered that a rehearsal was entered in that yard, and entered that no person has been appointed to the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

A man of about thirty-one who digs loam came up that bank at about half past one and looked at about nine people standing along that second table and said nothing at all to anybody, and nobody in that yard asked him one word, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a man was in that yard and entered that nobody in that yard said one word to him, and the record about the not asking says not asked.

At about half past one the road keeper came up that lane, read the four figures off that wall as he went by, and was not asked about the eleven miles, and about four people at that wall have said since that a yard said a question to itself nine times this morning and that the man it was about was down that bank, and that about four of them have said the road keeper heard at least two of them and went on up the lane. The cart came up at about three with the near wheel still dragging and nothing on it but the shafts, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives it got it up about nine inches off the ground with one arm and went on up that bank, and a barrow came up after it at the pace of a man in a hurry. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, and nobody offered him anything and nobody asked him anything, and the record about the not offering says nothing was asked and nothing was given, and about four people near that wall have said since that about nineteen people in that yard spent a morning saying a question to each other and that about four of them have said the man on those stones has not been said a question to once in about two hundred and twenty-eight nights. A clerk of nineteen years entered at about four that this is his two hundred and twenty-eighth night of that run, that he has slept on two hundred and twenty-seven of them, and that she is not going to set down what he is going to do with his hands tonight because nobody asked her.

Two hundred and thirty-seven days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run on this day, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. A hundred and ninety-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and it has not been altered, and nothing correct has been written beside it, and the third line is a date and the date is a figure of a man, and the fourth line says that he is not named here. A hundred and ninety-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Two hundred and sixty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is three hundred and seventy days back, it is open, nothing has been pulled off it, and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is two hundred and six days past a printing it did not make, and nobody in that yard watched it.

A hundred and forty-six marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are a hundred and thirty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table, and neither moved this morning, and two marks went on the underside of those boards before about eleven. The count of things this district has made is thirteen and did not move, and nine questions said out loud in a yard are not nine things made and are not a fourteenth thing, and a clerk entered that out loud at about half past eleven with the reason, which is that a rehearsal is a thing a yard does and it goes in the yard and not in the book. Two of the four figures on that wall are out and both of them were said out loud this morning as they have been for three hundred and fourteen mornings, and the figure on the wall stands.

The number of pages on the end of that second table is three and did not move, and the number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move, and the number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a copy has now been on the end of that second table for eighty-seven mornings while two pages lie on a shop counter up that bank with a door open. The reading of that lot is the fourth of the five, the fourth of them is a person, the fifth of them is a remedy, and it is unpaid, and nine people saying a question out loud in that yard did not advance it, and a clerk entered that out loud with the reason, which is that a rehearsal of a question is not a reading of a lot and the fourth of the five does not move because about nine people practised. Nothing was read out loud in that yard today, a question was said out loud in that yard nine times, and the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud was ruled and empty at about six, and it has been named on every one of these mornings and it is empty on every one of them, and no person has been appointed to it.

The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not paid, the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and nine people saying a question out loud in that yard is not a payment and did not pay it, and the count of the five is still five.

Nobody in that yard went up that bank after the buckets, and the buckets went down that bank empty at about half past five and were carried back up it full, and the woman of about fifty-eight read three lines in that lot book standing up on her way past, stopped in the gateway to do it, and said nothing to anybody at all, and about four people at the foot of that bank have said since that a man said out loud in that yard this morning why he has not asked a man anything for a month, and that about four of them have said the woman who has read those three lines every evening for about four months is the only person in that parish who has not said why she does not do the thing she does. The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall for the last time that day, got all four of them, and about nine people near that wall have said since that a yard said a question to itself nine times this morning without one person being asked, and that about four of them have said the ninth of those was better than the first and that about four of them have said that is the trouble with it.

The boy of about nineteen said a question out loud in the yard of Lot Seventeen on the twenty-sixth of the fourth month at about half past nine and a man answered it, and a clerk of nineteen years entered that a rehearsal was entered and that no person has been appointed to the column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and the number of pages on the end of that second table is three, and a man of about thirty-four who mends fencing was at the end of that table at about half past ten with his right hand flat on the boards and about nine feet off a man who was not in that yard.
