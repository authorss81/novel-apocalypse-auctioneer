# Chapter 783: The Sixty-Seventh Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Woman With A Page Asked A Yard Which Of Two People Was The Market And The Clerk Of That District Said So Out Loud Before She Had Been Asked Twice

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and a woman with a page stood about four feet off it at about a quarter to eight yesterday morning and did not look at it. Five hundred and twenty-two days is the age of that figure.

The board carries six hundred and eighty-one. The train on that siding has stood nine hundred and ninety-seven days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and eleven days. Six hundred and seventy-two days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and thirty-one days is how long the bid has been open and it was not run, and nothing has been proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page on any morning of this week. Three hundred and eighty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Three hundred and ninety-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and sixty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and sixty-four days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and forty marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and twenty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and has not moved since a clerk of nineteen counted one off a wall in about ten minutes in front of about nineteen people. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the fifth of those five lines is one of them, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid and was not paid yesterday although a stranger asked about it in a yard.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank ninety-seven mornings and moved this morning from ninety-six. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for forty-seven mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is sixty-seven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered, and the man who keeps a tally said it at a quarter to eight this morning, which is the time he says it, and he entered it at a quarter past nine, which is the time he enters it, and on every other morning of this book the two have differed by an hour and a half, and he entered no reason for either. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move this morning although a woman said a word in a yard on purpose. The lane count is twenty-two. The lot book is on the boards at the top of that bank with five lines on it and a margin in the fifth and a column with one name in it, and it has been the market of this lot for fifteen years without a figure or a term on any of the five.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and eighth of those mornings, and yesterday a woman who has a key asked him nothing at all, which is the first morning anybody has stood in that yard and got nothing off him.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and twenty-second night of that run, having slept on four hundred and twenty-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the twelfth afternoon running, and a woman with a page came down the eleven feet at about ten past four and stood at the top of them and did not come the rest of the way, and the man of sixty-one did not look round and neither did the man of about sixty-four, and she went back up.

---

She spent the morning not asking anybody anything, and about nine people in that yard found that harder than being asked, and about four of them found it harder than they would have found the other thing.

She looked at the boards of the first table for about half an hour with the page under her arm and did not sit down. She asked the man who puts tables up what the second table was for and he told her it was a table and she wrote that down. She asked the woman who keeps a scale how long she had had her shop and the woman told her nine years and said *that is not a figure, that is a length* and Sabra Holt agreed that it was a length and wrote it as a length. She asked the man who drives the cart how many times a year he went up the hill and he said four and then said nine and then said the nine was the better answer and she wrote nine down and underlined it and did not say why.

She did not go down the bank.

---

"You have been here two mornings," said the man of fifty-six, at about eleven, from about eleven feet off the wall. "I have read four figures off this wall twice a morning for five hundred and eight mornings and I have never once been asked what they are for, and you are the first person who has come up that lane in nine years who has stood in that yard and not asked me, and I would like to know what that is, because I have had a morning and a half to work out what a person with a key would be for and I have not got there and it is bothering me more than you would think it is."

"It is bothering you because you are a careful man and careful men do not like not knowing," said Sabra Holt. "I will tell you what it is. I have about three weeks of work and two things I am allowed to carry up that hill, and there is a third thing I am allowed to carry that nobody has ever given me, and I am not going to spend the first morning of a visit on the man who would give me anything I asked for. You would have told me about those four figures in the first hour and I would have written it down and it would have been worth about four words in a room."

---

"It would have been worth more than that," said the man of fifty-six, "and I would have given it to you, and I would like that said, and I am saying it out loud on purpose, and I would like somebody in this district to notice a pattern if there is one, and there may not be one."

"Noted," said Sabra Holt, and did not write it down, and the boy of seventeen, who was on the boards with his sheet open, looked at that and then at her and then at the boards, and moved the pencil about four inches to the right on the back of his own paper, which was the first time in four years he had moved anything on that sheet to get away from a thing.

---

At about half past eleven she got the yard to stop, and she did it by not doing it. She put her page under her arm and stood at the near end of the first table and waited, and about nine people stopped before about four of them were asked to, and the man who digs loam put his hand down on the rail and the man who puts tables up stopped with a board half off the second table and left it there.

"I asked a question on that lane yesterday morning," she said, "and I got *nobody*, and I have carried the answer up a hill in my head overnight and decided that I do not want to carry it. So I am going to ask it again, in this yard, in the ordinary voice, and I am going to ask it in a slightly worse form, and I would like the yard to understand that I am making the form worse on purpose."

---

"Which of you two," said Sabra Holt, "is the market."

She said it to Adrian and to Mara Quill and she said it in the middle of the boards and not at the near end of them, and the getting of herself into the middle of that yard was the single most deliberate thing she did in two days and about four people saw it happen.

"Not which of you two is right about anything," she said. "Not which of you two is going up the lane. Which of you two is the body that a payment can come out of, because that is the only question the ninth line of your notice is asking and it is a worse question than the one it appears to be."

---

"Neither," said Adrian.

"Write that down and I will carry it," said Sabra Holt, "and I have carried worse than that for people I liked more. But I am going to put it to the yard, because I have been in nineteen years of this and the reason a district says *neither* to that question is never that there is nobody. It is always that there is somebody and saying so would cost something. So: is there somebody."

---

Nineteen people looked at about nine other people in that yard, and it went on for longer than an answer should have taken, and the boy of seventeen was the only one who moved.

"The money," he said.

"Go on," said Sabra Holt.

"There is no money. There are nine coppers and there is a book and there is a clerk, and the nine coppers belong to nine people and the clerk can tell you which person every one of them belongs to, and that is exactly why they do not go anywhere." He was still on the boards. "A market is the thing the payment comes out of. You said that yourself yesterday morning at the top of that bank, and I was about nine feet below you and heard all of it. The payment comes out of the record of what the district owes, and the record is that book, and the book is kept by her, and if you want the name of the thing the payment would come out of then it is not a market, it is a woman with a book, and there is a difference and I have been trying to say the difference for four years and this is the first time anybody has asked me a question about it."

---

Everybody in that yard looked at Mara Quill, and she had been in that yard two mornings and had not once had a question put to her in it.

"I am the market of Lot Seventeen," said Mara Quill.

She said it in the ordinary voice and to the yard and not to the woman, and then she said the rest of it before anybody could put a question on the back of it, and she said it fast and she said it the way she says figures.

"And before you ask me what that is worth, here is what there is not. There is no figure, because a market without a figure in it is a market that has not decided anything, and this one has not decided anything since the morning I was nineteen. There is no term, because a term is a length and this is a length with no end on it and there are four of them in that book and the fifth is a man. And there is no person I can pay, and there never has been, and I have been the market of that lot for fifteen years and I have not paid anybody one copper because there is no instrument in this district that can take a copper off a person and put it against a year, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and I am the market, and so the fifth of the five is me."

---

"You have just told a woman with a key that you are a person who cannot pay a person," said Adrian.

"Yes."

"Then say what follows from it. I conceded a cause in that yard six mornings ago and somebody stood in the next morning and said what followed, and the only reason anybody had to was that the man who conceded it could not think of it himself. This morning I would like to do it myself." He had both hands on the boards. "It follows that there is no market here. It follows that the ninth line of that notice cannot be answered by anybody, and that the answer to a question about a term is a term, and the answer to a question about a bearer is a bearer, and a market that cannot pay is not a market, it is a woman with a book. Say that instead. Say that in a yard and I will hold the boards while you say it and about nineteen people will hear it and it will be true, and it is the only version of the answer that I can stand behind without lying down."

---

"Do it yourself," said Mara Quill.

She did not raise her voice and she did not stop, which are the two things that had made her a clerk for fifteen years.

"Say in this yard that I am a woman with a book and not a market, and I will enter it, and I will read it out at four o'clock in the ordinary voice, and it will be the only entry in that book in fifteen years that a person has made about themselves in their own words and not about a figure, and I will enter that too. And when you have said it, this district will have a market on a page that says it is not a market, and a woman with a key is going to carry that up a hill, and the room is going to do what rooms do."

---

Adrian did not say it.

He stood on the boards of the first table in front of about nineteen people with his hands at the sides of the boards and worked out what he was going to say and then found that he did not have it, and then found that he had it, and then found that what he had was a sentence about procedure, and he did not say that one either, and about nine people in that yard watched a man decline to make a move that had been handed to him in the open, and the woman who keeps a scale said one word from her own step at about forty feet, and the word was *there*, and nobody wrote it down.

---

Sabra Holt did not write down any of it.

"I am not entering that," she said, to the yard, with her hand flat on the page under her arm. "I want to be exact about why, because I have not been exact about anything since I came down that lane and I would like to start. A clerk of this district has told me, in a yard, in front of about nineteen people, that she is the market of this lot and that she cannot pay a person. That is not a thing I can enter. It is not in a book, it is not on a page, it has no number on it, and if I write it down and it goes up that hill then in about four years somebody is going to say that the market of Lot Seventeen was asked in a market yard by an appointed administrator, and that is a different sentence, and it is the sentence that people in rooms have been saying about people in yards since before I was appointed."

---

"Then what will you write," said the boy of seventeen.

"The fact that you said it," said Sabra Holt. "I will write that a person in this yard said a thing out loud today and that I did not enter the thing and was asked why not, and that I gave a reason. That is what I am for. I am not for what is said. I am for the fact of a thing having been said in front of people, and I have been doing it nineteen years and I have got very good at it and I have got very good at not knowing what any of it means."

---

"Then I will tell you what it means," said Mara Quill, "because I have not been asked and because nobody in this yard is going to ask you and I would rather it came from me than from a room."

She took her hand off the book.

"You have made me findable. That is the whole of it and I am not going to dress it up and I am not going to thank you. There were six people in this district that anybody could look up before the fifty-eighth morning and there are about fourteen now, and a woman of fifty-eight named nine of them out loud in a yard and said the price before she said yes and one of the nine is two miles away on a working list and cannot be found by anybody who comes up that hill, and I have carried the other eight of those names for nine mornings, and I have not slept properly about three of them."

"And now there is a sixteenth," said Sabra Holt.

"And now there is a sixteenth, and it is me, and I did it to myself in a yard at about half past eleven this morning with a woman with a key standing in the middle of the boards, and I could have said the other thing and I did not, and I want that said out loud too, because if somebody writes down this morning they should have both halves or neither."

---

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about four and got all four of them, and the clerk's pencil went into a book, and about four people got out *as sent* under their breath, and Sabra Holt asked him for them and he gave them to her and she wrote them in her own book in her own hand and did not say thank you, and he did not want her to, and neither of them mentioned what had just happened, and the boy of seventeen entered the fact of it in the box with the mornings in it and ruled a space under it and left that one empty as well.
