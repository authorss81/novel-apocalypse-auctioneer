# Chapter 792: The Seventy-Sixth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Woman Who Keeps A Scale Came Up The Bank For The First Time In Nine Years And Said Out Loud That She Has Never Weighed A Person And Then Asked A Man Of Thirty-Eight What He Is Paid In

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and thirty-one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries six hundred and ninety. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and six days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and twenty days. Six hundred and eighty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and forty days is how long the bid has been open and it was not run, and nothing has been proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and ninety-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and seventy days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and seventy-three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and nine days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and forty-nine marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and thirty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a woman came up that bank this morning and said out loud that she has never used the only instrument in that yard on a person, and it is still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and six mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and five. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for fifty-six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is seventy-six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move, and a woman carried a thing up that bank this morning that nobody else in that yard has ever used on a person and it is not the fifteenth of anything. The lane count is twenty-two. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and there is no more in it this morning than there was yesterday morning.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and seventeenth of those mornings, and about four people in that yard heard the second one from further away than they usually hear it, because there were nine feet of bank between the wall and about nine of them and the second one came up off the bottom of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and thirty-first night of that run, having slept on four hundred and thirty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the twenty-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of fifty-six read four figures out loud above their heads at about four o'clock and neither of them heard a word of it.

---

She came up at about half past ten with the scale in both hands.

It was a beam scale with a pan on each end and the beam was older than the pan on the left, which was older than the pan on the right, and she carried it the way a person carries a thing they have carried up a bank before, which was with the beam against her hip and both hands on the frame and no help offered by anybody, including a man of about thirty-two who was standing nine feet off the boards with a stone in his coat and could have taken it.

---

"Nobody move it," she said. "It is nine years old and the left pan has settled and if a person who is not me picks it up wrong it will read a half-pound light for the rest of the season."

---

"I am going to ask why you have come up," said Sabra Holt.

"Because a stone came off a table in that yard yesterday," said the woman who keeps a scale. "That is the whole of the reason and I would like it entered before I say anything else, because I know what eleven mornings of a woman with a key looks like from up here and it looks like a person collecting things."

---

She put the scale down on the boards of the first table at the near end, set of legs first, and squared it, and stood back and looked at it.

"Nine years," she said. "I have kept that on my own step about forty feet from the bottom of that bank, and I have had nine years of other people's weight go across it, and I have never once put a person on it. There is no figure in this district that a person can be weighed against. There is not one. And that is not a poverty and it is not a failure, it is a shape, and the shape is the fifth of the five things this district does not have, and I have known it since I was forty years old and I have never said it out loud in that yard because a thing said out loud in that yard gets used, and I have watched a thing I did not say get used anyway and I have watched a thing I did say get used inside a week."

---

"Then it is not paid," said the man of fifty-six, from about eleven feet off the wall. "You have said a true thing about the fifth of the five and it is not paid."

---

"No," she said. "I have said what it is shaped like. Paying it would be a way to weigh a person, and there is not one, and I have just come up eleven feet of bank to tell you that the nearest thing to one in this district is a beam scale with an old pan and a woman who has never used it on anybody, and that is not a payment and I would like it noticed that I came up here to say it and that saying it did not make it one."

---

Adrian had been about nine feet off the near end of the boards for the whole of it and had not interrupted once.

She turned and looked at him and did not ask him whether she might.

"What are you paid in," she said.

---

The yard went quiet in a different place from the other quiet.

"I am not asking it to be difficult and I am not asking it in front of a key," she said. "I am asking it because I have had nine years of other people's weight across that beam and not one of the nine has ever stood on it, and I have not asked, and I am asking now. If you have an answer I will not use it. If you have not one, then you are the first person in nine years that I have asked and got nothing from, and I would like you to say so out loud so that it is in a yard."

---

"I have not got a bed and I have not got a wage and I have not got an office," said Adrian, "and I have not asked for any of the three of them, and that is the answer and it took me about nine years to be able to give it without making it sound like something I have chosen."

---

"You have got ninety-seven mornings," she said.

---

"Yes."

---

"Then that is what you are paid in and it is the same as what he is paid in and it is the same as what I am paid in and there are three of us in that yard being paid in mornings and not one of us is in a household that anybody could put in a line." She looked at the man of fifty-six. "You. Five hundred and seventeen. Nobody has ever paid you one copper for that and you have never asked, and I am going to say the rest of it because I have only come up here once. You are the fourth thing in this yard that can be named and cannot be paid and I have been the fifth of the five for nine years and I did not know until this morning that there were four of you."

---

"Mavis Dorr named nine on the fifty-eighth morning," said the man at the end of the second table, without turning round. "Eight of those nine are two miles from here or less. She has never been weighed either and she has never been asked, and I am not saying that to put a figure on her and I am saying it because you have just made a shape out of the yard and the shape has got her in it whether she was standing on it or not."

---

"I know it has," said Mavis Dorr. "I have had eighteen mornings of knowing it has."

---

Sabra Holt had been watching the scale on the boards for about as long as she had been listening.

"I have been in this yard twelve mornings," she said, "and I did not know there was an instrument nine feet off the wall that had never been used on anybody, and I have been asking that yard for twelve mornings what it has that it is not using, and it has been standing on a step forty feet off this whole time in plain sight, and nobody told me and I am going to record that as a thing about me and not about this yard, because the woman carried it up and I did not ask her to."

---

"You could have asked."

---

"I could have asked her name, for one, and it is not on any page in this district and she has not given it, and I have held a key for nineteen years and I have never once put a name in a page because I could reach it."

---

The woman who keeps a scale looked at her for a moment, and it was the first thing about her in nine years that had been directed at a person rather than at a scale.

"You would have got it," she said. "That is not a compliment. I have had three people in nine years who could have reached it and I have given it to none of them, and if you had asked I would have said, and then I would have been a woman with a name on a page that I did not choose and I would have been, from that morning, a thing in a record instead of a woman on a step. That is the whole of what I have been doing out here."

---

Then she asked the other one, and she asked it plainly, in the ordinary voice, at the top of eleven feet with about nineteen people in the yard.

"Are you going to be here in ten mornings."

---

"I do not know," said Adrian.

---

"Neither do I," she said. "That is the answer I get off that step every morning, every single morning, and it has been the answer for nine years and it is the only figure I have ever had out of this district and it has never once changed." She set the beam straight with one finger. "This morning it was the same answer and I have got a different figure out of it, and I am not going to tell either of you what the figure was, and if you ask me again I will tell you, and if you do not ask me again then that is the correct way for this to go."

---

Nobody in that yard asked her again, and about four people in it stood in a way they had not stood before, which is what happens in a yard when a person has said a thing that is going to cost them something specific and has named the cost before doing it.

---

"I would like to come and stand on your step," said Adrian.

"No."

She did not say it sharply and she did not turn round, and she said it while she was setting the beam straight a second time, which is what a person does with their hands when they have been asked something they are not going to change their answer about.

"Then that is the end of that and I am glad you asked me in front of them, because if you had asked me on my own at about forty feet off I would have had to think about it, and I have not got anything to think with."

---

"Why not."

---

"Because in about four years somebody is going to be writing about this yard." She set the pan down. "And they will write that a man of thirty-eight came and stood on a step with a woman who keeps a scale, and that is the whole of what they will write, and there will be a photograph of it in the middle of a page somewhere and it will be a picture of a man being given something by a person who has nothing, and there is not one of those photographs in this basin that is not a lie and I have grown tired of being the woman in them."

"You have never been in one."

"I have been in about four, in about nine years, all of them with men in them, and in three of them I was holding the thing I was giving." She picked the frame up. "Then stand on the boards in the light like everybody else here does. That is the whole of what I have got, and I have given it to you in a yard with about nineteen people in it, and that is a great deal more than the step has ever had."

---

He stood there afterwards with the thing she had said still going round, and it went round the way the third person had gone round in the third person about ten mornings ago on a lane, when he had described a valve to a woman who was walking beside him and had been caught at it by her, and this time the third person had been his own and there was nobody to catch it.

*He is paid in mornings. So am I. So is the woman on the step. So is the man at the wall.*

Four of them. And every one of the four had said out loud, in this yard, in front of about nineteen people, the name of a thing they had made or refused or done, and not one of the four has ever been paid anything, and one of the four has a name in a column and it is in a column that says *whoever* and it went in before anybody made a rule about it.

Five mornings ago a woman with a key had stood at the foot of that wall and said the words *there is nobody* out loud, and he had asked her for the other half of it, and she had given it to him in front of about nineteen people: that a settlement is built so that the only people who could stand in a room are the people it has already used. He had watched about four people in that yard understand that the sentence had been said about them, and he had understood that it had been said about him, and he had said nothing at the time, and it had taken him five mornings to notice that he had said nothing at the time.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and the woman who keeps a scale heard them from the top of eleven feet instead of from forty feet off and did not repeat any of them, which nobody had thought she would do and about four people had expected her not to.

She picked the scale up when the light had come off the boards and went down the bank with it the way she had come up, and nobody followed her down, and the boards of the first table at the near end were bare and warm.

The boy of seventeen got the pencil out at the near end of the boards and did not write anything, and put it away again in front of about four people who had been watching for it.
