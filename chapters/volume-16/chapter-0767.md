# Chapter 767: The Fifty-First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Who Puts Tables Up Said A Figure Out Loud In A Yard And Was Thanked For A Rail And Understood In About Four Seconds That Being Thanked Was The Receipt

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. Five hundred and six days is the age of that figure. The board carries six hundred and sixty-five, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and eighty-one days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and ninety-five days, and six hundred and fifty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifteen days is how long the bid has been open and it was not run, and nothing has been proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page on any morning of this fortnight. Three hundred and seventy days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Three hundred and seventy-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and forty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and forty-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and eighty-four days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and twenty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and ten marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move, and four pages have come down that lane in a fortnight and not one of them was a month. The number of documents this district does not own is four and it did not move, and there is a page in a corridor in Alder Reach with a name on it that this district does not own and has now read out loud. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and it did not move, and there are three pages on a shop counter at the top of that bank and one of them is not this district's, and that is the fifth place and it stays uncounted on the word of a man who sits at the end of a table. The number of conditions with no end on it is four and it did not move, and a man stood at the end of a table yesterday afternoon and asked whether one of them could be ended and was told no in a yard, and four is still four and the fifth of those five lines is one of them.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank eighty-one mornings. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for thirty-one mornings, and nobody had thanked him for it on any of the thirty that came before this one, and he had said on the forty-fourth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that he was going to be thanked on some morning or he was not.

The count of things this district has made is fourteen and it did not move, and a rail fitted by a man is not a fifteenth and has never been a fifteenth, and the man who fitted it was thanked for it this morning by about eleven people and did not get thanked for a figure. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is fifty-one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the four hundred and ninety-second of those mornings, and he was in that yard from about eleven until about half past two and did not say one word in it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, on his four hundred and sixth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and five of them, and a clerk's own book has a line in it about him and a lot book has nothing, and he has now been asked one question in his life.

---

There was a figure on a page in Alder Reach and it was forty coppers for a year and the two words on the back of it were *as sent*, and there were nine coppers in this district, and there was a name in the column, and Adrian had understood by about a quarter to eight that morning that the only person in that yard who could do anything about any of it was a man about thirty-two years old who was at the foot of that bank at about half past nine with his hands in his pockets.

"He is the only one of us who says numbers out loud in a yard and has them entered," Adrian said to the clerk, at about ten to eight, at the near end of the first table. "That is his whole office. He is the reason the count of the mornings a man has been at that bank is a count and not a figure behind which anything was bought or owed, and he said that out loud in this yard about a year ago in front of nineteen people, and nobody has contradicted him since, including me."

"You're going to ask him to be a guarantor."

"I'm going to ask him a question," Adrian said. "He will hear the rest of it before I have finished this sentence, because he has been in this district for nine years and he is the fastest person in it, and I am not going to pretend that a question is different from a request. I'm going to say that I am asking him, and I am going to say why, and I am going to say what it costs, and then he is going to say no and I am going to have wasted a morning."

---

He said no for about eleven seconds and then he said yes, and the eleven seconds were the most useful part of the morning.

"No," Seth said. "Right. Now. You've come and asked me the exact thing I have been waiting nine years for somebody to ask me, and you did it in the wrong order, and I am going to do the right order and then I am going to do your version, and I want everybody here to hear both."

He came up the bank for the second time in about eleven days.

"I have been at the bottom of this bank instead of the top of it since I was about twenty-three," he said. "I fitted a rail on the near side of that table in my own time in about ten minutes with two hands and nobody asked me to and nobody thanked me, and I stood in this yard on the first morning of that book and said that a rail fitted by a man is a thing a person did and not a thing this district has made, and I have argued it since, and the count has been fourteen for about eleven mornings and it did not move on the morning I cut that rail and it did not move on the morning I fitted it, and I have never once been sorry about that and I have thought about it more than anything except my hand."

He put his right hand up, palm out, in the yard, the way you do, and about nine inches of the skin at the base of the thumb was gone and had been gone since he cut a length of wood, and nobody in that yard had ever asked him about it and about four people looked at it and one of them was a boy of seventeen.

"And this morning there is a page nine miles from here that says a person can be paid forty coppers for a year, in full, at a sitting, out of the funds of the market sending him. And the funds of this market are nine coppers, and there is a person attached to every one of them, and the only person in this district who has ever said a number that a stranger could hold him to is me, because I have been standing at the foot of that bank saying figures out loud and having them entered by a clerk for about nine years, and every one of those figures is a count of mornings, and I have said in this yard that they are counts and not figures behind which anything is bought or owed."

"I remember," Adrian said.

"So do I. I said it on the first morning of that book." He turned round. "And I have never once wanted a figure behind which anything was bought or owed. I want to be thanked. That is all of it. That is the whole of the thing I have wanted for nine years and I have never once said it out loud in this yard and I said a version of it on the forty-fourth morning and I have been ashamed of that version for about a week."

---

"Say the figure."

"Forty coppers is theirs, not mine. I can't say theirs." He turned the hand over and looked at it. "Here is the condition and I am putting it in the open because I have watched four people do this in a fortnight and not one of them has said what has to be true first. A figure is not a payment to a market that cannot pay a person. It is a figure the moment a market has said out loud, in a yard, in front of about nineteen people, that a person can be put in a room for a term and be found again afterwards."

He put his thumb on the boards.

"Nobody in this yard has said that," Seth said. "Not her, and not you, and not the man who puts his name in a book about things with words on them. You said a name could be sent and you said a figure could not, and the woman of fifty-eight said out loud that sending nobody is now a question that will be asked of him, and none of those is the sentence. So I am going to stand behind forty coppers in a yard in a few minutes on a condition that is not met, and I want that in the air in the ordinary voice, and I want somebody in this yard to understand that I am doing it anyway, and that a man who stands behind a figure on a condition that is not met is not a guarantor and will never be one and knows it."

"Mara," Adrian said. "Is that enough."

The clerk was at the near end of the boards with her pencil not moving.

"That is *precisely* enough," she said, "and I have watched three people in this yard in eleven mornings offer a true thing with a price on it, and one of them has been you twice, and I have never once watched anybody hand me a condition in advance. I am going to enter it, and I am going to enter the condition, and I am going to enter the figure, and I am going to enter that this district holds nine coppers."

"You know the figure can't be paid."

"I know the figure cannot be paid," she said. "I am the clerk. I have known that the figure cannot be paid since the man said it and I entered it in a book of my own that has nothing ruled on it. You did not come to me to find out whether forty coppers is in this district. You came to me so that there is a piece of paper on which it is written down that somebody said forty coppers out loud in this yard and that a man here stood behind it."

---

He said it at about eleven, on the boards, with the sun behind him and about nineteen people in that yard.

"Forty coppers a year," Seth said, "is what a person is worth in a room nine miles from here, and this district cannot pay it and is saying so out loud, and I have heard the nine coppers and I know whose they are, and I am standing behind forty for one year, and the term ends on the day before the first sitting following and it only starts again if the people this yard represents agree to start it again fresh, and I am the person they will have to ask, and I have never asked anybody for anything in nine years and I am not going to start."

The yard was quiet, and then it was not, and about nine people in it said thank you to Seth Arlow for the rail, and none of them had planned to and none of them decided to.

---

They did not decide to. That is the part he could not get past for the rest of the day. It came out of the middle of the yard the way weather comes, and the woman of fifty-eight said it first, and then the man who keeps a tally said it, and then the woman who keeps a scale said it from the step of her own shop, and then about nine other people said it, and Seth Arlow stood on the boards of the first table at the top of that bank with his right hand open and did not say one word for about eleven seconds.

Then he said, "Right," and got off the boards, and went down the bank, and fitted nothing, and sat on the edge of his own table with his hands on his knees for about an hour.

Orren did not count it. That was the thing Adrian noticed at about half past eleven, from about nine feet away, and it was the first morning in about four years that the boy had not counted a thing said out loud in that yard, and Adrian looked at the margin of the sheet and there was nothing written on it, and he understood that the boy had decided that a number of words was not the thing at issue and had not wanted it on the page at all.

---

"The rail is a thing this district has made now," Seth said, to nobody in particular, at about one. "That's what happened. I stood up here and said a figure and nine people thanked me for a rail, and those two went into the same breath, and the rail is the district's, and the district has got fourteen things and it has still got fourteen, and the fourteen did not move this morning at a quarter to eight and it is not going to move this afternoon, and it does not have to, because I have just put it in nineteen mouths and a mouth is not a book and there is nothing anybody in this yard can do about that."

"That is a hard thing to have won and a worse thing to have lost," Adrian said.

"Aye."

"You said in this yard twice that the rail was a thing a person did and not a thing the district has, and you were right both times, and I want to say that as a man who has been in this yard two months: you were right both times and it has stopped being true and it stopped being true because about eleven people were pleased."

"Then say the rest of it," Seth said, "and say it in a yard, and don't do me the favour of only saying the half that's kind."

---

"I said in this yard on the first morning of that book," Seth said, "that a count of mornings is a count of mornings and not a figure behind which anything was bought or owed. That was true at about eight o'clock this morning. It is not true now. I have said forty coppers in a yard and I cannot pay forty coppers and I have said it in front of a clerk who has written it down, and about eleven people have thanked me, and a thank you is the only thing in this basin that attaches a number to a man without anybody having to write a word."

He put his hand on the near rail, which he had fitted himself, with two hands, in about ten minutes, on a morning about thirty-one mornings back, in his own time, for nothing.

"Every figure I have entered in this district is now a figure behind which something was bought or owed, and there is a man at the far end of this table who has been standing here for fifteen years on a line with no end on it and no pay, and he says out loud that he would rather be fetched than be nothing, and he is right, and I have been that man for nine years in everything but the one thing that matters, which is that somebody came up that bank and thanked me."

---

"Nobody is thanking you again," the man of fifty-six said.

He had been in that yard from about eleven until about half past two and had not said one word, and he said that one from about eleven feet off the wall with the ordinary voice, and Seth Arlow turned round and looked at him.

"You have read four figures off that wall twice a morning for four hundred and ninety-two mornings," Seth said, "and you have never once asked me for one, and you are now telling a yard of nineteen people not to thank me for a rail, and I would like to know whether that is one of the four or whether that is a separate thing."

"It is a separate thing," said the man of fifty-six, "and I am saying it because I know exactly what a thank you is and nobody else in this yard does, and I have wanted to say a thing about it for about four years and I have never had a subject. I am the man in this district that a figure gets said to. That is all I have ever been. And this morning I watched a figure get said in a yard and turn into a promise before the man who said it had finished putting his hand down, and I could have said the sentence that would have stopped it, and I did not say it, because I have never once in four hundred and ninety-two mornings said anything at all and I did not know how to start, and by the time I had worked out how to start the thanking had finished."

He looked at the wall.

"Never again," he said. "Not the rail. Not the two rails. Not the length of wood. Nobody in this yard is to thank that man for anything, and if one of you does it in the next four years I will say the four figures out loud in the ordinary voice and everybody present will know what it means, and that is the only instrument I have got and I am going to spend it on this."

Nobody thanked him either, which Seth Arlow noticed and did not comment on, and Adrian noticed and did comment on, much later, to the boy, on a road, about a mile on.

---

At about four the clerk entered it, and read it out, because that is what she does now, and it was four lines and it was the longest entry in that book in fifteen years and it was not a figure.

> *Entered on the fifty-first morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, at about four, by M. Quill. A figure was said out loud in this yard at about eleven and a man stood behind it. The figure is forty coppers for one year, and the figure is not the figure on the sheet at that gatepost and the two are not in one line.*
>
> *Entered that this district holds nine coppers and that a person is attached to every one of them and that the clerk can name all nine.*
>
> *Entered that the figure said cannot be paid and was not paid and is not paid this morning and is not going to be paid on any morning this month, and that a figure is not a payment, and that a person standing behind a figure he cannot pay is not a guarantor and is not called one in this book.*
>
> *Entered that about eleven people thanked a man for a rail, and that I have entered no reason for that, because I do not have one, and that I have entered reasons on four mornings out of eleven and that this is the first time in fifteen years that a thing has happened in that yard that I have wanted a reason for and could not write one, and that I am entering the want instead of the reason, and that I would like somebody to tell me whether that is allowed.*

"No," said the man she was talking about.

The clerk did not look up.

"No," Seth Arlow said. "It is not allowed, and you should not have entered it, and you have entered it because you are the only person in this district who can and I am the only person in this district who loses by it, and that is exactly the arrangement you told me about on the fortieth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month and I have not forgotten a word of it. Enter the no. Enter who said it. And enter that I said it about myself, because in about four years somebody is going to want to know whether a clerk can enter a want against the man it is about and the answer is no unless the man lets her."

She wrote it. She wrote it slower than the rest.

A form is not one. A rail is not one. A thing with a name on it is not one. A figure said in a yard is not one, and there is now a man in that yard who has said one out loud that he cannot stand behind in the ordinary sense of the word, and the count of the five things this district does not have is still five, and the clerk entered that number last and it has not moved all day.
