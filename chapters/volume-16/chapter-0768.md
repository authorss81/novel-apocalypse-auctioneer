# Chapter 768: The Fifty-Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Went Down That Bank And Asked The Man At The End Of The Second Table What He Would Give To Put An End On Something And Was Told No And Let The No Be Written Down

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and it did not move. Five hundred and seven days is the age of that figure, and it did not move either.

The clerk entered them first this morning, in that order, before the board, and read them back to herself with a gap between them the length of a hand, the way she has done for fifteen years.

The board carries six hundred and sixty-six, the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and eighty-two days, nobody has entered anything for six hundred and ninety-six days, and six hundred and fifty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and sixteen days is how long the bid has been open and it was not run. Three hundred and seventy-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Three hundred and seventy-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and forty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and forty-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and eighty-five days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and twenty-five marks have been cut off that board since the mark for the first of the twelfth month, and there are three hundred and eleven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and it did not move, and it did not move because nobody counted one, and a clerk in this district has been putting a figure under her own hand for fifteen years that is nine years out of date, and she has never once corrected it, and that is not a reason and it is the reason. The number of documents this district does not own is four and did not move. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and it did not move, and it is four and not five, and a man put his no in the book about nine mornings ago and was right, and a woman with a scale read four lines out loud in this yard yesterday about a person who is on no page anywhere, and that is a fifth thing and it is not a place. The number of conditions with no end on it is four and did not move this morning, and the fifth of those five lines is one of them, and it is the only one of the five that could be ended by a person in this district in the time it takes to pick up a pen, and Adrian had worked that out on the forty-ninth morning at about four o'clock and had not been able to put it down for two days.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank eighty-two mornings. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for thirty-two mornings and nobody is to thank that man for it. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move, and it has not moved on any morning since a map was drawn with a hole in it, and a margin is not a fifteenth thing this district has made. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is fifty-two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the four hundred and ninety-third of those mornings.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, on his four hundred and seventh night of that run, having slept on four hundred and six of them, and about nine people in that district have now been told by two different people that there is a page in a clerk's own book with a line in it about him, and he has been asked one question.

---

A term is the bit where somebody stops, and a man of about thirty-two said that in a yard on the thirty-eighth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, and it was the best definition anybody in that district has ever given of anything, and Adrian had spent two days failing to get away from it.

The page that came back down the lane wants three things: a name, a figure, and a term. The name is Adrian's and it is in a column and it cannot come out. The figure is forty coppers for a year and a man stood behind it yesterday morning and cannot pay it. The term is one year, ending on the day before the first sitting following, and it starts again only by fresh consent of the persons the market represents.

Two of the three can be given, sort of. The third one cannot, and the reason it cannot is a sentence in a clerk's hand at the bottom of a bank that has been working exactly as it was written for fifteen years.

Adrian went down the bank at about ten and stood at the far end of the second table, which is four feet further on than where he stops, which he noticed and did not think about until afterwards.

"I'm going to ask you the worst thing anybody in this yard has asked anybody in two months," he said, "and I'll tell you what I want first, because you taught me that on the forty-third morning and I have not stopped using it."

"Go on."

"I want to end the fifth line."

---

The man who digs loam did not move at all, and neither did the spade against the leg of the table, and about nine feet above them on the boards a boy of seventeen stopped walking.

"Say that again," the man said.

"I want to end the fifth line. Not strike it. Nothing gets struck. Ended. Termed. 'So long as he agrees to it and says so on the day, and can be paid something.' Because a notice has come back down that lane wanting a term, and a term is the bit where somebody stops, and there is one thing in this district that can be stopped and it is that sentence, and it is the only reason this district cannot answer a page."

"And when it's ended," the man said, "what's got four men up that bank in fifty mornings?"

"Nothing."

"Nothing," the man agreed. "It'll be nothing and I know it's nothing, because I'm the one it fetches. You said that to me nine mornings ago and I said I'd rather be fetched than be findable, and you have been carrying that around ever since, and I can see it, and I would like it noted that I can see it."

"I'm not arguing that. I'm telling you what I have come to say and I am going to say all of it before you answer, because you gave me that and I am not going to cheat you out of it."

---

"The fifth of the five things this district does not have is not money," Adrian said.

The man at the far end of the table actually laughed. It was not a good laugh.

"That," he said, "is wrong, and you said the opposite of it in this yard four mornings ago and did not say it was the opposite, and I noticed, and I have been waiting to see whether you would come back down here and say it again."

"Then tell me."

"It is money," the man said, "and it is a line, and the line is the only reason the money has ever been in question, and you have got that round backwards and you have got it round backwards because you are a man who makes instruments and you cannot imagine a thing being true without something having been made. Come down here and look at it."

"I am down here."

"Then do not look at me, look at the book. That is the fifth line. It is a standing offer of labour at the use of any person who requires a man with nobody, at any hour, without end, without pay. That sentence is not a shortage of money. It is a shortage of a line." He put one hand flat on the boards. "If somebody had written a line down on that first afternoon, fifteen years ago, when I came and asked for one, and the line had said *and a figure*, then I would have been a man with a wage and you would not be standing here now with a problem. The money would have been the easy half. It has taken me fifteen years to work out that every single person in this district who is owed something is owed it because there is no place to stand the owing."

---

"All right," Adrian said. "That is better than what I came down here with, and I am not going to pretend otherwise in a yard. And I am still asking."

"You are still asking."

"Here is the trade and I am not dressing it up. A term is a bit of time with money on it and then it stops. There is exactly one thing in this district with no end on it, and it is that sentence, and if it had an end then this district could say a true thing about a person in a room, and I would be sending a name and a figure and a term to Alder Reach and all three of them would be true on a day. The fifth line is the term. You are the term."

The man at the far end of the second table did not say anything for about eleven seconds.

"No," he said.

---

He said it the way he had said it on the forty-second morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, when he had refused to let a count of four become five, and he said it to the boards and not to Adrian.

"No," he said. "And I want the second half of it out loud in this yard, and I have never asked for a thing in fifteen years and I am asking now, and I would like Mara Quill to come down that bank and put her pencil on those boards and write down what I am about to say, because saying it is the only part of it I can still choose, and I have been choosing for nine years, and it has not gone well."

She came down. She did not say anything when she got there, which is not her way, and Adrian noticed.

"Ten mornings ago I said no to a count," the man said, "and I told you all to keep the places at four, and let anybody who came down that lane find an empty shop, because an empty shop is the only thing I have got and I was going to use it. That was on the forty-second morning. I have thought about it every morning since and I have had nine full mornings to be pleased with myself and I have not managed it once."

He turned round.

"That was a good trick and it is the wrong trick, and it is wrong because it is a thing I did. I did it, in a yard, to about nineteen people, and a thing I did in a yard is a thing nine people heard, and nine people can be asked, and I have spent nine years making sure I could not be found and I did it by being extremely careful in front of a yard, which is the one place being careful does not work."

---

"So no," he said, "and you may write it down, and here is the whole of it and then I am going back to the end of my table. Ask me again in a year and I will give you a different answer and you can enter both, and I will not be able to un-say the first one, and that is what a term is and I have never had one."

That sentence on that book says *and the man*. It has said it for fifteen years and it is in a fair copy and it is recopied once a year and it is sent up a hill in rooms I have never been in, and every time it goes up that hill it arrives as *and the man*, and *and the man* is a door. There are about nine rooms in Alder Reach where a page like that gets read and there is a man in at least four of them who fills in the rest of it, and the rest gets filled in with whatever is nearest, and I have seen what the rest gets filled in with and I have been it.

"You cannot close a door by standing in front of it being careful. You can only close it by taking the door off the hinges and putting something else in the hole." He put his hand flat on the book. "If there is a line in that margin that says a man stood at the end of this table this morning and was asked for an end and said no, then that is not a door. That is a fact about a person. And a fact about a person can be asked about, and a man can be asked what he said no to and can answer it, and I have not been able to answer one question about myself in nine years and I would like to be able to answer one."

He looked up at Adrian.

"You have spent two months making this yard into a place where a man can be found. You did it on purpose, you did it out loud, and it has cost you a good many things and one of them was the only answer this district had left, and a woman of fifty-eight came and took it off you to your face. And you are standing at the end of my table asking me to be found, and you did not have the right to ask and I am going to say yes to it anyway, and I would like both of those things entered."

---

Mara Quill wrote in the margin of the fifth line, and not on a line, and she said so out loud before she did it.

"This is a margin," she said, "and not a sixth line, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five, and the count of the four conditions with no end on it is four, and the fifth of those five lines has an end on it now in the only sense that anybody in this district is ever going to be able to give one, which is that a person said out loud that it is not to be ended, and a person can be asked about that, and the day somebody asks is the day this yard has a thing it can be held to. I am entering it and I would like it read out at four."

She wrote four lines and then stopped and looked at them for a while.

"I am also entering," she said, "that I have been the clerk of that yard for fifteen years and that the fifth line is the only line in that book that has a man behind it, and that on the forty-second morning I found out that I had written it, and that this morning the man it is about asked me to write down that he had been asked for an end and had said no, and that between those two mornings I have stopped being a woman who copies a sentence and become a woman who keeps a record, and I did not decide to, and I have not got anybody to decide it with, and that is the entry and I would like somebody to tell me whether it is allowed."

---

The woman of fifty-eight was at the top of that bank at about four when it was read out and she had heard every word of it from the top of eleven feet, and she came down the bank afterwards, which she does not do.

"You told me on the thirty-eighth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month that there was one thing left this district could put up that lane and it was *we have nobody*," she said, "and that I had taken it off you, and that from then on if we send nobody up that hill somebody is going to ask why not, and they will ask it of you, and you will have to stand there and say *because I offered*."

"I remember."

"I want to say the next part," she said, "and I have been standing up there for an hour deciding whether to say it and I am going to. That is not an answer. A man who says no in a yard about a line he wrote himself when he was twenty-two is not an answer to a notice that wants a figure and a term, and anybody who tells you it is has found themselves a comfortable morning. And it is the first thing this district has had in fifteen years that is not an answer, and I have been waiting nine years for a thing in that yard that was not an answer, because a place that can only produce answers has stopped being able to say anything at all."

She picked her bag up off the boards.

"And I will tell you the price of it, because you are the only person in this yard who has ever asked me for the price of a thing, and because I am going to be asked for it again and I would rather set it now. He is findable from this morning. Not findable by the book. Findable by the fact that a woman of fifty-eight and a clerk and a boy of seventeen were all in a yard when he said it, and that a man with a cart was at the top of that bank the same afternoon. There is no instrument in this basin that will stop a person who has been in four yards, and in a few months somebody is going to use one, and he will not be the one who decides what it is for."

She held the strap with both hands and did not go on down the bank yet.

"And I am not going to tell him he was right to do it," the woman said, "because he is not the only one in this yard who has just made themselves findable this morning and I have watched the rest of you do it and I have not heard one of you ask the man with the cart whether he wanted to be twenty-one either. He did. He said so. He is the only one of the lot of you who has said the price out loud before he paid it, and I would like that noticed, and I would like the rest of you to go and notice the other things about yourselves, because I am sixty-one and I have carried for people for nine years and I have watched nine of you work out for yourselves that being found is a thing that happens to you."

She went down the bank. Nobody followed her.

The clerk stood at the near end of the boards with her hand on the cover and waited, which she has never had to do in fifteen years because there has never been a question in that yard that she had not already decided the answer to before it was asked.

"You asked whether it is allowed," the man at the end of the second table said. "It is not. You have entered a want against a man and you have done it on your own authority and about nine people in this yard have just watched you do it, and if a clerk can enter a want against a man then in about four years she can enter a figure against a man, and then where is this district."

"Then where is it," the clerk said.

"Nowhere that I can find from this table," the man said, "and I have been standing at this table for fifteen years and I know where everything in this district is."

---

Adrian went back up the bank at about half past four and sat down on the boards of the first table, and found that he had nothing at all to do with his hands, which had not happened to him in nine years.

The notice wants a name and a figure and a term. The name is in a column and it cannot come out. The figure is forty coppers for a year and a man stood behind it in a yard and cannot pay it. And the third one — the term, the bit where somebody stops — was the only one of the three he had ever believed he could answer, and it turns out that answering it means going down a bank and asking nineteen people in a yard whether they consent to the year after the year, and there is no instrument in this district that can ask nineteen people anything, and the number of documents this district does not own is four, and the count of the five things it does not have is five, and the fifth of them is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and it is not paid, and a margin on a book is not a payment, and a man saying no in a yard is not a payment, and the bid is open at four hundred and sixteen days and was not run, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven.
