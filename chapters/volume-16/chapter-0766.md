# Chapter 766: The Fiftieth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month Adrian Asked The Woman Of Fifty-Eight The Question He Had Promised To Ask And A Boy Of Seventeen Asked A Man Of Fifty-Six For Something In A Yard And Got It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and did not move. Five hundred and five days is the age of that figure. The clerk entered the two of them on two lines, in that order, and has entered them on two lines every morning for fifteen years and will enter them on two lines for as long as there is a sheet at that gatepost.

The board carries six hundred and sixty-four. The train on that siding has stood nine hundred and eighty days. Nobody has entered anything for six hundred and ninety-four days. Six hundred and fifty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fourteen days is how long the bid has been open, and it was not run, and nothing was proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Three hundred and sixty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is, and nothing correct has been written beside it. Three hundred and seventy-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and forty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and forty-seven days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and eighty-three days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and twenty-three marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and it did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four and it did not move, and none of the four has ever been one of the things that would pay a person. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and it did not move, and it stays four, and the fifth would be a shop counter, and the man who would have been found by it put his no in the book and was right to. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and it did not move this morning, and it is four and not five. The count of the five things this district does not have is five, and none of the four documents has ever been one of the things that would pay a person.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank eighty mornings, and the near rail has stood on the near side of it for thirty mornings, and the count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is fifty, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the four hundred and ninety-first of those mornings.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall with his coat folded on the stones beside him and nothing in his hands, on his four hundred and fifth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and four of them, and on the morning after that one he was asked a question, and on the morning after that he was not asked anything at all, and a clerk has a line about it in a book of her own and no line in the lot book.

---

There was nothing on at ten for the first time in ten mornings and Adrian knew it before he got to the top of the bank, because the boy had arranged it.

That was the arrangement. On the forty-ninth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, at about four, a boy of seventeen had come into a shop at the top of that bank and asked the woman who keeps a scale to shut her counter for an hour, and had then gone down the hill and told about nine people one at a time, separately, that if anybody had anything to say to Adrian Vale then today was the morning to say it, because there was nothing on.

He had counted the yard. He had looked at the boards at about a quarter to eight and worked out from the way people were standing that no page was going to be read, no figure was going to be said, and no stranger was coming, and he had gone round nine people in about forty minutes and had not told one of them why.

The woman of fifty-eight was at the foot of that bank at about ten with her bag on the boards, as she is every working morning, and Adrian came down and stood about nine feet off the near end of the second table, which is where he stops.

"You said to ask on a morning when there's nothing on."

"Aye."

"There isn't anything on."

"I know there isn't," she said. "That's why I said it and not you. You would have asked me on the thirty-ninth morning and I would have said no, and you would have gone away being careful at me, and I have had four mornings of you being careful at me and I would rather be asked properly or not at all."

---

"You carry for a woman who went to Alder Reach four months back."

The woman of fifty-eight did not move and neither did anything else about her, and Adrian, who had been in four hundred rooms where a sentence landed, understood that this one had landed.

"I did say I would."

"You did, and I am asking, and I am telling you what I am going to do with the answer before you give it, because I owe you that and nobody in this yard has done it except once and it went wrong."

"Go on."

"Whatever you tell me stays in a room or it goes into the air. If it goes into the air it is a thing nine people heard and I will not be the one who decides that, you will. And if it goes in a book I will not put it there myself, and I have a pencil and there is no instrument in this district to put a person's private business into, and I have known that for two months and I have spent nine years pretending I did not." He put his hands where she could see them, which was an old habit from a life spent standing in front of frightened rooms. "That is the whole of what I have got and I am aware it is not very much."

"You have also got a page in Alder Reach with your name on it and forty coppers on the back of it," the woman of fifty-eight said, "and you stood in a yard yesterday and said *as sent*, and the yard is going to be full of that for about nine mornings. So go on. Ask."

---

"Her name is Nell Cray," the woman of fifty-eight said.

She said it out loud at the foot of a bank at about ten on the fiftieth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, and Adrian understood, before he had got to the end of the second line, that the woman had picked a name out of every name in the basin because it was the plainest one she could say out loud and because she had known, before he had, that saying it in a room with a clerk in it was the same as saying it in a yard.

"I know that name is in about four places in this basin and that I have never once heard it said in this yard," Adrian said, carefully, "and I am not going to pretend I have, and I am not going to ask you how many."

"I know how many places. I carry for her twice a week and I have never once said her name in a room where there is writing, and I have not said it to you on any of those occasions either, and I have been carrying for her about nine years." She set the bag down. "So listen, because I have thought about how to say this for four months and I have not said it and it has gone bad in the bag with the rest of it."

---

"She went four months back," the woman said. "She got on a cart at about two in the morning with a bag and a coat and she did not tell me where she was going, and I did not ask her, because I do not ask. And I want you to understand that I did not ask her because not asking is the whole of what I am good at, and because she has come to me about nine hundred times and has never once wanted a question, and because I have asked her about forty other people over nine years and got nine answers, and I have never once asked her about herself and I was proud of that."

Adrian did not say anything. He had learned four days ago what it costs to break into this and he had been taught it by a woman of about thirty-six in a shop.

"She came back on the second morning. She came back and she sat on my step and she said, *I've been*, and I said *aye*, and she said nothing else, and I did not ask her what for, and she has not told me since, and that is four months." The woman of fifty-eight's hands were flat on the boards and she was not looking at him. "I have carried for a woman for four months who went somewhere and came back and could not tell me one word about it, and she could have told me one word. Not all of it. One word. And she did not, because I am the woman she does not say things to, and I made her that about nine years ago by never once giving her a reason to."

---

"Did she say it out loud to you on that step, or did she say it to a person she did not have to look at," Adrian said, after a while.

"She said it to a step," the woman of fifty-eight said. "That is what a person does when they have said a thing out loud to somebody and have got nothing for it and are not going to say it twice to a person who has already not asked."

"Yes."

"Aye." She picked her bag up. "Now. I have said it and you have not written it, and I looked while you were not writing, and you did not move your hands, and that is the first time in four months that anybody has done anything about it and I have not got a thing to give you for it and I am not going to pretend I have."

---

And then, at about eleven, the boy asked.

He had been standing at the top of the eleven feet for about an hour with the sheet under his arm and everybody in that yard had known he was going to do something and had decided, in about nine separate conversations that morning, to let him get on with it.

Orren Pike came down off the boards and stood in front of the man of fifty-six at about eleven feet off the wall, and the man of fifty-six turned round, and about nineteen people in that yard worked out at the same moment what was happening and about nine of them looked at Adrian and Adrian looked at the ground.

"Mr Sarr isn't a woman of fifty-eight," the boy said.

"Orren—"

"I'm not asking you anything about the wall." The boy had the sheet against his chest and he was not using it as a shield, which Adrian could tell, because a boy who is using a sheet as a shield holds it in front of his face. "I'm asking you a different thing and you asked for it, and you asked for it in front of about nine people on the forty-fourth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, and you said you did not want a man with a name in a book to do it, and you were right, and you said *whoever's here*, and here I am, and I am seventeen, and I have never been anything else in this yard."

The man of fifty-six looked at him.

"I want to ask you one question and I want to ask you in a yard with about nineteen people in it, out loud, so that it is not yours," the boy said, "and you said it should not be my document. It is not my document if I ask it here. It is nineteen people's question."

---

"Go on, then," said the man of fifty-six.

"There is one of the four that is doing something you cannot describe," Orren said. "You have said that out loud once in a yard and you said you could not tell anybody which one, and you said you would not guess in a yard, and I have thought about that for eleven mornings and I have come to the conclusion that you were half right, and that the half you were wrong about is the part where you thought saying it out loud made it cheaper."

"That is a very bold thing to say to a man of fifty-six before lunch."

"I know what it is."

The man of fifty-six took his hand off his pocket and looked at the wall, and did not say anything for about eleven seconds, and Adrian counted them, because he was that kind of man and because he had nothing else to do.

"You are going to ask me which one," the man said.

"No."

"Then what."

"I'm going to ask you what you want to happen now that about nine people know," the boy said, "and then I am going to write down what you say on this page, and I am not going to enter it in the book, and I am going to read it out in this yard myself, and it is going to be the one thing on my page that I have never read out and everybody in this yard is going to hear it at the same time as I write it, and there will be about nine people who can say they were here."

The man of fifty-six looked at him for a long moment.

"You have been at this four years," he said.

"Eleven months of the page and four years of the counting."

"Then you have it exactly wrong and you have got it exactly wrong in the way that people get things exactly wrong when they have never once had to hold a thing for four hundred and ninety mornings." He turned back to the wall, and he took about two seconds longer over it than he needed to, and Adrian saw him do it. "I do not want anything to happen. I want it to go on being four figures that a man says out loud twice a morning, because the day it becomes a thing that is being *looked into* is the day this yard has a subject, and a yard with a subject is a yard where the man who is being looked into is the only person in the room who is not allowed to leave."

He turned round again.

"And that is the answer, and it cost me about four hundred and ninety-one mornings to be able to say it in a yard without my hands going, and you have just taken the cheapest thing I own and put it in a boy's pocket in front of a yard, and I would like you to understand that I am not angry, and that I am going to be frightened for about a year."

Then he said the four figures off that wall, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and got all four of them, and about nine people heard them, and the man who reads that wall has now been read alongside it.

---

Orren wrote it down in the margin of a page that is not a record of anybody, and read it out in that yard at about half past eleven in about ninety words, and did not put it in the book, because the rule he had argued for on the forty-fourth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month forbids exactly that, and he said so, in the yard, when he had finished, and about four people said *fair* and nobody said anything else.

The woman of fifty-eight had gone up the bank at about a quarter to eleven and had not stayed, and Adrian had not asked her to stay, and that had been the only available move and it had cost him the whole of the rest of the morning.

The name is not in the lot book. The name is not in the clerk's own page, and it is on no page in this district, and the woman of fifty-eight said it once at the foot of a bank and it is therefore in the air, and there is no instrument in this district that can hold a name in the air, and there is not one in the basin either, and the count of counted months is six and did not move and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and it is not paid.

A page came back down that lane yesterday with a figure on the back of it and a term and two words, and a man with a cart was counted as twenty-one in front of about eleven people because a boy got to a figure before a clerk did, and the bid is open at four hundred and fourteen days and was not run, and the figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and a woman of fifty-eight carried a bag down that bank this morning with a cloth in it and has done for fifteen years and will not be able to say why for another year.
