# Chapter 774: The Fifty-Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Woman Of Fifty-Eight Said The Price Before She Said Yes And Then Named Nine People Out Loud And One Of The Nine Was Not In The District

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven, and it is not on any page in this district. Five hundred and thirteen days is the age of that figure, and the two of them are on two lines in a clerk's book every morning and have never once been in a yard.

The board carries six hundred and seventy-two, and the train on that siding has stood nine hundred and eighty-eight days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and two days. Six hundred and sixty-three days separate the second of January and this morning, and there is no month in this district that anybody has counted since a clerk of nineteen counted one off a wall in about ten minutes in front of about nineteen people.

Four hundred and twenty-two days is how long the bid has been open and it was not run. Three hundred and seventy-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Three hundred and eighty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and fifty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and fifty-five days back. A body four hundred miles off is three hundred and ninety-one days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and thirty-one marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and seventeen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and it did not move, and none of the six is this one. The number of documents this district does not own is four, and it did not move either, and a page is coming up that lane in three or four days that would make it five if anybody in this district were the sort of place that counted what it was given. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four and did not move, and a man at the top of that hill said on the fifty-seventh morning that he would send a page up that lane inside a week, and it has not come, and it is not counted and it is not a place. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and there is a clause nine miles up that hill with no end on it and it is on no page of this district, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank eighty-eight mornings and moved this morning from eighty-seven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for thirty-eight mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is fifty-eight, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered, and the man who keeps a tally said it twice this morning because a man of about forty-three was standing at the top of the eleven feet and had asked him how he knew. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move, and it did not move this morning before eleven and it did not move after eleven either, and that has been the case since a map was drawn on the back of a stranger's page with a hole in it. Nothing has come down that lane with a page in his hand since the twenty-first of them, and the lane count is twenty-one and still is.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the four hundred and ninety-ninth of those mornings.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and thirteenth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and twelve of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him and had said one whole sentence to him yesterday afternoon and nothing since.

---

She was on the boards of the first table at ten with her bag at her feet, the way she is there about nine mornings out of ten, and Adrian had been at the bottom of the bank since about half past seven because he had decided to be there before her rather than after.

She looked at him for about as long as it takes to put a bag down.

"Mavis," he said.

Four people in that yard heard it. She had given her name in this yard at about ten to six on the fifty-fourth morning and it was on the back of a clerk's book and had been said in about nine mouths, and this was the first time anybody had used it at the top of that bank.

"You have been practising that," she said.

"I have."

---

"I am going to say the thing that is wrong with it before you ask me, because you have been telling me what you will do with an answer since the fiftieth morning and I have never once heard you say what is wrong with the question."

"Go on."

"The term on that page does not ask me whether eighteen people consent. It says *renewable only by fresh consent of the persons the market represents*." She had the strap of the bag in both hands and she was not taking her coat off. "That is a different thing. It does not ask me. It asks who *we* is."

"You wrote it."

"You wrote *we*," she said. "Seven times, and *our* twice, and I told you a district is easier to be than a man and you went up that bank and read it out loud anyway, and it came back down with a figure on the back of it and two words in a cold hand. So the question is not whether eighteen people will agree to a year. The question is what *we* is, and there is no *we* in this district, and there has not been one for nine years, and the only list of the people in this basin who are not on anything is in this bag and has been in this bag for nine years and I have never taken it out in a yard."

---

Adrian put his hands where she could see them.

"Tell me the price," he said. "Before you say yes. Not after."

"I have been waiting eight mornings for you to say that sentence to me in those words," said the woman of fifty-eight. "All right. The price."

She sat down on the boards, which she did not do.

"If I say who *we* is in a yard, then everybody who hears it can be asked about it afterwards, and that is the only thing this yard has ever done for anybody. You have all spent fourteen days learning it and I have spent nine years knowing it, and six people in this district are now findable who were not, and one of them is me, because I said my own name out loud on this board, and one of them is a man at the end of that table who spent nine years making himself unfindable and who decided in the end that a line was worth more than a favour, and he was right, and I am the reason he had to decide it."

"I know."

"Then here is the rest of it." She took her hands off the strap. "There are nine people in this basin who hold one copper each of this district's, and I could name all nine, and the clerk can name all nine, and neither of us has ever said a name. If I say the nine, then the district's money has nine owners in about nine mouths instead of none, and a stranger who comes up that hill with a piece of paper can say the name of any one of them and be believed by a man who has been told by a woman who carries for people."

---

"And one of them is not in this district," Adrian said.

"One of them is two miles away and has not been in this yard in nine years," said the woman of fifty-eight, "and I am not going to say her name in this yard this morning, and I am telling you why, and then I am going to say her name, and I would like you to notice that those are two different things I have said back to back."

"Mavis—"

"I told you I would tell you the price before I said yes. I have not said yes." She stood up off the boards. "I have said no. And here is why. There is a note in a corridor in a building nine miles up that hill with a name in it, on a working list of forty-one, and the fourth line says *ask the market that sent her and you will be told she was never there*. This market sent nobody. And I have carried for that woman about twice a week for nine years and I have never sent her anywhere, and four months back she went somewhere on her own and came back and told me one word about it on my own step."

---

Adrian did not say anything.

"So you are not going to be *we*," he said.

"No."

"Then who is."

The woman of fifty-eight looked at the near end of the first table and then at the eleven feet of bank going down to a second table with a rail on it, and then at about nineteen people who had all stopped what they were doing, and every one of them had stopped it differently, and she had watched about nine years of them not do that.

"I am a list," she said. "I want that said out loud in this yard before I say anything else, and I want it said by me and not by you, because if you say it then it is a thing a man from Alder Reach did to a woman of fifty-eight, and if I say it then it is a thing a woman of fifty-eight did to herself with a witness, and that is the only difference there is and it is the whole of the difference."

She turned round to the yard.

---

She said it at about eleven with the sun behind her and about nineteen people standing about, and she said it the way a woman with a scale reads a weight out, without putting anything of her own into it.

"I am not a market," she said. "There is no market in this district and there is not going to be one this morning. I carry for people who are not on anything and there are nine of them in this basin who hold one copper each of this district's money, and I have carried for all nine, and here are their names, and I am going to say them once, out loud, in this yard, and I am not going to say them again for anybody, and any person who wants to ask me about one of them after this morning can ask me, and I will answer, and that is the arrangement and I have made it and I am not being asked into it."

She said eight of them quickly, in the flat voice of a woman reading out a list of things she has carried a great many times and could carry again tomorrow, and not one of the eight people in that yard wrote any of them down.

And then she stopped for about the length of a breath, and Adrian understood what the pause was, and the whole yard understood it at the same time and looked at her.

"The ninth is Nell Cray," said the woman of fifty-eight. "Two miles. Working list forty-one in a building nine miles up that hill, on a page a man took out of a corridor, and she has never been in this yard in nine years and she was not asked about anything and she does not know that her name is on a piece of paper about four claims in this basin, and I have now said it in this yard for the third time in ten mornings and it is on no page of this district and there is no instrument in this basin that can hold a name that is only in the air."

---

The clerk had her book shut in front of her and did not open it.

"I am not entering that," she said.

"You are entering the fact and the reason and you are not entering the names," said the woman of fifty-eight, "and I know the difference and I am not going to insult you by pretending I do not."

"There is a page with nothing ruled on it in this book," said Mara Quill, "and I have put two lines on it in eleven mornings, one about a man at the foot of a wall and one about the name a woman of fifty-eight gave in this yard on the fifty-fourth morning, and neither of them is in the lot book, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and I entered that number last each time and not the number of lines on my own page, because those are not the same thing and I have been wrong about that once already."

She opened the book to the last page and wrote until the light went off the boards and came back on them, and about four people who had been waiting for her to finish went and did something else.

> *Entered on the fifty-eighth morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, at about eleven, by M. Quill. Nine names were said out loud in this yard by a person of this district at the word of the person it is about, and no name goes in. Entered that one of the nine is not in this district and has not been asked about anything and cannot be, and entered that the reason given for that, out loud, is a page taken out of a corridor in Alder Reach on which her name stands four times. Entered that a person who carries for people who are not on anything has said in this yard that she is a list and not a market. Entered that I am not entering the names because I entered a man's name in that column on the thirty-ninth morning at the word of a boy of seventeen who asked me to look for a place to put one, it was not mine, and I have not been able to put it down in fifteen mornings, and I do not wish to be a woman who enters names because it is easier at eleven o'clock than at nine.*
>
> *Entered that this is a want and not a reason, and that I have now entered two of them in eleven mornings and that a man at the end of a table told me on the fifty-second morning what happens to a clerk who enters wants against people, and that I have entered this one against nobody, because there is nobody to enter it against but me.*

She read it out, because that is what she does now.

---

And then the woman of fifty-eight turned round and looked at Adrian, who had been standing about nine feet off the boards with his hands at his sides for about a quarter of an hour and had not said one word.

"Now you know what it cost," she said. "And I would like to hear you work out whether it was me."

"Mavis."

"No. I am not going to let that go by, and I have said your name once today and it is the first time in nine years that anybody has used it here." She came down off the boards and stood in front of him, which she had not done in nine years of knowing him. "You did not ask me to name nine people. You asked me a question and I answered it. That is a thing people in rooms have been *not* doing for nine years in this basin and you came down this hill and made me do it in a yard in about eleven minutes. You want to be careful about how you carry that."

---

"You told me on the fiftieth morning that you would not write her name on a page," Adrian said.

"I did."

"And you have now said it three times in a yard and put nine more beside it, and one of the nine is not in the district and cannot be asked."

"Yes," said the woman of fifty-eight. "And I would like it noticed that the reason I could do it is nine years of practice at doing it for people who never knew, and that I have never once been paid for it, and that nobody in this district knew that was happening, and that a man with a length of fencing wire in his pocket has been the only person in about two months who has been able to see me do it."

---

The two of them stood at the foot of that bank in front of about nineteen people for a while, and then she picked her bag up and said the thing that the whole morning had been walking towards.

"The term cannot be answered by the nine," she said. "It cannot be answered by the one who is not here. It can be answered by the other eighteen, if they will, and they will not all, and it will take about nine years, one at a time, in rooms, which is what I told you the instrument was."

"So we send nothing."

"We send something," said the woman of fifty-eight, "and I do not know what it is worth, and I am the only one of the three of us who does not know."

---

"And what is it."

"That we cannot ask everybody," she said. "That we have said out loud in a yard, in front of about nineteen people, that one of the nineteen is not in this district and has not been asked and cannot be found by anybody who comes up that lane, and that we are answering with the eighteen we can reach and saying so first." She stopped. "And I am not going to tell you in this yard that it is the right answer, because I do not know, and I have been the person in this district who knows for nine years and I am not going to do it in a voice."

Adrian looked at the eleven feet of bank.

"That is a page saying *we cannot ask everybody*," he said.

"Yes," said the woman of fifty-eight. "And a man nine miles up that hill is going to read it and put it in a column called markets that cannot answer, and there will be about nine other markets in this basin that cannot answer either and none of them will have said so, and in about four years somebody will use that column against about forty households who have never heard of it. And I am going to write it anyway, and I would like it noticed that writing it and sending it are two different things and only one of them is in my hands. Because the alternative is forty coppers, and the figure of forty coppers was said out loud in this yard by a man who cannot pay it, and I have watched that figure go into about nineteen mouths in seven mornings, and a market that cannot say what it cannot do is a market that somebody else decides for."

---

The man who drives the cart was at the top of that bank at about half past four with his hand on the tail of it, and he said no.

He said it in the ordinary voice and he did not soften it and he did not add anything to it, and every person in that yard heard a man decline a piece of work, which had not happened in nine years, and four of them thought he was going to say something else and he did not.

"Not because of the nine," he said. "Because of what I am. That boy made me twenty-one on the forty-ninth morning and he did it out loud in front of you lot before she could write it, and the rule was the rule and I was right to stand still for a minute and a half. If I take that page up that hill this afternoon then up that hill there is a district with a figure in it, and a figure needs a man, and the man will be me, and I have had a name in about nineteen mouths since the forty-ninth morning and there is not room in it for a second one."

He put his hand off the tail of the cart.

"I will take a man's page up that lane," he said. "I will not take the market's."

Nobody went up the lane that afternoon. The page went on the boards of the second table at the foot of that bank with a stone on the corner of it, because the wind comes up that hill at about five and there is nothing to hold a page down at the bottom of a bank, and it was still there the next morning and the morning after that, and people walked past it and read the first line and did not touch it.

The count of things this district has made is fourteen and it did not move. The lane count is twenty-one and the yard watched a carrier decline a page. The clerk entered the number of the five things this district does not have and it is five, and the fifth of those five lines is a way to pay a person who is not in a household, and it is not paid, and a page with nine names on it that were not written on it has been lying on the boards of the second table at the foot of that bank since about half past four on the fifty-eighth morning with a stone on the corner of it and nobody in this district has an instrument for carrying it up a hill.
