# Chapter 798: The Eighty-Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of Thirty-Eight Asked Out Loud In A Yard In Front Of About Nineteen People To Be Entered In The Column For The Name Of Whoever Read A Thing Out Loud And A Clerk Said No And Gave The Reason And Entered The Asking Somewhere Else

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and thirty-seven days is the age of that figure.

The board carries six hundred and ninety-six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and twelve days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and twenty-six days. Six hundred and eighty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and forty-six days is how long the bid has been open and it was not run, and nothing has been proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Four hundred and one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and seventy-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and seventy-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and fifteen days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and fifty-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and forty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man asked this morning for the one thing that would make him a person on a page in that book and a clerk said no and entered the asking on a page with no heading on it, and the count is still five.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and twelve mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and eleven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for sixty-two mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is eighty-two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-three and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the name in it is the name of the man who asked to be put in it this morning, and it was entered on the thirty-ninth morning, which is forty-three mornings back, at the direction of that yard.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and twenty-third of those mornings, and nobody in that yard told him that anything had been said about his four figures in the last twenty mornings because there has not been, and he has not asked.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and thirty-seventh night of that run, having slept on four hundred and thirty-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the twenty-seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and for as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures neither of them said anything else, and neither of them has ever referred to it since.

---

Adrian had it in his mouth at about half past ten and let it sit there until about eleven, which is longer than he has ever sat on anything in this yard.

"I want to be entered in the column," he said. "The one for whoever read a thing out loud. My name is already in it and I would like to be in it for the reason you have."

---

"Read the column out," said Mara Quill.

---

She did not open the book to do it and she did not need to, and that was the first thing that made about four people in that yard sit down.

"It says *Adrian Vale. Four figures off that wall, this morning, and nine lines of a stranger's page, at about eleven. At the direction of the yard. Not appointed to anything.*" She shut it. "Entered on the thirty-ninth morning by me, in my hand, at the word of a boy of seventeen who stood on those boards and asked me to find a place to put a name, and his name was not the one I wrote. There has been nothing else in it since. The heading over it says whoever."

---

"That is my name on a page and it is not me on a page," said Adrian.

---

"That is exactly what it is and I have been saying so out loud in that yard for eight mornings and you have not once asked me the question that would settle it, and this morning you have, and I am going to answer it, and I am going to answer it in front of about nineteen people, and I am not going to do it kindly, and I want that understood before I start."

---

"Go on."

---

"I am not going to enter your asking there." She had her hand flat on the cover and did not open it. "There are two reasons and the second one is the one that matters, and I have never had a reason in my hand before in fifteen years, so I am going to be careful with it. The first reason is arithmetic. That column holds one figure and the figure is your name and a figure in this district may not be corrected, struck, replaced, superseded or taken out, and I said that out loud in that yard eight mornings ago and about nine people heard me say it, and I am not going to do a thing today that I told this yard I could not do. To enter your asking under your name would be a second line against a figure. To do it instead of your name would be to take your name out. There is no third thing and I have looked."

---

"And the second one."

---

"The heading says *whoever read a thing out loud*." She picked the book up off the boards and held it the way you hold something when you are about to open it in front of witnesses and then did not open it. "And the rule made in this yard fourteen mornings ago says that when a person asks for themselves, in a yard, in front of people, what is written down is the asking and not the reading. So if I enter your asking in that column this morning, then from this morning that column stops being a column for the name of whoever read a thing out loud, and becomes a column for people who have asked. And the heading will be lying, because it is ruled about the reading and it will have been filled about the asking, and every person who comes along in about four years and reads that heading and looks at that name will conclude that a man read something out loud and wanted to be recorded, and both of those will be true, and the heading will be the only false thing about it, and that is how you get a book that is careful and hides."

---

Nobody in that yard said anything.

---

"So no," said Mara Quill.

---

"Thank you," said Adrian.

---

"Do not thank me yet. Enter it," she said, and opened the book to the page at the back with nothing ruled on it, and there were seven lines on that page and none of the seven was in the lot book. "There are eight of them now. What is written down here is the asking and not the reading, exactly as the rule says, and this page has no heading, because I ruled it myself about nine years ago and I have never once put a heading on anything I wrote myself."

She wrote it and read it out before she wrote it, in the ordinary voice, and it was the first thing she had entered in fifteen years at the word of a person about themselves that she had entered in the same breath with a no.

*Entered on the eighty-second morning after the thirtieth of the eighth month, at about eleven, by M. Quill. That a man asked, out loud, in this yard, in about nineteen people's hearing, on his own asking and not at anybody's word, to be entered in the column in this book for whoever read a thing out loud, and that his name is already in that column, entered on the thirty-ninth morning at the word of a boy of seventeen and not at his own, and that I said no, and that the reason I gave was that the heading over that column is about the reading and the rule made on the sixty-eighth morning is about the asking, and that a column which has been filled according to the asking does not answer to the heading over it, and that I have been the clerk of this book for fifteen years and I am not able to make a heading true.*

---

"That is the whole of it," she said, "and I have read it out and I want the yard to notice that the last sentence is about me and not about him, and that it is the first time in fifteen years that I have entered a thing that was against me and in my own hand at the word of nobody."

---

"It is against you," said the man who digs loam.

---

"It is against me," she agreed, "and I entered it anyway, and in about four years the reason I entered it will be invisible and the sentence will read like a rule, and that is the whole of how a book gets to be a thing that hides, and I have just done it, in a yard, about nine people watching."

---

Adrian stood at the far end of the boards with his hands at his sides.

"I asked to find out one thing this morning," he said, "and I have found out two, and neither of them was what I came up that lane for." He was not looking at the clerk and he was looking at the column. "I asked whether a rule about who may go in a column works. It works. It is the best rule anybody in this basin has made about anything in about nine years and a man made it in the light before anybody could sit down and it is perfect. And I have found out that the rule has never once been tested, because there was only ever one name in there to begin with and it went in before the rule existed."

---

"So the rule has never been tested," said the man at the end of the second table.

---

"By about fourteen mornings," said Adrian. "And it is going to be tested today, because I asked in the ordinary voice in front of about nineteen people, which is what the rule requires, and what the rule requires is a person who is willing to do that. There is one of me. That is the whole of the supply."

---

The man at the end of the second table came about four feet along the boards to the place where the light was, which is a thing he does.

"Then the rule is not the problem," he said, "and I would like to know what is, and I have got about four years and it is about time I started on it, and you are the only person in that yard who has just found something out and I am not going to waste it."

---

"I do not know yet," said Adrian, "and I have got about two mornings of it, and I am not going to tell you that I have got more than that, because I have spent a hundred and three mornings in this yard being careful about exactly this."

---

"Two is fine," said the man at the end of the second table. "It is more than I have had since about the ninth month of the year this table was set up."

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and nobody in that yard said *as sent*, and about four of them noticed that nobody had.

Orren Pike wrote nothing at all for about half of that morning, which everybody in that yard noticed, and then at the end of it he turned the sheet over and wrote two lines under the rule and did not read them out to anybody. One of the two lines said that a thing had been said out loud in that yard and entered twice, once in a column with a heading over it and once on a page with nothing on it, and the other line said that he had not known which of the two was the mistake and was not going to find out.
