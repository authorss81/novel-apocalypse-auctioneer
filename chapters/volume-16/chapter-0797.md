# Chapter 797: The Eighty-First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Stranger Came Down That Lane With A Page In Her Right Hand And A Boy Of Seventeen Said Twenty-Three Out Loud Before The Clerk's Hand Moved And The Notice Named The Officer Who Filed The Application And Not The Person It Burdens

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and thirty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries six hundred and ninety-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and eleven days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and twenty-five days. Six hundred and eighty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and forty-five days is how long the bid has been open and it was not run, and nothing has been proposed about closing it in a mouth or in a page. Four hundred days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Four hundred and seventy-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is five hundred and seventy-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and fourteen days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and fifty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and forty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a notice came down that lane this morning that is a record of a thing with a person's name in it and it did not pay the fifth of the five and it is not going to.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and eleven mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and ten. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for sixty-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is eighty-one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move, and a page came down that lane this morning and a page a stranger carried in from outside this basin is not a thing four people made. The lane count is twenty-three, and it moved this morning for the first time since the sixty-fifth morning, and it moved because somebody came down that hill with a page and a boy of seventeen said the figure out loud before the clerk had the book open. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the clerk had the cover flat under her forearm and had not lifted it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and twenty-second of those mornings, and he was standing about eleven feet off that wall when a stranger came down the lane at about half past two and he heard about nine people at the top of the bank say a figure before the clerk said anything at all.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and thirty-sixth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and thirty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the twenty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and neither of them has any idea what came down that lane at half past two and neither of them would have wanted to know.

---

The stranger came down that lane on foot with a page in her right hand and she came down it fast, the way people come down a hill they have just been told has a time on it.

Orren Pike said it from the boards before Mara Quill had the book open.

"Twenty-three."

He said it out loud, in the ordinary voice, and about nine people in that yard turned, and the clerk's hand was still on the cover of the lot book.

---

"It moved," said the woman who keeps a scale, from about forty feet off, without coming up the bank. "I have been standing on this step since the seventy-sixth morning and I have watched that figure every morning and it has not moved since a woman came down that hill on foot with a page in her hand sixteen mornings ago. It has moved and I would like the yard to mark the morning."

---

"Twenty-three," said the clerk. "And no name."

---

"No name," said the boy of seventeen. "The count takes no name. That is not my rule, it is the rule of the count, and I did not invent it and I cannot change it, and if I could I would not, because the figure would still move."

---

The stranger came up the eleven feet and stood at the near end of the boards and put the page down on them, face up, the way people put a thing down when they have been holding it for four miles.

"My name is not on it and you are not going to get it," she said. "I am a hand and I am here because a hand has to be somewhere, and I would rather be a hand on a hill than a name on a page this week."

---

"Read it," said Sabra Holt. "Out loud, in this yard, in about nineteen people's hearing, and I am saying that first because I have been telling people in this yard, for as long as I have been in it, that the better instrument is a reading, and this morning the better instrument has come down a hill and is worse than a reading, and I would like that said."

---

The stranger read it. It is short. It has a heading that is about a thing and not about a person, and the first line of it is a thing, and it says that an application has been received in respect of a district, and it says that the application cannot be entertained at the sitting without a named bearer who can be paid for a term, and it gives a form of words for that.

Then it goes to the officer.

It describes her as the person who made the application, and it gives her age, and it gives the fact that she is an appointed administrator of nineteen years, and it gives that she holds the third of the nine oldest keys, and it gives that she has stated in writing that the district cannot produce a person.

Nobody in that yard made a sound.

---

"Say it again," said Sabra Holt.

---

"There is no part of it I can say again better."

---

"Say the part about the officer again."

The stranger read it again, in the ordinary voice, at the top of eleven feet, in the sun, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the words *third of the nine oldest keys* come out of a stranger's mouth for the second time inside the length of a page being read twice.

---

Sabra Holt did not say anything for a while, and when she spoke it was to Adrian and not to the yard, and it was the first thing she had said to him directly in sixteen mornings.

"I have been an appointed administrator for nineteen years," she said. "I have signed things. I have held the third of the nine oldest keys since the year after the one you would have been a grown man in. I have never once been named in a page in that corridor. Not in nineteen years. There is a person there who writes the headings and there has been a person there for all nineteen years and none of them has ever had to write my name because nobody has ever filed anything with me as the officer."

---

"You filed one," said Adrian.

---

"I filed one on the first morning I came up this hill that I did not know I was filing." She was not looking at him. "That is the whole of what I did and it took the time it takes to write a name at the foot of a form, and I have spent seven mornings carrying four things up that hill and refusing a fifth, and this morning the hill has sent something back with my name at the bottom of it in a stranger's hand."

---

"You could complain about it," said the man who drives the cart, from the end of the second table. He had not said a whole sentence in that yard in seven mornings, which is the whole of his trade, and everybody standing near him this morning had noticed it before he had said anything.

---

"I could put my hand up and say *that is not a thing about me, that is a thing about the district*. And the room would agree with me, and it would be right to agree with me, and it would look at the rest of the page and see that it does not contain one single name of anybody in this district, and it would decide that the absence is a fact about this district and not about me, and I would have spent nineteen years of being invisible in one morning and I would have bought it with the only thing I have ever wanted, which is to be findable."

---

"Then it stays," said the man of fifty-six, from about eleven feet off the wall. "It stays and it is read out and nobody in that room gets to look at your face while they read it."

---

"Yes," said Sabra Holt.

---

"Do you understand what has just happened to you," said Adrian, "in terms I can carry up that hill."

---

"No. I have understood what has happened to this district and I have got quite good at that in sixteen mornings and it is not the same thing at all and I have spent the whole of today failing to do the other one." She took the page off the boards and folded it once, which is what people do with paper they are going to keep. "I am going to say one thing in that room about that page. One. And I am not going to say it in this yard, and I am telling this yard that it exists and nothing else, and if anybody in this yard follows me up that hill to hear it then the thing I have spent this whole run refusing to do will have been done in the time it takes to write a figure down by about four people."

---

"I will come up," said Adrian.

---

"You will not."

---

"I have been in this yard a hundred and two mornings and I have never been in that room and there is not one instrument in this basin that can do what I would like done this morning, which is stand next to a woman with a key while somebody reads a page out loud."

"You cannot stand next to me," said Sabra Holt. "You are a man of thirty-eight in a district that cannot produce a person. If you walk into that room there is not one thing in it about the district any more and there is a man in it about himself, and in about four years that is the only sentence anybody will remember, and I have had it written for me twice this morning already and I would like to stop."

---

Adrian did not answer that and it was the first time in sixteen mornings that he had not answered her and about four people in that yard noticed and about nine did not.

---

"The fifth of the five," said the man at the end of the second table, to nobody, "and it took a page from outside this basin to say it this morning and not one of us in sixteen mornings."

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and the stranger had gone back up the lane an hour before and the page had gone with her, and Mara Quill entered one line on the page at the back of the lot book with nothing ruled on it and did not read it out, which is the first thing she has entered in fifteen years without reading out first, and about nine people in that yard noticed and two of them knew why.

Orren Pike wrote the new figure at the foot of the back of his own sheet in pencil, and beside it, in the same pencil and the same hand: *moved. down. with a page. twenty-three.* He did not write the notice itself and he did not write what was in it and he did not write who read it out, and the box on that sheet is for mornings and not for any of those three things.
