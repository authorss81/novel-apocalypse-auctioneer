# Chapter 859: The One Hundred And Forty-Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man At The End Of The Second Table Said One Thing Out Loud About That Page At About Half Past Two This Afternoon And About Nine People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Heard It And Not One Of Them Answered Him And A Young Man At The Foot Of That Bank Said Out Loud That He Goes Up That Lane At About Ten To Five Whether There Is Anything On It Or Not And Nothing Was Entered And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and ninety-eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and fifty-seven. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and seventy-three days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and eighty-seven days. Seven hundred and forty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and sixty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and sixty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and thirty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and forty days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and seventy-six days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and sixteen marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the man at the end of the second table said one thing out loud about that page at about half past two this afternoon and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and nobody answered him, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and seventy-three mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and seventy-two. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and twenty-three mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and forty-three, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and eighty-fourth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and the man at the end of the second table said one thing out loud about that page at about half past two this afternoon and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and not one of them answered him.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and ninety-eighth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and ninety-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the eighty-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the second time in nine years, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of that bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood fifty-eight days, and a page came down that lane and the man at the end of the second table said one thing about it this afternoon and nobody answered him, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and then said a figure, and he said the figure before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"One hundred and sixty-four," he said, at the near end of the first table.

About four people said it after him at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it after him, and about four of them have said since that they have stopped saying that figure after a boy when there is a page at the foot of a bank, and that about four of them have not stopped and are not going to and do not know how to.

---

Mavis Dorr said at about eleven that she was not going to try anything else that day.

"I have run out," she said, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nine people. "Not out of things to say. Out of doors. I went at three on the one hundred and thirty-eighth morning and at three again yesterday afternoon and there are none left, and about four people have been at those boards every morning this week and about four of them have said the same thing to each other about not having any more, and I am saying it out loud at about eleven so that about nine people at the top of eleven feet have heard it from a person and not from each other."

---

Nobody answered her.

---

"That was not for an answer," she said. "That was so it would be in about nine sets of ears."

---

At about half past two the man at the end of the second table said his one thing, and he said it without getting up and without moving his forearms off the boards, and he had been in the same place about four feet off the lot book since about a quarter to eight.

He is about thirty-seven and has been at that table for fifteen years and wrote his line at about twenty-two and it is on no page, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard which could not put a name on a piece of paper had a man at the end of its own table stand up about the page on the one hundred and forty-third morning, and that about four of them noticed that he had got up first.

---

"I have been at this table for fifteen years," he said, "and I have written one line and it is on no page and I have said five things at these boards in about four years and about nine people have not answered one of them, and I am not going to start expecting it this afternoon."

He put one finger on the boards.

"A yard hands things up that lane. That is what a yard is for. Every thing that has ever gone up that lane in the time I have been at this table went up because somebody in this yard put it in somebody's hand, and on the one hundred and forty-third morning there is going to be a thing go up that lane and nobody in this yard has put it in anybody's hand, and that is the first time I have watched that happen in fifteen years."

---

He took his finger off the boards.

"I am not going to say what I think that is," he said. "I have thought about it for about an hour and I have got about four things I could say and about four of them would be me having an opinion, and a man who has been at a table for fifteen years does not get to have an opinion at about half past two on the one hundred and forty-third morning because a piece of paper came down a lane."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard every word of it and not one of them answered him.

About four of them have said since that they had something to say to it by about ten past three and about four of them have said that what they had to say was for themselves and not for him, and that about four of them can see that those are the same thing and have not acted on it.

---

Adrian Vale said one sentence at about three and it was about himself and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

"I am not going to write it and I am not going to say it and I am not going to carry it up," he said, "and I would like about nine people at the top of eleven feet to hear me say that I did not, because in about four days somebody at the top of nine miles of hill is going to ask this district who did, and there is not going to be anything on the paper to tell them, and about four of them will assume it was me, and I am putting it in about nine people's ears this afternoon so that when they assume that they know that they are assuming it."

---

Nobody said anything about that either.

---

The turner said nothing at all on the one hundred and forty-third morning except good afternoon to a man of about sixty-four at about half past three, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who said as sent at those boards on the one hundred and twenty-fourth morning for the first time in nine years and who offered on the one hundred and fortieth morning to carry a page up that hill has said good afternoon and nothing else, and that about four of them think that is the first thing he has done in nine years that anybody in this district could put a meaning on, and that about four of them have not looked for one.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the thirty-second time running.

---

At about ten to five the young man stood up off the flat stone at the foot of that low wall with the page in his hand, and Sabra Holt gave it to him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had come down about four feet of that bank in the last half hour and were standing on the slope and looking down at the two of them.

"I go up now," he said. "Whether there is anything on it or not. I am going to say that I brought it down on the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning and that this district did not answer it, and I am not going to say anything else, and that is what I was told to say and it is the only thing I know about what happens at the top of that hill when a piece of paper has nothing on it."

---

"Is there a morning after this one," said Mavis Dorr.

"There is," said the young man, "and I will be at the bottom of this bank at about a quarter to ten tomorrow morning, and I am not going to stand here and tell you that I will be here for ever, because there is other work on that hill and I am not the only one who carries it. I am telling you that I will be here at a quarter to ten, and that after that I do not know."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet stood on that slope at about ten to five on the one hundred and forty-third morning and knew that they had until a quarter to ten the next morning to put something on a piece of paper, and about four of them looked at the boards and about four of them looked at the page at the back of the lot book and about four of them looked at the lane, and about nine of them had not decided to do any of the three things.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. The column carries one name and the heading over it still says whoever and the one name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is still empty. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid. Four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and nobody in this basin has computed by how much it is out. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move, because nobody came down that lane with a page in their own hand this morning, and the toll is ninety-eight and it is spent, and the ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five, and a man of thirty-eight is standing at about four feet off the near end of the first table with his hands behind his back and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.
