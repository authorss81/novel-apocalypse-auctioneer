# Chapter 881: The ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FIFTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of Thirty-Eight Went Up That Lane Before It Was Light With Nothing In His Hand And Was Not At The Foot Of That Bank At About Ten And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and twenty days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and seventy-nine. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and ninety-five days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and nine days. Seven hundred and seventy days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eighty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and eighty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and fifty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and sixty-two days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and ninety-eight days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and thirty-eight marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and twenty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of thirty-eight went up that lane before it was light with nothing in his hand and was not at the foot of that bank at about ten, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and ninety-five mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and ninety-four. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and forty-five mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and sixty-five, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and not one of the thirty-three is in the lot book, and three of the thirty-three on it are lines nobody in this district heard read and that count is not going to be lower.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and sixth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and the yard said the four after him out of step with each other.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twentieth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and nineteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and tenth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the twenty-first morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood eighty days, a man of thirty-eight went up that lane and nothing has come down it, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up yet and the man who digs loam went past the bottom of that lane with his barrow, and he said that a man of thirty-eight had gone up it with nothing in his hand and had gone up it before there was light on the tops, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him say that and about four of them said it after him out of step with each other.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and there was nothing on the boards yet to say a figure against.

---

The second table went up at about ten, one trestle lighter than the man who puts tables up has been putting it up for about nine people, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet noticed that and about four of them have said since that a table can be lighter because a man is not at it, and that they did not know that before this morning.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and it is twenty-nine and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he did not say a figure this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen has now said no figure on two mornings out of the last three and has not explained either of them.

---

Orren Pike asked her at about four feet off the near end of the first table, out loud and before her hand had moved, what goes on that board about a man who is not in the yard.

---

"Nothing goes on it," she said.

---

And the boy said that part out loud again to about nine people at the top of eleven feet, and about four of them have said since that a boy who has put a figure on a sheet every morning for four years has now said out loud that there is nothing to put one against, and that he said it the way he says figures, which is before anything else has moved. She read the morning out loud before she wrote it, which she does not do every morning, and she read it out in the ordinary voice to about nine people at the top of eleven feet, and about four of them have said since that a woman with fifteen years at that book read out a morning that had a missing man in it and did not put a word of that in her mouth, and that the morning went on the board with nothing in it about the missing man at all.

---

Sabra Holt said one thing at about half past ten, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and she said it standing up and she said it once.

---

"A yard does not lose a person when they walk out of it," she said. "A yard loses them when it stops saying the number out loud, and we are not going to stop saying the number out loud this morning because one of us went up a hill."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and about four of them have said since that a woman with nineteen years of keys behind her has now said out loud in that yard that a yard does not lose anybody by not mentioning them, and that about four of them said she has said things very like that before and got nothing for them, and about four of them said that was the thing about it. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard can go on doing what it does with one man out of it, and that about four of them said it can do it for about a day, and about four of them said they would like somebody to say what the yard does on the third morning, and about four of them said they would rather it found out on its own.

---

About half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into the middle of that yard and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and then he stood in it, which is what he does, and nobody entered it before he had said it and nobody entered it after.

---

Mavis Dorr asked the one question she had left at about a quarter to twelve, and she asked it at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and about nineteen people heard it.

---

"Is anybody going up that lane after him," she said.

---

Nobody answered it for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall. Then the turner said, at about ten past twelve, that it is nine miles and the near end of that lane is not on any board in this district, and that a man said out loud yesterday that he was going up it and was not going up it so that anybody knew, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man can stand in a yard and say he will not be watched and be right about it and be gone before the light anyway.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack of something at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and she has said nothing to anybody since the one hundred and forty-ninth morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they have stopped waiting for her to say something and have not said what they are going to do instead. At about half past two the man who digs loam came back up that bank and said that the lane is the same lane it was at seven, and that a man going up it does not change it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said that after him out of step with each other.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the fifty-fourth time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twentieth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and nineteen of them, and nobody went near him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the twenty-first morning running of not asking that man anything is a thing people have started keeping count of, which is a new thing to keep count of in that yard. At about half past four the light came off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up the bank, and about four of them have said since that they found out at about half past four that they had been waiting for a man all day without knowing that they were waiting for a man.

---

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is in the lot book and three of them nobody in this district heard read. Six is what a clerk has counted and it is six and it did not move this morning and no seventh of it was counted anywhere today. Fourteen is what this district has made and the fourteen has not moved on any morning this week. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this week. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out nobody in this basin has ever done the arithmetic on. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move this morning, because a man going up that lane with nothing in his hand is not a page coming down it. The toll is ninety-eight and it was spent a long time ago and there is nothing in that yard that would unspend it. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight went up nine miles of hill before it was light with nothing in his hand and he has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and nobody has told him he is a good man.
