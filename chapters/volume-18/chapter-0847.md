# Chapter 847: The One Hundred And Thirty-First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month About Nine People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Were Standing At The Near End Of The Second Table At About Half Past One Who Were Not Standing There At Half Past One The Morning Before And A Man At The End Of That Table Said Out Loud That Boards And About Four Feet Of Stone And Eleven Feet Of Bank Do Not Make A Queue And A Man Of Sixty-One Said Good Afternoon And Said That A Question Asked Twice In A Yard Is Not Two Questions

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and eighty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and forty-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and sixty-one days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and seventy-five days. Seven hundred and thirty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and fifty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and fifty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and twenty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and twenty-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and sixty-four days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and four marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and ninety marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were standing at those boards this morning for a reason that is not going to be said out loud by any of them, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and sixty-one mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and sixty. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and eleven mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and thirty-one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and seventy-second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a man at the end of the second table said out loud that boards and about four feet of stone and eleven feet of bank do not make a queue, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not answer him.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-sixth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said out loud that a question asked twice in a yard is not two questions, and a man of about sixty-four said good afternoon and nothing else, and about four feet of stone carried both of them, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of that bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood forty-six days, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were standing at those boards this morning for a reason that is not going to be said out loud by any of them, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

Orren Pike said no figure out loud at all on the one hundred and thirty-first morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were standing at the near end of the second table at about half past one waiting for something that had not happened yet.

---

The yard did what the yard does, and about four people at the top of eleven feet got to the near end of the second table before about half past one, and about four of them have said since that they had not meant to come early and that they had got to work early because there was more to do at that end this week, and that about four of them have said the same sentence in the same words to about four different people.

---

At about half past one there were nine people at the near end of the second table.

Mara Quill was there with the lot book open. Mavis Dorr was there with her hands on the boards. The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart was there with the cart stopped nine feet short. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was standing about four feet off the boards in the place he stands in when he has said the number, which is a place about four feet off the boards and not the same four feet every morning. Sabra Holt was there and had come to the boards for the first time in about four mornings. About nine people at the top of eleven feet were standing in about nine different ways and about four of them had their hands in their pockets.

Nothing was being asked.

---

"There is nobody here," said Mavis Dorr, at about twenty to two, to about nine people. "I want to say that out loud. There is nobody here. A man of thirty-eight stood up at these boards on the one hundred and twenty-second morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stopped, and a woman with a beam stood at these boards on the one hundred and twenty-seventh morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stopped, and this morning there are nine people at these boards and nobody has said anything and about four of us got here before half past one."

---

Nobody answered her.

---

"It is not a complaint," said Mavis Dorr. "I have been on about four mornings of this week standing in this yard thinking there ought to be a place where a person stands when they are going to ask something, and there is not one, and I have looked for one, and this morning I have found out what happens if you wait long enough in a yard and do not decide to be there, and what happens is that nine people turn up and none of them can say why."

---

"They can say why," said Sabra Holt. "They can each say why and about four of them will say the same why, and that is not a place, and I have been looking at this yard for nineteen years from the position of a person who is supposed to have a hand in it and I am telling you that what has happened this week is not a place. A place has a person in charge of it and a time and something written down. This has a man who put a beam on the boards and went back down a bank."

---

"It is the same question," said Mavis Dorr.

"It is the same question and it is the same answer and it came up a bank the same way both times," said Sabra Holt, "and I am not saying it is not worth having, and I am saying that a thing which has happened twice with nobody deciding anything is not a custom yet, and I have watched a yard for nineteen years and I have seen a custom start and I know what one looks like at the beginning, and this is not what one looks like at the beginning."

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman of fifty-two with nineteen years and the third of the nine oldest keys told a yard of about nineteen people at about twenty to two on the one hundred and thirty-first morning that a thing which has happened twice with nobody deciding anything is not a custom yet, and that about nine people heard it, and that not one of them disagreed, and that the not-disagreeing is the part they have gone on thinking about.

---

The man at the end of the second table had been sitting at the near end of his own table about four feet off the lot book with his forearms on the boards and he had not moved for the whole of the hour, and at about half past two he said out loud that boards and about four feet of stone and eleven feet of bank do not make a queue.

"I have been at this table for fifteen years," he said. "I have watched a yard stand in a place before and it has been a queue once, and that was for a thing that was going to be paid, and about nine people knew they were standing in it, and one of them was a man of about forty-eight who said a number and a number was taken, and everybody knew what the standing was for and everybody who stood there had been told. This morning there are nine people at these boards and one of them is a woman who has been in this yard nine years and one of them is a man of thirty-eight who has not asked anything for about nine mornings, and the two of you have not spoken to each other today, and I have watched both of you from about four feet off all morning, and neither of you has come here for the other one."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not answer him.

---

"I am not asking you to answer me," said the man at the end of the second table. "I have said five things at these boards in about four years and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have not answered one of them and I am not going to start expecting it on the one hundred and thirty-first morning. I am saying that I have been the only person in this yard who has watched all of it from about four feet off since the one hundred and ninth morning, and that if I say that a queue is a queue and this is not one, then in about four years a person can walk into a room nine miles up that hill and ask this district what it did on the one hundred and thirty-first morning, and the answer will not be that it stood in a line."

He put one finger on the boards.

"It will be that nine people stood near a table, and that is the whole of it, and I would like about four people at the top of eleven feet to notice that I have just said the only sentence I have said in about four years that I would be willing to be asked about separately tomorrow, and that I have not said it to any of you. I have said it to the boards."

---

Adrian Vale was at about four feet off the near end of the first table and had not come to the second table at all this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet had looked at him about four times during the hour between half past one and half past two, and about four of them have said since that the man who started the whole thing did not come and stand with the nine people, and that this was noticed, and that nobody has worked out whether he did it on purpose.

He had not done it on purpose and he could not have said why he had not, and he had nine words in his mouth that he was not allowed to say at those boards, and he did not say them, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they have never once seen that man not know where to stand, and that on the one hundred and thirty-first morning he did not know where to stand.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-sixth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said out loud that a question asked twice in a yard is not two questions, and a man of about sixty-four said good afternoon and nothing else.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people heard it for the twentieth time running.

---

At about half past four the nine people at the near end of the second table were still there, and about four of them have said since that they did not decide to stay and that the light went off the boards after the cart wheel finished turning and after four of them had begun, one at a time, to put their hands in their pockets and say they had to go, and that about four of them have said that the first person to leave was the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart, and that the cart had been standing nine feet short since about ten, and that nobody in that yard has said out loud why a cart would be standing short all day on a morning in which nothing was being asked at all.
