# Chapter 877: The One Hundred And Sixty-First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Twelve A Boy Of Seventeen Was Asked In About Nineteen People's Hearing What The Mark In His Own Sheet Against The One Hundred And Thirty-Third Morning Is For And Did Not Answer And Has Not Answered On Any Morning Since And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and sixteen days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and seventy-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and ninety-one days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and five days. Seven hundred and sixty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eighty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and eighty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and fifty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and fifty-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and ninety-four days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and thirty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and twenty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a boy of seventeen was asked at about twelve what the mark in his own sheet against the one hundred and thirty-third morning is for and did not answer, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and ninety-one mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and ninety. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and forty-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and sixty-one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a boy of seventeen was asked at about twelve this morning what his own mark is for and did not answer.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and sixteenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the seventeenth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood seventy-six days, and a boy was asked about a mark in his own sheet and did not answer, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen has not said a figure on four mornings out of the last six and has not explained that on any of them.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning.

---

The boy said the lane count at about ten to eight and the lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he gave the number and no figure with it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said the number after him and about four of them went on looking at the first table.

---

Then nothing happened for about two hours, and everybody in that yard found something to do, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a morning where nothing happens is a morning where about four people at the top of eleven feet are looking at a boy of seventeen and have not decided to say anything.

At about twelve Mavis Dorr said it, and she said it in the ordinary voice at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and about nineteen people heard her ask.

---

"What is the mark in your sheet for," she said. "You have had it in there since the one hundred and thirty-third morning and about four people at the top of eleven feet have seen that page and about four of them can see that it is not a figure of money and it is not in the hand you write the other figures in. I am not asking you because I have got a use for it. I am asking you because about nine people at the top of eleven feet have been looking at a page with one mark on it that nobody in this yard can read for eleven mornings and I am tired of it."

---

And the boy did not answer, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the question and heard nothing come back, and about four of them have said since that they had been waiting all morning for one of two things and both of those things would have been a sound, and that nothing is a sound as well, and that about four of them have never in four years known that boy to leave a question with nothing in it.

The clerk did not say anything for about as long as it takes a person to decide not to.

---

"I have not asked him," she said, at about ten past twelve, to about nine people, "and I am not going to. I have kept that book for fifteen years and there is one mark in that boy's own sheet that is not one of mine and I have never once asked him about it and I am not going to start on a morning when somebody else has already."

---

And then she said the other half of it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

"I would like it noticed," she said, "that not one of about four people at the top of eleven feet can read anything at all on that page, and that the reason this yard has one question it cannot read is that the person who keeps the book does not ask what is in it. And I would like it noticed that this yard asked him about it at about twelve this morning and it was not me."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at her and about four of them have said since that a clerk has just told about nineteen people that the one unreadable page in this yard is unreadable on purpose, and that about four of them have said that she did not say who started it, and that about four of them have said she has not asked anybody in that yard for a reason in five mornings and she has not given one this morning either.

---

The turner asked him at about half past twelve, and he asked it as though he were asking about the weather, and about four people heard it and about nine did not.

"Did you not want it in the book," he said.

---

And the boy said one thing, and it was not the answer to the turner's question.

---

"That is not for you," he said. "It is not for anybody in this yard this morning and I am not going to say no to you, because I have said no out loud to one person in this yard on the one hundred and forty-seventh morning and that was the correct thing to do and I am not going to be doing it again about something that is not a question anybody asked out loud."

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the boy said the plainest thing anybody in that yard has said all week, which is that he is not refusing them, and that about four of them said afterwards that the difference between a boy who says no and a boy who says nothing is the difference between a line in a book and no line at all, and that about four of them found that they had been hoping all morning for the line.

---

He came back at about one and picked up his own sheet off the boards where it had been lying all morning and put it under his arm, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched him do it, and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen has not once in four years refused to put a figure on that sheet and that this morning the yard asked him about the only thing on it that is not a figure and got the first refusal of his life that was not a refusal.

---

Adrian Vale was at about four feet off the near end of the first table and said one sentence about it at about half past one, and it was about the boy and not about himself.

"He has put a number in the air before a clerk's hand has moved for four years," he said, "and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have been treating that as a habit. It is not a habit. It is the whole of what he has and if he stops doing it there is nothing else, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have spent eleven mornings trying to find out what a boy of seventeen is for and have not got one step nearer it, and I would like it said at about half past one this afternoon that the answer to that is a thing he will not tell us and not a thing he owes us."

---

At about two the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into the middle of that yard and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and about four people said it after him out of step with each other, and the woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at the foot of that bank and did not look up at anybody.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and sixteenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall could see that nothing was being said down there.

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the fiftieth time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that there is a man of fifty-six who has said the same sentence at about four for fifty mornings and has never been asked anything, and that about four people at the top of eleven feet found out this morning that there is a boy of seventeen who has been keeping one mark of his own for eleven mornings and did not explain it to anybody, and that about four of them said that this yard is two people not telling each other things and has been for longer than eleven mornings.

---

About half past four took the light off the boards and the boards went the colour of the ground behind them, and about nineteen people did not go up the eleven feet, and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said once, out of about four people, and not by the rest of them.

The page at the back of that book still carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is in the lot book, and one mark in a boy's own sheet about four feet off the boards is not on that page either and never has been. Whoever is still the heading over the one name in that column and that name still does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form has been empty since the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning and is empty this morning. Six months is what a clerk counted and she has counted nothing else this morning and has not counted a seventh month. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move this morning and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that it has not moved all week. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid. There is a sheet at a gatepost that says four hundred and eleven and by how much it is out is not anybody in this basin's business this morning. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move this morning, because that lane has not had a page come down it in anybody's own hand since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight has said one sentence this afternoon about a boy of seventeen and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
