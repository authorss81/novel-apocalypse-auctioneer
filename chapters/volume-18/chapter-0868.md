# Chapter 868: The One Hundred And Fifty-Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man At The End Of The Second Table Said One Thing Out Loud At About Half Past Two This Afternoon About A Question That Was Put Down And Not Answered On The One Hundred And Forty-Ninth Morning And Not One Word About Himself And Nobody Answered Him And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and seven days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and sixty-six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and eighty-two days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and ninety-six days. Seven hundred and fifty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and seventy-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and seventy-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and forty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and forty-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and eighty-five days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and twenty-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and eleven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the man at the end of the second table said one thing this afternoon about a question that was not answered and said not one word about himself, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and eighty-two mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and eighty-one. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and thirty-two mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and fifty-two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and ninety-third of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and the man at the end of the second table said one thing this afternoon about a question that was not answered and not one word about himself.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and seventh night of that run, having slept on five hundred and six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninety-seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the eighth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood sixty-seven days, and a page went back up that lane with nothing written on it, and a man at the end of the second table said one thing this afternoon and nobody answered him, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and then said a figure, and he said the figure before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"One hundred and seventy-three," he said, at the near end of the first table.

About four people said it after him at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said it after him too, and the man at the end of the second table said it after him as well, which about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since is the first figure that man has repeated out loud in that yard in about fifteen years, and about four of them have said that they have no idea why he did it and are not going to ask him.

---

The morning was ordinary until about half past two and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that ordinary is the right word for it and that it is the first morning in this block where nobody had to work out what to do before about half past two.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the edge of that second table has carried chalk marks for as long as anybody in that yard can remember, and that a clerk rubs them out at the end of a month and they go back to nothing, and that this morning about nine people looked at that edge while the light was low and about four of them counted them and about four of them counted them again and about four of them did not say what they had got, and that the marks are still there and nobody has written one of them down.

---

The turner was at the boards for about nine feet of the morning and then went down to the rail and stood about four feet off the near end of the second table and looked at the chalk and said nothing at all about it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who works with metal every day looked at a line of chalk the way he would look at something he could not tell the age of.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed things at the foot of that bank all morning and said nothing at all to anybody, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman who put a question down in about nineteen people's hearing on the one hundred and forty-ninth morning and has not said one word since is either being careful or has finished, and that about four of them have said they would like to be told which and about four of them have said they would rather not.

---

At about half past two the man at the end of the second table said it, and he said it without getting up and without moving his forearms off the boards, and he had been in the same place about four feet off the lot book since about a quarter to eight.

---

He is about thirty-seven and has been at that table for fifteen years and wrote his line at about twenty-two and it is on no page.

---

"I am going to talk about the asking and not about me," he said, "and I am going to say that at the start so that about four people at the top of eleven feet can stop listening if they came here for the other thing."

---

Nobody stopped listening.

---

"On the one hundred and forty-ninth morning a woman asked a question in about nineteen people's hearing and not one of the nineteen answered her," he said. "That is the fact. I am not going to say what it means and I am not going to say she should not have asked it, and I have thought about it since about half past two yesterday and I have got about four things I could say and I am only going to say one of them."

---

He put one finger on the boards and left it there.

"A question that gets asked and not answered is not the same as a question that is not asked," he said. "I know that sounds like nothing. It is not nothing and I am not able to give you why it is not nothing, and I want it said out loud at about half past two that a man at this table who has been here fifteen years has said that sentence and then stopped, and has not given you the rest of it, and is not going to be asked for the rest of it by anybody in this yard."

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet had something to say to that by about a quarter to three, and about four of them have said since that what they had to say was for themselves and not for him, and that about four of them can see that those are the same thing and have not acted on it.

---

"I am not going to say which of the three is the worst," he said. "A question about a name and a morning and an answer that came down a lane and went back up it with nothing on it. Two words a man said at a wall that nine people in this yard cannot agree on. A question at about four yesterday afternoon that about nineteen people did not answer. That is three and I have been at this table about four feet off that book for about four days watching this yard not answer three, and I am not going to stand here at about half past two on the one hundred and fifty-second morning and rank them, because ranking them is the answer and I have got no more right to it than the woman who keeps a scale has."

---

Then he took his finger off the boards and said, "That is the whole of it," and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the whole of it and not one of them answered him.

---

Mavis Dorr opened her mouth at about a quarter to three and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she did not say anything, and then she shut it, and about four people have said that they have never seen her do that in five mornings and that about four of them have said that it was the first thing in this block she had not planned.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and seventh night of that run, having slept on five hundred and six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninety-seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall at about half past three could see that nothing was being said down there.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the forty-first time running.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. Whoever is the heading over the one name in that column, and that name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty. Six months is what a clerk counted this morning and she has counted nothing else today. Fourteen is what this district has made and did not move this morning. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is as unpaid as the rest of them. The sheet at that gatepost says four hundred and eleven, and nobody in this basin has worked out by how much it is out. The lane count is twenty-eight and it did not move this morning, because that lane has not had a page down it in anybody's own hand since the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight is at the near end of the first table and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.
