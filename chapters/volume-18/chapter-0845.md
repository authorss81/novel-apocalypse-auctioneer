# Chapter 845: The One Hundred And Twenty-Ninth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Who Digs Loam Asked A Man Of About Forty-Eight A Question Out Loud At About Half Past Ten And Was Answered And A Boy Of Seventeen Said Out Loud In That Yard That The Asking Has Been Done At Two Different Hours On Two Different Mornings And That Nobody Arranged Either Of Them And About Nine People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Have Said The Word Asked Out Loud In That Yard This Morning

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and eighty-four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and forty-three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and fifty-nine days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and seventy-three days. Seven hundred and thirty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and forty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and fifty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and twenty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and twenty-six days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and sixty-two days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and two marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and eighty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a boy of seventeen said out loud in that yard that the asking has been done at two different hours on two different mornings and that nobody arranged either of them, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and fifty-nine mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and fifty-eight. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and nine mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and twenty-nine, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and seventieth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said the word asked out loud in that yard this morning and about four of them have said since that it is a new word in that yard and that it was not new in it in the morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-fourth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man who digs loam asked a man of about forty-eight a question out loud at about half past ten, and was answered in the ordinary voice, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the answer and about four of them said afterwards that it was not a figure, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood forty-four days, and a question was asked in that yard this morning and not one of the people who asked it has been asked whether they were going to, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

Orren Pike said his figure out loud at about ten to eight on the one hundred and twenty-ninth morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him for the hour of it, and they have asked him every morning of the last four mornings and he has not given them one and they have stopped asking, and about four of them have said since that stopping is the first thing that has changed in that yard this week and that they did not decide it.

---

The yard did what the yard does. The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart got the cart out of the gateway and stopped it about nine feet short of where he stops it, which is the first morning in about nine years that the cart has been stopped short, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed and about four of them have said since that a man who drives a cart every morning stops it in the same place and that on the one hundred and twenty-ninth morning he stopped it nine feet short and did not say anything about it.

---

At about half past ten the man who digs loam put his spade down and asked the man of about forty-eight a question.

He asked it in the ordinary voice, at about four feet off the boards of the first table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it, and the man of about forty-eight had his hands behind his back and did not turn round.

"Does that number change on a morning when somebody comes down that lane with a page in their hand," said the man who digs loam, "or does it change at about one whatever has happened up that hill."

---

"It changes at about one," said the man of about forty-eight, "whatever has happened up that hill, and that is what makes it a number and not a report."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not make a sound, and about four of them have said since that a man who has said a number out loud into about four hundred yards of air every morning for about a hundred mornings has just told about nine people that the number is not about the lane, and that nobody in that yard had ever asked him and that about four people had worked it out for themselves and had been wrong about it for about five days.

---

"You should have said that on the one hundred and twenty-fifth morning," said Mavis Dorr.

"I did say it on the one hundred and twenty-fifth morning," said the man of about forty-eight. "You asked me the wrong half of it."

---

At about eleven Orren Pike put his sheet against his chest and said out loud, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of the second table, that the asking has been done at two different hours on two different mornings and that nobody arranged either of them.

"It was about two on the one hundred and twenty-fifth morning and about two on the one hundred and twenty-seventh, and it was about half past ten this morning, and the three of them were not fixed by anybody, and I have been in this yard every morning of this run of mornings and I can tell you that nobody in this yard decided to do any of them at any hour, because I have been standing about four feet off the boards at every hour of every one of them with a sheet in my hand and I write down what people say and nobody has ever said one word to me about what hour it was going to be."

---

"Two o'clock is when the boards are free," said the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart.

"It is not," said the boy. "It is free at about two on the one hundred and twenty-fifth and the one hundred and twenty-seventh and it was not free at about two on this morning because there were nine people standing at it waiting for something, and I have written that down too, and I am not saying what it was they were waiting for, because I do not know, and I have not asked, and there is a difference between not knowing and not asking and I have got quite good at it this week."

---

"Ask," said Mavis Dorr. "Go on. Ask one of them what they were waiting for at about two this morning."

"No," said the boy. "If I ask, then the asking is a thing I have done, and then there is a person in this yard who has asked a question about the asking, and then somebody is going to want a figure of that, and I have already told you what happens to a figure about something that has not got to the end of the afternoon."

---

That is the first time in this run of mornings that anybody at the top of eleven feet has been told no by two different people in the same hour, and about four people have said since that a yard which has two people in it saying no out loud is a yard with a habit, and that a habit is not a rule and is not a page and does not have a column, and that nobody in that yard has decided to have one.

---

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the number into the middle of that yard in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and about four of them at the top of eleven feet heard it and were listening to the speed of it as well as the figure, because the man of about forty-eight had said at about half past ten that it changes at about one whatever has happened up that hill, and about four of them have said since that they have been listening to a number change at about one this week and had no idea that was a thing a person could do on purpose.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-fourth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and nothing else, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall at about half past three could see that nothing was being said down there.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people heard it for the eighteenth time running, and about four of them have said since that they have been noticing that he puts his hand flat on the stone before he says it and always has, and that nobody in that yard had ever said out loud that a man puts his hand on a stone before he says a thing, and that about four of them have said it this week to about four other people who did not know it either.

---

Sabra Holt had been in that yard since about ten and had said one thing in it, at about two, to nobody in particular, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

"There is no hour at which this yard asks," she said, "and there is no place at which it asks, and there is no person who has been decided on in advance to ask, and I have been in this yard four mornings and I have not been asked one question, and I would like about four people at the top of eleven feet to notice that a woman with nineteen years and the third of the nine oldest keys is standing in a yard at about two on a morning being told the time of day by a boy of seventeen who will not write it down, and that nobody in this yard thinks that is strange, and that I do."

Nobody answered her and she did not appear to want an answer, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she is not wrong and that nobody is going to do anything about it, and that those two things are in the same yard about four feet apart and have been since about ten.
