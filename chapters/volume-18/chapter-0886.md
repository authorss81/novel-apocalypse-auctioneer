# Chapter 886: The ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTIETH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Eleven A Man Who Digs Loam Asked The Man Of Fifty-Six What The Four Figures On That Wall Are For And A Clerk Read The Morning Out Loud Before She Wrote One Line At The Back Of That Lot Book And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and twenty-five days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eighty-four. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and fourteen days. Seven hundred and seventy-five days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eighty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and ninety-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and sixty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and sixty-seven days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and three days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and forty-three marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and twenty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man who digs loam asked the man of fifty-six out loud what the four figures on that wall are for and one line went on the page at the back, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and ninety-nine. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and fifty mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seventy, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-four lines now and not one of the thirty-four is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three are lines nobody in this district heard read.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and eleventh of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a man who digs loam asked him out loud at about eleven and a clerk read the morning out loud before she wrote one line.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-fifth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-four of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fifteenth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the twenty-sixth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood eighty-five days, one line of a kind that was not on that page before this morning went on it, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the man who digs loam said out loud at the bottom of that lane that he was going to be doing the north end today, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them said it after him out of step with each other, which is a thing about four of them have said since they do it now without hearing a reason for it.

---

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and nobody said anything else. Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a boy of seventeen has said a figure out loud on four mornings out of the last seven and has not said why on the three in between.

---

The clerk had a clean page on the boards all morning and did not put anything on it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clean page on those boards in that yard is not a thing anybody looks at twice. At about eleven the man who digs loam came up off the north end with his barrow and put it down at about four feet off the near end of the second table and asked the man of fifty-six a question out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing.

---

"What are the four figures on that wall for," he said.

---

And the man of fifty-six gave him the answer he gives at about four every morning, in the same words, at the same speed, flat, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard both of those things and about four of them said the second thing after the first out of step with each other. About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the man who digs loam has been in that yard every morning of the whole of this and has never once asked anything, and that about four of them have said that a man who has been standing in a place for a hundred and seventy mornings can turn out to be the first one to say the thing out loud, and that about four of them have not said what the thing was.

---

Then the clerk read the morning out loud, and she read it before she wrote anything, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people at the foot of that bank.

---

"The one hundred and seventieth morning," she said. "Somebody asked out loud this morning and it is being written on the page at the back of that book and the question is not going on it and the answer is not going on it, and that is the whole of it and I am not going to say anything else about it in advance of having done it."

---

And she wrote one line on the page at the back of that lot book, in her own hand, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk write one line for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and about four of them have said since that they have watched that clerk enter four hundred pages in fifteen years and that they have never once watched her this slow. Nobody at the foot of that bank asked her afterwards what the line said, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they wanted to ask and did not, and that about four of them have said a line is better if nobody explains it, and about four of them have said they do not agree and did not say so out loud.

---

The back page of that book had thirty-three lines on it yesterday and has thirty-four this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a page at the back of a book is the one place in this district where a thing can stand without anybody having to say what it is for.

---

Sabra Holt asked, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing, who has to ask, and the clerk said no out loud and gave no reason.

---

"Who has to ask," Sabra Holt said.

---

"No," the clerk said.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman who keeps putting a rule on her own mouth out loud asked again this morning and got the same answer a man of thirty-eight got, and about four of them have said since that whatever it is that the yard has got now it is not a rule.

---

Then the turner asked whether those lines can be carried up that lane to whoever reads that kind of page, and the clerk said no out loud, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it, and about four of them have said since that a page goes up that lane in the hand of whoever carried it down and that nobody in that yard has ever carried anything up. The turner said that the lane count would not move for a line going anywhere and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him say it out loud, and about four of them have said since that a man who has carried a question up that hill and come back with a question about a name knows which direction things go in.

---

Nobody thanked the man who was asked and nobody thanked the man who asked him, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they looked for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall to see whether anybody was going to and that nobody was.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the fifty-ninth time running. Nobody asked him what any of them is for. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-fifth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-four of them, and nobody went near him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the twenty-sixth morning running of not asking that man anything is the morning a line went on the back page for the first time, and that about four of them have said they do not know whether those two things are in the same yard.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

Thirty-four lines stand on the page at the back of that lot book and the lot book itself has five lines on it and not one of the thirty-four is one of those five, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk counted out loud in a yard on the one hundred and sixty-eighth morning, it did not go back, and it is not on anything. Fourteen is the count of the things this district has made and it did not move this morning. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid, and a line on the back of a book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much it is out is still a sum nobody in this basin has begun. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move, and a line on that back page is not a page coming down that lane. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. Public lot has been the standing of the ground at the foot of that low wall since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and it is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid stands in a ledger at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. Nobody has been thanked for a line and nobody has been given a bed or a wage or an office for one, and a man of thirty-eight has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
