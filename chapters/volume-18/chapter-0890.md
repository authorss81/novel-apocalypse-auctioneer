# Chapter 890: The ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOURTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month Nobody Asked Anything On This Morning And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Six Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Seventh And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and twenty-nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eighty-eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and four days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and eighteen days. Seven hundred and seventy-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and ninety-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and ninety-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and sixty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and seventy-one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and seven days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and forty-seven marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and thirty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and four mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and fifty-four mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seventy-four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and fifteenth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-ninth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and nineteenth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the thirtieth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood eighty-nine days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table and about nine people at the top of eleven feet who had come down that bank earlier than they had to and were standing about the same as they stood yesterday.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other and did not look at the boards. Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and then he said a figure, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other.

---

"One hundred and ninety-five," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet said that after him too and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen has said that figure out loud four times in this stretch of ten mornings and that on this morning it was the last figure anybody said out loud in that yard before the light went off the boards, and that about four of them have not said what they make of a morning that ends on a number.

---

And then nothing was asked. That is the whole of that morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard of about nineteen people spent a morning waiting for a sound that a man of thirty-eight had told them on the day before that nobody was going to make, and that about four of them said they did not know they were waiting until about half past ten and about four of them said they had known since the boards went up. About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the man of fifty-six got the four off that wall twice this morning and put his hand flat on the stone at about four and said his sentence out loud in the ordinary voice as he has said it every morning since before the middle of the winter, and that about four of them said he said it the same way he says it and that about four of them said nobody asked him anything.

---

Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything, and he is standing at that wall every morning of this and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the man who gets four figures off that wall twice a morning has said that he does not know what any of them is for six hundred and fifteen times and that not one of those six hundred and fifteen was said this morning by anybody but him.

---

Mavis Dorr came down that bank at about eleven and stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table and said nothing at all, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the woman who said out loud on the one hundred and sixty-seventh morning that she was not going to ask anything else came down that bank on the one hundred and seventy-fourth and said nothing, and that about four of them said that this is what stopping sounds like.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she has now said nothing for a stretch of mornings that is longer than anybody in that yard has been keeping count of, and that about four of them have said they have stopped noticing and about four of them have said that is not the same as not caring. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the man who digs loam asked at the bottom of that lane this morning whether anybody had heard him ask anything yet, and was told by about four of them that they had not, and that about four of them said he said the right word for it, which is yet.

---

About half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into the middle of that yard and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-ninth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and nineteenth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who held a thing for seventeen years without anybody counting how long came back to that stone on the last afternoon of this stretch and said one word and no more, and that about four of them have said that a yard can say a number into the air for a long time and nobody has ever come down that lane for it.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the sixty-third time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for. Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line, and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-six lines and none of the thirty-six is in the lot book.

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it and about four of them have said since that this is the second morning in this stretch of ten on which that page did not take a line, and that the two mornings are both named on it, and that about four of them said that a name is not a figure and about four of them said that it is the first thing on that page that anybody in this district could call a record of something that did not happen.

---

Then Adrian Vale said one thing out loud at about ten past four, to about nineteen people, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and he said it before the light went off the boards.

---

"I am going up that lane again on the next morning," he said. "With nothing in my hand, same as the last time. Nobody asked me to and nobody has to, and I am not going to be thanked for it, and if I knock on that door again I will knock on it at a time when somebody is in, and if nobody is in then I will come back down and say so."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and about four of them said good afternoon after him out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that a man of thirty-eight has now got a reason for knocking and that the reason is a thing he made up this morning at the boards.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said it this morning and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning and not one of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move on any of the ten mornings of this stretch. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move, and it is not going to move for anything this yard does at those boards. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled. Nobody asked anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
