# Chapter 862: The One Hundred And Forty-Sixth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month Mavis Dorr Got About Nine People To Say Out Loud The Two Words A Man Said At The Foot Of That Wall Yesterday Afternoon And About Four Of The Nine Said A Different Thing From The Other Five And Nothing Was Written Down And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and sixty. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and seventy-six days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and ninety days. Seven hundred and fifty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and sixty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and seventy days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and forty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and forty-three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and seventy-nine days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and nineteen marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said two words out loud yesterday afternoon and about four of the nine said a different thing from the other five, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and seventy-six mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and seventy-five. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and twenty-six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and forty-six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and eighty-seventh of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said two words out loud at about eleven this morning and four of the nine said a different thing from the other five and nothing was written down.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and first night of that run, having slept on five hundred of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninety-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the second morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood sixty-one days, and a page went back up that lane with nothing written on it, and two words a man said at the foot of that wall yesterday afternoon have been said nine times this morning by nine people and four of them said a different thing, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

---

The turner was in that yard at about ten past nine and stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table and said nothing at all, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who said good afternoon and nothing else on the one hundred and forty-third morning and good afternoon and nothing else on the one hundred and forty-fifth and the one hundred and forty-fourth has now said nothing at all for three mornings, and that about four of them cannot agree whether that is a thing a man does or a thing a yard is doing to him.

---

Mavis Dorr put it at about eleven and she put it to about nine people and she did it in the ordinary voice.

"I am going to say them one at a time," she said, "and I am not going to let anybody say the words for anybody else, and if somebody has not got them I would rather they said so than made them up, because the whole of what we are about to find out is about whether nine people who were all standing in the same yard heard the same two words."

---

She went along the bank from the near end of the second table to the end of the rail, and about nine people said the two words, and the fourth one along said something else.

---

"The turn," said the first one.

---

"The turn," said the second one.

"At the turn," said the third one.

"It went past the turn," said the fourth one, and about four people at the top of eleven feet turned round and looked at him.

---

Nobody said anything for a while, and the boards were the colour of the boards, and the second table stood about four feet along from the near end of the first with the lot book on it and nothing else.

---

"It is not the same," said Mavis Dorr.

"No," said the man who had said it. "It is not. I have been about nine years in this yard and I heard a thing about a turn and I have put a verb on it because I am a man who puts verbs on things. That is all that is the difference and I would like it noticed that I have said so out loud before anybody has said that I have got it wrong."

---

"You have not got it wrong," said Mavis Dorr. "You have got it the way anybody in a yard gets a thing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have got it your way and about four have got it the other and there is nobody in this yard who is going to be able to tell you which of you is right, and I have been standing here for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall listening to five of you say a thing that was said once."

---

"Is there a space for it," said the man who drives the cart, from about nine feet further along the bank.

"There is a space on a form with a heading over it that says whoever," said Mavis Dorr, "and there are thirty-three lines on the back of that book and not one of the thirty-three is in the book and three of them nobody in this district has heard read, and I am not going to put this on any of them, and about four of us worked out on the one hundred and thirty-seventh morning that a name in that column is this yard and this yard is about nineteen people and about nine of us have just proved that this morning."

---

The man at the end of the second table said nothing out loud at all during any of that, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he was at that table from about a quarter to eight and did not say one word about nine people failing to agree on two words, and that about four of them think that is the most he has ever declined to do.

---

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the number into the middle of that yard and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said the number after him out of step with each other, which about four of them have not done in about ten mornings.

---

At about half past two a woman with fifty-two years and the third of the nine oldest keys said the thing that has been standing in that yard since yesterday afternoon, and she said it at about four feet off the near end of the second table and to about nine people and not to the yard.

"There is a difference between a thing that has been said and a thing that is known," she said. "Those are two words for one thing and I want to say them both before anybody else uses one of them. A thing that has been said is nine people and a yard and eleven feet of bank. A thing that is known is a man of about sixty-four and about four years of nights and whatever it is he keeps in his head about where his own wire went. This yard has the first of those this morning and it has not got the second of them, and it is not going to get them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet need to stop saying the second one because it is nicer."

---

Nobody said anything to that either.

"I am not being hard," she said. "I have held nineteen years of a thing that most of the people standing at the top of that bank were not here for, and I have never once in nineteen years got an answer I liked out of anybody, and I would like about nine people to hear me say that the answer I liked this morning is the wrong one and I am putting it down myself so that nobody else has to."

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the thirty-fifth time running, and about four of the nineteen had said the two words about the turn at about eleven that morning and had not written either of them down.

---

The man of thirty-eight was at the near end of the first table at about half past four with his hands behind his back and he was not at the foot of that wall at all this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who said two words at the foot of that wall yesterday afternoon spent today not saying them again, and that about four of them have said that they can see the sense in that and about four of them have said that they would not have managed it.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. Whoever is the heading over the one name that is in that column and that name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty and has been empty since the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning. Six months is what a clerk has counted this morning and nothing else has been counted. Fourteen is what this district has made. Five is what it does not have and the fifth of the five is as unpaid as the other four. The figure on a sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and nobody in this basin has ever said by how much it is out. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move, because nobody came down that lane this morning with a page of their own hand in it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it is not going anywhere. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight is standing at the near end of the first table and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.
