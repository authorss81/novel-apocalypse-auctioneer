# Chapter 849: The One Hundred And Thirty-Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Thirty-Two Left A Trestle Standing At The Near End Of The Second Table When He Went Down The Bank At About Ten And A Woman Asked A Man Of Thirty-Eight A Question Out Loud At Those Boards At About Half Past Two And He Answered It And About Four People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Heard It And Nothing Is Going To Be Entered Anywhere

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and eighty-eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and forty-seven. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and sixty-three days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and seventy-seven days. Seven hundred and thirty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and fifty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and fifty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and twenty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and thirty days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and sixty-six days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and six marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and ninety-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of about thirty-two left a trestle standing at the near end of the second table when he went down the bank at about ten, which he has not done in nine years, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at it and about nine of them did not say one word about it, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and sixty-three mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and sixty-two. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and thirteen mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and thirty-three, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and seventy-fourth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a woman asked a man of thirty-eight a question out loud at those boards at about half past two and he answered it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard it, and nothing is going to be entered anywhere.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-eighth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said out loud that a yard can hold a question without counting it, and that the holding is the whole of it and there is no figure for the holding, and a man of about sixty-four said good afternoon and nothing else, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood forty-eight days, and the second space on that form is still empty and the one name in that column does not satisfy the rule and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

Orren Pike said no figure out loud at all on the one hundred and thirty-third morning and there is a mark in his sheet against this morning that is not a figure of money and he has not said out loud what it is for.

---

The trestle was standing at the near end of the second table at about ten.

The man of about thirty-two had put it up at about ten the way he puts it up every morning and then he had picked up the other one and gone down the eleven feet with it, and he had not come back up, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at the trestle at about ten past ten and about four of them looked at it again at about one and again at about three, and about nine of them did not say one word about it, and about four of them have said since that a man who has taken a trestle down at the same hour every morning for about nine years has left one standing, and that nobody asked him to, and that about nine people have decided over the course of a morning not to say anything to a man about a piece of wood.

---

At about eleven Orren Pike was asked about the mark.

"Orren," said Mavis Dorr. "There is a mark in that sheet against this morning and it is not a figure and I can see it is not a figure because I have been looking at that sheet's shape for about five mornings, and I am not going to ask you what the mark is for. I am going to ask you whether the mark is in your hand or in mine."

---

"It is in mine," said the boy.

---

"Good," said Mavis Dorr, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she said the word *good* in that yard and that it was the first time anybody had said it about a thing anybody in that yard had done about the asking, and that about four of them have spent about four days not saying it to anybody and that hearing somebody else say it about a mark in a boy's sheet took a thing off them that they had not known they were carrying, and that nobody in that yard has worked out what the thing was.

---

At about half past two Mavis Dorr came to the near end of the first table where Adrian Vale was standing with his hands behind his back.

"Adrian."

---

He turned round, which he does about four mornings of about a hundred and forty-nine.

---

"You said on the one hundred and twenty-fourth morning that you were staying," said Mavis Dorr, "and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and about four of them have been asking each other since whether anybody was going to be told it was a good thing, and nobody has been told it is a good thing, and I have watched about four of them not ask each other about it this week, and I know why they stopped, because a woman with a beam asked a man a question at these boards on the one hundred and twenty-seventh morning and that made the other thing look like a small thing being said by a person who had run out of larger ones."

She put her hands flat on the boards.

"Did you know it was going to spread."

---

Adrian Vale stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table in the ordinary light of the one hundred and thirty-third morning and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard the question, and he had been asked things in that yard before and he had said *I am not going to name a morning* and he had said *I am staying* and he had held a shape in his mouth nine words long and had not used it.

"No," he said. "I thought it was going to be one question on one afternoon and then it was going to be a thing a man said once and a clerk entered. I thought I was going to be a person who asked one question in a yard, and about nine people were going to be able to be asked about it separately tomorrow, and that was going to be the whole of it, and I have been standing at these boards for about nine mornings waiting for the yard to be a yard where a thing like that was said, and it has not stopped being that, and that is the part I did not think about."

---

"Would you have asked a second one," said Mavis Dorr, "if nobody else had."

---

"No," said Adrian Vale. "There is only one and it was the same one, and I have not been to the foot of that wall to ask it and I am not going to, and the woman who asked it on the one hundred and twenty-seventh morning said out loud that it was not a good question and that she had not got a better one, and I have been at about four feet off these boards for six days being asked by about four people whether I was going to beat her to it, and I want to say out loud at these boards that no, I was not going to, and that if I had been asked that on the one hundred and twenty-seventh morning at about ten to two I would have stood here and said nothing, and that a woman with a beam would have asked it anyway."

---

Mavis Dorr took her hands off the boards.

"Write that down," she said.

---

"No," said Adrian Vale.

---

"You have just said the only thing anybody has said at these boards this week that I would pay money to have written down."

---

"I know you have," said Adrian Vale. "That is exactly why I am not going to say it to a woman who has told me she would pay money for it, in a yard, at about half past two, in the hearing of about four people, and about four people in this yard would remember it, and about four people in this district nine miles up that hill could be asked about it, and I have been in that room. I have been in that room and I have come back down that lane with somebody else's question in my hand, and I am not going to be the second man in this basin to carry a thing out of a yard and have it come back as something else."

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet did not make a sound.

---

The turner, who had come up the eleven feet at about half past one and had been standing about nine feet off the near end of that second table for an hour, said one thing.

"He is right," said the turner. "And I want to say out loud that I have been standing at about nine feet off those boards for an hour this afternoon waiting for a moment like that one, and that I got it, and that the reason I got it is that a man of thirty-eight knows what a yard is for. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have spent this week asking each other whether the asking ought to be written down and all nine of them have been asking it the wrong way round, and I have been up nine miles of hill twice this week refusing to say a thing, and neither of us has done anything this afternoon except say no to a person who wanted to pay for a line."

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-eighth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said out loud that a yard can hold a question without counting it, and that the holding is the whole of it and there is no figure for the holding, and a man of about sixty-four said good afternoon and nothing else.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people heard it for the twenty-second time running.

Mara Quill had the lot book closed at about ten to four and had not opened it, and the page at the back of it carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book, and the trestle was still standing at the near end of the second table when the light went off the boards, and the man of about thirty-two had not come back up the bank, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has put a trestle up at about ten every morning for about nine years has left one standing for about seven hours and has not come back for it, and that nobody in that yard has gone down that bank to fetch it, and that the fifth of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and nobody in this basin has computed by how much it is out.
