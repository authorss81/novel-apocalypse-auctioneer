# Chapter 864: The One Hundred And Forty-Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Woman Who Keeps A Scale Said Out Loud At About Two This Afternoon That She Has Watched That Figure Every Morning Since The Seventy-Sixth And That She Has Run Out Of A Second Question And That She Has One And Is Going To Put It Down At About Four Tomorrow Afternoon And Nobody Asked Her To And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and three days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and sixty-two. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and seventy-eight days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and ninety-two days. Seven hundred and fifty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and sixty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and seventy-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and forty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and forty-five days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and eighty-one days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and twenty-one marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a woman who keeps a scale said out loud at about two this afternoon that she has one question and is going to put it down at about four tomorrow afternoon, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and seventy-eight mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and seventy-seven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and twenty-eight mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and forty-eight, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and eighty-ninth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a woman who keeps a scale said out loud at about two this afternoon that she has one question and is going to put it down at about four tomorrow afternoon.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and third night of that run, having slept on five hundred and two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninety-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fourth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood sixty-three days, and a page went back up that lane with nothing written on it, and a woman who keeps a scale said at about two this afternoon that she is going to put a question down at about four tomorrow afternoon, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

---

The man who digs loam was at the top of that bank from about a quarter to eight and put about nine feet of the eleven feet back into the state it was in, which is a job nobody in this yard has said out loud is a job, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who digs loam is the only person in this yard who still gets paid for something he does every morning and that about four of them have never once asked him what it is for.

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came down about four feet of that bank at about ten with the cart behind him and stood it on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and did not say one word about that, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a public lot is the only ground in this district a man can stand a cart on without somebody eventually asking him what he is doing there.

---

Sabra Holt was at those boards from about a quarter to eight and read nothing out loud and entered nothing and stood with her hands in front of her about four feet off the near end of the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the only morning in this block on which nothing was read out loud at those boards was also the morning on which the yard's second question was announced, and that about four of them do not believe that was a coincidence and about four of them do.

---

At about two the woman who keeps a scale put her weight down on the thing she was holding and turned round, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were within about four feet of her and about four of them were not.

"I am going to say something at about two this afternoon," she said, in the ordinary voice, "and I am going to say it before the light goes off the boards so that about nine people at the top of eleven feet have got it in their ears before this morning is over, and I am not going to say it to a person. I am going to say it to that bank."

---

Nobody said anything.

---

"I have been weighing things at the foot of this bank for nine years," she said, "and I have watched that figure every morning since the seventy-sixth, and I have watched it the way I watch a scale, which is to say I have not once gone near it to see whether it is right. And what I have noticed, and what I have not said out loud until this afternoon, is that this yard has been asking one question for thirteen mornings and that I have been standing about four feet off the boards the whole time waiting for a second one to turn up."

---

She stopped there, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet waited, and she went on.

"I have run out," she said. "There is no second question. I have gone through about nine years of things a person weighs and there is not one more of them in this yard. And I have got one, and I am not going to say what it is this afternoon, and I am going to put it down tomorrow afternoon at about four, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing."

---

Sabra Holt said, "You do not have to do that."

---

"I know I do not," said the woman who keeps a scale. "That is why I am saying it at about two this afternoon instead of about four tomorrow afternoon with everybody standing here already. If anybody in this yard wanted to stop me they have had about half past two this afternoon to do it and about four of them have not and about four of them were not going to."

---

"You are not a clerk," said Sabra Holt. "You have never been anything in this district but a woman with a scale and I have just watched you do the only thing in nine years that I could not do, which is say something out loud that there is no line for, and I would ask you out loud, here, in about nine people's hearing, whether you have thought about that."

---

"I have," said the woman who keeps a scale. "I have thought about it the whole way through saying it and I am still going to do it, and here is the whole of what I have got to say about it and I am saying it once. There is no line for it. That is why there is no line for it. Every line in this district got put in by somebody who had a thing to put in it, and I have not got a thing to put in this yard and I am not asking for one and I am not going to be given an office over it."

---

She turned back to her weighing and about nine people at the top of eleven feet stood about four feet off that bank and let her, and about four of them have said since that it is the first time in this block that anybody has said out loud that she is asking for nothing, and that about four of them have said that she has never asked for anything at all and that saying so about it is itself a new thing.

---

The clerk was told at about half past two, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, by a woman of fifty-two with nineteen years, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she was told the whole of it and that she said nothing at all back, and that her hand did not move at any point while she was being told.

---

A woman of about fifty with a barrow came along the near rail at about half past two and turned back at the end of it and did not stop, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were standing about four feet off that rail and about four of them stepped out of her way and about four of them did not and about four of them have said since that about nine people at the top of eleven feet are now walking differently in that yard and that about four of them do not know when they started.

---

The woman who keeps a scale went back to the thing she had set down and picked it up again and said, to nobody, that a scale is the only thing in this yard that tells you when you are wrong without asking you anything first, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard her and about four of them have said since that they do not know what she weighs and have decided not to ask and about four of them have said that not asking is the first sensible thing anybody has done at those boards this week.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and third night of that run, having slept on five hundred and two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninety-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall at about half past three could see that nothing was being said down there.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the thirty-seventh time running, and about four of the nineteen had spent the morning being told that this yard has got one more question coming and had worked out nothing at all about what it is.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. Whoever is the heading over the one name in that column, and that name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty. Six months is what a clerk counted this morning and there is nothing else on that list of hers. Fourteen is what this district has made. Five is what this district does not have and not one of the five has been paid for. Four hundred and eleven is what the sheet at that gatepost says, and by how much it is out is a thing nobody in this basin has put a number to. The lane count is twenty-eight and it did not move, because this morning nobody came down that lane with a page in their own hand. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight is standing at the near end of the first table and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.
