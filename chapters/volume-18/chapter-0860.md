# Chapter 860: The One Hundred And Forty-Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Page Went Back Up That Lane At About Ten To Five This Afternoon With Nothing Written On It And Nothing Crossed Out And Not One Of The Three Lines On It Filled And About Nineteen People Came Down That Bank To Watch It Go And This Yard Has Found Out That It Cannot Hand An Answer On And Nothing Was Entered Anywhere And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and ninety-nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and fifty-eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and seventy-four days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and eighty-eight days. Seven hundred and forty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and sixty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and sixty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and thirty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and forty-one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and seventy-seven days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and seventeen marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a page went back up that lane this afternoon with nothing on it and nothing crossed out and nothing added anywhere, and the lane count did not move, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and seventy-four mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and seventy-three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and twenty-four mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and forty-four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and eighty-fifth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about nineteen people in that yard came down that bank at about ten to five and watched a page go back up that lane with nothing on it, and about four of them have said since that a yard of about nineteen people cannot hand an answer on.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and ninety-ninth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and ninety-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the eighty-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the second time in nine years, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood fifty-nine days, and a page came down that lane and it went back up that lane this afternoon with nothing on it and not one of the three lines on it filled and not one of the three entered anywhere, and the record did not move on it, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and then said a figure, and he said the figure before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"One hundred and sixty-five," he said, at the near end of the first table.

About four people said it after him at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said it after him too, and that is the largest number of voices that figure has had in that yard and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they noticed and have not been able to say what they noticed.

---

About nine people were at the near end of the second table at about half past one on the one hundred and forty-fourth morning and about four of them had come before half past one, and about nine of them had been doing that for about nine mornings, and nobody had arranged any part of it.

---

Mavis Dorr said the whole of the day out loud at about two, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nine people at the top of eleven feet.

"I am not going to do anything this morning," she said. "I want to say that at about two in the afternoon in front of about nine people so that nobody spends the rest of the day waiting for me to. I am not going to write on that page, and none of the three of us is going to, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have known that since the one hundred and thirty-seventh morning and about four of them have been hoping that a person would come up that bank this morning with a way round it, and he came down it at about a quarter to ten on the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning and he has been sitting on a flat stone ever since and he has not brought one."

---

Nobody said anything.

---

"And I am not saying that to be hard," she said. "I am saying it because about four of us have spent five days being careful with that page as though careful were a way of answering it, and it is not. Careful is what we have got instead of an answer, and it has looked like work, and about nine people have watched it."

---

At about three the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the number into the middle of that yard and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said the number after him out of step with each other, which about four of them have not done in about nine mornings, and about four of them have said since that they said it out loud because there was nothing else to do with their mouths and not because of the figure.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and ninety-ninth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and ninety-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the eighty-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall at about half past three could see that nothing was being said down there.

He went off up the bank at about half past four and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched him go and about nine of them did not.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the thirty-third time running.

---

At about ten to five about nineteen people came down that bank.

Nobody arranged it and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they went down because the light went off the boards and about four of them have said that that is not why they went down and have not said what is.

Mavis Dorr stood about four feet off the near end of the low wall with the light going off the boards behind her and about nineteen people in that yard heard her, and she said it in the ordinary voice, and it was the last thing anybody in this yard said about a page on the one hundred and forty-fourth morning.

"That page went down that lane on the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning with three questions on it," she said. "It is going back up it at about ten to five this afternoon with three questions on it and not one of the three answered, and there is no line on it, and there is no mark on it, and it is going up exactly as it came down."

---

"That is the whole of what we have learned since the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning," she said, "and I am going to say it once at about ten to five in about nineteen people's hearing so that about four people can tell somebody else about it tomorrow. We have learned that this yard cannot hand an answer on. Not will not. Cannot. There are three lines on that piece of paper and this yard has got none of them and about nine people have spent five days finding that out separately and out loud, and not one of us can fill one line without making this yard into one person, and about four of us worked that out at about half past one on the one hundred and thirty-seventh morning and the rest of us have been catching up."

---

"And I want about nineteen people to hear the next part, because it is the part that nobody is going to enjoy." She looked up the bank. "We have not said no. That is what I want said. A yard that has said no can be asked again and can be asked properly and can put the no on something. We have not said no. We have got nothing to say no with. There is no column for a no and no line on that page for a no and no place in this district to put a no, and so when that page opens in a room nine miles up that hill there will be nothing on it, and nobody in that room will be able to say that this district refused, because a refusal is a thing and this is not a thing. It is a blank."

---

"So the answer is going to be upstairs," she said, "and it is not going to be in this yard, and there is nothing anybody in this yard can do about that before about ten to five tomorrow morning, and about nineteen of us have stood about four feet off that wall for about five days waiting for it to be a thing we could carry."

---

The young man was standing on the flat stone about nine feet off the near end of the low wall with the page in his hand.

He said that he was going up now and that he was going to say that he brought it down on the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning and that this district did not answer it, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and then he said the rest of it to about nineteen people and not to a room.

"And I am going to say that there was nothing on it when it went up," he said, "because you have all been watching it for five days and about nineteen of you know that and it is the only thing about it that I can say without being wrong. A blank goes up a lane the same as anything else does."

---

And then Orren Pike, who had come down about four feet of that bank with everybody else and was standing at the bottom with his sheet in his hand, said the one thing that was left to say out loud, and he said it to about nineteen people and it was a figure and he said it before anybody's hand had moved.

"The lane count is twenty-eight," he said, "and it did not move this morning, and it did not move this afternoon, and a page went back up that lane at about ten to five and it did not move, because the count moves for a page coming down and not for a page going up. That is the rule and I have had it for four years and it is not mine and I have not said anything about it that is not on it."

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a boy of seventeen read out a rule about a lane at about ten to five on the one hundred and forty-fourth morning and that it was the truest thing anybody said at the foot of that wall all week, and that about four of them can see that the only figure in this yard that reports honestly about what happens in it does not count the things that leave.

---

The young man went up the lane with the page in his hand, and about nineteen people at the foot of that bank watched him go about forty feet and then about four hundred yards of air took him and the lane took him and about four feet of stone and about eleven feet of bank carried nothing with him. Nothing was written on that page. Nothing was crossed out. It is not in the lot book. It is not on the page at the back of that lot book, which carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book and not one of the thirty-three is about a question.

The heading over that column is still whoever and the one name in it does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty and has been empty for five days. Six months is the whole of what a clerk has counted this morning. Fourteen is the whole of what this district has made. Five is what this district does not have and it is not paid. Four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and by how much it is out nobody in this basin has worked out. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight is standing at about four feet off the near end of the first table with his hands behind his back and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.

And about four hundred yards of air in that yard carries a voice the way it carries everything else anybody in that yard has ever said, and about nineteen people at the foot of that bank have learned the shape of what they have got, and the shape is one question that about nineteen people have asked and no column and no page and no name and no answer on its way up nine miles of hill, and nobody in that yard has decided anything about it at all.
