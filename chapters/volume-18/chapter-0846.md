# Chapter 846: The One Hundred And Thirtieth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Forty-One Came Up That Hill At About Eleven And Said He Was Not Going To Say What A Room Asked Him And About Nine People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Asked Him To And A Boy Of Seventeen Said A Figure Out Loud And A Man At The End Of The Second Table Said It After Him And A Man Of Sixty-One Was On The Stones And Said That He Is Not Going To Say How Many People In That Yard Have Asked Somebody Something Out Loud

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and eighty-five days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and forty-four. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and sixty days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and seventy-four days. Seven hundred and thirty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and forty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and fifty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and twenty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and twenty-seven days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and sixty-three days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and three marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and eighty-nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of about forty-one came up that hill at about eleven and said he was not going to say what a room asked him this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet asked him to, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and sixty mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and fifty-nine. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and ten mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and thirty, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and seventy-first of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a boy of seventeen said a figure out loud at about ten to eight and a man at the end of the second table said it after him, which is the first morning either of them has done that.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-fifth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-four of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-fifth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him and said that he is not going to say how many people in that yard have asked somebody something out loud, and that he has been asked twice about it and has said no twice, and that a man of sixty-one is not a clerk and does not keep a count of anybody, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of that bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood forty-five days, and a man at the end of the second table made a note on the side of his own hand and about four people at the top of eleven feet saw him do it and about nine of them did not ask what it said, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

"One hundred and fifty-one," said Orren Pike.

He said it at the near end of the first table at about ten to eight and a man at the end of the second table said it after him out loud, at the same moment and not in step with him, and stopped, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at the two of them and about four of them have said since that a man who has been at that table for fifteen years has not said a figure out loud in that yard once, and that he said one before the light was properly on the boards, and that nobody in that yard is going to be told by him why.

---

At about nine the woman who keeps a scale was on her own step about forty feet off with the beam across her knees, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked across at her and she did not look back, and about four of them have said since that on the one hundred and twenty-eighth morning she was on her step and on the one hundred and twenty-ninth morning she was on her step and that on the one hundred and thirtieth morning she has been on her step from about nine, which is the first morning anybody in that yard has noticed the hour she arrives.

---

At about eleven the man of about forty-one came up the eleven feet.

He came up it in the ordinary way and he got to the top of that bank and stood about four feet off the near end of the first table and about nine people at the top of eleven feet turned round, which they have now done twice running, and about four of them have said since that the second time is not a thing either.

"I am not going to say what a room asked me this morning," he said.

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet asked him to.

About four of them said it in the same words, which were *say it here so that it is in the yard*, and about four of them said it in other words, and Mavis Dorr said it in none.

---

"You have been carrying a question up that hill for about fourteen mornings," said Mavis Dorr, "and on the one hundred and twenty-second morning you stood at those boards and said a number about yourself in a yard of about nineteen people, and you said that if you did both then a room could say a man of about forty-one put a question to a person in this district, and today a room has asked you something and you have brought nine miles of road up a hill and you are not going to say it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have just asked you to and not one of us has any standing to."

---

"I know that none of you has any standing to," said the turner. "I said the whole of that on the one hundred and twenty-first morning and about nine people heard it and about nine people have been quoting it back at me all week without knowing they were quoting it."

---

"Then say it here so that it is in the yard," said the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart.

---

"No," said the turner.

---

"Man of about thirty-two, ask him," said the man of about twenty-nine, to the man who puts tables up, who was about nine feet off with a trestle under his arm.

---

The man of about thirty-two said nothing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at him, and about four of them have said since that a man who has been putting tables up in that yard for about nine years was asked a question in the middle of a morning by a man who drives a cart and did not answer it, and that this has never happened before, and that it happened before about nine people had asked the turner the same thing and been refused.

---

"You have all done it now," said the turner. "About nine people at the top of eleven feet have asked me for one thing this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have heard themselves do it, and I am going to say the thing I came up here to say and then I am going to go back down that bank at about ten to twelve and go on saying a number in a room nine miles off tomorrow morning at about ten to ten."

He put his hands behind his back.

"A room nine miles up that hill has been asking this district one question every morning for about fourteen mornings and I have carried it up that hill every morning and I have not put it to anybody, and this morning it asked me something else, and I am not going to say what, and not because it is a secret and not because it is about a person. It is because it is a question. And I am not going to carry a question out of a room in my mouth and put it in a yard, because the moment it is in that yard it is the yard's, and nobody in that room has agreed to that, and neither has anybody in this yard."

---

"That is the first time in about fourteen mornings," said Sabra Holt, from about four feet in from the boards, "that anybody in this yard has said out loud that a question can be in a room and not be in a district. I would like about nine people at the top of eleven feet to notice that I have heard it and that I am not going to say whether it is right, and that I have held the third of the nine oldest keys for nineteen years and I have never in nineteen years been given a thing to carry and I have just been told by a man of about forty-one that the not being given one is the correct arrangement, and that I do not have a better arrangement."

---

The turner went down the eleven feet at about ten to twelve and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched him go, and about four of them have said since that he said he was going to go at about ten to twelve before it was ten to twelve, and that a man who has been at the end of that lane for nine years has begun saying out loud what he is going to do and when he is going to do it, and that about four people in that yard found that harder to sit through than the refusing.

---

Orren Pike said the figure out loud at about ten to eight, and the man at the end of the second table said it after him, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at the two of them, and about four of them have said since that the boy has said a figure out loud at the same hour every morning for four years and that this morning he said it and then somebody else said it, and that the boy did not look round and that the man did not look round, and that between the two of them they have made a thing out of a figure in about half a second without either of them deciding to.

---

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the number into the middle of that yard in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they have started saying the number after him, out loud, out of step with each other, and that the man has not said one word about it, and that a man who keeps a number has not said one word about nine people in his yard saying it after him, and that this is the second time this week that something has happened at the top of eleven feet in about nine people's hearing and neither of the two times had a person at the bottom of it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-fifth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-four of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-fifth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said that nobody in that yard has asked him one question this morning, and a man of about sixty-four said good afternoon and nothing else.

---

"I am sixty-one," said Iven Tallow, to about nine people at the top of eleven feet, at about ten past four, from the stones, "and I have held a thing for seventeen years and I have not named the condition on it and I am not going to name it this afternoon either. And I have been asked how many of you in this yard have asked somebody something out loud, and I am not going to say, and I want the not-saying entered somewhere and I know there is nowhere to enter it, and that is the whole of what I have come up that bank for."

---

Nobody entered it, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is a refusal, and at about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people heard it for the nineteenth time running.
