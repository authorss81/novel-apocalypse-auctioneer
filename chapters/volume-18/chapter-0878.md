# Chapter 878: The One Hundred And Sixty-Second Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Ten A Man Of About Forty-Eight Who Keeps A Tally Asked A Woman How Many People In That Yard Have Noticed That One Sentence Has Been Used About Two Different Things And She Said Out Loud That She Was Not Going To Count It And No Figure Came Of It And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and seventeen days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and seventy-six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and ninety-two days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and six days. Seven hundred and sixty-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eighty-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and eighty-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and fifty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and fifty-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and ninety-five days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and thirty-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and twenty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and Mavis Dorr said out loud at about ten that she is not going to count how many people in that yard have noticed that one sentence has been used about two different things, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and ninety-two mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and ninety-one. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and forty-two mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and sixty-two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and third of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a woman said out loud this morning that she is not going to count a thing.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and seventeenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and sixteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the eighteenth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood seventy-seven days, and a woman said out loud that she is not going to count a thing and no figure came of it, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and then said a figure, and he said the figure before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about four people said it too from further along.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is twenty-nine and has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and then he said a figure, and about four people said it after him at the same moment and out of step with each other.

"One hundred and eighty-three," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet said it after him too, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that this boy is counting the mornings a man of thirty-eight has stood in that yard, and that about four of them have said he has never once said so out loud what the number is for, and that about four of them have said that a boy who writes a number down before a clerk's hand has moved is not going to be asked about his own reasons by anybody in that yard this morning.

---

At about ten the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally asked the question, and he asked it standing about nine feet off the boards with his hands at his sides, and about nineteen people heard him ask it.

"How many of them," he said. "Not the wire. The other one. The one about a sentence that has been said about two things. How many people in this yard have noticed it and not said so."

---

And Mavis Dorr said no, and she said it out loud, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the whole of it.

---

"I am not going to count it," she said. "That is the whole of my answer and I am giving it in front of about nineteen people so that it cannot be said afterwards that I was asked quietly. Not because it is a secret. Because a number about people in this district goes on a page, and there is a heading over one space on that page that says whoever, and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk write one line on the back of somebody else's page two mornings ago and go up nine miles of hill with it. I am not going to be the reason there is a second one about people instead of a form."

And nobody counted it, and no figure came of it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man of about forty-eight has spent a working life saying a number into about four hundred yards of air and has just had one refused him in front of the whole yard, and that about four of them said he took it standing up, and that about four of them found that the hardest thing about it is that he did not look as though he had been struck.

---

"You have said that number out loud every afternoon for longer than I have been standing at the end of that table," he said, "and not one person in this yard has ever refused you one. I have been waiting about nine years for somebody to and I did not think it was going to be about that."

---

"I know," said Mavis Dorr. "That is why I said it out loud instead of saying it under my breath at about four feet off the boards. About four people at the top of eleven feet would have taken it as nothing and about nine people would have gone on as they were, and you have been standing out there every afternoon long enough for me to want it to be a thing that was said."

And then about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed that she had just told about nineteen people that she knows something about that sentence, and not one of the about nineteen asked her how she knows it, and about four of them have said since that the not-asking was the loudest thing that happened at about ten this morning.

---

Adrian Vale said one thing about the boy at about half past ten, and it was about the boy and not about himself.

---

"He said one hundred and eighty-three at about ten to eight this morning," he said, "and about nine people at the top of eleven feet know what he was counting and about four of them have known for about four days. He has never once told anybody what the number is for and he is not going to today, and I have stood in this yard one hundred and eighty-three mornings and I have never asked him and I am not going to this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that two people in this yard have now said out loud in about nineteen people's hearing that they are not going to ask, and that nobody has asked either of them why."

Sabra Holt said one thing at about eleven, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and about nineteen people heard it.

---

"There are four documents this district does not own," she said, "and four places where those three lines can be read, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that this morning a woman stood at those boards and would not put a fifth thing on the list of what this yard has, and that about four of them said she did it on purpose, and that about four of them said that a thing that is not put on a list is not in this district at all, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said that this is the second time in three days that somebody has refused to write a thing down and that both times were in front of me."

---

At about two the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into the middle of that yard and about nine people heard it and about four people said it after him out of step with each other, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he said it the same way he has said it every afternoon and that about four of them could not tell from the outside whether this morning had changed anything about him at all, and that about four of them hope it did and about four of them hope it did not.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and seventeenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and sixteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and seventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall could see that nothing was being said down there.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the fifty-first time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man of fifty-six has said that sentence at about four for fifty-one mornings and that about four people at the top of eleven feet have spent three days being careful about where they say it and where they do not, and that about four of them said that being careful is a thing you do about a wall and this yard has been being careful for about three days and nobody has ever admitted that out loud, and that about four of them found that this morning was the morning somebody admitted a different thing instead.

---

The light came off the boards at about half past four and went, and about nineteen people were at the foot of that bank, and about four of them said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard it and about nine people said nothing.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book and no line went on the back of it this morning and none has gone on it since the one hundred and thirty-ninth morning. Over the one name in that column there is a heading that says whoever and nobody has changed it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the name under it does not satisfy the rule and the space under it is empty. Six months is what a clerk counted and it is six and did not move and nobody has counted a seventh. The count of what this district has made is fourteen and there is no fifteenth of it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman refused to put a number about people on a page this morning and the fourteen did not move either. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much it is out is not in this district's arithmetic. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move this morning, because nothing has come down that lane in anybody's own hand since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight has said out loud this morning that he is not going to ask a boy of seventeen what a number is for and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
