# Chapter 861: The One Hundred And Forty-Fifth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of Thirty-Eight Went Down The Eleven Feet At About Half Past Three This Afternoon And Asked A Man Of About Sixty-Four One Question In The Ordinary Voice And Was Answered And The Answer Is Where The Wire Is And About Nineteen People Heard It And It Is Not On Anything And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and fifty-nine. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and seventy-five days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and eighty-nine days. Seven hundred and fifty days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and sixty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and sixty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and thirty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and forty-two days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and seventy-eight days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and eighteen marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of about sixty-four said a second sentence at the foot of that low wall this afternoon and about nineteen people heard it and it is not on anything, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and seventy-five mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and seventy-four. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and twenty-five mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and forty-five, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and eighty-sixth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a man of about sixty-four said a second sentence at the foot of that low wall at about half past three this afternoon and about nineteen people heard it and it is not on anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundredth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and ninety-nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninetieth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was asked one question this afternoon and answered it, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood sixty days, and a page went back up that lane two mornings ago with nothing written on it, and a sentence a man said at the foot of that wall this afternoon is not on that page or on any other, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and then said a figure, and he said the figure before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"One hundred and sixty-six," he said, at the near end of the first table.

About four people said it after him at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it after him this morning, and about four of them have said since that they stood at the top of that bank from about a quarter to eight and did not take their eyes off the bottom of it.

---

The man who puts the tables up was in the yard at about ten and put his trestle where he puts it, which is about four feet along from the near end of the second table, and he said good morning to about nine people and nothing else, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard has watched a man put a table in the same place for five mornings and that nobody has worked out what watching a man does.

---

Mavis Dorr said it out loud at about eleven, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nine people.

"I am going to say the thing I have been not saying since the one hundred and second morning," she said, "and I am going to say it at about eleven in about nine people's ears so that it is out before half past three. There is one man in this yard who has been asked one question in nine years and that was the one hundred and twentieth morning and he was not asked anything on the one hundred and forty-fourth or the one hundred and forty-third and I have been standing about four feet off this table for two mornings waiting to find out whether anybody in this yard was ever going to ask him a second one."

---

"None of us is going to," said Sabra Holt.

---

"I know," said Mavis Dorr. "I have known it since about two. I am saying it anyway, because a yard that is waiting for a person to do a thing and saying out loud that it is waiting is not the same as a yard that has decided the thing will not happen, and about nine people need to hear which of the two this is before about half past three."

---

Nobody said anything for a while, and the boards were wet with the light coming across them at an angle, and the second table stood about four feet along from the near end of the first with nothing on it but the lot book and a piece of wood.

---

At about quarter past three a man of thirty-eight was at the near end of the first table with his hands behind his back and then he was not, and he went across the top of that bank and down the eleven feet, which he has done about four times in nine years, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched him go down and about four of them came down after him about four feet and stopped.

---

At about half past three the two men were where they are every afternoon.

The man of about sixty-four had his back to that low wall and his hands behind him and the man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him with his elbows on his knees, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else.

---

About nineteen people were at the top of eleven feet and about four of them were about four feet down the slope, and a man of thirty-eight came the rest of the way and stood about nine feet off the near end of that low wall with nothing in his hands.

He had been carrying nine words since the one hundred and second morning. He has not said them at those boards and he did not say them then.

---

"You told me something on the one hundred and second morning," he said, in the ordinary voice. "You said it to me and I did not answer you, and I have been asked about that morning three times since by people who did not know what I had been told, and I have said nothing to any of them, and I am not going to say anything about that morning again."

---

Nobody at the foot of that bank said anything.

---

"I am going to ask you where it went," he said. "That is the question and it is the whole of it and I am not going to ask you anything after it. I am not going to ask you what it was for. I am not going to ask you what the four figures on that wall are for, because about nineteen people at the top of eleven feet have had that off a man of fifty-six twice a morning for longer than any of them have been standing there."

---

The man of about sixty-four did not move.

"It is at the turn," he said.

The two words went out into about four hundred yards of air and about nineteen people heard both of them and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had come down about four feet by then without deciding to.

---

"Not at the culvert," said the man of thirty-eight.

"The wire," he said, "is not at the culvert."

---

"No," said the man of about sixty-four. "That is where it was in my head for a long while and it is not where it is."

---

And then he said one more thing, which is the only other thing he said that afternoon, and he said it to the man of thirty-eight and not to the yard.

"I did not tell you it was there," he said. "I told you where it went. Those are two different sentences and about four people at the top of that bank are about to hear only one of them from the man they are looking at."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man of about sixty-four has said three, four and two words to that yard across a morning and an afternoon and a stretch of days and has never once made a sentence of them, and that the two-word one on the one hundred and forty-fifth afternoon is the first of them that was a whole answer to a question somebody had the right to ask.

---

The man of thirty-eight stood at about nine feet off that wall for a while and did not go up the eleven feet.

Mavis Dorr came down the last of it at about a quarter to four and stood about four feet off the near end of that low wall and said the thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, to about nineteen people.

"I heard it," she said, "and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and about four of them were standing where they could not have heard it and heard it anyway, and I want it said at about a quarter to four in about nineteen people's hearing that nobody has written it down, and that nobody is going to, and that that is not a decision anybody took this afternoon."

---

"You could write it down," said a man who digs loam, from about nine feet further along the wall. "There is a board."

---

"There is a board," said Mavis Dorr. "And there is a column over one space on a form that says whoever, and there is one name in it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet know that a name in that column is this yard, and about nineteen people in this yard are not one name. And that is about what the four of us found out on the one hundred and thirty-seventh morning and it is about what we have found out every morning since."

"So it is gone," said the man who digs loam.

"No," said the man of thirty-eight. "It is not gone and I am not going to say it in this yard again this afternoon, because he said it once and he is not a man who says a thing twice, and because about four people heard the two words and about four people heard the rest of it and I have not asked anybody in this yard which of the two they heard, and I would like this afternoon to go on being the afternoon it was."

---

Nobody asked him which of the two they had heard.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the thirty-fourth time running, and about four of them had been eleven feet above the man of thirty-eight before he had said the first of it.

---

At about half past four the man of about sixty-four went off up the bank, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched him go, and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched a man of thirty-eight stand at the foot of a low wall with his hands at his sides and not go up after him.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. Whoever is still the heading over the one name that is in that column, and that name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form has been empty since the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning. A clerk counted six months this morning and there is no seventh of it anywhere in this district and she is not going to look for one. Fourteen is the whole of what this district has made. Five is the whole of what it does not have and not one of the five has been paid for. Four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and by how much it is out nobody in this basin has worked out and nobody in this yard is going to work it out this afternoon either. The lane count is twenty-eight and it did not move, because this morning nobody came down that lane with a page of their own hand. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight is standing at about nine feet off the near end of that low wall and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.
