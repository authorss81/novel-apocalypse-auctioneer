# Chapter 865: The One Hundred And Forty-Ninth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Four This Afternoon The Woman Who Keeps A Scale Put Down Out Loud In The Ordinary Voice In About Nineteen People's Hearing The One Question Nobody In This Yard Has Asked And Nobody Answered It And The Reason Was Said Out Loud Once By A Person And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and sixty-three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and seventy-nine days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and ninety-three days. Seven hundred and fifty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and sixty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and seventy-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and forty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and forty-six days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and eighty-two days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and twenty-two marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a woman who keeps a scale put a question down out loud at about four this afternoon and nobody answered it, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and seventy-nine mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and seventy-eight. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and twenty-nine mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and forty-nine, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and ninetieth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about nineteen people heard the woman who keeps a scale at about four this afternoon and not one of them answered her.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fourth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninety-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fifth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood sixty-four days, and a page went back up that lane with nothing written on it, and a question was put down out loud at about four this afternoon and it went nowhere because there was nowhere for it to go, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

---

The man who makes the stone was at the near end of the first table from about a quarter to eight and did not lift anything at all this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that there are men in this yard who come every morning and cannot begin, and that about four of them have said that a yard which has a thing to do at the boards is a different shape from a yard which has not.

---

The woman who keeps a scale asked nobody anything at about ten and at about eleven and at about half past one, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman who has weighed things at the foot of that bank for nine years stood in three places this morning that she has not stood in since the one hundred and twenty-seventh morning, and that about four of them worked out at about half past one that she was walking the yard.

---

At about half past three the two men were where they are every afternoon and about nine people at the top of eleven feet could see that they said good afternoon and nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and nobody in this yard asked him anything about the two words he said on the one hundred and forty-fifth afternoon, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard that has something new has spent two mornings not using it.

---

At about four the man of fifty-six came across the eleven feet to that low wall and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were standing about four feet off him because it is public lot and he reads the four off it twice a morning and everybody knows that.

He put his hand flat on the stone.

---

Before his hand was on the stone the woman who keeps a scale said it.

---

"I am going to say a question," she said, in the ordinary voice, "and I am going to say it to about nineteen people at about four this afternoon, and I have not asked anybody anything since the one hundred and twenty-seventh morning and I am not asking anybody now. I am saying it to that bank."

---

Nobody said anything.

"The man of thirty-eight put a question down on the one hundred and twelfth morning," she said. "He has not said it at those boards since and he is not going to and everybody in this yard knows what it is. I have watched this yard say one question thirteen mornings running and get nowhere with it, and I have had a hand on that wall nine years, and I want to know one thing out loud and then I will not ask anything ever again."

She looked along the top of that bank and about nineteen people were on it and about four of them were on the slope.

"Is the question that man put down on the one hundred and twelfth morning ever going to be the thing the tally pays for," she said. "And if it is not, then what is."

---

Nobody said anything at all.

---

She stood there with her hand on the thing she had been weighing and she did not say it again, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman asked this yard a question at about four on the one hundred and forty-ninth morning and about nineteen people heard it and about nineteen people heard the same silence, and that the silence was not one person thinking and it was nineteen people not knowing in the same direction at the same moment.

---

She finished what she had been weighing and set it down, and a man of about thirty-two came across about nine feet of that yard and moved a trestle from one side of the second table to the other and went back, and about four people at the top of eleven feet walked about nine feet to the left and stood about four feet further off the boards than they had been standing, and the light came across the boards and went along them and nothing in that yard was said.

---

Adrian Vale was at about four feet off the near end of the first table when she said it and he has said since that he heard every word of it and that he was about nine feet from the man of fifty-six the whole while.

---

"You have heard me," said the woman who keeps a scale, to him, in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard her ask him a question. "I am not asking it again. I am asking it once and I have asked it, and I would like to know what you are going to do with it."

---

Adrian Vale looked at her and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched him and about four of them have said since that they could see a man deciding whether to speak and about four of them have said that he did not decide and it happened.

---

He said, "I am not going to say anything about that this afternoon."

---

And then, in the same voice, as though it were the same matter and not two matters, "and I am not going to carry it up nine miles of hill either, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have heard me say both of those things and there is nothing on any paper in this district that says I did."

---

Sabra Holt was at about four feet off the near end of the second table from about a quarter to eight and she said nothing at all out loud for the whole of it.

She did not say no and she did not say wait and she did not say that it was not hers to ask and she did not say that it was hers to ask. She stood with her hands in front of her and her mouth shut, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at her about four times during it and about four of them have said since that they have never in this block seen a woman with nineteen years and the third of the nine oldest keys keep her mouth shut through something, and that about four of them do not know whether she was deciding or already decided, and that about four of them noticed that the clerk was watching Sabra Holt and not the man of fifty-six.

---

The clerk had a page with nothing ruled on it in front of her on the boards and she did not put anything on it.

She did not write. She did not turn it over. She put her hand flat on the boards beside it and left it there, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk who has been told there is nothing to enter has been the easiest person in this yard to work beside all morning.

---

And then the man at the end of the second table said it, and he said it without getting up and without moving his forearms off the boards, and he said it once.

"The five figures a room pays for," he said, "are a toll, a split, a status, a ground and a bell. Not one of them is a figure in this district's own ledger. I have been at this table fifteen years and I have watched that ledger and I am telling you that a question is not one of the five, and that is not me saying there is no answer. That is me saying there is no column, and a thing with no column in it is not refused, it is not answered, and it is not even heard, and about nine people have been standing here for four days working out that a page with nothing on it is the only shape this yard has."

---

Nobody said anything to that.

---

And then the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the thirty-eighth time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for.

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the sentence came straight on the heels of a question it has nothing to do with, with a wall in it, at about four in the afternoon, in about nineteen people's hearing, and that about four of them noticed, and that about four of them have not said one word about it to anybody, and that about four of them have been waiting to find out whether anybody else noticed and have not wanted to be the one who said so first.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. Whoever is the heading over that one name, that name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty. Six months is what a clerk counted this morning and nothing else was counted this morning. Fourteen is what this district has made. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five has not been paid for either. A sheet at that gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much it is out is not a thing this basin has done. The lane count is twenty-eight and it did not move, because nobody came down that lane this morning with a page in their own hand. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
