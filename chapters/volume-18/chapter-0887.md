# Chapter 887: The ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FIRST Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Half Past Ten The Man Of About Twenty-Nine Who Drives The Cart Asked The Man Of Fifty-Six A Question Out Loud And One More Line Went On The Page At The Back And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and twenty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eighty-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and one days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and fifteen days. Seven hundred and seventy-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and ninety days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and ninety-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and sixty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and sixty-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and four days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and forty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and thirty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart asked the man of fifty-six a question out loud at about half past ten and one more line went on that page, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and one mornings and moved this morning from two hundred. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and fifty-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seventy-one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and that page at the back carries thirty-five lines and the lot book has none of the thirty-five, and three of them nobody in this district heard read and that is not going to be lower.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and twelfth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart asked him something out loud at about half past ten.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-sixth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and sixteenth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the twenty-seventh morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood eighty-six days, one more line went on the page at the back and it is not in the lot book and it is not in any other book, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the chalk along the edge of that second table had been gone over in the night by somebody who used the back of a thumb, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the chalk comes back every morning whether anybody puts it there or not and that nobody has ever admitted to putting it there.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and the light came off the boards at about four and went along them and found the far side of that second table, and about four of them have said since that there is a morning for everything in that yard and that the light has one too.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he said no figure this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen has not said a figure on the two mornings after a line went on that page and that about four of them think he is being careful and about four of them have not said what they think he is being careful about.

---

At about half past ten the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came in from the siding and asked the man of fifty-six a question out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, at about four feet off the near end of the first table.

---

"Is there anybody you would ask about them," he said.

---

And the man of fifty-six said, in the same words and at the same speed and flat, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and about four of them said the second part after the first out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that the question was not a bad one and that the answer was not about the question and that those are two separate facts about that yard.

---

Then the clerk read the morning out loud before she wrote anything and wrote one line on the page at the back of that lot book, and that page carries thirty-five lines and none of the thirty-five is in the lot book, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk write one line and about four of them have said since that it is the second of these and that a thing which has happened twice is not yet a practice.

---

The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally asked her, out loud, in about nineteen people's hearing, whether the number of lines on that page is the number of times anybody has asked, and the clerk said no out loud and gave the reason in one sentence.

---

"A line is a morning somebody asked on," she said. "It is not a count of the askings. If it were a count of the askings it would want an intercept and I have not got one and I am not going to make one up, and I am not going to write a figure down that I would then have to carry."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man asked for a figure and got a reason instead and that about four of them have said the reason was better than the figure would have been and about four of them have said they would have liked the figure. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that two people in that yard have now been asked for a figure by somebody who counts for a living and have both refused out loud, and that about four of them said the second refusal was easier and about four of them said that it was not.

---

Nothing came of that question and nothing was written and kept, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that two people in that yard have now said out loud that they are not going to count a thing and that neither of them has given a number and that about four of them have said they would like it written down that neither of them did.

---

Then the man at the end of the second table said one thing, and nobody asked him for it.

---

"I have put a rule on my own mouth out loud twice in this yard and I am not going to put a third one on for a page at the back of a book," he said. "I would like it noticed that I have not asked what a line is for either."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and about four of them have said since that a man of about thirty-seven at the end of that table has now said three things out loud in five mornings and none of them about himself, and that about four of them said the third one was the least of the three and about four of them said that was not the point. The man at the end of that table went back to what he does and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that nobody asked him for a fourth, and about four of them have said that if anybody did he would give the same answer, and about four of them have said they did not hear him say that.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the sixtieth time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-sixth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-five of them, and nobody went near him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that there is a man standing at the foot of that wall every afternoon saying nothing while a clerk writes down mornings a few feet away, and that about four of them have said they do not know whether he knows about the lines.

---

At about half past four the light came off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-five lines and none of them is in the lot book and three of the earlier ones are lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk counted, it did not go back this morning, and nobody has put it anywhere. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen has not moved on any morning of this stretch. This district does not have five things and the fifth of the five is unpaid and nothing on that back page pays any of them. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much the figure on that gatepost sheet is out nobody here has ever done. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move and two lines on a back page do not move it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. That ground at the foot of the low wall has been public lot since the one hundred and seventeenth morning afternoon and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. That bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and never run and it is not a lot. A man of about thirty-seven at the end of the second table has not been asked for the rest of it by anybody in that yard and he has not been thanked, and nobody in that yard has been given a bed or a wage or an office for a line on a page at the back of a book.
