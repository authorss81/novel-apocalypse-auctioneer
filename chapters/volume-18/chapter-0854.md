# Chapter 854: The One Hundred And Thirty-Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Ten This Morning About Nine People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Turned To The One Figure That Yard Makes Out Loud Every Morning And Asked A Man Of About Forty-Eight Whether It Could Go On That Page And He Said No Out Loud And A Man Of About Thirty-Two Said A Board Could Go Up That Lane On The Cart And The Man Who Drives The Cart Said He Stops At The Bottom And Not At The Room And Nothing Was Entered And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and ninety-three days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and fifty-two. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and sixty-eight days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and eighty-two days. Seven hundred and forty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and fifty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and sixty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and thirty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and thirty-five days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and seventy-one days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and eleven marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and ninety-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and at about ten this morning the man who keeps a tally was asked out loud whether the figure he says into about four hundred yards of air every morning could go on that page as this district's answer and he said no, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and sixty-eight mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and sixty-seven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and eighteen mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and thirty-eighth, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and seventy-ninth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and the man who keeps a tally was asked at about ten this morning whether his figure could go on that page and he said no out loud in about nine people's hearing, and the man of about thirty-two said a board could go up that lane on the cart and the man who drives the cart said he stops at the bottom and not at the room.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and ninety-third night of that run, having slept on four hundred and ninety-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the eighty-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the second time in nine years, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of that bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood fifty-three days, and three ways of getting a thing to a room nine miles up that hill were named out loud at those boards this morning and all three were closed by the person each one would have used, and a page came down that lane and it is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and then said a figure, and he said the figure before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"One hundred and fifty-nine," he said, at the near end of the first table.

About four people said it after him at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that they have stopped counting how many times a morning of a man of thirty-eight has been said in this yard and that they did not decide to stop and have not said why.

---

At about ten Mavis Dorr asked the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally a question, out loud, at about nine feet off the boards of the second table, in about nine people's hearing.

She said the question in the ordinary voice and she did not put her hands on the boards, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had not expected her to ask it and about four of them looked at the man of about forty-eight before he looked at her.

"Can your number go on that page," she said. "Not as your number. As this district's answer to the third line. That is all I am asking. You say it out loud every morning at about one and about nine people hear it and about four hundred yards of air take it, and I am asking whether that can be the thing we put on a room's paper."

---

The man of about forty-eight looked at her for a moment and then at the boards.

"No," he said.

---

"I am going to say why out loud," he said, "and I have said at about four feet off those boards before that I do not know what I am keeping this for, and I am going to say it again this morning because it is about to be useful to somebody. A figure I say into about four hundred yards of air is mine and it is a figure of work and it goes into about nine ears and into nobody's book. If a room nine miles up that hill writes it on that page as this district's answer, then a room has made one man into a district by writing down one figure of mine, and I would be that for as long as the paper is in a drawer, and I have never asked to be that and I am not going to be it on the strength of a lane count."

---

"You would be that," said Mavis Dorr, "and about nine people would know that you are that, and I am not going to argue with you about it."

---

Then the man of about thirty-two said the second thing, and he said it standing at the near end of the trestle that had been standing four mornings, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has been thinking about since about half past eight.

"A board goes up that lane," he said. "That is all. A board goes up the side of the hill with the cart and it goes in a room and a man in a room reads it and that is how a thing gets from a yard to a room, and I have been carrying things up that hill for about nine years and nobody has ever told me I could not."

---

"Then what would be on it," said Mavis Dorr.

---

"That is not my part," said the man of about thirty-two. "I have been putting boards up at about ten every morning for about nine years and I have never once been asked what to write on one and I am not going to start guessing in front of about nine people."

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart had the cart stopped about nine feet short, which is where it is on every morning this week, and he said the third thing without being asked for it.

"I do not go up that lane," he said. "I go as far as that wall. I have never gone up that lane in my life and I am not going to on the strength of a piece of paper, and if a board goes up that hill then it goes up on a shoulder and not on a cart, and a thing that goes up on a shoulder is a thing somebody carried."

---

"A thing that goes up on a shoulder," said Sabra Holt, from about four feet further on, "is a thing a person carried, and that is the same sentence I said at about a quarter to eleven on the one hundred and thirty-sixth morning about myself and a page in my hand, and I would like about nine people at the top of eleven feet to notice that this yard has now tried it three ways and every one of the three has come out at the place where a person would have to be the one carrying."

---

Nobody at the top of eleven feet said anything.

---

The man of about forty-eight said the last thing of the morning at about twenty past ten and said it to nobody in particular.

"You have closed three doors in about an hour and each one of them was closed by the man or the woman who would have had to walk it," he said, "and I am not going to stand here and tell about nine people at the top of eleven feet that this yard is being careful. A yard with three doors and about nineteen people who each shut their own is not a yard with three shut doors. It is a yard with no doors and about nineteen people who have each found out what that is like separately and on their own, and I have been in this yard since before the middle of the winter and I have not seen that before and I am not saying it is good."

---

At about eleven the turner came up the eleven feet and had not said *as sent*, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were standing at the near end of the second table waiting for a fourth thing to be tried and about four of them have said since that they waited until about half past three.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and ninety-third night of that run, having slept on four hundred and ninety-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the eighty-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the twenty-seventh time running.

---

At about ten to five the light went off the boards and about nine people were within about four hundred yards of them and about nine of them had come early.

The trestle was still standing at the near end of the second table and the man of about thirty-two had not come back up the bank for it. The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. The column carries one name and the heading over it still says whoever and the one name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is still empty. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid. Four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and nobody in this basin has computed by how much it is out. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move, because nobody came down that lane with a page in their own hand this morning, and the toll is ninety-eight and it is spent, and the ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five, and a man of thirty-eight is standing at about four feet off the near end of the first table and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the one hundred and thirty-eighth morning was the morning the yard found out it had nothing to send, and that the finding was made by about nine people separately and out loud, and that none of them said the word *nothing* and every one of them meant it.
