# Chapter 874: The One Hundred And Fifty-Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Page Came Down That Lane Yesterday And Lay On Those Boards All Night And At About Ten A Clerk Carried One Line On The Back Of It And Read The Morning Out Loud Before She Wrote It And Said Out Loud That She Was Not Going To Be Told Why She Was Doing It And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and thirteen days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and seventy-two. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and eighty-eight days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and two days. Seven hundred and sixty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and seventy-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and eighty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and fifty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and fifty-five days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and ninety-one days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and thirty-one marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and seventeen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a clerk carried one line on the back of a page that came down that lane and read the morning out loud before she wrote it, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and eighty-eight mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and eighty-seven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and thirty-eight mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and fifty-eight, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and ninety-ninth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a clerk carried one line on the back of that page and read the morning out loud before she wrote it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirteenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twelve of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fourteenth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood seventy-three days, and a clerk carried one line on the back of a page and read the morning out loud before she wrote it, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed that he looked at the page before he said it.

---

The page came down that lane at about a quarter past eight yesterday morning and it lay on the first table from about nine yesterday until the light went off the boards at about half past four yesterday, and it lay there all night, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a sheet of paper with somebody else's handwriting on it lay out in the open at the foot of that bank through the whole of a night and not one of the about nineteen people in that yard thought to cover it, and that about four of them found that the reason none of them covered it is that not one of them is the person it belongs to.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is twenty-nine and has not moved since, and he did not say a figure, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said the number after him out of step with each other.

At about ten the clerk turned the page over.

She did it in the ordinary way, the way a person turns a sheet that has been lying face up all night, and about four people at the top of eleven feet saw the back of it come up and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came about four feet further off the boards without deciding to.

---

"There is nothing on the back of this," she said. "I am going to write one line on the back of it and I am going to read it out loud before I write it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet are going to hear it before it is on this piece of paper or not, and I am doing that because a thing that has been read out loud before it is written down is a thing that somebody in this yard cannot say afterwards that nobody said."

She stood up and turned round about half a turn so that she was facing the bank instead of the boards.

---

"The one hundred and fifty-eighth morning," she said. "The second space on that form is still empty and I have not asked anybody for a name."

About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the whole of it, and nobody said anything, and she wrote it on the back of that page in her own hand in one line, and the second space on that form is still empty.

---

Sabra Holt asked her why at about a quarter past ten, and she asked it in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard the question.

"Why," said Sabra Holt.

---

"You are not going to be told why I am doing it," said the clerk, "and you did not tell me why you asked me to carry it yesterday morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have been waiting five mornings for one of us to give a reason and I am telling you at about a quarter past ten this morning that it is not going to be one of us. I said no yesterday and I am not giving a reason for the no. I did a thing this morning and I am not giving a reason for that either. Both of those are mine and I am keeping both of them."

"You were asked to carry the asking yesterday," said Sabra Holt. "You have written down that nobody asked for a name. Those are not the same thing."

---

"No," said the clerk, "and about four people at the top of eleven feet have noticed that I have not pretended they were."

The man who digs loam asked two questions at about half past ten, and he asked them in order and he waited for the answer to the first one.

---

"Does that line go in the book," he said.

"It does not go in the book," said the clerk. "The book is a Council's and there is a form in it with five lines on it and one of the five has got a name in it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have seen me say that I will not put about nineteen people into a space with a heading over it that says whoever. It goes up that lane in the hand of the man who brought this page down."

---

"And if it comes back blank," said the man who digs loam.

And the clerk said nothing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she heard all three words of that question and did not answer any part of it, and that the last thing anybody in this yard has come to expect from a morning at these boards is an answer.

---

At about one Adrian Vale said the thing that made the rest of the day go the way it went, and he said it standing at about four feet off the near end of the first table with the page behind him and about nine people heard it.

"I said on the one hundred and fifty-fourth afternoon that I would go up that lane the next time a page came down it," he said. "A page came down it yesterday at about a quarter past eight. I had all of yesterday afternoon and all of this morning and I have not gone, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have been watching me not go since about ten, and I am going to say why and then I am going to stop."

---

Nobody said anything.

"The line on the back of that page is hers," he said. "It is not mine and it is not about me and it does not ask anybody anything. If I take it up nine miles of hill I am asking the room a question, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet would then be able to say that a man of thirty-eight carried a line up there on the one hundred and fifty-eighth morning, and the day somebody asks that man what he wanted, he will have to stand there and admit he wanted something. He did not want anything yesterday morning and he does not want anything this afternoon, and a line in a clerk's hand on the back of a page is the one thing in this block that nobody in this yard has to be given anything for."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say anything to that, and about four of them have said since that a man of thirty-eight has now turned down the only errand this yard has had in two days, and that about four of them found that the reason he turned it down was not that it was trouble.

The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart stood his cart on public lot from about eleven and about four people watched him not get asked about it, and at about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into the middle of that yard and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and the page sat on the first table with one line on the back of it and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at the back of it about nine times between about one and about four.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirteenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twelve of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall could see that nothing was being said down there.

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the forty-seventh time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk read a line out loud at about ten this morning before she wrote it, and that about four of them have said since that they have been in this yard a long time and have not known before this morning that anybody in it could do that, and that about four of them have said that the doing of it is not the same as the reason for it and that this yard has now watched both halves of that in about five hours.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the page was still on the first table with one line on the back of it, and about nineteen people were still at the foot of that bank, and about four of them said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book and none of the thirty-three is the line that went on the back of the page that came down that lane. Whoever is over the one name in that column, that name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty and a clerk has written on the back of somebody else's page this morning that it is empty and that she has not asked anybody for a name. Six months is what a clerk counted and there is no seventh of it in this district. Fourteen is what this district has made and did not move this morning. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid and a public lot does not pay it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much it is out is not a thing anybody has done the arithmetic on. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move this morning, because nobody came down that lane with a page in their own hand. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight has turned down the carrying of one line up nine miles of hill and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
