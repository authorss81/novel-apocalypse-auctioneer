# Chapter 880: The One Hundred And Sixty-Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Morning Nothing Has Come Down That Lane In Three Mornings And A Month Was Not Counted This Morning And A Man Of Thirty-Eight Said Out Loud That He Is Going Up That Lane In The Morning With Nothing In His Hand And Has Not Been Given A Bed Or A Wage Or An Office And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and nineteen days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and seventy-eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and ninety-four days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and eight days. Seven hundred and sixty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eighty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and eighty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and fifty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and sixty-one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and ninety-seven days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and thirty-seven marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and twenty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nothing has come down that lane in ten mornings and a month was not counted this morning, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and ninety-four mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and ninety-three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and forty-four mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and sixty-four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and fifth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nothing has come down that lane in ten mornings and no month has been counted.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and nineteenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and eighteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the twentieth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood seventy-nine days, and nothing has come down that lane in ten mornings and no month has been counted, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and then said a figure, and he said the figure before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is twenty-nine and has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and then he said a figure, and about four people said it after him at the same moment and out of step with each other.

"One hundred and eighty-five," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet said it after him too, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a boy of seventeen has now said a figure out loud on seven mornings out of the ten, and that about four of them have said the three he did not say it on were the morning a page came down that lane, the morning after it, and a morning in between when nothing came down that lane at all, and that about four of them have said they do not think he is being careful.

---

Then the morning went ordinary, which is a thing about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said this block used to mean there was nothing to do, and about four of them said this morning that it means something else and about four of them said they did not ask what.

At about eleven the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart asked the clerk a question he had been holding since about ten, and he asked it at about four feet off the near end of the first table and about nineteen people heard it.

---

"Are you writing another one this morning," he said.

---

And she did not answer it straight, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman who has read about four hundred pages out loud at those boards in fifteen years took long enough over a question with four words in it that about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at each other.

---

"Yesterday morning I said that I did not know," she said, "and I said that I was not going to say that there was going to be another one and I was not going to say that there was not. About four people at the top of eleven feet heard me say both of those and about four of them have been waiting two mornings for me to change one of them, and I am not going to change either this morning, and I am going to say why in one sentence because about nine people at the top of eleven feet are owed one sentence. If I say yes then this yard has a rule. If I say no then this yard has a rule. A rule is a thing that has a morning in it and a rule is the one thing six mornings of this block have not had."

Nobody at the foot of that bank said anything to that, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk has told about nineteen people what she is not going to do with a thing she has already done once, and that about four of them said that is the whole of what anybody in this yard has wanted since the one hundred and fifty-eighth morning, and that about four of them found it a smaller thing than they had been hoping for and about four of them found it a larger one.

---

Sabra Holt said one thing at about half past eleven, and she said it standing at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and about nineteen people heard it.

---

"I have been the third of the nine oldest keys for nineteen years," she said, "and the hardest thing I have ever had to do at these boards was keep my mouth shut on the one hundred and forty-ninth morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have noticed that I have had three more mornings of doing it since and that I have not said one word about any of the three. I am not going to say what I make of it. I would like it noticed that it has happened and I am not going to say anything else about it."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at her, and about four of them have said since that a woman with nineteen years has now twice this week put a rule on her own mouth in front of witnesses, and that about four of them said the second time was harder than the first and that about four of them could see from about four feet off that second table that it was.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and nineteenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and eighteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall could see that nothing was being said down there.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the fifty-third time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for.

---

Adrian Vale said his one thing at about ten past four, and he said it out loud to about nineteen people before the light went off the boards, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he said it at the top of his voice on purpose.

"On the one hundred and fifty-fourth afternoon I said I would go up that lane with nothing in my hand so that nobody in that room could be told I came up it anyway," he said. "A page came down that lane on the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning and I did not go, and I have given a reason for that and the reason is on about four people's ears and not on any paper. I am going up that lane in the morning, and I am not going up it so that anybody knows. I have been at the foot of that bank one hundred and sixty-four mornings and I have never once been up that hill and about four people at the top of eleven feet know that. I want to find out what is up there. That is the whole of it and it is not about the asking and it is not about the wire and about four people at the top of eleven feet can decide for themselves whether it is worth anything."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and about four of them said after him, out of step with each other, that he was going, and about nine people did not say anything.

---

The light went off the boards at about half past four, and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people at the foot of that bank out of about nineteen, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book and none of the thirty-three went on it this week. The heading over the one name in that column says whoever and has said it since before the clerk came to that book, the name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody in this yard asked for it to be filled this week. Six months is what a clerk counted and it is six and did not move and no seventh of it was counted this morning. Fourteen is what this district has made and the fourteen has not moved on any morning this week. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this week. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much it is out is not a thing anybody in this basin has done this week or any other week. The lane count is twenty-nine and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight is going up that lane in the morning with nothing in his hand and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
