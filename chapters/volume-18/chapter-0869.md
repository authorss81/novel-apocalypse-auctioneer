# Chapter 869: The One Hundred And Fifty-Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month Three People At The Top Of That Bank Began A Sentence This Morning At About Ten And At About Eleven And At About Half Past One And None Of The Three Finished And Nobody Answered The One Hundred And Forty-Ninth Morning's Question And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and sixty-seven. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and eighty-three days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and ninety-seven days. Seven hundred and fifty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and seventy-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and seventy-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and forty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and fifty days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and eighty-six days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and twenty-six marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and twelve marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and three people at the top of that bank began a sentence this morning and none of the three finished it, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and eighty-three mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and eighty-two. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and thirty-three mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and fifty-three, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and ninety-fourth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and three people at the top of that bank began a sentence this morning and none of the three finished it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and eighth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninety-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the ninth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood sixty-eight days, and a page went back up that lane with nothing written on it, and three sentences were started at the top of that bank this morning and none of them was finished and none of them was entered, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

---

Nobody said a figure out loud at those boards at about ten to eight this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed that before the light had finished coming across them, and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen who has said a figure at those boards four mornings running did not say one this morning and that nobody asked him where it had gone.

---

At about ten the man who digs loam put his spade down and looked along the top of that bank at about nine people and said, "I have been trying since about a quarter to eight to work out what it is that we—"

---

A woman of about fifty with a barrow came along the rail behind him and he stopped and went back to the loam, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who digs loam has begun a sentence four times in nine years and that about four of them have said that a barrow coming past is the worst possible reason to stop talking and that about four of them did not know that until this morning.

---

At about eleven the turner, who is about forty-one, said, "About nineteen people heard her say it at about four on the one hundred and forty-ninth morning and not one of us has done anything about it in four mornings, and what I want to say is that I have—"

---

He stopped, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he put his thumb and his forefinger on his own jaw and left them there until about half past eleven, and that about four of them have said that a man who has said one word of his own name in that yard for four years put his fingers on his jaw and that about four of them did not know a turner did that.

---

At about half past one the clerk said it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

"I have been a clerk for fifteen years," she said, "and there is one thing I have never been able to do in that time and I have worked out what it is this morning and I—"

---

She put her hand flat on the boards and left it there and did not say the rest of it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she was about four feet from a page with nothing ruled on it and that about four of them have said that the sentence she did not finish went into a book of five lines with a heading over one space that says whoever.

---

Nobody answered the question that was put down at about four on the one hundred and forty-ninth morning.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the question has now been standing unanswered in that yard for a morning and an afternoon and a night and a morning, and that it is the only one of the three in this yard that anybody could answer if anybody had anything, and that about four of them have said that this is the worst of the three and about four of them have said that it is at least out in the open now, and that about four of them have said those two sentences out loud to about nine people this morning and that nobody picked either of them up.

---

Nobody picked either of them up.

---

At about two Mavis Dorr said the thing that had been assembled out of those three sentences, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people.

"Three people," she said, "and about four hours, and every one of us got as far as the word and not one of us got past it, and I want to say at about two this afternoon that I have been one of the three and that I have been the first one all morning and that the reason I did not finish is that I have got four sentences and I do not know which of the four is the one that is true, and I am not going to stand here and give you all four."

---

"Give us the shortest one," said the man of about thirty-two, who puts the tables up.

"That is the one I have least confidence in," said Mavis Dorr.

---

He laughed, once, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that it is the first time anybody in that yard has laughed in this block and that about four of them have said they did not find it funny and about four of them have said they were glad of it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and eighth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninety-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were watching that wall at about half past three and could see that nothing was being said down there.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the forty-second time running.

---

Adrian Vale said one sentence at about half past four and it was about himself and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard it.

"I have begun to be able to say those two words again without going up nine miles of hill to check them," he said, "and I do not trust it, and I am not going to say them out loud a second time in this yard as long as I am standing in it."

---

Nobody said anything about that either.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. Whoever is the heading over the one name in that column still, that name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty. Six months is what a clerk counted this morning and it is still six. Fourteen is what this district has made. Five is what this district does not have and not one of the five has been paid for. Four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at that gatepost, and by how much it is out is not a thing anybody in this basin has done the arithmetic on. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move, because nobody came down that lane this morning with a page in their own hand. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight is standing at about four feet off the near end of the first table and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.
