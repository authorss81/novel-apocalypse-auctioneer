# Chapter 889: The ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-THIRD Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Ten Past Eleven A Man Of Thirty-Eight Asked The Man Of Fifty-Six Out Loud Whether He Has Ever Written Anything Down And One More Line Went On That Page And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and twenty-eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eighty-seven. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and three days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and seventeen days. Seven hundred and seventy-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and ninety-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and ninety-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and sixty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and seventy days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and six days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and forty-six marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and thirty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of thirty-eight asked the man of fifty-six out loud whether he has ever written anything down and one more line went on that page, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and three mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and two. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and fifty-three mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seventy-three, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and that page at the back now carries thirty-six lines and the lot book has none of them, and three of the earlier ones are lines nobody in this district heard read.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and fourteenth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a man of thirty-eight asked him out loud at about ten past eleven whether he has ever written anything down.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-eighth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and eighteenth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the twenty-ninth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood eighty-eight days, a man of thirty-eight asked out loud in about nineteen people's hearing and one more line went on the page at the back, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the man who made the stone was at the near end of the low wall with a chisel and a piece of stone and no particular hurry, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has been making that stone for years got up earlier this morning than he has ever got up, and that about four of them have not said why.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and a man who makes the stone put his chisel down and picked it up again.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a boy of seventeen has now said no figure on three mornings out of the last four and that on one of the three he said it at the boards and on the other two he said it at the low wall, and that about four of them noticed which and about four of them did not.

---

At about ten past eleven Adrian Vale asked the man of fifty-six a question out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet went quiet while he asked it.

---

"Have you ever written anything down," he said.

---

And the man of fifty-six said, in the same words and at the same speed and flat, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it, and about four of them said the second part after the first out of step with each other.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the man who put that question in the one hundred and twelfth morning has now put a different one into the same yard, and that the answer came back the same and that about four of them said the same answer is not a worse answer and about four of them said it is not a better one either. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has put nine words in his pocket since the one hundred and second morning and has not said them said a question in that yard this morning instead, and that about four of them have said they do not know what the nine words are and about four of them have said they would not want to be told.

---

Then the clerk read the morning out loud before she wrote anything and wrote one line on the page at the back of that lot book, and that page carries thirty-six lines and none of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a line has now gone on that page on three mornings and that three is the first number in this stretch of mornings that anybody in that yard has been able to say out loud about anything that started on the one hundred and seventieth morning.

---

Nobody thanked the man who asked and nobody thanked the man who was asked, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they looked to see whether a man of thirty-eight would be thanked and that he did not look to see, and that about four of them found that harder to watch than the thanking would have been.

---

Then Adrian Vale said one thing more, out loud, to about nineteen people, at about ten past four, and he said it before the light went off the boards.

---

"That line is not mine," he said. "I am not carrying it up that hill and I am not going to claim it and if anybody in this yard wants to claim it they can say so out loud, and I have put one down on that page this morning and I would like it noticed that I did that and I am not going to be thanked for it."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them have said since that a man of thirty-eight has now said two things out loud in that yard on the same morning and about four of them said the first of them was a question and the second of them was not. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who told about nineteen people not to thank him for putting a line on a page has done the same thing twice on one morning, and that about four of them have said that asking for nothing is a thing he has been doing in that yard since the one hundred and twenty-fourth morning, and about four of them have said they did not notice it until this morning.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the sixty-second time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-eighth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-seven of them, and nobody went near him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the twenty-ninth morning running of not asking that man anything is now one morning short of thirty, and that about four of them have said that nobody has said out loud what happens at thirty and about four of them have said they would rather nobody did.

---

At about half past four the light came off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-six lines and none of the thirty-six is in the lot book and three of the earlier ones are lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk counted in a yard two mornings ago and it did not go back this morning. Fourteen things is what this district has made and the fourteen did not move this morning. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid, and a man asking a question in a yard is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that gatepost figure is out is a sum this basin has never done. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move and a question asked in a yard does not move it. The toll was ninety-eight and it is spent and nobody in that yard has ever watched it be spent twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall is public lot and has been since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and it is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and nothing was ever done with it and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight asked a question in that yard this morning and told about nineteen people not to thank him for it, and he has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
