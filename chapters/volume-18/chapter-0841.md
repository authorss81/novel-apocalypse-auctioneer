# Chapter 841: The One Hundred And Twenty-Fifth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Woman Asked A Boy Of Seventeen At Those Boards For A Figure Of Mornings On Which A Question Had Been Asked And He Gave Her A Reason Out Loud And Did Not Give Her A Figure And At About Two Four People Began Asking Each Other Things Out Loud And Nobody In That Yard Decided To Do It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and eighty days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and thirty-nine. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and fifty-five days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and sixty-nine days. Seven hundred and thirty days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and forty-four days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and forty-nine days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and nineteen days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and twenty-two days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and fifty-eight days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and ninety-eight marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and eighty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a woman asked a boy of seventeen at those boards for a figure of mornings on which a question had been asked, and he did not give her one, and he gave a reason out loud and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the reason, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and fifty-five mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and fifty-four. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and five mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and twenty-five, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and sixty-sixth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about four people at the top of eleven feet asked each other something out loud in the ordinary voice at about two in the afternoon and about four of them have said since that they did not decide to and that they would not be able to say who started.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eightieth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and seventy-nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventieth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of about thirty-two was at the near end of that second table putting a trestle up and took it down again and put it up again, which he has done about four mornings running and not on the one before, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood forty days, and a question was asked in that yard this afternoon by a person who is not a man of thirty-eight and the clerk wrote nothing and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

"One hundred and forty-six," said Orren Pike.

He said it at the near end of the first table at about ten to eight and nobody at the top of eleven feet said it after him, and about four of them have said since that they were listening for it.

---

The yard did what the yard does, and about four people at the top of eleven feet worked slower than the morning before, and about four of them have said since that they could not say what the yard was waiting for.

The man who digs loam turned over about four feet of ground and stopped, and started again, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed him stopping and none of them said one word, because a man who digs loam has stopped for a hundred reasons and the yard does not ask.

---

At about half past nine Mavis Dorr came to the near end of the first table where the boy was standing with his sheet against his chest.

"Orren," she said, and he waited, and she said, "I want a figure out of you and I am going to tell you what I want it for so that you can decide whether to give it, and I have never once in my life done that before this morning and I would like you to notice that I noticed it."

About nine people at the top of eleven feet stopped what they were doing, which is what about nine people at the top of eleven feet do when a person in that yard says the word *figure* out loud anywhere near those boards.

"I want the number of mornings on which somebody in this yard has asked somebody else in this yard a question out loud," said Mavis Dorr. "Not the man at the foot of the wall. Everybody. Including me."

---

The boy did not answer for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched him not answer.

"I do not have one," he said.

"You have got four years of that sheet in a coat."

"I have written every other thing anybody has said at these boards for four years," said the boy, "and I have not written a question, and that is not the same as not having one."

He turned the sheet about a quarter of the way round on the boards so that she could see the shape of it without being able to read any of the writing on it, which is how he has shown that sheet to about four people in four years and not to anybody else.

"There is a line for every morning since I was thirteen. On the one hundred and twelfth morning there is a line with a question in it, and on this morning there is a line with nothing in it yet, because it is not ten to eight, it is half past nine, and I write the morning at the end of it."

---

"And you could put a mark against the ones with a question in them," said Mavis Dorr.

"I could."

"Why not."

The boy looked at her for longer than he had looked at her at any point in about four years of standing about four feet off the same boards.

"Because a mark is a figure, and a figure is a promise that somebody counted something once in a place where a person could be found," he said. "If I put a mark against the mornings with a question in them then in about four years there is a page in this basin that says this district asked itself four questions in about four years, and there is not one word in that page about the four years of the sheet, and not one word about the man at the foot of that wall, and the page is going to be true and it is going to be about four lines long."

He put his hand flat on the boards.

"And I am seventeen, and I am the only person in this district keeping a thing for four years so that it cannot be got hold of, and I am not going to use it to make a figure out of a yard this afternoon that has not got to the end of the afternoon."

---

Nobody at the top of eleven feet said anything for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

"Four years," said Mavis Dorr. "That is a figure."

"That is a figure I have got and that is not the one you asked for," he said, "and I have said the difference out loud and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it. A man of thirty-eight asked a man of fifty-six a question on the one hundred and twelfth morning and the man asked for the asking to be entered and the clerk entered it, and that is the only line in four years of that sheet that has got anything in it that anybody can be asked about separately tomorrow."

Mavis Dorr put both hands flat on the boards of the first table, which she has not done in that yard in this run of mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at her hands.

"You are right," she said, "and I asked you for it in a yard and about nine people heard me ask and I have been told no in front of them. I am going to go and ask somebody else, and if they give me one I am going to come back and tell you that they gave me one, and you are going to have to sit there."

"There is no different one," said the boy.

"Then I will go and ask somebody else," said Mavis Dorr, and went and did not ask anybody else.

---

Adrian Vale was at about four feet off the near end of the first table and he had been in that yard one hundred and forty-six mornings, and he had listened to a boy give a woman a reason instead of a figure, and the reason was better than anything Adrian Vale had said at those boards in about fourteen mornings, and he had not known that on the one hundred and twenty-fourth morning, and he could not tell from where he was standing whether the boy had done it for him or in spite of him.

He had nine words in his mouth that he was not allowed to say at those boards and he did not say them.

---

The man of about thirty-two put a trestle up at the near end of the second table at about ten, took it down at about eleven, and put it up again and did not take it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at it, and about four of them have said since that he has taken it down at the same hour every morning for nine years and that nobody asked him to.

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the number into the middle of that yard in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and then he stood about four feet off the boards with his hands behind his back and waited.

---

At about two it began, and nobody can say which of the four of them it began with.

Mavis Dorr asked the man who drives the cart whether the gateway at the back of that yard had been scraped out or had always been that shape, and the man of about twenty-nine said he did not know and thought about it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard a question asked out loud in that yard at about two in the afternoon.

After the cart wheel finished turning the man who digs loam asked the man of about forty-eight whether the number he had said at about one changed on a morning when nobody came down that lane, and the man of about forty-eight said it did not, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the answer.

And then the man of about thirty-two, who had a trestle standing at the near end of the second table and had not taken it down, asked the man who digs loam whether the ground wanted turning at that end that week or at the other one, and the man who digs loam said he would look at it.

---

"Four," said Mavis Dorr, to nobody in particular, at about four feet off the boards, and then she stopped, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at her.

"I was going to say a figure and I have not got one," she said, "and I am not going to get one out of that boy, so I am not going to say it."

---

"There have been four questions in that yard this afternoon," said Sabra Holt, "and I have been standing in it since about ten, and not one of them was asked of the man at the foot of that wall, and I have been waiting all morning for one of them to be. I would like about four people at the top of eleven feet to notice that I have been waiting and it did not come, and that the yard has done four things this afternoon that it has never done before and none of them needed me."

Nobody answered her and she did not appear to want an answer.

"That is the first morning of nineteen years of holding the third of the nine oldest keys that I have been in a yard and not been the reason for anything in it," she said, "and I have got no idea what to do with the afternoon."

---

Mara Quill had the page at the back of the lot book open in front of her with her hand on the corner of it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at her hand, and she did not move it.

"Four questions," said the clerk, to nobody. "I have been the clerk of that book for fifteen years and I have had four lines written at the back of it in the whole of that time that anybody in this district can be asked about separately tomorrow, and there have been four questions asked in that yard this afternoon, and I am not writing one of them, and I have not got a reason, and I have not been asked for one this afternoon."

"Ask her," said Mavis Dorr. "You do not have to say why.""

"I have said four times in about nineteen mornings that I would not give a reason," said Mara Quill, "and a man of about forty-eight has been the only person in that yard who has never once asked me for one, and this afternoon he has not asked me for one either. About four people in this yard have been waiting eight mornings for a reason, and they have just watched me not be asked for one."

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of about sixty-four said good afternoon and nothing else, because that is not a question, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard both of the greetings and about four of them have said since that they have got good at telling one from the other.

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people heard it for the fourteenth time running, and about four of them heard it and thought about the four questions that had gone out into that yard at about two, and did not say one word about the two things being in the same afternoon.

---

The woman who keeps a scale was on her own step about forty feet off and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked across at her when the man of fifty-six said it, and she looked back at none of them.

Mavis Dorr stood at the boards with her hands flat on them and looked at the trestle the man of about thirty-two had left standing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at the trestle, and about four of them have said since that nobody in that yard was asked to leave it standing, and that it was the only thing done on purpose that day which was not a question.

Orren Pike wrote the morning down at the end of it, at about half past four, in the ordinary way, and there was no mark against the one hundred and twenty-fifth morning except the mark he puts against every morning, and the clerk did not write anything on the page at the back of that book, and the fifth of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and nobody in this basin has computed by how much it is out, and about four hundred yards of air carried four questions from the top of that bank to the foot of that wall between about two and about ten past two, and the man at the foot of that wall heard none of the four of them, and nobody has asked him whether he heard them.
