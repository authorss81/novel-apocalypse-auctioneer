# Chapter 884: The ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-EIGHTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month In The Afternoon On The Stones A Clerk Counted A Month Out Loud In About Nineteen People's Hearing And It Is Seven And It Was Six And It Was Not Entered Anywhere And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and twenty-three days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eighty-two. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and ninety-eight days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and twelve days. Seven hundred and seventy-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eighty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and ninety-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and sixty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and sixty-five days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and one days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and forty-one marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and twenty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and it moved this morning and it was six and it was said out loud and not entered. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a clerk counted a month out loud in the afternoon in about nineteen people's hearing and it is seven and it was six, and counting a month is not a thing this district made, it is a thing the month did, and the fourteen did not move this morning either.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and ninety-eight mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and ninety-seven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and forty-eight mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and sixty-eight, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back carries thirty-three lines and the count of counted months is not one of them, and three of those lines are ones nobody in this district ever heard read.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and ninth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and the count of counted months moved this morning and it was said out loud and it was not entered anywhere.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-third night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and thirteenth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the twenty-fourth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood eighty-three days, a month was counted in a mouth on the stones and it was not entered, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and nobody went past the bottom of that lane at all this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the lane is the same lane and that a yard notices a lane most when nothing is on it.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with one another. Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he said no figure this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them have said since that the last three mornings have been the quietest three mornings this yard has had since the one hundred and fifty-eighth, and that about four of them do not know yet whether that is a good thing.

---

The turner put his thumb on the chalk along the edge of that second table at about ten and found the place where the clerk rubs it out at the end of a month, and he did not say anything about it, and she looked at where his thumb was and about nine people at the top of eleven feet saw her look, and about four of them have said since that two people in that yard know where that chalk goes and neither of them has said so.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack and then weighed it again and got the same figure and said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman who weighs a thing twice and says nothing is not doing the arithmetic badly and is doing something else. Then the yard got on with what it does until the middle of the afternoon, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the middle of the afternoon is when that yard is at its most ordinary, and that about four of them said that they had noticed that only because the morning had been the opposite.

---

The man of about fifty with a barrow put his barrow down on the stones at about half past two and said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing.

---

"Does anybody know how long it has been," he said.

---

Nobody said anything to that at first, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at one another, and about four of them have said since that a question about a length of time is not a question about the four figures on that wall and that nobody in that yard had ever been asked it before. Then the clerk stood up from that board.

---

She did not say anything straight away. She put her hand flat on the boards and left it there, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it, and then she took her hand off and moved it and put it down again about an inch along, and about four of them have said since that a woman who is about to count something moves her hand twice, and that about four of them have not been able to stop thinking about the inch.

---

The light came along the boards while she did it, off the near end and up the edge and across the top of that second table and out the far side, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they watched a clerk's shadow go the length of a board in that yard and that they have never had anything to do while it happened. Orren Pike came up to that board and stood at about four feet off the near end of it and put his own finger on his own sheet an inch behind where her hand was, and his finger was not on the paper and it was not on hers, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have seen a boy do that twice before and about four of them have said since that he has never once been on the page and has never once been late.

---

Nobody went home. That is the thing about it. About nine people at the top of eleven feet stood on the stones for as long as it took and about four of them have said since that they could have gone at any point and did not, and that about four of them said they did not know what they were waiting for, and that about four of them said they did. About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who makes the stone put his chisel down about halfway through it and did not pick it up again until it was over, and that about four of them have said he has never stopped for anything in this yard before and that about four of them have not said what he stopped for. Then she said it, and she said it out loud, in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard it.

---

"Seven," she said.

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet said *seven* after her at the same moment and out of step with each other, and Orren Pike said *six* out loud on the boards before her hand had moved, and she did not stop him and she did not look at him. And then she said the other half of it out loud, in the same voice, to about nineteen people, because about nine people at the top of eleven feet were still standing there. "It was six yesterday and it is seven today and I counted it out loud and I have not written it anywhere," she said. "It is not in that book. It is not on the page at the back of that book. It is not on a gatepost and it is not on that wall and if this yard wants to know how long it has been, it has just been told in a yard and not on anything, and that is the whole of what happened this afternoon."

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk counted a month out loud in front of about nineteen people and did not put it on paper, and that about four of them have said that the last time anybody in this district counted anything out loud the figure went on a board before the end of the sentence. Orren Pike wrote nothing on his own sheet this afternoon and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he has never once failed to write a figure down, and that about four of them have said he has never once had a reason. Then Iven Tallow said one thing from the stones, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard it, and he did not look at the clerk while he said it.

---

"A thing can be held for a long time without anybody counting how long," he said. "I have done it. Nobody asked me to. This is not a complaint." About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them have said since that a man who has stood on that stone for nineteen years has said one sentence out loud this afternoon and it was not about the count and it was not about a condition, and that about four of them could not tell from the outside whether that sentence had anything to do with the counting, and about four of them have not said what they think it had to do with.

---

About four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the fifty-seventh time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-third night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-two of them, and nobody went near him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the twenty-fourth morning running of not asking that man anything happened on the same afternoon that a clerk counted a month out loud on those stones, and that about four of them have said those are two different things and about four of them have not said what makes them different.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it, and about four of them have said since that a yard that has said a number out loud in the afternoon is a yard that does not want to be finished for the day.

---

That page at the back carries thirty-three lines, the lot book has none of them, and three of the thirty-three are lines nobody in this district ever heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it was six yesterday and it was said out loud this afternoon and it is not on anything. Fourteen is what this district has made and it did not move this morning, and counting a month is not a thing this district made, it is a thing the month did. Five is the count of the things this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and nobody in this basin has ever done the sum on by how much that figure is out. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move and it does not move for a count either. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. That ground has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and the toll that made it was spent the same day, and neither is a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and nothing has been done with it since, and it is not a lot. Nobody has been given a bed or a wage or an office for saying a number out loud, and a man of thirty-eight has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
