# Chapter 888: The ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SECOND Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month Nobody Asked The Man Of Fifty-Six Anything Out Loud This Morning And The Page At The Back Did Not Move And The Clerk Wrote The Morning On It In Her Own Hand And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and twenty-seven days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eighty-six. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and two days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and sixteen days. Seven hundred and seventy-seven days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and ninety-one days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and ninety-six days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and sixty-six days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and sixty-nine days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and five days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and forty-five marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and thirty-one marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk named the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and two mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and one. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and fifty-two mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and seventy-two, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-five lines and it did not carry a thirty-sixth this morning, and the first asking was not on the page and it was not on anything and it cannot be recovered by anybody.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and thirteenth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet noticed that and said nothing.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-seventh night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-six of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and seventeenth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the twenty-eighth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood eighty-seven days, the page at the back did not move this morning and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand instead of taking a line for it, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart said out loud that he had been down to the siding before there was light and that the train on that siding had not moved and had not been moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them said it after him out of step with each other.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and nobody at the foot of that bank turned round. Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and then he said a figure, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other.

---

"One hundred and ninety-two," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

And then nothing was asked. That is the whole of that morning and it is the first morning in this stretch of mornings on which nobody in that yard asked the man of fifty-six anything at all, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that most of them got to about eleven before they noticed it, and that about four of them noticed earlier and told nobody, and that about four of them have not been able to say why they told nobody. About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the man at the end of the second table put his elbow on that table at about eleven and looked at the back page for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and that about four of them said he did not ask anything either, and about four of them have said that this morning the whole of that yard held its hand in.

---

Orren Pike asked her at about eleven, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, out loud and before her hand had moved, whether a morning with nothing in it is a line.

---

"It is not," she said. "A line is a morning somebody asked on. This morning nobody asked on it, so there is no line on it, and I am not going to write one because the day was empty and a day being empty is not a thing anybody has asked me to record."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard her say the first part and about four of them heard the second part and about four of them have said since that a clerk in that yard has just told about nineteen people that she will not write down a day because nothing happened on it, and that about four of them have said that this is the first time in fifteen years anybody in that yard has heard a reason for leaving a space empty.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and said nothing to anybody and nobody said anything to her, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman who has said nothing since the one hundred and forty-ninth morning spent a morning on which the whole of that yard was waiting for a sound and did not get one from her, and that about four of them said that they had not expected to and about four of them said that they had.

---

About half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into the middle of that yard and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that he said it in the same voice he says it in every afternoon and that on a morning when nothing was asked it sounded the same as on a morning when something was.

---

And then in the afternoon the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round, and she wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line, and she did not take a line for it. About nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do that and about four of them have said since that a clerk wrote a morning down that had nothing on it, and that about four of them said that she has named the day rather than left it out, and that those are not the same thing, and that about four of them have not said which of the two they would rather she had done. The clerk said afterwards that she had been asked to carry an asking on the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning and had said no out loud and given no reason, and that about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard her say that she was not going to give a reason for this either, and that about four of them have said since that the same woman has now declined two things in that yard in about nineteen days.

---

Then she said one thing out loud about it to about nineteen people, in the ordinary voice, and she said it once.

---

"I am a clerk and not a person who finds out," she said. "And there were askings before this morning and none of them was on that page, and I cannot tell you how many there were, and I am not going to tell you a number I have not got. The first one was not on the page and it was not on anything and nobody is going to be able to get it back."

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman said out loud in a yard that a figure nobody will ever have cannot be arrived at by anybody, and that about four of them have said that is a different sentence from refusing to count something and that about four of them have not been able to say what the difference is worth.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the sixty-first time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they heard it sixty-two times and that this morning was the first morning they heard it and could not afterwards remember having heard it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-seventh night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-six of them, and nobody went near him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the twenty-eighth morning running of not asking that man anything is the morning a clerk wrote down a day on which nothing was asked, and that about four of them have said those are two different kinds of nothing and about four of them have not said what makes them different.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-five lines and it did not carry a thirty-sixth this morning, and not one of the thirty-five is in the lot book and three of them were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and it did not move this morning, and a morning written on a page is not a fifteenth of it. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid, and a morning written on a page is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and the sum on by how much it is out has not been started by anybody in this basin. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move and a morning written on a page is not a page coming down a lane. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent, and a morning written on a page does not spend it again. The ground at the foot of that low wall was made public lot on the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and has stayed that and is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days, was not run, and is not a lot. Nobody was thanked this morning because there was nothing to thank anybody for, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
