# Chapter 844: The One Hundred And Twenty-Eighth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month About Four People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Said Out Loud In That Yard That The Question Asked At Those Boards The Morning Before Was Not The Asking Of A Man Of Thirty-Eight And A Man At The End Of The Second Table Said One Thing About The Column And The Heading Over It And Nobody Answered Him And A Boy Of Seventeen Said His Figure And Then Said Nothing At All For The Rest Of The Day

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and eighty-three days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and forty-two. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and fifty-eight days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and seventy-two days. Seven hundred and thirty-three days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and forty-seven days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and fifty-two days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and twenty-two days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and twenty-five days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and sixty-one days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and one marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and eighty-seven marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man at the end of the second table said one thing out loud about the column and the heading over it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not answer him, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and fifty-eight mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and fifty-seven. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and eight mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and twenty-eight, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and sixty-ninth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said out loud after the asking had stopped that it was not the asking of a man of thirty-eight, and about four of them said nothing at all, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard both halves.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-third night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a woman who keeps a scale was on her own step about forty feet off from about eleven and did not come up the bank, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at her about four times and she did not look back at any of them, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of that bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood forty-three days, and a thing was said at those boards this morning that about four people at the top of eleven feet understood and about four of them have not said one word about, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

Orren Pike said his figure out loud at about ten to eight on the one hundred and twenty-eighth morning and then said nothing at all for the rest of the day, and he has said that figure every morning for four years and he has never once stopped in the middle of a morning about it, and the man at the end of the second table looked at him once and then looked away, and about four people at the top of eleven feet saw him do it.

---

The yard did what the yard does, and about four people at the top of eleven feet got to those boards earlier than they got to them the morning before, and about four of them have said since that they were not doing anything about it.

Mavis Dorr was at the near end of the first table at about ten to nine with her hands on it, and Orren Pike was at the near end of the second table with his sheet against his chest, and about four people at the top of eleven feet were standing in the place where a question had been asked the morning before as if it were a place that could be asked at again.

---

"It was not his," said Mavis Dorr.

She said it in the ordinary voice at about ten to nine to about nine people at the top of eleven feet, and about four of them said nothing at all, and about four of them have said since that the sentence is about four words long and that it is the shortest sentence anybody in that yard has said out loud this week and that it went about four hundred yards in about half a second.

---

"Whose," said a man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart.

---

"The man of thirty-eight's," said Mavis Dorr. "That is what I am saying. It was not the man of thirty-eight's. He has not asked anything in this yard since the one hundred and twenty-second morning and everybody at the top of these eleven feet knows that, and yesterday afternoon a woman with a beam asked it and he was standing about four feet off that table while she did it, and I would like about nine people to hear me say that out loud this morning before the yard gets back to work."

---

Nobody answered her.

---

"It does not make it a different question," said the man of about twenty-nine.

"No," said Mavis Dorr, "it does not make it a different question, and that is not what I said, and I am not going to be asked at about ten to nine in the morning whether I have said a different question from the one that was asked, because the question was the same and the person was not, and I do not know what a person is worth in that and I am not going to work it out at these boards before about eleven."

---

The man at the end of the second table had his forearms on the boards and he had been in that yard for fifteen years and his line had been written at about twenty-two on no page, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had been waiting since about ten to nine for him to say something about the column, and he said it at about half past ten.

"The heading over that column says whoever," he said, "and whoever has been in this yard for about four years and about four of us have been in it the whole time, and about nine of us have been told by about four different people that a room nine miles up that hill pays for a name in a column, and I have been at this table for fifteen years and I can tell you what the column is for, and it is not for a name and it is not for whoever. It is for the person who reads a thing out loud."

He put one finger on the boards about four inches from the lot book.

"There has not been one thing read out loud in this yard by anybody in this yard since I have been at this table, and the one name in that column is not mine and the heading over it still says whoever, and there is a second space on a form nine miles up that hill with somebody else's heading over it and it is empty, and it is going to be empty this afternoon."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not answer him.

---

"You have just said four things," said Mavis Dorr.

"I have said four things," said the man at the end of the second table, "and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have not answered one of them, and I have been saying things at these boards for about four years that nobody answers, and I have got a fifth one written at the side of my hand from about four days ago, and I am not going to say it this morning, and I would like about four people at the top of eleven feet to notice that I have not said it this morning and that nobody has asked me for it, and that the only person in this yard who has never once asked me for anything is the woman who asked the question yesterday afternoon and she is on her step about forty feet off and she is not going to come up this bank for me."

---

Adrian Vale was at about four feet off the near end of the first table and he heard the whole of it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at him while the man at the end of the second table was saying it, and about four of them have said since that they were looking at him because they were waiting to see whether a man of thirty-eight was going to say the thing that the fifth line is about, and that he did not, and that he has said nothing in that yard since the one hundred and twenty-second morning except the word *staying* on the one hundred and twenty-fourth.

He had a shape in that yard that he had used three times in about fourteen mornings and he did not use it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they have got good at watching him not use it.

---

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the number into the middle of that yard in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at the man at the end of the second table while he said it, because the man at the end of that table has never once said a figure out loud in that yard, and about four of them have said since that they were checking.

---

The woman who keeps a scale was on her own step about forty feet off and she had the beam across her knees and she was doing nothing to it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked across at her at about ten, at about eleven, at about one and at about two, and she did not look back at any of them, and about four of them have said since that a woman who has been weighing things at the foot of that bank for nine years came up about four feet of that bank yesterday afternoon and asked a man of fifty-six a question out loud, and that this morning she has been on her step about forty feet off with a beam across her knees doing nothing to it, and that there is nobody in that yard who can say what the two of those are.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-third night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-two of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-third afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four said good afternoon and nothing else, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall at about half past three could see that nothing was being said down there either.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people heard it for the seventeenth time running.

---

Mara Quill had the page at the back of the lot book open on the boards all morning and she did not write on it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk leave a book open at a page she has not written on for about eight mornings, and about four of them have said since that the woman who asked the question at those boards yesterday afternoon is on her own step about forty feet off, and that the page at the back of that book has thirty-three lines on it, and that there is a person in that yard who has asked a question and a person in that yard who has not been asked anything, and that neither of those two facts is on the page and that the clerk has not been asked to put either of them there.
