# Chapter 882: The ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-SIXTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of Thirty-Eight Came Back Over That Bank At About Half Past Four And Said One Sentence Out Loud About A Door At The Top Of That Lane And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and twenty-one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eighty. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and ninety-six days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and ten days. Seven hundred and seventy-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eighty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and ninety days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and sixty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and sixty-three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and ninety-nine days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and thirty-nine marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and twenty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of thirty-eight came back over the bank at about half past three and said one sentence about a door, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and ninety-six mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and ninety-five. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and forty-six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and sixty-six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and thirty-three lines are on the page at the back of that book and none of them is in the lot book, and three of them are lines nobody in this district heard read and nobody is going to hear the other thirty read either.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and seventh of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a man of thirty-eight said one sentence at about half past three about a door at the top of that lane.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-first night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and eleventh afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the twenty-second morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood eighty-one days, a man of thirty-eight came down that lane and brought back nothing that anybody could enter, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the man who digs loam was already at the bottom of that lane with his barrow, and he said that a man of thirty-eight had gone up it before there was light on the tops and had nothing in his hand, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them said it after him out of step with each other.

---

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* together and out of step with one another.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a boy of seventeen said no figure yesterday and said no figure today and that nobody in that yard has asked him why and nobody is going to.

---

The second table went up at about ten and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up put it up one trestle lighter than he has been putting it up, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet noticed that and about four of them have said since that a table is lighter because a man is missing from it and that they did not know a table could be lighter for that.

---

Then the clerk read out the morning before she wrote it, which she does not do every morning, and read it out to about nine people at the top of eleven feet, and about four of them have said since that a woman with fifteen years at that book read a morning out loud that had nothing new in it, and that she read it out loud in the ordinary voice, and that is a different thing from saying it mattered. She read the whole of it, including the morning number, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard every word of it, and about four of them have said since that about four hundred pages have gone through that clerk's mouth at those boards in fifteen years and that nobody has ever counted how many of them she read twice.

---

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into the middle of that yard and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and about four of them at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, which is a thing about four people have said since he stopped noticing that they do it.

---

At about eleven the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart asked the woman who keeps a scale whether the cart should go up the lane this afternoon, and she looked at him, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman who has said nothing to anybody since the one hundred and forty-ninth morning looked at a man for a while instead of answering him, and that nobody in that yard has said since what she was thinking, and that about four of them have stopped waiting to be told. Then she put the sack on the scale and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she wrote nothing down, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet saw her see it.

---

At about one the turner asked, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing, whether a man who is not in a yard is still in it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the question and about four of them have said since that it was a question about a rule and that there is no rule in that yard about a man who is not in it, and that this is the fifth morning in a row that a thing has been asked that there is nothing to answer it with.

---

Nobody answered the turner out loud. Then Mavis Dorr asked the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart whether the cart goes up that lane on the day the loam goes, and the man said that the cart goes where it is sent, and Mavis Dorr said that is not the same thing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard that and about four of them have said since that Mavis Dorr said a thing this morning that was not a question about the four figures on that wall, and that it was the first thing she had said in four mornings that was about anything else at all.

---

At about two Iven Tallow was not in that yard and the man who digs loam said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man of sixty-one turns up on that low wall on that afternoon more often than not and that this morning he did not, and that nobody asked him where he was. At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the fifty-fifth time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for. At about half past three the man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-first night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty of them, and he stood there with his back to the stones, and nobody went near him, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the twenty-second morning running of nobody asking that man anything is the longest anybody can remember, and that about four of them have said they do not think that is an accident and have not said what they think it is. A man of sixty-one was on that stone for the one hundred and eleventh afternoon and said good afternoon and said nothing else, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he said it to the man at the foot of that wall and not to the yard.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

At about half past four Adrian Vale came over the top of that bank with nothing in his hand, and about four people at the top of eleven feet saw him do it and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said the four after each other and out of step while they did it.

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet saw him come over that bank and about four of them have said since that he came up it the way a man comes up a bank when he has walked nine miles and come back down nine, and that his boots were the only thing about him that had changed. He said one sentence, and he said it out loud to about nineteen people at the foot of that bank, and then he did not say anything else for the rest of that evening.

---

"There is a door at the top of that lane and a step worn in the middle of it and a slate on the wall beside the door with figures chalked on it in a hand I have not seen before, and I knocked for a while, and nobody came."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and about four of them said the word *door* out loud at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that a yard that has spent a hundred and sixty-five mornings counting things it cannot use has just been told that there is a door, and that nobody in this basin has walked up that lane to knock on it in a long while, and that four of them have not stopped saying the word. The clerk looked at him and then at the board, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she did not enter a line about the slate, and that she entered the morning, and that a slate with figures on it nine miles up a hill is not a thing this district has a column for.

---

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into the middle of that yard and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that a man can come down nine miles of hill and say one sentence and then still be in a yard where somebody else is saying a number into the air for nobody.

---

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. Six is what a clerk has counted and it is six and did not move. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move this morning. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and the fifth of them is unpaid on ten mornings running. A sheet at a gatepost says four hundred and eleven and by how much it is out is a sum nobody in this basin has ever sat down to. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move this morning because a man coming down that lane is not a page coming down it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent, and the ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was put in at four hundred and fifty days, it was never called, and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight came back down nine miles of hill with nothing in his hand and a sentence in his mouth, and he has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told that he is a good man.
