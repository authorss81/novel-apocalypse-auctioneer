# Chapter 850: The One Hundred And Thirty-Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month About Nineteen People In That Yard Were Standing Within About Four Hundred Yards Of Those Boards At About Half Past One And About Nine Of Them Had Come Early And A Woman Said The Whole Of The Shape Of That Yard Out Loud At The Near End Of The Second Table And About Nineteen People Heard It And No Person In That Yard Has Decided To Make It So And No Person Has Decided Not To

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and eighty-nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and forty-eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and sixty-four days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and seventy-eight days. Seven hundred and thirty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and fifty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and fifty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and twenty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and thirty-one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and sixty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and seven marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and ninety-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and about nineteen people in that yard were standing within about four hundred yards of those boards at about half past one and about nine of them had come early, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and sixty-four mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and sixty-three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and fourteen mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and thirty-four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and seventy-fifth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a woman said the whole of the shape of that yard out loud at the near end of the second table and about nineteen people heard it, and no person in that yard has decided to make it so and no person has decided not to.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-ninth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the second time in nine years and the first time in about fourteen mornings, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood forty-nine days, and the asking has a shape and nobody drew it, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

"One hundred and fifty-five," said Orren Pike.

He said it at the near end of the first table at about ten to eight and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said it after him at the same moment and in step with each other, and about four of them have said since that they have never once said a figure after a boy in step with each other, and nobody in that yard has said out loud why.

---

Orren Pike said his figure at about ten to eight and about nine people said it after him at the same moment and in step with each other, and about four of them have said since that a figure said after by about nine people at once is not a thing that happens in a yard, and that on the one hundred and thirty-fourth morning it happened before the light was properly on the boards, and that the boy looked round and saw it and did not write it down.

---

The trestle was still standing at the near end of the second table at about ten, and the man of about thirty-two came up the bank at about half past ten and looked at it and did not take it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched a man who has taken a trestle down at the same hour every morning for about nine years look at one that has been standing since about ten the morning before, and about nine of them did not say one word, and about four of them have said since that the not-taking-it-down is the first thing in about six days that anybody in that yard has done on purpose and said nothing about.

---

At about half past one there were about nineteen people standing within about four hundred yards of those boards.

About nine of them had come early. The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart had the cart stopped about nine feet short, which he has done every morning since the one hundred and twenty-ninth and which is now the sixth morning of it. The man who digs loam had his spade down. The woman who keeps a scale was on her own step about forty feet off and had not come up. Mara Quill had the lot book open at the five lines and not at the page at the back of it. The man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was about four feet off the boards in the place he stands in. Sabra Holt was at the boards. Adrian Vale was at about four feet off the near end of the first table and had not come to the second table on any morning this week, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at him standing there and about four of them have said since that this morning he was the only person in that yard who came to the same place he always comes to, and that about four of them did not know what to do with that.

Nothing was being asked.

---

At about twenty to two Mavis Dorr put both hands flat on the boards of the second table and about nineteen people heard what she said, because about four hundred yards of air in that yard carries a voice the way it carries everything else anybody in that yard has ever said.

"I am going to say the whole of it and I am going to say it once and I am not going to be asked whether I have a right to say it, and if anybody asks me that I am going to carry on."

---

About nineteen people did not make a sound.

---

"There are about nineteen people standing within about four hundred yards of these boards on the one hundred and thirty-fourth morning and about nine of the nineteen got here before half past one and I do not know which nine and neither do you, and I am going to tell you what this yard has got, and then I am going to stop, and then it will be the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning and this afternoon will be over and nobody will be able to check any of it except the people who were standing in it."

She took her hands off the boards and put them flat again.

"It has got a question. That is the whole of what it has got. A man of thirty-eight asked a man of fifty-six what the four figures on that wall are for on the one hundred and twelfth morning and a clerk wrote down that the asking happened, and then a woman with a beam asked the same question at about ten to two on the one hundred and twenty-seventh morning, and a man of about forty-eight asked a clerk a question about it at about ten to eight on the one hundred and thirty-second morning, and a man who digs loam asked a man about his number at about half past ten, and about four people asked a man of about forty-one what a room had asked him, and I asked a boy for a figure on the one hundred and twenty-fifth morning and he said no to me out loud and gave a reason, and not one of us arranged any of it and not one of us has been asked whether we were going to."

---

"Nineteen people in that yard can be asked separately tomorrow about what they did at those boards this morning," said Mavis Dorr, "and about nine of them have not decided to be asked, and the nine who have not decided to be asked are not going to be asked, and I am saying that here so that the ones who have decided to be asked know that the ones who have not are not going to come round afterwards to say they were not there."

---

Nobody said anything.

---

"Here is what it has not got," said Mavis Dorr. "It has not got a rule. It has not got an hour. It has not got a place, and the nine people standing at these boards at about half past one on the one hundred and thirty-first morning were not standing in a place, and a woman of fifty-two with nineteen years and the third of the nine oldest keys told us that at about twenty to two and about nine of us did not disagree with her. It has not got a column, and the column on that book is for whoever read a thing out loud and nothing has been read out loud in this yard, and the one name in it does not satisfy the rule and the heading over it still says whoever and the second space on that form nine miles up that hill is still empty and it is going to be empty at about half past four this afternoon."

She looked down about eleven feet.

"It has not got a page. That page at the back of that book has thirty-three lines on it and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and three of the thirty-three are ones nobody in this district heard read and that count is not going to be lower, and the clerk has said out loud that she is not going to be asked to keep a thing that has no column, and she said it at about ten to eight on the one hundred and thirty-second morning to about nine people, and about four of the nine heard both halves of it."

---

"And it has not got a person," said Mavis Dorr. "Nobody in this yard is in charge of it. I am not. The woman with the beam is not. The man of thirty-eight is not and he has not asked anything since the one hundred and twenty-second morning and he said yesterday afternoon that he was not going to beat her to it. The clerk is not, and she will tell you that herself. The man of about forty-eight is not, and he keeps a number. A man of sixty-one said out loud from the stones on the one hundred and thirtieth morning that he was not going to say how many of us have asked anybody anything, and he meant it, and he has not come up that bank once in this run of mornings, and he is not a clerk and he does not keep a count of anybody."

---

She stopped for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall.

"And I am not going to say the last part," she said, "and I want about four of the nineteen to notice that I have stopped on purpose and that I am going to go on stopping on purpose until somebody in this yard makes me go on, and I am not going to be asked to make a rule about it and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I have put my hands flat on this table and about nineteen people have heard me and that is the whole of what I have done today."

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman stood at the boards of the second table at about twenty to two on the one hundred and thirty-fourth morning and gave about nineteen people the whole of what a thing is and the whole of what it is not, in that order, and stopped before the last part, and that the stopping is the only part any of them have been able to remember since, and that about four of them have not been able to remember it as a thing that was done on purpose either.

---

The turner had come up the eleven feet at about half past one and had not said *as sent*, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at him when Mavis Dorr said that no person was in charge of it, and he put a hand out flat at nothing in particular.

"I have been up nine miles twice this week refusing to say a thing," he said, "and I would like to say out loud that I heard all of that, and that a woman has just described the thing I have been refusing to say, and that she described it better than I would have, and that I am not going to take any of it up that hill, and that if a room nine miles up that hill asks me on the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning what this district has got I am going to say that I stood in a yard at about twenty to two and listened to a woman say it out loud, and that is the only sentence I am going to take up there, and that I have not got a page and I am not going to write one."

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-ninth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was not asked one question this morning by anybody in that yard, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall at about half past three could see that nothing was being said down there, and about four of them have said since that the whole of the one hundred and thirty-fourth morning came up eleven feet and the man at the bottom of it is the only person in that yard it has not reached, and that he is on the four hundred and eighty-ninth night of that run and has been asked one question in his life.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the twenty-third time running, and about four of the nineteen were standing at the top of that bank when they heard it and about four of them were within about four hundred yards of those boards and about nine of them had come to that bank that morning to do something they have not said out loud what it was.

Nobody asked him anything that afternoon. The last person in that yard to ask him a question had been a woman with a beam at about ten to two on the one hundred and twenty-seventh morning, and about nineteen people in that yard can be asked separately tomorrow about that, and about nine of them have not decided to be asked, and the man at the foot of that low wall has not been asked one question since.

---

The light went off the boards at about ten to five and about nineteen people were within about four hundred yards of them, and about nine of them had come early, and about four of them put their hands in their pockets one at a time and said they had to go, and the first one to go was the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart, and the cart had been standing nine feet short for the third morning running.

The trestle was still standing at the near end of the second table and the man of about thirty-two had not come back up the bank for it. The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book and not one of the thirty-three is about a question. The column carries one name and the heading over it still says whoever and the one name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is still empty. The count of counted months is six and it is six on every morning this block has run and it is not going to be seven on the one hundred and thirty-fifth. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid. Four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and nobody in this basin has computed by how much it is out. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move, because nobody came down that lane this morning with a page in their own hand, and the toll is ninety-eight and it is spent, and the ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five, and a man of thirty-eight is standing at about four feet off the near end of the first table with his hands behind his back and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.

A thing that one man started in a yard on the one hundred and twelfth morning is now a thing that about nineteen people do, and no person in that yard has decided to make it so, and no person in that yard has decided not to, and the man who did not say the last part is standing at the boards of the second table with her hands flat on them and she is not going to say it tomorrow either unless somebody makes her, and the boy of seventeen put a mark in his own sheet on the one hundred and thirty-third morning and has not said out loud what it is for, and the figure on the boards of the second table has a shape and the shape is nine people arriving early and not one of them knowing why, and about four feet of stone and about eleven feet of bank and about four hundred yards of air carried all of it and carried none of it anywhere, and the man at the foot of that low wall heard every word of the four figures and none of the rest of it, and nobody has asked him about any part of it.
