# Chapter 866: The One Hundred And Fiftieth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man Of About Forty-Eight Who Keeps A Tally Asked The Man Of Fifty-Six At About Half Past Three This Afternoon What The Figure He Says Out Loud In That Yard Every Morning Is For And He Was Given The Sentence He Says About A Wall Every Morning At About Four And About Four People Noticed And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and five days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and sixty-four. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and eighty days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and ninety-four days. Seven hundred and fifty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and sixty-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and seventy-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and forty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and forty-seven days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and eighty-three days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and twenty-three marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and nine marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was given at about half past three this afternoon the sentence a man of fifty-six says about a wall every morning at about four, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and eighty mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and seventy-nine. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and thirty mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and fifty, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and ninety-first of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard the man of about forty-eight get his sentence at about half past three and have not said one word about it since.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and four of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninety-fifth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the sixth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood sixty-five days, and a page went back up that lane with nothing written on it, and a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally was given a sentence at about half past three this afternoon that does not belong to him or to his figure or to this bank, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and then said a figure, and he said the figure before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"One hundred and seventy-one," he said, at the near end of the first table.

About four people said it after him at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said it after him too, and about four of them have said since that a boy said that figure this morning with his chin up, which is a thing he has not done, and that about four of them have decided not to say why.

---

The morning went the way mornings in this block have been going. The man of about thirty-two put his trestle about four feet along from the near end of the second table. The man who digs loam went about nine feet of the eleven feet and came back up it. A woman of about fifty with a barrow came along the rail and turned back at the end of it and did not stop, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman with a barrow has been coming down that bank and turning back for about four mornings and that nobody has said one word to her and that about four of them have started getting to the top of that bank early so as to be there when she does it.

---

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the number into the middle of that yard and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who keeps a figure has got a way of touching himself when he has said it out loud that nobody who has got nothing to keep has ever needed.

---

At about half past three he was at the foot of that low wall, which he is not every afternoon, and the man of fifty-six was there with his hand on the stone and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had come down about four feet of that bank without arranging it.

---

"I have got a question and I am going to put it to you down here," said the man of about forty-eight, "because you are down here and I would not do it up there."

---

"Go on," said the man of fifty-six.

---

"What I say at about one every afternoon is for," said the man of about forty-eight.

The man of fifty-six did not take his hand off the stone.

"I do not know what any of them is for," he said.

---

About nineteen people heard that and about four of them were eleven feet higher up the bank and about four of them have said since that a man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally stood in about four hundred yards of air this afternoon and was given a sentence that belongs to a different set of figures altogether, and that the sentence did not get bent, and that about four of them noticed and about four of them have not said one word.

---

The man of about forty-eight stood there and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he did not take it badly, and that he nodded, and that he said one thing, and that the one thing was this.

"That is fair," he said. "I did say for. I am not going to take it back."

---

"Nobody is asking you to," said the man of fifty-six, and put his hand back on the stone and read the four off it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the thirty-ninth time running, and about four of the nineteen had heard him say the same sentence about nine feet lower down about as long as it takes to carry a trestle round a table.

---

Mavis Dorr said the rest of it out loud at about half past four, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nine people.

"He did not ask him about the wall," she said. "I want that said first, because about four of us have been standing at the top of that bank for two days waiting for somebody to ask him about the wall and get a different answer, and nobody asked him about the wall this afternoon. A man asked him about his figure. He got the same sentence."

---

"So what," said the man who digs loam.

---

"I do not know what," said Mavis Dorr. "I have been in this yard four days and I have watched about four people find out that this yard cannot hand an answer on and I have watched it find out yesterday that nine people do not agree on two words, and this afternoon I have watched a man ask a question about the only figure that gets said in this yard out loud and be given a sentence that has been about something else for longer than any of us have been standing here, and I am telling you that I do not know what that is and I am not going to call it anything this afternoon."

---

Nobody called it anything.

---

Sabra Holt had been at those boards from about a quarter to eight and she had not moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she heard the whole of the exchange at about half past three from about nine feet higher up than the two men, and that about four of them have said that she did not come down it and about four of them have said that she did not look away.

---

"Do you want to say something about that," she said at about half past four, to the man of about forty-eight, and in the ordinary voice, and to him and not to the yard.

"No," said the man of about forty-eight. "I have got a figure of work and I have got a man at the foot of a wall and I got the second one first and the second one had nothing to do with my figure and he did not need it to. If I go up to that bank and say anything about it then I am a man in this yard with an opinion about a thing I do not keep, and I have been keeping a figure for longer than I have been keeping anything and I am not going to start this week."

---

"Then it is about four of us who have noticed," said Sabra Holt, "and about four of us is not the same as a yard, and I have had nineteen years of learning the difference between the two and I am not going to spend this afternoon pretending four is a yard."

---

She came up about four feet of the slope and stopped there.

"I am going to say one thing about it and then I am going to stop," she said. "A man said ten words this afternoon about figures he does not keep, to a man who keeps one, and it was not wrong, and it was not right either, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard both halves of that sentence and not one of us is going to be the one who goes down there and tells him which of the two it was. That is the whole of what I have got and I am putting it down out loud so that about four people know that I have noticed it too."

---

And about four feet further along the slope the man who digs loam said, out loud, to nobody in particular, "That was the plainest thing anybody has said at those boards this week," and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about four of them have said since that they have no idea which of the two things he meant and are not going to ask him.

---

At about half past four a man of about sixty-four went off up the bank and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched him go, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were still standing at the top of that bank looking at a man of fifty-six reading figures off a wall for the second time.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. Over the one name in that column the heading still says whoever, the one name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty. Six months is what a clerk counted this morning and she has counted nothing else. Fourteen is the whole of what this district has made. Five is what it does not have and not one of the five has been paid for. The figure on a sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven and nobody in this basin has ever said by how much it is out. The lane count is twenty-eight and it did not move this morning, because that lane had nothing come down it in anybody's own hand. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight is at the near end of the first table and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.
