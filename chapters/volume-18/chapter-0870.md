# Chapter 870: The One Hundred And Fifty-Fourth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month About Nineteen People In That Yard Have Been Using The Same Sentence About Two Different Things For Five Mornings And Not One Of Them Has Said So To Anybody And Nothing Came Down That Lane And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and sixty-eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and eighty-four days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and ninety-eight days. Seven hundred and fifty-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and seventy-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and seventy-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and forty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and fifty-one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and eighty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and twenty-seven marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and thirteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and about nineteen people in that yard have been hearing the same sentence used about two different things for five mornings and not one of them has said so to anybody, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and eighty-four mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and eighty-three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and thirty-four mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and fifty-four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and ninety-fifth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and about nineteen people in that yard have been hearing the same sentence used about two different things for five mornings and not one of them has said so to anybody.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and ninth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninety-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the tenth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood sixty-nine days, and a page went back up that lane with nothing written on it, and nothing has come down that lane since, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and then said a figure, and he said the figure before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"One hundred and seventy-five," he said, at the near end of the first table.

About four people said it after him at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said it after him too, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said the lane count after him as well, which is twenty-eight and did not move this morning because that lane has been empty since a page went back up it with nothing on it.

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a boy of seventeen has now said a figure at those boards six mornings out of ten and that about four of them have got to the same place on their own, which is that the mornings he does not say it on are the mornings when there is nothing in the lane, and that about four of them have decided not to ask him how he knows that.

---

The morning was the same as the morning before it and the morning before that, and the man of about thirty-two put his trestle about four feet along from the near end of the second table, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart stood his cart on public lot and about four people watched him not get asked about it, and the woman who keeps a scale weighed things at the foot of that bank and said nothing at all to anybody for the sixth morning running.

---

At about eleven Sabra Holt said one thing, and she said it to about nine people at the top of eleven feet, and she said it in the ordinary voice.

"I have been the third of the nine oldest keys for about nine years," she said, "and in all that time the only thing I have been able to do with it is stop things going up that hill, and I have stopped four, and about four people at the top of eleven feet can tell you what each of the four was and I am not going to."

---

"Will you stop another," said Mavis Dorr.

---

"I do not know," said Sabra Holt. "What I am going to say is that a yard that has spent five mornings finding out what it cannot do has about nine people at the top of eleven feet who now know exactly what is coming up that hill before it arrives, and that is a thing this yard has never had, and I am not going to stand here at about eleven this morning and tell anybody what to do with it because I have not got it and about four people at the top of eleven feet have not either."

---

Nobody worked it out.

---

Then about nine people at the top of eleven feet went and stood along the rail and looked at the lane, and about four of them have said since that a yard does not look at a lane on a morning unless it has been thinking about what comes down it.

Mavis Dorr stood at the near end of that rail with her arms folded and looked up it for a while and then said, to nobody in particular and to about nine people, "He said he would be there at a quarter to ten and he was there at a quarter to ten yesterday, and there is no reason in this world for him to be there again, and I am not going to stand here this morning getting ready for a thing that might be nothing at all."

---

"I have been getting ready for it since about a quarter to eight," said the turner.

---

"Then you and I have both been getting ready for nothing," said Mavis Dorr, "and that is the honest position of this yard at about half past eleven on the one hundred and fifty-fourth morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet can hear me say it and about four of them have already been getting ready for it too and have not said so."

---

Nobody said so.

---

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the number into the middle of that yard and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and about four people said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has said a figure out loud in about four hundred yards of air for longer than any of them have been standing there has not got anybody to give it to and has not asked for one.

---

At about two the man of about twenty-one who carried a page up that lane with nothing on it did not come down it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked up the lane about nine times between about two and about four and about four of them have said since that they were not watching for anybody and that about four of them were.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and ninth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the ninety-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall at about half past three could see that nothing was being said down there.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the forty-third time running.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a sentence has gone about that wall about four hundred times in ten mornings and has been about two different things, and that about nine people at the top of eleven feet know both of the things it has been about, and that about four people at the top of eleven feet have noticed that the man of about forty-eight got it first and that he has not mentioned it since, and that not one of the nineteen has said one word about any of it to anybody, and that about four of them have been waiting four days for somebody else to say it first.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and about nineteen people were still at the foot of that bank, and about four of them said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard and about nine people did not go up the eleven feet straight away and about four of them stood at the near end of the second table in the dark with the lot book shut in front of them.

Adrian Vale put his hand on the boards and took it off again.

"I am going up that lane the next time there is a page coming down it," he said, "and I am not going up it with nothing this afternoon, because there is nothing this afternoon, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have heard me say that so that nobody in that room on the top of that hill can be told that I came up it anyway."

---

And then he went, and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched him get about forty feet up it before the lane took him, and the lane count was twenty-eight and it did not move, because a man going up a lane with nothing in his hand is not a page coming down it, and that is the rule the boy has had for four years and the boy is not the only one who knows it.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. Whoever is the heading over the one name in that column, and that name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty. Six months is what a clerk counted this morning and she has not counted a seventh and is not going to. Fourteen is what this district has made. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five has not been paid for. Four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and by how much it is out nobody in this basin has worked out. The lane count is twenty-eight and it did not move, because that lane has not had a page come down it in their own hand since the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight has gone up that lane with nothing in his hand and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.
