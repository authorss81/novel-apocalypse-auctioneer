# Chapter 855: The One Hundred And Thirty-Ninth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Woman Of About Fifty Came Up About Four Feet Of That Bank At About Half Past One With A Barrow And Came About A Boundary Between Two Pieces Of Ground And Stood At About Four Feet Off The Near End Of The First Table Until About Ten To Five Waiting For A Man Who Digs Loam To Be Free And Nobody Said One Word To Her About Anything Else And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and ninety-four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and fifty-three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and sixty-nine days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and eighty-three days. Seven hundred and forty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and fifty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and sixty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and thirty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and thirty-six days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and seventy-two days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and twelve marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and ninety-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a woman of about fifty came up about four feet of that bank at about half past one with a barrow and came about a boundary and not about anything that is being said at those boards, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and sixty-nine mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and sixty-eight. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and nineteen mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and thirty-nine, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and eightieth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a woman of about fifty was at about four feet off the near end of the first table from about half past one until about ten to five and nobody in that yard asked her one question and nobody told her one thing about a page that came down that lane on the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and ninety-fourth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and ninety-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the eighty-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the second time in nine years, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood fifty-four days, and a page came down that lane and it is in a woman's hand at the foot of that bank, and nobody in that yard has put a line on the page at the back of that book about it, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said that it did not move, and he said no figure at all after that.

---

The trestle was still standing at the near end of the second table at about ten and it had been standing four mornings.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at it at about ten past ten and about four of them looked at it at about one and again at about two, and about nine of them did not say one word about it, and the man of about thirty-two who puts tables up had not come up the bank for it since the morning he left it.

---

At about half past one a woman of about fifty came up about four feet of that bank with a barrow.

She came up it the way a person comes up it with a barrow, which is slowly and with two goes, and she got to the top of it and set the barrow down on its handles and stood about four feet off the near end of the first table and looked at the near end of the second table for about as long as a woman looks at a thing before she says anything about it.

---

Nobody in that yard had seen her at that end of the yard on any morning this week.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet were standing at the boards of the second table and about four of them looked at her and about four of them went back to what they were doing, and she stood about four feet off the near end of the first table with her hands on the barrow handles and looked at the trestle.

---

"You have got a leg on my side of the ditch," she said.

She said it to the man of about thirty-two and he was not in the yard, and she said it at the volume of a woman who has said a thing to a yard before and does not mind that the person it is for is not standing there.

---

That is how about nine people at the top of eleven feet found out that she had come about the trestle, and about four of them looked at the trestle, and about four of them have said since that a trestle had been standing at the near end of that table for four mornings and about nine people had looked at it about four times a day and not one of them had thought about whose ground it was on, and that about four of them were ashamed of that on the one hundred and thirty-ninth morning and about four of them were not, and that the difference between those two groups has not been worked out by anybody.

---

Mavis Dorr came about four feet along the first table at about ten past two and did not go to the boards first. She stood about four feet off the woman with the barrow and she did not ask her anything.

What she said she said to the yard and not to the woman, and it was that a trestle had been standing at the near end of that table for four mornings and that it was on a boundary, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at the trestle when she said it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman with a barrow said about forty words at about ten past two and every one of them was about a ditch and a leg and a soaking, and that not one of them was about a page that came down a lane.

---

The woman said the rest of it herself, to about nine people at the top of eleven feet, without being asked.

"Four mornings," she said, "and if it stays on it a week longer then the next time anybody digs that ditch the line goes where the leg is, and after that there is no arguing about where the line was, because the ditch will be in the wrong place and everybody will know it and nobody will be able to say whose fault it was. He has never moved one off a line. He puts it at the near end and it has been his near end since before I had this barrow."

---

Then nobody asked her anything at those boards for the rest of the afternoon.

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the hardest part of the one hundred and thirty-ninth morning was not the page and was not the trestle, and was the rest of the afternoon, in which about nine people in that yard had a question they were allowed to ask and did not spend on the one person standing at the table who had come about a ditch, and that about four of them can see that they were being careful with her and not careful about her, and that those are not the same thing, and that about four of them have not been able to stop turning that over.

---

Mara Quill brought a sheet out at about half past two on her own account and put about four inches of it in front of the woman with her finger on it and said nothing about it at all.

The woman looked at about four inches of it for a while and said that it was where she said it was and that she had not thought so since the winter before last, and then Mara Quill put the sheet away and wrote nothing on it.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard which three days ago could not put a name on a piece of paper found the old line on a sheet on the one hundred and thirty-ninth morning, in about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and put it in front of a woman with a barrow, and that about four of them have said the two things out loud in the same conversation without noticing, and that about four of them noticed.

---

The woman stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table from about half past two until about half past three with her hands on the barrow handles and she did not go to the boards and she did not come up about four feet of that bank, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were saying things at about nine feet off her about a page that came down that lane on the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning, and nobody in that yard said one word to her about it.

---

At about half past three the man of about thirty-two came up the bank.

He came up the eleven feet and went straight to the near end of the second table and took the trestle down off the boards with both hands and carried it about four feet and set it down where he sets it, which is four feet further along and not on anybody's line, and he did not say one word to about nine people at the top of eleven feet who were standing there watching him do it.

Then he said one sentence to the woman with the barrow.

"It was mine and not a mistake," he said. "You are right."

---

"Thank you," said the woman.

---

And that was the whole of what was said to her at those boards on the one hundred and thirty-ninth morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that it was the only piece of business that got finished at those boards that afternoon, and that it took a man about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and that about nine people had watched a piece of wood stand on a line for four mornings and had not once asked whose line it was on.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the twenty-eighth time running.

A woman of about fifty was about four feet off the near end of the first table at about four o'clock on the one hundred and thirty-ninth morning with her barrow beside her and she heard it with about four other people who are not in that yard every morning, and nobody asked her anything at about four and nobody asked her anything at about half past four either.

---

At about ten to five the light went off the boards and the woman took her barrow up onto its handles and went down about four feet of that bank with it, one go at a time, and she did not say anything to anybody on the way down.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her go and about four of them have said since that she came up about four feet of that bank at about half past one with a barrow and went down it at about ten to five with a barrow and did not stay one hour longer than she had to and was not asked one question in between, and that about four of them did not know what she would have said if they had asked her one, and that they were not going to find out this afternoon.

The trestle was standing about four feet along from the near end of the second table where the man of about thirty-two puts it and it will be there at about ten tomorrow. The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. Whoever is still the heading over that column, and the one name under it does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty. Six months is what a clerk counted this morning and she counted nothing past six. Fourteen is what this district has made and nothing was added to it. Five is what this district does not have and it is unpaid. Four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and nobody in this basin has worked out how far out that figure is. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move, because nobody came down that lane in their own hand with a page this morning, and the toll is ninety-eight and it is spent, and the ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five, and a page is still in a woman's hand at the foot of that bank with three lines on it and not one of the three filled and not one of the three entered anywhere.
