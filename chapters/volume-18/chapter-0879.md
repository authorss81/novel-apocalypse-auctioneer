# Chapter 879: The One Hundred And Sixty-Third Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Morning At About Half Past Ten A Man Of About Forty-One Asked A Clerk Whether One Line Written On The Back Of Somebody Else's Page Could Go Into The Book That Belongs To A Council And She Said That The Yard Had Not Asked And Nobody Asked And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and eighteen days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and seventy-seven. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and ninety-three days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and seven days. Seven hundred and sixty-eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eighty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and eighty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and fifty-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and sixty days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and ninety-six days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and thirty-six marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and twenty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a man of thirty-eight was asked at about two whether he would carry a line up nine miles of hill and said no and gave a reason, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and ninety-three mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and ninety-two. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and forty-three mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and sixty-three, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and fourth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a man of thirty-eight said no at about two this afternoon to carrying a line up nine miles of hill.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and eighteenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and seventeen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the nineteenth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood seventy-eight days, and a man of thirty-eight said no to carrying a line up nine miles of hill and gave a reason, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed that the boards were bare and that about four of them have said since that nobody has been able to say what a morning looks like when there is nothing on them.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at about ten to eight, and it is twenty-nine and has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and no figure went with it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said the number after him out of step with each other and about four of them looked up the lane before they said it.

---

Then about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at the first table for a while without saying anything, because for the first time in four days there was nothing on it, and about four of them have said since that a yard that has spent three mornings looking at a piece of paper does not have a morning ready when the paper is gone.

At about half past ten the turner asked it, and he asked it across the near end of the first table in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard him ask it.

---

"Can it go in the book," he said. "Not in the page at the back. In the book. One line saying that a clerk wrote one line on the back of a page on the one hundred and fifty-eighth morning and that it went up that lane in a man's hand. That is a thing this yard did. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have watched it happen. Can a thing this yard did go into the book that belongs to a Council."

---

And the clerk said it in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard all three parts of what she said.

"No," she said. "And I am going to tell you the three reasons separately so that about four people at the top of eleven feet do not go away with only one of them. One. I enter what a person asks. This yard has not asked. Two. If this yard asks, then somebody has to be named, because that is what the form is, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have looked at the heading over one space on that page this morning and I have watched all nine of you do it. Three. I am not going to enter a thing in a Council's book that this yard has not asked me to enter."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at the column over one space in the lot book, and the heading over it still says whoever, and the one name in it does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty.

---

"Ask," said the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart. "Somebody ask. That is all that is missing. One of you ask out loud and she puts it in."

And about nineteen people at the foot of that bank stood there, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at each other in the ordinary way people look at each other when a thing has been handed to the middle of a room, and not one of them asked.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard of about nineteen people had a thing to do at about half past eleven on the one hundred and sixty-third morning and did not do it, and that about four of them said the word for it out loud in about nine people's hearing afterwards, and that about four of them said that nobody stopped them and nobody told them not to, and that about four of them found that to be a different thing from the four days before it.

---

Mavis Dorr asked Adrian Vale at about two, and she asked him in front of about nine people, and she asked it straight.

"You watched that page go up that lane," she said. "Will you say so. Not here. To whoever asks. Will you say that you were standing at the foot of that bank and you saw one line go up that hill on the one hundred and fifty-eighth afternoon."

---

And he said no, and about nine people heard it, and he gave a reason.

---

"No," he said. "Because the moment I say I saw it, somebody in this yard is going to ask me what I made of it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet know that I have not been let to carry anything up that hill since the one hundred and second morning, and I am not going to be asked what I made of it in order to be allowed to say that I was there. I have been carrying nine words since the one hundred and second morning and I have never once used them to get anything into a book and I am not going to start this afternoon by borrowing a line out of a clerk's hand."

About nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say anything to that, and about four of them have said since that a man of thirty-eight has now turned down two things in six mornings and given a reason for both, and that about four of them said the second reason was about himself and about nothing else, and that about four of them found that harder to sit with than the first.

---

The man at the end of the second table said one thing at about half past two, without getting up, and about nine people heard it and not one of them answered him.

---

"There is a form nine miles up that hill and a form on that table and about nine people at the top of eleven feet standing between them," he said, "and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the thing standing between them is not a hill and is not a rule and is not a person, it is about nine people at the top of eleven feet who have not asked. I am not going to say that about myself and I am not going to say one word about what it is for anybody. That is the whole of it and I have been at this table fifteen years and I am not going to be asked for the rest of it."

Nobody asked him for the rest of it.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and eighteenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and seventeen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall could see that nothing was being said down there.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the fifty-second time running.

Nobody asked him what any of them is for, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that about nine people in that yard have had a form in front of them in six mornings and not one of them has asked a single question, and that about four of them said that out loud this afternoon to about nine people at the foot of that bank, and that about four of them did not say who, and that about four of them noticed that nobody asked who either.

---

At about half past four the light came off those boards and went over the top of them, and about nineteen people were still standing at the foot of that bank, and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four of them and was not taken up by anybody else.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book and none of the thirty-three is the line that went up that lane on the one hundred and fifty-eighth afternoon. Whoever is over the one name in that column, that name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty and has been since the one hundred and thirty-fifth morning. Six months is what a clerk counted and there is no seventh of it anywhere in this district. Fourteen is what this district has made and did not move this morning. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five has not been paid for. The figure on that gatepost sheet is four hundred and eleven, and by how much it is out is a thing no person in this basin has done and about four people at the top of eleven feet said this afternoon that they were not going to be the first. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move this morning, because that lane has not had a page come down it in anybody's own hand since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight has said no twice in six mornings and given a reason both times and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
