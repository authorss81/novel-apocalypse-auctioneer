# Chapter 842: The One Hundred And Twenty-Sixth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Woman Asked The Clerk Out Loud At Those Boards Whether The Asking Is Going On The Page At The Back Of That Book And The Clerk Said That It Is Not And Gave A Reason Out Loud For The First Time And A Man Of About Forty-One Came Up That Hill At About Eleven And Stood About Four Feet Off The Boards And Said Nothing At All About It

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and eighty-one days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and forty. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and fifty-six days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and seventy days. Seven hundred and thirty-one days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and forty-five days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and fifty days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and twenty days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and twenty-three days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and fifty-nine days past a printing it did not make.

Three hundred and ninety-nine marks have been cut off that board, and there are three hundred and eighty-five marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the clerk was asked out loud at those boards whether the asking is going on the page at the back of that book, and she said that it is not, and she gave a reason out loud, which she has not done before, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and fifty-six mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and fifty-five. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and six mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and twenty-six, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-seven and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and sixty-seventh of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a man of about forty-eight was asked a question out loud at about ten past one in the ordinary voice and he answered it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the answer and about four of them heard a question and not an instruction.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-first night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of about forty-one came up that hill at about eleven and stood about four feet off the near end of the first table and said nothing about the question that was asked at those boards the day before, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of that bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood forty-one days, and the clerk gave a reason out loud at about half past ten and not one of the thirty-three lines on the page at the back of that book is a reason, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

Orren Pike was nine miles up that hill at about ten to eight and he was at the top of that bank at about eleven with his sheet in his coat, and nothing came down that lane, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked down it at about eleven and about four of them have said since that they had got used to a morning starting with him.

---

The yard did what the yard does, and for about nine people at the top of eleven feet it did it more slowly than the morning before, and about four of them have said since that they cannot say what the yard was waiting for and that the not-waiting was visible from about forty feet off.

Mara Quill had the lot book open on the boards of the second table and had it open at the five lines and not at the page at the back, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed the difference and about nine of them did not.

---

At about half past ten Mavis Dorr came the length of the second table and put her hands on it.

"Mara. I am going to say this once and then I am going to leave you alone with it. Is the asking going on the page at the back of that book."

"I am not going to write it."

"You have not heard my question."

"You have had your hands on that table for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall," said the clerk, "and you have not said it and I have said it for you, and I would like about nine people at the top of eleven feet to notice that I have said a question out loud at those boards and that I am the clerk."

About nine people at the top of eleven feet stopped what they were doing.

"There were four questions in that yard yesterday afternoon," said Mavis Dorr. "Four, and I counted them, and there is nowhere in this district that those four are."

"There is a page at the back of that book with nothing ruled on it."

"That is not nowhere. That is a page."

---

Mara Quill took her hand off the corner of the back page and put it flat on the boards, and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched her do that.

"I am going to tell you why I am not going to write it," she said, "and I have never told anybody why I have not written a thing. I have been asked four times in about nineteen mornings and I said no four times, and this morning I am going to say it out loud, and I want about nine people at the top of eleven feet to hear a reason from me while there is still nothing on that page, because if I say it after there is a thing on it then it is a reason for having written it, and that is a different object."

She turned the page round so that about nine people at the top of eleven feet could see the ruled nothing.

"There is nothing ruled on that page. There has been nothing ruled on it for fifteen years. Thirty-three lines have gone on it and not one of the thirty-three is in the lot book, and about four people in this district can be asked separately about about three of them, and one of those four has not been asked by anybody."

She put the page back.

"If I write a line about the asking on it this morning, then that page stops being the place where this district puts the things it will not enter, and it becomes the place where this district puts the things it has decided to enter, and those are not the same page. The difference between them is the whole of what that page has been for fifteen years, and I would be the one who made it, and nobody in this yard has asked me to make it."

"Who decides it now," said Mavis Dorr.

"That is the question I have been refusing for about nineteen mornings," said the clerk, "and I have been refusing it as a matter of not having a reason, and I have got a reason now and the reason is that I do not know, and I am not going to find out by being the first person in this district to put a thing on a page that has nothing ruled on it."

---

"You have just said a rule out loud," said Adrian Vale.

Mara Quill turned round and looked at him, which she has not done in this run of mornings, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed her do it.

"I have said a reason out loud," she said. "That is not a rule. A rule is a thing with an end on it and a person at the end of it, and what I have said has got about four people in it who are not going to agree with each other about it, and I have said it because a woman asked me a question in a yard, and I would rather be held to a thing I chose than to a thing I did not."

"Nobody is holding you to it."

"Then I have said it for nothing," said the clerk, "and I have said a lot of things for nothing in fifteen years, and this is the first one I have said in front of about nine people, and I would like about four of them to notice that it is the first one."

---

At about eleven the man of about forty-one came up the eleven feet.

He came up it in the ordinary way and he got to the top of that bank and he stood about four feet off the near end of the first table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at him and waited. He had said *as sent* out loud in that yard the afternoon before for the first time in nine years and nobody had turned round, and this morning about nine people at the top of eleven feet turned round.

"Nobody is going to ask you about the question," said Mavis Dorr.

"I know," said the turner. "I have been standing here since about eleven and about nine people have looked at me four times and not one of them has asked me about the question that a woman at the foot of that bank asked at those boards yesterday afternoon, and I would like it said out loud in that yard that a room nine miles up that hill has been asking this district a question every morning for about fourteen mornings and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have not asked me one word about it."

"Then say the thing you came up for."

"I have not come up for a thing," said the turner. "I said *as sent* in this yard yesterday afternoon for the first time in nine years and about nine people heard it and nobody turned round, and I got up this morning and thought the second half of that was going to be worse than the first, and it was not. I have come up to find out whether it was going to be worse."

"It was not," said Mavis Dorr, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at her for saying it.

"That is all I came up for," said the turner. "I have got nine miles of road in my legs and I have not got a question and I have not got a page, and I am going to go back down that bank at about half past eleven and go on saying a number in a room nine miles off at about ten to ten tomorrow morning. I would like about four people at the top of eleven feet to notice that I have come nine miles to find out that nothing happened, and that nothing happening is the second time in two mornings that it has happened in that yard, and that I have never in nine years been the person who comes up a hill to find out that nothing happened."

---

Adrian Vale stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table and understood that the man of about forty-one had come nine miles up a hill to be told by about nine people saying nothing that the thing he had said out loud the day before had not turned into anything yet, and that this was a thing a man does, and that he had been standing in that yard one hundred and forty-seven mornings doing a version of it every single day, and that his version was worse, because his had a man of fifty-six at the bottom of it and the turner's had about nine people not asking.

He did not say that. About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he looked at the turner for longer than he has ever looked at anybody in that yard, and that the turner looked back, and that neither of them said one word.

---

At about one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said the number into the middle of that yard in the ordinary voice, and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and about four of them had their hands at their sides in a different way from the way they had them the morning before, and about four of them have said since that they had been waiting since about ten to eight to ask him something.

"Ask me," said the man of about forty-eight, without turning round.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet looked at each other and about four of them looked at the ground, and then Mavis Dorr asked it, because Mavis Dorr has asked out loud in that yard more times this week than any other person in it.

"Is that the same number every morning, or does it change on a morning when somebody comes down that lane?"

"It is the same number every morning," said the man of about forty-eight, "and it changes on every morning, and I say it out loud before anybody enters it, and those two are not the same, and the second one is the one that is hard about it. About four people have been listening to me say it for a hundred mornings and about four of them noticed the second half of it yesterday afternoon and nobody asked me about it, and I have been waiting since yesterday afternoon for somebody to ask me about it, and you have asked me the wrong half of it."

"What is the right half of it," said Mavis Dorr.

"That I do not know what I am keeping it for," said the man of about forty-eight. "I have been keeping a number in my head and putting it out loud into about four hundred yards of air every morning for a hundred mornings, and I have never known what the number is for, and I have known that I did not know every single one of them, and about four people in this district have known that I knew, and about four of those four are standing in this yard. This afternoon I have been asked a question about the lane and not about that, and I would like to say out loud that I noticed the difference, and that I am not complaining, and that I am going to go on saying it in the same voice tomorrow."

About four people at the top of eleven feet did not make a sound.

---

The woman who keeps a scale was on her own step about forty feet off from about ten, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked across at her about four times during the morning and she did not look back at any of them, and about four of them have said since that a woman who came up about four feet of that bank on the one hundred and twenty-seventh morning to ask a man of fifty-six a question out loud has been on her own step all morning the morning after, and that nobody in that yard knows what a person does the morning after they have done a thing like that.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and eighty-first night of that run, having slept on four hundred and eighty of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the seventy-first afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and neither of them said anything else, and about four feet of stone and about eleven feet of bank carried both of the greetings about two hundred yards in each direction, and neither of the two of them was asked anything by anybody in that yard this morning or the morning before.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nine people heard it for the fifteenth time running.

Sabra Holt was in that yard and had been in it since about ten and had said four sentences in it and all four of them had been about herself, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman who has said four sentences about herself in a morning is a woman who has noticed that she has nothing else to say in it, and that nobody in that yard was ever going to hand her a subject, and that the not being handed one has now been going on for about five days.

The clerk shut the lot book at about half past four and put the page at the back of it flat with her thumb along the edge, and it has thirty-three lines on it, and she did not add one, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk shut a book that she has left open at the back of it for about eight mornings, and about four of them have said since that they are not going to ask her about it.
