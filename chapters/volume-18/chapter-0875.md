# Chapter 875: The One Hundred And Fifty-Ninth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Page Went Back Up That Lane At About Half Past Four In The Hand Of The Man Of About Twenty-One Who Brought It Down And About Nineteen People At The Foot Of That Bank Watched It Go And One Line Is On The Back Of It And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and fourteen days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and seventy-three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and eighty-nine days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and three days. Seven hundred and sixty-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and seventy-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and eighty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and fifty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and fifty-six days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and ninety-two days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and thirty-two marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and eighteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the page went back up that lane this afternoon with one line on the back of it and nothing has come down that lane since, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and eighty-nine mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and eighty-eight. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and thirty-nine mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and fifty-nine, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundredth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and the page went back up that lane this afternoon with one line on the back of it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fourteenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fifteenth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood seventy-four days, and a page went back up that lane this afternoon with one line on the back of it, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and then said a figure, and he said the figure before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and the sheet with one line on the back of it lay on the first table where it had lain all night.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is twenty-nine and has not moved since yesterday, and then he said a figure, and about four people said it after him at the same moment and out of step with each other.

"One hundred and eighty," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet said it after him too, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the boy has now said a figure on four mornings out of the six, and that on the two mornings he did not, one of them was the morning a page came down that lane and one of them was the morning after it.

The turner read the line on the back of that page out loud at about ten, and the man who digs loam read it out loud after him at about half past ten, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard both of them and about four of them have said since that the two men said the same nine words in the same order and that about four of them said them differently at the ends, and that about four of them noticed that nobody said anything about the difference.

---

"The second space on that form is still empty and I have not asked anybody for a name," said the turner, and stopped there.

"The second space on that form is still empty," said the man who digs loam, "and nobody in this yard has been asked for a name and there is one name in the book over there that does not satisfy the rule," and stopped there.

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet had a line in their heads that was nine words long and about four of them had noticed that one of those two men had left a word off the end of it, and about four of them noticed that the other one had put a word on the end of it, and not one of the about nineteen people at the foot of that bank said so to anybody.

At about eleven the man of about twenty-one came down that lane for the second time in two days, and he came down it on foot and with his hands empty.

---

"It came out of that room at about six yesterday and it is still in the room," he said. "I am here about eleven this morning because I came down at about nine to say that I will take it back up this afternoon, and I came down on foot and I have not got it with me because it is lying on your boards and it is not mine."

Nobody thanked him and he did not wait for it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man of about twenty-one walked down nine miles of hill twice in two days for a piece of paper that is not his, and that about four of them found that harder to say out loud than anything else that happened yesterday.

---

Adrian Vale was not in that yard from about a quarter to eight until about half past two, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed that he had gone down the eleven feet and had not come back up, and about four of them have said since that a man who said at about one yesterday that he was not going to carry a line up that hill went down the slope in the morning as though he had to get out of the yard to be in the same yard.

At about half past two he came back up the eleven feet and went to about four feet off the near end of the first table and stood there.

---

"Are you going to write another one," he said.

It was the clerk he said it to and about nine people heard the question and about four of them have said since that a man of thirty-eight asked the only question anybody in that yard has asked about yesterday's line, and that about four of them found it an impertinent question and about four of them found that it was the first sensible thing anybody said about it.

---

"I do not know," said the clerk.

And she said it in the ordinary voice, and then she looked at the sheet on the boards and said the rest of it out loud to about nine people because she had been reading things out loud at those boards for fifteen years and could not do it quietly.

---

"I am not going to tell you that there is going to be another one tomorrow morning," she said, "and I am not going to tell you that there is not. About four people at the top of eleven feet have been waiting five mornings for somebody to say what a morning does in this yard now, and I have read a room's page out loud at these boards about four hundred times in fifteen years and not one of those four hundred times did the room ask me what happens next. I do not know what happens next and I have written one line and I am going to stop there until somebody asks me for a second."

Nobody asked her for a second.

---

At about half past four the man of about twenty-one came down the last of that lane, and the clerk stood up and gave him the sheet with the back of it turned out, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came about four feet closer to see a line they had read at about ten the day before, and about four of them looked at the back of it and not the front, and the man of about twenty-one put it in his inside coat and said nothing about it at all.

Then he went back up that lane, and about nineteen people at the foot of that bank watched him go the whole way, and about four of them have said since that not one of the about nineteen people at the foot of that bank said one word while he was doing it.

The lane count did not move, because a page going up that lane is not a page coming down it, and nothing has come down that lane since.

---

Sabra Holt said one thing about it at about ten past four, and she said it to about nine people at the top of eleven feet, and about nineteen people heard it.

"I did not stop that," she said. "I want it said at about ten past four this afternoon by a person with nineteen years and the third of the nine oldest keys that she did not stop that, because I stopped four things going up that hill in nineteen years and I know exactly what stopping one looks like from the top, and this was not it, and I would have stopped it and I did not, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that this is the first time in this block she has said out loud that she was in a position to do something and did not do it."

---

At about four o'clock, before that, the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the forty-eighth time running, and nobody asked him what any of them is for.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a line went out of that yard this afternoon in a man's hand that was not a clerk's and not a yard's, and that about four of them said out loud to about nine people after he had gone that this yard cannot tell anybody what happened to it and is not going to be able to for as long as it takes, and that about four of them said that they had found out more about carrying a thing in two days than about nineteen people had found out in five mornings of trying to put it down, and that nobody picked any of that up either.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the first table had nothing on it where the sheet had lain for a day and a night, and about nineteen people were still at the foot of that bank, and about four of them said the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book and none of the thirty-three went up that lane this afternoon on the back of somebody else's page. Whoever is over the one name in that column, that name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty and it went up that lane empty. Six months is what a clerk counted and she has not counted a seventh. Fourteen is what this district has made, and it stood at fourteen this morning and it stood at fourteen yesterday. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five has not been paid for and a line on the back of a page is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much it is out nobody has ever worked out. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move this afternoon, because a page going up that hill is not a page coming down it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight asked one question at about half past two this afternoon and did not get an answer and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
