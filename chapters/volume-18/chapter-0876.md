# Chapter 876: The One Hundred And Sixtieth Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Eleven The Woman Who Keeps A Scale Put A Sheet Of Paper On Her Own Scale And Took It Off Again And Said Nothing To Anybody And Nobody Has Gotten One Word Out Of Her Since The One Hundred And Forty-Ninth Morning And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and fifteen days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and seventy-four. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and ninety days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and four days. Seven hundred and sixty-five days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and seventy-nine days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and eighty-four days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and fifty-four days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and fifty-seven days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and ninety-three days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and thirty-three marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and nineteen marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the woman who keeps a scale put a sheet of paper on her own scale at about eleven and took it off again and said nothing to anybody, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and ninety mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and eighty-nine. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and forty mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and sixty, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and first of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and the woman who keeps a scale put a sheet of paper on her own scale this morning and took it off again and said nothing.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifteenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fourteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fifth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the sixteenth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of that bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood seventy-five days, and a woman put a sheet of paper on a scale and took it off again and said nothing to anybody, and the page is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet noticed that the boards had nothing on them at all this morning.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and the first table was bare where the sheet had lain for a day and a night.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and it was still twenty-nine, and then there was nothing else out of him this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said the number after him and waited for a second one that did not come.

---

At about a quarter past ten the clerk put a blank sheet of her own on the boards at about four feet off the near end of the first table, face up, with nothing on it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet looked at it, and the woman who keeps a scale came and took it.

She has been weighing things at the foot of that bank for nine years and she weighs iron and she weighs lead and she weighs a piece of the second table that came off in the winter, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that in nine years she has never once put anything on that scale that was not a thing, and that this morning she did.

---

She put it on the pan, and she let the arm come down, and she stood looking at where it came to rest.

It came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, which is what a blank sheet of a clerk's own comes to, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet could see the mark from where they were standing, and she took it off the pan and put it back on the boards and squared it up and did not say one word to anybody.

---

Mavis Dorr asked her at about ten past eleven why, in the ordinary voice, and in about nineteen people's hearing.

---

And the woman who keeps a scale did not answer, and she went back to the foot of that bank and picked up the piece of lead she had been holding and went on weighing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that this is the eleventh morning running of that and that on the one hundred and forty-ninth morning she asked the yard a question out loud and has not said one word since, and that about four of them had assumed the not-saying was a kind of patience, and that about four of them can now see that it is not patience, because a woman with something on a scale and nine years of a habit of saying what a thing is called did not answer.

The turner said the thing that about four people at the top of eleven feet had not thought of, and he said it at about half past eleven to about nine people.

---

"A scale is the only thing at the foot of that bank that gives you the same figure twice," he said. "I have watched her weigh that bar of iron for nine years and it has never once come out different and I have never once seen her doubt it. And about four people at the top of eleven feet have been treating what she said on the one hundred and forty-ninth morning as a thing she wanted answered. It is not a thing she wants answered. It is a thing she wanted to weigh, and this morning she weighed it and got a figure and she is not going to say the figure out loud, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have got to the same place on their own, which is that the figure she got is the same every morning and is the same for everybody, and that is why she is not saying it."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the turner put two things together at about half past eleven this morning that about four of them had been carrying separately for eleven mornings, and that about four of them have said that a woman who asks one question and then goes silent is not the same as a woman who asks one question and is waiting, and that it took a man who works with metal every day to see that, and that about four of them said so out loud to about nine people and that nobody argued with any of it.

Adrian Vale was at about four feet off the near end of the first table and looked at the sheet on the boards for a while and then at the place where the arm had come to rest.

---

"I have never trusted one of those," he said, to about four people. "Nine years I have been watching that arm come down and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said that is the first time I have ever said a word to her about it and about four of them found that the not-saying on this one side and the not-trusting on that side have been running along next to each other since before the middle of the winter. A thing that comes back the same every morning tells you how much of it there is. It has never once told me anybody."

---

He did not go on, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that he said about nine words and every one of them was about her scale and not one of them was about himself, and that about four of them noticed that as well.

At about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into the middle of that yard and about nine people heard it and nobody wrote it down, and about four people said it after him out of step with each other, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that about nine people in that yard now know what the figure on the back of a clerk's own sheet of paper weighs and not one of them has said the figure out loud and no piece of paper in this district has it on it.

---

Sabra Holt said one thing at about two, and she said it at about four feet off the near end of the second table to about nine people.

---

"Somebody has been standing about nine feet off that woman for eleven mornings waiting for her to say something," she said. "I have been one of them and I am going to stop. She asked one question out loud on the one hundred and forty-ninth afternoon in about nineteen people's hearing and nobody answered her, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have decided that the polite thing to do is wait. It is not polite. It is about four people at the top of eleven feet standing in a ring around a person, and if she never says anything again then about four people at the top of eleven feet will have spent eleven mornings waiting on somebody who has finished."

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifteenth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fourteen of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fifth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet who were watching that wall could see that nothing was being said down there.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the forty-ninth time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the whole of that yard spent this morning watching one arm come down onto a stop about nine feet from the boards, and that about four of them have said that a yard which can do that can do something, and that about four of them have said that they would like somebody to say what the something is.

---

The light left the boards at about half past four and went along the edge of them and off the end, and about nineteen people were still at the foot of that bank, and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four of them and by nobody else.

That page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and three of them are lines nobody in this district has ever heard read and that count is not going to be lower and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book and none of them went anywhere this week. Whoever is over the one name in that column, that name does not satisfy the rule, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody in this yard asked anybody for a name this morning. Six months is what a clerk counted and the count of counted months is six and did not move, and a woman put a blank sheet on a scale this morning and did not say the figure and the two of those are not the same thing and she would not be asked about it. Fourteen is what this district has made and did not move this morning. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid. Four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and by how much it is out nobody has ever done the arithmetic on and nobody in this basin is going to do it this morning. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move this morning, because that lane has not had a page down it in anybody's own hand since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot. A man of thirty-eight has said about nine words about a woman's scale this morning and not one of them about himself and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
