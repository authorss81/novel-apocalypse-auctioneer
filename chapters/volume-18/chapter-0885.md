# Chapter 885: The ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-NINTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Morning After The Counting The Count Is Seven And It Did Not Go Back And A Woman With Nineteen Years Asked A Clerk A Question At About Ten And Was Told No Out Loud And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and twenty-four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eighty-three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and ninety-nine days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and thirteen days. Seven hundred and seventy-four days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eighty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and ninety-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and sixty-three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and sixty-six days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and two days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and forty-two marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and twenty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and the count of counted months is seven and it did not go back and nobody entered it, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and ninety-nine mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and ninety-eight. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and forty-nine mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and sixty-nine, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is in the lot book, and three of them are lines nobody in this district heard read.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and tenth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and the count of counted months is seven and it did not go back this morning and it was not on a page.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-fourth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fourteenth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the twenty-fifth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood eighty-four days, the count of counted months did not go back and the figure on a sheet at that gatepost did not either, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and about four people at the top of eleven feet were already standing on the stones where the counting was done yesterday afternoon, and about four of them have said since that nobody asked them to stand there and that they have not been able to give a reason for standing there.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about four of them said it after him out of step with each other as well. Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and then he said a figure, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

"One hundred and eighty-nine," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen said that figure out loud this morning and has not said what it is about, and that about four of them have said it is about a man who was not in this yard yesterday and about four of them have said it is about nothing in particular and are the same three people.

---

Sabra Holt asked one question at about ten, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing, and she asked it in the ordinary voice and she asked it once.

---

"Can the seven go into a ledger with a day against it," she said.

---

The clerk said no out loud, and she said why in one sentence, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard both of those things.

---

"No," she said. "I counted it out loud in a yard and it belongs to that yard and if it goes on a page with a day against it then the first thing anybody will do with it is go back to the page and change the day, and a number that can be changed is not a count, it is a thing somebody wrote down. I have said out loud before that I am not going to count a month I am standing in and I am not saying anything different today."

---

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman with nineteen years of keys behind her asked a clerk a question and got a sentence back that was longer than the question, and that about four of them have said that the sentence did not refuse the ledger so much as refuse the day, and that about four of them have not said which of those two things is worse for the five things this district does not have.

---

Then the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally asked her, out loud, in about nineteen people's hearing, whether the next one of those is going to be seven as well.

---

"I counted a month yesterday afternoon and I have not counted one this morning and I am not going to count the one I am standing in," she said. "If you want a figure about the one after that you will have to come back on a morning when I am not in it, and I am not going to promise you seven and I am not going to promise you anything else."

---

Nobody entered it anywhere, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard of about nineteen people has now got a number in the air that it has had in the air since yesterday and no place at all to put it, and that about four of them said that they thought the count was supposed to be the easy thing. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the whole of that morning went through the same shape at those boards, which is a man saying a figure and a clerk not writing it, and that about four of them have said that shape has been happening for a long time and that about four of them have said it has not felt like this before.

---

At about one the woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody about any of it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard that has counted a month out loud gets very quiet the morning after.

---

About half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into the middle of that yard and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that he said it in the same voice he says it in every afternoon and that about four of them have started listening for whether it changes and that it does not.

---

Adrian Vale was at the foot of that bank all afternoon and did not go up that lane and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who went up nine miles on the one hundred and sixty-fifth morning and came down on the one hundred and sixty-sixth has stayed at the bottom of the bank since and has not said why and has not been asked.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the fifty-eighth time running. Nobody asked him what any of them is for. At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-fourth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-three of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the twenty-fifth morning running of not asking that man anything is now longer than any run of mornings this yard has counted out loud, and that about four of them have said they did not know that was a thing anybody was counting. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard which has counted a month out loud and put nothing on paper has two figures with nowhere to go in it this morning, and that about four of them said that is what the seventh of the five is like without anybody putting it that way.

---

At about half past four the light came off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up.

---

The back page of that book carries thirty-three lines and the lot book has none of them, and three of those lines were read out once by nobody in this district. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it was six yesterday and it did not go back this morning and it is not on a ledger and it is not on a page. The fourteen that this district has made did not move this morning and there is no fifteenth of them. The five things this district does not have are five and unpaid and the fifth of the five did not move this morning. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out is not a sum anybody in this basin has ever started. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move and a count said out loud does not move it. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall is public lot, it has been since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and it is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days, it was not run, it is not open, and it is not a lot. A woman with nineteen years of keys behind her was told no out loud this morning by a clerk, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
