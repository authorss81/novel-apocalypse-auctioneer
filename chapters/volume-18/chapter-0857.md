# Chapter 857: The One Hundred And Forty-First Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month Four People At Those Boards Began To Say One Sentence For That Yard Out Loud This Afternoon At About One And At About Two And At About Half Past Two And At About Three And Every One Of Them Stopped Before The End Of It And About Nine People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Heard Four Starts And Nothing Was Entered And The Lane Count Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Five hundred and ninety-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and fifty-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and seventy-one days. Nobody has entered anything for seven hundred and eighty-five days. Seven hundred and forty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and sixty days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and sixty-five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and thirty-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and thirty-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is four hundred and seventy-four days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and fourteen marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and four people at those boards began to say one sentence for that yard out loud this afternoon and every one of them stopped before the end of it, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and seventy-one mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and seventy. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and twenty-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and forty-one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the page at the back has thirty-three lines on it and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the five hundred and eighty-second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and four people at those boards started to say one sentence for that yard out loud this afternoon and all four of them stopped before they had finished it.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and ninety-sixth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and ninety-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the eighty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the second time in nine years, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of that bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood fifty-six days, and four people at those boards started to say one sentence for this yard this afternoon and not one of the four finished, and a page came down that lane and it is not in the lot book and it is not on the page at the back of that book, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and not one of the thirty-three is about a question, and the second space on that form is still empty, and the page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said that it did not move, and he said no figure at all after that.

---

At about one the man who digs loam said a thing at the boards and stopped in the middle of it.

About nine people at the top of eleven feet had been standing about four feet off him for about a quarter of an hour waiting for something and about four of them had said out loud that they wanted to see what a person in that yard would do if a person in that yard did something, and then he said it.

"This district says that the asking happened."

---

He got that out and then he stopped, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet waited for the rest of it and there was no rest of it.

"That is all of it," he said, after a moment. "That is where I have got to. I have started about four of them this morning in my own head and I do not know what comes after the first nine words, and I am not going to stand at these boards and guess at the end of a sentence that a man at the top of nine miles of hill is going to read."

---

"I have stopped thinking about it," said Mavis Dorr, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, to about nine people. "He is not braver than me and he is not stupider than me and he has just told me what the rest of us are doing and none of us knew we were doing it."

---

At about two Mavis Dorr started one herself and she got about four times as far as he did.

"This district says that a question was asked out loud in this yard on the one hundred and twelfth morning, and that it has been asked since by about nineteen people, and that not one of us arranged it and not one of us has been asked whether we were going to, and that"

---

And she stopped.

---

"That is the end of what I have got," she said. "I have got about eleven words in my mouth that are true and I have not got the twelfth word and the twelfth word is the one that makes it a sentence instead of a list, and the twelfth word is one that says what the yard thinks about it, and I have not been able to find out what the yard thinks about it and I have been at about four feet off these boards for about nine days looking."

---

"You do not want the yard to think anything about it," said Sabra Holt, from about four feet further on. "You want it true. Those are different and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have been treating them as the same all week."

---

"Then I do not want the yard to think anything about it," said Mavis Dorr, "and I have wanted that since about the one hundred and thirty-first morning and I did not know it until about two o'clock this afternoon."

---

At about half past two the turner started one and got about nine words into it.

"This district says that it cannot answer the three questions on that page and that it is not refusing to."

---

And he stopped and looked at about nine people at the top of eleven feet and did not finish it.

"I am not carrying it up," he said, "and I am not writing it down, and I said on the one hundred and thirty-third morning that I was not going to be a person who says a thing at a yard and then carries it out of the yard in his mouth, and that is still what I am not going to be, and a sentence that begins *this district says* is a thing being carried out of a yard whoever says it and however many of them say it together."

---

"Nobody carried that one up," said Mavis Dorr.

---

"Nobody carried it up because I did not finish it," said the turner. "Finish it and it goes. That is the whole of what I have been doing for about four days and about four people at the top of eleven feet can see it and about four of them cannot."

---

At about three Adrian Vale started one and got three words out.

"This district says"

---

And he stopped, and he put his hands behind his back again, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not make a sound.

"That is the beginning of the one I am not going to finish either," he said, "and I am the last person in this basin who ought to be finishing it, because I said out loud on the one hundred and thirty-third morning that I am not going to be the second man in this basin to carry a thing out of a yard and have it come back as something else, and this sentence is that thing. It is not a speech and it is not a figure of money and it is not a column and it does not name anybody. It is a thing carried out of a yard by about nineteen people in about nine words and it would come back as a thing a room has."

---

Sabra Holt waited until about half past three and then said the whole of it once.

"Four people have started one sentence at these boards this afternoon," she said, "and every one of you stopped in a different place, and I want about nine people at the top of eleven feet to notice that none of you stopped because you were frightened. The man who digs loam stopped because he did not know the twelfth word. The woman who has been at these boards every morning this week stopped because she did not want the yard to think anything about it. The man who came up nine miles of hill with a thing in his mouth stopped because a sentence that begins *this district says* is a thing carried out of a yard. And a man of thirty-eight stopped because he has already been the second man in this basin to carry a thing out of a yard."

---

"That is not courage failing four times," she said. "That is about nineteen people being able to say a great deal about themselves and not being able to say one sentence for themselves, and there is a difference between those two things and about four people at the top of eleven feet have known it since the one hundred and thirty-first morning and about four of them have not, and I have not got time to find out which is which before that young man goes up that hill."

---

Nobody at the top of eleven feet said anything.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his four hundred and ninety-sixth night of that run, having slept on four hundred and ninety-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the eighty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about nine people were standing on the ground at the foot of that bank because it was public lot and about four of them said it too, and then the man of fifty-six put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the thirtieth time running.

---

At about ten to five the light went off the boards and about nine people were within about four hundred yards of them and about four of them put their hands in their pockets one at a time and said they had to go, and the first one to go was the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart.

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book. The column carries one name and the heading over it still says whoever and the one name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is still empty. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid. Four hundred and eleven is on a sheet at a gatepost and nobody in this basin has computed by how much it is out. The lane count is twenty-eight and did not move, because nobody came down that lane with a page in their own hand this morning, and the toll is ninety-eight and it is spent, and the ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five, and a man of thirty-eight is standing at about four feet off the near end of the first table with his hands behind his back and has not been told he is a good man and has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three.

About four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard of about nineteen people can say four different true things about itself in an afternoon and cannot say one sentence, and that about four of them have gone home thinking about the twelfth word, and that none of the four of them has the twelfth word, and that the twelfth word is the only thing anybody at the top of nine miles of hill has asked for.
