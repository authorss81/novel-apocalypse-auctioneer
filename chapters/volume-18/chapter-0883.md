# Chapter 883: The ONE HUNDRED AND SIXTY-SEVENTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Eleven A Woman Said Out Loud In About Nineteen People's Hearing That She Is Not Going To Ask Anything Else In That Yard And Nobody Thanked Her And The Lane Count Is Twenty-Nine And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and twenty-two days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and eighty-one. The train on that siding has stood one thousand and ninety-seven days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and eleven days. Seven hundred and seventy-two days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Four hundred and eighty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Four hundred and ninety-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and sixty-one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and sixty-four days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and forty marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and twenty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is six and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and a woman said out loud at about eleven that she is not going to ask anything else in that yard, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank one hundred and ninety-seven mornings and moved this morning from one hundred and ninety-six. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and forty-seven mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and sixty-seven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is twenty-nine and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and that page at the back of that book still carries thirty-three lines and the lot book has none of them, and three of them were read out once and not heard by anybody in this district.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and eighth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and a woman said out loud that she is not going to ask anything else and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard her.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and twenty-second night of that run, having slept on five hundred and twenty-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and twelfth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the twenty-third morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood eighty-two days, a woman stopped asking out loud and nobody thanked her for stopping, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the man who digs loam was at the bottom of that lane again, and he said that he had seen the man of thirty-eight on the top of that hill at first light and had said good morning to him and had got a word back, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them said it after him out of step with each other.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* at the same moment and out of step with each other, and about four of them said it after him out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that a man who says four figures out loud twice a morning has never once been early and has never once been late, and that about four of them have started checking.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is twenty-nine, and it has not moved since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning, and he said no figure this morning, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen has now said no figure three mornings out of the last four and has not given a reason on any of them.

---

Adrian Vale was at the foot of that bank before the boards were up and he was sitting on the low wall at about eleven feet below the top of that bank with his boots in the dust and nothing in his hands, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who walked nine miles up and nine miles down yesterday sat down on a stone at about seven in the morning and waited, and that about four of them have said they do not know what he was waiting for. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has been at the foot of that bank one hundred and sixty-seven mornings has never once sat down on the low wall at eleven feet below the boards, and that about four of them said this morning he did it twice and about four of them said they counted.

---

At about ten Mavis Dorr came down that bank with a question she has been carrying since the one hundred and fifty-seventh morning and she put it at about four feet off the near end of the second table in about nineteen people's hearing, and the clerk said no out loud and gave no reason, and Mavis Dorr said that she had a second one and that it was hers and not the yard's, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard both of those things.

---

Then she stood there and did not say it, and about four of them have said since that a woman stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall with a question in her mouth, and that she put it back in her mouth and kept it there in front of about nineteen people.

---

At about eleven she said the thing instead, and she said it in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard it.

---

"I am not going to ask anything else in this yard," she said. "That is all of it. I have been the one who asks things in this yard and I am going to stop, and I am not going to say why, and you are not going to get me to say why, and I would like it noticed that I have said it out loud in front of all of you and not walked off and done it quietly. I have asked a man in this yard for a name and been refused in front of all of you and I am not going to say anything about that either, because it is not the same as this and I am not going to mix it up with this in front of all of you."

---

The man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart asked her out loud, in about nineteen people's hearing, at about four feet off the first table, why she was stopping, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard the question and about four of them have said since that it was the first thing anybody in that yard had the standing to ask her and that she had already said out loud that nobody was going to get an answer to it.

---

"I said I was not going to say why," she said.

---

And nobody at the foot of that bank thanked her for it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that they noticed it was not going to be thanked and that they had noticed it in advance, and that about four of them have not said what they are going to do about having noticed it in advance.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and nobody asked her anything, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the whole of this morning went past that woman and that not one of the about nineteen people who passed her said a word to her about any of it.

---

About half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into the middle of that yard and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and about four of them have said since that a yard without a woman who asks things has got a man who says a figure into about four hundred yards of air and that these are not the same shape and that only one of the two shapes has somebody in it.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the fifty-sixth time running.

---

Nobody asked him what any of them is for. At about ten past four Adrian Vale said one thing out loud, to about nineteen people, at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it, and nobody answered it.

---

"I am going to be at these boards this afternoon," he said. "If anybody in this yard has something for me they should say it before the light goes off them, and I will stand here while the light goes off them."

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet stood there until the light went off the boards at about half past four and nobody said anything to him, and about four of them have said since that a man of thirty-eight asked a yard of about nineteen people to hand him a thing and got nothing, and that about four of them said they had had something and about four of them said they had not had anything, and that about four of them did not say which. About nine people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a woman who has asked things in that yard for a long time took a morning back off a boy of seventeen at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and that about four of them have said the boy gave it back the moment she put her hand on it, and about four of them have said they did not see him do that.

---

The page at the back of that book carries thirty-three lines and none of the thirty-three is in the lot book and three of them nobody in this district heard read. Six is what a clerk has counted and it is six and it did not move this morning. The count of what this district has made is fourteen and there is no fifteenth of it. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on ten mornings running. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out nobody has ever sat down to it in this basin. The lane count is twenty-nine and it did not move, and a woman stopping asking does not move it either. Ninety-eight is the toll and it is spent, and there is no column in this district for spending a thing like that twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall became public lot on the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning and nothing since has made it a payment of the fifth of the five. Four hundred and fifty days is what that bid stood at, it was never run, and it is not a lot. A woman who has asked things in this yard for a long time said out loud this morning that she is not going to ask anything else, and nobody thanked her, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
