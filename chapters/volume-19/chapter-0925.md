# Chapter 0925: The TWO HUNDRED AND NINTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Clerk Said Out Loud At About Four O'Clock What A Line On A Page Is Not And It Is Four Things And The Four Of Them Are It Is Not A Rule And It Is Not A Scale And It Is Not A Payment And It Is Not A Trust Sentence And Nobody Asked Her To Say Any Of It And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Nine Lines And It Did Not Carry A Fortieth And Saying What It Is Not Is Not A Reason And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and sixty-four days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and twenty-three. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and thirty-nine days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and fifty-three days. Eight hundred and fourteen days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and twenty-eight days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and thirty-three days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and three days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and six days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and forty-two days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and eighty-two marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and sixty-eight marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before the two hundred and eighth morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and thirty-nine mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and thirty-eight. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and eighty-nine mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and nine, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and eighth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-nine lines and it did not carry a fortieth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and fiftieth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and sixty-fourth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and sixty-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fifty-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the sixty-fifth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and twenty-four days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The morning after a name was said out loud in that yard is not different from any other morning at about half past eight, and that is the whole of what happened about it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard that has had something said in it tends to look at the boards the next morning to see whether the something is still on them.

The woman who keeps a scale came down that bank with the beam on her hip and set her scale up at about four feet off the near end of the second table and said nothing out loud to anybody, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that nobody asked her one thing this morning and that about four of them have said that not asking her is not the same as not wanting to.

---

At about half past ten the man of about thirty-four said one thing out loud at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people heard it.

"A clerk has told this yard what a line on a page is not," he said. "That is four things said out loud in a yard that has not been told one thing yet that a line on a page is."

---

And nobody answered it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard which has spent ten mornings listening to people say things out loud wanted one of the four things said yesterday to be a thing it could put its hand on, and that nothing was said out loud this morning that anybody could put a hand on.

---

The turner had his wheel going with a piece of ash in it and got the shavings into the heap he keeps kicking back into one place. The man who digs loam was at the north end with his barrow. The man of about thirty-two put his table up and put his apron on and did not look at anybody. The man of about thirty-four stayed at about four feet off the near end of the second table with his hands in his pockets.

The clerk stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with the front of that book open from about eleven until about half past twelve and said nothing out loud to anybody, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came and looked at the front of it and went away again. The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack of something at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody.

And about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and sixty-fourth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and sixty-three of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fifty-fourth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the ninety-eighth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And then the clerk stood up off the second table at about four feet off the near end of the second table and said four things out loud in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, and nobody had asked her for any of the four of them.

"It is not a rule," she said. "It is not a scale. It is not a payment. And it is not a trust sentence."

---

And she said the four of them one after another in the same voice and at the same speed and did not put anything between them, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk said four things this morning and that about four of them said the four of them are all the same size and that about four of them have not said what the size is.

And about four of them said the four things were all the same size because she said them in the same voice at the same speed and did not put anything between them.

And then she wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it, and about four of them have said since that saying what a thing is not out loud in about nineteen people's hearing is not the same as giving a reason out loud.

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and the boards went down the way they go down.

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a yard has now got four things said out loud about a line on a page and not one thing said out loud about what it is, and that about four of them have not said whether that is a strange way round for the yard or only for them.

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-nine lines and it did not carry a fortieth this morning and not one of the thirty-nine is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and twenty-four days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and twenty-four days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and this morning went like the others, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk has now said out loud four things that a line on a page is not, and that about four of them said the four of them are all the same size, and that about four of them have not said what the size is.
