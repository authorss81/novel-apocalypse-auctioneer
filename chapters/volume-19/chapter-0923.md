# Chapter 0923: The TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month The Man Of About Thirty-Two Who Puts Tables Up Has Put A Table Up At The Foot Of That Bank For The Two Hundred And Sixth Morning And Has Never Been Asked Why And This Morning He Said Out Loud That Somebody Else's Page Is Not His Table And It Is The First Refusal In This Stretch Made By A Person Who Has Nothing To Do With It And The Page At The Back Did Not Move Because A Refusal Is Not A Reason And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and sixty-two days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and twenty-one. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and thirty-seven days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and fifty-one days. Eight hundred and twelve days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and twenty-six days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and thirty-one days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Six hundred and one days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and four days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and forty days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and eighty marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and sixty-six marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and no line on that page was written for a reason before the two hundred and eighth morning because that page was not a place a reason could go before the two hundred and eighth morning, and that reason is not counted anywhere, and no figure has been given out loud in that yard for the number of mornings a reason was said out loud in it.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and thirty-seven mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and thirty-six. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and eighty-seven mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and seven, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The table of the man of about thirty-two has stood at the foot of that bank for the two hundred and sixth morning and it is not the second table and it is not the near rail. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-eight lines and it did not carry a thirty-ninth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and forty-eighth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and sixty-second night of that run, having slept on five hundred and sixty-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fifty-second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the sixty-third morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and twenty-two days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down. At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on the stone afterwards and left it there a while.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said no figure, and said so out loud, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

The man of about thirty-two came down that bank at about half past eight with his table on his head and a folded apron under his arm, the way he has come down that bank every morning. He set the table up at the foot of that bank about four feet along from the second table, squared it with the back of his heel, unfolded the apron and put it on, and turned round once to look at the top of that bank before he started on anything.

---

And that is how the morning went for a while, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has stood a table at the foot of that bank for two hundred and six mornings without saying one word out loud has been looked at, this morning more than he ever has, by people who have nothing to look at.

---

And that looking did not stop when the work started, and about four of them said the man of about thirty-two knew it was going on and did not do one thing about it, and about four of them said he did not look up off the table more than four times between about nine and about eleven.

---

Then at about eleven the man of about thirty-two put both hands flat on the top of his own table and said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people in that yard heard it, and he said it without raising it at all.

"That page at the back of that book is not my table," he said. "It is not my table and it is not my work and nothing that gets written on it is anything to do with me, and I have never once asked why anybody does anything with any of it and I am not going to start."

---

And about nine people at the top of eleven feet went quiet, and about four of them have said since that a yard full of people who have spent ten mornings saying things out loud went quiet in about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall because a man who has never said anything said something, and that about four of them have not said whether the quiet was for him or for themselves.

---

And then he picked up the cloth and went along the top of his table, and about four people at the top of eleven feet watched a man say one thing and go back to work, and about four of them have said since that the thing he said was that a thing belongs to somebody, and that about four of them have not said whether that is a rule or an opinion.

---

The man of about thirty-four was at about four feet off the near end of the second table all morning and did not say one word, and at about half past twelve he turned round and looked at the man of about thirty-two's table for a while and then turned back.

---

The clerk stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with the front of that book open and said nothing out loud to anybody, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came and looked at the front of it and went away again. The woman who keeps a scale weighed something at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody.

---

And about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

The man of thirty-eight was at the top of that bank at about two with his hands showing and he came down the slope and stood about nine feet above the near end of the first table for a while with the light on him, and nobody in that yard asked him one thing about that morning.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and sixty-second night of that run, having slept on five hundred and sixty-one of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fifty-second afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the ninety-sixth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

And then the clerk wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a page that has taken a line twice in forty mornings did not take a line this morning, and that about four of them have said that a man refusing a thing out loud is not the same as a man giving a reason out loud, and that about four of them have not said whether it ought to be.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and the boards went down the way they go down.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has stood a table at the foot of that bank for two hundred and six mornings said one sentence this morning and then went along the top of his table with a cloth, and that about four of them have not said what a yard is supposed to do with a sentence like that one.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-eight lines and it did not carry a thirty-ninth this morning and not one of the thirty-eight is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a line on the back of that book is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a morning that goes like the others is not a page coming down that lane, and the measure of this stretch does not go up that lane at all. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and twenty-two days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and twenty-two days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and this morning went like the others, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has said nothing for two hundred and six mornings has said one thing, and that about four of them said the one thing was that a thing belongs to somebody, and that about four of them have not said whether that is a rule or an opinion.
