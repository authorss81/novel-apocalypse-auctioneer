# Chapter 911: The ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-FIFTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Boy Of Seventeen Said A Figure Out Loud At The Near End Of The First Table Before The Clerk's Hand Had Moved And About Four Of Them Have Said Since That A Figure Said Out Loud In That Yard Is A Morning And Not An Answer And That About Four Of Them Said A Word Is A Figure And Nobody In That Yard Said What Any Of The Figures Said Out Loud This Morning Is A Figure Of And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and fifty days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and nine. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and twenty-five days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and thirty-nine days. Eight hundred days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and fourteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and nineteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and eighty-nine days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and ninety-two days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and twenty-eight days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and sixty-eight marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and fifty-four marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and twenty-five mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and twenty-four. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and seventy-five mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and ninety-five, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and thirty-sixth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fiftieth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and fortieth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fifty-first morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and ten days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up. The light came over that bank in a thin line first and then all at once, and it found the eleven feet, and the low wall, and the second table, and the near rail with the frost still in the hollow of it along the bottom where nobody walks.

---

About nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and the frost on the stones took the sound out of their boots, and the form was still under the clean page at about four feet off the near end of the first table with the space on it empty, and about four of them have said since that a piece of paper that has lain under another piece of paper for ten mornings is not going to be put away this morning by anybody.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them without stopping, the way a man says four things he has said for years, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and the man of thirty-eight was at the top of that bank with his hands showing and had not come down the slope, and about four of them have said since that a man who has stood at the top of that bank since before the middle of the winter has not come down it once this week, and that about four of them have not said why.

---

At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said a figure, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"Two hundred and nineteen," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, the way they say a figure, and about four of them have said since that a boy has been standing at the near end of that first table saying a number into about four hundred yards of air since before the middle of the winter and has never once said what it is about, and the clerk did not look up, and she wrote the morning on the board in her own hand where she writes it, and about four of them have said since that a figure said out loud at those boards before the clerk's hand has moved has never once gone into anything and that nobody in that yard has asked it to.

---

Then at about half past eight the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came down off the boards with the cloth in his hand and stood about four feet off the near end of the second table and said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, and nobody asked him anything before he said it.

---

"A figure said out loud in this yard is a morning," he said, "and not an answer, and there is a difference and most of you have stopped noticing it."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who says a number into about four hundred yards of air every morning at about half past one said a sentence out loud at about half past eight this morning and that nobody in that yard answered him, and that about four of them said the sentence is the same one that has been said in that yard for ten mornings and that nobody has said whether anybody agrees with it.

---

At about ten the man who made the stone came across with his chisel in his apron, set the stone on the low wall and looked at the boards longer than he usually does, and about eleven the clerk turned the lot book round on those boards, which she does most mornings, and opened it at the front and left it open there, and nobody in that yard said anything about the front of that book and about four of them have said since that there are five lines in it and that every one of the five has a morning against it.

---

At about eleven the man at the end of the second table put his hand flat on the table and said one thing out loud to nobody in particular, and about nineteen people in that yard heard it, and nobody answered him.

---

"A word is a figure," he said, "and anybody who says it is not is saying a thing about the mouth and not about the figure."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man at the end of a table has now said out loud in about nineteen people's hearing that a word and a number are the same kind of thing, and that about four of them said a clerk said a word out loud at about eleven on the one hundred and ninety-fourth morning and nobody asked her what it was a figure of, and that that yard has now got a clerk who has said a word out loud and a boy who says a number out loud and about nine people who say both after them, and that about four of them have not said whether any of those three is the thing the tally pays for, and that about nine people in that yard can all say a figure out loud this morning and not one of them has said what a figure is of.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack of something at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that she has said nothing on every morning of this stretch and that nobody in that yard has taken it as a refusal.

---

And about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does, and about four of them have said since that a man who said a sentence out loud at about half past eight this morning and then said his number at about half past one has said two different kinds of thing out loud in one morning for the first time this stretch.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fiftieth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-nine of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the eighty-fourth time running, and nobody asked him anything, and then the clerk wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and about four of them have said since that a figure agreed about out loud is not a decision and that nobody in that yard put a question this morning and that that is the whole of what happened in it.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a figure said out loud at those boards is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a figure said out loud is not a page coming down that lane. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and ten days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and ten days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and at about ten to eight this morning a boy of seventeen said a figure out loud at the near end of the first table before the clerk's hand had moved and nobody in that yard has said what it is a figure of.
