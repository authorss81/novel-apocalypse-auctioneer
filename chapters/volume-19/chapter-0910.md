# Chapter 910: The ONE HUNDRED AND NINETY-FOURTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month At About Eleven A Clerk Read The Page At The Back Of That Lot Book To Herself And Not Out Loud At About Four Feet Off The Near End Of The Second Table In About Nineteen People's Hearing And Said One Word And The Word Was Three And Nobody In That Yard Asked Her What It Was A Figure Of And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and forty-nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and eight. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and twenty-four days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and thirty-eight days. Seven hundred and ninety-nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and thirteen days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and eighteen days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and eighty-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and ninety-one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and twenty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and sixty-seven marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and fifty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and twenty-four mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and twenty-three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and seventy-four mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and ninety-four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and thirty-fifth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-ninth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and thirty-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fiftieth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and nine days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and the form was still under the clean page at about four feet off the near end of the first table with the space on it empty, and about four of them have said since that a thing that has lain on those boards for fifteen mornings is not going to be put away this morning by anybody, and that about four of them said nobody in that yard has given a reason for leaving it there and that about four of them have said nobody in that yard has given one for leaving anything anywhere.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and the man of thirty-eight was at the top of that bank with his hands showing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who went up that lane in the afternoon of the one hundred and eighty-ninth morning and came back down it in the evening has not said what he went to look at and that about four of them said he said he had not got it yet and that about four of them have said since that he has not got used to saying a thing like that twice.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved on the one hundred and eightieth morning from twenty-nine, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the boy said no figure out loud on a morning when the whole of that yard was waiting to hear what a clerk would say, and that about four of them said a boy who has said a figure on about three mornings in ten knows which mornings those are and that about four of them have not said how he knows.

---

Then at about eleven the clerk took the lot book off the boards and turned it round and opened it at the back, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet were within about four feet of her and about nineteen people were in that yard, and she put one finger on the top line of that page and moved it down the page and her mouth was moving and nothing came out of it that anybody in that yard could hear, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk read a page to herself in about four hundred yards of air.

---

And when she got to the bottom of it she stopped and took her finger off the page and said one word out loud, in the ordinary voice, and about nineteen people in that yard heard it.

---

"Three," she said.

---

And nobody repeated it, and nobody asked her what it was a figure of, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a clerk who has said no out loud four times without a reason read a page to herself in front of about nineteen people this morning and said one word out loud, and that about four of them said the word is a figure and that about four of them said it is not an answer to anything anybody asked in that yard, and that about four of them have not said which of those two they are going to go on believing.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that that yard has been holding a figure about a question it cannot ask for a long time now and that this morning it is holding a figure about a question it does not know how to put, and that about four of them said the first one has been in that yard longer than the second one and that about four of them said the second one came out of a person's mouth in about nineteen people's hearing and that about four of them have said since that neither of those is an answer and that about four of them have not said what it is.

---

And the man of about thirty-four was at the near end of the second table and heard the word and said nothing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who knows three people and would not say which three was standing nine feet from a clerk who has just read a page to herself and said a number, and that about four of them said he did not say one single word after it and that about four of them have not said what they think he is keeping to himself.

---

And the man at the end of the second table said one thing out loud, at the end of that table, in the ordinary voice, and he said it to nobody in particular and nobody answered him.

---

"That is a morning," he said, "and not an answer, and anybody in this yard who puts those two together is going to be wrong about one of them."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man at the end of a table has now said out loud in that yard what about four of them have been saying since the word was said, and that about four of them said he said it after the word and not before it, and that about four of them have said since that it would have been a different thing entirely if he had said it before.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack of something at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and forty-ninth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and forty-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the eighty-third time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched a clerk do that an hour after she read the page to herself, and about four of them have said since that a page that has been read to oneself has not been read, and that about four of them said that is not a reason for anything, and that about four of them have said since that a clerk who knows that better than anybody in that yard wrote the morning on it in her own hand anyway.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and about four of them have said since that the last morning of that stretch of ten is the morning a clerk said one word out loud at those boards, and that nobody in that yard has done one single thing about it since the light went off.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and a word said out loud at about eleven is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a page read to oneself is not a page coming down that lane. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and nine days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and nine days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and at about eleven this morning a clerk read the page at the back of that lot book to herself in front of about nineteen people and said one word out loud, and the word was three, and about nineteen people in that yard heard it, and it is not an answer to anything anybody asked this morning, and nobody in that yard has called it one.
