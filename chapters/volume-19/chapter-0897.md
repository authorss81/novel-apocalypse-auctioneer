# Chapter 897: The ONE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FIRST Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month About Nineteen People In That Yard Stood And Looked At A Form And The Man Of About Forty-Eight Who Keeps A Tally Said Out Loud That The Heading Over That Space Is Not The Heading Over The One Name In That Column And The Two Headings Are Not The Same Heading And Neither Of Them Is A Rule And The Page At The Back Did Not Move And The Lane Count Is Thirty And Has Not Moved Since

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and thirty-six days is the age of that figure.

The board carries seven hundred and ninety-five. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and eleven days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and twenty-five days. Seven hundred and eighty-six days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and five days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and seventy-five days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is six hundred and seventy-eight days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and fourteen days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and fifty-four marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and forty marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and eleven mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and ten. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and sixty-one mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is one hundred and eighty-one, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and has not moved since. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and twenty-second of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-sixth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and twenty-sixth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the thirty-seventh morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood ninety-six days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him and about four of them have said since that a boy of seventeen has now said no figure on four mornings out of the last eight.

---

At about a quarter to seven the boards were not up and the light came up over that bank and found the eleven feet and the low wall and the second table, and the form was still lying where it was left at about four feet off the near end of the first table, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet saw it before the boards went up, and about four of them have said since that a piece of paper that came down that lane at about a quarter to ten and has been on those boards since has been looked at by about nineteen people this morning and no more.

---

At about a quarter to eight the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet said *as sent* out of step with each other, and about four of them said it while looking at a piece of paper and about four of them said it while not.

---

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight, which is thirty, and it moved yesterday morning from twenty-nine, and he said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the lane count has a figure in it that somebody else put there and that about four of them said the boy says it the same either way and about four of them said that is the correct way to say a figure that came down a lane.

---

Then about nineteen people in that yard looked at that form, standing round it at about four feet off the near end of the first table, in about four hundred yards of air, and about four of them said out loud that the space on it is for a name, and about four of them said that whatever else is on it, that is the one thing on it that anybody in that yard has read.

---

And then the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally put his thumb on the heading over that space and left it there for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and then he took it off and said one thing out loud, at about four feet off the near end of the second table, in about nineteen people's hearing.

---

"That is not the heading over the one name in that column," he said, "and if you want to know what it is over, then it is over nothing, because there is nothing under it."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that the two headings are not the same heading, and that about four of them said one of them still says whoever and the other one does not say whoever, and that about four of them said neither of the two of them is a rule and that about four of them said the yard has now got two headings and no joiner between them.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that about nine people in that yard looked at both this morning and about four of them said nobody in that yard knows where either of those headings was written and about four of them said nobody has asked, and that about four of them have said the yard has been asking questions out loud for nine mornings and this is the first thing it has wanted to ask about and has not asked.

---

And then the man at the end of the second table said one thing out loud about the two headings, in the ordinary voice, and he said it to nobody in particular, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard it and did not answer him.

---

"Two hands," he said, "or one hand at two times. Both of those are possible from where I am standing and I cannot get past those two from here."

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man at the end of a table has said out loud that a yard of about nineteen people cannot tell two pieces of paper apart this morning and that about four of them said he stopped at two possibilities and did not say what the third would be, and that about four of them said nobody asked him for a third and about four of them have not said whether they wanted one.

---

Nobody filled the space, and the young woman who brought that page down that lane was not in that yard this morning and went back up it at about half past four yesterday, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that nobody in that yard knows whether she wrote the heading on that page or whether she carried it down with somebody else's heading on it, and that about four of them said nobody has asked her and about four of them said she is not here to ask.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a bar of iron at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and he put his hand flat on his own coat afterwards and went to stand about nine feet further along, which is what he does.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and thirty-sixth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and thirty-five of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the seventieth time running, and nobody asked him anything.

---

Then the clerk turned the page at the back of that lot book round and wrote the morning on it in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet watched her do it, and about four of them have said since that a morning on which about nineteen people in that yard read a heading out loud took no line on that page, and that about four of them said a heading is not an asking, and that about four of them have said since that this is the third morning in a row that something has arrived in that yard or been said out loud in it and the page has not taken a line for any of it.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in that yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and about four of them have said since that it is the only thing that gets said out loud at the end of a day in that yard that nobody writes down.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-six lines and it did not carry a thirty-seventh this morning and not one of the thirty-six is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and two headings with nothing under either of them is not a payment of it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a page lying on a board is not a page coming down a lane. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the heading over the space on the form that came down that lane is not that heading, and nobody in that yard knows where either one of them was written. The record has stood ninety-six days and nobody in that yard has asked what it is ninety-six days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man.
