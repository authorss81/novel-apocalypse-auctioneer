# Chapter 0920: The TWO HUNDRED AND FOURTH Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Morning That Went Like The Others And About Four People At The Top Of Eleven Feet Who Have Said Since That They Do Not Know What The Word Was A Figure Of And That About Four Of Them Said They Are Not Going To Keep Looking And That About Four Of Them Have Not Said Why They Said That And A Man At The End Of The Second Table Said Nothing Out Loud At All This Morning And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And It Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and fifty-nine days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and eighteen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and thirty-four days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and forty-eight days. Eight hundred and nine days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and twenty-three days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and twenty-eight days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and ninety-eight days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred and one days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and thirty-seven days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and seventy-seven marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and sixty-three marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and thirty-four mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and thirty-three. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and eighty-four mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and four, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and forty-fifth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifty-ninth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifty-eight of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and forty-ninth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the sixtieth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and nineteen days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said no figure this morning, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

The form was still under the clean page at about four feet off the near end of the first table with the space on it empty, and the lot book was on the boards turned round with five lines in it and a column with one name in it and the heading over that column still saying whoever. At about half past eight the man of thirty-eight was at the top of that bank with his hands showing, and he came down the slope for the first time in several days and stood about nine feet above the near end of the first table, and nobody in that yard asked him one thing about where he had been that morning.

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who has been at the top of that bank since before the middle of the winter came down it this morning and stood in the sun, and that about four of them said nobody said anything to him and that about four of them said the man of about thirty-four was four feet off the near end of the second table and looked at him. At about nine the man who digs loam was at the north end with his barrow, and the man who makes things round had his wheel going and a piece of ash in it, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came through the gap in the bank and went through that yard and did not stop, and nobody in that yard said one word about any of it. At about half past nine a woman of about fifty-two came down that bank and stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table and looked at the form under the clean page for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall, and she asked nothing, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a person who asked a question out loud in that yard once has now come down that bank three times without asking anything.

The clerk stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with the front of that book open from about ten until about twelve and said nothing out loud to anybody, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came and looked at the front of it and went away again, and the woman of about fifty-two went back up that bank at about ten to twelve and nobody watched her go up it this time.

And about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that nobody in that yard has put what is on the front of that book anywhere today, and that about four of them said nobody in that yard has asked for what is at the back of it to be put anywhere either. At about twelve the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came off the boards and looked at the form under the clean page and did not touch it, and at about one the man at the end of the second table said nothing out loud at all this morning and worked at the far end of it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man who said the fifth of the five is unpaid two mornings ago has said nothing since, and that about four of them have not said whether that is a man thinking or a man finished. The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack of something at about one and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other.
