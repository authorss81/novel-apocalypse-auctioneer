# Chapter 0919: The TWO HUNDRED AND THIRD Morning After The Thirtieth Of The Eighth Month A Man At The End Of The Second Table Said Out Loud At About One That The Fifth Of The Five Is Unpaid And Nobody Thanked Him For Saying It And Nobody Entered Anything And Nobody Asked About It And The Ninth Of The Nine Printed Nights Reached Seven Hundred Days On Those Boards This Morning And Not One Person In That Yard Looked At It And The Boy Said A Figure At About Ten To Eight And The Page At The Back Carries Thirty-Seven Lines And It Did Not Carry A Thirty-Eighth And The Lane Count Is Thirty And It Did Not Move

The figure on the sheet at that gatepost is four hundred and eleven. Six hundred and fifty-eight days is the age of that figure.

The board carries eight hundred and seventeen. The train on that siding has stood one thousand one hundred and thirty-three days. Nobody has entered anything for eight hundred and forty-seven days. Eight hundred and eight days separate the second of January and this morning.

Four hundred and fifty days is how long the bid was open, and it was not run, and it is not open. Five hundred and twenty-two days is how far behind the figure on the second line of that lot book is. Five hundred and twenty-seven days is how long the rule said out loud in that yard has stood. Five hundred and ninety-seven days is how long it has been since the first day of the eighth month. The ninth of the nine printed nights is seven hundred days back and no night has been named. A body four hundred miles off is five hundred and thirty-six days past a printing it did not make.

Four hundred and seventy-six marks have been cut off that board, and there are four hundred and sixty-two marks in chalk along the edge of that second table. The count of counted months is seven and did not move. The number of documents this district does not own is four. The number of places in this district where those three lines can be read is four. The number of conditions with no end on it is four, and the count of the five things this district does not have is five and is not paid, and nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything out loud this morning and the page at the back did not move and the clerk wrote the morning on it in her own hand, and the count of things this district has made did not move and the five things are still not paid. A reason was said out loud in that yard on the one hundred and eighty-second morning and it was before that page was a place a reason could go, and it is not said out loud again on any morning of this stretch, and that page did not take a line this morning.

The second table has stood at the foot of that bank two hundred and thirty-three mornings and moved this morning from two hundred and thirty-two. The near rail has stood on the near side of it for one hundred and eighty-three mornings and nobody is to thank the man who fitted it. The number of mornings a man has been at the foot of that bank is two hundred and three, on the word of the man who keeps a tally, said before it was entered. The count of things this district has made is fourteen and did not move. The lane count is thirty and it did not move this morning and it is not moved by a decision. The lot book is on the boards with five lines on it and a column with one name in it, and the heading over that column still says whoever, and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the page at the back carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning, and the first asking was not on that page and it was not on anything and nobody is able to recover it.

The man of fifty-six said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and again at about four and got them both times, and that is the six hundred and forty-fourth of those mornings, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard him, and nobody asked him anything out loud this morning and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not ask him anything.

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifty-eighth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifty-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him for the one hundred and forty-eighth afternoon running, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that wall and was not asked one question this morning, which is the fifty-ninth morning running of that, and about nine people were standing on ground that has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and there are about eleven feet between that wall and the top of this bank and about four miles between the top of this bank and the culvert, and neither of those two distances is what anybody in that yard thinks about on a morning.

The record has stood one hundred and eighteen days, the page at the back did not take a line this morning and the morning itself was written on it in her own hand, and nothing that came down that lane is in the lot book or on the page at the back of that book, and the second space on that form is still empty.

Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards at about ten to eight and said a figure out loud before the clerk's hand had moved, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him.

---

The boards went up after the light found the eleven feet and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came down that bank at the hour they come down, and the light lay on the boards flat and showed the whole of what was on them, all nine lines of it, for about as long as it takes a man of fifty-six to say four figures off a wall. The man of fifty-six had said the four off that wall at about a quarter to eight and got all four of them, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet had said *as sent* out of step with each other, and nobody in that yard had asked him anything. At about ten to eight Orren Pike said the lane count out loud at those boards, which is thirty, and then he said a figure, and he said it before the clerk's hand had moved.

---

"Two hundred and twenty-three," he said, at the near end of the first table.

---

And about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other, and not one of them looked up at those boards while they said it, and about four of them have said since that the ninth of the nine printed nights came to seven hundred on that board this morning and that not one person in that yard looked at the line that says so. And about four of them have said since that about nine people stand under those boards twice a morning and that about four of them said the ninth line has been there longer than most of what is on the boards and that about four of them have not said why it is the one nobody looks at.

---

The form was still under the clean page at about four feet off the near end of the first table with the space on it empty, and the lot book was on the boards turned round with the front of it open, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that twelve mornings of a form with one space on it under a clean page is longer than most things anybody in that yard has ever been asked to wait about.

---

At about half past nine the man who digs loam was at the north end with his barrow and the man who makes things round had his wheel going, and the man of about twenty-nine who drives the cart came through the gap in the bank and went through that yard and did not stop, which is the third time this week he has gone through without stopping. At about ten the man of about thirty-four was at the near end of the second table with his hands in his pockets, and the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally came off the boards and stood at about four feet off the near end of the first table and looked at the form under the clean page and did not touch it and did not say anything about it.

---

The clerk stood at about four feet off the near end of the second table with the front of that book open and said nothing out loud to anybody, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet came and looked at the front of it and went away again, and nobody in that yard asked the man of fifty-six one thing. At about one the man at the end of the second table put down what he was holding and said one thing out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nineteen people's hearing, and about nine people at the top of eleven feet heard every word of it. "The fifth of the five is unpaid," he said.

---

And nobody in that yard said one word back, and nobody asked him what the fifth of the five is, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man said a thing out loud in that yard that everybody in that yard has known for longer than anybody can remember and that about four of them said he is the first one to have said it in about nineteen people's hearing.

---

And nobody in that yard thanked him for saying it, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a man is not thanked for saying out loud a thing that is already true, and that about four of them said he did not go near the boards afterwards to put it anywhere.

---

And nothing was entered, and about four people at the top of eleven feet have said since that a thing being five and unpaid is not the same as a thing being asked about, and that about four of them said nobody in that yard has asked about the fifth of the five.

---

And about four of them have said since that nobody has asked about it and that that is the whole of what is known about it, and that about four of them have said since that the yard has had one reason out loud since the morning before the one where it had it, and that about four of them said naming a thing as unpaid is not giving a reason for it.

---

The woman who keeps a scale weighed a sack of something at about one and a bit and the needle came to rest a long way short of the mark where the lead weights begin, and she said nothing to anybody, and about half past one the man of about forty-eight who keeps a tally said his number into about four hundred yards of air and nobody wrote it down, and about four people at the top of eleven feet said it after him out of step with each other.

---

And about four of them have said since that the second space on that form is still empty and the heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and that about four of them said a man saying a thing is unpaid has not filled anything in and has not asked for anything to be filled in.

---

At about half past three a man of about sixty-four was at the foot of that low wall on his five hundred and fifty-eighth night of that run, having slept on five hundred and fifty-seven of them, and a man of sixty-one was on the stones about four feet off him, and they said good afternoon, and a man of sixty-one said good afternoon and said nothing else, and nobody went near the man at the foot of that wall.

---

At about four o'clock the man of fifty-six said the four off that wall and got all four of them, and then he put his hand flat on the stone and said, in the ordinary voice, that he does not know what any of them is for, and about nineteen people heard it for the ninety-second time running, and nobody asked him anything, and about four people at the top of eleven feet heard him say it a quarter of an hour after a man at the end of a table had said a different thing out loud in the same yard.

---

Then the clerk wrote the morning on the page at the back of that lot book in her own hand under the last line and she did not take a line for it, and that page carries thirty-seven lines and none of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and nothing was entered anywhere else in that yard this morning either.

---

At about half past four the light went off the boards and the goodnight that gets said at the end of a day in this yard was said by about four people out of about nineteen and about nine people at the top of eleven feet did not say it and did not go up, and about four of them have said since that a man named a thing out loud as unpaid this afternoon and that about four of them said it is going to be unpaid on the next ten mornings as well.

---

The page at the back of that lot book carries thirty-seven lines and it did not carry a thirty-eighth this morning and not one of the thirty-seven is in the lot book, and three of the first thirty-three were lines nobody in this district heard read. Seven is what a clerk has counted and it is seven and it did not go back this morning. This district has made fourteen things and the fourteen did not move. Five is what this district does not have and the fifth of the five is unpaid on every morning of this stretch, and saying that out loud at about one this afternoon is not a payment of it and nobody was thanked for it. A sheet at a gatepost carries four hundred and eleven and by how much that figure is out has never been done by anybody in this basin. The lane count is thirty and it did not move, and a figure said out loud at those boards is not a page coming down that lane. The toll is ninety-eight and it is spent and it may not be raised or lowered or paid twice. The ground at the foot of that low wall has been public lot since the afternoon of the one hundred and seventeenth morning, and a public lot is not a payment of the fifth of the five. The bid was appended at four hundred and fifty days and it was not run and it is not a lot and it will not be. The heading over the one name in that column still says whoever and the name does not satisfy the rule and the second space on that form is empty and nobody has asked for it to be filled, and the space on the form that came down that lane is a different space on a different piece of paper and it is empty as well, and nobody in that yard has filled either one of them. The record has stood one hundred and eighteen days and nobody in that yard asked what it is one hundred and eighteen days of. Nobody asked the man of fifty-six anything this morning and the morning was written on that page in a clerk's own hand and not one line was taken for it, and a man of thirty-eight has not been given a bed or a wage or an office and has not asked for any of the three and has not been told he is a good man, and at about one this afternoon a man at the end of the second table said the fifth of the five is unpaid and nobody entered anything and nobody thanked him.
